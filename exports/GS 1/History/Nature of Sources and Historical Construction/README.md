
## Introduction


History is reconstructed through **sources**. Ancient Indian history is mainly studied through:

1. **Archaeological Sources**
2. **Numismatic Sources (Coins)**
3. **Epigraphic Sources (Inscriptions)**
4. **Literary Sources**
5. **Foreign Accounts**
6. **Scientific & Village Studies**

This chapter explains:

- How historians reconstruct ancient Indian history.
- Which sources are most reliable.
- Limitations of each source.
- Importance of archaeology, inscriptions, coins, and literature.

---


# 1. Archaeological Sources (Material Remains)


## Meaning


Archaeology is the science of studying the past through:

- material remains,
- tools,
- pottery,
- bones,
- buildings,
- inscriptions,
- coins,
- burial sites, etc.

It is especially important for:

- **Prehistory**
- **Proto-history**
- Ancient periods where written records are absent.

---


# 2. Prehistory, Proto-history and History


| Term              | Meaning                                  |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Prehistory**    | Period with no written records           |
| **Proto-history** | Writing existed but not deciphered       |
| **History**       | Period with decipherable written records |


---


## Prehistoric India


Prehistoric sites contain:

- fossils,
- plants,
- animal remains,
- stone tools,
- climatic evidence.

These sites are usually found:

- on hill slopes,
- river terraces,
- plateaus,
- mountain regions.

### Importance


Help us know:

- climatic conditions,
- food habits,
- tools,
- hunting patterns,
- early human life.

---


## Proto-historic India


### Indus Valley Civilization

- Writing known by **mid-third millennium BC**
- Script still undeciphered
- Therefore classified as **Proto-historic**

### Chalcolithic Cultures

- Copper-Stone Age cultures
- No deciphered writing

---


## Historical Period

- Decipherable inscriptions begin from **3rd century BC**
- Ashokan inscriptions provide firm historical evidence.

---


# 3. Mounds and Excavations


## Mound


A mound is:

> an elevated land area covering remains of old settlements.

---


## Types of Mounds


### (1) Single-Culture Mound


Represents only one culture.


Examples:

- Painted Grey Ware culture
- Satavahana culture
- Kushan culture

---


### (2) Major-Culture Mound


One dominant culture + minor cultures.


---


### (3) Multi-Culture Mound


Several cultures succeeded one another.


Importance:

- Helps understand chronological development.

---


# 4. Types of Excavation


## (1) Vertical Excavation

- Digging downward.
- Shows sequence of cultures layer by layer.

### Importance


Provides:

- chronology,
- cultural sequence.

---


## (2) Horizontal Excavation

- Digging large area horizontally.

### Importance


Provides:

- complete picture of settlement,
- town planning,
- social life.

### Limitation


Very expensive.


---

# 7. Dating Methods


## Radiocarbon Dating (C-14 Dating)


### Principle


All living objects contain radioactive carbon (C14).


After death:

- C14 decays at uniform rate.

### Half-life of C14


T1/2=5568 yearsT_{1/2}=5568\ \text{years}T1/2=5568 years


Meaning:

- Half radioactive carbon disappears every 5568 years.

---


## Example


| Time Passed | Remaining C14 |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| 5568 years  | 1/2           |
| 11136 years | 1/4           |


---


## Limitation


Cannot date objects older than:


70000 years70000\ \text{years}70000 years

# 13. Importance of Coins


## (1) Political History


Coins help reconstruct:

- dynasties,
- kings,
- chronology.

Especially useful for:

- Indo-Greeks.

---


## (2) Economic History


Coins indicate:

- trade,
- commerce,
- monetization.

### Important Facts

- Post-Maurya period → many coins.
- Gupta period → maximum gold coins.
- Post-Gupta period → decline in coins.

Inference:

- decline in trade after Guptas.
