
# Overview


After the establishment of the **State** and **Varna-based Social Order**, ancient Indian thinkers identified **Four Purusharthas (Goals of Life)**:


| Goal   | Meaning                        | Related Text                   |
| ------ | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Dharma | Social order, duties, morality | Dharmashastra                  |
| Artha  | Wealth, economy, polity        | Arthashastra (Kautilya)        |
| Kama   | Physical pleasures             | Kamasutra                      |
| Moksha | Salvation/liberation           | Philosophical texts (Darshana) |


### Moksha

- Freedom from cycle of **Birth and Death (Samsara)**.
- Initially emphasized by **Gautama Buddha**.
- Later became the central theme of Brahmanical philosophy.

---


# Six Orthodox Schools (Shad-Darshana)


### Timeline


By the beginning of the Christian Era, six schools emerged:

1. Samkhya
2. Yoga
3. Nyaya
4. Vaisheshika
5. Mimamsa
6. Vedanta

# 1. SAMKHYA PHILOSOPHY


### Meaning

- Samkhya = "Count" or Enumeration.

### Founder

- Traditionally associated with **Kapila**.

---


## Early Samkhya


### Main Features

- No necessity of God in creation.
- World created through **Prakriti (Nature)**.
- Rational and scientific approach.
- Materialistic outlook.

### Sources of Knowledge


| Method     | Meaning           |
| ---------- | ----------------- |
| Pratyaksha | Perception        |
| Anumana    | Inference         |
| Shabda     | Hearing/Testimony |


### Salvation

- Achieved through **Real Knowledge (Jnana)**.
- Misery can be permanently removed.

---


## Later Samkhya (4th Century AD)


### New Element Added

- **Purusha (Spirit)** introduced.

### Creation Theory


```plain text
Prakriti + Purusha
        ↓
 Creation of World
```


### Significance

- Shift from Materialism → Spiritualism.

---


## Prelims Facts

- Earliest among six schools.
- Kapila associated with Samkhya.
- Initially atheistic/non-theistic.
- Emphasized knowledge for salvation.

---


# 2. YOGA PHILOSOPHY


### Core Idea


Salvation through:

- Meditation
- Physical discipline
- Sense control

---


## Main Practices


### Asana

- Physical postures.

### Pranayama

- Breathing exercises.

---


## Objective


```plain text
Control of senses
        ↓
Concentration
        ↓
Detachment from world
        ↓
Salvation
```


---


## Importance


### Scientific Aspect


Shows ancient knowledge of:

- Physiology
- Anatomy

### Limitation

- Encouraged withdrawal from worldly problems.

---


## Prelims Facts

- Focus on meditation.
- Asana and Pranayama central.
- Emphasis on discipline and concentration.

---


# 3. NYAYA PHILOSOPHY


### Meaning

- School of Analysis.
- System of Logic.

---


## Main Idea


Salvation through:

- Correct Knowledge
- Logical Reasoning

---


## Means of Testing Truth


| Method    | Meaning           |
| --------- | ----------------- |
| Inference | Logical deduction |
| Hearing   | Testimony         |
| Analogy   | Comparison        |


---


## Famous Nyaya Example


```plain text
There is fire on the mountain
        ↓
Because smoke is visible
        ↓
Wherever there is smoke,
there is fire (hearth example)
        ↓
Conclusion:
Mountain contains fire
```


---


## Significance

- Developed systematic reasoning.
- Influenced Indian logic and debate traditions.

---


## Prelims Facts

- Nyaya = Logic.
- Truth verified through reasoning.
- Promoted analytical thinking.

---


# 4. VAISHESHIKA PHILOSOPHY


### Focus


Study of:

- Matter
- Material Elements (Dravya)

---


## Material Elements

1. Earth
2. Water
3. Fire
4. Air
5. Ether (Akasha)

---


## Atom Theory


### Main Belief


All material objects consist of:

- Atoms (Paramanu)

---


## Contribution


### Beginning of Physics in India


```plain text
Atoms
   ↓
Combination
   ↓
Material Objects
```


---


## Limitation

- Scientific outlook diluted by:
    - Belief in God
    - Heaven
    - Salvation

---


## Prelims Facts

- Known for Atomism.
- Concerned with material substances.
- Earliest Indian physics tradition.

---


# 5. MIMAMSA PHILOSOPHY


### Meaning

- Art of Reasoning and Interpretation.

---


## Main Objective


Reasoning used to justify:

- Vedic Rituals
- Sacrifices

---


## Core Beliefs


### Vedas

- Eternal Truth.

### Salvation


Dependent on:

- Performance of rituals.

---


## Concept of Heaven


```plain text
Virtuous Actions
        ↓
Heaven
        ↓
Merit exhausted
        ↓
Return to Earth
```


### Moksha

- Permanent freedom from birth and death.

---


## Social Impact


### Promoted

- Vedic sacrifices
- Role of priests
- Brahmanical authority
- Varna hierarchy

---


## Significance


Used by Brahmanas to:

- Preserve ritual supremacy.
- Maintain social order.

---


## Prelims Facts

- Ritual-centric philosophy.
- Vedas regarded as eternal.
- Salvation linked to sacrifices.

---


# 6. VEDANTA PHILOSOPHY


### Meaning

- End of the Veda.

---


# Basic Text


### Brahmasutra


| Feature    | Detail                      |
| ---------- | --------------------------- |
| Author     | Badarayana                  |
| Date       | 2nd Century BC              |
| Importance | Fundamental text of Vedanta |


---


# Major Commentators


| Thinker  | Century         | View            |
| -------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Shankara | 9th Century AD  | Advaita         |
| Ramanuja | 12th Century AD | Vishishtadvaita |


---


# Shankara's View


### Brahma

- Without attributes (Nirguna).

### Salvation

- Through Knowledge (Jnana).

---


# Ramanuja's View


### Brahma

- With attributes (Saguna).

### Salvation

- Through Devotion (Bhakti).

---


# Fundamental Concepts


## Brahma

- Ultimate Reality.

## Maya

- World is unreal appearance.

## Atma

- Soul/Self.

---


## Relationship


```plain text
Atma = Brahma
        ↓
Knowledge of Atma
        ↓
Knowledge of Brahma
        ↓
Moksha
```


---


# Theory of Karma


### Karma


Present life determined by:

- Actions of previous birth.

### Punarjanma

- Rebirth.

### Consequence


Explained suffering through:

- Past actions
- Not social causes

---


## Significance


Promoted:

- Stability
- Continuity
- Acceptance of social order

---


## Prelims Facts

- Brahmasutra = core text.
- Badarayana = author.
- Shankara → Knowledge.
- Ramanuja → Devotion.
- Brahma = reality.
- Maya = illusion.

---


# CHARVAKA PHILOSOPHY (Lokayata)


## Importance


Most important Materialistic School.


---


## Meaning


### Lokayata


```plain text
Loka = World/Common People
Ayata = Derived from
```


Ideas derived from common people.


---


## Main Features


### Rejected

- God
- Brahma
- Heaven
- Rituals
- Salvation
- Supernatural forces

---


## Accepted


Only what can be perceived by:

- Eyes
- Ears
- Nose
- Tongue
- Skin

### Knowledge Source

- Sense perception only.

---


## Criticism of Brahmanas


Claimed:

- Rituals were designed to obtain Dakshina.

---


## Famous Statement


```plain text
Enjoy life while living.
Borrow if necessary to eat ghee.
```


(Usually highlighted by opponents to discredit Charvaka.)


---


## Real Contribution


### Materialism


```plain text
Man at Centre
        ↓
No Divine Agency
        ↓
Rational Explanation
```


---


## Significance

- Most scientific outlook.
- Rejected supernaturalism.
- Encouraged rational thinking.

---


# AJIVIKAS AND MATERIALISM

- Heterodox sect during Buddha's period.
- Materialistic tendencies visible.
- Charvaka became chief representative of materialism.

---


# Materialism vs Idealism


| Materialism           | Idealism                  |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Nature explains world | God/Spirit explains world |
| Rational approach     | Spiritual approach        |
| Sense perception      | Faith                     |
| Charvaka              | Vedanta                   |
| Scientific outlook    | Salvation-centered        |
| Human agency          | Divine agency             |


---


# Historical Background of Materialism


## Period


### 500 BC – AD 300


Materialistic thought flourished because of:

- Agricultural expansion
- Iron technology
- New settlements
- Metal currency
- Trade growth
- Handicraft expansion

---


## Cause → Effect Chain


```plain text
Iron Technology
        ↓
Agricultural Growth
        ↓
Economic Expansion
        ↓
Urbanization
        ↓
Scientific Outlook
        ↓
Materialist Philosophy
```


---


# Decline of Materialism


### By 5th Century AD


Materialism overshadowed by:

- Idealistic philosophies
- Ritualism
- Spiritualism

---


## Consequences

- Scientific inquiry weakened.
- Rational thinking declined.
- Priesthood strengthened.
- Varna hierarchy accepted.
- State authority legitimized.
