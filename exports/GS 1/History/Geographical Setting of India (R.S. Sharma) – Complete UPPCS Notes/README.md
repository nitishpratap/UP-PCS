# 2. Emergence of the Indian Subcontinent


## Theory of Continental Drift


Originally, the earth had one supercontinent:


### Pangaea


It was divided into:

1. **Laurasia (Northern part)**
2. **Gondwanaland (Southern part)**

---


## Gondwanaland Included:

- India
- Antarctica
- Africa
- Arabia
- South America

---


## Separation Process

- Around **225 million years ago**, Gondwanaland started breaking.
- Peninsular India separated.
- India moved northwards.
- Between **58–37 million years ago**, India joined the Eurasian landmass.

---


## Formation of Himalayas


The Himalayas are geologically young mountains.


### Uplift occurred in 4 phases.


Final uplift:

- During **Pleistocene Epoch**
- Around:
    - **2 million BC – 12,000 BC**

---


## Importance of Himalayas


### Formation of Indo-Gangetic Plains

- Himalayan rivers brought:
    - Alluvial soil
    - Sediments

This created:

- Fertile Indo-Gangetic plains

# 4. Role of Monsoon in Indian History


## Meaning


Monsoon = Seasonal reversing winds bringing rainfall.


---


# South-West Monsoon


Duration:

- June to October

Importance:

- Main source of rainfall in India
- Ancient agriculture depended heavily on it.

---


## Kharif Crops


Dependent on SW monsoon:

- Rice
- Millets
- Cotton

---


# Western Disturbances


Winter rainfall in North India.


Helped cultivation of:

- Wheat
- Barley

---


# North-East Monsoon


Important for:

- Tamil Nadu coast

Time:

- Mid October to Mid December

---


# Importance in Trade


Around **1st century AD**, direction of monsoon winds was discovered.


This revolutionized sea trade.


### Traders:

- Used SW monsoon to come to India.
- Used NE monsoon to return.

---


# Result of Monsoon Discovery


India established:

- Trade relations
- Cultural contacts

With:

- Western Asia
- Mediterranean world
- Southeast Asia

---


# UPPCS Important Point


### Monsoon Discovery → Maritime Trade Expansion


Very important for:

- Roman trade
- Indianization of Southeast Asia
