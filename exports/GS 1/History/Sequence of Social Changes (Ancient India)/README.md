
# Overview


### Evolution of Ancient Indian Society


```plain text
Palaeolithic Food Gatherers
        ↓
Neolithic Food Producers
        ↓
Chalcolithic Communities
        ↓
Harappan Urban Society
        ↓
Rig Vedic Tribal-Pastoral Society
        ↓
Later Vedic Agricultural Society
        ↓
Varna-Based Class Society
        ↓
Land Grants & Landlordism
        ↓
Feudal Society (5th–6th Century AD)
```


---


# 1. Pre-Vedic Social Formation


## Sources

- No written records.
- Knowledge derived from:
    - Archaeology
    - Material remains
    - Settlements

---


## Palaeolithic Society


### Characteristics

- Small groups in hilly regions.
- Food-gathering economy.
- Subsistence based on:
    - Hunting
    - Fishing
    - Wild fruits
    - Roots

### Social Nature

- Nomadic
- Egalitarian
- No social stratification

---


## Neolithic Society


### Developments

- Food production begins.
- Agriculture emerges.
- Permanent houses appear.

### Settlements

- Near:
    - Hills
    - Rivers
    - Uplands

---


## Chalcolithic Society


### Features

- Copper-Stone technology.
- Village-based life.
- Expansion of agriculture.

---


## Harappan Society


### Development


Peasant Villages


↓


Agricultural Surplus


↓


Urban Growth


↓


Harappan Civilization


### Features

- Planned cities.
- Large and small houses.
- Urban social differentiation.

### Important Fact

- After Harappan decline:
    - Urbanization disappeared for nearly **1500 years**.

### Prelims Fact


Urbanism reappeared only around **600 BC (Second Urbanization).**


---


# 2. Tribal and Pastoral Phase (Rig Vedic Society)


## Sources

- Rig Veda

---


# Nature of Society


### Main Characteristics


| Feature          | Description  |
| ---------------- | ------------ |
| Economy          | Pastoral     |
| Settlement       | Semi-nomadic |
| Society          | Tribal       |
| Property         | Cattle       |
| Social Structure | Egalitarian  |


---


## Importance of Cattle


### Evidence


Frequently mentioned:

- Cow
- Bull
- Horse

### Wealth

- Wealth = Cattle
- Wealthy man = **Gomat**

### Political Titles


| Title  | Meaning           |
| ------ | ----------------- |
| Gopa   | Protector of cows |
| Gopati | Lord of cows      |


---


## Daughter = Duhitr


Meaning:

- "One who milks"

Shows:

- Centrality of cattle economy.

---


## Buffalo


Called:

- **Govala** (Cow-haired)

---


# Agriculture in Rig Vedic Age


### Position

- Known but secondary.
- References limited.
- Mostly found in later hymns.

### Conclusion

- Cattle-rearing remained principal occupation.

---


# Tribal Economy


## Surplus Production

- Very limited.
- Bare subsistence economy.

### Consequences

- No regular taxation.
- No large state machinery.
- No standing army.

---


# Sources of Chief's Income


### 1. War Booty


Captured from enemy tribes.


### 2. Tribute


Called:

- **Bali**

Collected from:

- Defeated tribes
- Tribal members

---


# Distribution System


```plain text
War Booty / Bali
       ↓
Chief
       ↓
Sacrifices
       ↓
Priests receive lion's share
```


---


# Social Distribution


| Group            | Share      |
| ---------------- | ---------- |
| Priests          | Highest    |
| Princes          | Large      |
| Common Tribesmen | Amsa/Bhaga |


---


## Amsa / Bhaga

- Share distributed among common people.
- Distributed through assemblies.

---


# Social Composition


Present:

- Priests
- Warriors
- Peasants
- Artisans

Absent:

- Rigid social classes

### Important Statement

- High ranks existed.
- High social classes absent.

---


# Slavery


### Present

- Women slaves gifted.
- Domestic service.

### Absent

- Large-scale slavery.
- Shudra serving order.

---

## Geographical Shift


From:

- Afghanistan
- Punjab

- Western Uttar Pradesh

# Changes


## Permanent Settlements

### Result


```plain text
Permanent Settlements
        ↓
Territorial Chiefdoms
        ↓
Growth of Nobility
```

# Emergence of Upper Orders


Peasant


↓


Produces Food


↓


Supports Warriors


↓


Supports Priests


↓


Emergence of Elite Classes

# Role of Peasants


Supported:

- Priests
- Warriors
- Smiths
- Chariot-makers
- Carpenters

---


# Limitations


Could NOT generate:

- Trade boom
- Urbanization

Reason:

- Primitive technology
- Lack of money economy

---


# Military Organization


## No Professional Army


Army = Tribal Peasantry


### Important Terms


| Term | Meaning          |
| ---- | ---------------- |
| Vis  | Tribal peasantry |
| Sena | Army             |
| Bala | Peasantry/Force  |


---


# Ashvamedha Army


Included:


### Kshatriyas

- Military commanders

Weapons:

- Bow
- Shield
- Quiver

### Vis

- Rank and file soldiers

Weapons:

- Staves

---


# Taxation


### Not Fully Developed


No:

- Tax collectors
- Revenue bureaucracy

Payment to king resembled:

- Religious offerings.

---


# Relationship Between Classes


### Important Observation


Gap between:

- Rajanya (Warriors)
- Vaishya (Peasants)

was still narrow.


Reason:

- Tribal traditions continued.

# 4. Varna System of Production and Government


## Three Major Processes


### 1. Aryanization


### Meaning


Spread of:

- Sanskrit
- Prakrit
- Pali

Also:

- Dominance of upper varnas
- Subordination of women

### Arya


Later Vedic:

- Brahmana
- Kshatriya
- Vaishya

Excluded:

- Shudra
- Dasa

---


### 2. Ironization


Meaning:


Spread of iron technology.


Effects:


```plain text
Iron Tools
      ↓
Forest Clearance
      ↓
Agricultural Expansion
      ↓
Surplus Production
      ↓
State Expansion
```


---


### 3. Urbanization


Effects:

- Growth of towns
- Rise of traders
- Rise of artisans
- Increased state revenue

---


# Iron Revolution


### Tools

- Iron axe
- Iron ploughshare
- Sickle

### Effects

- Forest clearance.
- Gangetic plains opened.
- Agricultural expansion.

---


# Second Urbanization


### Around 500 BC


Development of:

- Villages
- Towns
- Mahajanapadas

# Varna System: Purpose


### Economic Purpose


Ensure extraction of surplus.


### Political Purpose


Maintain state authority.


---


# Structure of Varna System


| Varna     | Role              |
| --------- | ----------------- |
| Brahmana  | Priests           |
| Kshatriya | Rulers/Warriors   |
| Vaishya   | Producers         |
| Shudra    | Service providers |


---


# Twice-Born (Dvija)


Included:

- Brahmana
- Kshatriya
- Vaishya

Rights:

- Sacred thread
- Vedic education

---


# Excluded

- Shudras
- Women

---


# Social Position


## Brahmanas

- Avoid agriculture.
- Avoid manual labour.
- Highest ritual purity.

---


## Kshatriyas

- Tax collection.
- Political authority.

---


## Vaishyas


Occupations:

- Agriculture
- Herding
- Craft production
- Trade

### Important


Main taxpayers.


---


## Shudras

- Service providers.
- Labouring population.
- Often linked with slavery.

---


# Economic Privileges


Interest Rates


| Varna     | Rate |
| --------- | ---- |
| Brahmana  | 2%   |
| Kshatriya | 3%   |
| Vaishya   | 4%   |
| Shudra    | 5%   |


**Classic UPPCS Prelims Fact**

Brahmana
    +
Kshatriya
    ↓

    ↓

# 5. Social Crisis and Rise of Landed Classes


## Golden Phase


### Period


From:

- Buddha age
To:
- Gupta age

Features:

- Strong states
- Trade
- Urbanization
- Art

---


# Crisis (3rd–4th Century AD)


Known through:

- Puranas

### Called


**Kali Age Crisis**


---


# Varnasankara


Meaning:


Intermixture of varnas.


---


## What Actually Happened?


### Vaishyas

- Refused taxation.

### Shudras

- Refused labour obligations.

### Social Norms Weakened

- Marriage rules broken.
- Social boundaries weakened.

---


# Response of State


### Danda (Coercion)


Emphasized in:

- Epics
- Manusmriti

King portrayed as:

- Defender of Varna System.

---


# Rise of Land Grants


## Earlier


Community controlled land grants.


## Later


King granted land directly.


---


# Beneficiaries

- Brahmanas
- Officials
- Military chiefs
- Administrators

---


# Consequences


```plain text
Land Grants
      ↓
Revenue Rights Transfer
      ↓
Administrative Rights
      ↓
Local Control
      ↓
Landlord Class
```


---


# Tribal Integration


Land grants in tribal areas led to:

- Agricultural expansion.
- Spread of Sanskrit.
- Spread of Prakrit.
- Spread of Ayurveda.
- Spread of writing.

---


# Social Effects


### Tribal People


Integrated into Brahmanical society.


Mostly classified as:

- Shudras

---


# Decline of Vaishyas


### Post-Gupta


Independent peasantry weakened.


Gap between:

- Vaishya
- Shudra

became smaller.


---


# Emergence of Feudalism


### Most Important Result


Landlord Class


↓


Control over Peasants


↓


Feudal Relations


↓


Early Medieval Society


---


# Women in Feudal Society


## Upper Classes


Status deteriorated.


### Example

- Sati became common in Rajasthan.

---


## Lower Classes


Greater freedom:

- Economic work
- Remarriage

---
