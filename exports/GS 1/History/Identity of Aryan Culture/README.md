# 1. Introduction — What is Aryan Culture?


Aryan culture refers to the culture of the early Indo-European / Indo-Iranian speaking people whose traits are reconstructed mainly from:

- Literary Sources
- Archaeological Evidence
- Linguistic Similarities
- Genetic Evidence

The chapter tries to explain:

- Origin of Aryans
- Migration
- Cultural traits
- Horse and chariot culture
- Rituals
- Burial and cremation
- Fire worship
- Language spread

---

## Literary Sources


| Source                  | Region | Approx Date    | Importance                  |
| ----------------------- | ------ | -------------- | --------------------------- |
| Rig Veda                | India  | 1500–1000 BC   | Main source of Vedic Aryans |
| Zend-Avesta             | Iran   | Around 1400 BC | Source of Iranian Aryans    |
| Iliad & Odyssey (Homer) | Greece | 900–800 BC     | Greek Indo-European culture |

# 4. Aryan Homeland Theory


## Main Idea


Aryans originated in:

- Eastern Europe
- Central Asia
- Steppes region

NOT in tropical India.


---


## Evidence for Central Asian Origin


### (A) Climatic Evidence


Words in Indo-European languages indicate:

- Cold climate
- Temperate vegetation
- Absence of tropical flora-fauna

### (B) Animal Evidence


Frequently mentioned:

- Horse
- Cow
- Bull

Rarely mentioned:

- Tiger
- Elephant
- Rhinoceros
- Buffalo

This suggests non-tropical origin.


---


## Genetic Evidence


### M17 Genetic Marker


| Region                    | Presence |
| ------------------------- | -------- |
| Central Asia              | 40%      |
| Hindi-speaking Delhi area | 35%      |
| Dravidian speakers        | 10%      |


### Conclusion


Suggests migration from Central Asia to India.


---


# 5. Horse — Most Important Aryan Trait


## Importance of Horse


Horse = central feature of Aryan culture.


### Sanskrit Word

- Asva = Horse

### Cognates in Many Languages

- Sanskrit
- Avestan
- Greek
- Latin

This proves common Indo-European heritage.


---


## Frequency in Rig Veda


| Word           | Number of Occurrences |
| -------------- | --------------------- |
| Horse (Asva)   | 215                   |
| Cow (Go)       | 176                   |
| Bull (Vrsabha) | 170                   |


### Interpretation

- Cattle important economically
- Horse important politically and militarily

Thus:

> Horse-based aristocracy dominated cattle rearers.

---


## Horse in Religion


### Rig Veda

- Two complete hymns dedicated to horse
- Associated with Indra and Maruts

### Avesta

- Mithra associated with horse and chariot

### Greece

- Important in Homeric literature

Horse became important only in:

- Late Harappan
- Post-Harappan cultures

## Two Branches


| Branch                       | Region       |
| ---------------------------- | ------------ |
| Western                      | Europe       |
| Eastern (Proto-Indo-Iranian) | Iran + India |


---


## Important Inscriptions


| Region               | Date                 | Importance         |
| -------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------ |
| Iraq (Agade dynasty) | 2200 BC              | Indo-Iranian names |
| Anatolia             | 19th–17th century BC | Hittite language   |
| Greece               | 14th century BC      | Mycenaean Greek    |
| Mesopotamia          | 16th–14th century BC | Kassite & Mitanni  |


---


## Important Argument Against “Out of India”


If Aryans spread from India:

- Indo-European inscriptions should appear first in India.
- But earliest evidence found outside India.

Also:

- Munda words appear in Rig Veda.
- If Aryans migrated outward from India, these words should appear outside too.

They do not.


Hence:

> R.S. Sharma rejects indigenous Aryan theory.

---


# 19. Anatolian Theory (Renfrew Theory)


## Main Idea


Aryan language originated in Anatolia.


### Supported By

- Gordon Childe
- Colin Renfrew

---


## Renfrew’s Argument


Spread of agriculture spread Indo-European language.


---


## R.S. Sharma’s Criticism


### Problems:

1. Agriculture existed elsewhere too.
2. Early Indo-Europeans were not primarily agricultural.
3. Common agricultural vocabulary absent.
4. If Anatolia was homeland, why did Indo-European disappear there?

# 23. Important UPPCS Mains Analytical Points


## Was horse central to Aryan culture?


YES:

- Highest references in Rig Veda
- Military role
- Religious role
- Political prestige

---


## Why is horse evidence important?


Because:

- Mature Harappan civilization lacks clear horse evidence.
- Vedic culture heavily depends on horse.

This becomes major evidence in Aryan migration debate.
