
### maurya empire: why is it important?

- First empire to establish a **highly centralized state system** over most of the Indian subcontinent.
- Created the first large-scale **bureaucratic machinery** in Indian history.
- Developed a sophisticated **taxation system**, intelligence network, provincial administration, and imperial currency.
- Spread the **material culture of the Middle Ganga Valley** to Bengal, Odisha, Andhra, Karnataka and beyond.
- Introduced **stone architecture**, polished pillars, cave architecture, and imperial inscriptions.
- Under Ashoka, state power was combined with **Dhamma policy** and administrative integration.
- Became the model for later states such as the **Satavahanas, Chetis, Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas**.

---


## state control and royal absolutism


### concept of kingship

- Dharmashastras prescribed that the king should rule according to:
    - Dharma
    - Customs
    - Varna order
- Kautilya:
    - Called king **Dharmapravartaka** (Promulgator of Dharma/Social Order).
    - King intervenes when Varna-Ashrama order collapses.
- Ashoka:
    - Asserted superiority of royal orders.
    - Promulgated Dhamma through inscriptions.
    - Appointed officers to spread and enforce Dhamma.

### emergence of royal absolutism


Result of continuous Magadhan expansion:


Magadha annexed:

- Anga
- Vaishali
- Kashi
- Kosala
- Avanti
- Kalinga
- Others

Consequences:

- Military conquest converted into administrative control.
- State exercised coercive authority over people's lives.
- Led to emergence of centralized monarchy.

Highest officers received:

- 48,000 panas annually

Lowest officers:

- 60 panas annually

Some employees:

- Only 10–20 panas

**Conclusion:** Massive income inequality existed in bureaucracy.

- Slaves

India was **not a slave society** like:

- Greece
- Rome

Functions performed by slaves elsewhere were performed by:

- Shudras

- Artisans

- Domestic workers

# causes of fall of maurya empire


## 1. brahmanical reaction


Reasons:


### language policy


Ashoka used:

- Prakrit

Not:

- Sanskrit

### anti-sacrifice policy

- Restricted animal killing.
- Criticized rituals.

Result:

- Reduced income of Brahmanas.

### equality before law


Rajukas implemented:

- Vyavaharasamata (civil equality)
- Dandasamata (equal punishment)

Contradicted:

- Varna-based discrimination.

Result:

- Brahmanical hostility.

### evidence


Rise of Brahmana dynasties:

- Shungas
- Kanvas
- Satavahanas

Revived:

- Vedic sacrifices.

---


## 2. financial crisis


Reasons:

- Huge standing army.
- Large bureaucracy.
- Administrative expenses.
- Donations to Buddhist Sangha.

Tradition states:

- Treasury became depleted.
- Gold images melted to meet expenses.

---


## 3. oppressive administration


Evidence:


### taxila revolt


Under Bindusara:

- Complaints against Dushtamatyas (wicked officials)

Again during Ashoka's reign:

- Similar complaints emerged.

### ashoka's response

- Officer rotation.
- Administrative inspections.
- Pilgrimages for supervision.

Yet provincial resentment continued.


---


## 4. diffusion of knowledge


As:

- Iron technology spread
- Administrative knowledge spread
- Urban culture spread

Peripheral areas gained independence.


Result:


Rise of:

- Shungas
- Kanvas
- Chetis
- Satavahanas

---


## 5. neglect of north-west frontier


### central asian threat


Nomadic groups:

- Scythians

Created pressure on:

- China
- India

### china's response


Shih Huang Ti built:

- Great Wall of China (c.220 BC)

### mauryan response

- No comparable frontier defence.

Result:


Successive invasions:

- Greeks (206 BC)
- Shakas
- Parthians
- Others

---


## end of maurya empire


### last ruler


Brihadratha


### founder of successor dynasty


Pushyamitra Shunga


Event:

- Brihadratha assassinated in 185 BC.
- Pushyamitra seized throne.

Result:

- Beginning of Shunga rule.
- Revival of Brahmanical traditions.
