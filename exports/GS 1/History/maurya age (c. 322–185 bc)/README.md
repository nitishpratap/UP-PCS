
### significance

- First empire covering almost entire Indian subcontinent.
- Political unification after Magadhan expansion.
- Strongest centralized administration in ancient India.
- First ruler to communicate directly with people through inscriptions.
- Beginning of state-directed economy on large scale.
- Peak of Mauryan power under Ashoka.

---


## chandragupta maurya (322–298 bc)


### origin


### brahmanical tradition

- Son of Mura, a Shudra woman in Nanda court.
- Hence called Maurya.

### buddhist tradition

- Mauryas belonged to ruling clan of Pipphalivana.
- Pipphalivana located near Gorakhpur region close to Nepal Terai.

### probable conclusion

- Chandragupta likely belonged to Maurya clan of Pipphalivana.

---


### rise to power

- Took advantage of weakening Nanda rule.
- Assisted by Chanakya/Kautilya.
- Overthrew Nandas.
- Founded Maurya Dynasty.

---


### chanakya / kautilya


### names

- Chanakya
- Kautilya
- Vishnugupta

### contribution

- Political strategist behind Mauryan rise.
- Authored Arthashastra (core material may belong to Mauryan age).

### literary source

- Mudrarakshasa by Vishakhadatta.
- Describes Chanakya's intrigues against enemies.

---


### conquests


### north-west india

- Defeated Greek satraps after Alexander's withdrawal.

### war with seleucus nicator (305 bc approx.)


Opponent:

- Seleucus I Nicator

Result:

- Chandragupta victorious.

Terms:

- Seleucus ceded:
    - Eastern Afghanistan
    - Baluchistan
    - Territory west of Indus
- Chandragupta gave:
    - 500 war elephants
- Matrimonial alliance:
    - Daughter of Seleucus married Chandragupta (according to classical sources)

---


### extent of empire


Included:

- Bihar
- Bengal
- Odisha (large part)
- Western India
- North-West India
- Deccan

Excluded:

- Kerala
- Tamil Nadu
- Some North-East regions

Special significance:

- Mauryan territory exceeded even British India in north-west.

---


### conquest of republics


Conquered:

- Republics (Ganas/Samghas)

Reason:

- Kautilya considered republics obstacles to imperial expansion.

---

## ashoka (273–232 bc)


### position in indian history

- Greatest ruler of Maurya dynasty.
- First Indian ruler whose history is reconstructed mainly through inscriptions.
- First ruler to communicate directly with subjects through royal edicts.
- Associated with spread of Buddhism beyond India.
- Pioneer of policy of peace, tolerance and cultural conquest.

---


## accession


Father:

- Bindusara

Grandfather:

- Chandragupta Maurya

According to Buddhist tradition:

- Killed 99 brothers to secure throne.

Historical view:

- Considered legendary and exaggerated.
- Not supported by contemporary evidence.

---

# kalinga war


### date


Approximately:

- 261 BC

Occurred:

- 8th regnal year of Ashoka.

Mentioned in:

- 13th Major Rock Edict

---


### reason


Kalinga remained independent.


Strategic importance:

- Controlled eastern coast.
- Controlled trade routes.

Present location:

- Coastal Odisha

---


### consequences according to ashoka


Killed:

- 100,000

Deported:

- 150,000

Died later:

- Several lakhs

Historical view:

- Figures exaggerated.

---


## impact of kalinga war


### psychological impact


Ashoka deeply disturbed by:

- Killing
- Deportation
- Human suffering

Particularly affected by suffering of:

- Brahmanas
- Shramanas (Buddhist monks)
- Householders
- Families

---


### transformation of policy


Earlier:


### bherighosha


Meaning:

- Sound of war drums.
- Military conquest.

Replaced by:


### dhammaghosha


Meaning:

- Sound of dhamma.
- Moral conquest.

Very Important UPPCS Fact.


---


## cultural conquest


Ashoka abandoned:

- Expansion through warfare.

Adopted:

- Expansion through ideas.
- Moral influence.
- Diplomacy.

---


## did ashoka become pacifist?


### answer: no


Common misconception.


Evidence:

- Retained Kalinga.
- Did not disband army.
- Continued administration.
- Warned tribes against disorder.
- Maintained punishment system.

Hence:

> Ashoka was not an absolute pacifist; he adopted practical imperial policy.

# ashoka's dhamma


## meaning


Dhamma ≠ Buddhism


Very Important.


Ashoka never imposed Buddhism as state religion.


Dhamma was:

- Ethical code
- Social morality
- Administrative morality

---


## objectives

- Social harmony
- Moral upliftment
- Loyalty to king
- Religious tolerance
- Preservation of social order

---


## main principles


### respect for parents

- Obey parents.
- Care for elders.

---


### respect for teachers

- Honour teachers.

---


### respect for brahmanas and shramanas

- Respect all religious persons.

---


### kindness to servants

- Humane treatment.

---


### compassion for animals

- Avoid unnecessary killing.

---


### tolerance

- Respect all sects.

---


### loyalty to king

- Firm devotion (Dridha Bhakti).

---


## what dhamma did not teach


Ashoka never emphasized:

- Nirvana
- Four Noble Truths
- Eightfold Path

Hence:


Dhamma ≠ Pure Buddhism.


---


## attitude towards rituals


Ashoka criticized:

- Meaningless rituals.
- Costly ceremonies.

Especially:

- Ceremonies performed by women.

---


## attitude towards animal sacrifice


Prohibited:

- Many forms of animal killing.
- Animal sacrifice.
- Slaughter in royal kitchen.

---


## social reforms


Discouraged:

- Excessive festivities.
- Drunken celebrations.
- Wasteful gatherings.

---


## religious tolerance


Kandahar Greek inscription advocates:

- Harmony among sects.

Ashoka donated to:

- Buddhist sects
- Non-Buddhist sects
- Anti-Buddhist sects

Very Important.


---


## dhammamahamatras


Special officers appointed by Ashoka.


Purpose:

- Spread Dhamma.
- Welfare activities.
- Supervise moral conduct.

Worked among:

- Women
- Elderly
- Various social groups

UPPCS Fact:


Dhammamahamatras introduced by Ashoka.

## criticisms


### policy not long lasting


After Ashoka:

- Governors became independent.
- Vassals revolted.

---


### external invasions


Within few decades:

- North-west attacked by foreign powers.

---


### dhamma not universally accepted


Could not permanently transform politics.


---


## historian's evaluation


Wrong view:

> Ashoka's peace policy destroyed Mauryan Empire.

Better conclusion:

- Administrative weaknesses and later rulers were more important factors.
- Ashoka strengthened imperial integration during his reign.
