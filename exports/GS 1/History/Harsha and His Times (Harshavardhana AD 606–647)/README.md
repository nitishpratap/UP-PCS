# Historical Background


## After the Guptas


### Political Situation

- Gupta Empire declined by mid-6th century.
- North India fragmented into several kingdoms.
- White Hunas (Hunas) established supremacy over:
    - Kashmir
    - Punjab
    - Western India
- Gupta territories divided among feudatories.

### Rise of Vardhanas

- Pushyabhuti dynasty ruled from:
    - Thanesar (Present Haryana)
- Harshavardhana unified many feudatory states.
- Reigned:
    - **AD 606–647**

### Archaeological Evidence


**Harsha ka Tila (Thanesar, Haryana)**


Findings:

- Brick structures discovered.
- Cannot be conclusively identified as royal palace remains.

---


# Rise of Kanauj


## Shift from Pataliputra to Kanauj


### Why Pataliputra Declined?


### Earlier Strengths

- Prosperity based on:
    - Trade
    - Commerce
    - Monetized economy
- Located near four rivers.
- Collected tolls from traders.

### Causes of Decline


Trade decline


↓


Money scarcity


↓


Officials paid through land grants


↓


Urban decline


↓


Loss of political importance


---


## Why Kanauj Rose?


### Location Advantages


**Present Location:** Kannauj district, Uttar Pradesh


Features:

- Central location in Ganga-Yamuna Doab.
- Elevated ground.
- Easily fortifiable.
- Controlled eastern and western Doab.
- Land and water transport available.

### Historical Significance


| Pataliputra       | Kanauj                       |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Pre-feudal order  | Feudal order                 |
| Trade-based power | Military-strategic power     |
| Urban economy     | Land-based political economy |


### Exam Point


**Kanauj symbolizes rise of feudalism in North India.**

# Kanauj Assembly


## Purpose


Promotion of Mahayana Buddhism.


---


## Participants

- Hsuan Tsang
- Bhaskaravarman (Kamarupa)
- Kings of 20 states
- Thousands of monks

---


## Major Features


### Structures

- Two halls (1000 persons each)
- Huge tower

### Statue


Golden Buddha statue.


---


## Incident


Hsuan Tsang defended Mahayana.


Opposition arose.


Conspiracy against Hsuan Tsang.


Harsha threatened punishment.


Later:

- Tower caught fire.
- Attempt made on Harsha's life.

### Consequence

- 500 Brahmanas arrested.
- Many banished.
- Some executed.

### Historical Interpretation


Questions Harsha's image as completely tolerant ruler.

# UPPCS Mains Enrichment

1. Harsha's reign marks transition from ancient to early medieval India.
2. Shift from Pataliputra to Kanauj reflects decline of trade-based urbanism.
3. Land grants indicate rise of feudal economy.
4. Decentralization increased power of feudatories.
5. Growth of untouchability visible in Hsuan Tsang's account.
6. Shudras increasingly became agriculturists.
7. Nalanda reflects international intellectual exchanges.
8. Religious patronage used as political legitimacy.
9. Court biographies became tools of royal propaganda.
10. Harsha achieved partial political unification after Gupta decline.
