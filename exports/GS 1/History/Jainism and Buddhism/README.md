# 1. Background: Rise of New Religious Movements (6th–5th Century BC)


Around the 6th century BC, north India witnessed the emergence of many religious sects. Ancient sources mention nearly **62 sects**. Among them, the two most important reform movements were:

1. **Jainism**
2. **Buddhism**

These movements emerged mainly in the **mid-Gangetic plains** (Eastern UP and Bihar).


Magadha became the main centre of these reform movements.


---


# 2. Causes for the Rise of Jainism and Buddhism


This is one of the most important UPPCS topics.


---


# A. Reaction Against Varna System


Post-Vedic society became rigidly divided into four varnas:


| Varna      | Functions                          | Position |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------- | -------- |
| Brahmanas  | Priests, teachers                  | Highest  |
| Kshatriyas | Rulers, warriors                   | Second   |
| Vaishyas   | Agriculture, cattle rearing, trade | Third    |
| Shudras    | Service to upper varnas            | Lowest   |


---


## Problems in Society


### Brahmanical Privileges


Brahmanas enjoyed:

- Gifts
- Tax exemption
- Freedom from punishment
- Social superiority

### Position of Shudras


Shudras:

- Could not study Vedas
- Worked as:
    - slaves
    - labourers
    - craftsmen
- Some treated as untouchables

### Unequal Punishment


Punishment depended on varna:

- Higher varna → lighter punishment
- Lower varna → harsher punishment

This created social tension.


---


# B. Kshatriya Reaction Against Brahmanas


The kshatriyas opposed:

- Ritual domination of brahmanas
- Birth-based superiority

Important point:

- Both **Mahavira** and **Buddha** belonged to **Kshatriya clans**.
- Both challenged brahmanical authority.

---


# C. Agricultural Expansion in North-East India


This is the REAL economic cause.


Regions:

- Eastern UP
- North Bihar
- South Bihar

These regions:

- Received heavy rainfall (~100 cm)
- Were densely forested

---


## Role of Iron Technology


Large-scale settlement became possible because of:

- Iron axes
- Iron ploughshares

Around **600–500 BC**, iron tools started being used extensively.


Effects:

- Forest clearance
- Agricultural expansion
- Rise of villages and cities

---


# D. Importance of Cattle


Agriculture depended on:

- Bullocks
- Animal husbandry

But Vedic sacrifices involved large-scale cattle killing.


Hence:

- New agricultural society opposed animal sacrifice.
- Jainism and Buddhism supported non-violence.

---


# E. Rise of Cities


Around 500 BC many towns emerged.


Important cities:


| Ancient City | Modern Location |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| Kaushambi    | Near Prayagraj  |
| Kusinagara   | Deoria district |
| Varanasi     | UP              |
| Vaishali     | Bihar           |
| Rajgir       | Bihar           |
| Pataliputra  | Patna           |
| Champa       | Bhagalpur       |


Important for UPPCS mapping.


Use entity memory:

- Varanasi
- Vaishali
- Rajgir
- Pataliputra

---


# F. Rise of Trade and Vaishya Class


Features:

- Use of punch-marked coins
- Growth of trade
- Rise of merchants (setthis)

Vaishyas supported Buddhism and Jainism because:

1. These religions opposed rigid varna system.
2. They supported peace and non-violence.
3. Brahmanical texts looked down upon moneylending.

---


# G. Reaction Against Materialism


People opposed:

- Luxury
- Wealth accumulation
- New inequalities

Both religions promoted:

- Simple life
- Asceticism
- Renunciation

Thus they represented:

- Protest against social inequality
- Protest against excessive materialism

Mahavira

| Number | Name         | Importance             |
| ------ | ------------ | ---------------------- |

# Parshvanatha

- Belonged to Banaras
- Renounced royal life
- Preached four vows

---

- Related to Lichchhavis
- Connected with Magadhan royal family

# Renunciation


At age 30:

- Left home
- Became ascetic

For 12 years:

- Wandered continuously
- Practised severe austerity

At age 42:

- Attained **Kaivalya** (omniscience)

Because he conquered desires:

- Called **Jina** = conqueror
- Followers called **Jainas**

# 4. Doctrines of Jainism


Very important for prelims.


---


# Five Principles (Panch Mahavratas)


| Principle    | Meaning        |
| ------------ | -------------- |
| Ahimsa       | Non-violence   |
| Satya        | Truth          |
| Asteya       | Non-stealing   |
| Aparigraha   | Non-possession |
| Brahmacharya | Celibacy       |


Mahavira especially emphasized:

- Extreme austerity
- Complete non-violence

---


# Ahimsa


Most important doctrine.


Jainism prohibited:

- Killing animals
- Agriculture (because insects die)
- Warfare

Result:

- Jainas moved towards trade and commerce.

---


# Triratna (Three Jewels)


Path to liberation:


| Jewel           | Meaning         |
| --------------- | --------------- |
| Right Faith     | Samyak Darshan  |
| Right Knowledge | Samyak Jnana    |
| Right Conduct   | Samyak Charitra |


---


# View on Varna


Jainism:

- Did NOT completely reject varna system
- Said status depends on karma

Thus:

- Even lower castes could attain liberation through good conduct.

---


# Jain Sects


Later Jainism divided into two sects:


| Sect        | Meaning       |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| Shvetambara | White-clad    |
| Digambara   | Sky-clad/nude |


Cause:

- Differences in monastic discipline

---

Kalinga

# Causes of Limited Spread


Jainism:

- Did not clearly separate itself from Brahmanism
- Followed very strict rules
- Extreme ahimsa difficult for masses

Hence Buddhism spread more rapidly.

# 8. Doctrines of Buddhism


---


# Four Noble Truths


Core teaching:

1. World is full of suffering
2. Cause of suffering = desire
3. Desires can end
4. End of desires leads to Nirvana

---


# Nirvana


State:

- Freedom from cycle of birth and death

---


# Eightfold Path


Most important for UPPCS.

| Step                | Meaning               |
| ------------------- | --------------------- |

| Right Action        | Ethical conduct       |

| Right Effort        | Self-discipline       |

---


# Middle Path


Buddha opposed:

- Extreme luxury
- Extreme austerity

He advocated:

- Madhyama Marga (Middle Path)

---


# Buddhist Ethical Code


| Rule                    | Meaning                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ |

# 9. Features of Buddhism


---


# Revolutionary Features


Buddhism:

- Rejected authority of Vedas
- Rejected sacrifices
- Did not recognize permanent soul
- Did not emphasize God

---


# Equality


Buddhism:

- Opposed caste distinctions
- Allowed entry of women
- Open to all classes

Thus:

- More democratic and liberal.

---


# Sangha


Buddhist religious order.


Three pillars of Buddhism:


| Element | Meaning        |
| ------- | -------------- |
| Buddha  | Teacher        |
| Dhamma  | Doctrine       |
| Sangha  | Monastic order |


---


# Language


Buddha preached in:

- Pali (Prakrit language)

Hence teachings reached common people.


---

# 10. Causes of Decline of Buddhism


Very important mains topic.


---


# A. Corruption in Monasteries


Monasteries became:

- Wealthy
- Corrupt
- Luxury-oriented

Example:

- Nalanda received revenue from 200 villages.

---


# B. Idol Worship and Ritualism


Buddhism adopted:

- Rituals
- Idol worship
- Sanskrit language

Thus lost simplicity.


---


# C. Vajrayana Buddhism


New form:

- Tantric practices
- Moral degeneration

---


# D. Brahmanical Revival


Brahmanas:

- Reformed Hinduism
- Absorbed Buddhist ideas

---


# E. Persecution


Important rulers:


| Ruler              | Action                |
| ------------------ | --------------------- |
| Pushyamitra Shunga | Anti-Buddhist actions |
| Mihirakula         | Destroyed monasteries |
| Shashanka          | Cut Bodhi tree        |


---


# F. Turkish Invasions


Turks:

- Looted monasteries
- Killed monks

Monks fled to:

- Nepal
- Tibet

By 12th century:

- Buddhism nearly disappeared from India.
