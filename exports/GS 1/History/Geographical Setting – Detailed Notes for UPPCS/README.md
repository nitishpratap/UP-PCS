# 2. Emergence of the Indian Subcontinent


## Gondwanaland and Pangaea


### Pangaea

- Originally, the entire earth’s landmass formed a single supercontinent called:

    **Pangaea**


It had two parts:

1. **Laurisia (Northern supercontinent)**
2. **Gondwanaland (Southern supercontinent)**

---


## Laurisia Included

- North America
- Greenland
- Europe
- Most of Asia north of Himalayas

---


## Gondwanaland Included

- Peninsular India
- Antarctica
- Africa
- Arabia
- South America

---


# Formation of India

- Due to tectonic movements:
    - Gondwanaland broke apart.
- Peninsular India separated from Gondwanaland.

### Timeline


| घटना                                    | समय                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Break-up of Gondwanaland began          | ~225 million years ago  |
| India became separate geographical unit | ~40 million years ago   |
| India joined Eurasian continent         | 58–37 million years ago |


---


# Formation of Himalayas


The Himalayas are geologically young mountains.


### Uplift occurred in four phases.


### Final uplift:


| Phase       | Time                    |
| ----------- | ----------------------- |
| Last uplift | Pleistocene epoch       |
| Period      | c. 2 million – 12000 BC |


---


# Importance of Himalayas in Formation of Plains

- Himalayan rivers brought:
    - alluvial soil
    - sediments

This created:

- Indo-Gangetic plains

during:

- Pleistocene epoch

---


# Size of Indian Subcontinent

- Area:
    - 4,202,500 sq km
- Comparable to:
    - Europe without Russia

---


# Countries in Indian Subcontinent

1. India
2. Pakistan
3. Bangladesh
4. Nepal
5. Bhutan

# 3. Role of Monsoon


## Importance of Monsoon


The monsoon was the backbone of:

- agriculture
- trade
- economy
- cultural contacts

---


# South-West Monsoon


### Duration

- June to October

### Importance

- Main source of rainfall in India

### Ancient Agriculture

- Irrigation was less developed.
- Agriculture depended mainly on rainfall.

---


# Kharif Crop

- Depended on:
    - South-west monsoon

### Examples

- Rice
- Millets
- Pulses

---


# Western Disturbances


### In winter:


Western disturbances bring rainfall to:

- Northern India

### Helped cultivation of:

- Wheat
- Barley

---


# North-East Monsoon


### Time

- Mid-October to Mid-December

### Main Beneficiary

- Tamil Nadu coastal region

---


# Monsoon and Trade


## Discovery of Monsoon Winds

- Around:
    - 1st century AD

This revolutionized sea trade.


---


# Trade Route


### Traders came from:

- Western Asia
- Mediterranean region

using:

- South-west monsoon

### Returned using:

- North-east monsoon

---


# Impact of Monsoon Discovery


Enabled:

1. Foreign trade
2. Cultural contacts
3. Spread of religions
4. Maritime connections

with:

- West Asia
- Mediterranean
- Southeast Asia

---


# UPPCS MAINS POINT


### “Monsoon shaped Indian civilization.”


Explain through:

- agriculture
- trade
- settlement
- cultural exchange

# 4. Northern Boundaries and Passes


# Importance of Himalayas


## Climatic Role

- Block cold Arctic winds from Siberia and Central Asia.
- Keeps North India warmer.

---


## Social Impact


Because of moderate climate:

- less need for heavy clothing
- people could live outdoors longer

---


## Security Role

- Protected India from northern invasions.

Especially important:

- before modern transport

---


# North-Western Passes


The Himalayas were not completely impassable.


### Important Passes


| Pass        | Mountain Region |
| ----------- | --------------- |
| Khyber Pass | Sulaiman Range  |
| Bolan Pass  | Kiarthar Range  |
| Gomal Pass  | Sulaiman Range  |


---


# Importance of Passes


Facilitated:

- invasions
- migrations
- trade
- cultural contacts

between:

- India
- Iran
- Afghanistan
- Central Asia

---


# Hindu Kush

- Extension of Himalayan system.
- Did not fully block interaction between:
    - Indus basin
    - Oxus basin

---


# Kashmir Valley


## Features

- Surrounded by mountains
- Developed unique culture

### Yet connected through passes.


---


# Importance of Kashmir

1. Cultural exchange
2. Seasonal migration
3. Centre of Buddhism transmission to Central Asia
4. Major repository of Sanskrit manuscripts

---


# Nepal Valley


## Importance

- Accessible through passes from Gangetic plains
- Important centre of Sanskrit learning

---


# Himalayan Foothills


## Advantages

- Easier forest clearance
- Narrow rivers easier to cross

Thus:

- earliest settlements developed here

---


# Terai Route


Ancient trade routes followed:

- Himalayan foothills
- Terai belt

# 5. Rivers and Civilization


# Importance of Rivers


Rivers were:

- lifeline of civilization
- communication routes
- political boundaries
- centres of cities

---


# Major River Systems

1. Indus system
2. Gangetic system
3. Brahmaputra system

---


# Rainfall Pattern


| Region | Rainfall |
| ------ | -------- |
| West   | 25 cm    |
| East   | 250+ cm  |


Rainfall increased west → east.


---


# Forest Clearance and Iron


## Western Areas

- Could be cleared using:
    - stone tools
    - copper tools

---


## Eastern Gangetic Plains


Dense forests + hard soil required:

- iron tools

Thus:

- settlements expanded west → east gradually.

---


# Agricultural Pattern


| Region                         | Main Crop      |
| ------------------------------ | -------------- |
| Indus & Western Ganga plains   | Wheat & barley |
| Middle & Lower Gangetic plains | Rice           |


Rice also staple in:

- Gujarat
- South India

---


# Civilizational Sequence


| Civilization          | Region                        |

| Vedic Civilization    | Punjab & Western Ganga plains |
| Post-Vedic Culture    | Mid-Gangetic basin            |

| Early medieval Assam  | Brahmaputra valley            |
