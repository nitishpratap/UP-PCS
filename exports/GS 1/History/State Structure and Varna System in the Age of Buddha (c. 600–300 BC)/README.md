
# 1. AGE OF BUDDHA (6th–5th Century BC): HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE


The Age of Buddha marks a decisive turning point in Indian history because during this period:

- Second Urbanization began.
- Large territorial states (Mahajanapadas) emerged.
- Iron technology expanded rapidly.
- Coinage appeared.
- Trade and guilds developed.
- Varna system became rigid.
- State machinery became stronger.
- Permanent taxation system emerged.
- Standing armies appeared.
- Buddhism and Jainism challenged Vedic orthodoxy.

This period laid the foundations of:

- Ancient Indian polity
- Ancient Indian economy
- Social stratification
- Legal system
- Urban civilization of Gangetic plains

---


# 2. SECOND URBANIZATION IN INDIA


## Meaning


Second Urbanization refers to the emergence of towns in the Gangetic Valley around 600–300 BC after nearly 1500 years of decline following the collapse of Harappan Civilization.


### First Urbanization

- Harappan Civilization (2600–1900 BC)

### Gap

- No major urban centres for nearly 1500 years

### Second Urbanization

- Mid-Gangetic basin
- 6th–5th century BC onwards

---


# Archaeological Basis


## Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)


### Period

- Begins around 500 BC
- Peak during Mauryan Age

### Features

- Very glossy
- Highly polished
- Fine quality pottery
- Luxury tableware of wealthy classes

### Importance


NBPW is considered the archaeological hallmark of the Second Urbanization.


Associated discoveries:

- Iron implements
- Agricultural tools
- Craft tools
- Metal money
- Burnt bricks
- Ring wells

---


# Settlement Expansion


## Sequence


### 1. Black-and-Red Ware Phase


Earliest settlements.


### 2. Painted Grey Ware (PGW) Phase

### 3. Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) Phase

## Example: Kanpur District

# 26. MONARCHY VS REPUBLIC 


| Feature            | Monarchy                    | Republic (Gana-Sangha)             |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Ruler              | One King                    | Many Rulers                        |
| Power              | Centralized                 | Shared among oligarchs             |
| Revenue            | King alone received revenue | Every oligarch claimed revenue     |
| Army               | Standing army under king    | Each raja maintained own army      |
| Administration     | Centralized                 | Decentralized                      |
| Brahmana Influence | Strong                      | Weak initially                     |
| Decision Making    | King-centric                | Assembly-centric                   |
| Succession         | Hereditary                  | Election/Selection among oligarchs |
| Example            | Magadha, Koshala            | Lichchhavis, Shakyas               |
