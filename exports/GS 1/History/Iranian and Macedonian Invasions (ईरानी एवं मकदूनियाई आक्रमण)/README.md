
# 1. Background: Political Situation of North-West India (6th Century BC)


### North-East India

- Small principalities and republics gradually merged into the expanding **Magadha Empire**.
- Magadha emerged as the strongest political power in northern India.

### North-West India


Unlike Magadha, north-west India remained politically fragmented.


Major states:

- Kambojas
- Gandharas
- Madras
- Several tribal republics

### Features

- Constant warfare among small kingdoms.
- No powerful state to unify the region.
- Fertile agricultural land.
- Rich in natural resources.
- Accessible through passes of the Hindu Kush.

### Importance for Exam


**Political disunity + geographical accessibility = foreign invasions.**

### Year


**516 BC**

- Sindh

These territories became:


### 20th Satrapy (Province) of Iran


### Satrapy

## Significance of Indian Satrapy


### Economic Importance


Indian Satrapy was:

- Most fertile province.
- Most populous province.

### Revenue

**360 talents of gold**


This formed:


**1/3rd of total revenue**


received by Iran from all Asian provinces.


### UPPCS Fact

> Indian Satrapy was the richest province of the Achaemenian Empire.

# HISTORICAL ANALYSIS


## Why did the Persian Invasion succeed?


### Political Causes

- North-West India lacked political unity.
- Several small kingdoms fought among themselves.
- No strong empire like Magadha existed in the region.

### Geographical Causes

- Hindu Kush passes provided easy entry.
- Khyber Pass served as the main invasion route.

### Economic Causes

- Punjab and Sindh were fertile regions.
- Rich agricultural surplus attracted foreign powers.

### Administrative Causes

- Persian Empire had a highly organized military and administrative system.

---


# Why did Alexander succeed initially?


## Internal Weaknesses of India


### Political Fragmentation

- Numerous monarchies and tribal republics.
- Absence of national unity.

### Rivalries among Indian rulers


Example:

- Ambhi supported Alexander.
- Porus fought Alexander.

This division helped the invader.


### Unguarded Khyber Pass


No coordinated frontier defence existed.


---


# Why did Alexander fail to advance beyond Beas?


### Military Factors

- Continuous campaigning for 10 years.

### Psychological Factors

- Homesickness.
- Fear of unknown territories.

### Intelligence Reports


Reports of:


**Nanda Army**

- Huge infantry
- Huge cavalry
- Numerous elephants

discouraged Greek soldiers.


### Climate

- Humid and hot conditions.
- Monsoon difficulties.

### Indian Resistance


Strong resistance from local tribes and kingdoms.

# HISTORIAN'S ASSESSMENT

Persian rule:

- Politically limited.
- Culturally significant.

Most important contribution:

- Kharoshthi script.
- Artistic influence on Mauryan architecture.

---


## On Alexander's Invasion


R.S. Sharma does not consider Alexander's invasion a permanent political success because:

- Greek rule was short-lived.
- Mauryas soon absorbed the region.

However, its importance lies in:

- Trade routes.
- Cultural contacts.
- Historical records.
- Rise of Mauryan Empire.

# MAINS EXAM QUESTION FRAMEWORK


## Q. Assess the impact of Iranian invasion on India.


### Introduction


Iranian ruler Darius annexed north-west India in 516 BC and incorporated it into the Achaemenian Empire.


### Political Impact

- Introduction of Satrapy system.
- Administrative influence.

### Economic Impact

- Growth of Indo-Iranian trade.
- Circulation of foreign coins.

### Cultural Impact

- Introduction of Kharoshthi script.
- Influence on Mauryan art.

### Conclusion


Iranian influence was more cultural than political and laid foundations for later developments in Mauryan administration and art.
