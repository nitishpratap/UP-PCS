
# OVERVIEW


## Period: c. AD 300–750


This period marks the **Second Historical Phase of Peninsular India**.


### Major Features

- Rise of regional states after Satavahanas.
- Rapid expansion of agriculture.
- Large-scale **tax-free land grants**.
- Growth of **Brahmanism** and decline of Buddhism.
- Beginning of **Temple Architecture**.
- Spread of **Sanskritization and Aryanization**.
- Rise of Pallavas, Chalukyas, Pandyas.
- Kalabhra Peasant Revolt.
- Bhakti movement beginnings (Alvars & Nayanars).

---


# I. THE NEW PHASE (AD 300–750)


## First Historical Phase (200 BC–AD 300)


### Characteristics

- Satavahana dominance in Deccan.
- Tamil kingdoms dominant in south Tamil Nadu.
- Flourishing:
    - Crafts
    - Trade
    - Coinage
    - Urban centres

### Important Regions without Independent States

- Northern Tamil Nadu
- Southern Karnataka
- Southern Maharashtra
- Region between Godavari and Mahanadi

---


## Second Historical Phase (AD 300–750)


### New Developments

- Around **two dozen states** emerged.
- Rise of:
    - Pallavas (Kanchi)
    - Chalukyas (Badami)
    - Pandyas (Madurai)

### Economic Change


| Earlier Phase       | Later Phase          |
| ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Trade-based economy | Agrarian economy     |
| Numerous towns      | Decline of towns     |
| Coin circulation    | Decline in coinage   |
| Commerce dominant   | Agriculture dominant |


---


## Agrarian Expansion


### Reasons

- Tax-free land grants.
- Settlement of new areas.
- Forest clearance.
- Brahmana colonization.

### Result


➡ More cultivation


➡ New villages


➡ Expansion of rural economy

# C. UPPCS MAINS ENRICHMENT

1. Land grants transformed South India from trade-oriented to agrarian economy.
2. Brahmanization acted as an instrument of state formation.
3. Sanskritization integrated tribal societies into emerging kingdoms.
4. Temple institutions became centres of economy, ideology and administration.
5. Kalabhra revolt reflected tensions within agrarian expansion.
6. Bhakti movement challenged ritual dominance while supporting temple culture.
7. Pallava-Chalukya conflict demonstrates competition over resource-rich river valleys.
8. Land grants strengthened Brahmanas but weakened peasant autonomy.
9. State formation and rural expansion were mutually reinforcing processes.
10. South Indian political legitimacy increasingly rested on Brahmanical support.
