
## 🌍 Interior of the Earth — Revision Notes

---


# 1. Importance of Studying Earth's Interior


### Why it is studied


Understanding Earth’s interior explains:

- **Earthquakes**
- **Volcanism**
- **Tsunamis**
- **Mountain building**
- **Plate tectonics**
- **Distribution of continents & ocean basins**

**Key Fact**


| Parameter      | Value        |
| -------------- | ------------ |
| Earth's Radius | **~6371 km** |


⚠ Direct observation of Earth's interior is **not possible** → knowledge comes from **indirect scientific evidence**.


---


# 2. Sources of Information about Earth's Interior


## Classification


| Category             | Sources                                       | Importance                |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Direct Sources**   | Mining, drilling, volcanic material           | Actual rock samples       |
| **Indirect Sources** | Seismic waves, gravity, magnetism, meteorites | Most reliable information |


---


# 3. Direct Sources of Information


## 3.1 Mining


Provides rock samples from **upper crust**.


Example:


| Location                 | Depth      |
| ------------------------ | ---------- |
| South African Gold Mines | **3–4 km** |


⚠ Limitation


Temperature increases rapidly → deep mining difficult.


---


## 3.2 Deep Drilling Projects


Major drilling programs:


| Program                                      | Purpose                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Deep Sea Drilling Project (DSDP)**         | Study oceanic crust        |
| **Integrated Ocean Drilling Program (IODP)** | Study deep Earth structure |


Example:

- Arctic drilling reached **~12 km depth**

Information obtained:

- Rock samples
- Temperature gradient
- Mineral composition

---


## 3.3 Volcanic Eruptions


Volcanoes bring **deep Earth material to surface**.


| Term      | Meaning                  |
| --------- | ------------------------ |
| **Magma** | Molten rock inside Earth |
| **Lava**  | Magma reaching surface   |


Scientists analyze:

- Lava composition
- **Xenoliths** (rock fragments from deep mantle)

⚠ Exact depth of origin is uncertain.


---


# 4. Indirect Sources of Information


These provide **most reliable knowledge** about Earth's interior.


Major sources:

1. **Seismic waves**
2. **Gravity anomalies**
3. **Magnetic field**
4. **Meteorites**

---


# 5. Temperature, Pressure & Density Inside Earth


General pattern:


| Property    | Trend with Depth |
| ----------- | ---------------- |
| Temperature | Increases        |
| Pressure    | Increases        |
| Density     | Increases        |


Example:

- Core temperature may exceed **5000°C**.

---


# 6. Meteorites


Meteorites help understand Earth because:

- They formed from **same solar nebula materials**.

Composition:

- **Iron**
- **Nickel**
- **Silicates**

This supports the **NIFE composition of Earth's core**.


---


# 7. Gravity Studies


Gravity varies across Earth.


Reasons:

1. Earth's rotation
2. Latitude variation
3. Uneven distribution of mass

---


## Gravity Anomaly


**Definition**


Difference between **actual gravity** and **expected gravity**.


| Gravity Value | Interpretation |
| ------------- | -------------- |
| High gravity  | Denser rocks   |
| Low gravity   | Lighter rocks  |


Importance:

- Detect mineral deposits
- Identify crustal structures
- Study density variations

---


# 8. Magnetic Field


Earth behaves like a **giant magnet**.


Magnetic surveys help identify:

- Magnetic minerals
- Geological structures
- Composition of crust

---


# 9. Seismic Activity (Most Important Source)


### Definition


**Seismic activity** = vibrations produced by earthquakes.


These waves reveal:

- Layered structure of Earth
- Density changes
- State of internal materials

---


# 10. Earthquake


### Definition


Sudden shaking of Earth due to **release of stored energy in rocks**.


Energy release generates **seismic waves**.


---


# 11. Causes of Earthquakes


Main cause:


Movement along **faults**.


### Fault


Fracture in crust where **rocks move relative to each other**.


---


### Earthquake Formation Process


```plain text
Stress accumulation
      ↓
Rock deformation
      ↓
Sudden rupture along fault
      ↓
Energy release
      ↓
Seismic waves
      ↓
Earthquake
```

| Term                   | Meaning                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Focus (Hypocentre)** | Point inside Earth where earthquake originates |
| **Epicentre**          | Point on surface directly above focus          |


⚠ Maximum damage occurs near **epicentre**.

# 15. Types of Earthquake Waves


```plain text
Earthquake Waves
│
├── Body Waves
│     ├── P Waves
│     └── S Waves
│
└── Surface Waves
```


---


# 16. Body Waves


Travel through **interior of Earth**.


---


## P Waves (Primary Waves)


| Property | Description          |
| -------- | -------------------- |
| Speed    | Fastest              |
| Medium   | Solid + Liquid + Gas |
| Motion   | Parallel vibration   |
| Arrival  | First                |


Similar to **sound waves**.


---


## S Waves (Secondary Waves)


| Property | Description             |
| -------- | ----------------------- |
| Speed    | Slower than P           |
| Medium   | Solid only              |
| Motion   | Perpendicular vibration |


⚠ Cannot travel through liquids.


---


# 17. Surface Waves


Produced when body waves reach Earth's surface.


| Feature | Description      |
| ------- | ---------------- |
| Speed   | Slowest          |
| Damage  | Most destructive |


Types:

- **Love waves**
- **Rayleigh waves**

---


# 18. Behaviour of Seismic Waves


When seismic waves encounter materials of different densities:


| Process    | Meaning           |
| ---------- | ----------------- |
| Reflection | Wave bounces back |
| Refraction | Wave bends        |


These behaviours help detect **internal layers**.


---


# 19. Seismic Shadow Zones


Regions where seismic waves **do not reach**.


---


## P-wave Shadow Zone


Located between:


**103° – 142° from epicentre**


Reason:


Refraction at **core–mantle boundary**.


---


## S-wave Shadow Zone


S waves cannot travel through **liquid outer core**.


Result:

- Large shadow zone covering **~40% of Earth**.

---


### Important Scientific Conclusion


From seismic wave behaviour scientists discovered:

- **Outer core → liquid**
- **Inner core → solid**

# 22. Layered Structure of Earth


Earth consists of **three main layers**.


| Layer      | Depth        | Characteristics   |
| ---------- | ------------ | ----------------- |
| **Crust**  | 0–35 km      | Thin outer layer  |
| **Mantle** | 35–2900 km   | Largest layer     |
| **Core**   | 2900–6371 km | Metallic interior |

# 24. Earth's Crust


### Thickness


| Location    | Thickness |
| ----------- | --------- |
| Ocean floor | 5–10 km   |
| Continents  | 30–70 km  |


Average thickness: **~35 km**


---


## Types of Crust


### Continental Crust


Composition:


**Silica + Aluminium → SIAL**


Examples:

- Continents
- Himalayas
- Deccan Plateau

---


### Oceanic Crust


Composition:


**Silica + Magnesium → SIMA**


Characteristics:

- Thinner
- Denser

Examples:

- Pacific Ocean floor
- Indian Ocean basin

# 25. Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho)


Boundary between:


**Crust and Mantle**


| Feature        | Detail              |
| -------------- | ------------------- |
| Discoverer     | Andrija Mohorovičić |
| Year           | 1909                |
| Identification | Seismic waves       |


Wave velocity increases due to **denser mantle rocks**.

- Iron
- Magnesium silicates

Dominant rock:


**Peridotite**

| --------------- | ------------ |

| Lower mantle    | 1000–2900 km |

| Feature   | Description |

| Behaviour | Plastic     |


Importance:

- Enables **plate tectonics**
- Lithospheric plates move over it.

# 29. Gutenberg Discontinuity


Boundary between:


**Mantle and Core**


| Parameter | Value    |
| --------- | -------- |
| Depth     | ~2900 km |


Evidence:

- S waves disappear
- P waves slow

Conclusion:


**Outer core is liquid.**

**NIFE**

# 12. Body Waves


Travel through **interior of Earth**.


## 12.1 P Waves (Primary Waves)


### Characteristics

- Fastest seismic waves
- First to reach seismograph
- Similar to **sound waves**
- Travel through:
    - Solids
    - Liquids
    - Gases

### Motion


Particles vibrate **parallel to wave direction**.


---


## 12.2 S Waves (Secondary Waves)


### Characteristics

- Slower than P waves
- Reach after P waves
- Travel **only through solids**

⚠️ Cannot travel through liquids.


### Motion


Particles vibrate **perpendicular to wave direction**.


---


# 13. Surface Waves


Generated when body waves reach the surface.


### Characteristics

- Travel along Earth’s surface
- **Slowest but most destructive**

Types:

1. Love waves
2. Rayleigh waves

---


# 14. Behavior of Seismic Waves


Seismic waves change behavior when they encounter materials with different densities.


### Two Important Phenomena


| Phenomenon | Meaning                                   |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Reflection | Wave bounces back                         |
| Refraction | Wave bends when entering different medium |


This helps scientists determine:

- Internal layers
- Density changes

---

**103° – 142° angular distance from epicentre**


Reason:

- Refraction at core-mantle boundary.

---


## S-Wave Shadow Zone


S waves **do not travel through liquid outer core**.


Result:

# 3. Earth's Crust


### Definition


The **outermost thin layer** of the Earth.


### Thickness


| Location    | Thickness |
| ----------- | --------- |
| Ocean floor | 5–10 km   |
| Continents  | 30–70 km  |


Average thickness:

- **~35 km**

---


## Types of Crust


### 1. Continental Crust


Composition:

- Dominated by **Silica + Aluminium**

Hence called **SIAL**


Examples:

- Continents
- Mountain ranges

Example in India:

- **Himalayas**
- **Deccan Plateau**

---


### 2. Oceanic Crust


Composition:

- **Silica + Magnesium**

Hence called **SIMA**


Characteristics:

- Thinner
- Denser

Example locations:

- **Pacific Ocean**
- **Indian Ocean basin**

---


# 4. Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho)


### Definition


Boundary between:


**Crust and Mantle**


### Important Points


| Feature          | Description         |
| ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Discoverer       | Andrija Mohorovičić |
| Year             | 1909                |
| Identified using | Seismic waves       |


Seismic waves **suddenly increase speed** here because mantle rocks are **denser**.


---


# 5. Mantle


### Definition


The **layer between crust and core**.


### Depth Range

- **35 km to 2900 km**

### Volume

- Contains **~83% of Earth’s volume**
- **~67% of Earth’s mass**

---


## Mantle Composition


Major minerals:

- **Iron**
- **Magnesium**
- Silicate rocks

Common rock:


**Peridotite**


---


## Mantle Subdivisions


| Layer           | Depth          |
| --------------- | -------------- |
| Upper Mantle    | 35 – 400 km    |
| Transition Zone | 400 – 1000 km  |
| Lower Mantle    | 1000 – 2900 km |


---


# 6. Asthenosphere


Located within **upper mantle**.


### Depth


Approximately:


**100–400 km**


---


### Characteristics


| Feature          | Description             |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- |
| Physical state   | Semi-molten             |
| Plastic behavior | Allows movement         |
| Importance       | Enables plate tectonics |


---


### Why Important?


The **lithospheric plates float and move over asthenosphere**.


This movement causes:

- Earthquakes
- Volcanoes
- Mountain building

---


# 7. Lithosphere


### Definition


Rigid outer layer including:

- **Crust**
- **Uppermost mantle**

---


### Thickness


Approx.


**100 km**


---


### Importance


Contains:

- Tectonic plates
- Continents
- Ocean basins

---


# Lithosphere–Asthenosphere System


```plain text
Surface
│
Lithosphere (Rigid plates)
│
Asthenosphere (Semi-molten)
│
Mantle
```


This system explains **plate tectonics**.

# 8. Gutenberg Discontinuity


### Definition


Boundary between:


**Mantle and Core**


---


### Depth


Approximately:


**2900 km**


---


### Key Evidence

- **S waves disappear**
- **P waves slow down**

This proved:


**Outer core is liquid.**

### Depth


From:


**2900 km to 6371 km**


---


### Composition


Mainly metallic:

- **Iron**
- **Nickel**

Hence called:


**NIFE**

**2900 – 5150 km**

Liquid

**Earth’s magnetic field**

### Depth


**5150 – 6371 km**

### Characteristics
