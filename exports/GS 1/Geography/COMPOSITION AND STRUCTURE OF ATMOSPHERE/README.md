# 1. ATMOSPHERE (Basic Concept)


## Definition


**Atmosphere** = A **mixture of gases, water vapour and dust particles surrounding the Earth**.


### Key Characteristics

- Colourless
- Odourless
- Tasteless
- Felt only when it **moves as wind**

### Importance for Life


Atmosphere makes Earth **habitable** because it:


| Function                  | Explanation                           |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Provides Oxygen           | Required for respiration of animals   |
| Provides CO₂              | Required for photosynthesis in plants |
| Regulates temperature     | Prevents extreme heating & cooling    |
| Protects from radiation   | Ozone absorbs harmful UV radiation    |
| Enables weather & climate | Rainfall, clouds, winds etc           |


---


## Survival Importance (NCERT Example)


Humans can survive:


| Without | Survival time |
| ------- | ------------- |
| Food    | Weeks         |
| Water   | Few days      |
| Air     | Few minutes   |


Thus **air is the most essential element of life**.


---


# 2. EXTENT OF ATMOSPHERE

- Atmosphere extends **hundreds of kilometres above Earth**.
- **99% of atmospheric mass lies within first 32 km** of Earth.

### Reason


Gravity pulls gases towards Earth → density decreases with altitude.


---


# 3. COMPOSITION OF ATMOSPHERE


Atmosphere consists of **three components**


1️⃣ **Gases**


2️⃣ **Water Vapour**


3️⃣ **Dust particles / Aerosols**


---


# 3.1 Major Gases of Atmosphere


### Composition of Dry Air


| Gas                           | Percentage | Importance                          |
| ----------------------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Nitrogen (N₂)**             | **78%**    | Dilutes oxygen, controls combustion |
| **Oxygen (O₂)**               | **21%**    | Respiration, combustion             |
| **Argon**                     | 0.93%      | Inert gas                           |
| **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)**      | 0.03–0.04% | Photosynthesis, greenhouse effect   |
| **Neon, Helium, Krypton etc** | Trace      | Rare gases                          |


📌 These proportions remain **almost constant up to 90 km**.


---


# 3.2 Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)


### Characteristics

- Meteorologically important gas. ( Meteorologically :  मौसम विज्ञान की दृष्टि से )
- Transparent to **incoming solar radiation**
- Absorbs **outgoing terrestrial radiation**.

### Role in Earth’s Temperature


Cause–Effect Chain


```plain text
Solar radiation enters atmosphere
        ↓
Earth absorbs heat
        ↓
Earth emits longwave radiation
        ↓
CO₂ absorbs and re-radiates heat
        ↓
Greenhouse effect
        ↓
Earth remains warm
```


### Importance


| Role           | Explanation                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Photosynthesis | Plants use CO₂                 |
| Greenhouse gas | Regulates temperature          |
| Climate change | Increase causes global warming |


### Sources Increasing CO₂

- Burning fossil fuels
- Deforestation
- Industrialization

---


# 3.3 Ozone (O₃)


### Location


Found mainly between **10 – 50 km altitude**.


### Function


| Function                      | Explanation                      |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Absorbs UV radiation          | Protects life                    |
| Maintains atmospheric balance | Controls heating in stratosphere |


### Exam Tip


UPPCS often asks about **Ozone layer location** → **Stratosphere**


---


# 3.4 Water Vapour


Water vapour is the **most variable gas** in the atmosphere.


### Percentage

- **0 – 4% of atmosphere**

### Distribution


Highest in:

- **Tropical regions**
- **Near oceans**

Lowest in:

- **Deserts**
- **Polar regions**

### Vertical Distribution


Decreases rapidly with altitude.


---


## Importance of Water Vapour


| Function                | Explanation                   |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Source of clouds        | Condensation produces clouds  |
| Source of precipitation | Rain, snow                    |
| Heat regulator          | Absorbs terrestrial radiation |
| Climate control         | Acts like thermal blanket     |


### Thermal Blanket Effect


```plain text
Sun heat absorbed by Earth
        ↓
Earth radiates heat
        ↓
Water vapour absorbs heat
        ↓
Heat retained in atmosphere
```


Thus Earth **does not become extremely cold or hot**.


---


# 3.5 Dust Particles (Aerosols)


Atmosphere contains **microscopic solid particles**.


### Sources


| Natural Sources | Human Sources     |
| --------------- | ----------------- |
| Sea salt        | Industrial smoke  |
| Fine soil       | Vehicle emissions |
| Volcanic ash    | Burning fuels     |
| Pollen          | Construction dust |


---


### Distribution


Dust concentration higher in:

- **Subtropical regions**
- **Temperate regions**

Reason → **Dry winds**


Dust concentration lower in:

- **Equatorial regions**
- **Polar regions**

---


### Importance of Dust Particles


| Function            | Explanation                      |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Cloud formation     | Dust acts as condensation nuclei |
| Scattering of light | Causes red sunsets               |
| Weather processes   | Helps precipitation              |


---


### Cloud Formation Process


```plain text
Water vapour in air
        ↓
Condensation around dust particles
        ↓
Formation of tiny droplets
        ↓
Cloud formation
```

# STRUCTURE OF ATMOSPHERE


Atmosphere is divided into **layers based on temperature variation with altitude**.


## Basis of Classification


Temperature changes with height.


### Five Major Layers


| Layer                     | Height             | Temperature Trend |
| ------------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------- |
| Troposphere               | Surface – ~8–18 km | Decreases         |
| Stratosphere              | ~18 – 50 km        | Increases         |
| Mesosphere                | 50 – 80 km         | Decreases         |
| Ionosphere (Thermosphere) | 80 – 400 km        | Increases         |
| Exosphere                 | Above 400 km       | Very thin         |

# 1. TROPOSPHERE


### Definition


The **lowest layer of atmosphere where all weather phenomena occur**.


### Height


| Region  | Height |
| ------- | ------ |
| Poles   | ~8 km  |
| Equator | ~18 km |
| Average | ~13 km |


Reason:


Equatorial heating causes **strong convection currents** → air rises higher.


---


### Important Characteristics


| Feature                 | Explanation              |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Weather zone            | Clouds, rainfall, storms |
| Highest air density     | Maximum gases            |
| Contains water vapour   | Responsible for humidity |
| Contains dust particles | Cloud formation          |


---


### Temperature Change


Temperature decreases with altitude.


### Normal Lapse Rate


Temperature decrease with height.


Rate:


**1°C per 165 m**


or


**6.5°C per km**


---


### Importance


Troposphere is important because:

- All **weather and climate processes** occur here
- Supports **life**
- Contains **most atmospheric mass**

---


### Boundary


Upper boundary = **Tropopause**


Characteristics:


| Feature           | Description                                  |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Separates         | Troposphere & Stratosphere                   |
| Temperature       | Nearly constant                              |
| Temperature value | About **−80°C (equator)**, **−45°C (poles)** |


---


# 2. STRATOSPHERE


### Location


Above tropopause.


Height:


**18 km – 50 km**


---


### Key Feature


Presence of **Ozone Layer**


---


### Ozone Layer


| Property   | Explanation          |
| ---------- | -------------------- |
| Altitude   | 15 – 35 km           |
| Function   | Absorbs UV radiation |
| Importance | Protects life        |


---


### Temperature Trend


Temperature **increases with height**.


Reason:


Ozone absorbs **ultraviolet radiation** → heating.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature               | Explanation            |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Stable layer          | Very little turbulence |
| No clouds/weather     | Almost absent          |
| Jet aircraft fly here | Smooth air             |


---


### Boundary


Upper boundary = **Stratopause**


Height:


≈ **50 km**


---


# 3. MESOSPHERE


### Location


Above stratosphere.


Height:


**50 km – 80 km**


---


### Temperature Trend


Temperature **decreases with altitude**.


Lowest temperature of atmosphere occurs here.


Approx:


**−100°C near mesopause**


---


### Special Feature


Most **meteors burn in this layer**.


Reason:


Friction with atmospheric gases.


---


### Boundary


Upper boundary = **Mesopause**


Height:


≈ **80 km**


---


# 4. IONOSPHERE (THERMOSPHERE)


### Location


**80 km – 400 km**


---


### Key Feature


Contains **ionized particles**.


These particles are electrically charged.


---


### Formation


Solar radiation → ionization of gases.


---


### Temperature Trend


Temperature **increases rapidly with altitude**.


---


### Importance


| Function                | Explanation                |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Radio communication     | Reflects radio waves       |
| Satellite communication | Signal transmission        |
| Auroras                 | Northern & Southern lights |


---


### Example


Aurora seen near:

- **Arctic region**
- **Antarctic region**

---


# 5. EXOSPHERE


### Location


Above **400 km**


This is the **outermost layer of atmosphere**.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature                    | Explanation             |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Extremely thin air         | Almost vacuum           |
| Hydrogen & helium dominate | Light gases             |
| Gradual merging            | Merges into outer space |
