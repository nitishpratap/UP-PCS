# PART 1 — IMPORTANCE OF WATER & HYDROLOGICAL CYCLE


## 1. Importance of Water 🌍


### Key Facts

- **Water is essential for life** on Earth.
- Earth is called the **“Blue Planet”** because of abundant water.
- Life cannot exist without water.

### Why Earth has Water

- Earth has a **suitable temperature range**.
- Presence of **atmosphere** prevents water loss.
- Water exists in **three states**:
    - Liquid
    - Solid
    - Gas

---


## 2. Hydrosphere


### Definition


**Hydrosphere** = All water present on Earth in **oceans, seas, rivers, lakes, glaciers, groundwater, and atmosphere**.


### Forms of Water in Hydrosphere


| Form   | Example               |
| ------ | --------------------- |
| Liquid | Oceans, rivers, lakes |
| Solid  | Glaciers, ice sheets  |
| Gas    | Water vapour          |


---


## 3. Distribution of Water on Earth


| Source     | Approx Share |
| ---------- | ------------ |
| Oceans     | **97%**      |
| Freshwater | **3%**       |


Freshwater distribution:


| Source                    | Share |
| ------------------------- | ----- |
| Ice caps & glaciers       | ~68%  |
| Groundwater               | ~30%  |
| Lakes, rivers, atmosphere | <1%   |


⚠️ **UPPCS Prelims Fact:**


Only **0.3% freshwater is easily usable**.

## Steps of Hydrological Cycle


### 1️⃣ Evaporation


Water changes from **liquid → vapour**


Sources:

- Oceans
- Lakes
- Rivers

**Oceans contribute ~84% evaporation**


---


### 2️⃣ Transpiration


Water released from **plants**


Together called:


➡ **Evapotranspiration**


---


### 3️⃣ Condensation


Water vapour cools → forms **clouds**


---


### 4️⃣ Precipitation


Water falls as:

- Rain
- Snow
- Hail
- Sleet

Distribution:


| Area   | Share |
| ------ | ----- |
| Oceans | ~77%  |
| Land   | ~23%  |


---


### 5️⃣ Runoff


Water flows back to oceans via rivers.


Example (India):

- **Ganga**
- **Brahmaputra**
- **Godavari**

---


### 6️⃣ Infiltration


Water penetrates soil → **Groundwater**


Example in **Uttar Pradesh**:

- Indo-Gangetic Plain has **high groundwater recharge**.

---


## Hydrological Cycle Flowchart


```plain text
Solar Energy
     ↓
Evaporation
     ↓
Condensation
     ↓
Cloud Formation
     ↓
Precipitation
     ↓
Runoff / Infiltration
     ↓
Rivers / Groundwater
     ↓
Oceans
```

# 2. Ocean Floor Relief


Ocean floors are **not flat**.


They contain **mountains, plains, ridges, trenches, and valleys**.


These features are formed by:

- **Tectonic activity**
- **Volcanic activity**
- **Sedimentation**

| Division                 | Description                         |

| Continental Shelf        | Shallow submerged part of continent |
| Continental Slope        | Steep descent to ocean basin        |
| Deep Sea Plain           | Flat deep ocean floor               |
| Oceanic Deeps (Trenches) | Deepest parts                       |

# 3. Continental Shelf


### Definition


The **continental shelf** is the **extended margin of continents submerged under shallow seas**.


---


## Key Characteristics

- **Shallowest part of ocean**
- Gentle slope (**<1° gradient**)
- Ends at **Shelf Break**

---


### Depth

- Usually **0–200 meters**

---


### Width


Average: **~80 km**


But varies:


| Region                        | Width       |
| ----------------------------- | ----------- |
| Siberian Shelf (Arctic Ocean) | ~1500 km    |
| Chile Coast                   | Very narrow |


---


## Importance


1️⃣ **Fishing grounds**


Example:

- Grand Banks (Atlantic Ocean)

2️⃣ **Petroleum reserves**


Example:

- **Bombay High (India)**

3️⃣ **Rich in minerals**


---


# 4. Continental Slope


### Definition


The **continental slope connects the continental shelf with the deep ocean basin**.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature  | Value            |
| -------- | ---------------- |
| Gradient | **2°–5°**        |
| Depth    | **200 – 3000 m** |


---


### Important Points

- Indicates **true boundary of continents**
- Contains **submarine canyons**

---


### Example


**Hudson Canyon** (Atlantic Ocean)


---


# 5. Deep Sea Plains (Abyssal Plains)


### Definition


**Deep sea plains are gently sloping areas of the ocean basin**.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature   | Value                     |
| --------- | ------------------------- |
| Depth     | **3000–6000 m**           |
| Surface   | Smoothest region on Earth |
| Sediments | Clay & silt               |


---


### Why So Smooth?


Because of:

- Fine sediment deposition
- Long geological time

---


# 6. Oceanic Deeps (Trenches)


### Definition


**Ocean trenches are long, narrow, and very deep depressions on the ocean floor**.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature  | Value            |
| -------- | ---------------- |
| Depth    | Up to **11 km**  |
| Shape    | Narrow & steep   |
| Location | Near island arcs |


---


### Formation


Associated with:

- **Subduction zones**
- **Plate tectonics**
- **Volcanic activity**
- **Earthquakes**

---


## Famous Ocean Trenches


| Trench             | Ocean    |
| ------------------ | -------- |
| Mariana Trench     | Pacific  |
| Tonga Trench       | Pacific  |
| Puerto Rico Trench | Atlantic |


---


## Important Exam Fact


Total trenches discovered:


| Ocean    | Trenches |
| -------- | -------- |
| Pacific  | 32       |
| Atlantic | 19       |
| Indian   | 6        |

A **mid-oceanic ridge** is a **long underwater mountain chain formed by volcanic activity where tectonic plates diverge**.

| Length    | Continuous across oceans                           |
| Formation | **Plate divergence & magma upwelling**             |

# 2. Seamounts


### Definition


A **seamount** is an **isolated submarine mountain rising from the ocean floor that does not reach the surface**.


---


## Characteristics


| Feature | Description           |
| ------- | --------------------- |
| Origin  | Mostly **volcanic**   |
| Height  | Up to **3000–4000 m** |
| Shape   | Conical mountain      |
| Surface | Submerged             |


---


### Example

- **Emperor Seamounts**

# 3. Guyots


### Definition


A **guyot** is a **flat-topped seamount** formed due to erosion and subsidence.


---


## Formation Process


Flowchart:


```plain text
Volcanic Island
        ↓
Wave erosion flattens top
        ↓
Island sinks due to subsidence
        ↓
Flat-topped underwater mountain (Guyot)
```


---


## Key Characteristics


| Feature  | Description            |
| -------- | ---------------------- |
| Top      | Flat                   |
| Origin   | Eroded volcanic island |
| Location | Mostly Pacific Ocean   |

## 1. Concept of Ocean Water Temperature


### Definition


**Ocean water temperature** refers to the **amount of heat stored in ocean water**, mainly received from **solar radiation (insolation)**.


Important point:

- Oceans **heat and cool more slowly than land**.

Reason:

- Water has **high specific heat capacity**.

---


# 2. Sources of Heat in Oceans


| Source                     | Contribution            |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Solar radiation            | **Main source of heat** |
| Heat from Earth's interior | Very small              |
| Heat from ocean currents   | Redistributes heat      |


---


# 3. Factors Affecting Ocean Temperature


According to NCERT, the distribution of temperature depends on several factors.


---


## (1) Latitude


### Key Idea


Temperature **decreases from the Equator toward the Poles**.


Reason:

- Insolation decreases with latitude.

Example:


| Region            | Avg Temperature |
| ----------------- | --------------- |
| Equatorial oceans | 27–28°C         |
| Polar oceans      | Near freezing   |


---


## (2) Unequal Distribution of Land and Water

- Oceans in **Northern Hemisphere are warmer**.

Reason:

- Northern Hemisphere has **more landmass** which absorbs heat and transfers it to oceans.

---


## (3) Prevailing Winds


### Offshore winds

- Blow **from land to ocean**
- Push warm surface water away
- Cause **upwelling of cold water**

### Onshore winds

- Blow **from ocean to land**
- Accumulate warm water near coast
- Increase temperature

Example:

- **Peru coast upwelling**

---


## (4) Ocean Currents


### Warm Currents


Increase temperature.


Example:


| Warm Current | Effect               |
| ------------ | -------------------- |
| Gulf Stream  | Warms Western Europe |


---


### Cold Currents


Decrease temperature.


Example:


| Cold Current     | Effect                     |
| ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| Labrador Current | Cools North American coast |


---


# 4. Horizontal Distribution of Temperature


This refers to **temperature variation across the ocean surface**.


---


## General Pattern


| Region     | Temperature |
| ---------- | ----------- |
| Equator    | Highest     |
| Subtropics | Moderate    |
| Poles      | Lowest      |


---


## Important Facts

- Warmest oceans occur near **equator**.
- Coldest oceans occur near **polar regions**.

# 5. Vertical Distribution of Ocean Temperature


Temperature also changes **with depth**.


---


## Temperature Profile of Oceans


Ocean water has **three layers**.


| Layer         | Depth      | Characteristics        |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------------- |
| Surface Layer | 0–200 m    | Warm                   |
| Thermocline   | 200–1000 m | Rapid temperature drop |
| Deep Zone     | >1000 m    | Cold and stable        |


---


## Thermocline


### Definition


**Thermocline** is the **zone where temperature decreases rapidly with depth**.
