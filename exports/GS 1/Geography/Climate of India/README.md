
# 1. Weather vs Climate (VERY IMPORTANT)


| Feature    | Weather                          | Climate                      |
| ---------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Definition | Short-term atmospheric condition | Long-term average of weather |
| Time scale | Hours / Days                     | 30 years or more             |
| Example    | Rain today in Lucknow            | Monsoon climate of India     |


### Definition (Exam Ready)


**Weather:**

> State of atmosphere at a specific place and time.

**Climate:**

> Average weather conditions of a region over a long period (generally **30 years**).

Important elements of weather:

- Temperature
- Pressure
- Wind
- Humidity
- Precipitation

✔ Climate is determined by **long-term patterns of these elements**.


---


# 2. Monsoon Climate of India


India has a **Monsoon Type Climate**.


### Meaning of Monsoon


Monsoon means:

> **Seasonal reversal of winds.**

Flowchart:


```plain text
Summer
Land heats faster
↓
Low pressure on land
↓
Winds blow from sea to land
↓
Heavy rainfall
(Southwest Monsoon)

Winter
Land cools faster
↓
High pressure on land
↓
Winds blow from land to sea
↓
Dry winds
(Northeast Monsoon)
```


---


# 3. Unity of Monsoon Climate


India and Southeast Asia share a **common monsoon system**.


Regions sharing monsoon climate:

- India
- Bangladesh
- Myanmar
- Thailand
- Vietnam
- Sri Lanka

But **regional variations exist** within India.


---


# 4. Regional Variations in India's Climate


Even though India has monsoon climate, **huge regional differences exist**.


## 4.1 Temperature Variation


Examples:


| Place              | Season | Temperature |
| ------------------ | ------ | ----------- |
| Western Rajasthan  | Summer | ~50°C       |
| Jammu & Kashmir    | Winter | −20°C       |
| Thiruvananthapuram | Winter | ~25°C       |


Explanation:

- Desert areas → very hot
- Himalayas → very cold
- Coastal areas → moderate

---


### Example for Mains Answer


Example showing variation:

- **Drass (Ladakh)** → extremely cold winters
- **Jaisalmer (Rajasthan)** → extremely hot summers

---


## 4.2 Diurnal Variation (Day-Night Difference)


Example:


| Region      | Day   | Night |
| ----------- | ----- | ----- |
| Thar Desert | ~50°C | ~20°C |


Reason:

- Dry air
- Lack of humidity
- No clouds

---


## 4.3 Rainfall Variation


Rainfall distribution is extremely uneven.


| Place                               | Annual Rainfall |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Mawsynram / Cherrapunji (Meghalaya) | >1000 cm        |
| Jaisalmer (Rajasthan)               | <10 cm          |


Example:

- Meghalaya may receive **rain in one day equal to several years of rainfall in desert areas**.

---


# 5. Seasonal Distribution of Rainfall


India receives most rainfall from:


**June → September**


(Southwest Monsoon)


But exceptions exist.


### Tamil Nadu Rainfall


Tamil Nadu receives rainfall during:


**October – December**


Reason:


**Northeast Monsoon**


---


# 6. Climatic Rhythm of India


Despite variations, India shows a **clear seasonal rhythm**.


India has **distinct seasons**:


1️⃣ Winter


2️⃣ Summer


3️⃣ Southwest Monsoon


4️⃣ Retreating Monsoon


This seasonal rhythm defines **Indian Monsoon Climate**.

# 8. Latitude


Important geographical line:


**Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N)**


It divides India into two parts.


Map idea:


```plain text
Northern India → Subtropical Zone
Southern India → Tropical Zone
```


---


### Climatic Impact


| Region          | Climate             |
| --------------- | ------------------- |
| South of Tropic | Tropical climate    |
| North of Tropic | Subtropical climate |


Features:


Tropical areas:

- High temperature
- Small annual range

Subtropical areas:

- Larger seasonal temperature variation

---


# 9. Himalayas – Climatic Barrier


The **Himalayas act as a climatic wall**.


Functions:


### 1️⃣ Block Cold Winds


Cold winds from:

- Central Asia
- Siberia

cannot enter India.


Result:


India has **warmer winters** than many places at similar latitudes.


---


### 2️⃣ Trap Monsoon Winds


Himalayas block moisture-laden monsoon winds.


Result:


```plain text
Monsoon winds rise
↓
Cooling
↓
Condensation
↓
Heavy rainfall
```


This creates rainfall over:

- Indo-Gangetic plains
- Northeast India

---


# 10. Distribution of Land and Sea


India is surrounded by the **Indian Ocean on three sides**.


Difference in heating:


| Surface | Heating       |
| ------- | ------------- |
| Land    | heats quickly |
| Water   | heats slowly  |


This causes **pressure differences**.


Result:


```plain text
Summer → land low pressure
Winter → land high pressure
```


This pressure difference creates **monsoon winds**.


---


# 11. Distance from the Sea


Climate types:


| Type        | Features             | Example |
| ----------- | -------------------- | ------- |
| Maritime    | Moderate temperature | Mumbai  |
| Continental | Extreme temperature  | Delhi   |


---


### Example


Mumbai:

- Small temperature variation

Delhi:

- Very hot summers
- Cold winters

---


# 12. Altitude


Temperature decreases with height.


Important rule:


Temperature decreases **6.5°C per 1000 m**


Example:


| City       | Temperature |
| ---------- | ----------- |
| Agra       | Warmer      |
| Darjeeling | Cooler      |


Even though both are near same latitude.


---


# 13. Relief (Topography)


Mountains influence rainfall patterns.


Example:


### Western Ghats


Windward side:

- Heavy rainfall

Leeward side:

- Rain shadow region

Example rain shadow areas:

- Deccan Plateau
- Interior Karnataka
- Maharashtra Plateau

# 1. Mechanism of Indian Monsoon


Indian monsoon occurs due to **interaction of several atmospheric and geographic factors**.


### Main Factors


| Factor               | Role                              |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Differential heating | Creates pressure difference       |
| ITCZ shift           | Brings monsoon trough             |
| Tibetan Plateau      | Heat source strengthening monsoon |
| Jet Streams          | Control onset and withdrawal      |
| Southern Oscillation | Controls monsoon intensity        |

### Concept


Land heats faster than ocean.


### Summer Situation

- **North India → Low pressure**
- **Indian Ocean → High pressure**

Moist winds move toward India.

Zone where **trade winds from both hemispheres converge**.

# 5. Jet Streams


### Definition


Jet streams are:


✔ Narrow bands of **fast moving winds**


✔ Located in upper atmosphere (~12 km altitude)


Speed:


150–300 km/hr


---


### Important Jet Streams for India


| Jet Stream               | Season | Effect                      |
| ------------------------ | ------ | --------------------------- |
| Subtropical Westerly Jet | Winter | Causes western disturbances |
| Tropical Easterly Jet    | Summer | Helps monsoon rainfall      |

# Seasons of India


India experiences **four major seasons**.


| Season             | Months              |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Winter             | December – February |
| Summer             | March – May         |
| Southwest Monsoon  | June – September    |
| Retreating Monsoon | October – November  |


---


# 1. Winter Season


### Temperature Pattern


Temperature decreases **south → north**.


Example:


| City    | Temperature |
| ------- | ----------- |
| Chennai | ~24°C       |
| Delhi   | ~10°C       |


---


### Characteristics


✔ Dry weather


✔ Clear skies


✔ Western disturbance rainfall in north India


---

Months:


March – May


---

- Rajasthan
- Madhya Pradesh
- Uttar Pradesh

### Loo

- Punjab

- Uttar Pradesh

# Winter Weather Conditions in India


Winter months:


**December – February**


---


## Pressure Distribution in Winter


### Situation

- **High pressure develops over Central Asia**
- Air flows **toward the Indian subcontinent**

---


### Result


Winds blow:


**Central Asia → India**


These winds are:


✔ Cold


✔ Dry


✔ Continental


---


# Upper Air Circulation


Air circulation near the earth surface is influenced by **upper atmosphere winds**.


At about:


**12 km altitude**


There exist strong winds called **Jet Streams**.


---


## Jet Stream Path


Jet streams flow from:


**West → East**


across **Central Asia and India**.


---


## Tibetan Plateau Influence


The **Tibetan Plateau acts as a barrier**.


This causes the jet stream to split into **two branches**.


---
