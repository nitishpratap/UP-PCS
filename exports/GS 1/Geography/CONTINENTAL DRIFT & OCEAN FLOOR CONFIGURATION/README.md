
## 🌍 Interior of the Earth — Revision Notes

---


# 1. Importance of Studying Earth's Interior


### Why it is studied


Understanding Earth’s interior explains:

- **Earthquakes**
- **Volcanism**
- **Tsunamis**
- **Mountain building**
- **Plate tectonics**
- **Distribution of continents & ocean basins**

**Key Fact**


| Parameter      | Value        |
| -------------- | ------------ |
| Earth's Radius | **~6371 km** |


⚠ Direct observation of Earth's interior is **not possible** → knowledge comes from **indirect scientific evidence**.


---


# 2. Sources of Information about Earth's Interior


## Classification


| Category             | Sources                                       | Importance                |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Direct Sources**   | Mining, drilling, volcanic material           | Actual rock samples       |
| **Indirect Sources** | Seismic waves, gravity, magnetism, meteorites | Most reliable information |


---


# 3. Direct Sources of Information


## 3.1 Mining


Provides rock samples from **upper crust**.


Example:


| Location                 | Depth      |
| ------------------------ | ---------- |
| South African Gold Mines | **3–4 km** |


⚠ Limitation


Temperature increases rapidly → deep mining difficult.


---


## 3.2 Deep Drilling Projects


Major drilling programs:


| Program                                      | Purpose                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Deep Sea Drilling Project (DSDP)**         | Study oceanic crust        |
| **Integrated Ocean Drilling Program (IODP)** | Study deep Earth structure |


Example:

- Arctic drilling reached **~12 km depth**

Information obtained:

- Rock samples
- Temperature gradient
- Mineral composition

---


## 3.3 Volcanic Eruptions


Volcanoes bring **deep Earth material to surface**.


| Term      | Meaning                  |
| --------- | ------------------------ |
| **Magma** | Molten rock inside Earth |
| **Lava**  | Magma reaching surface   |


Scientists analyze:

- Lava composition
- **Xenoliths** (rock fragments from deep mantle)

⚠ Exact depth of origin is uncertain.


---


# 4. Indirect Sources of Information


These provide **most reliable knowledge** about Earth's interior.


Major sources:

1. **Seismic waves**
2. **Gravity anomalies**
3. **Magnetic field**
4. **Meteorites**

---


# 5. Temperature, Pressure & Density Inside Earth


General pattern:


| Property    | Trend with Depth |
| ----------- | ---------------- |
| Temperature | Increases        |
| Pressure    | Increases        |
| Density     | Increases        |


Example:

- Core temperature may exceed **5000°C**.

---


# 6. Meteorites


Meteorites help understand Earth because:

- They formed from **same solar nebula materials**.

Composition:

- **Iron**
- **Nickel**
- **Silicates**

This supports the **NIFE composition of Earth's core**.


---


# 7. Gravity Studies


Gravity varies across Earth.


Reasons:

1. Earth's rotation
2. Latitude variation
3. Uneven distribution of mass

---


## Gravity Anomaly


**Definition**


Difference between **actual gravity** and **expected gravity**.


| Gravity Value | Interpretation |
| ------------- | -------------- |
| High gravity  | Denser rocks   |
| Low gravity   | Lighter rocks  |


Importance:

- Detect mineral deposits
- Identify crustal structures
- Study density variations

---


# 8. Magnetic Field


Earth behaves like a **giant magnet**.


Magnetic surveys help identify:

- Magnetic minerals
- Geological structures
- Composition of crust

---


# 9. Seismic Activity (Most Important Source)


### Definition


**Seismic activity** = vibrations produced by earthquakes.


These waves reveal:

- Layered structure of Earth
- Density changes
- State of internal materials

---


# 10. Earthquake


### Definition


Sudden shaking of Earth due to **release of stored energy in rocks**.


Energy release generates **seismic waves**.


---


# 11. Causes of Earthquakes


Main cause:


Movement along **faults**.


### Fault


Fracture in crust where **rocks move relative to each other**.


---


### Earthquake Formation Process


```plain text
Stress accumulation
      ↓
Rock deformation
      ↓
Sudden rupture along fault
      ↓
Energy release
      ↓
Seismic waves
      ↓
Earthquake
```


---


# 12. Focus vs Epicentre


| Term                   | Meaning                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Focus (Hypocentre)** | Point inside Earth where earthquake originates |
| **Epicentre**          | Point on surface directly above focus          |


⚠ Maximum damage occurs near **epicentre**.


---

# 15. Types of Earthquake Waves


```plain text
Earthquake Waves
│
├── Body Waves
│     ├── P Waves
│     └── S Waves
│
└── Surface Waves
```


---


# 16. Body Waves


Travel through **interior of Earth**.


---


## P Waves (Primary Waves)


| Property | Description          |
| -------- | -------------------- |
| Speed    | Fastest              |
| Medium   | Solid + Liquid + Gas |
| Motion   | Parallel vibration   |
| Arrival  | First                |


Similar to **sound waves**.


---


## S Waves (Secondary Waves)


| Property | Description             |
| -------- | ----------------------- |
| Speed    | Slower than P           |
| Medium   | Solid only              |
| Motion   | Perpendicular vibration |


⚠ Cannot travel through liquids.


---


# 17. Surface Waves


Produced when body waves reach Earth's surface.


| Feature | Description      |
| ------- | ---------------- |
| Speed   | Slowest          |
| Damage  | Most destructive |


Types:

- **Love waves**
- **Rayleigh waves**

---


# 18. Behaviour of Seismic Waves


When seismic waves encounter materials of different densities:


| Process    | Meaning           |
| ---------- | ----------------- |
| Reflection | Wave bounces back |
| Refraction | Wave bends        |


These behaviours help detect **internal layers**.


---


# 19. Seismic Shadow Zones


Regions where seismic waves **do not reach**.


---


## P-wave Shadow Zone


Located between:


**103° – 142° from epicentre**


Reason:


Refraction at **core–mantle boundary**.


---


## S-wave Shadow Zone


S waves cannot travel through **liquid outer core**.


Result:

- Large shadow zone covering **~40% of Earth**.

---


### Important Scientific Conclusion


From seismic wave behaviour scientists discovered:

- **Outer core → liquid**
- **Inner core → solid**

**Silica + Aluminium → SIAL**

- Continents

Composition:


**Silica + Magnesium → SIMA**


Characteristics:

- Thinner
- Denser

Examples:

- Pacific Ocean floor
- Indian Ocean basin

# PART 1 – CONTINENTAL DRIFT THEORY


## 1. Basic Question of Geography


Geographers asked two important questions:

- Why are **continents and oceans distributed unevenly**?
- Have the **continents always remained in the same position**?

Earlier it was believed that:

- Continents and oceans were **fixed and permanent**.
- Later evidence suggested that **continents moved over time**.

---


# 2. Continental Drift Theory


### Scientist


**Alfred Wegener (German Meteorologist)**


Proposed in **1912**


### Definition


**Continental Drift Theory:**


The continents were once joined together as a **single landmass** and later **drifted apart to their present locations**.


---


## 3. Supercontinent and Superocean


| Feature               | Description                       |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Supercontinent**    | **Pangaea** (meaning "all earth") |
| **Surrounding ocean** | **Panthalassa**                   |
| **Time period**       | About **200 million years ago**   |


---


### Diagram (Conceptual)


```plain text
Panthalassa Ocean
     -------------------------------
           PANGAEA (Supercontinent)
     -------------------------------
           Panthalassa Ocean
```


---


## 4. Breakup of Pangaea


Around **200 million years ago**, Pangaea split into two major landmasses:


| Landmass         | Hemisphere          | Present Continents                                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Laurasia**     | Northern Hemisphere | North America, Europe, Asia                         |
| **Gondwanaland** | Southern Hemisphere | South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica |


---


### Flowchart


```plain text
PANGAEA
   ↓
Split into
   ↓
Laurasia (North)
Gondwanaland (South)
   ↓
Further fragmentation
   ↓
Present continents
```


---


# 5. Evidence Supporting Continental Drift


Wegener provided **multiple evidences**:

1. **Fit of the Continents**
2. **Rocks of Same Age Across Oceans**
3. **Tillite Deposits (Glacial deposits)**
4. **Placer Deposits**
5. **Fossil Evidence**

---


# 6. Fit of Continents (Jigsaw Fit)


### Observation

- The **coastlines of South America and Africa match closely**.

### Evidence


Computer analysis (Bullard, 1964) showed the **best fit at 1000 m depth contour**.


### Diagram


```plain text
South America      Africa

   ( )              ( )
  (   )            (   )
   ( )              ( )

Matching coastlines
```


---


# 7. Rocks of Same Age Across Oceans


### Observation


Radiometric dating revealed:

- Rocks on **Brazil coast** match those on **Western Africa**.

### Implication

- The **Atlantic Ocean formed later**, after continents separated.

---


# 8. Tillite Deposits (Glacial Evidence)


### Tillite Definition


**Tillite:** Sedimentary rock formed from **glacial deposits**.


### Gondwana Tillite System


Found in:

- India
- Africa
- Falkland Islands
- Madagascar
- Antarctica
- Australia

### Interpretation


These continents were once part of **Gondwanaland** and shared **similar glacial history**.


---


### India Example


**Gondwana Tillite deposits** found in:

- Talchir Formation (Odisha region)

---


# 9. Placer Deposits Evidence


### Example

- Rich **gold deposits** found on **Ghana coast**.

However,

- Source rocks exist in **Brazil**.

### Conclusion

- Ghana and Brazil were once **connected**.

---


# 10. Fossil Evidence


Fossils of identical species found across separated continents.


### Example


| Fossil         | Found In                |
| -------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Mesosaurus** | Brazil and South Africa |


Mesosaurus:

- Small reptile
- Lived in **freshwater**

### Logic


It could **not cross oceans**, so continents must have been **connected earlier**.


---


### Map-based Locations


Important locations:

- **Brazil**
- **South Africa**
- **India**
- **Madagascar**
- **Antarctica**
- **Australia**

These together formed **Gondwanaland**.


---


# 11. Forces Responsible for Drift (According to Wegener)


Wegener proposed two forces:


---


## 1. Pole-Fleeing Force


Cause:

- Earth rotation
- Equatorial bulge

Effect:

- Continents move toward the **equator**.

---


## 2. Tidal Force


Cause:

- Gravitational attraction of **Moon and Sun**

Effect:

- Produces **tidal movements** that move continents.

---


### Problem


Scientists later found:

- These forces were **too weak** to move continents.

Thus, Wegener's theory was **initially rejected**.


---


# 12. Limitations of Continental Drift Theory


| Limitation          | Explanation                       |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Weak forces         | Proposed forces insufficient      |
| No mechanism        | Exact process of movement unclear |
| Ocean floor ignored | Evidence mainly from continents   |

# 13. Mantle Convection Theory


Scientist: **Arthur Holmes**


### Concept

- Radioactive elements generate heat in mantle.
- Heat creates **convection currents**.

### Process


```plain text
Heat in mantle
      ↓
Convection currents
      ↓
Movement of crust
      ↓
Continental movement
```

- Ocean floor is **not flat**

This helped explain **plate tectonics later**.

# PART 2 – NEW DEVELOPMENTS AFTER CONTINENTAL DRIFT THEORY


---


# 1. Why New Evidence Was Needed


Although Wegener provided strong evidence, scientists rejected the theory because:

- He **could not explain the mechanism of movement**
- The forces he suggested were **too weak**
- Evidence was mostly from **continents, not oceans**

Later research, especially **ocean floor mapping after World War II**, provided new information.


---


# 2. Ocean Floor Mapping


After World War II, scientists used:

- **Sonar technology**
- **Deep-sea exploration**

They discovered that the **ocean floor is not flat**.


Instead it contains:

- **Mid-ocean ridges**
- **Deep sea trenches**
- **Abyssal plains**
- **Volcanic mountains**

This revolutionized geology.


---


# 3. Major Divisions of the Ocean Floor


The ocean floor is divided into **three major parts**.


| Division                | Description                      |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Continental Margins** | Zone between continent and ocean |
| **Deep-Sea Basins**     | Flat deep ocean floors           |
| **Mid-Ocean Ridges**    | Long submarine mountain chains   |


---


# 4. Continental Margins


These form the **transition between continents and ocean basins**.


### Components

1. **Continental Shelf**
2. **Continental Slope**
3. **Continental Rise**
4. **Deep Sea Trenches**

# 5. Deep Sea Basins


### Abyssal Plains


Definition:

- **Extensive flat areas of ocean floor**

Characteristics:

- Located between **continental margins and mid-ocean ridges**
- Covered with **thick marine sediments**

Important fact for Prelims:


⭐ **Abyssal plains are the flattest areas on Earth**


---


# 6. Mid-Ocean Ridges


Definition:


A **continuous submarine mountain chain** present in all oceans.


Important features:

- Central **rift valley**
- Volcanic activity
- Earthquake zones
- Formation of new ocean crust

---


### Key Example


**Mid-Atlantic Ridge**


It runs:

- From **Arctic Ocean**
- Through **Atlantic Ocean**
- Towards **Indian Ocean**

---


### Important Fact


⭐ **The Mid-Ocean Ridge system is the longest mountain chain on Earth.**


---


# 7. Characteristics of Mid-Ocean Ridges


Features include:

- Central **rift system**
- Fractured plateau
- Volcanic eruptions
- Formation of **new oceanic crust**

These areas show:

- **Frequent earthquakes**
- **Volcanic activity**

---


# 8. Distribution of Earthquakes and Volcanoes


When scientists mapped earthquakes and volcanoes, they noticed **specific patterns**.


Two main belts:


---


## 1. Mid-Ocean Ridge Belt

- Earthquakes are **shallow**
- Associated with **volcanic activity**

Example:

- **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**

---


## 2. Circum-Pacific Belt


Also known as:


⭐ **Pacific Ring of Fire**


Characteristics:

- High volcanic activity
- Deep earthquakes
- Subduction zones

---


### Important Countries in Ring of Fire

- Japan
- Philippines
- Indonesia
- Chile
- Peru
- USA (California, Alaska)

---


# 9. Earthquake Depth Pattern


| Location         | Earthquake Type     |
| ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Mid-ocean ridges | Shallow earthquakes |
| Pacific margins  | Deep earthquakes    |


---


# 10. Oceanic Trenches


Definition:


Deep narrow depressions on ocean floor.


Example:

- **Mariana Trench**
- **Java Trench**
- **Peru–Chile Trench**

Importance:

- Sites of **subduction**
- Associated with **volcanic arcs**

---


# 11. Key Observations from Ocean Studies


Scientists discovered:

1. Ocean floor is **younger than continents**
2. **Volcanic eruptions** occur along mid-ocean ridges
3. Rocks on both sides of ridges are **symmetrical in age**
4. Earthquakes occur along **specific belts**

These discoveries eventually led to the **Plate Tectonic Theory**.


---


# 12. Connection to Plate Tectonics


Modern theory explains:

- Continents move because of **tectonic plates**
- Plates move due to **mantle convection currents**

---


### Process Flow


```plain text
Radioactive heat in mantle
        ↓
Mantle convection currents
        ↓
Movement of tectonic plates
        ↓
Continental drift
```
