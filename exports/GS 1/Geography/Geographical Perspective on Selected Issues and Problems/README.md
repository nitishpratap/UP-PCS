
# PART 1 — Environmental Pollution


## 1. Environmental Pollution (Definition)


**Environmental Pollution:**


Release of **substances and energy from waste products of human activities** which degrade the environment.


### Classification of Pollution


Based on the **medium through which pollutants spread**:


| Type            | Medium              |
| --------------- | ------------------- |
| Air Pollution   | Atmosphere          |
| Water Pollution | Water bodies        |
| Land Pollution  | Soil / Land surface |
| Noise Pollution | Sound environment   |


---


# 2. Types of Pollution and Sources


| Pollution Type      | Pollutants                                              | Major Sources                                |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Air Pollution**   | SO₂, NOx, CO, hydrocarbons, lead, ammonia               | Fossil fuel combustion, industries, vehicles |
| **Water Pollution** | Nitrates, pesticides, heavy metals, bacteria            | Sewage, industrial waste, agriculture        |
| **Land Pollution**  | Garbage, chemicals, fertilizers, radioactive substances | Waste disposal, agriculture                  |
| **Noise Pollution** | High decibel sound                                      | Aircrafts, traffic, industries               |


_(Derived from Table 12.1 in the chapter)_


---


# 3. Water Pollution


## Definition


Water becomes polluted when the **concentration of suspended particles, organic and inorganic substances increases beyond self-purification capacity.**


### Important Concept


**Self-purification capacity of water**

- Natural ability of rivers/lakes to clean pollutants
- Works only when pollution load is low

---


## Sources of Water Pollution


### 1️⃣ Natural Sources

- Soil erosion
- Landslides
- Decay of plants and animals

### 2️⃣ Human Sources (Major Concern)


**Industrial Activities**

- Chemical industries
- Textile industries
- Paper and pulp industries
- Leather industries

**Agriculture**

- Fertilisers
- Pesticides
- Herbicides

**Cultural Activities**

- Pilgrimages
- Religious fairs
- Tourism

---


## Example: Water Pollution in Indian Rivers


| River      | Location         | Source of Pollution                   |
| ---------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Ganga**  | Kanpur, Varanasi | Industrial waste, domestic sewage     |
|            | Farrakka Barrage | Carcass dumping                       |
| **Yamuna** | Delhi → Chambal  | Domestic sewage + agricultural runoff |


_(Table 12.2 in chapter)_


### Map Insight (Important for Exam)


Key polluted stretches:

- **Kanpur – Ganga pollution hotspot**
- **Varanasi – religious + sewage pollution**
- **Delhi – Yamuna most polluted stretch**

UPPCS often asks:


**Which river stretch is most polluted in India? → Yamuna in Delhi**


---


## Agriculture and Water Pollution


Modern agriculture uses:

- Chemical fertilizers
- Pesticides
- Herbicides

### Process


```plain text
Chemical Fertilizers
        ↓
Rainfall / Irrigation runoff
        ↓
Chemicals enter rivers
        ↓
Nitrate concentration increases
        ↓
Water pollution
```


Also contaminates **groundwater**.


---


## Health Impacts of Water Pollution


Water pollution causes **water-borne diseases**:

- Diarrhoea
- Hepatitis
- Intestinal worms
- Cholera

Important Fact:


**About 1/4th communicable diseases in India are water-borne**.

# 4. Air Pollution


## Definition


Addition of **dust, fumes, gases, smoke, vapours or odours** in air in harmful concentration.


---


## Major Causes


### 1️⃣ Combustion of Fossil Fuels

- Coal
- Petrol
- Diesel

### 2️⃣ Industrial Activities


### 3️⃣ Mining


---


## Major Air Pollutants

- Sulphur Oxides (SO₂)
- Nitrogen Oxides (NOx)
- Carbon monoxide
- Hydrocarbons
- Lead
- Asbestos

---


## Effects of Air Pollution


### 1️⃣ Human Health


Diseases related to:

- Respiratory system
- Nervous system
- Circulatory system

---


### 2️⃣ Urban Smog


**Smog = Smoke + Fog**


Occurs in **large cities with heavy industrial emissions**.


Example cities:

- Mumbai
- Delhi
- Beijing
- Los Angeles

_(Photo example given in the chapter)_


---


### 3️⃣ Acid Rain


Air pollutants react with water vapor.


### Process


```plain text
SO₂ + NOx
     ↓
React with atmospheric water
     ↓
Sulphuric acid + Nitric acid
     ↓
Acid Rain
```


Impact:

- Crop damage
- Soil acidification
- Monument corrosion

Example: **Taj Trapezium Zone issue (UP)**

# 1. Urban Waste Disposal


## Meaning


**Urban waste** refers to the **solid waste generated in urban areas from domestic, commercial and industrial activities.**


Examples:

- Broken glass
- Plastic containers
- Polythene bags
- Metals
- Ashes
- Electronic waste
- CDs / floppies

These discarded materials are called:

- **Refuse**
- **Garbage**
- **Rubbish**

---


# Sources of Urban Waste


| Source                    | Waste Generated             |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Household                 | Food waste, plastics, paper |
| Commercial establishments | Packaging waste             |
| Industries                | Ash, debris, chemicals      |


_(From the section “Urban Waste Disposal” in the chapter)_


---


# Waste Disposal System in Cities


Urban waste is usually disposed through:


### 1️⃣ Landfills


Low-lying public grounds used for dumping waste.


### 2️⃣ Municipal Collection


City corporations collect waste from households.


### 3️⃣ Industrial Waste Disposal


Industries dump waste separately.


---


# Problems Caused by Urban Waste


### Health Hazards


Solid waste leads to:

- Foul smell
- Spread of flies
- Rodents (rats)
- Mosquito breeding

Diseases spread through waste:

- Typhoid
- Cholera
- Diarrhoea
- Malaria
- Diphtheria

---


### Environmental Problems


```plain text
Urban Waste
      ↓
Open dumping
      ↓
Fermentation of waste
      ↓
Release of toxic gases
      ↓
Methane emission + pollution
```


---


# Waste Management Situation in India


| Category       | Situation             |
| -------------- | --------------------- |
| Metro cities   | 90% waste collected   |
| Smaller cities | Only 30–50% collected |


Uncollected waste accumulates in:

- Streets
- Open spaces
- Vacant lands

This creates **serious public health hazards**.


---


# Waste as Resource (Important Concept)


Waste can be converted into:

- **Biogas**
- **Electricity**
- **Compost manure**

Concept used in:

- Waste-to-energy plants
- Compost plants

# 2. Rural–Urban Migration


## Meaning


Movement of population **from rural areas to urban areas**.


---


# Causes of Rural-Urban Migration


## Push Factors (Village Problems)

- Low employment
- Agricultural distress
- Poverty
- Small land holdings

## Pull Factors (Urban Attraction)

- Higher wages
- Job opportunities
- Better infrastructure
- Education and healthcare

---


### Migration Process Flow


```plain text
Rural poverty
     ↓
Lack of jobs
     ↓
Migration to cities
     ↓
Search for livelihood
     ↓
Urban population growth
```


---


# Urbanisation Trends


Important fact from the chapter:

- By **2050 → about 2/3 population will live in urban areas**.

---


# Urban Growth in India


Urban population increases due to:


1️⃣ Natural population growth


2️⃣ Rural-urban migration


3️⃣ Reclassification of rural settlements


Important statistic:

- After **1961**, about **60% urban growth** due to **natural increase**
- **29% due to rural-urban migration**

# 3. Problems of Slums


## What is a Slum?


**Slums are overcrowded residential areas with poor housing, poor sanitation and lack of basic amenities.**


Characteristics:

- Dilapidated houses
- Poor ventilation
- Narrow streets
- Lack of drinking water
- No sanitation facilities

---


# Why Slums Develop?


Main cause:


```plain text
Rural → Urban migration
        ↓
High land prices
        ↓
Poor migrants cannot afford housing
        ↓
Settlement in unused land
        ↓
Slums develop
```


---


# Features of Slums


| Feature        | Description                |
| -------------- | -------------------------- |
| Housing        | Temporary structures       |
| Infrastructure | Lack of water, electricity |
| Sanitation     | Very poor                  |
| Density        | Overcrowded                |
| Employment     | Informal sector            |


---


# Social Problems in Slums


Slum residents face:

- Malnutrition

- Poor education
- Drug abuse
- Crime
- Social exclusion

# 1. Land Degradation


## Meaning


**Land Degradation:**


Decline in the **productive capacity of land** due to natural or human factors.


This decline can be:

- **Temporary**
- **Permanent**

If degradation continues, land may turn into **wasteland**.


---


# Causes of Land Degradation


### Natural Causes

- Soil erosion
- Floods
- Landslides
- Desertification

### Human Causes

- Overgrazing
- Deforestation
- Mining
- Excessive irrigation
- Chemical fertilizers
- Urban expansion

---


# Major Processes of Land Degradation


| Process      | Explanation                 |
| ------------ | --------------------------- |
| Soil erosion | Removal of top fertile soil |
| Waterlogging | Excess water accumulation   |
| Salinisation | Salt accumulation in soil   |
| Alkalisation | Increase in soil alkalinity |


These processes reduce **soil fertility and agricultural productivity**.


---


# Land Degradation Process


```plain text
Deforestation / Overuse of land
            ↓
Loss of vegetation cover
            ↓
Soil erosion increases
            ↓
Loss of topsoil
            ↓
Decline in productivity
            ↓
Land degradation
```


---


# 2. Wasteland


## Definition


**Wasteland:**


Land that is **degraded and not suitable for cultivation** due to natural or human causes.


Important point:


👉 All degraded land may not be wasteland,


but **continued degradation can convert land into wasteland**.


---


# Classification of Wastelands in India


According to **National Remote Sensing Agency (NRSA)**.


### Categories


| Type                                  | % of Geographical Area |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Total wasteland                       | **17.98%**             |
| Barren & uncultivable land            | 2.18%                  |
| Natural degraded cultivable wasteland | 2.4%                   |
| Natural + human degraded land         | 7.51%                  |
| Man-made degraded land                | 5.88%                  |
| Total degraded cultivable wasteland   | **15.8%**              |


_(From Table 12.3)_


---


# Types of Wasteland


## Natural Wastelands


Created mainly due to natural factors.


Examples:

- Gullied land
- Ravines
- Desertic sands
- Coastal sands
- Barren rocky land
- Glacial areas
- Steep slopes

Example in India:

- **Chambal ravines**
- **Thar desert sand dunes**

---


## Natural + Human Induced Wastelands


Examples:

- Waterlogged areas
- Marshy lands
- Salinity affected land
- Alkaline soils

Example:

- **Punjab canal irrigation areas**
- **Indo-Gangetic plains salinity patches**

---


## Human-Induced Wastelands


Examples:

- Mining areas
- Industrial wasteland
- Degraded forests
- Degraded pasture lands
- Shifting cultivation areas

Important observation:


👉 **Human causes contribute more to wasteland formation than natural causes.**


---

# 3. Watershed Management


## Meaning


**Watershed Management:**


Integrated management of **land, water and vegetation resources** to improve livelihood and reduce land degradation.


Goal:

- Soil conservation
- Water conservation
- Sustainable agriculture

---


# Concept of Watershed


A **watershed** is an area where **all water drains into a single river or stream**.


Example:

- Ganga watershed
- Yamuna watershed

---


# Objectives of Watershed Management

1. Prevent soil erosion
2. Improve groundwater recharge
3. Increase agricultural productivity
4. Improve rural livelihoods
5. Restore ecological balance

---


# Watershed Management Strategy


```plain text
Soil Conservation
        +
Water Conservation
        +
Vegetation Restoration
        +
Community Participation
        ↓
Sustainable Land Use
```


---


# Case Study — Jhabua District (Madhya Pradesh)


Jhabua is one of the **most backward districts in India**.


Characteristics:

- Located in western MP
- High tribal population (Bhils)
- Severe land degradation
- Poverty

Watershed management programs were introduced by:

- Ministry of Rural Development
- Ministry of Agriculture

---


# Achievements in Jhabua


Key achievements:

- **20% of district area treated**
- Soil fertility improved
- Water availability increased
- Agricultural productivity improved

_(According to evaluation report mentioned in chapter)_


---


# Community Participation — Key Factor


Example: **Petlawad Block**


Villagers implemented:

- Tree plantation
- Pasture development
- Social fencing
- Controlled grazing
- Stall feeding of cattle

---


# Example of Community Initiative


Village: **Sat Rundi Hamlet**


Actions taken:

1. Each household planted **one tree**
2. Fodder grass planted
3. Pasture protection
4. Ban on open grazing

Result:

- Improved vegetation
- Soil conservation
- Increased fodder availability

---


# Innovative Conflict Resolution


Problem:


Encroachment on common land by villager.


Solution:


Villagers made the encroacher **member of user group** and shared benefits.


Lesson:


👉 **Community participation is key to resource management.**


---


# Concept: Common Property Resources (CPR)


Resources shared by community:


Examples:

- Grazing lands
- Forests
- Village ponds
- Pastures

Proper management of CPR is essential for **sustainable development**.
