
# 1. Meaning of Trade


### Trade

- **Trade** = voluntary **exchange of goods and services** between two parties.
- Two parties involved:
    - **Seller**
    - **Buyer**
- Trade is **mutually beneficial**.

### Levels of Trade


| Type                    | Meaning                                            |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **National Trade**      | Trade within a country                             |
| **International Trade** | Trade between countries across national boundaries |


**International Trade = Exchange of goods and services between different countries.**


---


# 2. Why Countries Engage in Trade


Countries trade because:


1️⃣ Some goods **cannot be produced domestically**


2️⃣ Some goods are **cheaper to import**


3️⃣ Countries specialise in production


### Core Principle


**Comparative Advantage**


```plain text
Country A produces wheat cheaply
Country B produces electronics cheaply

Trade occurs:
A exports wheat → B
B exports electronics → A
```


Result → **Both countries benefit**

# 3. Early Forms of Trade


## Barter System

- Direct exchange of goods without money.
- Example:
    - Pottery exchanged for plumbing services.

Example from India:


**Jon Beel Mela (Assam)**

- One of the **few places where barter still exists**.
- Tribes exchange goods after harvest.

### Problems of Barter

- Double coincidence of wants
- Lack of standard value
- Difficult to store wealth
- Difficult long-distance trade

# 4. History of International Trade


## 1️⃣ Ancient Trade


Trade mostly **local** due to:

- Poor transportation
- Security risks
- High costs

Luxury goods traded:

- Silk
- Jewelry
- Precious metals
- Spices

---


## 2️⃣ Silk Route


One of the **earliest long-distance trade routes**


Length → **~6000 km**


Connected:


```plain text
China → Central Asia → Persia → India → Rome
```


Goods traded:


| Region       | Goods           |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| China        | Silk            |
| Rome         | Wool            |
| India        | Spices          |
| Central Asia | Horses          |
| Persia       | Precious metals |


Map concept (important for exam):


```plain text
China
  ↓
Central Asia
  ↓
Persia
  ↓
India
  ↓
Rome
```


---


## 3️⃣ Medieval Trade Expansion


After the fall of the Roman Empire:

- Europe revived trade
- Development of **ocean-going ships**
- Trade expanded between **Europe and Asia**
- Discovery of **America**

---


## 4️⃣ Colonial Trade (15th Century onwards)


European powers:

- Portuguese
- Dutch
- Spanish
- British

New trade form emerged:


### Slave Trade

- Africans captured
- Sent to plantations in Americas
- Extremely profitable trade

Abolition:


| Country | Year |
| ------- | ---- |
| Denmark | 1792 |
| Britain | 1807 |
| USA     | 1808 |


---


## 5️⃣ Industrial Revolution Impact


Demand increased for:

- Raw materials
- Food
- Wool
- Grain

Trade pattern:


```plain text
Colonies → Raw materials → Europe
Europe → Finished goods → Colonies
```


Example:


India exported:

- Cotton
- Indigo
- Raw silk

Imported:

- British textiles

## 6️⃣ Modern Trade System

Example:

Different cultures produce unique goods.


Examples:

| ------------ | --------------- |

| Iran         | Carpets         |
| Indonesia    | Batik cloth     |

# 7. Balance of Trade


### Definition


Difference between **exports and imports**.


```plain text
Balance of Trade = Exports − Imports
```


---


### Types


| Type             | Meaning           |
| ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Positive balance | Exports > Imports |
| Negative balance | Imports > Exports |


Negative balance → **trade deficit**


Consequences:

- loss of foreign exchange
- economic instability

---


# 8. Types of International Trade


## Bilateral Trade


Trade between **two countries**.


Example:


India ↔ Russia oil trade


---


## Multilateral Trade


Trade with **many countries**.


Example:


India trading with:

- USA
- EU
- China
- ASEAN

---


# 9. Free Trade


Definition:


Removal of **trade barriers** like tariffs.


Benefits:

- cheaper goods
- larger markets
- higher competition

But problems:

- domestic industries may suffer
- developing countries disadvantaged

---


# Dumping


### Definition


Selling goods abroad **below production cost**.


Example:


Cheap Chinese steel exports.


Effect:

- Domestic industries collapse.

---

Origin:


```plain text
GATT (1948)
   ↓
```

Headquarters:


📍 **Geneva, Switzerland**


Functions:

- regulate global trade

- resolve disputes
- regulate trade in services

# 14. Gateways of International Trade


## Ports


Ports are **entry and exit points of trade**.


Functions:

- docking ships
- loading and unloading
- cargo storage
- maintenance of navigation channels

Importance:


Cargo handled by port = **indicator of economic development** of hinterland.


---


# 15. Types of Ports


## Based on Cargo


### Industrial Ports


Handle bulk cargo:

- grain
- sugar
- oil
- chemicals

Example:


Visakhapatnam


---


### Commercial Ports


Handle:

- manufactured goods
- passenger traffic

Example:


Mumbai Port


---


### Comprehensive Ports


Handle **both bulk and general cargo**


Most major ports belong here.


Example:


Rotterdam


---


# Based on Location


## Inland Ports


Located away from sea.


Connected by river or canal.


Examples:

- Kolkata (Hooghly river)
- Memphis (Mississippi river)
- Mannheim (Rhine river)

---


## Out Ports


Deep-water ports serving main port.


Example:


Athens → Piraeus


---


# Based on Function


### Oil Ports


Handle petroleum trade.


Examples:

- Maracaibo
- Tripoli
- Abadan

---


### Ports of Call


Ships stop for:

- refuelling
- food
- water

Examples:

- Singapore
- Aden
- Honolulu

---


### Packet Stations


Ferry ports for short routes.


Example:


```plain text
Dover (UK) ↔ Calais (France)
```


---


### Entrepot Ports


Goods imported and re-exported.


Examples:


| Region | Entrepot   |
| ------ | ---------- |
| Asia   | Singapore  |
| Europe | Rotterdam  |
| Baltic | Copenhagen |


---


### Naval Ports


Used for military purposes.


Examples:


India:

- Kochi
- Karwar
