# 1. ECONOMIC ACTIVITIES – CONCEPT


## Meaning


Economic activities are activities performed to **earn income or livelihood**.


### Classification of Economic Activities


| Sector         | Activity Type                   | Example                      |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Primary**    | Extraction of natural resources | Agriculture, Fishing, Mining |
| **Secondary**  | Processing of raw materials     | Manufacturing                |
| **Tertiary**   | **Services provided to people** | Transport, Trade, Banking    |
| **Quaternary** | **Knowledge-based services**    | Research, IT, Education      |
| **Quinary**    | **High-level decision making**  | CEOs, Policy makers          |


---


# 2. TERTIARY ACTIVITIES (SERVICE SECTOR)


## Definition


**Tertiary activities are activities that provide services rather than producing goods.**


Examples:

- Doctor
- Teacher
- Lawyer
- Driver
- Barber
- Electrician
- Shopkeeper

These activities involve **specialised skills and professional knowledge** rather than production of goods.


---


## Key Characteristics of Tertiary Activities

- Provide **services instead of tangible goods**
- Require **skilled labour and professional training**
- Output measured in **wages and salaries**
- Support **production and distribution of goods**

---


## Development Pattern of Economic Sectors


| Stage               | Dominant Sector               |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Early stage         | Primary sector                |
| Developing stage    | Secondary sector              |
| Developed economies | **Tertiary sector dominates** |


Example:

- **USA → 75% workforce in services**

---

# 3. TRADE AND COMMERCE


## Definition


**Trade is the buying and selling of goods produced elsewhere.**


Example:

- Selling agricultural produce
- Retail shops
- Wholesale markets

---


## Evolution of Trade


```plain text
Barter System

Money Exchange
     ↓
Regional Trade
     ↓
International Trade
```


This evolution created:

- **Trading centres**
- **Market networks**

# 4. TRADING CENTRES


Trading centres are places where goods are **collected, distributed and sold**.


## Classification


### 1. Rural Marketing Centres


Characteristics:

- Serve nearby villages
- Small-scale trade
- Limited services
- Known as **quasi-urban centres**

Examples:

- **Mandis**
- Weekly markets

Example from India:

- **Azadpur Mandi (Delhi)** – Asia’s largest vegetable market.

---


### Periodic Markets


Markets organised at **fixed intervals** such as:


| Type              | Frequency    |
| ----------------- | ------------ |
| Weekly markets    | Once a week  |
| Bi-weekly markets | Twice a week |


Features:

- Move from place to place
- Serve large rural areas
- Meet **temporary demand**

Example:

- **Haat markets in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar**

---


### 2. Urban Marketing Centres


Urban markets provide:

- **Specialised goods**
- **Professional services**
- **Manufactured products**

Examples of specialised markets:

- Labour markets
- Housing markets
- Financial markets

Services available:

- Teachers
- Doctors
- Lawyers
- Consultants

## TRANSPORT, COMMUNICATION, SERVICES & TOURISM


---


# 1. TRANSPORT SERVICES


## Definition


**Transport is a service that carries people, goods, and property from one place to another.**


It is an **organised industry** that satisfies the human need for **mobility and connectivity**.


---


## Importance of Transport


Cause–Effect Chain:


```plain text
Transport Development
        ↓
Better Connectivity
        ↓
Expansion of Trade
        ↓
Industrial Growth
        ↓
Economic Development
```


Transport **adds value to goods** because goods become more useful when they reach markets.


Example:

- Coal mined in **Jharkhand → transported to thermal plants in UP**

---


# Measurement of Transport Distance


Transport efficiency is measured using **three distances**.


| Distance Type       | Meaning                 |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Actual Distance** | Physical distance in km |
| **Time Distance**   | Time taken to travel    |
| **Cost Distance**   | Cost of transportation  |


Example:


Delhi → Lucknow

- Physical distance: ~550 km
- Time distance: 6–8 hours by expressway
- Cost distance: Depends on transport mode.

---


# Isochrone Concept


**Isochrone lines** join places that can be reached in **equal time from a point.**


Example:


```plain text
City Centre
   │
   ├─ 10 min travel zone
   ├─ 20 min travel zone
   └─ 30 min travel zone
```


Used in:

- Urban planning
- Transport network design.

---


# Transport Networks


Transport systems create **networks** consisting of:


| Term     | Meaning                     |
| -------- | --------------------------- |
| **Node** | Meeting point of routes     |
| **Link** | Road/route connecting nodes |


Example:


```plain text
Delhi (Node)
     │
     ├── Delhi–Agra Expressway (Link)
     │
Agra (Node)
```


Well-developed networks = **high connectivity**.


---


# Factors Affecting Transport


### 1 Population


Large population → higher demand for transport.


Example:

- Mumbai suburban railway.

---


### 2 Location of Settlements


Transport routes depend on:

- Cities
- Industrial centres
- Resource locations

Example:

- Railways connect **coal mines → industries**.

---


### 3 Physical Factors

- Mountains
- Rivers
- Climate

Example:

- Himalayas → difficult railway construction.

---


### 4 Economic Factors


Availability of **capital and technology**.


Example:

- **Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC)**.

## QUATERNARY, QUINARY ACTIVITIES & MODERN SERVICE ECONOMY


---


# 1. QUATERNARY ACTIVITIES


## Definition


**Quaternary activities are knowledge-based services involving information processing, research, and development.**


These activities involve:

- Collection of information
- Production of information
- Processing and dissemination of information

---


# Characteristics of Quaternary Activities


| Feature               | Explanation                            |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Knowledge based       | Requires high education                |
| Highly skilled labour | Scientists, IT professionals           |
| Information oriented  | Data processing and analysis           |
| Innovation driven     | Research and technological development |


---


# Examples of Quaternary Activities

- Research and Development (R&D)
- Information Technology (IT)
- Software development
- Education and training
- Financial consulting
- Data analysis

Example from India:

- **Bangalore → IT hub**
- **Hyderabad → IT and biotech hub**

---


# Importance of Quaternary Sector


Cause–Effect Chain:


```plain text
Knowledge Economy
        ↓
Technological Innovation
        ↓
Higher Productivity
        ↓
Economic Growth
```


Developed countries now rely heavily on the **knowledge sector**.


Example:

- Over **half of workers in developed countries** work in knowledge-based services.

---


# 2. QUINARY ACTIVITIES


## Definition


**Quinary activities involve top-level decision making and policy formulation.**


These services involve **high-level intellectual work**.


---


# Characteristics

- Highest level of decision making
- Highly paid professionals
- Strategic leadership roles

Often referred to as **“Gold Collar Professions.”**


---


# Examples of Quinary Activities


| Profession           | Example                       |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Senior executives    | CEOs of MNCs                  |
| Government officials | Policy makers                 |
| Research scientists  | Nobel-level researchers       |
| Financial advisors   | Global investment consultants |


---


# Quaternary vs Quinary Activities


| Feature  | Quaternary          | Quinary                  |
| -------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Nature   | Knowledge services  | Decision-making services |
| Skills   | Technical expertise | Strategic leadership     |
| Examples | IT, research        | CEOs, ministers          |


---


# 3. OUTSOURCING


## Definition


**Outsourcing is the practice of contracting work to external organisations to reduce costs and improve efficiency.**


---


# Types of Outsourcing


## Offshoring


When outsourcing work to **foreign countries**.


Example:

- US company outsourcing IT services to India.

---


# Reasons for Outsourcing


| Reason                 | Explanation          |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- |
| Cheap labour           | Lower wages          |
| Skilled workforce      | Educated workers     |
| Lower operational cost | Cheap infrastructure |


Example countries:

- India
- China
- Philippines
- Israel
- Eastern Europe

---


# Business Process Outsourcing (BPO)


BPO involves outsourcing **business operations**.


Examples:

- Call centres
- Customer support
- Payroll processing
- Data entry

Example in India:

- Gurgaon call centres.

---


# Knowledge Process Outsourcing (KPO)


KPO involves **highly skilled knowledge-based services**.


Examples:

- Research and development
- Intellectual property research
- Legal services
- Financial analytics

Difference:


| Feature     | BPO                | KPO                 |
| ----------- | ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Skill level | Moderate           | Highly skilled      |
| Work type   | Routine operations | Knowledge intensive |


---


# Outsourcing Flowchart


```plain text
Developed Countries
        ↓
High Labour Cost
        ↓
Outsource Work
        ↓
Developing Countries
        ↓
Job Creation
```
