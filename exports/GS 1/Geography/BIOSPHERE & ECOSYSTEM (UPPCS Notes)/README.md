
# 1. Biosphere


## Definition


**Biosphere** = The **zone of life on Earth** where living organisms interact with land, water and air.


### Components of Biosphere


The biosphere interacts with three environmental realms:


| Sphere          | Meaning                |
| --------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Lithosphere** | Land surface           |
| **Hydrosphere** | Water bodies           |
| **Atmosphere**  | Air / gaseous envelope |


➡ Living organisms survive due to interaction between these three spheres.


### Important Point (UPPCS Prelims)


Most organisms live in:

- **Upper lithosphere**
- **Lower atmosphere**
- **Hydrosphere**

---


## Example from India

- **Sundarbans ecosystem** → interaction of **land + water + organisms**
- **Himalayan ecosystem** → interaction of **climate + altitude + vegetation**

---


# 2. Ecology


## Definition


**Ecology** = Study of **relationships between organisms and their environment**.


### Word Origin


Ecology comes from Greek words:


| Word      | Meaning                 |
| --------- | ----------------------- |
| **Oikos** | Household / environment |
| **Logos** | Study                   |


➡ Ecology literally means **study of the household of nature**.


### Scientist


**Ernst Haeckel (1866)** first used the term **Ecology**.


---


# 3. Environment


Environment consists of two types of components:


| Type        | Meaning               | Examples                  |
| ----------- | --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Biotic**  | Living components     | plants, animals, microbes |
| **Abiotic** | Non-living components | temperature, soil, water  |


---


# 4. Ecosystem


## Definition


An **ecosystem** is a **system of interaction between biotic and abiotic components**.


✔ Includes:

- plants
- animals
- microbes
- soil
- climate
- water

➡ Together they form a **functional ecological unit**.

> An ecosystem is a system consisting of **biotic and abiotic components interacting with each other.**

---


# 5. Habitat


## Definition


**Habitat** = The **physical environment in which an organism lives**.


Example:


| Organism   | Habitat |
| ---------- | ------- |
| Camel      | Desert  |
| Polar bear | Arctic  |
| Fish       | Aquatic |
| Tiger      | Forest  |


---


# 6. Ecological Adaptation


### Definition


**Ecological Adaptation** = The ability of organisms to adjust to environmental conditions.


Examples:


| Region    | Adaptation           |
| --------- | -------------------- |
| Desert    | Cactus stores water  |
| Arctic    | Thick fur in animals |
| Mountains | Coniferous trees     |

# 8. Structure of Ecosystem


An ecosystem has **two main components**.


## 1️⃣ Abiotic Components


Non-living elements.


Examples:

- rainfall
- temperature
- sunlight
- humidity
- soil
- minerals

Important inorganic substances:


| Substance      | Role           |
| -------------- | -------------- |
| Carbon dioxide | photosynthesis |
| Nitrogen       | proteins       |
| Phosphorus     | ATP / energy   |
| Calcium        | bones          |
| Potassium      | plant growth   |


---


## 2️⃣ Biotic Components


Living organisms.


Three groups:


| Group           | Role                      |
| --------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Producers**   | Produce food              |
| **Consumers**   | Eat producers or animals  |
| **Decomposers** | Break down dead organisms |


---


# 9. Producers


### Definition


Organisms that **make their own food through photosynthesis**.


### Examples

- Green plants
- Algae
- Phytoplankton

---


# 10. Consumers


Organisms that depend on other organisms for food.


### Types


| Type                                    | Example     |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| **Primary consumers (Herbivores)**      | deer, goat  |
| **Secondary consumers (Carnivores)**    | snake       |
| **Tertiary consumers (Top carnivores)** | lion, tiger |


---


# 11. Decomposers


### Definition


Organisms that break down **dead organic matter**.


### Examples

- bacteria
- fungi

Also include **scavengers** like:

- vultures
- crows

➡ They recycle nutrients back to soil.


---

# 12. Food Chain


## Definition


A **Food Chain** is the **sequence of organisms through which energy flows when one organism eats another.**


📖 NCERT concept:


Energy is transferred **from one trophic level to another through feeding relationships.**


---


# Trophic Levels


Each step in the food chain is called a **Trophic Level**.


| Trophic Level | Organism Type       | Example       |
| ------------- | ------------------- | ------------- |
| 1             | Producers           | Plants        |
| 2             | Primary consumers   | Herbivores    |
| 3             | Secondary consumers | Carnivores    |
| 4             | Tertiary consumers  | Top predators |


---


# Example Food Chain (Agricultural Ecosystem)


```plain text
Sun → Paddy plant → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk
```


### India Example


```plain text
Grass → Deer → Tiger
```


### Aquatic Example


```plain text
Phytoplankton → Zooplankton → Small fish → Large fish
```

# Key Concept: Energy Transfer


Energy flows **only in one direction**.


```plain text
Sun
 ↓
Producers
 ↓
Primary Consumers
 ↓
Secondary Consumers
 ↓
Tertiary Consumers
```


⚠ Important:


Energy **decreases at every trophic level** because of:

- respiration
- metabolism
- heat loss

---


# Energy Loss Rule (UPPCS Concept)


Only **10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level**.


Example:


| Level      | Energy     |
| ---------- | ---------- |
| Producers  | 1000 units |
| Herbivores | 100 units  |
| Carnivores | 10 units   |


➡ Known as **Lindeman’s 10% Law**.

```plain text
Sun
 ↓
Plants
 ↓

 ↓
```


### India Example


```plain text
Grass → Deer → Tiger
```

Starts from **dead organic matter (detritus)**.

```plain text
Dead organic matter

Carnivores
```


### Example


```plain text
Dead leaves → Earthworms → Birds
```

# 15. Energy Flow in Ecosystem


## Main Source of Energy


The **Sun** is the **primary source of energy for all ecosystems.**


---


# Energy Flow Process


```plain text
Solar Energy
 ↓
Photosynthesis
 ↓
Producers
 ↓
Consumers
 ↓
Decomposers
```


---


# Important NCERT Concept


Only a **small fraction of solar energy (~0.1%)** is fixed during photosynthesis.


Most energy is:

- reflected
- absorbed by atmosphere
- lost as heat

---


# Photosynthesis Process


Plants convert:


```plain text
Carbon dioxide + Water + Sunlight
↓
Glucose + Oxygen
```


---


# Why Energy Flow is Unidirectional?


Energy cannot return to the sun.


Flow:


```plain text
Sun → Plants → Animals → Decomposers
```


Energy is **lost as heat at every level**.


---


# Important Prelims Facts


| Concept         | Fact                        |
| --------------- | --------------------------- |
| Food Chain      | Linear feeding relationship |
| Food Web        | Interconnected food chains  |
| Energy Source   | Sun                         |
| Energy Flow     | Unidirectional              |
| Energy Transfer | ~10%                        |

# 16. Biogeochemical Cycles


## Definition


**Biogeochemical Cycles** are the **cyclic movement of chemical elements between organisms and the environment.**


Chemical elements circulate **through plants, animals, air, water and soil** and return again through decomposition.


---


## Meaning of the Term


| Word         | Meaning                    |
| ------------ | -------------------------- |
| **Bio**      | Living organisms           |
| **Geo**      | Earth (soil, rocks, water) |
| **Chemical** | Nutrients / elements       |


➡ Hence, **movement of nutrients between living and non-living components.**


---


# Types of Biogeochemical Cycles


Two major types exist.


| Type                  | Main Reservoir     |
| --------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Gaseous cycle**     | Atmosphere / ocean |
| **Sedimentary cycle** | Soil / rocks       |


---


# Examples


| Cycle            | Type        |
| ---------------- | ----------- |
| Carbon cycle     | Gaseous     |
| Oxygen cycle     | Gaseous     |
| Nitrogen cycle   | Gaseous     |
| Phosphorus cycle | Sedimentary |


---


# 17. Carbon Cycle


## Importance


Carbon is the **basic building block of life**.


It is found in:

- atmosphere (CO₂)
- oceans
- living organisms
- fossil fuels

---


## Carbon Cycle Process


```plain text
Atmospheric CO₂
      ↓
Photosynthesis by plants
      ↓
Plants eaten by animals
      ↓
Respiration releases CO₂
      ↓
Decomposition releases CO₂
      ↓
Back to atmosphere
```

# 18. Oxygen Cycle


## Importance


Oxygen is essential for:

- respiration
- combustion
- life processes

---


## Oxygen Cycle Process


```plain text
Photosynthesis
      ↓
Oxygen released into atmosphere
      ↓
Animals use oxygen for respiration
      ↓
CO₂ produced
      ↓
Plants use CO₂ again
```


---


# Oxygen Reservoirs


| Reservoir  | Percentage       |
| ---------- | ---------------- |
| Atmosphere | ~21% oxygen      |
| Oceans     | dissolved oxygen |
| Rocks      | large amount     |

# 19. Nitrogen Cycle


## Importance


Nitrogen is necessary for:

- proteins
- DNA
- plant growth

But atmospheric nitrogen **cannot be used directly by plants**.


---


# Nitrogen Cycle Steps


### 1️⃣ Nitrogen Fixation


Conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into usable compounds.


Agents:

- lightning
- bacteria
- industrial fixation

---


### 2️⃣ Nitrification


Ammonia → Nitrites → Nitrates


---


### 3️⃣ Assimilation


Plants absorb nitrates.


Animals obtain nitrogen by eating plants.


---


### 4️⃣ Denitrification


Bacteria convert nitrates back to nitrogen gas.


---

# 20. Phosphorus Cycle


## Key Feature


Unlike other cycles:


⚠ **No atmospheric component**


Reservoir:

- rocks
- sediments

---


# Process


```plain text
Weathering of rocks
      ↓
Phosphate enters soil
      ↓
Plants absorb phosphate
      ↓
Animals eat plants
      ↓
Decomposition returns phosphorus to soil
```

# 21. Major Biomes of the World


## Definition


A **Biome** is a **large ecological region with similar climate, vegetation and animal life.**


Biome = **total assemblage of plant and animal species interacting within specific environmental conditions.**


---


# Factors Determining Biomes


Biomes depend mainly on:

- climate
- rainfall
- temperature
- soil

---


# Major Biomes of the World


| Biome     | Climate           | Example  |
| --------- | ----------------- | -------- |
| Forest    | high rainfall     | Amazon   |
| Grassland | moderate rainfall | Prairies |
| Desert    | very low rainfall | Sahara   |
| Tundra    | extremely cold    | Arctic   |
