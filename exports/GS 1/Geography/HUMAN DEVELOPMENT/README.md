
# Concept of Development & Human Development


## 1. Story of Rekha – Development Paradox


The chapter begins with the example of **Rekha**, a rural woman who migrated from **Uttarakhand to Delhi**.


### Key Observations


Urban development looked attractive initially but later revealed:

- **Slums & jhuggi clusters**
- **Traffic congestion**
- **Poverty & begging children**
- **Pollution**
- **Homeless people sleeping on footpaths**

### Conceptual Question Raised


✔ Can **development and underdevelopment coexist?**


✔ Does development benefit **some groups more than others?**


✔ Does development create **“haves” and “have-nots”?**


### Core Insight


Development often produces **economic growth for some but deprivation for others**.


---


# 2. Western / Euro-centric Concept of Development


### Development commonly associated with:


| Indicator                        | Meaning                   |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Industrialisation                | Growth of industries      |
| Computerisation                  | Technological advancement |
| Modern transport & communication | Connectivity              |
| Large education systems          | Knowledge economy         |
| Advanced medical facilities      | Better health             |
| Safety and security              | Stable society            |
| Leisure & comfort                | High living standards     |


These are considered **symbols of development**.


### But this view is **limited**


It ignores:

- Social inequality
- Environmental degradation
- Marginalisation
- Regional disparities

Thus called the **Western or Euro-centric view of development**.


---


# 3. Problems of Conventional Development Model


Development has created **three declining capabilities among the poor**:


| Capability                   | Cause                                | Impact                    |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| **Social capability**        | Displacement & weakening social ties | Loss of social capital    |
| **Environmental capability** | Pollution & ecological degradation   | Reduced natural resources |
| **Personal capability**      | Diseases, accidents                  | Lower quality of life     |


### Result


➡ Decline in **Human Development**


➡ Rise of **social injustice and ecological crisis**


---


# 4. Emergence of Human Development Concept


To overcome limitations of the traditional development model.


### Major Milestone


**1990 – First Human Development Report**


Released by:


**UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)**


### Features

- Defines human development
- Develops indicators
- Ranks countries using **Human Development Index (HDI)**
- Published annually

---


# 5. Definition of Human Development


### UNDP Definition


Human Development is:

> **A process of enlarging people's choices and improving their opportunities for education, health and income.**

### Core Components


Human development ensures:


1️⃣ **Long and healthy life**


2️⃣ **Education / knowledge**


3️⃣ **Access to resources for decent living**


Additional aspects:

- Political freedom
- Human rights
- Self-respect
- Clean environment

---


# 6. Core Principle of Human Development


### Key idea

> **Development should be centred around people, not people around development.**

---


# Human Development Flowchart


```plain text
Economic Growth
       ↓
Access to Education + Healthcare + Resources
       ↓
Expanded Choices
       ↓
Better Quality of Life
       ↓
Human Development
```

# 3. Limitations of HDI


Some scholars criticize HDI because it ignores:


### Historical factors

- Colonialism
- Imperialism
- Neo-imperialism

### Socio-cultural factors

- Gender discrimination
- Caste inequality
- Religious conflicts

### Political factors

- Democracy vs dictatorship
- Level of empowerment

These are **very important in developing countries like India**.
