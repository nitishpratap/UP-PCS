- Birth rate

- Literacy
- Occupation
- Urbanization
- Migration patterns

# 5. Population Growth


## Definition


**Population Growth**


→ Change in the number of people in a region during a specific period of time.


Population change may be:

- **Positive**
- **Negative**

It can be expressed in:

- **Absolute numbers**
- **Percentage (growth rate)**

---


## Basic Concepts (Important for Prelims)


### 1. Growth of Population


[


Population\ Growth = Population_{later} - Population_{earlier}


]


Example (India)


| Year | Population   |
| ---- | ------------ |
| 1991 | 84.63 crore  |
| 2001 | 102.70 crore |


Growth:


**18.07 crore**


---


### 2. Growth Rate of Population


Population growth expressed in **percentage**.


---


### 3. Natural Growth of Population


Population increase due to **births and deaths only**.


[


Natural\ Growth = Births - Deaths


]


---


### 4. Actual Growth of Population


[


Actual\ Growth = Births - Deaths + Immigration - Emigration


]


---


### 5. Positive Growth


Occurs when:

- **Birth rate > Death rate**

OR

- Immigration increases population

---


### 6. Negative Growth


Occurs when:

- **Death rate > Birth rate**

OR

- Large scale emigration

Example:

- Japan
- Germany
- Russia

---


# 6. Components of Population Change


Population change depends on **three components**:


| Component | Meaning                  |
| --------- | ------------------------ |
| Births    | Add population           |
| Deaths    | Reduce population        |
| Migration | Redistributes population |


---


# 7. Birth Rate


## Definition


**Crude Birth Rate (CBR)**


Number of live births per **1000 people per year**.


Formula:


[


CBR = \frac{Births}{Mid\ year\ population} \times 1000


]


---


# 8. Death Rate


## Definition


**Crude Death Rate (CDR)**


Number of deaths per **1000 people per year**.


Formula:


[


CDR = \frac{Deaths}{Mid\ year\ population} \times 1000


]


Factors affecting mortality:

- Economic development
- Healthcare
- Nutrition
- Sanitation

---

## Important Terms

## Immigration


People **entering a region**.

People **leaving a region**.

# Push and Pull Factors


## Push Factors


Conditions that **force people to leave a place**.


Examples:

- Unemployment
- Poverty
- Political instability
- Natural disasters
- Epidemics

---


## Pull Factors


Conditions that **attract people to a place**.


Examples:

- Job opportunities
- Better living standards
- Security
- Good climate

---


### Migration Flowchart


# 15. Demographic Transition Theory


One of the **most important theories for exams**.


It explains how population changes as a country develops.


---


## Definition


Population changes from:


**High Birth & High Death → Low Birth & Low Death**


as society becomes **industrialized and urbanized**.


---


## Demographic Transition Model


### Stage 1 – High Stationary


Characteristics:

- High birth rate
- High death rate
- Low population growth

Reasons:

- Disease
- Poor healthcare
- Food shortages

Example:

- Tribal societies

---


### Stage 2 – Early Expanding


Characteristics:

- High birth rate
- Rapidly falling death rate
- Rapid population growth

Reasons:

- Better healthcare
- Improved sanitation
- Food availability

Examples:

- Bangladesh
- Kenya

---


### Stage 3 – Late Expanding


Characteristics:

- Low birth rate
- Low death rate
- Stable population

Reasons:

- Education
- Family planning
- Urbanization

Examples:

- USA
- Japan
- Canada

---

# 17. Malthus Population Theory


Proposed by **Thomas Malthus (1798)**.


---


## Main Idea


Population grows faster than food supply.


| Population       | Food              |
| ---------------- | ----------------- |
| Geometric growth | Arithmetic growth |


---


### Consequences


Population crash due to:

- Famine
- Disease
- War

---


### Solutions


Two types of checks:


**Preventive checks**

- Family planning
- Late marriage

**Positive checks**

- Famine
- Epidemics
- War

# UPPCS Mains Ready 150-Word Answer


### Question


Explain demographic transition theory.


### Answer (Structured)


Demographic Transition Theory explains the change in population growth patterns as a society develops economically. Initially, both birth rate and death rate are high, leading to slow population growth. In the second stage, death rates decline due to improvements in healthcare, sanitation and food supply, while birth rates remain high, causing rapid population growth. In the third stage, both birth and death rates decline because of urbanisation, education and family planning, resulting in stable population growth. Developed countries such as USA and Japan are in the final stage, whereas many developing countries are still in the second stage.
