# 1. LOCATION OF INDIA


## 1.1 Latitudinal and Longitudinal Extent


| Parameter               | Extent                  |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Latitudinal extent**  | **8°4′ N to 37°6′ N**   |
| **Longitudinal extent** | **68°7′ E to 97°25′ E** |
| **Total area**          | **3.28 million sq km**  |
| **World rank (area)**   | **7th largest country** |


India occupies **~2.4% of the world’s land area**.


---


## 1.2 Key Latitudes and Longitudes


### Tropic of Cancer

- Latitude: **23°30′ N**
- **Divides India into two parts**

| Region                    | Climatic Zone                    |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| North of Tropic of Cancer | **Subtropical / Temperate zone** |
| South of Tropic of Cancer | **Tropical zone**                |


### Standard Meridian of India

- **82°30′ E longitude**
- Passes near **Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh)**

This is used for **Indian Standard Time (IST)**.


---


## 1.3 India’s Territorial Extent


India extends from


| Direction | Location              |
| --------- | --------------------- |
| North     | **Kashmir / Ladakh**  |
| South     | **Kanyakumari**       |
| East      | **Arunachal Pradesh** |
| West      | **Gujarat**           |


The **southernmost point of India’s mainland** → **Kanyakumari**.


The **southernmost point of India (including islands)** → **Indira Point (Great Nicobar Island)**.


---


# 2. DISTANCE AND SPATIAL EXTENT


### North–South distance


≈ **3214 km**


### East–West distance


≈ **2933 km**


Even though the **latitudinal and longitudinal difference is ~30°**, the **north–south distance is larger**.


### Reason


Distance between **longitudes decreases towards the poles**, while distance between **latitudes remains constant**.


---


### Distance Between Latitudes


Value


---


**1° latitude ≈ 111 km**


---


Important for **map calculations in Prelims**.


---


# 3. IMPLICATIONS OF LATITUDINAL LOCATION


India’s latitude affects:

- Climate
- Vegetation
- Agriculture
- Soil
- Biodiversity

### Explanation


Because India lies in **both tropical and subtropical zones**, it has **great natural diversity**.


Example:


| Region          | Climate           |
| --------------- | ----------------- |
| Kerala          | Tropical monsoon  |
| Rajasthan       | Desert climate    |
| Ladakh          | Cold desert       |
| Northeast India | Humid subtropical |


---


## Example for UPPCS


### Uttar Pradesh


UP lies in **subtropical zone** (north of Tropic of Cancer).


Climate features:

- **Hot summers**
- **Cool winters**
- **Monsoon rainfall**

Major rivers:

- Ganga
- Yamuna
- Ghaghara
- Gomti

---


# 4. LONGITUDINAL EXTENT AND TIME


India has a **longitudinal extent of about 30°**.


This causes a **time difference of nearly 2 hours** between eastern and western India.


Example:


| Location          | Sunrise Time |
| ----------------- | ------------ |
| Arunachal Pradesh | Earlier      |
| Gujarat           | Later        |


However India follows **single time zone (IST)**.


---


## Indian Standard Time (IST)


| Parameter           | Value                   |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Standard Meridian   | **82°30′ E**            |
| Reference city      | **Near Mirzapur (UP)**  |
| Difference from GMT | **+5 hours 30 minutes** |


---


### Why Single Time Zone?


India follows one time zone because:

- Administrative convenience
- National integration
- Avoid confusion in transportation

But some experts suggest **two time zones** for India.


---

# 2. PENINSULAR LOCATION OF INDIA


### Meaning


A **peninsula** is land surrounded by water on **three sides**.


### India’s Peninsula


The **Deccan Plateau** forms the main peninsular part.


Advantages:

- Access to **international trade routes**
- Contact with **Africa, Europe, and Southeast Asia**
- Development of **maritime trade**

This location helped India historically.

# 5. ROLE OF MONSOON WINDS IN TRADE


Ancient sailors used **monsoon winds** for navigation.


### Mechanism


```plain text
Summer Monsoon
India → Arabia / Africa trade

Winter Monsoon
Arabia / Africa → India return journey
```


Thus monsoon winds helped create **Indian Ocean trade network**.
