
## ATMOSPHERE, PRESSURE & WIND SYSTEM (REVISION NOTES)

---


# 1. ATMOSPHERIC CIRCULATION


### 🔹 Definition


**Atmospheric circulation** = Large-scale air movement redistributing heat from equator → poles


### 🔹 Flow Mechanism


```plain text
Unequal heating → Temperature difference → Pressure difference → Air movement → Wind
```


### 🔹 Importance

- Heat balance of Earth
- Moisture distribution
- Cloud formation & precipitation

---


# 2. ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE


### 🔹 Definition


Pressure = **Weight of air column above unit area**


### 🔹 Units


| Unit | Use                    |
| ---- | ---------------------- |
| mb   | Traditional            |
| Pa   | SI                     |
| hPa  | Standard (Meteorology) |


### 🔹 Standard Pressure


```plain text
1013.25 mb / hPa
```


### 🔹 Measurement


| Instrument        | Type      |
| ----------------- | --------- |
| Mercury Barometer | Classical |
| Aneroid Barometer | Modern    |


---


# 3. VARIATION OF PRESSURE


### 🔹 With Altitude (Prelims)

- Pressure ↓ with height
- Approx: **1 mb / 10 m**

Reason → ↓ Air density


---


### 🔹 Vertical Distribution (Mains Concept)

- Rapid decrease near surface
- Non-uniform rate

⚠️ Important:


```plain text
Vertical pressure gradient > Horizontal
BUT balanced by gravity → No strong vertical winds
```


---


# 4. HORIZONTAL PRESSURE DISTRIBUTION


### 🔹 Isobars


**Isobars** = Lines joining equal pressure


📌 Interpretation:

- Close → Strong wind
- Far → Weak wind

---


# 5. PRESSURE SYSTEMS


| Type          | Feature     |
| ------------- | ----------- |
| Low Pressure  | Rising air  |
| High Pressure | Sinking air |


👉 Wind: High → Low


---


# 6. GLOBAL PRESSURE BELTS 🌍


| Latitude | Belt             | Feature        |
| -------- | ---------------- | -------------- |
| 0°       | Equatorial Low   | Rising air     |
| 30°      | Subtropical High | Descending     |
| 60°      | Subpolar Low     | Convergence    |
| 90°      | Polar High       | Cold dense air |


---


## 🔹 Key Belts


### 1. Equatorial Low (Doldrums)

- 5°N–5°S
- Rising air, heavy rainfall
- Calm winds

📍 Amazon, Congo, Indonesia


---


### 2. Subtropical High (Horse Latitudes)

- ~30°
- Dry descending air

📍 Deserts:

- Sahara
- Arabian
- Thar (👉 Important for UPPCS)

---


### 3. Subpolar Low

- ~60°
- Warm + cold air interaction
- Cyclonic storms

---


### 4. Polar High

- ~90°
- Cold dense sinking air

---


# 7. SEASONAL SHIFT OF PRESSURE BELTS


| Season      | Shift |
| ----------- | ----- |
| Summer (NH) | North |
| Winter (NH) | South |


👉 Causes **Monsoon**


---


# 8. JANUARY vs JULY PRESSURE


### ❄️ January (Winter)

- Land → High pressure (cold)
- Ocean → Low pressure

➡ Winds: Land → Ocean


---


### ☀️ July (Summer)

- Land → Low pressure (hot)
- Ocean → High pressure

➡ Winds: Ocean → Land → **Monsoon Rain**


---


# 9. INDIA / UP CONNECTION


### 🌧️ Monsoon Mechanism


```plain text
Low pressure (North India)
        +
High pressure (Indian Ocean)
        ↓
Moist winds → Rainfall
```


| Region     | Rain Source   |
| ---------- | ------------- |
| Eastern UP | Bay of Bengal |
| Western UP | Arabian Sea   |


---


# 🌬️ FORCES AFFECTING WIND


---


# 10. WIND


### 🔹 Definition


Wind = Horizontal movement of air (High → Low pressure)


---


## 🔹 3 Major Forces


| Force             | Role           |
| ----------------- | -------------- |
| Pressure Gradient | Initiates wind |
| Coriolis          | Deflects       |
| Friction          | Slows          |


---


# 11. PRESSURE GRADIENT FORCE (PGF)

- Rate of pressure change
- Wind ⟂ isobars

📌 Strong gradient → Strong wind


---


# 12. FRICTIONAL FORCE


| Surface | Effect        |
| ------- | ------------- |
| Land    | High friction |
| Ocean   | Low           |

- Acts up to 1–3 km
- Reduces speed & alters direction

---


# 13. CORIOLIS FORCE


### 🔹 Definition


Due to Earth’s rotation


### 🔹 Deflection


| Hemisphere | Direction |
| ---------- | --------- |
| NH         | Right     |
| SH         | Left      |


### 🔹 Variation

- 0 at equator
- Max at poles

---


### ❗ Cyclones not at Equator


```plain text
Coriolis = 0 → No rotation → No cyclone
```


---


# 14. GEOSTROPHIC WIND


Condition:


```plain text
PGF = Coriolis
```


👉 Wind flows **parallel to isobars**


👉 Occurs in upper atmosphere


---


# 15. CYCLONE vs ANTICYCLONE


| Feature      | Cyclone       | Anticyclone |
| ------------ | ------------- | ----------- |
| Pressure     | Low           | High        |
| Movement     | Converging    | Diverging   |
| NH Direction | Anticlockwise | Clockwise   |
| Weather      | Rainy         | Clear       |


---


# 16. CONVERGENCE vs DIVERGENCE


| Type        | Result              |
| ----------- | ------------------- |
| Convergence | Rising air → Rain   |
| Divergence  | Sinking air → Clear |


---

# ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE


## 1. Atmosphere and Air Movement


### Key Concept

- **Atmospheric circulation** refers to the **large-scale movement of air** which redistributes heat on Earth.

### Cause of Air Movement


Flowchart:


```plain text
Unequal heating of Earth
        ↓
Temperature differences
        ↓
Pressure differences
        ↓
Air moves from High → Low pressure
        ↓
Wind
```


### Importance

- Maintains **temperature balance on Earth**
- Distributes **moisture and heat**
- Responsible for **cloud formation and precipitation**

---


# 2. Atmospheric Pressure


### Definition


**Atmospheric Pressure**


= **Weight of the column of air above a unit area from Earth's surface to the top of the atmosphere**


---


### Units of Pressure


| Unit              | Value              |
| ----------------- | ------------------ |
| Millibar (mb)     | Traditional unit   |
| Pascal (Pa)       | SI Unit            |
| Hectopascal (hPa) | Most commonly used |


### Standard Sea Level Pressure


```plain text
1013.25 mb
or
1013.25 hPa
```


---


### Measurement of Pressure


| Instrument        | Type                       |
| ----------------- | -------------------------- |
| Mercury Barometer | Traditional                |
| Aneroid Barometer | Modern portable instrument |


---


### Variation with Height


Important fact for prelims.

- Pressure **decreases with altitude**

Approx rule:


```plain text
1 mb decreases for every 10 m increase in altitude
```


Example:


| Location      | Pressure |
| ------------- | -------- |
| Sea Level     | Highest  |
| Hill Stations | Lower    |
| Mount Everest | Very Low |


Reason:

- Air density decreases with altitude.

---


# 3. Vertical Variation of Pressure


Important for conceptual clarity.


Key points:

- In the **lower atmosphere**, pressure decreases rapidly.
- Rate of decrease is **not uniform**.

Important exam statement:


```plain text
Vertical pressure gradient is stronger than horizontal gradient
but it is balanced by gravity.
```


Hence:


→ We **do not experience strong vertical winds**


---

# 5. Pressure Systems


Two main types.


| Pressure System | Characteristics            |
| --------------- | -------------------------- |
| Low Pressure    | Pressure lowest at centre  |
| High Pressure   | Pressure highest at centre |


### Wind movement


```plain text
High pressure → Low pressure
```


---


# 6. Global Distribution of Pressure


The **world pressure distribution** forms **latitudinal pressure belts**.


These belts shift seasonally.


### Major Pressure Belts


| Latitude | Pressure Belt    | Characteristics  |
| -------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| 0°       | Equatorial Low   | Rising air       |
| 30°      | Subtropical High | Descending air   |
| 60°      | Subpolar Low     | Converging winds |
| 90°      | Polar High       | Cold dense air   |


---


# 7. Equatorial Low Pressure Belt


Also called:


```plain text
Doldrums
```


Location:


```plain text
5°N – 5°S
```


Features:

- Intense heating
- Air rises vertically
- Calm winds
- Heavy rainfall
- Convectional clouds

Reason:

- Maximum solar heating at equator.

Example regions:

- Amazon Basin
- Congo Basin
- Indonesia

---


# 8. Subtropical High Pressure Belt


Location:


```plain text
Around 30°N and 30°S
```


Also called:


```plain text
Horse Latitudes
```


Features:

- Descending dry air
- Clear skies
- Calm winds

Associated deserts:


| Desert            | Location       |
| ----------------- | -------------- |
| Sahara            | Africa         |
| Arabian Desert    | Middle East    |
| Thar Desert       | India–Pakistan |
| Australian Desert | Australia      |


Important for UPPCS:


**Thar Desert exists because of this high-pressure belt**


---


# 9. Subpolar Low Pressure Belt


Location:


```plain text
Around 60° N and S
```


Cause:

- Meeting of **warm westerlies**
- and **cold polar winds**

Features:

- Cyclonic activity
- Frequent storms

Important areas:

- North Atlantic
- North Pacific

---


# 10. Polar High Pressure Belt


Location:


```plain text
Around 90° latitude
```


Cause:

- Extremely cold temperatures
- Air becomes dense and sinks

Characteristics:

- High pressure
- Outward blowing winds

---


# 11. Seasonal Shift of Pressure Belts


Pressure belts **do not remain fixed**.


They move with **apparent movement of the Sun**.


| Season      | Movement    |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Summer (NH) | Shift north |
| Winter (NH) | Shift south |


Important impact:


This shift causes:


```plain text
Monsoon circulation
```


Example:

- Indian Monsoon
- West African Monsoon

# 13. January vs July Pressure Distribution


**January (Northern Hemisphere Winter)** ❄️

- Land cools **faster than oceans**.
- **Asia becomes very cold → air becomes dense → High Pressure over Siberia.**
- Oceans remain **comparatively warmer → Low Pressure over oceans.**

➡ Winds blow **from land (high pressure) to ocean (low pressure)**.


---


**July (Northern Hemisphere Summer)** ☀️

- Land heats **faster than oceans**.
- **Asia becomes very hot → air rises → Low Pressure over Asia.**
- Oceans remain **relatively cooler → High Pressure over oceans.**

➡ Winds blow **from ocean (high pressure) to land (low pressure)** bringing **moisture → Monsoon rains** 🌧️.

# FORCES AFFECTING WIND


(Pressure Gradient, Coriolis Force, Friction, Geostrophic Wind)


---


# 1. Wind – Basic Concept


### Definition


**Wind** = **Horizontal movement of air from high pressure to low pressure.**


Flow process:


```plain text
Unequal heating of Earth
        ↓
Pressure differences
        ↓
Air movement
        ↓
Wind
```


Important point:

- Wind direction and speed are controlled by **three major forces**.

### Three Forces Controlling Wind


| Force                   | Function                |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Pressure Gradient Force | Starts the wind         |
| Coriolis Force          | Deflects wind direction |
| Frictional Force        | Slows down wind         |


Also gravity acts vertically.


---


# 2. Pressure Gradient Force (PGF)


### Definition


**Pressure Gradient Force**


= **Rate of change of pressure with distance**


Formula concept:


```plain text
Pressure difference / Distance
```


### Characteristics

- Wind flows **perpendicular to isobars**
- Wind flows **from high pressure → low pressure**

### Important rule


| Isobar spacing | Wind speed   |
| -------------- | ------------ |
| Isobars close  | Strong winds |
| Isobars far    | Weak winds   |


Cause–effect chain:


```plain text
Large pressure difference
        ↓
Steep pressure gradient
        ↓
Strong winds
```


---


# 3. Frictional Force


### Definition


Force produced due to **Earth's surface roughness**.


### Characteristics


| Surface | Friction |
| ------- | -------- |
| Land    | High     |
| Ocean   | Low      |


Important points:

- Maximum near surface
- Influence extends up to **1–3 km altitude**

Effect:


```plain text
Friction
   ↓
Reduces wind speed
   ↓
Changes wind direction slightly
```


Example:


Wind over **forest / mountains** slows down more than wind over **oceans**.


---


# 4. Coriolis Force


### Definition


Force caused by **Earth's rotation**, which deflects winds.


Named after:


**Gaspard-Gustave Coriolis (1844)**


### Direction of Deflection


| Hemisphere          | Direction |
| ------------------- | --------- |
| Northern Hemisphere | Right     |
| Southern Hemisphere | Left      |


Example:


Wind moving northward in NH → deflects east.


---


### Variation of Coriolis Force


Depends on **latitude**.


| Latitude      | Coriolis effect |
| ------------- | --------------- |
| Equator       | Zero            |
| Mid-latitudes | Moderate        |
| Poles         | Maximum         |


Important concept:


```plain text
Coriolis Force ∝ Latitude
```


Meaning:

- Higher latitude → stronger deflection.

---


# Why Cyclones Do Not Form Near Equator


Key concept for exams.


Reason:


```plain text
Coriolis force ≈ 0 at Equator
        ↓
No rotation of air
        ↓
Cyclone formation impossible
```


Thus tropical cyclones usually form **beyond 5° latitude**.


---


# 5. Combined Effect of Forces


Wi nd movement is controlled by **combined action** of:


```plain text
Pressure Gradient Force
        +
Coriolis Force
        +
Frictional Force
```


Flowchart:


```plain text
Pressure difference
        ↓
Pressure gradient force
        ↓
Wind begins to move
        ↓
Coriolis force deflects it
        ↓
Friction reduces speed
```


---


# 6. Geostrophic Wind


### Definition


**Geostrophic wind** = wind that flows **parallel to isobars**.


Occurs when:


```plain text
Pressure gradient force = Coriolis force
```


Condition:

- Occurs in **upper atmosphere**
- **No friction**

### Diagram you can draw


```plain text
Isobars

1010  ─────────────

1012  ─────────────

1014  ─────────────

Wind →
parallel to isobars
```


Thus wind does not move directly from high to low pressure.


---


# 7. Pressure and Wind Relationship


Important conceptual rule.


| Condition                | Result       |
| ------------------------ | ------------ |
| Strong pressure gradient | Strong winds |
| Weak pressure gradient   | Weak winds   |


Example:

- Hurricanes
- Mid-latitude cyclones

| Dry winds  | Very little moisture |
| Weak winds | Less solar heating   |


---


# 6. Three-Cell Model of Atmospheric Circulation


Earth’s circulation is explained by **three circulation cells**.


| Cell        | Latitude | Description            |
| ----------- | -------- | ---------------------- |
| Hadley Cell | 0°–30°   | Trade wind circulation |
| Ferrel Cell | 30°–60°  | Westerlies             |
| Polar Cell  | 60°–90°  | Polar easterlies       |


---


### Hadley Cell


Process:


```plain text
Equator heating
       ↓
Air rises
       ↓
Moves toward 30°
       ↓
Cools and sinks
       ↓
Returns as trade winds
```


---


### Ferrel Cell


Occurs between:


```plain text
30° – 60° latitude
```


Characteristics:

- Indirect circulation
- Westerlies dominate

---


### Polar Cell


Occurs near poles.


Process:


```plain text
Cold dense air sinks
       ↓
Moves toward 60°
       ↓
Rises again
```


---


# 7. Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)


### Definition


Region where **trade winds from both hemispheres converge**.


Location:


```plain text
Near Equator
```


---


### Characteristics


| Feature           | Explanation   |
| ----------------- | ------------- |
| Low pressure zone | Rising air    |
| Heavy rainfall    | Convection    |
| Thunderstorms     | Strong uplift |


---


### Seasonal Movement


ITCZ moves with the sun.


| Season | Position  |
| ------ | --------- |
| Summer | Northward |
| Winter | Southward |


---


### Importance for India


During summer:


```plain text
ITCZ shifts north
        ↓
Monsoon trough forms
        ↓
Heavy rainfall in India
```


This is crucial for **Indian monsoon formation**.

# 1. Air Mass


### Definition


An **Air Mass** is a **large body of air having uniform temperature and humidity characteristics** over a large area.


Key features:


| Feature            | Explanation                      |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Large size         | Covers thousands of km           |
| Uniform properties | Temperature and moisture same    |
| Moves as unit      | Travels across continents/oceans |


---


### Source Region


Air masses originate in areas where:

- Temperature is uniform
- Winds are weak
- Surface conditions are stable

These regions are called **source regions**.


Examples:


| Region        | Air Mass Type       |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| Sahara Desert | Hot dry air mass    |
| Arctic Ocean  | Cold moist air mass |
| Antarctica    | Very cold air mass  |


---


# 2. Classification of Air Masses


Air masses are classified based on **moisture and temperature**.


---


### Based on Moisture


| Type            | Meaning   |
| --------------- | --------- |
| Continental (c) | Dry air   |
| Maritime (m)    | Moist air |


---


### Based on Temperature


| Type         | Meaning |
| ------------ | ------- |
| Tropical (T) | Warm    |
| Polar (P)    | Cold    |


---


### Major Air Mass Types


| Air Mass | Characteristics                  |
| -------- | -------------------------------- |
| mT       | Maritime Tropical – warm & moist |
| cT       | Continental Tropical – hot & dry |
| mP       | Maritime Polar – cold & moist    |
| cP       | Continental Polar – cold & dry   |


---


### Example for India


Winter winds over North India often bring **continental polar air masses from Central Asia**.


This causes:

- Cold waves in **Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh**

---


# 3. Fronts


### Definition


A **front** is the **boundary between wo different air masses**.


Important concept:


```plain text
Air mass A + Air mass B
      ↓
Boundary forms
      ↓
Front
```


Fronts are associated with:

- Clouds
- Rainfall
- Storms

---


# 4. Types of Fronts


There are **four main types**.


| Front Type       | Description                     |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Cold Front       | Cold air pushes warm air        |
| Warm Front       | Warm air moves over cold air    |
| Stationary Front | No movement                     |
| Occluded Front   | Cold front overtakes warm front |


---


# 5. Cold Front


### Definition


Occurs when **cold air mass advances and lifts warm air upward**.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature        | Explanation    |
| -------------- | -------------- |
| Steep slope    | Rapid uplift   |
| Heavy rainfall | Short duration |
| Thunderstorms  | Common         |


Process:


```plain text
Cold air moves forward
        ↓
Warm air rises quickly
        ↓
Cumulonimbus clouds
        ↓
Heavy rain / storms
```


---


# 6. Warm Front


### Definition


Occurs when **warm air moves slowly over cold air**.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature                | Explanation          |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- |
| Gentle slope           | Slow uplift          |
| Long duration rainfall | Light but widespread |
| Layer clouds           | Stratus clouds       |


Process:


```plain text
Warm air advances
        ↓
Gradually rises over cold air
        ↓
Cloud formation
        ↓
Light continuous rain
```


---


# 7. Occluded Front


### Definition


Occurs when **cold front catches up with warm front**.


Result:


```plain text
Cold air pushes warm air upward
        ↓
Warm air completely lifted
        ↓
Complex weather conditions
```


---


# 8. Stationary Front


Definition:


When **two air masses meet but neither advances**.


Result:

- Cloudy weather
- Light rain

---


# 9. Cyclones


### Definition


A **cyclone** is a **low pressure system where winds spiral inward toward the center**.


---


### Key Characteristics


| Feature             | Explanation     |
| ------------------- | --------------- |
| Low pressure centre | Air converges   |
| Rising air          | Cloud formation |
| Heavy rainfall      | Storms          |


---


### Wind Direction


| Hemisphere          | Direction     |
| ------------------- | ------------- |
| Northern Hemisphere | Anticlockwise |
| Southern Hemisphere | Clockwise     |


---


# 10. Types of Cyclones


Two major types.


| Type                  | Location          |
| --------------------- | ----------------- |
| Tropical Cyclones     | Tropical oceans   |
| Mid-latitude Cyclones | Temperate regions |


---


# 11. Tropical Cyclones


### Formation Region


Between:


```plain text
5° – 20° latitude
```


Because Coriolis force is required.


---


### Conditions Required


| Condition        | Explanation            |
| ---------------- | ---------------------- |
| Warm ocean water | Temperature above 27°C |
| High humidity    | Moist air              |
| Low wind shear   | Stable atmosphere      |
| Coriolis force   | Rotation               |


---


### Structure


Parts:


| Part      | Description     |
| --------- | --------------- |
| Eye       | Calm center     |
| Eyewall   | Strongest winds |
| Rainbands | Heavy rainfall  |


---


### Names of Cyclones


| Region       | Name      |
| ------------ | --------- |
| Atlantic     | Hurricane |
| Pacific      | Typhoon   |
| Indian Ocean | Cyclone   |


---

Major cyclone regions:

- Bay of Bengal
- Arabian Sea

Example storms:

- Cyclone Fani
- Cyclone Amphan

Also called:


```plain text
Temperate cyclones
```

Location:


```plain text
30° – 60° latitude
```


---


### Formation


Occurs due to **interaction of warm and cold air masses**.


Features:


| Feature                | Explanation        |

# 13. Anticyclones


### Definition


A **high pressure system where air moves outward from center**.


---


### Characteristics


| Feature           | Explanation     |
| ----------------- | --------------- |
| Descending air    | Clear weather   |
| Dry conditions    | Little rainfall |
| Stable atmosphere | Calm winds      |


---


### Wind Direction


| Hemisphere          | Direction     |
| ------------------- | ------------- |
| Northern Hemisphere | Clockwise     |
| Southern Hemisphere | Anticlockwise |


---


# 14. Thunderstorms


### Definition


A **violent local storm with thunder, lightning and heavy rain**.


---


### Formation Conditions


| Condition               | Explanation       |
| ----------------------- | ----------------- |
| High temperature        | Strong convection |
| Moist air               | Cloud formation   |
| Atmospheric instability | Rising air        |


---


### Process


```plain text
Surface heating
        ↓
Air rises rapidly
        ↓
Cumulonimbus clouds
        ↓
Thunder + lightning + rain
```


---

Local storms:

| Kerala      | Blossom showers |
