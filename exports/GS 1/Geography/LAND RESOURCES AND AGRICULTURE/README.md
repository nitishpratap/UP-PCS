
# LAND USE IN INDIA


---


# 1️⃣ Concept of Land Resource


### Land as a Resource


Land is used for:

- **Agriculture**
- **Settlements**
- **Industries**
- **Transport infrastructure**
- **Recreation**
- **Forests & grazing**

👉 Example:


| Land Type      | Example              |
| -------------- | -------------------- |
| Agricultural   | Crop fields          |
| Residential    | Houses, towns        |
| Infrastructure | Roads, canals        |
| Pastures       | Grazing land         |
| Forest         | Timber, biodiversity |


---


# 2️⃣ Land-Use Categories in India


Land use records are maintained by:


👉 **Land Revenue Department**


Important concept:


| Term                  | Meaning                                   |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Geographical Area** | Measured by Survey of India               |
| **Reporting Area**    | Area for which land-use data is available |


⚠️ **Reporting area may change but geographical area remains fixed.**


---


# 3️⃣ Major Land-Use Categories


### (1) Forest Land

- Land **officially classified as forest**
- Not always equal to **actual forest cover**

📌 Important Concept:


```plain text
Forest area (official)
        ≠
Actual forest cover
```


Example:


Government may declare land as forest even if trees are absent.


---


### (2) Land Put to Non-Agricultural Uses


Includes:

- Settlements
- Roads
- Railways
- Industries
- Canals
- Infrastructure

📈 Increasing due to:

- Urbanization
- Industrialization
- Infrastructure development

Example:

- Delhi NCR expansion
- Industrial corridors
- Smart cities

---


### (3) Barren and Wasteland


Land unsuitable for cultivation:


Examples:

- Desert land
- Rocky areas
- Ravines
- Steep hills

Example in India:

- **Chambal ravines**
- **Thar Desert**

---


### (4) Permanent Pastures and Grazing Land


Used for livestock grazing.


Ownership:

- Mostly **Panchayat land**
- Part of **Common Property Resources (CPR)**

Example:

- Village grazing lands in Rajasthan

---


### (5) Miscellaneous Tree Crops and Groves


Includes:

- Orchards
- Fruit trees
- Coconut groves
- Plantation trees

Example:

- Mango orchards (UP)
- Coconut groves (Kerala)

---


### (6) Culturable Waste Land


Land **not cultivated for >5 years** but **can be reclaimed**.


Examples:

- Saline land
- Marshy land

Possible improvement:

- Soil reclamation
- Irrigation

---


### (7) Current Fallow Land


Land left **uncultivated for 1 year or less**.


Purpose:

- Restore soil fertility

Example:

- Farmer leaves land empty for a season.

---


### (8) Fallow Other Than Current Fallow


Land left uncultivated for:


**1–5 years**


After **>5 years → Culturable wasteland**


---


### (9) Net Area Sown


Land actually used for cropping.


Definition:


➡️ **Physical area on which crops are grown.**


---


# 4️⃣ Land Use Structure (Important for Prelims)


| Category             | Meaning                    |
| -------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Forest               | Government forest area     |
| Non-agricultural use | Settlements, industries    |
| Barren land          | Unproductive land          |

| Culturable waste     | Unused but cultivable land |
| Current fallow       | 1 year uncultivated        |
| Other fallow         | 1–5 years uncultivated     |

# 5️⃣ Changes in Land Use in India


India experienced major economic transformation after 1960.


### Three Major Drivers


### (1) Growth of Economy


Population growth + income growth → increased land demand


```plain text
Population ↑
      ↓
Demand for land ↑
      ↓
Marginal land used
```


---


### (2) Structural Change in Economy


Economic sectors:


| Sector    | Trend        |
| --------- | ------------ |
| Primary   | Slow growth  |
| Secondary | Rapid growth |
| Tertiary  | Rapid growth |


Impact:


➡ Agricultural land → Non-agricultural uses


Example:

- Urban expansion around cities

---


### (3) Continued Pressure on Agricultural Land


Even though agriculture share in GDP declines:


Reasons:


1️⃣ Population dependent on agriculture still high


2️⃣ Food demand increasing


---

# 7️⃣ Common Property Resources (CPR)


Definition:

> **Natural resources owned by community for collective use.**

Examples:

- Community forests
- Grazing lands
- Village ponds
- Public water bodies

---


### Importance of CPR


1️⃣ Livelihood for poor farmers


2️⃣ Fodder for livestock


3️⃣ Fuel wood collection


4️⃣ Minor forest products


Example:

- Women collecting firewood in villages.

---


### CPR and Rural Economy


Very important for:

- **Landless labourers**
- **Marginal farmers**
- **Women**

---


# 8️⃣ Importance of Land in Agriculture


Land is crucial because:


### 1️⃣ Agriculture is Land Based


Unlike:

- Industry
- Services

Land directly determines production.


---


### 2️⃣ Quality of Land Determines Productivity


Example:

- Alluvial soil → high productivity
- Desert soil → low productivity

---


### 3️⃣ Social Value of Land


Land provides:

- Social status
- Credit security
- Economic stability

Example:


Landowners get loans easily.


---


# 9️⃣ Total Cultivable Land


Total cultivable land =


```plain text
Net area sown
+ Current fallow
+ Other fallow
+ Culturable wasteland
```


Observation:


➡ Cultivable land is **slightly declining** in India.


Reason:

- Urban expansion
- Infrastructure development

---


# 🔟 Need for Land-Saving Technologies


India has **limited land resources**.


Solution:


### Two Approaches


### 1️⃣ Increase Yield Per Hectare


Example:

- HYV seeds
- Fertilizers

### 2️⃣ Increase Cropping Intensity


Definition:


Cropping intensity =


```plain text
(Gross Cropped Area / Net Sown Area) × 100
```


Higher cropping intensity → more production.


---


### Example


| Country | Strategy                    |
| ------- | --------------------------- |
| India   | Increase cropping intensity |
| USA     | Expand farm size            |


---


### Why High Cropping Intensity is Good for India?


Because:

- India = **Land scarce**
- India = **Labour abundant**

Thus:


```plain text
More crops
→ More employment
→ Higher production
```

# 1️⃣ Cropping Seasons in India


India has **three major cropping seasons**.


| Season     | Months           | Major Crops                       |
| ---------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Kharif** | June – September | Rice, Cotton, Jowar, Bajra, Maize |
| **Rabi**   | October – March  | Wheat, Gram, Mustard, Barley      |
| **Zaid**   | April – June     | Vegetables, Fruits, Fodder        |


---


## Kharif Crops


Characteristics:

- Sown with **Southwest Monsoon**
- Require **high temperature and rainfall**

Major Crops:

- Rice
- Cotton
- Jowar
- Bajra
- Maize
- Tur (Arhar)

Example regions:

- Eastern India
- Coastal India
- Uttar Pradesh plains

---


## Rabi Crops


Characteristics:

- Grown in **winter season**
- Require **cool climate**
- Mostly **irrigated**

Major Crops:

- Wheat
- Gram
- Mustard
- Barley

Major region:


👉 **Indo-Gangetic Plain**


Example states:

- Uttar Pradesh
- Punjab
- Haryana

---


## Zaid Crops


Short duration crops grown **between rabi and kharif seasons**.


Examples:

- Watermelon
- Cucumber
- Vegetables
- Fodder crops

Important condition:


➡ **Irrigation is necessary**


---


### Important Concept (UPPCS)


Southern India does not strictly follow these seasons.


Reason:

- High temperature throughout year
- Crops grown **any time if moisture available**

---


# 2️⃣ Types of Farming


Farming is classified based on **source of moisture**.


### Two Major Types


| Type              | Source of Water       |
| ----------------- | --------------------- |
| Irrigated Farming | Artificial irrigation |
| Rainfed Farming   | Rainfall dependent    |


---


# Irrigated Farming


Two types exist based on objective.


### 1️⃣ Protective Irrigation


Purpose:


➡ Protect crops from **moisture deficiency**


Characteristics:

- Less water per hectare
- Large area coverage

Example:

- Canal irrigation in dry regions

---


### 2️⃣ Productive Irrigation


Purpose:


➡ **Maximize crop productivity**


Characteristics:

- More water per hectare
- Higher yield

Example:

- Intensive rice cultivation

---


# Rainfed Farming


Rainfed farming is further divided into:


| Type            | Rainfall           |
| --------------- | ------------------ |
| Dryland farming | < 75 cm            |
| Wetland farming | > Crop requirement |


---


### Dryland Farming


Regions:

- Rajasthan
- Deccan Plateau
- Central India

Crops:

- Bajra
- Ragi
- Gram
- Moong
- Guar

Characteristics:

- Drought resistant crops
- Soil moisture conservation

---


### Wetland Farming


Regions:

- High rainfall areas
- Eastern India

Problems:

- Floods
- Soil erosion

Crops:

- Rice
- Sugarcane
- Jute

---

# 7️⃣ Problems of Indian Agriculture


These are **very important for UPPCS Mains**.


---


## 1️⃣ Dependence on Monsoon


Only:


👉 **~33% cultivated area irrigated**


Thus agriculture heavily depends on rainfall.


Problems:

- Drought
- Flood

Example:

- Rajasthan drought
- Bihar floods

---


## 2️⃣ Low Productivity


Reasons:

- Traditional farming
- Small landholdings
- Poor irrigation

Example:


India yields lower than:

- USA
- Japan
- Russia

---


## 3️⃣ Financial Constraints


Modern agriculture needs expensive inputs:

- Fertilizers
- Seeds
- Machinery

Result:


👉 Farmers fall into **debt trap**.


---


## 4️⃣ Lack of Land Reforms


Historical exploitation under:


| System    | Region        |
| --------- | ------------- |
| Zamindari | North India   |
| Ryotwari  | South India   |
| Mahalwari | Central India |


Land reforms after independence were **poorly implemented**.


---


## 5️⃣ Small and Fragmented Landholdings


Statistics:

- **60% farmers < 1 ha**
- **40% farmers < 0.5 ha**

Problems:

- Mechanisation difficult
- Low productivity

---


## 6️⃣ Lack of Commercialisation


Many farmers produce crops only for **self consumption**.


Thus:

- Low market participation
- Low income

---


## 7️⃣ Underemployment in Agriculture


Rural workers get work only:


👉 **4–8 months per year**


Remaining time → disguised unemployment.
