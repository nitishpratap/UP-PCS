## 1. Nature of Ocean Water

- **Ocean water is dynamic (constantly moving).**
- Movement is influenced by:
    - **Temperature**
    - **Salinity**
    - **Density**
    - **External forces**

### External Forces

1. **Sun**
2. **Moon**
3. **Winds**
4. **Gravitational forces**

These forces cause different **types of ocean movements**.


---


# 2. Types of Ocean Water Movements


| Type of Motion        | Description                          | Ex.                                         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Horizontal Motion** | Movement along the surface of ocean  | 1. **Ocean Currents**
2. **Waves**          |
| **Vertical Motion**   | Upward or downward movement of water | 1. **Tides**
2. **Upwelling & Downwelling** |


---


# 3. Ocean Currents

- **Continuous flow of huge masses of water in a definite direction.**
- Acts like **rivers within oceans**.

Example:

- **Gulf Stream (Atlantic Ocean)**
- **Kuroshio Current (Pacific Ocean)**

India relevance:

- **Monsoon currents in the Indian Ocean**

---


# 4. Waves (Important Topic)


## Definition


**Waves are the movement of energy across the ocean surface, not the movement of water itself.**


✔ Water particles move **in circular motion**.


---


## How Waves Form


### Primary Cause


➡ **Wind**


Wind transfers **energy → water → waves**


### Other Causes

- Earthquakes → **Tsunami**
- Volcanic eruptions
- Underwater landslides

---


## Wave Movement Mechanism


```plain text
Wind energy
      ↓
Energy transferred to water
      ↓
Water particles move circularly
      ↓
Wave travels forward
```


Important concept:


**Water does not travel long distance — only energy travels.**


---


# 5. Motion of Water Particles in Waves

>  **Crest is the highest point (peak) of a transverse wave, representing maximum positive displacement, while a trough is the lowest point, representing maximum negative displacement**. Together, they represent one full cycle of a wave:

### Key Concept

- Water particles move in **small circular paths**.
- As the wave passes:
    - Water moves **up and forward**
    - Then **down and backward**

Result:


➡ Net water movement ≈ **almost zero**

# 6. Behavior of Waves Near Coast


When waves approach the shore:


1️⃣ **Wave slows down**


Reason:

- Friction between **water & sea floor**

2️⃣ **Wave height increases**


3️⃣ **Wave breaks**


Condition for wave breaking:


```plain text
Depth of water < 1/2 wavelength
```


Result → **Surf**

### 1️⃣ Wind Strength


Stronger wind → Larger waves


### 2️⃣ Duration of Wind


Longer wind duration → Larger waves


### 3️⃣ Fetch


**Fetch = Distance over which wind blows across water**


Example:

- **Southern Ocean has huge waves due to large fetch**

# 1. Tides – Definition


**Tides = Periodic rise and fall of sea level caused mainly by the gravitational attraction of the Moon and Sun.**


✔ Occurs **once or twice every day**


✔ Major cause → **Moon's gravitational force**


---


# 2. Difference Between Waves, Currents and Tides


| Feature  | Waves               | Currents        | Tides                 |
| -------- | ------------------- | --------------- | --------------------- |
| Movement | Surface oscillation | Horizontal flow | Vertical rise & fall  |
| Cause    | Wind                | Density, wind   | Gravity of Moon & Sun |
| Pattern  | Irregular           | Continuous      | Periodic              |


---


# 3. Forces Responsible for Tides


Two main forces generate tides.


### 1️⃣ Gravitational Force

- Attraction of **Moon and Sun**
- **Moon's effect is stronger** because it is closer to Earth.

### 2️⃣ Centrifugal Force

- Produced by the **Earth–Moon rotational system**

These two forces together create **tidal bulges**.

# 5. Why Do We Get Two High Tides Daily?


Because Earth rotates through **two tidal bulges**.


Result:

- **Two High tides**
- **Two Low tides**

in approximately **24 hours 50 minutes**.


---


# 6. Tidal Cycle


### Average time interval


| Event                      | Time                 |
| -------------------------- | -------------------- |
| High tide → next high tide | ~12 hours 25 minutes |
| High tide → low tide       | ~6 hours 12 minutes  |


Important exam fact:

- **Moon takes 24 hr 50 min to return to same position**

# 7. Factors Affecting Tide Height


### 1️⃣ Shape of Coastline

- Funnel-shaped bays increase tides.

Example:

- **Bay of Fundy (Canada)** → Highest tides in world

### 2️⃣ Ocean Depth

# 6. Causes of Ocean Currents


Ocean currents form due to several factors.


### 1️⃣ Planetary Winds


Major driver of currents.


Example:

- Trade winds
- Westerlies

---


### 2️⃣ Earth's Rotation (Coriolis Force)


Deflects currents:


| Hemisphere          | Deflection |
| ------------------- | ---------- |
| Northern Hemisphere | Right      |
| Southern Hemisphere | Left       |


---


### 3️⃣ Temperature Differences


Warm water:

- Less dense
- Moves toward poles

Cold water:

- More dense
- Moves toward equator

---


### 4️⃣ Salinity Differences


Higher salinity → denser water → sinks


Example:

- North Atlantic deep water circulation

---


### 5️⃣ Shape of Continents


Continents **block and redirect ocean currents**.


Example:

- Gulf Stream along North American coast

---


# 7. Ocean Gyres


### Definition


Large circular systems of ocean currents.


Five major gyres:


| Ocean          | Gyre          |
| -------------- | ------------- |
| North Atlantic | Clockwise     |
| South Atlantic | Anticlockwise |
| North Pacific  | Clockwise     |
| South Pacific  | Anticlockwise |
| Indian Ocean   | Anticlockwise |


---


# 8. Effects of Ocean Currents


## 1. Climate Regulation


Warm currents warm nearby regions.


Example:


➡ **Gulf Stream warms Western Europe**


---


## 2. Formation of Deserts


Cold currents reduce rainfall.


Examples:


| Cold Current     | Desert         |
| ---------------- | -------------- |
| Peru Current     | Atacama Desert |
| Benguela Current | Namib Desert   |


---


## 3. Fishing Grounds


Where warm and cold currents meet → **nutrient-rich waters**


Examples:


| Region               | Currents               |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Japan coast          | Kuroshio + Oyashio     |
| Grand Banks (Canada) | Gulf Stream + Labrador |


---


## 4. Navigation


Ships use currents to **reduce travel time**.


Example:

- Atlantic shipping routes use **Gulf Stream**

# 10. Upwelling (Important Concept)


### Definition


**Upwelling is the upward movement of cold, nutrient-rich water from deep ocean to surface.**


Causes:

# 11. Downwelling


Opposite of upwelling.


**Surface water sinks downward.**


Occurs when:

- Surface water becomes **dense**
- Water converges

- **Salinity (haline)**

🌊 **Global Conveyor Belt**


It connects all oceans.
