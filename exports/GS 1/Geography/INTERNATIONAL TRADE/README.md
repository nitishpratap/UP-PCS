
# 1. Meaning of International Trade


### Definition


**International Trade** = Exchange of goods and services **between two or more countries**.


✔ Also called **Foreign Trade / External Trade**


### Why International Trade Occurs


No country is **self-sufficient** in all resources.


Countries trade due to:


| Reason                | Explanation                                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Resource Distribution | Different countries have different resources |
| Climate Differences   | Agriculture and products vary                |
| Technology Level      | Developed countries export technology        |
| Cost advantage        | Cheaper production in some countries         |
| Specialisation        | Countries specialise in certain goods        |


---


# 2. Growth of India's International Trade


India’s external trade has **increased dramatically after independence**.


| Year    | Trade Value        |
| ------- | ------------------ |
| 1950-51 | ₹12,140 million    |
| 2004-05 | ₹8,371,330 million |


Reasons for Growth


1️⃣ Growth of **manufacturing sector**


2️⃣ **Liberal economic policies**


3️⃣ **Diversification of markets**


4️⃣ **Globalisation**


➡ Despite growth, India's share in world trade ≈ **1%**


(Important Prelims fact)


---


# 3. Trade Balance and Trade Deficit


### Trade Balance


Difference between **Exports – Imports**


### Trade Deficit


When **Imports > Exports**


India generally has **trade deficit**.


### Main Reason


**Crude petroleum imports**


---


### Example (NCERT data)


| Year    | Export    | Import    | Trade Deficit |
| ------- | --------- | --------- | ------------- |
| 1994-95 | 826,740   | 899,710   | -72,970       |
| 2000-01 | 2,035,710 | 2,308,730 | -273,020      |
| 2004-05 | 3,560,690 | 4,810,640 | -1,249,950    |


Trend


```plain text
Imports > Exports → Trade Deficit
```

# 4. Changing Composition of India's Exports


Major change:


India shifted from **primary goods → manufactured goods**


### Export Composition (% share)


| Commodity            | 1997-98 | 2003-04 |
| -------------------- | ------- | ------- |
| Agriculture products | 18.93%  | 11.8%   |
| Ore & minerals       | 3.03%   | 3.71%   |
| Manufactured goods   | 75.83%  | 75.96%  |
| Petroleum products   | 1.01%   | 5.59%   |
| Others               | 1.2%    | 2.94%   |


Source: Table page 2 of chapter


---


### Key Observations


### 1️⃣ Decline in Agricultural Exports


Examples

- Coffee
- Tea
- Spices
- Pulses

Reason

- International competition
- Domestic demand increase

---


### 2️⃣ Growth of Manufactured Exports


Manufactured goods = **~76% of exports**


Major items

- Engineering goods
- Chemicals
- Gems & jewellery
- Textiles

---


### 3️⃣ Increase in Petroleum Products


Reasons

- Growth in **refining capacity**
- Higher **global petroleum prices**

# 5. Changing Composition of Imports


### Early Period (1950s–1960s)


India imported mainly

- Food grains
- Machinery
- Capital goods

Reason

- Food shortage
- Low industrial capacity

---


### After Green Revolution (1970s)


Foodgrain imports reduced.


But imports increased for

- Petroleum
- Fertilisers
- Industrial goods

---


### Current Import Composition (NCERT)


| Commodity                | Share   |
| ------------------------ | ------- |
| Petroleum crude          | Highest |
| Capital goods            | High    |
| Pearls & precious stones | High    |

| Chemical products        | Medium  |


Source: Table page 3

# 8. Sea Ports: Gateways of International Trade


India has **long coastline (~7516 km)**.


Therefore seaports are very important.


### Total Ports in India


| Type                 | Number |
| -------------------- | ------ |
| Major Ports          | 12     |
| Minor / Intermediate | 185    |


Major ports handle **~75% of maritime trade**.


Source: page 5


---


# 9. Why More Ports on West Coast?


Reasons


| West Coast                   | East Coast            |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Indents and natural harbours | Deltaic coast         |
| Less sediment deposition     | Heavy silt deposition |
| Deep waters                  | Shallow waters        |


Example

- Mumbai (natural harbour)
