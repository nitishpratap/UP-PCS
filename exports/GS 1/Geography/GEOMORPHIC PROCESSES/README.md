
## 1. Earth’s Surface: A Dynamic System


### Key Idea

- The **Earth’s crust is dynamic** and continuously changing.
- Changes occur due to **two types of forces**:
    - **Internal forces (Endogenic forces)**
    - **External forces (Exogenic forces)**

### Interaction of Forces


```plain text
Internal Forces (Endogenic)
        ↓
Land Building Processes
(Uplift, Mountains)

External Forces (Exogenic)
        ↓
Land Wearing Processes
(Erosion, Weathering)

Result → Continuous modification of Earth’s surface
```


---


# 2. Endogenic vs Exogenic Forces


| Feature       | Endogenic Forces                          | Exogenic Forces                 |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Origin        | Interior of Earth                         | Atmosphere & surface            |
| Energy source | Internal heat                             | Solar energy                    |
| Main effect   | Land building                             | Land wearing                    |
| Examples      | Volcanism, earthquakes, mountain building | Weathering, erosion, deposition |


### Key Concept

- Earth’s surface evolves due to **continuous interaction between endogenic and exogenic forces**.

---


# 3. Degradation and Aggradation


### 1️⃣ Degradation

- **Wearing down of relief features** through erosion.

### 2️⃣ Aggradation

- **Deposition of sediments** filling depressions.

### Process Chain


```plain text
Weathering → Erosion → Transportation → Deposition
```


Example (India):


| Process     | Example                             |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Erosion     | Himalayan river valleys             |
| Deposition  | Indo-Gangetic Plains                |
| Aggradation | Delta formation (Ganga-Brahmaputra) |


---


# 4. Geomorphic Processes


### Definition


**Geomorphic processes** are the **physical and chemical changes that modify Earth’s surface**.


They include:

- **Weathering**
- **Mass movement**
- **Erosion**
- **Transportation**
- **Deposition**

---


# 5. Geomorphic Agents


### Definition


A **geomorphic agent** is a **mobile medium that erodes, transports, and deposits material**.


### Major Agents


| Agent         | Landforms Produced   |
| ------------- | -------------------- |
| Running water | Valleys, floodplains |
| Wind          | Sand dunes           |
| Glaciers      | U-shaped valleys     |
| Waves         | Sea cliffs           |
| Groundwater   | Karst topography     |


---


# 6. Role of Gravity


### Importance


Gravity is the **driving force behind all geomorphic processes**.


### Effects of Gravity

- Causes **downward movement of materials**
- Controls **mass movements**
- Creates **slope processes**

### Gravity Driven Movements


```plain text
High Elevation
      ↓
Gravity
      ↓
Material movement
      ↓
Lower elevation deposition
```


Example:

- Landslides in **Himalayas**
- Rockfalls in **Uttarakhand**

---

### 1️⃣ Internal Energy


Derived from Earth’s interior.

Result:

# 8. Endogenic Geomorphic Processes


### Definition


Processes originating **inside the Earth** that **build relief features**.


### Types of Endogenic Processes

1. **Orogeny**
2. **Epeirogeny**
3. **Earthquakes**
4. **Plate tectonics**
5. **Volcanism**

---


# 9. Orogeny (Mountain Building)


### Definition


**Orogeny** refers to **mountain-building processes due to intense folding and faulting**.


### Characteristics

- Occurs in **narrow belts**
- Associated with **plate convergence**

### Example

- **Himalayan Mountains**
- **Alps**
- **Andes**

### Process


```plain text
Plate Convergence
      ↓
Compression
      ↓
Folding & Faulting
      ↓
Mountain formation
```


---


# 10. Epeirogeny (Continental Building)


### Definition


**Epeirogeny** refers to **broad uplift or subsidence of large continental areas**.


### Characteristics

- Slow vertical movements
- Affect large regions

### Examples


| Region         | Type       |
| -------------- | ---------- |
| Deccan Plateau | Uplift     |
| Hudson Bay     | Subsidence |


---


# 11. Earthquakes

### Causes

# 18. Weathering


## Definition


**Weathering** is the **in-situ disintegration and decomposition of rocks at or near Earth’s surface without transportation of materials**.


### Key Point

- Weathering **breaks rocks into smaller particles**
- It **prepares material for erosion**

### Process Chain


```plain text
Weathering
      ↓
Erosion
      ↓
Transportation
      ↓
Deposition
```


---


# Characteristics of Weathering

- Occurs **in situ** (rock remains at the same place).
- Involves **mechanical, chemical, and biological processes**.
- Controlled by **climate, rock type, vegetation, and topography**.

---


# Types of Weathering


Weathering is classified into **three main types**:


| Type                  | Nature                                 | Dominant Regions        |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Mechanical (Physical) | Rock breakdown without chemical change | Cold and dry climates   |
| Chemical              | Chemical decomposition of minerals     | Humid tropical climates |
| Biological            | Caused by organisms                    | All climates            |


---


# 1. Mechanical Weathering (Physical Weathering)


### Definition


Breaking of rocks into **smaller fragments without altering their chemical composition**.


### Major Processes


| Process              | Explanation                                          |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Frost action         | Water freezes in rock cracks and expands             |
| Thermal expansion    | Rocks expand and contract due to temperature changes |
| Exfoliation          | Outer layers peel off due to heating                 |
| Salt crystallisation | Salt crystals expand and break rocks                 |


---


## Important Processes


### 1️⃣ Frost Weathering (Freeze–Thaw)


Process:


```plain text
Water enters cracks
       ↓
Freezes → expands (9%)
       ↓
Cracks widen
       ↓
Rock breaks
```


### Regions

- Polar regions
- High mountains

Example:

- **Himalayas**
- **Ladakh**

---


### 2️⃣ Exfoliation


Outer rock layers **peel off like onion layers**.


Cause:

- **High temperature differences**

Example:

- Granite domes

Indian Example:

- **Aravalli hills**

---


### 3️⃣ Salt Weathering


Occurs in **arid and coastal areas**.


Process:


```plain text
Salt solution enters rock pores
        ↓
Water evaporates
        ↓
Salt crystals expand
        ↓
Rock disintegration
```


Example:

- **Thar Desert**

---


# 2. Chemical Weathering


## Definition


Chemical reactions **change the mineral composition of rocks**.


### Major Reactions


| Reaction    | Explanation                  |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- |
| Solution    | Minerals dissolve in water   |
| Oxidation   | Reaction with oxygen         |
| Carbonation | Reaction with carbonic acid  |
| Hydration   | Water combines with minerals |
| Hydrolysis  | Chemical breakdown by water  |


---


## Important Chemical Weathering Processes


### 1️⃣ Carbonation


Most important in **limestone regions**.


Process


```plain text
CO₂ + H₂O → Carbonic acid
       ↓
Carbonic acid dissolves limestone
       ↓
Karst landforms
```


### Landforms

- Caves
- Sinkholes
- Stalactites
- Stalagmites

Example:

- **Meghalaya caves (India)**

---


### 2️⃣ Oxidation


Occurs mainly in **iron-bearing rocks**.


Example:


```plain text
Iron + Oxygen → Rust
```


Effect:

- Weakens rocks

Example Region:

- **Laterite soils in tropical regions**

---


### 3️⃣ Hydration


Minerals **absorb water and expand**.


Example:


```plain text
Anhydrite → Gypsum
```


Result:

- Rock disintegration

---

# 20. Mass Movement (Mass Wasting)


## Definition


**Downslope movement of rock and soil under gravity without a transporting agent.**


### Key Point


Gravity is the **main driving force**.


---


# Types of Mass Movement


| Type           | Speed      | Example      |
| -------------- | ---------- | ------------ |
| Slow movement  | Soil creep | Hill slopes  |
| Rapid movement | Landslides | Himalayas    |
| Very rapid     | Rockfall   | Steep cliffs |


---


# 1. Soil Creep


### Definition


Slow movement of soil downhill.


### Causes

- Freeze-thaw cycles
- Water saturation

Example:

- **Himalayan slopes**

Rapid downward movement of rock and soil.


### Causes

| -------------- | ----------------- |

Example:

- **Uttarakhand landslides**
- **Himachal Pradesh**

---


# 3. Rockfall

Sudden fall of rock fragments from cliffs.


Common in:

- Mountain regions
- Steep slopes

Example:

- **Western Himalayas**
