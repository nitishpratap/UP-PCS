# 1. Climate Classification


## Definition


**Climate classification** = Scientific grouping of different climatic regions of the world based on common climatic characteristics.


### Purpose

- To simplify understanding of global climate patterns
- To compare climatic regions
- To relate **climate with vegetation and human life**

---


# 2. Types of Climate Classification


| Type                         | Basis             | Key Idea                      |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Empirical classification** | Observed data     | Temperature and precipitation |
| **Genetic classification**   | Causes of climate | Air masses, pressure belts    |
| **Applied classification**   | Specific purpose  | Agriculture, water resources  |


### Example

- **Köppen Classification → Empirical**
- **Thornthwaite Classification → Water balance**
- **Air mass classification → Genetic**

---


# 3. Köppen Climate Classification


Developed by


**Wladimir Köppen (1900)**


### Basic Principle


Climate is closely related to **natural vegetation**.


Therefore Köppen used:

- **Temperature**
- **Precipitation**
- **Vegetation distribution**

to classify climates.


✔ Still **most widely used classification system**.


---


# 4. Important Features of Köppen System


### 1. Uses letters to represent climate types


| Symbol              | Meaning                     |
| ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Capital letters     | Major climate groups        |
| Small letters       | Seasonal precipitation      |
| Second small letter | Temperature characteristics |


---


### Example


**Cfa**


C = Warm temperate climate


f = No dry season


a = Hot summer


---

# 1. Tropical Humid Climate (A)


## Definition


**Tropical humid climates** occur between the **Tropic of Cancer and Tropic of Capricorn**, where temperatures remain **high throughout the year**.


### Key Characteristics


| Feature                  | Description             |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Latitude                 | ~0°–23.5° N & S         |
| Temperature              | High all year           |
| Annual temperature range | Very small              |
| Rainfall                 | Heavy                   |
| Vegetation               | Dense forests / savanna |


---


### Important Cause


The region lies under the influence of:

- **Overhead Sun throughout the year**
- **Inter Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)**

➡ This results in **high temperature + heavy rainfall**.

# 1. Dry Climate (B)


## Definition


**Dry climates** are regions where **evaporation exceeds precipitation**, so rainfall is **insufficient for vegetation growth**.


### Key Characteristics


| Feature     | Description            |
| ----------- | ---------------------- |
| Rainfall    | Very low               |
| Evaporation | Very high              |
| Vegetation  | Sparse                 |
| Sky         | Clear most of the year |


---


### Global Extent


Dry climates cover **about 30% of the Earth’s land area**.


---


# Latitudinal Distribution


Dry climates occur mainly between:


**15° – 30° latitude** in both hemispheres.


These regions coincide with **subtropical high pressure belts**.

# 4. Causes of Desert Formation


### 1. Subtropical High Pressure


Descending dry air prevents rainfall.


Example:

- Sahara Desert
- Arabian Desert

---


### 2. Cold Ocean Currents


Cold currents reduce evaporation.


Examples:


| Desert         | Cold Current     |
| -------------- | ---------------- |
| Atacama Desert | Peru Current     |
| Namib Desert   | Benguela Current |


---


### 3. Rain Shadow Effect


Mountains block moisture.


Example:

- **Patagonian Desert (Argentina)**

---


### 4. Continental Interior


Distance from oceans reduces moisture.


Example:

- **Central Asian deserts**

---

# 9. Mains Enrichment Point


**Merits of Köppen's Classification:**

- **Universal Application:** It is an empirical system that can be applied globally.
- **Simple & Objective:** Based on easily available temperature and precipitation data.
- **Symbol-Based:** Uses letter codes, which makes it easy to communicate and avoid repetition.
- **Links Climate & Vegetation:** Successfully establishes a broad correlation between climatic types and natural vegetation zones.

**Limitations of Köppen's Classification:**

- **Over-Simplified:** It does not account for causal mechanisms of climate (like jet streams, air masses).
- **Static Boundaries:** Based on averages and does not reflect short-term variability or the effects of climate change easily.
- **Faulty Basis:** The formula for precipitation effectiveness to distinguish humid and arid climates can be less accurate in regions with high seasonality.
- **Limited Use:** For the reasons above, it is of less utility for meteorologists and climatologists who study atmospheric processes.
