
## 1. Concept of Migration


### Definition


**Migration** = Permanent or semi-permanent movement of people from one place to another.


Key Characteristics

- Change of **residence**
- Movement across **space (distance)**
- Happens due to **economic, social, political, or environmental reasons**

### Importance


Migration is an important factor in:

- **Redistribution of population**
- **Urbanization**
- **Economic development**
- **Cultural interaction**

Example from India

- Migration from **Bihar & UP → Maharashtra, Delhi, Gujarat**

---


## 2. Historical Migration in India


India historically experienced **multiple waves of migration**.


### Migration INTO India


People came from:


| Region         | Examples             |
| -------------- | -------------------- |
| Central Asia   | Aryans, Turks        |
| West Asia      | Persians, Arabs      |
| Southeast Asia | Traders and settlers |


This shaped India’s **composite culture**.


Firaq Gorakhpuri wrote:

> “Caravans kept coming to India and Hindustan kept forming.”

---


## 3. Indian Migration to Other Countries


### Wave 1: Colonial Migration (Indentured Labour)


During **British rule**, Indians were taken as **indentured labourers**.


Destination countries:


| Region       | Countries                |
| ------------ | ------------------------ |
| Indian Ocean | Mauritius                |
| Caribbean    | Trinidad, Tobago, Guyana |
| Pacific      | Fiji                     |
| Africa       | South Africa             |


From India mainly:

- **Uttar Pradesh**
- **Bihar**

They worked in **plantations**.


System called **Girmit System**


(Legal contract labour system)


Conditions:

- Similar to **slavery**

---


### Wave 2: Post-Independence Migration


People migrated as:

- **Artisans**
- **Traders**
- **Workers**

Destination countries:

- Malaysia
- Singapore
- Thailand
- Indonesia
- African countries

Major reason:


**Oil boom in West Asia (1970s)**


→ Migration of Indian labour.


---


### Wave 3: Skilled Migration


From **1960 onwards**


Migrants include:

- Doctors
- Engineers
- IT professionals
- Financial experts
- Media professionals

Destination countries:

- USA
- UK
- Canada
- Australia
- Germany
- New Zealand

This created a powerful **Indian Diaspora**.


Indian diaspora ≈ **20 million people across 110 countries**.

Migration statistics collected since **Census 1881**.


Major improvements:


| Census Year | Change                                   |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1881        | Migration recorded by **place of birth** |
| 1961        | Added **duration of residence**          |
| 1971        | Added **place of last residence**        |
| 1981        | Added **reasons for migration**          |


---


## 5. Migration Measurement in Census


Two main categories:


### 1. Life-time Migrant


A person whose:


Place of birth ≠ Place of enumeration.


Example


Born in **Bihar**, living in **Delhi**


---


### 2. Migrant by Last Residence


Place of **previous residence ≠ present residence**.


Example


Moved from **Lucknow → Kanpur**


---


### Migration Numbers (Census 2001)


| Category                   | Population        |
| -------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Total population           | 1.029 billion     |
| Migrants by place of birth | 307 million (30%) |
| Migrants by last residence | 315 million (31%) |


Important statistic for **Prelims**.

# 6. Streams of Migration


Migration streams = **direction of movement**.


Four main streams:


| Stream        | Meaning            |
| ------------- | ------------------ |
| Rural → Rural | Village to village |
| Rural → Urban | Village to city    |
| Urban → Urban | City to city       |
| Urban → Rural | City to village    |


---


### Major Pattern in India


Most common stream:


**Rural → Rural**


Reason:


**Marriage migration of females**


---


### Gender Pattern


According to the migration charts on page 3:


| Migration Stream | Dominant Gender | Reason              |
| ---------------- | --------------- | ------------------- |
| Rural → Rural    | Female          | Marriage            |
| Rural → Urban    | Male            | Employment          |
| Urban → Urban    | Male            | Job transfer        |
| Urban → Rural    | Mixed           | Retirement / return |

# 10. Causes of Migration


People are generally **emotionally attached to their birthplace**, but many leave due to different pressures or attractions.


Migration causes are broadly divided into two categories:


| Category         | Meaning                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Push Factors** | Forces that **compel people to leave** their place |
| **Pull Factors** | Forces that **attract people to a new place**      |


---


## Push–Pull Migration Model


```plain text
Origin Area
  ↓
Push Factors
(Poverty, disasters, unemployment)

Migration Decision
  ↓
Pull Factors
(Jobs, education, healthcare)

Destination Area
```


---


# 11. Push Factors (Forcing Migration)


Push factors are **negative conditions in the place of origin**.


### Economic Push Factors

- Poverty
- Landlessness
- Unemployment
- Low wages
- Population pressure on land

Example


Eastern **Uttar Pradesh and Bihar → migration due to unemployment**


---


### Social Push Factors

- Lack of education facilities
- Lack of healthcare
- Social conflicts

---


### Environmental Push Factors


Natural disasters such as:

- Floods
- Droughts
- Cyclones
- Earthquakes
- Tsunami

Example

- **Odisha coastal migration after cyclones**
- **Bihar flood migration**

---


### Political Push Factors

- War
- Conflict
- Political instability

Example

- **Sri Lankan Tamil refugees to India**

---


# 12. Pull Factors (Attracting Migration)


Pull factors are **positive opportunities at the destination**.


### Major Pull Factors


| Factor       | Explanation                      |
| ------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Employment   | Better job opportunities         |
| Higher wages | Higher income                    |
| Education    | Universities and colleges        |
| Healthcare   | Hospitals and medical facilities |
| Lifestyle    | Entertainment and modern life    |


Example

- Migration from villages → **Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore**

---


# 13. Gender-Based Migration Patterns


According to migration data charts on **page 7 of the chapter**:


### Reasons for Male Migration


| Reason               | Percentage |
| -------------------- | ---------- |
| Work/Employment      | **38%**    |
| Moved with household | 25%        |
| Others               | 16%        |
| Business             | 10%        |
| Education            | 6%         |
| Marriage             | 2%         |


Main conclusion:


👉 **Male migration is mainly economic.**


---


### Reasons for Female Migration


| Reason               | Percentage |
| -------------------- | ---------- |
| Marriage             | **65%**    |
| Moved with household | 19%        |
| Work/Employment      | 3%         |
| Others               | 7%         |
| Education            | 1%         |


Conclusion:


👉 **Female migration mainly occurs due to marriage.**


---


### Special Case: Meghalaya


Female migration pattern differs because:

- **Matrilineal society**
- Women inherit property

Hence migration patterns differ.

# 14. Consequences of Migration


Migration produces **both positive and negative impacts**.


These impacts occur in:


1️⃣ Economic


2️⃣ Demographic


3️⃣ Social


4️⃣ Environmental


---


# 15. Economic Consequences


## Positive Impacts


### Remittances


Migrants send money back to their families.


Uses of remittances:

- Food
- Debt repayment
- Education
- Agriculture
- House construction
- Medical treatment

India received about **$11 billion remittances in 2002**.


Major beneficiary states:

- Kerala
- Punjab
- Tamil Nadu

---


### Role in Agricultural Development


Migration of labour helped:


**Green Revolution success**


Example:


Migration route:


```plain text
Eastern UP + Bihar + MP
        ↓
Punjab + Haryana + Western UP
        ↓
Agricultural labour supply
        ↓
Higher crop production
```


---


## Negative Economic Consequences


### Urban Problems


Uncontrolled migration leads to:

- Slums
- Overcrowding
- Informal economy

Major cities affected:

- Mumbai
- Delhi
- Bangalore
- Chennai

---


# 16. Demographic Consequences


Migration changes **population structure**.


### Major Effects


| Effect                    | Explanation               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Population redistribution | Rural → urban movement    |
| Urban population growth   | Cities expand             |
| Sex ratio imbalance       | Male migration dominates  |
| Age structure distortion  | Young population migrates |


---


### Example


Heavy out-migration from:

- Uttarakhand
- Rajasthan
- Eastern Maharashtra

Causes **age and sex imbalance**.


---


# 17. Social Consequences


Migration spreads **ideas and culture**.


### Positive Effects

- Diffusion of technology
- Spread of education
- Spread of family planning
- Cultural integration

Example


Urban ideas reach rural areas through migrants.


---


### Cultural Impact


Migration creates **composite culture**.


Example in cities:

- Mumbai
- Delhi
- Bangalore

Different cultures mix.


---


### Negative Social Effects

- Social isolation
- Identity crisis
- Crime
- Drug abuse

Because migrants may feel **alienation**.


---


# 18. Environmental Consequences


Migration increases pressure on cities.


### Urban Environmental Problems


| Problem               | Cause                  |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Groundwater depletion | Population pressure    |
| Air pollution         | Transport & industries |
| Sewage problems       | Poor urban planning    |
| Solid waste           | High consumption       |


---


### Slum Development


Unplanned migration leads to:

- Slums
- Shanty towns

Example:

- Dharavi slum in **Mumbai**

---


# 19. Migration and Women


Migration affects women in two ways.


### Negative Impacts


Male migration leaves women behind.


Consequences:

- More physical work
- Mental pressure
- Household responsibilities

---


### Positive Impacts


When women migrate for:

- Education
- Employment

They gain:

- Economic independence
- Higher social status

But also face **vulnerability and exploitation**.


---


# 20. Brain Drain


Brain Drain = Migration of **highly skilled professionals**.


Example migrants:

- Doctors
- Engineers
- Scientists
- IT professionals

Destination countries:

- USA
- UK
- Canada
- Australia

---


### Impact


| Positive           | Negative               |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Remittances        | Loss of talent         |
| Knowledge networks | Weakens source regions |


This reinforces **underdevelopment in poor regions**.
