
# 1. Meaning of Manufacturing Industries


### Definition


**Manufacturing:**


Process of **converting raw materials into finished goods** using **machines, labour, and technology**.


Examples

- Wheat → Flour
- Cotton → Cloth
- Iron ore → Steel

### Importance of Manufacturing

1. **Economic growth**
2. **Employment generation**
3. **Value addition to raw materials**
4. **Export earnings**
5. **Industrialisation & urbanisation**

Example (India)

- Steel → infrastructure
- Textiles → employment

---


# 2. Classification of Industries


Industries are classified on **4 major bases**.


---


# (A) Based on Size


| Type             | Features              | Example           |
| ---------------- | --------------------- | ----------------- |
| Large scale      | High capital & labour | Steel plants      |
| Medium scale     | Moderate investment   | Engineering units |
| Small scale      | Small capital         | Garment units     |
| Cottage industry | Household based       | Handloom          |


Example (UP)

- **Varanasi** → Silk cottage industry
- **Bhadohi** → Carpet industry

---


# (B) Based on Ownership


| Type           | Owner               | Example            |
| -------------- | ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Public sector  | Government          | SAIL               |
| Private sector | Private companies   | Tata               |
| Joint sector   | Govt + Private      | Maruti             |
| Cooperative    | Community ownership | Sugar cooperatives |


Example

- **Amul dairy cooperative**

---


# (C) Based on Use of Products


| Type               | Description                  | Example           |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Basic goods        | Raw materials for industries | Iron, steel       |
| Capital goods      | Machinery                    | Heavy engineering |
| Intermediate goods | Semi-finished                | Steel sheets      |
| Consumer goods     | Direct use                   | Clothes, TVs      |


---


# (D) Based on Raw Material


| Type                    | Raw material source | Example        |
| ----------------------- | ------------------- | -------------- |
| Agro-based              | Agriculture         | Cotton textile |
| Forest-based            | Forest              | Paper          |
| Mineral-based           | Minerals            | Iron & steel   |
| Industrial raw material | Processed inputs    | Petrochemicals |


---


# (E) Based on Product Nature


Eight industrial groups:

1. Metallurgical
2. Mechanical engineering
3. Chemical industries
4. Textile
5. Food processing
6. Electricity generation
7. Electronics
8. Communication industries

# 3. Footloose Industries


### Definition


Industries **not dependent on raw material location**.


### Characteristics

- Require **skilled labour**
- Need **good communication**
- Raw material **not bulky**

Examples

- IT industry
- Electronics
- Software industry

---


# 4. Factors Affecting Location of Industries


Industrial location depends on **minimum cost + maximum profit**.


### Main Factors


```plain text
Raw Material
      ↓
Power
      ↓
Transport
      ↓
Labour
      ↓
Market
      ↓
Capital
      ↓
Government policy
```


---


# (1) Raw Material


Industries using **weight-losing raw materials** locate near source.


Example


| Industry | Raw Material    | Location        |
| -------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Sugar    | Sugarcane       | UP, Maharashtra |
| Steel    | Iron ore + coal | Jharkhand       |
| Paper    | Wood pulp       | Forest regions  |


Reason


Transporting raw material becomes costly.


---


# (2) Power


Machines need energy.


Power intensive industries:


| Industry   | Reason             |
| ---------- | ------------------ |
| Aluminium  | Electrolysis       |
| Fertilizer | Chemical processes |


Example

- Aluminium plants near hydel power.

---


# (3) Market


Some industries locate near **consumers**.


Example


| Industry        | Location        |
| --------------- | --------------- |
| Cotton textiles | Mumbai          |
| Refineries      | Mathura, Koyali |


Reason


Transport of finished goods cheaper.


---


# (4) Transport


Industries cluster along **railways, ports, highways**.


Example

- Mumbai
- Chennai
- Kolkata

These were early **transport hubs**.


---


# (5) Labour


Industries need **skilled & cheap labour**.


India advantage

- Large population
- Migrant labour

Example

- Textile industries.

---


# (6) Historical Factors


Colonial trade centres became industrial hubs.


Examples


| City        | Industry |
| ----------- | -------- |
| Dhaka       | Muslin   |
| Surat       | Textile  |
| Murshidabad | Silk     |


---


# (7) Government Policies


Government promotes industries in backward areas.


Example


Steel plants in

- **Bhilai**
- **Rourkela**

To develop tribal regions.

# 1. Cotton Textile Industry


### Definition


Industry involved in **spinning cotton yarn and weaving cloth**.


It is one of the **oldest industries in India**.


---


# Historical Background


### Ancient Period


India was famous for:

- **Muslin (Dhaka)**
- **Calico**
- **Chintz**

These were **cottage industries**.


---


### British Colonial Period


British policy damaged Indian textile industry.


Flowchart:


```plain text
Indian cotton → exported to Britain
        ↓
Manufactured cloth produced in Manchester
        ↓
Finished cloth imported back to India
        ↓
Indian cottage industry declined
```


Reason


British machine-made cloth was **cheaper than handloom production**.


---


# First Modern Cotton Mill


| Year | Place  |
| ---- | ------ |
| 1854 | Mumbai |


Reasons Mumbai became textile hub:


| Factor       | Explanation                       |
| ------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Raw material | Cotton from Maharashtra & Gujarat |
| Port         | Export-import via Mumbai port     |
| Capital      | Financial centre                  |
| Labour       | Migrant labour availability       |
| Transport    | Machinery imported easily         |


---


# Expansion of Textile Industry


After **1921 railway expansion**, new centres emerged:


| Region        | Cities              |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| South India   | Coimbatore, Madurai |
| Central India | Nagpur, Indore      |
| North India   | Kanpur              |
| East India    | Kolkata             |

# Location of Sugar Industry


Sugar recovery ratio:


**Sugar : Sugarcane = 9–12%**


Sucrose content declines quickly after harvest.


Therefore:


```plain text
Sugarcane harvested

Sucrose decreases quickly
        ↓
Must crush within 24 hours
        ↓
Sugar mills near cane fields
```

# 2. LPG Reforms (1991)


LPG = **Liberalisation + Privatisation + Globalisation**


India introduced **New Industrial Policy in 1991**.


---


## Objectives of LPG Policy

1. Increase **industrial growth**
2. Improve **productivity**
3. Generate **employment**
4. Achieve **global competitiveness**

---


# Major Measures of New Industrial Policy


| Reform                         | Explanation                          |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Industrial licensing abolished | Most industries freed from licensing |
| Foreign technology allowed     | Easier technology imports            |
| FDI policy introduced          | Foreign investment encouraged        |
| Access to capital market       | Easier financing                     |
| Trade liberalisation           | Import restrictions reduced          |
| Industrial location policy     | Flexible location rules              |


---


# Changes in Public Sector


Earlier:

- **17 industries reserved**

After LPG:

- Only **4 industries remained under public sector**

Examples

- Atomic energy
- Railways

---


# Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)


FDI allowed through **automatic route**.


Benefits:

- Technology transfer
- Better management practices
- Increased capital investment

---


# Globalisation


### Definition


Integration of **national economy with global economy**.


Features:

1. Free movement of goods
2. Free movement of capital
3. International collaboration
4. Expansion of multinational companies

---


# Impact of LPG on India


### Positive Effects

- Increased foreign investment
- Growth of private sector
- Expansion of service sector

---


### Negative Effects


Regional inequality increased.


Example:


| State          | Share of investment |
| -------------- | ------------------- |
| Maharashtra    | 23%                 |
| Gujarat        | 17%                 |
| Andhra Pradesh | 7%                  |
| Tamil Nadu     | 6%                  |
| Uttar Pradesh  | ~8%                 |


North-Eastern states received **less than 1% investment**.


---


# 3. Industrial Regions of India


Industries are **not evenly distributed**.


They concentrate where **locational factors are favourable**.


---


## Indicators of Industrial Clustering

1. Number of industries
2. Industrial workers
3. Power consumption
4. Industrial output
5. Value addition
