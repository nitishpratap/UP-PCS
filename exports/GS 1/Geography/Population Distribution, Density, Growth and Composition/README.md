
# 1. Population – Basic Understanding


### Definition


Population refers to **the total number of people living in a specific area at a specific time.**


### Importance of Population


Population is the **most important component of a country** because:

- It provides **human resources**
- It determines **economic development**
- It affects **resource utilization**
- It influences **social and cultural patterns**

Example (India):


| Indicator                | Value                 |
| ------------------------ | --------------------- |
| Population (2001 Census) | **1028 million**      |
| Rank in World            | **2nd (after China)** |


India's population is **larger than the combined population of North America + South America + Australia.**

# 13. Types of Population Density


Simple density doesn't fully explain population pressure.


Therefore geographers use **three density concepts**.


---


## (1) Arithmetic Density


Formula:


Population / Total Land Area


Used for general comparison.


---


## (2) Physiological Density


\text{Physiological Density} = \frac{\text{Total Population}}{\text{Net Cultivated Area}}


Meaning:


Pressure of population on **cultivable land**.


Important for **agricultural countries like India.**


---


## (3) Agricultural Density


\text{Agricultural Density} = \frac{\text{Agricultural Population}}{\text{Net Cultivable Area}}


Agricultural population includes:

- Farmers
- Agricultural labourers
- Their families

---


# 14. Why These Densities Matter (UPPCS Concept)


| Density Type  | Indicates                   |
| ------------- | --------------------------- |
| Arithmetic    | General population pressure |
| Physiological | Pressure on farmland        |
| Agricultural  | Efficiency of agriculture   |


Example:


India has **high physiological density**, meaning:


➡ High pressure on farmland


➡ Small land holdings

# Population Growth in India


## 1. Definition


**Population Growth** refers to the **change in the number of people living in an area over a period of time.**


It is expressed in **percentage**.


Example:


Growth from **1991 → 2001**


---


## 2. Components of Population Growth


Population growth occurs due to **two components**:


### (1) Natural Growth


Natural Growth = **Birth Rate – Death Rate**


It depends on:

- Crude Birth Rate (CBR)
- Crude Death Rate (CDR)

---


### (2) Induced Growth


Induced growth occurs due to **migration**.


Migration includes:

- Immigration (in-migration)
- Emigration (out-migration)

But in this chapter, **only natural growth is discussed.**


---


# Population Growth in India


Important fact:


| Indicator          | Value          |
| ------------------ | -------------- |
| Annual growth rate | **2.4%**       |
| Doubling time      | **≈ 36 years** |


Meaning:


If this rate continues, **India’s population will double in 36 years.**


---


# Population Doubling Time


Definition:


**Population doubling time** is the **time required for a population to double at a given growth rate.**


Example:


If growth rate = **2.4%**


Population doubles in **36 years**.


---


# Decadal Growth Rate


### Definition


Growth of population between **two census years (10 years).**


Formula:


\text{Decadal Growth Rate} = \frac{P_2 - P_1}{P_1} \times 100


Where:


| Symbol | Meaning                    |
| ------ | -------------------------- |
| P1     | Population of base year    |
| P2     | Population of present year |


---

# Phases of Population Growth in India


India's population growth can be divided into **4 phases**.


---


# Phase 1: Stagnant Population (1901–1921)


### Key Feature


Population growth was **very low or negative**.


1921 is called **“Year of Great Divide”**.


### Reasons

- High birth rate
- High death rate
- Poor health facilities
- Frequent epidemics
- Famines
- Poor food distribution

Result:


Population growth remained **almost stagnant**.


---


# Phase 2: Steady Growth (1921–1951)


### Features

- Mortality rate decreased
- Birth rate remained high

### Causes

1. Improvement in **health services**
2. Better **sanitation**
3. Development of **transport**
4. Better **food distribution**

Even though this period saw:

- **Great Depression (1929)**
- **World War II**

Population growth still increased.


---


# Phase 3: Population Explosion (1951–1981)


### Key Feature


Rapid population growth.


Average growth rate:


**≈ 2.2% per year**


---


### Causes

1. **Rapid fall in death rate**
2. **High fertility rate**
3. Improvement in:
    - medical facilities
    - living standards
    - food production
4. Economic development after independence
5. Planning and development programs

---


### Migration Contribution


Some population increase also due to **international migration**:


Examples:

- Tibetans
- Bangladeshis
- Nepalese
- Pakistan migrants

---


# Phase 4: Slowing Growth (1981 – Present)


Growth rate still high but **gradually declining**.


### Reasons

- Declining birth rate
- Rising **female education**
- Higher **age at marriage**
- Family planning awareness
- Better living standards

---
