```plain text
     ↓

Runoff → Ocean
```

# 2. 💧 Humidity


## Definition

- **Humidity = Amount of water vapour in air**

---


## Types (VERY IMPORTANT)


### Absolute Humidity

- **Mass of water vapour per unit volume**
- Unit → **g/m³**

👉 Features:

- ↓ with altitude
- High near **equator & oceans**

---


### Relative Humidity (FREQUENT PYQ)

- % of actual moisture vs max capacity

```plain text
RH = (Actual / Maximum) × 100
```


👉 Key Concept:

- **Warm air → holds more moisture**

---


## Distribution (Prelims)


| Region        | Humidity |
| ------------- | -------- |
| Oceans/coasts | High     |
| Interior      | Low      |
| Deserts       | Very low |


---


## India Examples

- **High RH** → Kerala, Assam, WB
- **Low RH** → Rajasthan, Bundelkhand, W. UP

## Saturated Air

- Air cannot hold more moisture

    👉 **RH = 100%**


---


## Dew Point

- Temperature at which air becomes saturated

    👉 **Condensation begins**


📌 Example: Dew on crops in UP winters

# 4. 🔥 Evaporation


## Definition

- Liquid → Vapour due to heat

---


## Factors


| Factor       | Effect        |
| ------------ | ------------- |
| Temperature  | ↑ evaporation |
| Wind         | ↑ evaporation |
| Humidity     | ↓ evaporation |
| Surface area | ↑ evaporation |

- **Thar Desert, Gujarat → highest evaporation**

# 5. ☁️ Condensation


## Definition

- Vapour → Liquid due to cooling

---


## Conditions

1. **Saturation (dew point reached)**
2. **Condensation nuclei** (dust, smoke, salt)

👉 Called **Hygroscopic nuclei**


---


## Process Flow


```plain text
Cooling of Air
     ↓
Dew Point
     ↓
Condensation
     ↓
Droplets
     ↓
Cloud / Fog / Dew
```

# 1. Atmospheric Moisture


## Definition


**Atmospheric moisture** refers to the **water vapour present in the atmosphere**.


### Sources of Atmospheric Moisture


Water vapour enters the atmosphere mainly through:

1. **Evaporation** – from oceans, lakes, rivers
2. **Transpiration** – from plants
3. **Sublimation** – ice directly converting into vapour

### Hydrological Cycle (Moisture Exchange)


Flowchart for your notes:


```plain text
Solar Heat
     ↓
Evaporation + Transpiration
     ↓
Water Vapour in Atmosphere
     ↓
Condensation
     ↓
Cloud Formation
     ↓
Precipitation
     ↓
Runoff → Rivers → Ocean
```


This continuous exchange of moisture between:

- **Ocean**
- **Atmosphere**
- **Land**

is called the **Hydrological Cycle**.


---

Water exists in the atmosphere in **three forms**


| Form        | Example            |
| ----------- | ------------------ |
| **Gaseous** | Water Vapour       |
| **Liquid**  | Clouds, Rain       |
| **Solid**   | Snow, Ice crystals |

# 3. Humidity


## Definition


**Humidity = Amount of water vapour present in the atmosphere**


It strongly influences:

- Weather
- Climate
- Cloud formation
- Precipitation

---


# 4. Types of Humidity


## 1. Absolute Humidity


### Definition


**Absolute humidity = Actual amount of water vapour present in a unit volume of air**


Formula representation:


```plain text
Absolute Humidity
= grams of water vapour
  per cubic metre of air
```


Units:


```plain text
g/m³
```


### Characteristics

- Varies **from place to place**
- Decreases with **altitude**
- Highest near **equator and oceans**

---


## 2. Relative Humidity


### Definition


Relative Humidity = Percentage of moisture present compared to the **maximum moisture air can hold at that temperature**


Formula:


```plain text
Relative Humidity =
(Actual Moisture / Maximum Moisture Capacity) × 100
```


### Important Concept


**Warm air → holds more moisture**


Therefore:


| Temperature      | Moisture Capacity |
| ---------------- | ----------------- |
| High temperature | High capacity     |
| Low temperature  | Low capacity      |


---


### Geographic Distribution


| Region              | Relative Humidity |
| ------------------- | ----------------- |
| Oceans              | High              |
| Coastal areas       | High              |
| Interior continents | Low               |
| Deserts             | Very low          |


---


### India Example


High humidity regions:

- **Kerala**
- **West Bengal**
- **Assam**
- **Coastal Odisha**

Low humidity regions:

- **Rajasthan**
- **Bundelkhand**
- **Western Uttar Pradesh**

---


# 5. Saturated Air


## Definition


Air becomes **saturated** when it cannot hold any more water vapour.


At this stage:


```plain text
Relative Humidity = 100%
```


---


# 6. Dew Point


## Definition


**Dew Point = Temperature at which air becomes saturated**


When temperature falls to dew point:


```plain text
Condensation starts
```


### Example


Winter night in **Uttar Pradesh fields**


Temperature drops → dew forms on crops.


---

### Major factors

| Humidity     | Low humidity → high evaporation       |

### Cause-Effect Chain

```plain text
High temperature
       ↓

       ↓
More water vapour
       ↓
Higher humidity
```

# 1. Condensation


## Definition


**Condensation = Process by which water vapour changes into liquid water due to cooling of air.**


Key idea:


```plain text
Cooling of Air → Saturation → Condensation
```


Condensation occurs when:

- Air temperature **drops to dew point**
- Relative humidity becomes **100%**

---


# 2. Conditions Required for Condensation


Condensation requires **two essential conditions**:


### 1️⃣ Saturation of air


Air must reach **dew point temperature**.


### 2️⃣ Condensation nuclei


Tiny particles in atmosphere on which condensation occurs.


Examples:


| Condensation Nuclei | Source       |
| ------------------- | ------------ |
| Dust particles      | Desert winds |
| Smoke               | Industries   |
| Salt particles      | Oceans       |
| Pollen              | Vegetation   |


These particles are called **Hygroscopic nuclei** (water attracting particles).


---


# Condensation Process Flowchart


```plain text
Water Vapour in Atmosphere
           ↓
Cooling of Air
           ↓
Air reaches Dew Point
           ↓
Condensation on Dust Particles
           ↓
Water Droplets form
           ↓
Clouds / Dew / Fog
```


---

Forms of condensation depend on:

- **Temperature**
- **Surface conditions**
- **Height above ground**

# 1. Cloud


## Definition


**Cloud = A mass of minute water droplets or tiny ice crystals formed by condensation of water vapour in the atmosphere.**


Important features:

- Formed at **considerable heights**
- Float due to **air currents**
- Main source of **precipitation**

 clouds form when **condensed moisture particles grow and remain suspended in the atmosphere**.


---


# 2. Cloud Formation Process


Flowchart for your notes:


```plain text
Water Vapour in Air
        ↓
Air Rises & Cools
        ↓
Temperature reaches Dew Point
        ↓
Condensation around dust particles
        ↓
Tiny droplets form
        ↓
CLOUDS
```


---

| ---------------- | ------------------------------- |

| Formation height | Several km above earth          |

| Movement         | Driven by air currents          |

# 4. Classification of Clouds


Clouds are classified based on:

- **Height**
- **Shape**
- **Density**

There are **4 major cloud types**.


| Cloud Type | Appearance     | Height          |
| ---------- | -------------- | --------------- |
| Cirrus     | Feather-like   | High altitude   |
| Cumulus    | Cotton-like    | Middle altitude |

# 5. Cirrus Clouds


## Definition


**Cirrus clouds are thin, white, feather-like clouds formed at very high altitudes.**


Height:


```plain text
6000 m – 12000 m
```


---


## Characteristics

- Very thin
- White in colour
- Made mostly of **ice crystals**
- Look like **feathers or horse tails**

---


## Weather Indication

- Generally indicate **fair weather**
- Often appear **before storms**

---


# 6. Cumulus Clouds


## Definition


**Cumulus clouds are dense, cotton-like clouds with flat bases.**


Height:


```plain text
4000 m – 7000 m
```


---


## Characteristics

- Puffy appearance
- Bright white colour
- Flat base
- Rounded tops

---


## Weather


Usually indicate **fair weather**, but can grow into storm clouds.


---


# 7. Stratus Clouds


## Definition


**Stratus clouds are layered clouds covering large portions of the sky.**


---


## Characteristics

- Uniform grey colour
- Spread like a blanket
- Cover entire sky

---


## Weather


Associated with:

- Light drizzle
- Overcast sky

---


# 8. Nimbus Clouds


## Definition


**Nimbus clouds are dense, dark rain clouds.**


---


## Characteristics

- Thick
- Dark grey or black
- Produce **heavy rainfall**

---


## Appearance


Often appear very low and may seem to **touch the ground**.


NCERT describes them as **dense clouds opaque to sunlight**.


---

# 1. Precipitation


## Definition


**Precipitation = Release of moisture from clouds in liquid or solid form which falls to the Earth’s surface.**


After condensation occurs, droplets grow larger.


When air can no longer hold them against gravity, they **fall to the earth as precipitation**.


---


# Precipitation Formation Process


Flowchart for your notes:


```plain text
Evaporation
     ↓
Water Vapour
     ↓
Condensation
     ↓
Cloud Formation
     ↓
Droplets grow larger
     ↓
Gravity pulls them down
     ↓
PRECIPITATION
```


---


# 2. Forms of Precipitation


Precipitation may occur in **liquid or solid form**.


| Form     | Description      |
| -------- | ---------------- |
| Rainfall | Water droplets   |
| Snowfall | Ice crystals     |
| Sleet    | Frozen raindrops |
| Hail     | Large ice balls  |


---


# 3. Rainfall


## Definition


**Rainfall = Precipitation in liquid form when temperature is above 0°C.**


Characteristics:

- Most common form of precipitation
- Essential for **agriculture and water supply**

---


# 4. Snowfall


## Definition


**Snowfall = Precipitation in the form of snowflakes when temperature is below freezing point.**


Snowflakes form as **hexagonal ice crystals**.


---


## Areas of Snowfall


In India:

- **Himalayas**
- **Ladakh**
- **Jammu & Kashmir**
- **Himachal Pradesh**

---


# 5. Sleet


## Definition


**Sleet = Frozen raindrops that refreeze before reaching the ground.**


Formation condition:


```plain text
Warm air layer
     ↓
Cold air layer near surface
     ↓
Raindrops freeze again
     ↓
SLEET
```


---


# 6. Hail


## Definition


**Hail = Large rounded ice pieces formed during thunderstorms.**


Important features:

- Form in **cumulonimbus clouds**
- Have **concentric layers of ice**

---


## Hail Formation


```plain text
Strong updraft in thunderstorm
        ↓
Raindrops lifted upward
        ↓
Freeze into ice
        ↓
Repeated lifting & freezing
        ↓
Large ice balls
        ↓
HAIL
```


---


## Impact


Hailstorms can damage:

- Crops
- Vehicles
- Buildings

Example:


Hailstorms frequently damage **wheat crops in Uttar Pradesh and Punjab**.


---


# 7. Types of Rainfall


Rainfall is classified based on **origin**.


Three major types:


| Type                  | Cause                 |
| --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Convectional rainfall | Heating of air        |
| Orographic rainfall   | Mountain barriers     |
| Cyclonic rainfall     | Converging air masses |


---


# 8. Convectional Rainfall


## Definition


Rainfall caused by **heating of Earth's surface and rising warm air**.


---


## Process


```plain text
Sun heats land
      ↓
Air becomes warm & light
      ↓
Air rises upward
      ↓
Air cools
      ↓
Condensation
      ↓
Cumulus clouds form
      ↓
Heavy rainfall
```


---


## Characteristics

- Short duration
- Heavy rainfall
- Thunder and lightning

---


## Regions


Common in:

- **Equatorial regions**
- **Tropical forests**

Example:

- **Amazon Basin**
- **Congo Basin**

---
