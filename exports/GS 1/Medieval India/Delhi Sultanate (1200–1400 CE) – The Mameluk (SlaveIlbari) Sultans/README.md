
# PART A: CHAPTER NOTES


# 1. Establishment of Delhi Sultanate


### Background

- After the Turkish victories in North India, the **Delhi Sultanate** emerged.
- Initial challenges:
    - Foreign invasions
    - Internal conflicts among Turkish nobles
    - Resistance from Rajput rulers
    - Regional rebellions

### Outcome

- By the end of the 13th century:
    - Sultanate consolidated in North India.
    - Expansion towards:
        - Gujarat
        - Malwa
        - Deccan
        - South India

### Historical Significance

- Beginning of:
    - New administrative system
    - Centralized monarchy
    - Indo-Islamic cultural synthesis
    - New military institutions

---


# 2. Qutbuddin Aibak (1206–1210)


### Important Facts

- Slave of **Muhammad Ghori (Muizzuddin)**
- Became ruler after Ghori's death in **1206**
- Ruled from **Lahore**

### Challenge

- **Yalduz (Yildiz)** of Ghazni claimed authority over Delhi.

### Importance

- Delhi Sultanate severed political links with Ghazni.
- Helped India avoid entanglement in Central Asian politics.

### Death

- Died in **1210**
- Fell from horse while playing **Chaugan (Polo)**.

---


# 3. Iltutmish (1210–1236)


## Why called the Real Consolidator?


Because he:

- Defeated rivals
- Suppressed rebellions
- Recovered lost territories
- Saved Sultanate from collapse

---


## Challenges at Accession


### Political Rivals

- Ali Mardan Khan → Bengal/Bihar
- Qubacha → Multan and Punjab
- Turkish nobles near Delhi

### Rajput Resistance


Recovered:

- Kalinjar
- Gwalior
- Bayana
- Ajmer
- Eastern Rajasthan

---


## North-West Policy


### Khwarizm Threat

- Khwarizm Shah conquered Ghazni.
- Frontier reached Indus.

### Iltutmish's Action

- Occupied Lahore.
- Secured frontier.

### Mongol Factor

- Mongols destroyed Khwarizm Empire (1218).

### Achievement

- Recovered:
    - Multan
    - Uchch

### Frontier


Reached again up to:


**River Indus**


---


## Bengal Policy


### Independent Ruler


**Iwaz (Ghiyasuddin)**


Controlled:

- Bengal
- Bihar

### Campaign


1226–27:

- Defeated by Iltutmish's son.
- Bengal and Bihar brought under Delhi.

---


## Rajput Policy


### Recovered

- Gwalior
- Bayana

### Expeditions


Against:

- Ranthambhor
- Jalor

### Failure

- Attack on Nagda (Mewar)
- Gujarat Chalukyas repulsed Turkish forces.

---


## Historical Importance


### Political

- Saved Sultanate from fragmentation.

### Administrative

- Established Delhi's supremacy.

### Strategic

- Avoided direct confrontation with Mongols.

### Territorial

- Extended Sultanate from Bengal to Indus.

# 4. Razia Sultan (1236–1239)


## Succession


### Unique Feature

- Chosen by Iltutmish over his sons.

### Significance

- First and only woman Sultan of Delhi.

---


## Conflict with Turkish Nobility


### Chahalgani (The Forty)


Group of powerful Turkish nobles.


Wanted:

- Puppet ruler

Razia wanted:

- Independent monarchy

---


## Measures


### Administrative

- Held open court
- Appeared unveiled
- Led army personally
- Maintained law and order

### Military

- Expedition against Ranthambhor

### Political

- Tried to create loyal nobility

---


## Yaqut Issue


### Yaqut Khan

- Abyssinian noble
- Superintendent of Royal Stable

### Opposition


Turkish nobles opposed:

- Rise of non-Turks
- Razia's independent attitude

---


## Downfall


### Rebellions

- Lahore
- Sirhind

### Events

- Yaqut killed
- Razia imprisoned at Tabarhinda

### Marriage

- Married Altunia

### End

- Defeated near Delhi
- Killed by bandits while fleeing

---


## Historical Importance


### First major struggle:


**Monarchy vs Turkish Nobility**


### Significance

- Attempt at merit-based administration.
- Challenge to gender norms.

# 5. Balban (1266–1287)


---


# Rise of Balban


### Earlier Position

- Naib (Deputy) of Nasiruddin Mahmud

### Eliminated Rivals

- Imaduddin Raihan
- Other Turkish chiefs

### Became Sultan


1266 CE


---


# Theory of Kingship


## Objective


Strengthen monarchy.


### Claim


Descendant of:


**Afrasiyab (Legendary Iranian King)**


---


## Features


### Persian Influence


### Sijda


Prostration before Sultan


### Paibos


Kissing Sultan's feet


---


### Court Culture

- Magnificent court
- Strict protocol
- No laughter in court
- Stopped drinking wine

---


## Despotism


### Destroyed Chahalgani

- Broke power of Turkish nobles

### No power sharing

- Even with relatives

### Example


Poisoned cousin:


**Sher Khan** (as per sources)


---


## Administration


### Justice

- Harsh but impartial

### Intelligence

- Network of spies

### Army Reforms

- Reorganized **Diwan-i-Arz**
- Retired unfit soldiers

---


## Blood and Iron Policy


### Against Mewatis

- Forests cleared
- Military outposts established

### Against Rajput Rebels

- Villages destroyed
- Rebels suppressed

### Against Robbers

- Roads secured

---


## Importance


### Political


Strong centralized monarchy.


### Administrative


Model for later sultans.


### Military


Defensive military state.


### Institutional


Architect of Sultanate administration.

## Historiographical Insight

- Iltutmish = Real founder/consolidator of Delhi Sultanate.
- Razia = Early attempt at merit-based statecraft.
- Balban = Architect of centralized monarchy.
- Mongol pressure accelerated state formation in India.

---


## Administrative Features

- Centralized kingship
- Persian court traditions
- Intelligence network
- Professional army
- Military frontier policy

---


## Economic Implications

- Mongol invasions disrupted trade.
- Restoration of order later revived commerce.
- Protection of roads became major state objective.

---


## Social Implications

- Migration of scholars to Delhi.
- Rise of Indo-Islamic elite.
- Exclusion of Indian Muslims under Balban.

---


## Cultural Importance

- Delhi emerged as Islamic intellectual centre.
- Persian traditions became entrenched.
- Growth of Indo-Persian political culture.

# PART E: READY-MADE MAINS CONTENT


## Introductions


### Intro 1


The 13th century witnessed the consolidation of the Delhi Sultanate through the efforts of Iltutmish, Razia and Balban amidst Mongol invasions and regional rebellions.


### Intro 2


The Mameluk rulers laid the institutional foundations of the Delhi Sultanate by strengthening monarchy, administration and military organization.


### Intro 3


The struggle between monarchy and nobility shaped the political evolution of the early Delhi Sultanate.


---


## Conclusions


### Conclusion 1


The success of the Mameluk rulers transformed a fragile conquest state into a durable empire.


### Conclusion 2


The centralized monarchy built by Balban became the foundation on which the Khaljis and Tughlaqs expanded the Sultanate.


### Conclusion 3


Despite internal rebellions and Mongol pressure, the Delhi Sultanate survived due to strong leadership, military reforms and political adaptability.


---


## Value-Added Points

1. **Iltutmish saved the Sultanate from political disintegration.**
2. **Razia challenged both patriarchy and aristocratic dominance.**
3. **Balban's kingship was based on fear, dignity and centralized authority.**
4. **Mongol invasions indirectly strengthened Delhi by forcing administrative consolidation.**
5. **The Chahalgani represented the biggest internal challenge to monarchy.**
6. **Frontier defence became a permanent feature of Sultanate politics.**
7. **Persian political culture deeply influenced Delhi's institutions.**
8. **Territorial consolidation preceded imperial expansion under the Khaljis.**
9. **Delhi emerged as the principal centre of Islam in South Asia after Mongol devastation of Central Asia.**
10. **The Mameluk period marks the transition from conquest to governance.**
