
# PART A: CHAPTER NOTES


# 1. Overview: India in the First Half of the 17th Century


## Positive Features

- Period of **progress, stability and growth**
- Ruled by:
    - **Jahangir (1605–1627)**
    - **Shah Jahan (1628–1658)**
- Mughal administration strengthened further.
- Alliance with:
    - Rajputs
    - Afghans
    - Marathas
- Expansion of:
    - Foreign trade
    - Cultural activities
    - Architecture
- Better diplomatic relations with:
    - Iran
    - Uzbeks
    - Ottoman Empire

## Negative Features

- Prosperity remained confined to ruling classes.
- Peasants and workers remained poor.
- Mughal elite ignored:
    - Science
    - Technological advancements in Europe
- Succession disputes became chronic.
- Political instability emerged.

---


# 2. Jahangir (1605–1627)


## Accession

- Eldest son of Akbar.
- Ascended throne without major difficulty.

## Khusrau’s Rebellion


### Cause

- Desire for throne.

### Events

- Rebelled shortly after accession.
- Defeated near Lahore.
- Captured and imprisoned.

### Significance

- First major challenge to Jahangir.

# 3. Bengal under Jahangir


## Background


Afghan chiefs remained powerful despite Akbar's victories.


### Important Rebels

- Usman Khan
- Musa Khan
- Barah Bhuiyans (Twelve landlords/chiefs)

### Hindu Rulers Supporting Afghans

- Raja of Jessore
- Kamrup
- Cachar

---


## Islam Khan's Role (1608)


### Identity

- Grandson of Shaikh Salim Chishti.

### Measures

- Posted as Governor of Bengal.
- Won over local zamindars.
- Shifted headquarters to **Dacca (Dhaka)**.
- Provincial capital moved:
    - Rajmahal → Dacca

---


## Campaign against Barah Bhuiyans


### Sonargaon Campaign

- Directed against Musa Khan.
- After 3 years:
    - Sonargaon captured.
    - Musa Khan surrendered.

### Defeat of Usman Khan

- Crushed Afghan resistance.

---


## Results

- Annexation of:
    - Jessore
    - Kamrup
- Mughal authority firmly established in East Bengal.

---


## Jahangir's Policy of Conciliation


### Measures

- Released many Afghan chiefs.
- Restored estates of Musa Khan.
- Inducted Afghans into Mughal nobility.

### Example

- **Khan-i-Jahan Lodi**
    - Prominent Afghan noble.

### Significance

- Peace restored in Bengal.
- Integration rather than suppression.

By 1622:

- Mewar pacified.
- Bengal pacified.
- Malik Ambar subdued.

### 1. Persian Capture of Qandhar

- Blow to Mughal prestige.

### 2. Jahangir's Declining Health

# 5. Nur Jahan


## Marriage

- Married Jahangir in 1611.

## Family


### Father


**Itimad-ud-Daula**

- Chief Diwan.
- Competent administrator.

### Brother


**Asaf Khan**

- Khan-i-Saman.
- Father-in-law of Shah Jahan.

---


## Nur Jahan's Position


### Titles

- **Badshah Begum**

### Privileges

- Coins issued in her name.
- Nobles consulted her.
- Influenced appointments.
- Dominated royal household.

---


## Cultural Contributions


### Promoted

- Persian art
- Persian culture
- Persian fashions

### Significance

- Persianization of Mughal court accelerated.

---


## Historical Debate


### Traditional View


Nur Jahan formed a political "Junta" with:

- Itimad-ud-Daula
- Asaf Khan
- Shah Jahan

### Revisionist View

- Jahangir remained in control till 1622.
- Nur Jahan influenced but did not dominate state policy.

**UPPCS Mains Theme:** Debate on extent of Nur Jahan's political power.

# 6. Shah Jahan's Rebellion (1622–1626)


## Immediate Cause


Refusal to lead campaign to recover Qandhar.


### Fear

- Court intrigues during absence.

### Demands

- Full military command.
- Control of Punjab.
- Control of major forts.

Jahangir rejected demands.


---


## Course


### Initial Success

- Support from:
    - Gujarat
    - Malwa
    - Deccan army

### Defeat

- Defeated near Delhi by:
    - **Mahabat Khan**

### Later Developments

- Captured:
    - Orissa
    - Bihar
    - Bengal

### Final Defeat

- Again defeated by Mahabat Khan.

---


## Settlement (1626)


### Terms

- Shah Jahan pardoned.
- Sons sent as hostages:
    - Dara Shikoh
    - Aurangzeb
- Assigned Deccan territory.

---


## Significance


### Exposed

- Weakness of succession system.
- Princes becoming alternative power centres.

---


# 7. Mahabat Khan's Revolt


## Background

- Felt sidelined after suppressing Shah Jahan.

## Event

- Seized Jahangir near River Jhelum.

### Nur Jahan's Response

- Escaped initially.
- Later surrendered strategically.
- Won nobles away from Mahabat Khan.

### Outcome

- Mahabat Khan fled.
- Joined Shah Jahan later.

---


## Significance


Demonstrated:

- Nur Jahan's political skill.
- Fragility of imperial authority.

# 16. Mansabdari System under Jahangir & Shah Jahan


## Key Changes


### Reduction in Sawar Salary


Akbar:

- ₹240 annually

Jahangir:

- ₹200 annually

---


## Du-Aspah Sih-Aspah System


### Introduced by


**Jahangir**


### Meaning

- Double cavalry obligation.

Example:


3000 Sawar rank


→ Maintain 6000 troopers.


---


## Shah Jahan's Reforms


### One-Third Rule


Example:


3000 Sawar


→ Maintain only 1000 cavalry.


### Month Scale


Ranks assigned:

- 10 month
- 8 month
- 6 month
- 5 month

Reduced obligations further.


---


## Consequences


### Positive

- Reduced financial burden.

### Negative

- Cavalry efficiency declined.
- Fewer remount horses.

### 5. Historiography Question


**"Was Nur Jahan the real ruler during Jahangir's reign?"**


Arguments For:

- Coins in her name.
- Political influence.
- Title Badshah Begum.

Arguments Against:

- Jahangir remained decision maker.
- Imperial orders still issued by Jahangir.
- No evidence of complete domination.

**Balanced Conclusion:** Nur Jahan was highly influential but not a sovereign ruler.
