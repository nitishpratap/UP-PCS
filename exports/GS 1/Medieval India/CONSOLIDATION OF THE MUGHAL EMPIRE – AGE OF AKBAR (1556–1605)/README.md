
# A. CHAPTER NOTES


# 1. AKBAR'S ACCESSION & SECOND BATTLE OF PANIPAT (1556)


## Early Life

- Born at **Amarkot (1542)** in Sindh when Humayun was in exile.
- Rana of Amarkot gave shelter to Humayun.
- Captured by uncle **Kamran**, later reunited with parents.
- Crowned at **Kalanaur (Punjab)** in **1556** at age **13 years 4 months**.

## Challenges Before Akbar

- Afghan resurgence under **Hemu (Hemchandra Vikramaditya)**.
- Kabul threatened.
- Sikandar Sur active in Siwalik region.
- Mughal authority confined largely around Delhi-Agra.

## Role of Bairam Khan

- Tutor and guardian of Akbar.
- Became **Wakil** with title **Khan-i-Khanan**.
- Reorganized Mughal army.

## Hemu

- Began career as market superintendent under Islam Shah.
- Became Wazir of Adil Shah Sur.
- Won **22 battles consecutively**.
- Held title **Vikramajit**.
- Captured Agra and Delhi.

## Second Battle of Panipat (5 Nov 1556)


### Causes

- Hemu's expansion.
- Mughal attempt to recover Delhi.

### Course

- Hemu initially dominated battle.
- Arrow struck his eye.
- Afghan army became leaderless.

### Result

- Hemu captured and executed.
- Mughal authority restored.
- Akbar virtually reconquered the empire.

# 2. EARLY PHASE: CONTEST WITH NOBILITY (1556–1567)


## Bairam Khan's Regency


Achievements:

- Controlled nobility.
- Secured Kabul.
- Extended empire:
    - Kabul → Jaunpur
    - Ajmer included
    - Gwalior captured

### Reasons for Fall

- Accused of favouring Shias.
- Arrogance.
- Ignored Akbar's growing maturity.

### Removal

- Akbar dismissed him through a farman.
- Bairam briefly rebelled.
- Finally surrendered.

### Akbar's Conduct

- Offered:
    - Court service
    - Provincial service
    - Pilgrimage to Mecca

### Death

- Assassinated at **Patan near Ahmedabad**.

### Legacy

- Son: **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan**
- Akbar raised him like his own child.

---


## Maham Anaga & Adham Khan

- Maham Anaga = foster mother.
- Adham Khan led Malwa campaign.
- Murdered Wazir.
- Akbar personally ordered his execution (1561).

---


## Uzbek Rebellion


### Centres

- Eastern UP
- Bihar
- Malwa

### Importance

- Serious challenge to imperial authority.

### Outcome

- Repeated rebellions crushed.
- Final defeat in 1567.
- Akbar emerged supreme.

---


## Mirza Hakim Crisis

- Half-brother of Akbar.
- Controlled Kabul.
- Advanced into Punjab.
- Supported by rebel Uzbeks.

### Outcome

- Akbar marched to Lahore.
- Rebellion crushed.
- Nobility subdued permanently.

# DAHSALA SYSTEM (1580)


### Introduced By


Akbar


### Based On


Data collected by:

- Karoris
- Qanungos

### Features

- Average produce of last 10 years.
- Average prices of last 10 years.
- State share = 1/3rd produce.
- Revenue fixed in cash.

---


# ZABTI SYSTEM


### Meaning


Measurement-based assessment.


### Area

- Lahore to Allahabad
- Gujarat
- Malwa

### Measurement


Bamboo rods linked by iron rings.


### Advantages

- Certainty for state and peasant.
- Remission during natural calamities.

## Historical Background


Akbar's religious policy did not emerge suddenly.


### Earlier Developments


During the 15th century many rulers:

- Patronized local languages.
- Translated Sanskrit works into Persian.
- Appointed Hindus in administration.
- Encouraged Hindu-Muslim cooperation.

### Bhakti–Sufi Influence


Important saints:

- **Kabir**
- **Chaitanya**
- **Guru Nanak**

Their teachings:

- Unity of God.
- Equality of humans.
- Criticism of ritualism.
- Religious harmony.

### Significance


Akbar inherited an atmosphere where:

- Religious tolerance was gaining legitimacy.
- Sectarian divisions were being questioned.
- Composite culture was emerging.

# 13. TAUHID-I-ILAHI (DIN-I-ILAHI): ADVANCED ANALYSIS


---


## Real Nature of Tauhid-i-Ilahi


### Important Historiographical Point


Traditional View (Badayuni):

- Akbar founded a new religion.
- Combination of:
    - Hinduism
    - Christianity
    - Islam
    - Zoroastrianism

Modern View:

- Not a religion.
- Not a church.
- Not a mass movement.
- A spiritual order of Sufi type.

---


## Meaning


**Tauhid-i-Ilahi**


= Divine Monotheism


---


## Membership


### Eligibility

- Only selected individuals.
- Admission required emperor's approval.

### Initiation Day


**Sunday**


### Initiation Ceremony


Novice


↓


Places head at emperor's feet


↓


Emperor raises him


↓


Receives spiritual formula (Shast)


↓


Becomes disciple (Murid)


---


## Shast (Initiation Formula)


Contained Akbar's favourite motto:


### "Allah-o-Akbar"


Meaning:


**God is Great**


(Modern historians reject the interpretation that it meant "Akbar is God").


---


## Ethical Principles


Members were expected to:

- Avoid meat as far as possible.
- Especially avoid meat during birth month.
- Give alms.
- Organize charity.
- Arrange feasts on birthdays.
- Follow ethical conduct.

---


## What Tauhid-i-Ilahi Did NOT Have


### No Holy Book


❌ No scripture


### No Priesthood


❌ No clergy


### No Temple/Mosque


❌ No place of worship


### No Ritual System


❌ No elaborate ceremonies


Only initiation ceremony existed.


---


## Number of Followers


### Core Group


About **18 members**


### Thousands of Murids


Many became disciples of Akbar.


### Important Fact


Most leading nobles refused.


Including:

- Almost all major Rajput nobles.

### Exception


**Birbal joined.**


---


## Political Purpose


Akbar wanted:


### Personally Loyal Nobility


Emperor


↓


Murids


↓


Supporters of Sulh-i-Kul


### Aim

- Strengthen monarchy.
- Reduce orthodoxy.
- Create ideological unity.

---


## Badayuni's Criticism


Badayuni claimed:

- Flatterers convinced Akbar he was:

    **Insan-i-Kamil (Perfect Man)**

- Introduced:

    **Pabos** (kissing floor before emperor)


Previously reserved for God.


---


## Historical Significance


### Success

- Personal loyalty strengthened.
- Nobility moved away from orthodoxy.
- Strengthened Sulh-i-Kul.

### Failure

- Died with Akbar.
- No institutional structure.
- No mass appeal.

Jahangir briefly continued practice of enrolling disciples.

# 15. SOCIAL REFORMS OF AKBAR


---


## Reform of Sati


### Akbar's Position


Did not completely abolish Sati.


But:

- Forced sati prohibited.
- Widow's consent essential.

### Additional Rule


Widows who:

- Were very young, or
- Had not lived with husbands

Could not be burnt.


---


## Widow Remarriage


### Legalized


Widow remarriage received official sanction.


---


## Polygamy


Akbar discouraged:

- Multiple wives.

Allowed mainly when:

- First wife was barren.

---


## Broader Social Vision


### Aim


Create:

- Humane society.
- Rational governance.
- Social justice.

---


# MAINS ENRICHMENT


## Why Translation Movement Was Important?


### Political


Integrated Hindu elites into empire.


### Cultural


Created composite Indo-Persian civilization.


### Intellectual


Reduced religious misunderstandings.


### Administrative


Enabled rulers to understand local traditions.


---


## Was Din-i-Ilahi a Failure?


### Institutionally


YES


Because:

- Died after Akbar.
- No mass following.

### Ideologically


NO


Because:

- Promoted tolerance.
- Strengthened Sulh-i-Kul.
- Encouraged interfaith dialogue.

---


## Historiographical Debate


### Badayuni

- Considered Akbar heretical.
- Saw Din-i-Ilahi as anti-Islamic.

### Abul Fazl

- Presented Akbar as enlightened ruler.
- Defender of universal harmony.

### Modern Historians

- Consider it a spiritual fellowship.
- Not a separate religion.
