
# 1. War of Succession (1657–1659)


## Background


### Problem of Succession

- No fixed law of succession among:
    - Muslims
    - Timurids
    - Indian rulers
- Traditions available:
    - **Nomination by ruler**
    - **Partition among sons**
- Neither system worked effectively.

### Examples

- **Tulsidas**: ruler could give _tika_ to any son.
- **Rana Sanga** fought brothers despite claims.

---


## Shah Jahan's Illness (1657)

- Shah Jahan became seriously ill at Delhi.
- Rumours spread that he had died.
- Dara Shikoh allegedly concealed reality.

### Four Princes


| Prince      | Region     |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| Dara Shikoh | Court/Agra |
| Shuja       | Bengal     |
| Murad       | Gujarat    |
| Aurangzeb   | Deccan     |


All prepared for war.


---


## Shah Jahan Supports Dara


### Measures

- Declared Dara as **Wali-Ahd (heir apparent)**.
- Mansab increased:
    - 40,000 zat → 60,000 zat
- Chair next to throne.
- Nobles ordered to obey Dara.

### Result


Instead of ensuring succession:

- Other princes felt discriminated.
- Intensified succession struggle.

---


# Reasons for Aurangzeb's Success


## Political Reasons

- Better leadership.
- Better military planning.
- Stronger alliances.
- Dara underestimated opponents.

## Military Reasons

- Aurangzeb's troops:
    - Experienced
    - Battle-hardened
    - Well-commanded

---


# Important Battles


## Battle of Dharmat


**15 April 1658**


### Forces

- Raja Jaswant Singh (for Dara)
vs
- Aurangzeb + Murad

### Result

- Victory of Aurangzeb.

### Significance

- Raised Aurangzeb's prestige.
- Demoralized Dara.

---


## Defeat of Shuja


### Battle near Banaras


**February 1658**

- Sulaiman Shikoh + Jai Singh defeated Shuja.

### Mistake

- Continued pursuit into Bihar.
- Left Agra inadequately defended.

---


## Rajput Politics


### Dara's Efforts


Approached:

- Jaswant Singh
- Rana of Udaipur

### Aurangzeb's Diplomacy


Promised Rana:

- Rank of 7000
- Return of parganas
- Religious freedom
- Favours like Rana Sanga

### Result

- Dara failed to secure Rajput support.

---


## Battle of Samugarh


**29 May 1658**


### Strength

- 50,000–60,000 troops on each side.

### Result

- Decisive victory of Aurangzeb.

### Why Dara Lost?

- Poor generalship.
- Weak hastily recruited army.
- Overconfidence.

---


## Shah Jahan Imprisoned


### Method


Aurangzeb:

- Cut off water supply to Agra Fort.

### Result

- Shah Jahan surrendered.
- Confined for 8 years.

### Jahanara Begum

- Stayed with father voluntarily.
- Later restored by Aurangzeb.
- Pension increased:
    - 12 lakh → 17 lakh rupees.

---


## Fate of Murad

- Initially ally of Aurangzeb.
- Imprisoned at Gwalior.
- Executed after 2 years.

---


## Fate of Dara


### Events

- Fled:
    - Lahore
    - Sindh
    - Gujarat
    - Ajmer

### Battle of Deorai


**March 1659**


Last major battle.


### Capture

- Betrayed by Afghan chief.
- Handed to Aurangzeb.

### Execution


Declared enemy:

- Of faith
- Of state
- Of public order

### Historical Interpretation

- Religion used as political justification.

---


## Fate of Shuja


### Battle of Khajwah


**December 1658**


Defeated by Aurangzeb.


### Outcome

- Escaped to Arakan.
- Killed there with family.

# Moral Regulations


## Measures


### Coins

- Kalma removed from coins.

### Nauroz

- Festival discontinued.

### Muhtasibs Appointed


Functions:

- Public morality
- Check gambling
- Prevent intoxication
- Monitor prostitution
- Check weights & measures

### Limitation


No interference in private life.

# Puritanical Measures (1669)


## Music


### Actions

- Court singing banned.
- Musicians pensioned off.

### Exceptions

- Instrumental music continued.
- Naubat continued.
- Music flourished privately.

### Important Fact


Maximum Persian works on Indian music written during Aurangzeb's reign.


### Aurangzeb

- Played Veena.

# PART F: READY-MADE MAINS CONTENT


## Introductions

1. Aurangzeb's reign represented both the territorial zenith and the beginning of the structural decline of the Mughal Empire.
2. The second half of the seventeenth century witnessed increasing tensions between imperial centralization and regional aspirations.
3. Aurangzeb inherited the strongest empire in India but left behind a polity burdened with military and administrative overextension.
4. The Mughal crisis under Aurangzeb emerged from the interaction of political, economic, administrative and religious factors.
5. The reign of Aurangzeb remains one of the most debated phases in Indian historiography.

---


## Conclusions

1. Aurangzeb's reign symbolizes the paradox of imperial expansion accompanied by institutional weakening.
2. The Mughal Empire reached its greatest geographical extent under Aurangzeb but lost much of its political cohesion.
3. Regional forces suppressed during Aurangzeb's lifetime ultimately emerged as successors to Mughal authority.
4. Religious policy alone cannot explain Mughal decline; structural and administrative factors were equally important.
5. The era demonstrates the limits of military power in sustaining a vast pre-modern empire.

---


## 10 Value-Added Points

- Territorial climax became administrative overstretch.
- War of succession institutionalized military succession.
- Rajput breach weakened traditional Mughal support.
- Jizyah was primarily political-ideological, not fiscal.
- Ahom resistance exposed limits of Mughal expansion.
- Sikh militarization accelerated after 1675.
- Jat revolt evolved from agrarian protest to state formation.
- Afghan revolts reflected regional aspirations.
- Aurangzeb combined Sharia with pragmatic Zawabit.
- Mughal decline originated during its apparent peak.
