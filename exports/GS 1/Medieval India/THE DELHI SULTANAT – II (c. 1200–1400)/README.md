# PART 1: CHAPTER NOTES


# Background: Decline of Balban’s Dynasty


### After Balban's Death (1286)

- Balban's chosen successor **Prince Mahmud** had already died fighting Mongols.
- Son **Bughra Khan** preferred ruling Bengal and Bihar.
- Balban's grandson placed on throne but proved weak.
- Growing resentment against:
    - Turkish monopoly over high offices.
    - Exclusion of non-Turks and Indian Muslims.

### Factors Behind Rise of Khaljis

- **Khaljis** = Mixed Turkish-Afghan origin.
- Had served:
    - In Bengal and Bihar.
    - Against Mongols in northwest.
- Indian Muslims also sought higher offices.
- Balban's own rise showed that military leaders could seize power.

---


# THE KHALJI DYNASTY (1290–1320)


## Jalaluddin Khalji (1290–1296)


### Rise

- Led Khalji nobles.
- Overthrew weak successors of Balban in **1290**.
- Ended Turkish monopoly in administration.

### Policies


### Political Philosophy


First Sultan to openly argue:

- State should rest on **consent of governed**.
- Since most Indians were Hindus:
    - India could not be a purely Islamic State.

### Administrative Policy

- Tolerant approach.
- Avoided harsh punishments.
- Tried conciliation with nobility.

### Limitation

- Considered weak by contemporaries.
- Policy reversed by Alauddin.

---


# Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316)


## Accession

- Murdered uncle and father-in-law Jalaluddin.
- Looted **Deogir** before accession.
- Used treasure to buy support of nobles and soldiers.

---


## Consolidation of Power


### Rebellions Faced

- Disgruntled nobles.
- Relatives.
- Mongol converts in Delhi.

### Measures


### Harsh Punishments

- Rebels executed.
- Properties confiscated.

### Mongol Converts

- Demanded larger share of Gujarat loot.
- Rebellion crushed.
- Massacre ordered.

### Regulations on Nobility


Nobles forbidden to:

- Hold feasts.
- Organize gatherings.
- Form marriage alliances without permission.

### Social Restrictions

- Ban on wine and intoxicants.

### Spy System

- Extensive intelligence network.

---


## Impact


### Positive

- Nobility completely subordinated.
- No rebellion during reign.

### Negative

- Old nobility destroyed.
- New nobility became opportunistic.
- Succession instability increased.

---


## After Alauddin


### Malik Kafur

- Installed Alauddin's minor son.
- Blinded/imprisoned other princes.

### Khusrau Khan

- Hindu convert.
- Became Sultan.
- Accepted by:
    - Nobility.
    - Delhi population.
    - Nizamuddin Auliya.

### Significance


Shows:

- Decline of racial exclusiveness.
- Expansion of social base of nobility.

# EXPERIMENT 1: TRANSFER OF CAPITAL


## Delhi → Daulatabad


### Reasons

- Better control over Deccan.
- Strategic location.

### Measures

- Officials shifted.
- Sufis moved.
- Road built.
- Rest houses established.

### Problems

- Distance: 1500 km+
- Summer migration.
- Hardships and deaths.

### Failure

- Could not govern North India from Daulatabad.

### Long-Term Significance

- Cultural integration of North and South.
- Spread of Indo-Islamic culture in Deccan.

# READY-MADE MAINS INTRODUCTIONS


### Intro 1


The period between 1290 and 1412 marks the transformation of the Delhi Sultanate from a regional kingdom into a pan-Indian empire under the Khaljis and Tughlaqs.


### Intro 2


The Khalji and Tughlaq rulers combined military expansion with significant administrative experimentation, shaping medieval Indian polity.


### Intro 3


The Delhi Sultanate reached its territorial zenith under Muhammad bin Tughlaq but simultaneously sowed the seeds of political fragmentation.


### Intro 4


Alauddin Khalji's reforms represent one of the earliest examples of state-directed economic regulation in medieval India.


### Intro 5


The history of the Khaljis and Tughlaqs reflects the tension between imperial expansion and administrative sustainability.


---


# READY-MADE CONCLUSIONS


### Conclusion 1


The Khalji-Tughlaq phase represents both the peak and the beginning of the decline of the Delhi Sultanate.


### Conclusion 2


Despite failures, the administrative innovations of Alauddin and Muhammad Tughlaq influenced later rulers such as Sher Shah and Akbar.


### Conclusion 3


The disintegration of the Sultanate was rooted not merely in individual failures but in structural political and administrative challenges.


### Conclusion 4


The period deepened North-South interaction and accelerated cultural integration across the subcontinent.


### Conclusion 5


The Khaljis and Tughlaqs left a lasting legacy in administration, military organization, agrarian policy and statecraft.
