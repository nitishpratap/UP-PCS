# 1. POLITICAL SITUATION AFTER TIMUR'S INVASION (1398)


## Causes of Fragmentation of Delhi Sultanate

- Weakening of **Tughlaq dynasty**
- **Timur's invasion (1398)** devastated Delhi.
- Tughlaq ruler fled Delhi.
- Provincial governors and local rulers declared independence.

## Independent States that Emerged


### East

- **Bengal**
- **Jaunpur**

### West

- **Gujarat**
- **Malwa**
- **Sindh**
- **Multan**

### Rajputana

- Rajput states became independent after expulsion of Muslim governor from Ajmer.

---


## Balance of Power System


### Western India


Three powers checked each other:

- Gujarat
- Malwa
- Mewar

### Eastern India


Bengal checked by:

- Gajapati rulers of Orissa
- Jaunpur

### North India

- Lodis vs Jaunpur for control of Ganga-Yamuna valley.

---


## Importance of Malwa


### Why Malwa became the "Cockpit of North India"?

- Strategic location.
- Whoever controlled Malwa could dominate North India.

### Political Development

- Jaunpur annexed by Lodis.
- Lodis expanded into:
    - Eastern Rajasthan
    - Malwa

### Consequence


Three-way rivalry:

- Gujarat
- Mewar
- Lodis

### Historical Significance

- Rana Sanga allegedly invited Babur.
- Expected destruction of Lodis would make Mewar supreme.

---

# PART F: READY-MADE MAINS CONTENT


## Introductions


### Intro 1


The fifteenth century marked the transition from a centralized Delhi Sultanate to a regionalized political order characterized by competing kingdoms such as Bengal, Gujarat, Malwa, Mewar and Jaunpur.


### Intro 2


The post-Timur period witnessed the emergence of powerful regional states which shaped the political and cultural landscape of North India before the advent of the Mughals.


### Intro 3


Regional kingdoms of the fifteenth century represented both political fragmentation and remarkable cultural creativity.


---


## Conclusions


### Conclusion 1


Though regional powers failed to achieve lasting political unity, they laid the foundations of cultural synthesis and administrative experimentation later refined by the Mughals.


### Conclusion 2


The struggle among Gujarat, Malwa, Mewar and the Lodis ultimately paved the way for Babur's conquest and the rise of Mughal supremacy.


### Conclusion 3


The fifteenth century demonstrates that political decentralization does not necessarily hinder cultural growth and social integration.


---


## 10 Value-Added Points for Answers

1. Malwa was the **geopolitical pivot of North India**.
2. Bengal became a major centre of **maritime trade with China**.
3. Gujarat emerged as India's leading commercial state.
4. Jaunpur developed an independent architectural tradition.
5. Mewar became the foremost Rajput power under Rana Kumbha and Rana Sanga.
6. Sikandar Lodi laid foundations for later Mughal administration.
7. Zain-ul-Abidin anticipated Akbar's policy of tolerance.
8. Regional courts promoted vernacular languages.
9. Sanskrit-Persian translations accelerated cultural exchange.
10. The period reveals simultaneous trends of political rivalry and cultural synthesis.
