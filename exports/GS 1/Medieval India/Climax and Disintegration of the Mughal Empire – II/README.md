# PART 1: THE RISE OF THE MARATHAS


## Background


### Position of Marathas before Shivaji

- Marathas held important positions in:
    - Ahmadnagar Sultanate
    - Bijapur Sultanate
- Used extensively by **Malik Ambar** as military auxiliaries.
- Influential Maratha families:
    - **Mores**
    - **Ghatages**
    - **Nimbalkars**
- Unlike Rajputs, Marathas lacked a large consolidated state.

### Role of Shahji Bhonsale

- Father of Shivaji.
- Acted as kingmaker in Ahmadnagar.
- Opposed Mughal expansion.
- Treaty of 1636 forced him to yield territories.
- Joined Bijapur service.
- Built semi-independent base at **Bangalore**.
- Created political foundation for Shivaji.

# Afzal Khan Episode (1659)


## Bijapur's Response


Sent:


### Afzal Khan

- Premier noble
- 10,000 troops

### Event

- Personal meeting arranged.
- Both expected treachery.
- Shivaji killed Afzal Khan.

- Bijapuri army routed.
- Artillery captured.
- Panhala conquered.
- Expansion into:
    - South Konkan
    - Kolhapur

- Made Shivaji legendary.
- Massive recruitment into Maratha army.
- Even Afghan mercenaries joined him.

# TREATY OF PURANDAR (1665)


## Mughal Commander


### Raja Jai Singh of Amber


### Strategy

- Isolate Shivaji diplomatically.
- Attack Fort Purandar.

### Terms


### Forts


Out of 35 forts:


### To Mughals

- 23 forts
- Revenue = 4 lakh huns

### To Shivaji

- 12 forts
- Revenue = 1 lakh huns

### Territorial Grants


Granted:

- Bijapuri Konkan (4 lakh huns)
- Future Balaghat conquests (5 lakh huns)

### Payment

- Shivaji to pay 40 lakh huns.

### Mansab

- Sambhaji granted:
    - Mansab of 5000

- Persuaded by Jai Singh.

## Incident

- Felt insulted.

### Result

- Walked out of court.
- Detained.
- Escaped dramatically.

# Overview: Three Phases of Aurangzeb's Deccan Policy


```plain text
Phase I (1658–1668)
↓
Recover Ahmadnagar territories from Bijapur

Phase II (1668–1684)
↓
Contain Marathas through Bijapur-Golconda cooperation

Phase III (1684–1687)
↓
Direct conquest of Bijapur and Golconda
```


---


# Why Deccan Became Important?


### Treaty of 1636


Shah Jahan:

- Gave one-third Ahmadnagar territory to Bijapur.
- Promised not to annex Bijapur and Golconda.

### Later Change


Shah Jahan himself abandoned this policy.


### Consequences


✔ Destroyed trust in Mughal promises


✔ Made Deccan states suspicious


✔ Prevented united front against Marathas


**Exam Theme:**


Failure of diplomacy created long-term strategic problems for Mughals.


---


# PHASE I (1658–1668)


## Main Objectives


### Problem 1


Growing power of Shivaji


### Problem 2


Recovery of territories ceded to Bijapur


---


## Mughal Gains


Already acquired:

- Bidar
- Kalyani

### 1660


Parenda obtained by bribery.


### Remaining Objective


Sholapur


---


# Role of Jai Singh


### Advice to Aurangzeb


Famous statement:

> "It would be unwise to attack both these fools at the same time."

Meaning:

- Do not fight Shivaji and Bijapur simultaneously.

---


# Jai Singh's Larger Vision


### Strategic Thought


```plain text
Defeat Bijapur
↓
Control Deccan
↓
Control Karnataka
↓
Solve Maratha Problem
```


### Important Historiographical Point


Jai Singh understood much earlier than Aurangzeb:


**Maratha problem could not be solved without a comprehensive Deccan policy.**

# Failure of Bijapur Campaign (1665)


## Reasons


### 1. Deccan Unity


Golconda aided Bijapur.


### 2. Guerrilla Warfare


Deccanis:

- Avoided direct battle.
- Destroyed supplies.

### 3. No Siege Guns


Jai Singh could not storm Bijapur.


### 4. Lack of Resources


Aurangzeb unwilling to commit full resources.


---


# Consequences


✔ Campaign failed


✔ No territory gained


✔ No money gained


✔ Jai Singh died (1667)


---


### 1668


Sholapur acquired through bribery.


**End of First Phase**
