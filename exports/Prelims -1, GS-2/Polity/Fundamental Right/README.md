## 7. RIGHT TO EQUALITY (Art 14–18)


| Art | Provision                |
| --- | ------------------------ |
| 14  | Equality before law      |
| 15  | No discrimination        |
| 16  | Equality in employment   |
| 17  | Abolishes untouchability |

### ARTICLE 14 – Equality Before Law


| Concept                             | Source  |
| ----------------------------------- | ------- |
| Equality before law (negative)      | Britain |
| Equal protection of laws (positive) | USA     |


**Reasonable classification allowed if:** (1) Intelligible differentia (2) Rational nexus with objective


**Rule of Law (Dicey):** ① No arbitrary power ② Equality before law ③ Legal spirit – Basic Structure


**Exceptions to Art 14:** Art 361 (President/Governor immunity) | Art 105,194 (Parliament/State privileges) | Art 361A (Publication immunity) | Art 31C (DPSP laws) | Diplomatic immunity


### ARTICLE 15 – No Discrimination


**Cannot discriminate on:** Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of birth (word "only" matters)


**Access to:** Shops, restaurants, hotels, wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads – cannot be denied


**Affirmative action allowed for:** Women, Children, SEBC, SC/ST


**93rd Amendment (2005):** Reservation in private institutions (except minority institutions)


**OBC Reservation (2006):** 27% in IITs, IIMs, Central Universities – SC upheld (2008) with **Creamy Layer exclusion**


**Creamy Layer:** Children of constitutional post holders | Group A/B officers | Armed forces (Colonel+) | Professionals | Business persons | Large landowners | High income


### ARTICLE 16 – Equality in Public Employment


**No discrimination on:** Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Descent, Place of birth, Residence


**Exceptions:** Residence requirement (Parliament) | Reservation for OBC/SC/ST if inadequately represented | Religious institutions (e.g., temple priest)


**Mandal Commission (1979, Morarji Desai, Chairman B.P. Mandal, Art 340):** 3743 castes = 52% population → recommended **27% OBC reservation** (Total SC+ST+OBC = 50%)


**Implementation:** 1990 V.P. Singh | 1991 Narasimha Rao


**Indra Sawhney Case (1992 – Mandal Case):** ① 27% OBC valid ② Creamy layer exclusion ③ No reservation in promotions ④ **50% ceiling** ⑤ Carry forward allowed


**Reservation Amendments:**


| Amendment   | Provision                       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- |
| 77th (1995) | Promotion reservation for SC/ST |
| 81st (2000) | Backlog vacancies               |
| 85th (2001) | Consequential seniority         |
| 76th (1994) | TN 69% reservation              |


### ARTICLE 17 – Untouchability abolished (punishable)


**Law:** Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 (renamed 1976) – Penalty: 6 months + ₹500 fine


### ARTICLE 18 – Titles abolished


**Prohibited:** Rai Bahadur, Maharaja, Dewan Bahadur | **Allowed:** Military, Academic | **National awards:** Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri – cannot be prefix/suffix | **History:** Started 1954, abolished 1977, revived 1980

## 8. RIGHT TO FREEDOM (Art 19–22)


### ARTICLE 19 – Six Freedoms (Citizens only)


| Clause   | Freedom                      | Restrictions (Art 19(2))                                                                                    |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 19(1)(a) | Speech & Expression          | Sovereignty, security, foreign relations, public order, decency, morality, contempt, defamation, incitement |
| 19(1)(b) | Assembly (peaceful, unarmed) | Sovereignty, public order                                                                                   |
| 19(1)(c) | Association                  | Sovereignty, public order, morality                                                                         |
| 19(1)(d) | Movement                     | Public interest, tribal protection                                                                          |
| 19(1)(e) | Residence                    | Public interest, tribal protection                                                                          |
| 19(1)(g) | Profession/Trade/Business    | Public interest regulation                                                                                  |


**Speech includes:** Press, propagate, commercial ads, telecast, against tapping, right to know, against censorship, silence, demonstration | **Does NOT include:** Strike


**Does NOT include:** Right to strike (explicitly not a fundamental right)


**Includes specific:** Right to know government activities | Right against political bandh


**Assembly:** CrPC Sec 144 (emergency ban), IPC Sec 141 (5+ persons = unlawful)


**Association:** Right to form/continue/not join – BUT recognition NOT a FR | Right to strike NOT a FR


### ARTICLE 20 – Criminal protections (Citizens + Foreigners + Companies)


| Protection                 | Meaning                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ex-post-facto (20(1))      | No retrospective punishment (criminal only – not civil/tax) |
| Double jeopardy (20(2))    | No prosecution & punishment twice for same offence          |
| Self-incrimination (20(3)) | No compulsion to be witness                                 |


**Self-incrimination does NOT apply to:** Fingerprints, thumb impressions, signatures, blood samples, body examination


### ARTICLE 21 – Right to Life

> No person deprived of life/personal liberty except **procedure established by law**

| Phase    | Case                 | Interpretation                       |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Narrow   | A.K. Gopalan (1950)  | Literal procedure                    |
| Expanded | Maneka Gandhi (1978) | Due process – fair, just, reasonable |


**Right to life = life with human dignity (not animal existence)**


**Derived rights (SC expanded):** Dignity | Privacy | Livelihood | Shelter | Health | Environment | Free legal aid | Speedy trial | Against solitary confinement/handcuffing/custodial violence | Travel abroad | Fair trial | Reputation | Social security | Sleep | Against noise pollution | Electricity | Emergency medical care


### ARTICLE 21A – Right to Education (86th Amendment 2002)


Free & compulsory education for **6–14 years** | Also modified Art 45 & added Art 51A(k) | RTE Act 2009


### ARTICLE 22 – Protection against arrest


**Ordinary arrest rights:** Know grounds | Consult lawyer | Produce before magistrate within **24 hours** | No detention beyond 24 hrs without magistrate | **Not available to:** Enemy aliens, preventive detention detainees


**Preventive detention safeguards:** Max 3 months without advisory board | Board includes HC judges | Grounds communicated | Right to representation | **44th Amendment** (2 months – not implemented, so 3 months continues)


**Legislative power:** Parliament (defence, foreign, security) | Parliament+States (public order, supplies)

## 9. RIGHT AGAINST EXPLOITATION (Art 23–24)


| Article | Prohibition                                                         | Applies to State + Private persons |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **23**  | Human trafficking, Begar (forced labour without pay), Forced labour | Yes                                |
| **24**  | Child labour below 14 in factories, mines, hazardous work           | Yes                                |


**Trafficking includes:** Prostitution, Devadasi, slavery, sale of humans – Law: Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act 1956


**Forced labour:** Physical/legal/economic coercion | Laws: Bonded Labour Abolition 1976, Minimum Wages 1948, Contract Labour 1970, Equal Remuneration 1976


**Exception to Art 23:** State can impose compulsory service (military, disaster) – no discrimination


**Child labour laws:** Child Labour Act 1986 | Child & Adolescent Labour Act 2016 – Ban below 14 | 14-18 banned from hazardous work | Penalty: 6m-2y + ₹20k-50k (first), 1-3y (repeat) | SC 1996: ₹20,000 per child worker into Rehabilitation Fund

## 12. RIGHT TO CONSTITUTIONAL REMEDIES (Art 32)


**Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called it "Heart and Soul of the Constitution"** – Direct access to SC


| Provision                           | Details       |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Right to approach SC                | Yes           |
| SC can issue writs                  | Yes           |
| Parliament may empower other courts | Yes           |
| Can be suspended during emergency   | Under Art 359 |


### FIVE WRITS (from English system)


| Writ          | Meaning           | Purpose                                                 |
| ------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Habeas Corpus | Produce body      | Release illegal detainee (any citizen can file)         |
| Mandamus      | We command        | Order public authority (not President/Governor/private) |
| Prohibition   | To forbid         | Stop lower court (before decision)                      |
| Certiorari    | To certify        | Quash lower court order (after decision)                |
| Quo Warranto  | By what authority | Challenge illegal public office holding                 |


### Art 32 vs Art 226


| Feature      | Art 32 (SC)       | Art 226 (HC)      |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ----------------- |
| Scope        | Only FR           | FR + legal rights |
| Nature       | Fundamental Right | Not FR            |
| Jurisdiction | Original          | Original (wider)  |

# ARTICLE 14 — EQUALITY BEFORE LAW


Provision:

> State shall not deny **equality before law** and **equal protection of laws**.

Applies to:

- Citizens
- Foreigners
- Legal persons

---


### Sources


| Concept                  | Source      |
| ------------------------ | ----------- |
| Equality before law      | **Britain** |
| Equal protection of laws | **USA**     |


---


### Equality Before Law


Negative concept.


Meaning:

- No special privileges
- Equal subjection to law
- Nobody above law

---


### Equal Protection of Laws


Positive concept.


Meaning:

- Equal treatment under equal circumstances
- Like persons treated alike

---


### Reasonable Classification


Allowed if:

1. **Intelligible differentia**
2. **Rational nexus with objective**

Not allowed:

- Arbitrary
- Artificial
- Evasive classification

---


# RULE OF LAW (A.V. Dicey)


Three elements:

1. Absence of arbitrary power
2. Equality before law
3. Predominance of legal spirit

In India:

- First two fully apply
- Third partially applies

Rule of Law = **Basic Structure**


---

# FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (PART III) 


---


### Constitutional Position

- **Part III of the Constitution**
- **Articles 12–35**

Fundamental Rights are inspired by the **Bill of Rights of the USA Constitution**.


Part III is often called the **Magna Carta of India** because it guarantees extensive civil liberties.


---


### Why They Are Called Fundamental


They are fundamental because:

1. Guaranteed and protected by the **Constitution (fundamental law of the land)**
2. Essential for **all-round development of individuals**
    - Material
    - Intellectual
    - Moral
    - Spiritual

---


### Purpose of Fundamental Rights


Fundamental Rights aim to:

- Promote **political democracy**
- Protect **liberty and freedom of people**
- Prevent **authoritarian rule**
- Limit **arbitrary action of the state**
- Ensure **government of laws, not of men**

They uphold:

- Equality of individuals
- Dignity of individuals
- Unity of the nation
- Public interest

---


# 2. ORIGINAL FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS


Originally the Constitution contained **7 Fundamental Rights**

1. Right to Equality (Art 14–18)
2. Right to Freedom (Art 19–22)
3. Right Against Exploitation (Art 23–24)
4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25–28)
5. Cultural and Educational Rights (Art 29–30)
6. Right to Property (Art 31)
7. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32)

### Change after 44th Amendment (1978)

- **Right to Property removed from FR**
- Now a **Legal Right under Article 300A**
- Located in **Part XII**

Therefore **presently there are 6 Fundamental Rights**


---


# 3. FEATURES OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS


### 1. Available to Citizens and Non-Citizens


Some rights → **Citizens only**


Some rights → **All persons**


Persons include:

- Citizens
- Foreigners
- Legal persons (companies, corporations)

---


### 2. Not Absolute


Fundamental Rights are **qualified rights**.


State can impose **reasonable restrictions**.


Courts decide whether restrictions are **reasonable or not**.


Purpose:


Balance between:

- Individual liberty
- Social control

---


### 3. Available Mainly Against State


Most Fundamental Rights protect individuals against **arbitrary action of the State**.


However some rights are enforceable against **private individuals** also.


Examples:

- Article 17
- Article 23

---


### 4. Negative and Positive Rights


**Negative Rights**


Limit powers of state


Example


Article 14


**Positive Rights**


Grant privileges to citizens


---


### 5. Justiciable


Citizens can approach courts if rights are violated.


This means:


They are **legally enforceable**.


---


### 6. Supreme Court is the Guardian


Citizens can directly approach **Supreme Court under Article 32**.


Supreme Court defends Fundamental Rights.


---


### 7. Can be Amended


Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights through **Constitutional Amendment**.


But cannot violate **Basic Structure of Constitution**.


---


### 8. Suspension During National Emergency


Fundamental Rights can be suspended during **National Emergency**.


Exceptions:

- Article 20
- Article 21

These cannot be suspended.


Also:


Article 19 suspended only when emergency due to:

- War
- External aggression

Not during **armed rebellion**.


---


### 9. Limited by Articles 31A, 31B, 31C


These articles protect certain laws from being challenged under FR.


---


### 10. Restricted for Armed Forces


Parliament may restrict FR for:

- Armed forces
- Police forces
- Intelligence agencies
- Paramilitary forces

(Article 33)


---


### 11. Restricted During Martial Law


Martial Law = Military rule during abnormal conditions.


Parliament may restrict FR during martial law.


(Article 34)

Certain rights require law made by **Parliament only**.


Not by state legislatures.

# ARTICLE 14 – EQUALITY BEFORE LAW


State shall not deny to any person:

- Equality before law
- Equal protection of laws

Applicable to:

- Citizens
- Foreigners
- Legal persons

---


### Sources


Equality before law → **British concept**


Equal protection of laws → **American Constitution**


---


### Equality Before Law (Negative Concept)


Means:

1. No special privileges
2. Equal subjection to law
3. Nobody above law

---


### Equal Protection of Laws (Positive Concept)


Means:

1. Equal treatment under equal circumstances
2. Same laws applied to similar persons
3. Like should be treated alike

---


### Reasonable Classification Allowed


Article 14 allows **reasonable classification**.


But not:

- Arbitrary
- Artificial
- Evasive

Must be based on **intelligible differentia**.


---


# RULE OF LAW (A.V. DICEY)


Three elements:

1. Absence of arbitrary power
2. Equality before law
3. Primacy of rights of individuals

In India:


First two elements apply.


Third does not fully apply because **Constitution is source of rights**.


---


### Rule of Law = Basic Structure


Supreme Court held Rule of Law is **basic feature of Constitution**.


---


# EXCEPTIONS TO EQUALITY BEFORE LAW


Article 14 is not absolute.


Exceptions include:


### 1. President and Governor Immunity (Article 361)

- Not answerable to courts
- No criminal proceedings during tenure
- No arrest
- Civil proceedings require 2 months notice

---


### 2. Parliamentary Publication Immunity


True reports of parliamentary proceedings cannot be prosecuted.


(Article 361A)


---


### 3. Parliamentary Privileges


MPs immune for:

- Speech
- Vote

(Article 105)


---


### 4. State Legislature Privileges


MLAs immune for speech and vote.


(Article 194)


---


### 5. Article 31C


Laws implementing **Directive Principles under Article 39(b) and (c)** cannot be challenged under Article 14.


**Article 39(b):** Focuses on the "ownership and control of the material resources of the community" being distributed in a manner that best serves the common good.


**Article 39(c):** Focuses on operating the economic system to ensure that "the concentration of wealth and means of production" does not occur to the common detriment.


---


### 6. Diplomatic Immunity


Foreign rulers and diplomats immune from legal proceedings.


---


### 7. UN Immunity


United Nations and agencies enjoy immunity.

# ARTICLE 15 — PROHIBITION OF DISCRIMINATION


### Constitutional Provision


Article 15 states:

> The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of:
- Religion
- Race
- Caste
- Sex
- Place of birth

Two key words in Article 15:


### 1. Discrimination


Means making **adverse distinction** or treating someone **unfavourably**.


### 2. Only


The word **only** implies that discrimination is prohibited **only on these grounds**.


If discrimination occurs on **other valid grounds**, it may be permissible.


---


# SECOND PROVISION OF ARTICLE 15


No citizen shall be subjected to disability, restriction or condition regarding:


### 1. Access to public places

- Shops
- Public restaurants
- Hotels
- Places of entertainment

### 2. Use of public facilities

- Wells
- Tanks
- Bathing ghats
- Roads
- Public places funded by State

Important point:


| Clause           | Who is restricted           |
| ---------------- | --------------------------- |
| First provision  | State only                  |
| Second provision | State + Private individuals |


---


# EXCEPTIONS TO ARTICLE 15


Article 15 allows **protective discrimination (affirmative action)**.


### 1. Special provisions for women and children


State can provide:


Examples:

- Reservation for women in **local bodies**
- Free education schemes
- Welfare schemes for women

---


### 2. Special provisions for backward classes


State may provide benefits for:

- Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBC)
- Scheduled Castes (SC)
- Scheduled Tribes (ST)

Examples:

- Reservation in educational institutions
- Scholarships
- Fee concessions

---


### 3. Reservation in Educational Institutions (93rd Amendment)


The **93rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2005** inserted a provision allowing:


Special provisions for:

- SEBC
- SC
- ST

for admission to **educational institutions**, including **private institutions**.


Exception:


Minority educational institutions are excluded.


---


### Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006


Provides:


**27% reservation for OBCs** in central institutions such as:

- IITs
- IIMs
- Central universities

---


### Supreme Court Judgment (2008)


The Supreme Court upheld:

- 93rd Amendment
- OBC Reservation Act

But ordered exclusion of **Creamy Layer**.


---


# CREAMY LAYER


Creamy layer refers to **advanced sections among OBCs** who are **not eligible for reservation benefits**.


Children of following categories belong to creamy layer:


---


### 1. Constitutional Post Holders


Children of:

- President
- Vice President
- Judges of Supreme Court
- Judges of High Courts
- Chairman/Members of UPSC
- Chairman/Members of State PSC
- CAG
- Chief Election Commissioner

---


### 2. Government Officers


Children of:

- Group A/Class I officers
- Group B/Class II officers

Also employees of:

- PSUs
- Banks
- Insurance companies
- Universities
- Private sector equivalent posts

---


### 3. Armed Forces


Children of officers of rank:

- Colonel and above

Including equivalent posts in:

- Navy
- Air Force
- Paramilitary forces

---


### 4. Professionals


Examples:

- Doctors
- Lawyers
- Engineers
- Authors
- Artists
- Consultants

---


### 5. Business People


Persons engaged in:

- Trade
- Business
- Industry

---


### 6. Large Landowners


Persons holding:

- Large agricultural land
- Urban property

---


### 7. Income Criteria


Persons with **annual income above prescribed limit**.


Historical limits:


| Year | Income limit |
| ---- | ------------ |
| 1993 | ₹1 lakh      |
| 2004 | ₹2.5 lakh    |
| 2008 | ₹4.5 lakh    |
| 2013 | ₹6 lakh      |


_(Later revised further, but Lakshmikanth text refers up to this point.)_

# ARTICLE 16 — EQUALITY OF OPPORTUNITY IN PUBLIC EMPLOYMENT


Article 16 provides:


**Equality of opportunity for all citizens in public employment.**


No discrimination in government jobs based on:

- Religion
- Race
- Caste
- Sex
- Descent
- Place of birth
- Residence

---


# EXCEPTIONS TO ARTICLE 16


---


### 1. Residence Requirement


Parliament can prescribe residence requirements for certain jobs.


Example:


Special provisions existed for:

- Andhra Pradesh
- Telangana

Earlier law:


**Public Employment (Requirement as to Residence) Act, 1957**


Expired in 1974.


---


### 2. Reservation for Backward Classes


State may provide reservation if a class is **not adequately represented in services**.


Applicable to:

- OBC
- SC
- ST

---


### 3. Religious Institutions


Certain posts related to religious institutions may require:

- Membership of that religion.

Example:


Temple priest must belong to that religion.


---


# MANDAL COMMISSION


### Appointment


1979


By **Morarji Desai Government**


Official name:


**Second Backward Classes Commission**


Chairman:


**B.P. Mandal**


Constitutional basis:


**Article 340**


---


### Findings


Commission identified:


**3743 castes** as socially and educationally backward.


Population share:


Approximately **52%** (excluding SC/ST).


---


### Recommendations


Recommended:


**27% reservation for OBCs in government jobs**


Total reservation proposed:

- SC
- ST
- OBC

Total = **50%**


---


# IMPLEMENTATION


### 1990


Prime Minister **V.P. Singh**


Implemented **27% reservation for OBCs**.


---


### 1991 Changes (Narasimha Rao Government)


Two changes:

1. Preference for **poorer OBCs**
2. Additional **10% reservation for economically weaker upper castes**

---


# MANDAL CASE (INDRA SAWHNEY CASE), 1992


Supreme Court judgement on reservation.


---


### Major rulings

1. **27% OBC reservation valid**
2. **Creamy layer must be excluded**
3. **No reservation in promotions**

Reservation only in **initial appointments**

1. **Reservation ceiling = 50%**

Except in extraordinary situations.

1. **Carry forward rule allowed**

But must respect 50% limit.

1. **Permanent body needed**

To examine inclusion/exclusion in OBC list.


---


# GOVERNMENT ACTION AFTER MANDAL CASE


---


### 1. Ram Nandan Committee (1993)


Identified **Creamy Layer categories**.


---


### 2. National Commission for Backward Classes


Established in **1993**.


Functions:

- Inclusion in OBC list
- Exclusion from OBC list

---


### 3. 77th Constitutional Amendment Act (1995)


Added provision allowing **reservation in promotions for SC/ST**.


---


### 4. 85th Amendment Act (2001)


Introduced:


**Consequential seniority** for SC/ST promotions.


---


### 5. 81st Amendment Act (2000)


Allowed **backlog vacancies** to exceed the **50% reservation ceiling**.


Backlog vacancies treated separately.


---


### 6. 76th Amendment Act (1994)


Placed **Tamil Nadu Reservation Act (69% reservation)** in **Ninth Schedule**.


Protected from judicial review.

# ARTICLE 17 — ABOLITION OF UNTOUCHABILITY


Article 17 abolishes **untouchability**.


Practice of untouchability is an **offence punishable by law**.


---


### Important Point


Untouchability here refers to **caste-based untouchability**.


Not social boycott.


---


# LAW IMPLEMENTING ARTICLE 17


**Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955**


Originally:


**Untouchability Offences Act, 1955**


Renamed in **1976**.


---


### Civil Right


Means any right accruing due to abolition of untouchability.


---


### Offences Under the Act


Punishable acts include:

1. Preventing entry into public worship places
2. Justifying untouchability
3. Denying access to shops or hotels
4. Insulting SC persons
5. Denying admission to hospitals or schools
6. Preaching untouchability
7. Refusing sale of goods/services

---


### Punishment

- Imprisonment up to **6 months**
- Fine up to **₹500**
- Or both

---


### Electoral Disqualification


Convicted persons cannot contest:

- Parliament elections
- State legislature elections

---


### Important Supreme Court Observation


Article 17 applies **against private individuals also**.


State must ensure enforcement.


---


# ARTICLE 18 — ABOLITION OF TITLES


Article 18 abolishes titles.


---


### Four Provisions

1. State cannot confer titles

    (except military or academic)

2. Citizens cannot accept titles from foreign states
3. Foreigners holding state office cannot accept foreign titles without President’s consent
4. No citizen holding government office can accept gifts or offices from foreign states without President’s consent

---


### Titles Abolished


Colonial titles like:

- Maharaja
- Rai Bahadur
- Rai Saheb
- Dewan Bahadur

---


### National Awards


Supreme Court (1996) upheld validity of:

- Bharat Ratna
- Padma Vibhushan
- Padma Bhushan
- Padma Shri

Reason:


They are **not hereditary titles**.


But:


They cannot be used as **prefixes or suffixes**.


Example:


❌ Bharat Ratna XYZ


✔ XYZ (Bharat Ratna awardee)


---


### Historical Note

- National Awards started in **1954**
- Abolished in **1977 (Morarji Desai government)**
- Revived in **1980 (Indira Gandhi government)**

---


# ARTICLE 19 — PROTECTION OF SIX FREEDOMS


Article 19 guarantees **six fundamental freedoms** to **citizens only**.


Originally there were **7 freedoms**, but:

- **Right to property removed by 44th Amendment (1978)**

Therefore now **six freedoms remain**.


---


# SIX FREEDOMS UNDER ARTICLE 19


Article 19(1) guarantees:

1. Freedom of **Speech and Expression**
2. Freedom of **Assembly**
3. Freedom of **Association**
4. Freedom of **Movement**
5. Freedom of **Residence**
6. Freedom of **Profession**

---


# IMPORTANT FEATURES


### Available only to citizens


Foreigners cannot claim these rights.


However, shareholders of companies (citizens) can claim them.


---


### Protection only against State


These freedoms protect citizens **only against State action**, not private individuals.


---


### Reasonable restrictions allowed


State can impose restrictions **only on grounds mentioned in Article 19 itself**.


Restrictions must be:

- Reasonable
- Legal
- Justified

Courts decide whether restrictions are reasonable.


---


# 1. FREEDOM OF SPEECH AND EXPRESSION


Article 19(1)(a)


Every citizen has the right to express:

- Views
- Opinions
- Beliefs
- Convictions

Methods include:

- Speech
- Writing
- Printing
- Pictures
- Any other communication method

---


# RIGHTS INCLUDED IN FREEDOM OF SPEECH


Supreme Court expanded Article 19 to include:

1. Right to propagate one's views
2. Freedom of the press
3. Freedom of commercial advertisements
4. Right against telephone tapping
5. Right to telecast
6. Right against political bandh
7. Right to know government activities
8. Freedom of silence
9. Right against pre-censorship
10. Right to demonstration or picketing

Important:


**Right to strike is NOT included.**


---


# RESTRICTIONS ON FREEDOM OF SPEECH


State can impose restrictions on grounds of:

1. Sovereignty and integrity of India
2. Security of the state
3. Friendly relations with foreign states
4. Public order
5. Decency
6. Morality
7. Contempt of court
8. Defamation
9. Incitement to offence

These restrictions are listed in **Article 19(2)**.


---


# 2. FREEDOM OF ASSEMBLY


Article 19(1)(b)


Citizens have the right to **assemble peacefully without arms**.


Includes:

- Public meetings
- Demonstrations
- Processions

---


# CONDITIONS


Assembly must be:

- Peaceful
- Unarmed

Violent or armed assemblies are **not protected**.


Right to strike is **not included**.


---


# RESTRICTIONS


State may impose restrictions on:

1. Sovereignty and integrity of India
2. Public order

---


# CRPC SECTION 144


Magistrate can prohibit assemblies if there is risk of:

- Danger to human life
- Obstruction
- Public disturbance
- Riot

---


# IPC SECTION 141


Assembly of **5 or more persons becomes unlawful** if objective is:

1. Resisting law
2. Forceful possession of property
3. Committing criminal trespass
4. Forcing illegal acts
5. Threatening government authority

---


# 3. FREEDOM OF ASSOCIATION


Article 19(1)(c)


Citizens have right to form:

- Associations
- Unions
- Co-operative societies

Examples:

- Political parties
- Trade unions
- Clubs
- Societies
- Partnership firms
- Companies

---


# RIGHTS INCLUDED

1. Right to form association
2. Right to continue association
3. Right not to join an association

---


# IMPORTANT POINT


Right to **recognition of association is not a fundamental right**.


---


# RESTRICTIONS


State may restrict this freedom on grounds of:

1. Sovereignty and integrity of India
2. Public order
3. Morality

---


# TRADE UNION RIGHTS


Supreme Court held:


Trade unions **do not have fundamental right to strike**.


Strike can be regulated by labour laws.


---


# 4. FREEDOM OF MOVEMENT


Article 19(1)(d)


Every citizen can move **freely throughout the territory of India**.


Purpose:

- Promote **national integration**
- Prevent **regionalism**

---


# DIMENSIONS OF MOVEMENT


Two dimensions:


### Internal movement


Movement inside India.


Protected by **Article 19**.


### External movement


Right to travel abroad.


Protected under **Article 21**.


---


# RESTRICTIONS


State may restrict movement on grounds of:

1. Interest of general public
2. Protection of Scheduled Tribes

Example:


Entry restrictions in **tribal areas**.


Purpose:

- Protect tribal culture
- Prevent exploitation

---


# COURT OBSERVATIONS


Supreme Court allowed restrictions on:

- Movement of prostitutes
- Movement of persons with contagious diseases (public health)

---


# 5. FREEDOM OF RESIDENCE


Article 19(1)(e)


Citizens have right to:

- Reside anywhere
- Settle anywhere in India

Two components:

1. Right to reside temporarily
2. Right to settle permanently

---


# PURPOSE


Remove internal barriers in India.


Promotes:

- National unity
- Social integration

---


# RESTRICTIONS


State may impose restrictions for:

1. Interest of general public
2. Protection of Scheduled Tribes

Example:


Restrictions on settlement in tribal areas.


---


# OVERLAP WITH MOVEMENT


Freedom of movement and residence are **closely related**.


Movement → travelling


Residence → staying


---


# 6. FREEDOM OF PROFESSION


Article 19(1)(g)


Every citizen has right to:

- Practice any profession
- Carry on any occupation
- Trade
- Business

---


# PURPOSE


Ensures freedom to earn **livelihood**.


---


# RESTRICTIONS


State may impose restrictions in **interest of general public**.


---


# STATE REGULATION


State can:


### 1. Prescribe qualifications


Example:

- Doctors
- Lawyers
- Engineers

Need licenses or qualifications.


---


### 2. Create State monopolies


Government may run:

- Industries
- Services
- Businesses

Either:

- Fully monopolized
- Partially monopolized

Citizens cannot challenge this.


---


# ACTIVITIES NOT PROTECTED


Immoral or dangerous activities are not protected.


Examples:

- Human trafficking
- Drug trade
- Explosives trade

State may prohibit or regulate them.


---
