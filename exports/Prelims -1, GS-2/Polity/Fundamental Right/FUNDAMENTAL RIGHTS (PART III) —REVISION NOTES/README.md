## 7. RIGHT TO EQUALITY (Art 14–18)


| Art | Provision                |
| --- | ------------------------ |
| 14  | Equality before law      |
| 15  | No discrimination        |
| 16  | Equality in employment   |
| 17  | Abolishes untouchability |
| 18  | Abolishes titles         |


### ARTICLE 14 – Equality Before Law


| Concept                             | Source  |
| ----------------------------------- | ------- |
| Equality before law (negative)      | Britain |
| Equal protection of laws (positive) | USA     |


**Reasonable classification allowed if:** (1) Intelligible differentia (2) Rational nexus with objective


**Rule of Law (Dicey):** ① No arbitrary power ② Equality before law ③ Legal spirit – Basic Structure


**Exceptions to Art 14:** Art 361 (President/Governor immunity) | Art 105,194 (Parliament/State privileges) | Art 361A (Publication immunity) | Art 31C (DPSP laws) | Diplomatic immunity


### ARTICLE 15 – No Discrimination


**Cannot discriminate on:** Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of birth (word "only" matters)


**Access to:** Shops, restaurants, hotels, wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads – cannot be denied


**Affirmative action allowed for:** Women, Children, SEBC, SC/ST


**93rd Amendment (2005):** Reservation in private institutions (except minority institutions)


**OBC Reservation (2006):** 27% in IITs, IIMs, Central Universities – SC upheld (2008) with **Creamy Layer exclusion**


**Creamy Layer:** Children of constitutional post holders | Group A/B officers | Armed forces (Colonel+) | Professionals | Business persons | Large landowners | High income


### ARTICLE 16 – Equality in Public Employment


**No discrimination on:** Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Descent, Place of birth, Residence


**Exceptions:** Residence requirement (Parliament) | Reservation for OBC/SC/ST if inadequately represented | Religious institutions (e.g., temple priest)


**Mandal Commission (1979, Morarji Desai, Chairman B.P. Mandal, Art 340):** 3743 castes = 52% population → recommended **27% OBC reservation** (Total SC+ST+OBC = 50%)


**Implementation:** 1990 V.P. Singh | 1991 Narasimha Rao


**Indra Sawhney Case (1992 – Mandal Case):** ① 27% OBC valid ② Creamy layer exclusion ③ No reservation in promotions ④ **50% ceiling** ⑤ Carry forward allowed


**Reservation Amendments:**


| Amendment   | Provision                       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- |
| 77th (1995) | Promotion reservation for SC/ST |
| 81st (2000) | Backlog vacancies               |
| 85th (2001) | Consequential seniority         |
| 76th (1994) | TN 69% reservation              |


### ARTICLE 17 – Untouchability abolished (punishable)


**Law:** Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 (renamed 1976) – Penalty: 6 months + ₹500 fine


### ARTICLE 18 – Titles abolished


**Prohibited:** Rai Bahadur, Maharaja, Dewan Bahadur | **Allowed:** Military, Academic | **National awards:** Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri – cannot be prefix/suffix | **History:** Started 1954, abolished 1977, revived 1980

## 8. RIGHT TO FREEDOM (Art 19–22)


### ARTICLE 19 – Six Freedoms (Citizens only)


| Clause   | Freedom                      | Restrictions (Art 19(2))                                                                                    |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 19(1)(a) | Speech & Expression          | Sovereignty, security, foreign relations, public order, decency, morality, contempt, defamation, incitement |
| 19(1)(b) | Assembly (peaceful, unarmed) | Sovereignty, public order                                                                                   |
| 19(1)(c) | Association                  | Sovereignty, public order, morality                                                                         |
| 19(1)(d) | Movement                     | Public interest, tribal protection                                                                          |
| 19(1)(e) | Residence                    | Public interest, tribal protection                                                                          |
| 19(1)(g) | Profession/Trade/Business    | Public interest regulation                                                                                  |


**Speech includes:** Press, propagate, commercial ads, telecast, against tapping, right to know, against censorship, silence, demonstration | **Does NOT include:** Strike


**Does NOT include:** Right to strike (explicitly not a fundamental right)


**Includes specific:** Right to know government activities | Right against political bandh


**Assembly:** CrPC Sec 144 (emergency ban), IPC Sec 141 (5+ persons = unlawful)


**Association:** Right to form/continue/not join – BUT recognition NOT a FR | Right to strike NOT a FR


### ARTICLE 20 – Criminal protections (Citizens + Foreigners + Companies)


| Protection                 | Meaning                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ex-post-facto (20(1))      | No retrospective punishment (criminal only – not civil/tax) |
| Double jeopardy (20(2))    | No prosecution & punishment twice for same offence          |
| Self-incrimination (20(3)) | No compulsion to be witness                                 |


**Self-incrimination does NOT apply to:** Fingerprints, thumb impressions, signatures, blood samples, body examination


### ARTICLE 21 – Right to Life

> No person deprived of life/personal liberty except **procedure established by law**

| Phase    | Case                 | Interpretation                       |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Narrow   | A.K. Gopalan (1950)  | Literal procedure                    |
| Expanded | Maneka Gandhi (1978) | Due process – fair, just, reasonable |


**Right to life = life with human dignity (not animal existence)**


**Derived rights (SC expanded):** Dignity | Privacy | Livelihood | Shelter | Health | Environment | Free legal aid | Speedy trial | Against solitary confinement/handcuffing/custodial violence | Travel abroad | Fair trial | Reputation | Social security | Sleep | Against noise pollution | Electricity | Emergency medical care


### ARTICLE 21A – Right to Education (86th Amendment 2002)


Free & compulsory education for **6–14 years** | Also modified Art 45 & added Art 51A(k) | RTE Act 2009


### ARTICLE 22 – Protection against arrest


**Ordinary arrest rights:** Know grounds | Consult lawyer | Produce before magistrate within **24 hours** | No detention beyond 24 hrs without magistrate | **Not available to:** Enemy aliens, preventive detention detainees


**Preventive detention safeguards:** Max 3 months without advisory board | Board includes HC judges | Grounds communicated | Right to representation | **44th Amendment** (2 months – not implemented, so 3 months continues)


**Legislative power:** Parliament (defence, foreign, security) | Parliament+States (public order, supplies)

## 9. RIGHT AGAINST EXPLOITATION (Art 23–24)


| Article | Prohibition                                                         | Applies to State + Private persons |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **23**  | Human trafficking, Begar (forced labour without pay), Forced labour | Yes                                |
| **24**  | Child labour below 14 in factories, mines, hazardous work           | Yes                                |


**Trafficking includes:** Prostitution, Devadasi, slavery, sale of humans – Law: Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act 1956


**Forced labour:** Physical/legal/economic coercion | Laws: Bonded Labour Abolition 1976, Minimum Wages 1948, Contract Labour 1970, Equal Remuneration 1976


**Exception to Art 23:** State can impose compulsory service (military, disaster) – no discrimination


**Child labour laws:** Child Labour Act 1986 | Child & Adolescent Labour Act 2016 – Ban below 14 | 14-18 banned from hazardous work | Penalty: 6m-2y + ₹20k-50k (first), 1-3y (repeat) | SC 1996: ₹20,000 per child worker into Rehabilitation Fund

## 12. RIGHT TO CONSTITUTIONAL REMEDIES (Art 32)


**Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called it "Heart and Soul of the Constitution"** – Direct access to SC


| Provision                           | Details       |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Right to approach SC                | Yes           |
| SC can issue writs                  | Yes           |
| Parliament may empower other courts | Yes           |
| Can be suspended during emergency   | Under Art 359 |


### FIVE WRITS (from English system)


| Writ          | Meaning           | Purpose                                                 |
| ------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Habeas Corpus | Produce body      | Release illegal detainee (any citizen can file)         |
| Mandamus      | We command        | Order public authority (not President/Governor/private) |
| Prohibition   | To forbid         | Stop lower court (before decision)                      |
| Certiorari    | To certify        | Quash lower court order (after decision)                |
| Quo Warranto  | By what authority | Challenge illegal public office holding                 |


### Art 32 vs Art 226


| Feature      | Art 32 (SC)       | Art 226 (HC)      |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ----------------- |
| Scope        | Only FR           | FR + legal rights |
| Nature       | Fundamental Right | Not FR            |
| Jurisdiction | Original          | Original (wider)  |

# ARTICLE 14 — EQUALITY BEFORE LAW


Provision:

> State shall not deny **equality before law** and **equal protection of laws**.

Applies to:

- Citizens
- Foreigners
- Legal persons

---


### Sources


| Concept                  | Source      |
| ------------------------ | ----------- |
| Equality before law      | **Britain** |
| Equal protection of laws | **USA**     |


---


### Equality Before Law


Negative concept.


Meaning:

- No special privileges
- Equal subjection to law
- Nobody above law

---


### Equal Protection of Laws


Positive concept.


Meaning:

- Equal treatment under equal circumstances
- Like persons treated alike

---


### Reasonable Classification


Allowed if:

1. **Intelligible differentia**
2. **Rational nexus with objective**

Not allowed:

- Arbitrary
- Artificial
- Evasive classification

---


# RULE OF LAW (A.V. Dicey)


Three elements:

1. Absence of arbitrary power
2. Equality before law
3. Predominance of legal spirit

In India:

- First two fully apply
- Third partially applies

Rule of Law = **Basic Structure**


---

| Diplomatic immunity                | International law |
| UN immunity                        | UN conventions    |

# WRITS (ENFORCEMENT MECHANISM)


Borrowed from **English legal system**.


Also called **Prerogative Writs**.


---


# FIVE WRITS


| Writ          | Meaning           | Purpose                        |
| ------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Habeas Corpus | Produce body      | Release illegal detainee       |
| Mandamus      | We command        | Order public authority         |
| Prohibition   | To forbid         | Stop lower court               |
| Certiorari    | To certify        | Quash lower court order        |
| Quo Warranto  | By what authority | Prevent illegal office holding |


---


# 1. HABEAS CORPUS


Purpose:


Protect personal liberty.


Court orders authority to produce detained person.


If detention illegal → **release**.


Petition can be filed by:

- Detained person
- Friend
- Relative
- Any citizen

---


# 2. MANDAMUS


Meaning:


**“We command.”**


Issued to:

- Public authorities
- Government officials
- Lower courts

Not issued against:

- President
- Governor
- Private persons

---


# 3. PROHIBITION


Issued by **higher court** to stop lower court from exceeding jurisdiction.


Issued **before decision**.


---


# 4. CERTIORARI


Issued to **quash order of lower court**.


Grounds:

- Lack of jurisdiction
- Illegal action
- Violation of natural justice

---


### Difference


| Prohibition            | Certiorari              |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Prevents future action | Quashes completed order |


---


# 5. QUO WARRANTO


Meaning:


**“By what authority?”**


Used to challenge illegal occupation of public office.


Conditions:


Office must be:

- Public
- Created by law

---

| Court        | Supreme Court           | High Court            |
| Scope        | Fundamental Rights only | FR + legal rights     |
| Nature       | Fundamental Right       | Not fundamental right |
| Jurisdiction | Original                | Original              |
| Scope        | Narrow                  | Wider                 |
