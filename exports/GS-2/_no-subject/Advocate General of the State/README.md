
# Advocate General of the State


## 1. Constitutional Provision

- The **office of Advocate General for the State** is provided by the Constitution under

    **Article 165**.

- The **Advocate General (AG)** is the **highest law officer of the state**.
- He performs functions for the **State Government similar to the Attorney General of India at the Union level**.

Thus,


| Position                  | Level | Article     |
| ------------------------- | ----- | ----------- |
| Attorney General of India | Union | Article 76  |
| Advocate General of State | State | Article 165 |


---


# 2. Appointment


The **Advocate General is appointed by the Governor of the State**.


However, like other constitutional positions, the **Governor acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers**.


---


# 3. Qualifications


To be appointed as Advocate General, a person **must be qualified to become a Judge of a High Court**.


Thus he must satisfy the following conditions:

1. Must be a **citizen of India**.
2. Must have either:
    - **Held a judicial office for 10 years**

    OR

    - **Been an advocate of a High Court for 10 years**

### Meaning of Judicial Office


Judicial office means **an office within the judicial service of the state**.


---


# 4. Term of Office


The **Constitution does NOT fix the tenure** of the Advocate General.


Also, the Constitution **does not mention the procedure or grounds for removal**.


Therefore:

- He **holds office during the pleasure of the Governor**.

### Meaning of Pleasure of Governor


This means:

- The **Governor can remove him at any time**.

However, in practice:

- The Governor acts **on the advice of the Council of Ministers**.

---


# 5. Resignation


The Advocate General **may resign anytime** by submitting his **resignation to the Governor**.


### Constitutional Convention


Generally:

- When the **state government (Council of Ministers) resigns or is replaced**,
- The **Advocate General also resigns**.

Reason:

- He is **appointed on the advice of the government**.

---


# 6. Remuneration


The **Constitution does NOT fix the salary or remuneration of the Advocate General**.


Instead:

- He receives **remuneration as determined by the Governor**.

---


# 7. Duties and Functions


The **Advocate General acts as the chief legal advisor of the State Government**.


His duties include:


### 1. Legal Advice to State Government


He **advises the Government of the State on legal matters** referred to him by the Governor.


Examples:

- Constitutional issues
- Legal interpretation
- Policy legality
- Litigation matters

---


### 2. Performs Legal Duties Assigned by the Governor


He performs **legal duties assigned by the Governor**.


These may include:

- Representing the state in courts
- Reviewing legal documents
- Legal consultation for government departments

---


### 3. Functions Assigned by Constitution or Law


He performs **functions assigned by:**

- The Constitution
- Any other law

Example:

- Appearing in constitutional matters involving the state.

---


# 8. Rights of Advocate General


While performing official duties, the Advocate General enjoys certain **special rights and privileges**.


---


## 1. Right to Appear in Courts


The Advocate General **can appear before any court of law within the state**.


This includes:

- High Court
- Subordinate Courts
- Tribunals

---


## 2. Right to Participate in State Legislature


The Advocate General has the **right to speak and participate in the proceedings of the State Legislature**.


He can participate in:

- **Both Houses of State Legislature** (if bicameral)
- **Legislative Assembly**
- **Legislative Council (if present)**

He can also participate in:

- **Committees of the State Legislature**.

However,


### He does NOT have the right to vote.


---


## 3. Privileges and Immunities


The Advocate General enjoys:

- **Privileges**
- **Immunities**

similar to those **available to members of the State Legislature**.


These are provided under **Article 194**.


---

# 14. Legislative Rights of Advocate General


Under **Article 177**, the Advocate General has special legislative privileges.


He can:


✔ Speak in the **State Legislature**


✔ Participate in discussions


✔ Participate in **committees of the legislature**


But:


❌ **Cannot vote**


---


## Important Clarification


The Advocate General:

- **Is NOT a member of the legislature**
- But can **participate in proceedings**

This provision exists because:


➡ The government may require **legal clarification during legislative debates**.


---


# 15. Privileges and Immunities


Under **Article 194**, the Advocate General enjoys the same **privileges and immunities as members of the state legislature**.


These privileges ensure:

- Freedom of speech in the legislature
- Protection while performing official duties.

---


# 16. Nature of the Post


The Advocate General is:

- A **constitutional office**
- A **political-legal position**
- The **chief legal advisor of the state government**

However:


He is **not a full-time government servant**.


He may also:


✔ Continue private legal practice.


But he **cannot appear against the state government in court**.


---


# 17. Practical Role of Advocate General


In real governance, the Advocate General performs several important functions.


### 1. Represents State Government in Courts


He represents the state in cases involving:

- Constitutional issues
- Government policies
- Legislative validity
- Administrative disputes

Often appears in:

- High Court
- Supreme Court (in matters involving the state)

---


### 2. Advises Government Departments


Government departments consult the Advocate General on:

- Drafting legislation
- Legal interpretation
- Policy legality
- Administrative law issues

---


### 3. Legal Vetting of Bills


Before introducing important bills, the Advocate General may check:

- Constitutionality
- Legal validity
- Conflict with central laws

---

# 24. Constitutional Position in the State Executive


The **Advocate General is part of the State Executive structure**.


Under **Part VI of the Constitution (States)**, the executive consists of:

1. **Governor**
2. **Chief Minister**
3. **Council of Ministers**
4. **Advocate General**

Thus the Advocate General is the **legal pillar of the State Executive**.


Important constitutional reference:

- **Part VI – The States**
- **Chapter II – The Executive**
- **Article 165**

---


# 25. Nature of the Office


The Advocate General is:


✔ A **constitutional authority**


✔ The **highest legal officer of the state**


✔ The **legal advisor of the state government**


But he is **not a political executive** like:

- Chief Minister
- Ministers

His role is **advisory and legal**.


---


# 26. Relationship with the Governor


Although the **Governor appoints the Advocate General**, in reality the appointment happens:


➡ **On the advice of the Council of Ministers**


Thus practically:


State Government → Recommends → Governor appoints Advocate General.


---


# 27. Why the Constitution Created the Advocate General


The Constitution created this post for several reasons.


### 1. Legal Advice to Government


Governments regularly need legal advice regarding:

- Constitutional interpretation
- Validity of laws
- Administrative actions

The Advocate General provides **official legal guidance**.


---


### 2. Representation in Courts


The state government is involved in many cases like:

- Constitutional disputes
- Policy challenges
- Administrative matters

The Advocate General represents the **state's legal interests**.


---


### 3. Legislative Legal Assistance


While laws are debated in the legislature, legal questions may arise.


The Advocate General can:

- Clarify constitutional issues
- Provide legal interpretation.

---


# 28. Situations Where Advocate General Appears in Court


The Advocate General may appear in cases involving:

1. **State laws challenged in court**
2. **Constitutional validity of state legislation**
3. **State government policies**
4. **Inter-state disputes**
5. **Cases involving fundamental rights against the state**

These cases often occur in:

- **High Courts**
- **Supreme Court**

---


# 29. Advocate General and High Court


Since the Advocate General must have qualifications similar to a **High Court judge**, he usually has:

- Strong experience in constitutional law
- Experience in High Court practice

Therefore most Advocate Generals are **senior advocates of the High Court**.


---


# 30. Important Constitutional Limitation


The Advocate General:


❌ **Cannot vote in the State Legislature**


Reason:


He is **not an elected representative**.


This prevents **conflict between legal advisory role and legislative decision-making**.


---


# 31. Difference Between Advocate General and State Law Department


Students often confuse these.


### Advocate General

- Constitutional post
- Chief legal advisor
- Represents government in important cases

### Law Department of State

- Administrative department
- Drafts legislation
- Manages legal documentation

Thus:


Law Department → Administrative legal work


Advocate General → **Constitutional legal authority**
