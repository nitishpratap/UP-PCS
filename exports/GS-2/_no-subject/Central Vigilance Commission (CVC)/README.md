
# Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)


---


# 1. Introduction


**Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)** is the **apex vigilance institution** responsible for **preventing corruption in the Central Government.**


### Establishment

- Established in **1964**
- Created by **Executive Resolution of the Central Government**
- Recommendation by:
    - **Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption (1962-64)**

### Initial Status


Originally CVC was:

- **Not a Constitutional Body**
- **Not a Statutory Body**

### Statutory Status


Later Parliament passed:

- **Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003**

This **gave statutory status to CVC.**


---


# 2. Role as Whistleblower Authority


In **2004**, CVC was designated as the authority to handle **whistleblower complaints.**


Under:


**Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Informers’ Resolution (PIDPI)**


Popularly known as **Whistle Blowers Resolution**.


### Powers under this Resolution


CVC can:

1. Receive complaints about corruption
2. Investigate misuse of office
3. Protect whistleblower identity
4. Take action against **false or motivated complaints**

---


# 3. Nature of CVC


CVC is:

- **Apex vigilance institution**
- **Independent of executive control**
- Supervises **vigilance administration across the Central Government**

### Main Role


Monitoring and advising in matters related to:

- Vigilance (**सतर्कता**)
- Anti-corruption administration
- Disciplinary control

It advises government organisations in:

- Planning vigilance work
- Executing vigilance mechanisms
- Reviewing corruption cases
- Reforming vigilance systems

---


# 4. Composition of CVC


CVC is a **multi-member body**.


### Members

1. **Central Vigilance Commissioner**
    - Chairperson
2. **Vigilance Commissioners**
    - Maximum **2 members**

---


# 5. Appointment


Appointed by:


**President of India**


Appointment is made on recommendation of a **3-member committee**.


### Appointment Committee


| Member                           | Position    |
| -------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Prime Minister                   | Chairperson |
| Union Home Minister              | Member      |
| Leader of Opposition (Lok Sabha) | Member      |


---


# 6. Tenure


Term of office:

- **4 years**

    OR

- Until **65 years of age**

(whichever is earlier)


### Post-Tenure Restriction


After retirement they **cannot accept employment under:**

- Central Government
- State Government

---


# 7. Removal of Central Vigilance Commissioner


The **President of India** can remove the Central Vigilance Commissioner or Vigilance Commissioners.


### Removal Grounds


They can be removed if:

1. **Insolvency (दिवालियापन)**
2. **Convicted for offence involving moral turpitude (अधमता)**
3. **Engages in paid employment outside duties**
4. **Infirmity (कमज़ोरी) of mind or body**
5. **Financial interest affecting official duties**

---


# 8. Removal on Grounds of Misbehaviour or Incapacity


In this situation:

1. **President refers the matter to the Supreme Court**
2. **Supreme Court conducts inquiry**
3. If Court recommends removal → **President removes the member**

### Misbehaviour Includes


If the member:

- Has interest in **government contracts**
- Gains **profit or benefit from such contracts**

---


# 9. Salary and Service Conditions


Salary and allowances:


| Position                       | Equivalent to    |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| Central Vigilance Commissioner | Chairman of UPSC |
| Vigilance Commissioners        | Members of UPSC  |


Important rule:


Conditions **cannot be altered to their disadvantage after appointment**.


---


# 10. Organisation of CVC


CVC functions through **three main organisational wings**.


### 1. Secretariat


Consists of:

- Secretary
- Joint Secretaries
- Deputy Secretaries
- Under Secretaries
- Other staff

---


### 2. Chief Technical Examiners Wing (CTE)


This is the **technical wing of CVC**.


Composition:

- Chief Engineers
- Supporting engineering staff

Chief Engineers are called:


**Chief Technical Examiners**


### Functions of CTE Wing

1. Technical audit of government construction works
2. Investigation of complaints relating to construction
3. Assistance to CBI in technical investigations
4. Advice to CVC and Chief Vigilance Officers in technical cases

---


### 3. Commissioners for Departmental Inquiries (CDIs)


CDIs act as **Inquiry Officers**.


Role:

- Conduct **oral inquiries**
- Handle **departmental proceedings against public servants**

---


# 11. Key Functions of CVC


The Central Vigilance Commission performs many anti-corruption functions.


### 1. Investigation of Corruption Cases


CVC can:

- Conduct inquiry
- Order investigation

When reference is made by **Central Government**


If public servant is accused under:


**Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988**


---


### 2. Inquiry Against Specific Officials


CVC investigates corruption allegations against:

- **All India Service members serving under Union**
- **Group A officers of Central Government**
- Specified officers of Central Government authorities

---


### 3. Superintendence Over CBI


CVC supervises:


**Delhi Special Police Establishment (CBI)**


But **only for offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988**


---


### 4. Direction to CBI


CVC can issue **directions to CBI** regarding investigation of corruption cases.


---


### 5. Review of Investigations


CVC reviews:

- Progress of **CBI investigations**
- Cases related to **Prevention of Corruption Act**

---


### 6. Review of Sanction for Prosecution


CVC reviews:


Applications pending with authorities for **prosecution sanction** in corruption cases.


---


### 7. Advisory Role


CVC advises:

- Central Government
- Government authorities

On vigilance matters.


---


### 8. Supervision of Vigilance Administration


CVC supervises vigilance administration in:

- Central ministries
- Government departments
- Central organisations

---


### 9. Inquiry into Whistleblower Complaints


CVC investigates complaints under:


**Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Informers Resolution (PIDPI)**.


---


### 10. Consultation on Vigilance Rules


The **Central Government must consult CVC** while framing rules regarding:

- Vigilance
- Disciplinary matters
- Central Services
- All India Services

---


# 12. Additional Functions of CVC


Apart from the core vigilance functions, the Central Vigilance Commission performs additional statutory responsibilities.


### 1. Selection of Director of Enforcement


The **Central Vigilance Commissioner (CVC)** acts as **Chairperson of the Selection Committee** that recommends the **Director of Enforcement**.


### Composition of Selection Committee


Members include:

1. Central Vigilance Commissioner – Chairperson
2. Two Vigilance Commissioners
3. Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs
4. Secretary, Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT)
5. Secretary, Department of Revenue (Ministry of Finance)

This committee recommends to the **Central Government** the appointment of:

- **Director of Enforcement**

It also recommends officers for **posts above Deputy Director level** in the Enforcement Directorate.


---


### 2. Authority under Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002


CVC is notified as a **specific authority to receive information relating to suspicious financial transactions** under:


**Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002**


---


# 13. Changes Made by Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013


The **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act (2013)** amended:

1. **Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003**
2. **Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946**

These amendments expanded the role of the CVC.


---


### 1. Appointment of Director of Prosecution (CBI)

- The **Director of Prosecution in CBI** is appointed by the **Central Government**
- Based on the **recommendation of the CVC**

---


### 2. Appointment of Senior Officers in CBI


A **selection committee chaired by CVC** recommends appointments of officers **above SP level in CBI (except Director CBI)**.


### Members of this committee

1. Central Vigilance Commissioner – Chairperson
2. Two Vigilance Commissioners
3. Secretary – Ministry of Home Affairs
4. Secretary – Department of Personnel & Training

---


### 3. Inquiry into Complaints Referred by Lokpal


The **CVC can conduct preliminary inquiries into complaints referred by Lokpal**.


These complaints may relate to officers belonging to:

- Group A
- Group B
- Group C
- Group D

For this purpose, a **Directorate of Inquiry** is established within the CVC.


---


### Handling of Inquiry Reports


| Officer Category | Action                                                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Group A & B      | Report sent to Lokpal                                 |
| Group C & D      | CVC conducts further investigation and decides action |


---


# 14. Jurisdiction of CVC


The jurisdiction of CVC covers several categories of officials.


### 1. Central Government Officers

- Members of **All India Services serving in Union affairs**
- **Group A officers** of Central Government

---


### 2. Public Sector Banks


Officers of:

- **Scale V and above**

---


### 3. Financial Institutions


Officers of **Grade D and above** in:

- **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**
- **NABARD**
- **SIDBI**

---


### 4. Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)


For **Schedule A and B PSUs**


Jurisdiction includes:

- Chief Executives
- Board-level executives
- Officers of **E-8 and above**

---


For **Schedule C and D PSUs**


Jurisdiction includes:

- Chief Executives
- Board executives
- Officers of **E-7 and above**

---


### 5. Insurance Sector


Includes:


**General Insurance Companies**

- Managers and above

**Life Insurance Corporation (LIC)**

- Senior Divisional Managers and above

---


### 6. Government Societies and Local Authorities


Officers drawing salary of:

- **₹8700 per month (pre-revised) or above**
- Under **Central Government DA pattern**

This applies to organisations:

- Owned by Central Government
- Controlled by Central Government

---


# 15. Working of the CVC


### Headquarters


CVC operates from:


**New Delhi**


---


### Procedural Powers


The Commission has the authority to:

- Regulate its own procedure
- Conduct inquiries independently

---


### Civil Court Powers


CVC has **powers similar to a civil court**.


It can:

- Summon individuals
- Require documents
- Call reports from government authorities

Its proceedings therefore have a **judicial character**.


---


### Supervision of Anti-Corruption Work


CVC can request information from:

- Central Government
- Government departments
- Government organisations

This helps it supervise **vigilance and anti-corruption work**.


---


### Advice to Government


After inquiry reports are received from investigating agencies:


CVC advises:

- Central Government
- Relevant authorities

About **appropriate action** to be taken.


---


### If Government Disagrees with CVC


If the government does not agree with the advice of the CVC:


It must:

- Record reasons in writing
- Communicate those reasons to the CVC.

---


### Annual Report


CVC submits an **Annual Report to the President of India**.


The **President places this report before both Houses of Parliament.**


---


# 16. Vigilance Units in Ministries


Every ministry or department in the **Union Government** has a vigilance mechanism.


### Chief Vigilance Officer (CVO)


Each ministry has a:


**Chief Vigilance Officer (CVO)**


The CVO:

- Heads the **Vigilance Division**
- Assists the **Secretary or Head of Office**

---


### Role of CVO


CVO acts as a **link between three institutions**:

1. The Ministry/Department
2. Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)
3. Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)

---


### Functions of Chief Vigilance Officer

1. Collect intelligence regarding corrupt practices
2. Investigate verifiable allegations
3. Process investigation reports
4. Forward cases to disciplinary authority
5. Refer cases to CVC when required

---


# 17. Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2011


### Background


The Government introduced:


**Public Interest Disclosure and Protection to Persons Making the Disclosure Bill, 2010**


in the **Lok Sabha on 26 August 2010**.


---


### Parliamentary Process

1. Referred to **Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee**
2. Government amended the bill
3. Renamed:

**Whistle Blowers Protection Bill, 2011**


---


### Passage of Bill


| Stage                 | Date             |
| --------------------- | ---------------- |
| Passed by Lok Sabha   | 27 December 2011 |
| Passed by Rajya Sabha | 21 February 2014 |
| President Assent      | 9 May 2014       |


Thus it became the **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2011**.


---


# 18. Features of Whistle Blowers Protection Act


### 1. Protection of Whistleblowers


The Act protects the **identity of whistleblowers**.


Whistleblower = person who exposes corruption.


---


### 2. Encourages Disclosure


Encourages citizens to report:

- Corruption
- Misuse of power
- Irregularities by public servants

---


### 3. Complaint Authority


Complaints must be made to:


**Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)**


However, the government may designate another authority.


---


### 4. Punishment for False Complaints


Penalty includes:

- Up to **2 years imprisonment**
- Fine up to **₹30,000**

---


### 5. Good Faith Requirement


Disclosure must be made in **good faith**.


The complainant must provide:

- Declaration stating information is **substantially true**.

---


### 6. Form of Disclosure


Complaints can be made:

- In writing
- Through email

They must include:

- Full details
- Supporting documents

---


### 7. Anonymous Complaints


No action will be taken if:

- Identity of complainant is not given
- Identity of accused is incorrect

---


### 8. Exclusions


The Act does **not apply to**:

- National security matters
- Armed forces
- Special Protection Group (SPG)
- Jammu & Kashmir (as per original law)
