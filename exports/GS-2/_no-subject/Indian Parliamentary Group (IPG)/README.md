
## INDIAN PARLIAMENTARY GROUP (IPG)

---


# 1. RATIONALE / BACKGROUND OF IPG


Explained by **M.N. Kaul & S.L. Shakdher**


### Core Idea


Growing **global interdependence** requires **cooperation among national parliaments** to strengthen democracy and address international issues.


---


### Need for IPG


| Purpose                               | Explanation                                                               |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Promote Inter-Parliamentary Relations | National parliaments must maintain links with foreign parliaments         |
| Protect Democracy                     | Legislators across nations cooperate to safeguard democratic institutions |
| Address Global Problems               | Security, economic crises, development, peace, stability                  |
| Exchange of Ideas                     | Sharing parliamentary practices and experiences                           |
| Interaction of Parliamentary Systems  | Older vs younger parliaments, different institutional systems             |
| Frank Discussions                     | Parliamentary dialogue more open than diplomatic conferences              |
| Learning from Parliamentary Problems  | Issues faced in one parliament may emerge elsewhere                       |


---


### Mechanism for Parliamentary Diplomacy


India maintains relations through:

- Exchange of **parliamentary delegations**
- **Goodwill missions**
- **Correspondence**
- **Exchange of parliamentary documents**

These activities are coordinated through **Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)**.


---


### International Role of IPG


IPG acts as:


1️⃣ **National Group of Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU)**


2️⃣ **India Branch of Commonwealth Parliamentary Association (CPA)**


---

---


# 1. Rationale of the Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)


The rationale behind the formation of the Indian Parliamentary Group was explained by **M.N. Kaul and S.L. Shakdher**.


### Main Idea


The establishment and development of relations among parliaments is an important activity of national parliaments.


Due to increasing **global interdependence**, cooperation between parliaments has become more necessary.


### Reasons for Creating Such a Group

1. **Promoting Inter-Parliamentary Relations**
    - National parliaments must maintain relations with other parliaments around the world.
2. **Safeguarding Democracy**
    - Parliamentarians from different nations should work together to protect democratic institutions.
3. **Addressing Global Challenges**
    - Countries face similar global issues such as:
        - Security
        - Development
        - Economic crises
        - Peace and stability
4. **Forum for Exchange of Ideas**
    - Parliamentarians from different countries can meet and discuss common problems.
    - This leads to:
        - Exchange of experiences
        - Cross-fertilisation of ideas
5. **Interaction Between Different Parliamentary Systems**
    - Interaction occurs between:
        - Older parliaments
        - Younger parliaments
        - Different parliamentary systems
6. **More Frank Discussion than Government Conferences**
    - Although inter-governmental conferences discuss global issues,
    - Discussions among legislators are **more open and frank**.
7. **Sharing Experiences of Parliamentary Problems**
    - A problem faced by one parliament today may confront another tomorrow.
    - Hence coordination and learning are necessary.

### Link Between Parliaments


India maintains connections with foreign parliaments through:

- Exchange of delegations
- Goodwill missions
- Correspondence
- Parliamentary documents

This coordination is conducted through the **Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)**.


### IPG's International Role


The IPG acts as:

1. **National Group of the Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU)**
2. **India Branch of the Commonwealth Parliamentary Association (CPA)**

---


# 2. Composition of the Indian Parliamentary Group


### Nature of the Body

- **IPG is an autonomous body.**

### Formation

- Formed in **1949**.

### Legal Origin

- Formed following a motion adopted by the **Constituent Assembly (Legislative)**.

### Motion Adoption Date

- **16 August 1948**

---


## Membership


### Regular Members


Membership is open to:

- **All Members of Parliament**

This includes:

- Lok Sabha members
- Rajya Sabha members

---


### Associate Members


Eligible persons:

- **Former Members of Parliament**

They can join as **associate members**.


However, they have **limited rights**.


### Restrictions on Associate Members


Associate members:

1. Cannot represent India in:
    - IPU meetings
    - CPA conferences
2. Cannot receive certain **travel concessions** provided to regular members by CPA branches.

---


# 3. Office Bearers of the IPG


### President

- **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**
- Serves as **ex-officio President**

### Vice Presidents


Two ex-officio vice presidents:

1. **Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha**
2. **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha**

### Secretary-General

- **Secretary-General of Lok Sabha**
- Acts as **ex-officio Secretary-General of IPG**

---


# 4. Objectives of the Indian Parliamentary Group


The IPG aims to promote parliamentary interaction and knowledge among parliamentarians.


### Major Objectives


### 1. Promote Personal Contacts

- Develop relationships among **Members of Parliament in India**.

---


### 2. Study Questions of Public Importance


The group studies important issues that may arise before Parliament.


Activities include:

- Seminars
- Discussions
- Orientation courses

The group also publishes material to disseminate information among members.


---


### 3. Arrange Lectures


Lectures are organised on issues related to:

- Politics
- Defence
- Economy
- Social issues
- Education

Speakers may include:

- Members of Parliament
- Distinguished personalities.

---


### 4. Organise Foreign Visits


The group arranges visits to foreign countries in order to:

- Build contacts with members of foreign parliaments
- Promote parliamentary diplomacy.

---


# 5. Functions of the Indian Parliamentary Group


The IPG performs several important functions.


---


## 1. Link Between Indian Parliament and Other Parliaments


IPG acts as a **bridge between the Parliament of India and foreign parliaments**.


This connection is maintained through:

- Exchange of delegations
- Goodwill missions
- Correspondence
- Exchange of documents.

---


## 2. Role in International Parliamentary Organisations


The IPG performs two important international roles.


It acts as:

1. **National Group of the Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU)**
2. **Main Branch of the Commonwealth Parliamentary Association (CPA) in India**

---


## 3. Addresses by Foreign Leaders


The IPG arranges:

- Addresses to MPs by **visiting Heads of State**
- Addresses by **Heads of Government**
- Talks by eminent personalities.

---


## 4. Organising Seminars and Symposia


Seminars are organised on **parliamentary topics of current importance**.


These seminars may be held:

- Nationally
- Internationally

---


## 5. Assistance to Members Visiting Abroad


Members travelling abroad receive:

- Letters of introduction to:
    - Secretaries of National Groups of the **IPU**
    - Secretaries of **CPA branches**

Additionally:

- Indian diplomatic missions in the concerned countries are informed so they can provide support.

---


## 6. Eligibility for Parliamentary Delegations


Only those MPs can join foreign parliamentary delegations who:

- Have been members of the IPG for **at least 6 months** before the delegation is formed.

---


## 7. Information Dissemination


IPG publishes:


**IPG Newsletter**


Features:

- Published **quarterly**
- Sent to:
    - Members
    - Associate members

This newsletter keeps them informed about the activities of the group.


---


## 8. Outstanding Parliamentarian Award


An award called **Outstanding Parliamentarian Award** was instituted.


### Year of Institution


**1995**


### Selection Process

- A committee of **five persons**
- Constituted by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha**
- The committee invites and finalises nominations.

---


## 9. Parliamentary Friendship Groups


To promote bilateral relations, IPG establishes **Parliamentary Friendship Groups** with other countries.


### Objectives

- Promote political relations
- Strengthen social contacts
- Encourage cultural exchanges
- Exchange information regarding parliamentary activities.

### Composition


Each Friendship Group consists of **22 Members of Parliament**:

- **15 from Lok Sabha**
- **7 from Rajya Sabha**

Representation is based on **party strength in both Houses**.


### Leadership


The **Speaker of Lok Sabha appoints**:

- President
- Two Vice Presidents
    - One from Lok Sabha
    - One from Rajya Sabha

# 6. Indian Parliamentary Group and Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU)


### Nature of IPU


The **Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU)** is:

- An **international organisation of parliaments of sovereign states**.

### Membership

- Consists of **153 parliaments of sovereign nations**.

---


### Objectives of IPU

1. Promote **peace and cooperation among peoples**
2. Strengthen **representative institutions**
3. Encourage **exchange of parliamentary experiences**
4. Improve **working of democratic institutions**
5. Discuss **international issues of common concern**

---


### Activities of IPU


IPU promotes:

- Contacts among parliamentarians
- Coordination between parliaments
- Exchange of parliamentary experience

It also expresses opinions on:

- Important international questions
- Parliamentary reforms
- Strengthening international institutions.

---


## Advantages of IPG Membership (related to IPU)

1. Helps Indian MPs establish contacts with foreign parliamentarians.
2. Allows members to study reforms and political changes in other countries.
3. Provides opportunities to meet foreign parliamentarians during visits.
4. Allows participation in **Inter-Parliamentary Conferences** abroad.

---


### Representation in IPU


Members of the Indian Parliamentary Group have held positions such as:

- Office bearers in IPU committees
- Rapporteurs
- Chairpersons of drafting committees.

This allows India to effectively present its viewpoint on international issues.
