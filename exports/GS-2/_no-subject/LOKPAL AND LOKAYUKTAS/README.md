# 1. GLOBAL SCENARIO & NEED FOR OMBUDSMAN


## Welfare State → Expansion of Administration


Modern democratic states are **Welfare States**, therefore:

- Govt role in socio-economic development ↑
- Bureaucracy expanded
- Administrative discretion increased

### Consequences


Excessive administrative power leads to:

- Harassment
- Maladministration
- Corruption
- Abuse of authority
- Delay & arbitrariness

Hence, citizens need:


✔ Grievance redressal


✔ Administrative accountability


✔ Control over misuse of power

# 1. GLOBAL SCENARIO


Modern democratic states are **welfare states**.


Because of welfare orientation:

- Government role in **socio-economic development increased**
- Bureaucracy expanded
- Administrative processes multiplied
- Administrative discretion of civil servants increased

### Consequences


Excess administrative power leads to:

- Harassment
- Maladministration
- Corruption
- Abuse of authority

This leads to **citizens’ grievances against administration**.


### Importance in Democracy


Success of democracy depends on:


✔ Redressal of citizens’ grievances


✔ Administrative accountability


✔ Control over misuse of administrative power


Therefore many countries created **institutional mechanisms for grievance redressal**.


### Major Institutional Mechanisms

1. **Ombudsman System**
2. **Administrative Courts System**
3. **Procurator System**

---


# 2. OMBUDSMAN SYSTEM


## Origin

- Originated in **Sweden**
- Established in **1809**
- First democratic institution for **citizen grievance redressal**

### Meaning


"Ombud" = representative or spokesman of another person.


According to **Donald C. Rowat**:

> Ombudsman is an officer appointed by legislature to handle complaints against administrative and judicial actions.

---


# 3. FUNCTIONS OF SWEDISH OMBUDSMAN


The Swedish Ombudsman deals with complaints related to:


### 1. Abuse of Administrative Discretion


Misuse of official authority.


Example:

- Arbitrary decisions
- Misuse of power

### 2. Maladministration


Inefficiency in administration.


Example:

- Delay
- Non-performance of duties

### 3. Administrative Corruption


Demanding bribes to perform official duties.


### 4. Nepotism


Favoring relatives or friends in:

- Employment
- Promotions
- Contracts

### 5. Discourtesy


Misbehaviour by officials such as:

- Abusive language
- Harassment
- Unprofessional conduct

---


# 4. APPOINTMENT OF OMBUDSMAN


In Sweden:

- Appointed by **Parliament**
- Tenure = **4 years**

### Removal


Removed by Parliament if it **loses confidence** in him.


### Reporting

- Submits **annual report to Parliament**
- Hence called **Parliamentary Ombudsman**

However:


✔ Independent of legislature


✔ Independent of executive


✔ Independent of judiciary


---


# 5. POWERS OF OMBUDSMAN


The Ombudsman:

- Supervises **compliance of laws**
- Ensures **public officials perform duties properly**

It keeps watch over:

- Civil officials
- Judicial officials
- Military officials

Ensures that they function:

- Impartially
- Objectively
- According to law

---


# 6. LIMITATIONS OF OMBUDSMAN


Despite wide powers, it has limitations.


The Ombudsman **cannot**:

- Reverse administrative decisions
- Quash administrative orders
- Directly control administration
- Punish officials directly

### However


It **can prosecute erring officials**, including judges.


Punishment is imposed by **competent authorities**, not by Ombudsman.


---


# 7. INITIATION OF INVESTIGATION


The Ombudsman can act in two ways:


### 1. Complaint Based


Citizen files complaint against:

- Unfair administrative action

### 2. Suo Moto


Ombudsman can start investigation **on its own initiative**.


---


# 8. KEY CHARACTERISTICS OF OMBUDSMAN SYSTEM


Important exam points.


Features include:


### 1. Independence from Executive


Ensures neutrality and fairness.


### 2. Impartial Investigation


Objective examination of complaints.


### 3. Suo Moto Powers


Can initiate investigation independently.


### 4. Access to Administrative Files


Unrestricted access to government records.


### 5. Reports to Legislature


Ensures **administrative accountability to legislature**.


### 6. Publicity


Working widely reported through:

- Press
- Media

### 7. Citizen Friendly System


Complaint process is:

- Simple
- Informal
- Cheap
- Speedy

# 1. ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS COMMISSION (ARC) RECOMMENDATION


The **Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC)** recommended the establishment of:

- **Lokpal**
- **Lokayukta**

### ARC Details


| Feature    | Detail                                                                             |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ARC tenure | 1966–1970                                                                          |
| Chairman   | Morarji Desai                                                                      |
| Report     | Special Interim Report on **Problems of Redressal of Citizens’ Grievances (1966)** |


Purpose:


To establish a **mechanism to investigate complaints against public officials and ministers**.


---


# 2. STRUCTURE SUGGESTED BY ARC


ARC recommended **two authorities**.


### Lokpal


Deals with complaints against:

- Ministers
- Secretaries

At:

- Central level
- State level

---


### Lokayukta


Deals with complaints against:

- Other higher officials

ARC recommended:

- One Lokayukta at Centre
- One Lokayukta in each state

---


### Judiciary


ARC recommended:

- Judiciary should **remain outside Lokpal and Lokayukta jurisdiction**

Example:

- Similar to **New Zealand model**

But in **Sweden**, judiciary **is under Ombudsman**.


---


# 3. APPOINTMENT OF LOKPAL (ARC RECOMMENDATION)


According to ARC:


Lokpal should be appointed by **President** after consultation with:

1. **Chief Justice of India**
2. **Speaker of Lok Sabha**
3. **Chairman of Rajya Sabha**

---


# 4. FEATURES RECOMMENDED BY ARC


ARC recommended the following characteristics for Lokpal and Lokayukta.


### 1. Independence and Impartiality


They should be:

- Independent
- Neutral
- Non-political

---


### 2. Private and Informal Proceedings


Investigations should be:

- Conducted privately
- Informal in nature

Reason:


To avoid political controversy.


---


### 3. Non-Political Appointment


Appointment process should minimize **political influence**.


---


### 4. Status


Their status should be comparable to:


**Highest judicial functionaries of the country**


---


### 5. Matters Covered


They should deal with matters in the **discretionary field** involving:

- Injustice
- Corruption
- Favouritism

---


### 6. No Judicial Interference


Their proceedings should **not be subject to judicial interference**.


---


### 7. Access to Information


They should have **maximum powers** to obtain:

- Documents
- Files
- Information

---


### 8. No Expectation from Executive


They should not expect:

- Benefits
- Pecuniary advantages

from the government.


---

# 1. OBJECTIVE OF THE ACT


The **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013** aims to:

- Establish **Lokpal at the Centre**
- Establish **Lokayukta in States**
- Create a **uniform anti-corruption framework in India**

It deals with complaints against **public functionaries including high-level officials**.


---


# 2. ESTABLISHMENT OF LOKPAL


The Act establishes **Lokpal at the Central level**.


Lokpal functions as an **anti-corruption ombudsman**.


It investigates allegations of corruption against:

- Prime Minister
- Ministers
- Members of Parliament
- Government officials

---


# 3. COMPOSITION OF LOKPAL


Lokpal consists of:


| Position    | Number    |
| ----------- | --------- |
| Chairperson | 1         |
| Members     | Maximum 8 |


Thus:


Maximum **9 members total**.


---


### Judicial Members


At least **50% of members must be judicial members**.


---


### Reservation in Membership


**50% of members must belong to:**

- SC
- ST
- OBC
- Minorities
- Women

This ensures **social representation**.


---


# 4. SELECTION COMMITTEE


The **Chairperson and Members of Lokpal** are appointed through a **Selection Committee**.


### Composition


| Member                                                  | Position    |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Prime Minister                                          | Chairperson |
| Speaker of Lok Sabha                                    | Member      |
| Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha                       | Member      |
| Chief Justice of India (or nominee Supreme Court judge) | Member      |
| Eminent jurist nominated by President                   | Member      |


---


# 5. SEARCH COMMITTEE


A **Search Committee** assists the Selection Committee.


Role:

- Prepare panel of eligible candidates.

### Representation Rule


50% members must belong to:

- SC
- ST
- OBC
- Minorities
- Women

---


# 6. PRIME MINISTER UNDER LOKPAL


The **Prime Minister is included under Lokpal jurisdiction**.


However:


There are **certain subject-matter exclusions and special procedures** while handling complaints against PM.


---


# 7. JURISDICTION OF LOKPAL


Lokpal jurisdiction covers **all public servants**.


### Includes


| Category              | Included |
| --------------------- | -------- |
| Prime Minister        | Yes      |
| Ministers             | Yes      |
| Members of Parliament | Yes      |
| Group A officers      | Yes      |
| Group B officers      | Yes      |
| Group C officers      | Yes      |
| Group D employees     | Yes      |


Thus **entire central bureaucracy** comes under Lokpal.


---


# 8. ROLE OF CENTRAL VIGILANCE COMMISSION (CVC)


Lokpal may refer complaints to **CVC**.


### For Group A & B officers

- CVC conducts **preliminary inquiry**
- Sends report back to **Lokpal**

Lokpal then decides further action.


---


### For Group C & D employees


CVC can:

- Proceed independently under **CVC Act**

But Lokpal:

- Reviews and monitors the process.

---


# 9. POWERS OVER CBI


Lokpal has **superintendence and direction** over:


**Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)**


But only in **cases referred by Lokpal**.


This strengthens **independent investigation**.


---


# 10. APPOINTMENT OF CBI DIRECTOR


The **Director of CBI** is selected by:


A **High-Powered Committee chaired by the Prime Minister**.


---


# 11. ATTACHMENT AND CONFISCATION OF PROPERTY


Lokpal Act allows:

- **Attachment of property**
- **Confiscation of assets**

if they were acquired through **corruption**.


Important point:


This can happen **even while prosecution is pending**.


---


# 12. TIME LIMITS FOR INVESTIGATION


The Act provides **clear timelines**.


### Preliminary Inquiry


Duration:


**3 months**


Extension:


Additional **3 months**


---


### Investigation


Duration:


**6 months**


Extension:


Can be extended by **6 months each time**.


---


### Trial


Duration:


**1 year**


Extension:


Can be extended by **1 year**.


---


### Special Courts


To ensure speedy trials:


Government establishes **Special Courts**.


---


# 13. PUNISHMENT UNDER PREVENTION OF CORRUPTION ACT


The Act strengthens punishment provisions.


### Maximum Punishment


Earlier:


**7 years**


Now:


**10 years**


---


### Minimum Punishment


Under Sections **7, 8, 9, 12**


Minimum punishment:


**3 years**


---


### Attempt to Commit Corruption


Under **Section 15**


Minimum punishment:


**2 years**


---


# 14. COVERAGE OF ORGANISATIONS


Lokpal jurisdiction includes:


Institutions **fully or partly financed by government**.


But excludes:


Institutions **only aided by government**.


---


# 15. PROTECTION FOR HONEST OFFICIALS


The Act includes provisions to:


Protect **honest and upright public servants**.


Purpose:


Prevent harassment through false complaints.


---


# 16. SANCTION FOR PROSECUTION


Earlier:


Government permission was required.


Now:


Lokpal itself can **grant sanction for prosecution** of public servants.


This reduces **political interference**.


---


# 17. PROVISIONS TO STRENGTHEN CBI


The Act introduced reforms to strengthen CBI.


### 1. Directorate of Prosecution


Headed by:


**Director of Prosecution**


Under supervision of **CBI Director**.


---


### 2. Appointment of Director of Prosecution


Done on recommendation of:


**Central Vigilance Commission**


---


### 3. Panel of Advocates


CBI may maintain panel of lawyers other than government advocates.


Requires consent of **Lokpal**.


---


### 4. Transfer of CBI Officers


Officers investigating Lokpal cases **cannot be transferred without Lokpal approval**.


---


### 5. Adequate Funding


CBI must receive adequate funds for:


Investigating Lokpal-referred cases.


---


# 18. FOREIGN DONATIONS UNDER LOKPAL


Entities receiving foreign donations are also covered.


Condition:


Foreign contribution **exceeding ₹10 lakh per year**.


Law applicable:


**Foreign Contribution Regulation Act (FCRA)**.


---


# 19. LOKAYUKTA IN STATES


The Act mandates:


States must establish **Lokayukta**.


Deadline:


Within **365 days of commencement of Act**.


However:


States have freedom to design their **own structure of Lokayukta**.

# 1. DRAWBACKS OF LOKPAL AND LOKAYUKTAS ACT, 2013


Despite being a major anti-corruption reform, the Act has several criticisms.


### 1. No Suo Moto Power


Lokpal **cannot initiate investigation on its own**.


It can act only when:

- Complaint is filed.

---


### 2. Emphasis on Form of Complaint


The Act stresses more on:

- Proper format
- Documentation

Instead of focusing mainly on **substance of allegations**.


---


### 3. Punishment for False Complaints


Heavy penalties exist for **false or frivolous complaints**.


This may discourage genuine whistleblowers.


---


### 4. Anonymous Complaints Not Allowed


Complaint must include:

- Identity of complainant

You cannot simply submit:

- Anonymous letter
- Complaint without name

---


### 5. Legal Assistance for Accused Public Servant


Public servant accused of corruption may obtain:

- Legal assistance

Critics argue this may **slow down investigations**.


---


### 6. Limitation Period


Complaints can only be filed within:


**7 years of alleged offence**


Older corruption cases cannot be investigated.


---


### 7. Non-transparent Process for Complaints Against PM


Special procedure for investigating **Prime Minister** is not transparent.
