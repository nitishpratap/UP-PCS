| Aspect        | Details                                               |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Governing Law | **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**              |
| Amendment     | **2006 Amendment**                                    |
| Provides For  | **NHRC + SHRC + Human Rights Courts**                 |
| Objective     | Protection & promotion of human rights at State level |


---


# 2. ESTABLISHMENT OF SHRC


| Aspect         | Details                                   |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Established By | **State Government**                      |
| Mode           | Through **Official Gazette Notification** |


### States having SHRC


Assam, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Goa, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Kerala, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Manipur, Odisha, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Tripura.


---


# 3. JURISDICTION OF SHRC


| Can Inquire Into                                  | Cannot Inquire Into                         |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Matters related to **State List (List II)**       | Matters already under inquiry of NHRC       |
| Matters related to **Concurrent List (List III)** | Matters before another statutory commission |


### Important

- Jurisdiction derived from **Seventh Schedule**.
- SHRC deals only with **human rights violations** connected to state/concurrent subjects.

---


# 4. COMPOSITION


### Multi-member Body → Total = **3 Members**


| Post        | Qualification                                                                                                   |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Chairperson | Retired Chief Justice of High Court                                                                             |
| Member      | Serving/Retired High Court Judge                                                                                |
| Member      | District Judge (minimum 7 years experience) **OR** person having knowledge/practical experience in human rights |


### 2006 Amendment

- Members reduced from **5 → 3**
- Eligibility conditions modified.

# STATE HUMAN RIGHTS COMMISSION (SHRC)


_(Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993)_


---


# 1. Constitutional / Legal Basis

- The **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** provides for:
    - **National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)**
    - **State Human Rights Commissions (SHRC)** at state level.
- The Act was **amended in 2006**.

### Purpose


To **protect and promote human rights at the state level**.


---


# 2. Establishment of State Human Rights Commission

- Created through **Official Gazette Notification by the State Government**.

### States having SHRC

1. Assam
2. Andhra Pradesh
3. Telangana
4. Bihar
5. Chhattisgarh
6. Gujarat
7. Goa
8. Himachal Pradesh
9. Jammu and Kashmir
10. Kerala
11. Karnataka
12. Madhya Pradesh
13. Maharashtra
14. Manipur
15. Odisha
16. Punjab
17. Rajasthan
18. Tamil Nadu
19. Uttar Pradesh
20. West Bengal
21. Jharkhand
22. Sikkim
23. Uttarakhand
24. Haryana
25. Tripura

---


# 3. Jurisdiction of SHRC


The State Human Rights Commission can **inquire into violations of human rights only in relation to:**

- **State List (List II)**
- **Concurrent List (List III)**

    of the **Seventh Schedule of the Constitution**.


### Limitation


SHRC **cannot investigate** a case if:

- The same case is already being investigated by:
    - **National Human Rights Commission**
    - **Any other statutory commission**.

---


# 4. Composition of the Commission


The SHRC is a **multi-member body**.


### Members


Total = **3 members**


1️⃣ **Chairperson**

- Must be a **retired Chief Justice of a High Court**

2️⃣ **Member**

- A **serving or retired Judge of a High Court**

3️⃣ **Member**

- Either:
    - A **District Judge with minimum 7 years experience**, OR
    - A person with **knowledge / practical experience in human rights**.

---


# 5. Appointment of Chairperson and Members


Appointed by the **Governor**.


### Recommendation Committee


The Governor appoints them **on recommendation of a committee consisting of:**

1. Chief Minister – **Chairperson of Committee**
2. Speaker of Legislative Assembly
3. State Home Minister
4. Leader of Opposition in Legislative Assembly

### If the State has Legislative Council


Then also included:

1. Chairman of Legislative Council
2. Leader of Opposition in Legislative Council

---


### Appointment of Sitting Judges


If appointing:

- **Sitting High Court Judge**
- **Sitting District Judge**

Then **consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court is mandatory**.


---


# 6. Tenure


Chairperson and members hold office:

- **5 years OR**
- **Until 70 years of age**

**Whichever is earlier.**


### Reappointment

- A **member may be reappointed for another 5-year term**
- Subject to **maximum age limit of 70 years**.

---


# 7. Post-Tenure Restriction


After completing tenure:

- Chairperson and members **cannot accept employment under**
    - **Central Government**
    - **State Government**.

---


# 8. Removal of Chairperson or Members


Although they are **appointed by the Governor**, they are **removed only by the President**.


The removal procedure is **similar to NHRC members**.


---


### Grounds for Removal


The President can remove them if:

1. **Adjudged insolvent**
2. **Engages in paid employment outside official duties**
3. **Infirmity of mind or body**
4. **Declared of unsound mind by court**
5. **Convicted and sentenced to imprisonment**

---


### Removal for Misbehaviour or Incapacity


Procedure:

1. President refers matter to **Supreme Court**
2. Supreme Court conducts **inquiry**
3. If SC **upholds charges**
4. President removes the member.

---


# 9. Salary and Service Conditions

- Determined by **State Government**
- But **cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment**.

### Objective


To ensure:

- **Autonomy**
- **Independence**
- **Impartiality** of the Commission.

---


# 10. Functions of the State Human Rights Commission


The **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** defines several functions of the Commission.


The Commission performs the following functions:


---


## 1. Inquiry into Violation of Human Rights


The Commission can **inquire into human rights violations** in two situations:

1. **Violation of human rights**
2. **Negligence by a public servant in preventing such violation**

The inquiry can be initiated:

- **Suo motu (on its own initiative)**
- **On a petition submitted to it**
- **On an order of a court**

---


## 2. Intervention in Court Proceedings


The Commission may **intervene in any case pending before a court** if:

- The case involves **allegations of human rights violations**.

---


## 3. Inspection of Jails and Detention Centres


The Commission can:

- **Visit jails**
- **Visit detention centres**

Purpose:

- Study **living conditions of prisoners**
- Make **recommendations for improvement**.

---


## 4. Review of Safeguards for Human Rights


The Commission reviews:

- **Constitutional safeguards**
- **Legal safeguards**

Objective:

- Ensure **effective protection of human rights**.

It also recommends **measures for better implementation**.


---


## 5. Review Factors that Inhibit Human Rights


The Commission reviews factors that restrict the enjoyment of human rights, including:

- **Acts of terrorism**

It suggests **remedial measures** to address such problems.


---


## 6. Promotion of Research


The Commission:

- Undertakes **research in the field of human rights**
- Encourages **academic and institutional research**.

---


## 7. Human Rights Awareness


The Commission spreads awareness by:

- Promoting **human rights literacy**
- Educating citizens about **available safeguards**.

---


## 8. Encouraging NGOs


The Commission supports and encourages:

- **Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs)**
- Working in the **field of human rights protection**.

---


## 9. Other Necessary Functions


The Commission may perform:

- **Any other functions necessary for the promotion of human rights**.

---


# 11. Working of the Commission


---


## Power to Regulate Procedure


The Commission has the authority to:

- **Regulate its own procedure**.

---


## Powers of Civil Court


While conducting inquiries, the Commission has **all the powers of a Civil Court**.


Thus, it can:

- Summon witnesses
- Examine persons under oath
- Receive evidence
- Require production of documents

Its **proceedings are judicial in nature**.


---


## Power to Seek Information


The Commission can:

- Call for **information**
- Request **reports**

From:

- State Government
- Any authority subordinate to it.

---


# 12. Limitation on Inquiry


The Commission **cannot investigate cases older than one year**.


Meaning:

- It can inquire only if the complaint is filed **within one year of the violation**.

---


# 13. Actions After Inquiry


During or after completion of inquiry, the Commission may take the following actions:


---


## 1. Recommend Compensation


It may recommend to the state government:

- Payment of **compensation**
- Payment of **damages to the victim**.

---


## 2. Recommend Prosecution


It may recommend:

- **Prosecution of the guilty public servant**
- Or **other disciplinary action**.

---


## 3. Recommend Interim Relief


The Commission may suggest:

- **Immediate interim relief** for the victim.

---


## 4. Approach Higher Courts


The Commission may approach:

- **Supreme Court**
- **High Court**

For:

- **Directions**
- **Orders**
- **Writs**.

---


# 14. Nature of SHRC Powers


Important exam point ⚠️


The functions of the Commission are:


### **Recommendatory**


Meaning:

- It **cannot punish violators**
- It **cannot directly grant relief or compensation**.

---


### Recommendations Not Binding

- The state government **is not legally bound to accept recommendations**.

However:

- The government **must inform the Commission within one month** about the action taken.

---


# 15. Reports of the Commission


The Commission submits:

1. **Annual Reports**
2. **Special Reports**

To the **State Government**.


---


## Presentation Before Legislature


The State Government lays the report before:

- **State Legislature**

Along with:

- **Memorandum explaining action taken**
- **Reasons for rejecting recommendations**.

---


### If State Legislature has Two Houses


Reports are placed before:

- **Both Houses of the Legislature**.

---


# 16. Human Rights Courts


The **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** also provides for:


### Establishment of Human Rights Courts


Purpose:

- **Speedy trial of human rights violations**.

---


## Where Are They Established?

- In **every district**.

---


## Who Establishes Them?

- **State Government**

But only **with the concurrence of the Chief Justice of the High Court**.


---


## Public Prosecutor


For every Human Rights Court:


The State Government appoints:

- A **Public Prosecutor**

OR

- A **Special Public Prosecutor**

Eligibility for Special Public Prosecutor:

- Must have **7 years of practice as an advocate**.

---


# 17. Important Amendment (2006)


The **2006 Amendment to the Human Rights Act** made the following changes:

1. Reduced the **number of SHRC members from 5 to 3**.
2. Modified **eligibility conditions for members**.
