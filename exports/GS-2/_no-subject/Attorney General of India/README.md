
# Attorney General of India (भारत का महान्यायवादी)


## Constitutional Provision

- The **Constitution of India provides for the office of the Attorney General of India under Article 76.**
- He is the **highest law officer of the Government of India.**
- This office is located in:
    - **Part V – The Union**
    - **Chapter I – The Executive**

Thus, the **Attorney General acts as the chief legal advisor to the Government of India.**


---


# Appointment of the Attorney General


The **Attorney General is appointed by the President of India.**


### Qualification


To be appointed as Attorney General, a person must be **qualified to be appointed as a judge of the Supreme Court.**


Therefore, the person must:

1. Be a **Citizen of India**
2. Fulfil **any one** of the following conditions:

| Qualification          | Explanation                                                          |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| High Court Judge       | Must have served as a **judge of a High Court for at least 5 years** |
| Advocate of High Court | Must have been an **advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years** |
| Eminent Jurist         | Must be an **eminent jurist in the opinion of the President**        |


### Exam Points (UPPCS)

- Qualification same as **Supreme Court Judge**
- Appointment authority → **President**
- Mentioned in → **Article 76**

---


# Term of Office


The **Constitution does not fix the tenure** of the Attorney General.


Important aspects:

1. **No fixed tenure**
2. **No procedure for removal mentioned in the Constitution**
3. Holds office **during the pleasure of the President**

### Meaning of “Pleasure of the President”

- The President can **remove the Attorney General at any time**.

### Resignation

- The Attorney General may resign **by submitting resignation to the President.**

### Convention

- Usually, the Attorney General **resigns when the Council of Ministers resigns or changes**.
- Reason: He is appointed **on the advice of the Council of Ministers.**

---


# Remuneration (Salary)


The **Constitution does not fix the salary** of the Attorney General.

- The **President determines the remuneration.**

Thus:


| Feature | Provision                |
| ------- | ------------------------ |
| Tenure  | Not fixed                |
| Removal | At pleasure of President |
| Salary  | Determined by President  |


---


# Duties and Functions of the Attorney General


The Attorney General is the **Chief Law Officer of the Government of India**.


His duties include:


### 1. Legal Advice


He gives **legal advice to the Government of India** on matters referred to him by the **President**.


### 2. Other Legal Duties


He performs **legal duties assigned by the President**.


### 3. Constitutional Functions


He performs functions assigned to him by:

- **The Constitution**
- **Any other law**

---


# Specific Duties Assigned by the President


The President has assigned the following duties to the Attorney General:


### 1. Representation in Supreme Court


He **appears on behalf of the Government of India in all cases in the Supreme Court** where the Government is concerned.


### 2. Article 143 Reference Cases


He represents the Government of India when the **President seeks the opinion of the Supreme Court under Article 143.**


### 3. Representation in High Courts


He may appear in **any High Court** in cases involving the Government of India **when required by the Government.**


---


# Rights of the Attorney General


In the performance of his official duties, the Attorney General enjoys several important rights.


### 1. Right of Audience


He has the **right of audience in all courts in the territory of India.**


Meaning:

- He can appear in **any court in India**.

---


### 2. Rights in Parliament (Article 88)


The Attorney General has the right to:

- **Speak in both Houses of Parliament**
- Participate in **joint sittings**
- Take part in **Parliamentary committees**

However:


❗ **He cannot vote.**


---


### 3. Parliamentary Privileges


The Attorney General enjoys:

- **Privileges**
- **Immunities**

similar to those available to a **Member of Parliament.**


---


# Limitations on the Attorney General


Certain restrictions are imposed to **avoid conflict of interest.**


The Attorney General:


1.Cannot **advise or hold a brief against the Government of India.**


2.Cannot advise or appear in cases where he has already been called upon to **advise the Government of India.**


3.Cannot defend **accused persons in criminal prosecutions** without **permission of the Government of India.**


4.Cannot accept appointment as **Director of a company or corporation** without **permission of the Government of India.**


---


# Nature of the Office


Important facts about the Attorney General:


### 1. Not a Government Servant


The Attorney General is **not a government servant.**


### 2. Not a Full-Time Counsel


He is **not a full-time lawyer of the government.**


### 3. Private Practice Allowed


He is **allowed to do private legal practice.**


However, restrictions apply to avoid **conflict of interest.**


---


# Other Law Officers of the Government of India


Apart from the Attorney General, there are:

1. **Solicitor General of India**
2. **Additional Solicitor Generals of India**

### Role


They **assist the Attorney General in performing his duties.**


### Important Constitutional Fact


Only the **Attorney General is mentioned in the Constitution.**


The offices of:

- Solicitor General
- Additional Solicitor General

are **not mentioned in the Constitution.**


They are created by the **Government of India.**


---


# Attorney General and Council of Ministers


Important point:

- The Attorney General **is NOT a member of the Union Cabinet.**

Instead:

- The **Law Minister** in the Cabinet deals with legal matters at the government level.

# Important Historical Fact


During the **Prime Ministership of Jawaharlal Nehru**, the Central Government proposed:

- **Merging the office of Attorney General with the Law Minister.**

However,


❌ **This proposal was never implemented.**
