## ESTABLISHMENT, STATUS & PURPOSE OF CBI


## Establishment of CBI

- The **Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)** was established in **1963**.
- It was created through a **resolution of the Ministry of Home Affairs**.

Later administrative control changed.


### Administrative Control


Initially → **Ministry of Home Affairs**


Later → **Ministry of Personnel**


Presently → works under


**Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT)**


Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions.


It functions as an **attached office** of the ministry.


---


## Origin of CBI


The CBI evolved from an earlier agency.


### Special Police Establishment (SPE)

- Established in **1941**
- Purpose → investigate **vigilance cases and corruption in wartime procurement**

Later this **Special Police Establishment was merged into CBI**.


---


## Recommendation for Creation of CBI


The **Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption (1962-1964)** recommended creation of CBI.


This committee focused on:

- corruption in public administration
- need for a specialised central investigation agency

---


## Legal Status of CBI


⚠️ **Important for exams**


CBI **is NOT a statutory body**.


It derives powers from:


### Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946 (DSPE Act)


Thus


CBI = **Executive body created by resolution but powers derived from DSPE Act.**


---


## Nature of CBI


CBI is the **main investigating agency of the Central Government**.


Its key roles include:

- Prevent corruption
- Maintain integrity in administration
- Investigate serious crimes

It also assists:

- **Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)**
- **Lokpal**

# 1. ORGANISATION OF CBI


The CBI is divided into several **specialised divisions** to deal with different categories of crime and administrative functions.


As per the text, the CBI has the following **seven divisions**:


### 1. Anti-Corruption Division


This division investigates:

- Corruption cases
- Bribery cases
- Misconduct by **Central Government employees**
- Cases under **Prevention of Corruption Act**

This is one of the **most important divisions** of the CBI.


---


### 2. Economic Offences Division


This division investigates **financial and economic crimes**, such as:

- Bank frauds
- Financial scams
- Corporate fraud
- Foreign exchange violations
- Large financial irregularities

These offences usually involve **huge economic impact on the country**.


---


### 3. Special Crimes Division


This division investigates **serious conventional crimes**, including:

- Murder
- Kidnapping
- Terror-related offences
- Organised crime
- Crimes with national importance

These cases usually involve **inter-state or international dimensions**.


---


### 4. Policy and International Police Cooperation Division


This division handles:

- Policy matters of CBI
- International cooperation in investigations
- Coordination with foreign agencies
- Interaction with **Interpol**

This division plays a major role in **international criminal cooperation**.


---


### 5. Administration Division


This division manages:

- Personnel administration
- Recruitment
- Establishment matters
- Internal management of CBI

Essentially it handles the **administrative functioning of the organisation**.


---


### 6. Directorate of Prosecution


This division is responsible for:

- Prosecution of cases investigated by CBI
- Legal proceedings in courts
- Coordination with prosecutors

It ensures that **investigated cases are successfully prosecuted in courts**.


---


### 7. Central Forensic Science Laboratory (CFSL)


This division provides:

- Scientific investigation support
- Forensic examination
- Technical analysis of evidence

Examples of services:

- DNA analysis
- Ballistics
- Document examination
- Cyber forensics

Scientific evidence strengthens **criminal prosecution**.

# 6. CHANGES BY LOKPAL AND LOKAYUKTAS ACT, 2013


The **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013** made important amendments to the **Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946**.


These changes relate mainly to the **composition and functioning of CBI**.


---


## 1. Appointment of CBI Director


The **Director of CBI** is appointed by the **Central Government**, but on recommendation of a **three-member committee**.


### Composition of Committee


1️⃣ Prime Minister — **Chairperson**


2️⃣ Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha


3️⃣ Chief Justice of India or a **Supreme Court Judge nominated by him**


This system ensures **transparency and independence**.


---


## 2. Directorate of Prosecution


The Act also created a **Directorate of Prosecution**.


### Head


It is headed by a **Director of Prosecution**.


### Rank


Must be an officer **not below the rank of Joint Secretary to Government of India**.


### Appointment


Appointed by:


**Central Government on recommendation of Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)**.


### Tenure


The Director of Prosecution holds office for:


**Two years**.


---


## 3. Appointment of Senior Officers


Officers of the rank **SP and above** are appointed by the **Central Government**.


However they must be recommended by a **selection committee**.


### Composition of Committee


1️⃣ Central Vigilance Commissioner — **Chairperson**


2️⃣ Vigilance Commissioners


3️⃣ Secretary of Home Ministry


4️⃣ Secretary of Department of Personnel


---


# 7. CHANGE BY DSPE AMENDMENT ACT, 2014


Another important change was made in **2014**.


### Situation


Sometimes there is **no recognized Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha**.


In such cases:


The committee will include:


➡ **Leader of the single largest opposition party in Lok Sabha**.


This ensures the selection committee can still function.

# 1. FUNCTIONS OF CBI


The **CBI performs several important investigative and coordination functions** in India.


The main functions include:


---


## 1. Investigation of Corruption Cases


CBI investigates:

- Corruption
- Bribery
- Misconduct by **Central Government employees**

These cases are generally related to violations under:

- **Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988**

This is the **primary responsibility of CBI**.


---


## 2. Investigation of Economic and Fiscal Offences


CBI investigates cases involving violation of **economic and fiscal laws**.


Examples include:

- Export-import violations
- Customs violations
- Central excise fraud
- Income tax offences
- Foreign exchange violations

However such cases are taken up:

- **in consultation with**
- **or at the request of the concerned department**.

---


## 3. Investigation of Serious Crimes


CBI investigates **serious crimes having national or international implications**.


Examples:

- Organised crime
- International crime networks
- Professional criminal gangs

These cases often involve **multiple states or countries**.


---


## 4. Coordination with Anti-Corruption Agencies


CBI coordinates with:

- State police
- Anti-corruption agencies
- Other law enforcement agencies

This coordination helps improve **investigation and crime control**.


---


## 5. Investigation on Request of State Governments


CBI can investigate cases when:

- **State governments request investigation**

This usually happens in **cases of public importance**.


However states must give **consent** under the **DSPE Act**.


---


## 6. Crime Statistics and Criminal Information


CBI also performs an information role.


It maintains:

- Crime statistics
- Criminal databases
- Information related to investigations

This helps in **law enforcement coordination across the country**.


---


# 2. NATURE OF CBI INVESTIGATIONS


CBI is a **multidisciplinary investigation agency**.


It investigates three main types of cases:


### 1. Corruption-related cases

- bribery
- misuse of office
- misconduct

### 2. Economic offences

- financial fraud
- economic scams
- tax violations

### 3. Conventional crimes


Examples:

- murder
- kidnapping
- rape

However conventional crimes are usually taken up only when:

- requested by **state governments**, or
- ordered by **Supreme Court / High Courts**.

---


# 3. CBI AND INTERPOL


CBI acts as the **National Central Bureau (NCB) of Interpol in India**.


This means:

- CBI coordinates international police cooperation.

Functions include:

- handling requests from foreign law enforcement agencies
- sharing criminal intelligence
- assisting extradition processes
- facilitating international criminal investigations

Thus the **Interpol wing of CBI** manages communication between:

- Indian agencies
- Member countries of Interpol.

---


# 4. PROVISION OF PRIOR PERMISSION


Earlier there was a controversial provision related to **investigating senior bureaucrats**.


### Rule


CBI needed **prior approval of Central Government** before investigating:


➡ Officers of **Joint Secretary rank and above**.


This provision was under:


**Section 6A of Delhi Special Police Establishment Act**.


---


# 5. SUPREME COURT JUDGEMENT (2014)


On **6 May 2014**, the Supreme Court struck down this provision.


The Court declared:


**Section 6A of DSPE Act unconstitutional.**


---


## Reason for Striking Down


The Court held that the provision violated:


➡ **Article 14 (Right to Equality)**


Because it treated:

- senior officers
- junior officers

**differently in corruption investigations**.


---


## Key Observations of Supreme Court


The Court stated:

- Corruption is an **enemy of the nation**.
- Every corrupt public servant must be investigated **regardless of rank**.
- There cannot be **special protection for senior bureaucrats**.

The Court emphasized:


➡ **Rule of Law must apply equally to all public servants.**


---


# 6. CBI vs STATE POLICE


The **Special Police Establishment (SPE)**, which is a division of CBI, works alongside state police forces.


Both agencies have **concurrent powers of investigation and prosecution** under:


➡ **Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946**.


However administrative arrangements exist to avoid **overlapping of cases**.


---


## Case Allocation Rules


### 1. Cases Related to Central Government


Investigated by:


➡ **CBI / Special Police Establishment**


Even if they involve **some state government employees**.


---


### 2. Cases Related to State Government


Investigated by:


➡ **State Police**


Even if they involve **some central government employees**.


---


### 3. Cases Related to Central Public Sector Undertakings


Investigated by:


➡ **CBI**


If they involve:

- employees of
- public undertakings or statutory bodies

financed by the **Central Government**.


---
