
# FOREIGN POLICY OF INDIA


## Meaning


Foreign Policy = Policy through which India regulates relations with other countries to protect and promote national interests.


---


# DETERMINANTS OF INDIA’S FOREIGN POLICY


India’s foreign policy is influenced by:

1. Geography
2. History & tradition
3. Social structure
4. Political organisation
5. International milieu (global political climate)
6. Economic position
7. Military strength
8. Public opinion
9. Leadership

---


# PRINCIPLES / FEATURES OF INDIA’S FOREIGN POLICY


---


# 1. PROMOTION OF WORLD PEACE


## Objective


India aims to promote:

- International peace
- Security
- Friendly relations among nations

## Constitutional Basis


### Article 51 (DPSP)


Directs the State to:

- Promote international peace and security
- Maintain just and honourable relations between nations
- Foster respect for international law and treaty obligations
- Encourage settlement of disputes by arbitration

## Importance

- Peace necessary for economic development
- Avoids global conflicts and arms race

---


# 2. ANTI-COLONIALISM


## Meaning


India opposes:

- Colonialism
- Imperialism (साम्राज्यवाद)
- Neo-colonialism
- Neo-imperialism

## Why?


Because colonialism:

- Exploits weaker nations
- Threatens world peace
- Violates sovereignty

## India Supported Liberation Movements In:

- Indonesia
- Malaya
- Tunisia
- Algeria
- Ghana
- Namibia

## India Opposed Colonial Powers Like:

- Britain
- France
- Holland
- Portugal

---


# 3. ANTI-RACIALISM


## Meaning


India opposes racial discrimination.


### Racialism


Discrimination on the basis of race.


## India’s Stand


India considered racialism harmful because:

- Exploits black people
- Creates inequality
- Disturbs world peace

## Apartheid (**रंगभेद**) in South Africa

- India strongly criticised apartheid.
- Broke diplomatic relations with South Africa in 1954.

## Restoration of Relations

- Relations restored in 1994 after:
    - End of apartheid
    - Democratic government under Nelson Mandela

## Other Contributions


India supported liberation of:

- Zimbabwe (Rhodesia)
- Namibia

---


# 4. NON-ALIGNMENT


## Background


After independence (1947), world divided into two blocs during Cold War:


| Bloc            | Leader |
| --------------- | ------ |
| Capitalist Bloc | USA    |
| Communist Bloc  | USSR   |


India refused to join either bloc.


---


## Meaning of Non-Alignment


### According to Jawaharlal Nehru:


India would:

- Stay away from power politics
- Avoid military alliances
- Maintain independence in foreign policy

---


## Features


### 1. No military alliance


India did not join any bloc.


### 2. Independent foreign policy


Decisions based on national interest.


### 3. Friendly relations with all


India tried maintaining relations with both blocs.


---


## Importance

- Preserved sovereignty
- Avoided Cold War conflicts
- Allowed strategic autonomy

---


# 5. PANCHSHEEL


## Meaning


Five principles of peaceful coexistence.


## Origin


Included in:


### Indo-China Treaty on Tibet (1954)


Signed by:

- Jawaharlal Nehru
- Zhou Enlai

---


# FIVE PRINCIPLES OF PANCHSHEEL


| Principle                      | Meaning                            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Mutual respect for sovereignty | Respect territorial integrity      |
| Non-aggression                 | No attack on each other            |
| Non-interference               | No involvement in internal matters |
| Equality & mutual benefit      | Equal treatment                    |
| Peaceful coexistence           | Live peacefully                    |


---


## Importance

- Adopted by many nations:
    - Burma
    - Yugoslavia
    - Indonesia
- Major contribution of India to international relations

# GUJRAL DOCTRINE


## Introduced By


I. K. Gujral


## Year


### 1996


## Nature


Milestone in India’s foreign policy.


---


# Core Idea


India should:

- Give unilateral concessions to smaller neighbours
- Not insist on reciprocity

---


# Objective


Maintain friendly relations with neighbours.


---


# FIVE PRINCIPLES OF GUJRAL DOCTRINE


## 1. Non-reciprocity


India should help:

- Bangladesh
- Bhutan
- Maldives
- Nepal
- Sri Lanka

without expecting return.


---


## 2. Territory not used against neighbours


No country should allow anti-neighbour activities from its territory.


---


## 3. Non-interference


No interference in internal affairs.


---


## 4. Respect sovereignty


Respect territorial integrity.


---


## 5. Peaceful settlement


Disputes solved through bilateral negotiations.


---


# Significance

- Reduce influence of Pakistan and China
- Improve South Asian cooperation

---


# NUCLEAR DOCTRINE OF INDIA


## Main Features


---


## 1. Credible Minimum Deterrence


Maintain minimum nuclear weapons needed for deterrence.


---


## 2. No First Use (NFU)


India will use nuclear weapons only:

- In retaliation

---


## 3. Massive Retaliation


Response will inflict unacceptable damage.


---


## 4. Civilian Control


Use authorised only by civilian political leadership.


---


## 5. No Use Against Non-Nuclear States


India will not use nuclear weapons against non-nuclear countries.


---


## 6. Exception


India may use nuclear weapons if attacked by:

- Biological weapons
- Chemical weapons

---


## 7. Export Controls


Strict control on:

- Nuclear materials
- Missile technology

---


## 8. Commitment to Nuclear-Free World


India supports:

- Universal
- Verifiable
- Non-discriminatory disarmament

---


# NUCLEAR COMMAND AUTHORITY (NCA)

| Body              | Head                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| Political Council | Prime Minister            |
| Executive Council | National Security Advisor |


---


## Political Council

- Sole authority to authorise nuclear weapons use.

## Executive Council

- Gives inputs
