
---


## 1. ORIGIN


### Global

- **USA (1960s)** → To provide legal aid to:
    - Poor, minorities, consumers, environmental groups
- Reason → Failure of traditional legal system to represent disadvantaged groups

### India

- Emerged via **Judicial Activism (1980s)**
- Pioneers:
    - **Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer**
    - **Justice P.N. Bhagwati**

---


## 2. TERMINOLOGY

- Social Action Litigation (SAL)
- Social Interest Litigation (SIL)
- Class Action Litigation (CAL)

---


## 3. CORE CONCEPT


### Traditional Rule

- **Locus Standi** → Only aggrieved person can file case

### PIL Innovation

- **Relaxation of locus standi**
- Any:
    - Public-spirited individual
    - NGO

        → Can file for:

    - Poor, illiterate, disadvantaged

👉 Key phrase: **“Sufficient Interest”**


---


## 4. DEFINITION (SC)

> Legal action for enforcement of public/general interest where rights of a class/community are affected.

---


## 5. OBJECTIVES

- Rule of Law enforcement
- Access to justice (weaker sections)
- Realisation of Fundamental Rights

---


## 6. IMPORTANCE

- Democratisation of justice
- Judicial activism tool
- Protection of collective rights
- Accountability of State

---


## 7. FEATURES (Highly Important)


| Feature               | Essence                      |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Legal Aid Tool        | Helps marginalized           |
| Non-adversarial       | Not individual vs individual |
| Public Interest Focus | Community rights             |
| Court Activism        | Active judicial role         |
| Flexible Procedure    | Relaxed technicalities       |
| Cooperative Model     | Court + State + Petitioner   |
| Collective Rights     | Not private disputes         |

## 8. SCOPE OF PIL


### SC Guidelines

- Issued: **1988**
- Modified: **1993, 2003**

---


### ✅ ACCEPTED AREAS

- Bonded labour
- Neglected children
- Labour exploitation
- Prison reforms (incl. speedy trial, custodial deaths)
- Police atrocities (FIR refusal, custody deaths)
- Crimes against women
- SC/ST atrocities
- Environment & ecology
- Heritage protection
- Riot victims
- Family pension

---


### ❌ NOT ACCEPTED


| Category                  | Reason                |
| ------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Landlord–Tenant           | Private dispute       |
| Service matters           | Tribunal jurisdiction |
| Pension/Gratuity          | Personal claims       |
| Govt complaints (general) | Unless fits PIL scope |
| Education admissions      | Individual rights     |
| Early hearing requests    | Procedural            |


---


## 9. PRINCIPLES OF PIL (SC – 2003 Case)


### Constitutional Basis

- **Article 32 (SC)**
- **Article 226 (HC)**

---


### KEY PRINCIPLES

1. **Relaxed locus standi**
2. **Letters/telegrams allowed**
3. Use of **Art. 14 & 21**
4. Protection of disadvantaged groups
5. State cannot easily challenge maintainability
6. **Res Judicata applicable (conditional)**
7. No private disputes
8. Personal → Public conversion possible
9. Courts may appoint committees/commissions
10. Avoid policy interference
11. Stay within judicial review limits
12. HC cannot use **Article 142-like powers**
13. HC should avoid constitutional validity issues in PIL

---


### IMPORTANT DOCTRINES

- **Locus Standi → Relaxed**
- **Res Judicata → Finality of decisions**

---


## 10. MISUSE OF PIL


### SC Warning Terms

- Publicity Interest Litigation
- Political Interest Litigation
- Private Interest Litigation
- Paisa Interest Litigation
- Middle-class Interest Litigation

---


## 11. GUIDELINES TO PREVENT MISUSE (2010)


### Key Checks

1. Encourage genuine PIL
2. Frame procedural rules
3. Verify petitioner credentials
4. Verify facts
5. Ensure real public interest
6. Prioritize urgent matters
7. No personal gain/motive
8. Penalize frivolous petitions
