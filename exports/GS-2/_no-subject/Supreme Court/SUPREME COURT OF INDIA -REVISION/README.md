# 1. INDIAN JUDICIARY SYSTEM


**India → Integrated Judicial System**


Hierarchy


**Supreme Court → High Courts → District Courts → Subordinate Courts**


Key Feature


Same courts enforce **Central + State laws**


### India vs USA


| India                        | USA                    |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Integrated judiciary         | Dual judiciary         |
| Single court system          | Federal + State courts |
| Same courts enforce all laws | Separate enforcement   |


**Conclusion:** India = Federal polity + **Unitary judiciary**

# 6. COLLEGIUM SYSTEM (JUDGES CASES)


### First Judges Case (1982)


Consultation **≠ concurrence**


Government **not bound by CJI opinion**


---


### Second Judges Case (1993)


Consultation **= concurrence**


CJI opinion **binding**


Advice formed with **2 senior-most judges**


➡ **Collegium System created**


---


### Third Judges Case (1998)


Collegium expanded:


**CJI + 4 senior-most judges**


Rule:


If **2 judges oppose recommendation → cannot proceed**


---


### Fourth Judges Case (2015)


Government attempted **NJAC**


Created through:

- **99th Constitutional Amendment (2014)**

• **NJAC Act 2014**


Supreme Court (2015):


**Declared unconstitutional**


Reason → **Violation of judicial independence**


➡ **Collegium continues**

# 12. JUDGES ENQUIRY ACT 1968


### Procedure


1️⃣ Motion signed by

- **100 Lok Sabha members**

OR


• **50 Rajya Sabha members**


---


2️⃣ Submitted to

- Speaker / Chairman

---


3️⃣ They may

- Admit OR reject

---


4️⃣ If admitted → **3-member committee**


Members:

- SC judge

• HC Chief Justice


• Distinguished jurist


---


5️⃣ If guilty → Parliament debates


---


6️⃣ Both Houses pass with **special majority**


---


7️⃣ President removes judge


---


### Important Fact


**No SC judge impeached so far**


---


### Only Attempt


**Justice V. Ramaswami (1991–93)**


Committee found guilty


But motion failed in **Lok Sabha**

# JURISDICTION OF SUPREME COURT


SC has **7 jurisdictions**


1️⃣ Original


2️⃣ Writ


3️⃣ Appellate


4️⃣ Advisory


5️⃣ Court of Record


6️⃣ Judicial Review


7️⃣ Other powers


---


# 1. ORIGINAL JURISDICTION


**Article 131**


Deals with **federal disputes**


Between:

- Centre vs State

• Centre + states vs states


• States vs states


Nature:


**Exclusive + Original**


---


### Conditions

- Must involve **legal right**
- Political questions excluded
- Citizens cannot file

---


### Excluded Matters

- Pre-constitution treaties

• Water disputes


• Finance Commission matters


• Certain expenses/pensions


• Commercial disputes


---


### First Case


**State of West Bengal vs Union of India (1961)**


Coal Bearing Areas Act challenged


SC **upheld Act**


---


# 2. WRIT JURISDICTION


**Article 32**


SC protects **Fundamental Rights**


Dr Ambedkar called it


**“Heart and Soul of Constitution”**


---


### Five Writs


| Writ          | Meaning           |
| ------------- | ----------------- |
| Habeas Corpus | Produce the body  |
| Mandamus      | We command        |
| Prohibition   | Stop lower court  |
| Certiorari    | Quash order       |
| Quo Warranto  | By what authority |


---


### SC vs HC Writ Power


| SC       | HC                  |
| -------- | ------------------- |
| FR only  | FR + other purposes |
| Narrower | Wider               |


---


# 3. APPELLATE JURISDICTION


Highest court of appeal


4 types:


1️⃣ Constitutional


2️⃣ Civil


3️⃣ Criminal


4️⃣ Special Leave


---


### Constitutional Appeal


**Article 132**


Requires HC certificate


Substantial constitutional question


---


### Civil Appeal


**Article 133**


HC certificate required


Substantial question of **general importance**


---


Important Amendment:


**30th Constitutional Amendment (1972)**


Removed **₹20,000 property requirement**


---


### Criminal Appeal


**Article 134**


Appeal if HC:

- Reverses acquittal → **death sentence**
- Withdraws case → **death sentence**
- Certifies fit for appeal

---


1970 expansion:


Appeal allowed if

- Life imprisonment
- 10-year sentence

---


### Special Leave Petition


**Article 136**


Very wide power


Features:

- Discretionary

• Any case


• Any court or tribunal


Exception:

- Military courts

• Court martial


---

# ADVOCATES IN SUPREME COURT


Three categories:


---


### Senior Advocates


Designated by SC/HC


Restrictions:

- Cannot file pleadings

• Cannot appear without AOR


---


### Advocate-on-Record (AOR)


Only AOR can:

- File cases

• Submit documents


• Represent parties


---


### Other Advocates


Can argue cases


But **cannot file cases**
