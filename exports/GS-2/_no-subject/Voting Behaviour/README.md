
# Voting Behaviour 


## 1. Meaning of Voting Behaviour


**Voting Behaviour** is also known as **Electoral Behaviour**.


It refers to the **behaviour and decision-making pattern of voters during elections in a democratic political system**.


In simple words:

> Voting behaviour explains **why people vote for a particular candidate or party**.

It studies:

- Voters' **preferences**
- **Political choices**
- **decision-making patterns**
- **social and psychological influences on voting**

---


# 2. Definitions of Voting Behaviour


### (1) Plano and Riggs

> “Voting behaviour is a field of study concerned with the ways in which people tend to vote in public elections and the reasons why they vote as they do.”

**Key Idea:**


Study of **voting patterns + reasons behind voting decisions**


---


### (2) Gordon Marshall

> “The study of voting behaviour invariably focuses on the determinants of why people vote as they do and how they arrive at the decisions they make.”

**Key Idea:**


Focus on **determinants (factors) influencing voting decisions**


---


### (3) Oinam Kulabidhu

> Voting behaviour reflects voter’s **choices, preferences, ideologies, concerns, agreements and programmes** regarding social and national issues.

**Key Idea:**


Voting reflects **political ideology and social issues**


---


### (4) Stephen Wasby

> Voting behaviour studies the **psychological makeup of individuals** and its relation with **political action and institutional patterns such as communication systems**.

**Key Idea:**


Voting influenced by **psychology + institutions + communication**


---


# 3. Psephology (Important for Prelims)


### Meaning


**Psephology** is the **scientific study of voting behaviour**.


It is a branch of **Political Science**.


### Origin of Word


The word comes from the **Greek word “Psephos”**.


**Psephos = piece of pottery used in ancient Greece for voting.**


In the **Greek Polis**, votes regarding issues like **banishment of dangerous persons** were written on pottery pieces.


Thus,


**Psephology = scientific analysis of voting patterns and election results.**


---


# 4. Significance of Studying Voting Behaviour


The study of voting behaviour is important for understanding **democracy and political processes**.


### Major Reasons


### 1. Understanding Political Socialisation


Helps understand **how individuals learn political values and attitudes**.


Example:

- family influence
- education
- media influence

---


### 2. Internalisation of Democratic Values


Helps analyse whether **people truly accept democracy as a value**.


It shows:

- how citizens participate in democratic processes.

---


### 3. Real Impact of the Ballot Box


Demonstrates the **power of voting in bringing political change**.


Example:

- governments changing due to elections.

---


### 4. Continuity or Break in Electoral Politics


It shows whether:

- electoral politics **continue old patterns**

    OR

- **new political trends emerge**

Example:

- emergence of new parties.

---


### 5. Measure of Political Development


It helps determine whether politics is:

- **Modern** (based on ideology and policies)

    OR

- **Primordial** (based on caste, religion, identity)

---


# 5. Nature of Voting Behaviour (N.G.S. Kini)


According to **N.G.S. Kini**, voting behaviour can be understood as:


### 1. Mode of Legitimising Democratic Rule


Voting gives **legitimacy to government authority**.


Example:


People elect representatives through elections.


---


### 2. Political Participation


Voting represents **citizen participation in political processes**.


Example:


Citizens influence governance by voting.


---


### 3. Decision-Making Process


Voting is an **act of choosing between candidates and policies**.


---


### 4. Political Orientation


Voting reflects **political attitudes and cultural values** of society.


Example:

- ideology

---


### 5. Direct Relation Between Citizens and Government


Voting establishes a **direct link between citizens and formal government institutions**.

# 6. Determinants of Voting Behaviour


Indian society is **highly diverse**, so voting behaviour is influenced by **many factors**.


These factors are broadly classified into:


1️⃣ **Socio-economic factors**


2️⃣ **Political factors**


---


# Major Determinants of Voting Behaviour in India


## 1. Caste


Caste is one of the **most important factors influencing Indian voting behaviour**.


### Politicisation of Caste


Political parties often consider **caste composition of constituencies** when selecting candidates.


**Rajni Kothari said:**

> “Indian politics is casteist, and caste is politicised.”

Meaning:

- Politics is influenced by caste
- Caste becomes politically mobilised.

---


### Paul Brass Explanation


According to **Paul Brass**:

- At the **local level**, caste solidarity strongly influences voting.
- Large caste groups support:
    - candidates from their caste
    - parties associated with their caste.

However,


Voting can also be influenced by:

- **inter-caste alliances**
- **local political factions**

---


## 2. Religion


Religion is another **major factor in electoral behaviour**.


Political parties sometimes:

- exploit **religious sentiments**
- conduct **communal propaganda**

Despite India being a **secular state**, religion often influences:

- voting preferences
- political mobilisation.

---


## 3. Language


Language also influences voting behaviour.


Political parties sometimes **mobilise linguistic identity** during elections.


Example:


**Linguistic reorganisation of states (1956)** highlighted the importance of language in politics.


---


### Examples of Linguistic Political Parties

- **DMK** in Tamil Nadu
- **TDP** in Andhra Pradesh

These parties emerged partly due to **linguistic pride and identity politics**.


---


## 4. Region (Regionalism)


Regional identity plays an important role in elections.


**Regionalism and sub-regionalism** influence voting decisions.


This has led to the **rise of regional parties**.


Examples:

- parties appealing to **regional pride**
- parties demanding **greater autonomy**

Sometimes **secessionist groups even call for election boycotts**.


---


## 5. Personality of Leaders


The **charismatic personality of leaders** significantly influences voters.


Charismatic leadership means **exceptional and attractive leadership qualities**.


Examples of leaders who influenced voting behaviour:

- Jawaharlal Nehru
- Indira Gandhi
- Rajiv Gandhi
- Jay Prakash Narayan
- Atal Bihari Vajpayee
- Narendra Modi

At the **state level**, regional leaders also influence voters strongly.


---


# Determinants of Voting Behaviour (Continued)


## 6. Money Factor


The **role of money** is an important determinant of voting behaviour.


Although **election expenditure limits exist**, large amounts of money are still spent during elections.


Common practices include:

- distribution of **cash**
- distribution of **liquor**
- distribution of **goods**

Thus, sometimes **votes are exchanged for material benefits**.


However, money does not always influence voting behaviour, especially during **wave elections**.


---


## Wave Election (Important Concept)


According to **Paul Brass**:


A **wave election** occurs when a **strong political trend develops across the electorate in favour of a particular party or leader**.


### Characteristics of Wave Elections

1. Voters across different regions show **similar political preference**.
2. The wave is usually driven by **major national issues**.
3. Local factors become **less important**.
4. **Uncommitted voters shift towards one direction**.

The trend spreads rapidly among people.


Paul Brass described it as spreading:

> “from village to village and tea stall to tea stall.”

### Example

- **1977 General Election**
- **2014 General Election**

---


# 7. Performance of the Ruling Party


Before elections, political parties release an **Election Manifesto**.


### Election Manifesto


It is a document containing:

- promises
- policies
- programmes
- commitments made to voters.

Voters judge the **performance of the ruling party** based on whether it fulfilled these promises.


---


### Anti-Incumbency Factor


If voters are dissatisfied with the ruling party's performance, they vote against it.


This phenomenon is called:


**Anti-Incumbency**


Meaning:

> Voters reject the ruling party due to dissatisfaction.

Examples:

- **Congress defeat in 1977 elections**
- **Janata Party defeat in 1980 elections**

Thus, government performance strongly affects voting behaviour.


---


# 8. Party Identification


Some voters have **emotional or psychological attachment to a political party**.


Such voters:

- consistently support the same party
- vote for that party **irrespective of candidate performance**

This is known as **Party Identification**.


### Historical Trend in India


Strong party identification was common during:

- **1950s**
- **1960s**

However, since the **1970s**, strong party loyalty has **declined**.


---


# 9. Ideology


Political ideology also influences voting behaviour.


### Meaning of Ideology


Ideology refers to a **set of political beliefs or principles**.


Examples:

- Communism
- Capitalism
- Democracy
- Secularism
- Patriotism
- Decentralisation

Some voters support parties that **represent their ideological beliefs**.


However, in India the number of **ideology-driven voters is relatively small**.


---


# 10. Other Determinants of Voting Behaviour


Apart from the above factors, several **additional social and political factors** influence voting behaviour.


These include:


### 1. Political Events


Events occurring before elections may influence voters.


Examples:

- wars
- assassination of leaders
- corruption scandals

---


### 2. Economic Conditions


Economic situation at the time of elections influences voters.


Examples:

- inflation
- unemployment
- food shortages

---


### 3. Factionalism


Factionalism is a common feature of Indian politics.


It means:

> conflicts and divisions within political parties or groups.

Factional rivalries influence electoral outcomes.


---


### 4. Age


Voting behaviour differs between:

- young voters
- elderly voters

Young voters may prefer **change**, while older voters may prefer **stability**.


---


### 5. Sex (Gender)


Voting patterns may differ between:

- men
- women

Women voters sometimes prioritise **welfare policies and social security schemes**.


---


### 6. Education


Educational level influences political awareness.


Educated voters tend to:

- evaluate policies
- analyse candidates

Uneducated voters may rely more on:

- identity factors
- local influence.

---


### 7. Habitation


Voting behaviour varies between:

- rural areas
- urban areas.

Rural voters may prioritise:

- agriculture
- subsidies
- local leadership

Urban voters may focus on:

- employment
- infrastructure
- development.

---


### 8. Class (Income)


Income groups influence political preferences.


Example:

- poorer voters may support welfare policies
- richer voters may support economic reforms.

---


### 9. Family and Kinship


Family members often influence each other’s voting decisions.


Political preferences may be shared among:

- relatives
- community networks.

---


### 10. Candidate Orientation


Voters may evaluate the **individual qualities of candidates**, such as:

- honesty
- experience
- reputation
- leadership ability.

---


### 11. Election Campaign


Campaign methods strongly influence voters.


Examples:

- rallies
- speeches
- advertisements
- social media campaigns.

---


### 12. Political Family Background


Candidates belonging to **political families** may gain advantage due to:

- name recognition
- established networks.

---


### 13. Role of Media


Media has become a major factor influencing voting behaviour.


It shapes:

- public opinion
- political awareness
- electoral participation.

# Role of Media in Elections and Voting Behaviour


Media plays an important role in **modern democratic elections**.


The Election Commission recognises media as an important **partner in the electoral process**.


---


# 1. Information Dissemination


Media spreads information related to elections.


It informs voters about:

- election announcement
- nomination process
- scrutiny of nominations
- campaigning
- polling arrangements
- counting of votes
- declaration of results.

Thus, media answers the questions:


**What, When, Where, and How of elections.**


Media also reports:

- violations of Model Code of Conduct
- election disturbances
- administrative changes.

---


### Candidate Information


Media publishes information about candidates such as:

- educational qualifications
- financial assets
- criminal records.

This information is taken from **affidavits filed by candidates with nomination papers**.


This increases:

- **transparency**
- **accountability** in elections.

---


# 2. Enforcement of Model Code of Conduct (MCC)


Media acts as a **watchdog of democracy**.


It exposes:

- misuse of money power
- misuse of muscle power
- hate speeches
- divisive political campaigns.

Such reports help the **Election Commission take action**.


Media also spreads awareness about:

- ethical voting
- election laws
- Model Code of Conduct.

---


# 3. Compliance with Election Laws


The Election Commission does not directly regulate the media.


However, it ensures compliance with **election laws related to media activities**.


Important laws include:


---


## Section 126A – Representation of the People Act, 1951


This section **prohibits exit polls during certain periods**.


Exit polls cannot be conducted or published:

- from the beginning of polling in the **first phase**
- until **30 minutes after the last phase of polling ends**.

Purpose:


To prevent influencing voters during elections.


---


## Section 126 – Representation of the People Act, 1951


This section prohibits:

> displaying election-related matter on television, cinema or other electronic media **during the 48 hours before polling ends**.

This period is called:


**Silent Period**


Purpose:


Prevent last-minute propaganda.


---


## Section 127A – Representation of the People Act, 1951


This section regulates:

- printing
- publication

of election materials like:

- posters
- pamphlets
- leaflets.

Requirement:


Every printed election material must include:

- **name of printer**
- **name of publisher**
- **address of printer and publisher**.

---


## Section 171H – Indian Penal Code


This section prohibits **unauthorised election expenditure**.


No person can spend money on election advertisements **without authorisation from the candidate**.


---


# 4. Voter Education and Participation


Media helps increase **voter awareness**.


Many voters are unaware of important information such as:

- voter registration procedures
- identity proof requirements
- polling station location
- use of EVMs
- polling timings.

Media educates voters about:

- democratic values
- voting rights
- responsibilities.

Special focus is given to:

- youth voters
- uneducated citizens
- remote rural populations
- socially and economically weaker sections.

---


# 5. Responsibility of Government Media


Public broadcasters must remain **neutral and objective** during elections.


Major public broadcasters include:

- **All India Radio**
- **Doordarshan**

These organisations are managed by **Prasar Bharati**.


---


### Free Broadcast Time


The Election Commission provides **free broadcasting time** on:

- All India Radio
- Doordarshan

for recognised:

- National parties
- State parties.

Purpose:


To ensure a **level playing field in elections**.


This allows parties to communicate their policies to voters even in **remote regions of the country**.


---


# Other Government Organisations Supporting Election Awareness


Several organisations assist the Election Commission in voter awareness programmes.


These include:

- Press Information Bureau (PIB)
- Directorate of Advertising and Visual Publicity (DAVP)
- National Film Development Corporation
- Directorate of Field Publicity
- Song and Drama Division
- State Information Departments.

These organisations help spread awareness about:

- voting rights
- electoral participation.
