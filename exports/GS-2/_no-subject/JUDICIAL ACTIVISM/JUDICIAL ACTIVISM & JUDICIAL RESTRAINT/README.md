# 3. MEANING OF JUDICIAL ACTIVISM


### Core Idea


**Judicial Activism = proactive role of judiciary to enforce constitutional governance and protect rights.**


**In simple terms**

> Judiciary compels **Legislature + Executive** to perform constitutional duties.

### Key Functions

- Protection of **citizens’ rights**
- Promotion of **justice**
- Enforcement of **constitutional governance**

### Other Name


**Judicial Dynamism**


### Opposite Concept


**Judicial Restraint**


Meaning → Judiciary **limits interference** in executive and legislative functions.


---


# 4. SCHOLARLY DEFINITIONS


### Definition 1 (Legal Scholars)


Judicial activism =

> exercise of judicial power that **departs from strict precedent** to promote **progressive social policies**.

**Key characteristics**

- Departure from precedent
- Progressive policy orientation
- Social engineering by courts
- Possible intrusion into legislative/executive domains

---


### Definition 2 (Merriam-Webster Dictionary of Law)


Judicial activism =

> judiciary **protects or expands individual rights** through decisions beyond established precedent or legislative intent.

**Key elements**

- Expansion of individual rights
- Independent judicial interpretation
- May go beyond precedent

---


# 5. JUDICIAL ACTIVISM AND PIL


### Relationship


**Judicial Activism → Rise of Public Interest Litigation (PIL)**


### Key Points

- PIL = **most visible form of judicial activism**
- Expanded **access to justice**
- Liberalised **locus standi**

### Locus Standi


Meaning → **Right to approach the court**


Judicial activism allowed:

- NGOs
- Social activists
- Public-spirited citizens

to file petitions.

# 11. JUDICIAL ACTIVISM vs JUDICIAL RESTRAINT


| Judicial Activism                             | Judicial Restraint                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Judiciary plays **active role in governance** | Judiciary **limits intervention**    |
| Expands rights creatively                     | Strict constitutional interpretation |
| Intervenes in policy matters                  | Avoids policy matters                |
| Progressive interpretation                    | Conservative interpretation          |


---


# 12. MEANING OF JUDICIAL RESTRAINT


Judicial restraint = **self-limitation of judicial power**.


### Core Principles

- Courts **interpret law**, not create it.
- Law-making belongs to **Legislature + Executive**.
- Judges must avoid **policy-making**.
- Decisions must reflect **original intent of constitution framers**.

---


# 13. ASSUMPTIONS OF JUDICIAL RESTRAINT (USA DOCTRINE)


| Assumption                   | Explanation                              |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Court is undemocratic        | Judges not elected                       |
| Judicial review questionable | Not explicitly in US Constitution        |
| Separation of powers         | Courts must respect boundaries           |
| Federalism                   | Deference to state governments           |
| Institutional limitations    | Courts depend on other branches          |
| Aristocratic notion          | Judiciary should avoid political matters |


### Applicability to India


Most apply **except judicial review**, because:


**Judicial review is explicitly provided in Indian Constitution.**

# 14. SUPREME COURT OBSERVATIONS ON JUDICIAL RESTRAINT (2007)


In **December 2007**, the **Supreme Court emphasised judicial restraint**.


### Key Observations


| Observation                                               | Explanation                                              |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Judges should not perform executive/legislative functions | Judicial activism must not become **judicial overreach** |
| Judges must know their limits                             | Avoid behaving like **“emperors running government”**    |
| Montesquieu warning                                       | Separation of powers essential for liberty               |
| Avoid judicial adventurism                                | Decisions must remain within constitutional limits       |
| Respect administrative expertise                          | Courts lack specialised governance knowledge             |
| Judiciary also imperfect                                  | Example: cases pending **50+ years**                     |
| Democratic remedy exists                                  | Political failures corrected through **elections**       |
| Judiciary lacks administrative machinery                  | Cannot run government                                    |
| Judicial restraint protects balance                       | Maintains institutional harmony                          |
