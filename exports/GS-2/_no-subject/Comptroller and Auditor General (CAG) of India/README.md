# Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India


---


## 1. Constitutional Provision

- The **Constitution of India (Article 148)** provides for the **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India**.
- The CAG is the **head of the Indian Audit and Accounts Department**.

### Key Role

- **Guardian of the Public Purse**.
- Controls the **entire financial system of the country** at:
    - **Union level**
    - **State level**

### Main Duty


To ensure that **financial administration follows:**

- Constitution of India
- Laws passed by Parliament.

### Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's Statement


Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called the CAG:

> **“The most important Officer under the Constitution of India.”**

### Pillars of Indian Democracy


CAG is considered one of the **bulwarks (गढ़) of democratic system**, along with:

1. Supreme Court
2. Election Commission
3. Union Public Service Commission
4. CAG

---


# 2. Appointment of CAG


### Appointing Authority

- **President of India**

### Method

- By a **warrant under his hand and seal**.

---


# 3. Oath of Office


Before entering office, the CAG takes an oath before the **President** to:

1. Bear **true faith and allegiance to the Constitution**
2. Uphold **sovereignty and integrity of India**
3. Perform duties **without fear or favour, affection or ill-will**
4. Uphold the **Constitution and laws**

---


# 4. Term of Office


The CAG holds office:

- **6 years OR**
- **Until the age of 65 years**

Whichever occurs **earlier**.


---


# 5. Resignation


The CAG may resign by:

- Writing a **resignation letter to the President of India**

---


# 6. Removal of CAG


The CAG **cannot be removed easily**.


### Removal Authority


President of India


### Removal Procedure


Same as **Judge of Supreme Court**


Meaning:


Removal requires:

- **Resolution passed by both Houses of Parliament**
- **Special Majority**

### Grounds of Removal

1. **Proved Misbehaviour**
2. **Incapacity**

---


# 7. Independence of CAG


The Constitution ensures **high independence** of the CAG through several safeguards.


## 1. Security of Tenure

- Cannot be removed except by the **constitutional procedure**.
- Does **not hold office during the pleasure of the President**.

## 2. No Further Government Employment


After retirement, CAG:

- **Cannot hold any office** under:
    - Government of India
    - State Governments

Purpose:


Prevents **political influence or bias**.


---


## 3. Salary Determined by Parliament

- Salary decided by **Parliament**.
- Salary is **equal to a Supreme Court Judge**.

---


## 4. Salary Cannot Be Reduced


After appointment:


These cannot be changed to his disadvantage:

- Salary
- Leave rights
- Pension
- Retirement age

---


## 5. Administrative Independence

- Conditions of service of **Indian Audit and Accounts Department staff** are determined by the **President after consultation with the CAG**.

---


## 6. Expenses Charged on Consolidated Fund of India


Expenses of the CAG office including:

- Salaries
- Allowances
- Pensions

are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India**.


Meaning:

- **Not subject to vote of Parliament**

---


## 7. No Minister Responsible for CAG

- No minister can represent the CAG in Parliament.
- No minister is responsible for actions of CAG.

Meaning:


➡ CAG works **independently of the executive**.

# 8. Duties and Powers of CAG


### Constitutional Provision


**Article 149** empowers **Parliament** to define the **duties and powers of the CAG**.


Accordingly, Parliament enacted:


### CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971


Purpose:

- Defines the **audit functions of the CAG**

### Amendment in 1976


The Act was amended in **1976** to:


➡ **Separate accounts from audit in the Central Government**.


This reform is known as:


**Departmentalisation of Accounts**


Meaning:

- Central Government accounts are now maintained by **separate departments**
- CAG mainly performs **audit functions**

---


## Consolidated Fund Vs Contingency Fund


| Basis                  | Consolidated Fund                                                  | Contingency Fund                          | Public Account                                |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning                | Main government account where all revenues and loans are deposited | Emergency fund for unforeseen expenditure | Money held by government on behalf of others  |
| Constitutional Article | Article 266(1)                                                     | Article 267                               | Article 266(2)                                |
| Source of Money        | Taxes, borrowings, loan recoveries                                 | Amount placed by Parliament/Legislature   | Provident funds, small savings, deposits etc. |
| Withdrawal             | Requires approval of Parliament/Legislature                        | Can be used immediately by executive      | No parliamentary approval needed              |
| Purpose                | Regular government expenditure                                     | Emergency/unexpected expenses             | Custodial transactions                        |
| Controlled By          | Parliament/State Legislature                                       | President/Governor                        | Executive government                          |
| Examples               | Income tax, GST, government loans                                  | Disaster relief, urgent defence spending  | PPF, postal savings, GPF                      |

# 9. Duties and Functions of the CAG


The duties are laid down in the **Constitution and CAG Act 1971**.


---


# 1. Audit of Consolidated Fund


CAG audits all expenditure from:

- **Consolidated Fund of India**
- **Consolidated Fund of States**
- **Consolidated Fund of Union Territories with Legislature**

---


# 2. Audit of Consolidated Fund and Public Account


CAG audits expenditure from:


### At Union Level

- **Contingency Fund of India**
- **Public Account of India**

### At State Level

- **Contingency Fund of State**
- **Public Account of State**

---


# 3. Audit of Government Departments


CAG audits:

- Trading accounts
- Manufacturing accounts
- Profit and loss accounts
- Balance sheets
- Other subsidiary accounts

maintained by **Central and State government departments**.


---


# 4. Audit of Receipts and Expenditure


CAG verifies whether:

- Rules and procedures for **assessment**
- **Collection of revenue**
- **Allocation of revenue**

are properly followed.


Purpose:


➡ Ensure **proper financial control**.


---


# 5. Audit of Bodies and Authorities


CAG audits receipts and expenditure of:


### (a) Bodies substantially financed by Government


Example:

- Government-funded institutions

### (b) Government Companies


### (c) Corporations and Bodies


When required by law.


---


# 6. Audit of Government Transactions


CAG audits transactions related to:

- Public debt
- Sinking funds
- Deposits
- Advances
- Suspense accounts
- Remittance business

He may also audit:

- Receipts
- Stock accounts

But requires **approval of the President**.


---


# 7. Audit of Other Authorities


CAG can audit **any authority** when requested by:

- **President**
- **Governor**

Example:


➡ **Local bodies**


---


# 8. Advice on Form of Accounts


Under **Article 150**, CAG advises the President on:


➡ **The format in which accounts of Union and States should be maintained**.


---


# 9. Submission of Audit Reports


### For Union Government


CAG submits reports to:


➡ **President**


President then places them before:


➡ **Both Houses of Parliament**


---


### For State Government


CAG submits reports to:


➡ **Governor**


Governor places them before:


➡ **State Legislature**


---


# 10. Certification of Tax Proceeds


Under **Article 279**


CAG:

- **Ascertains**
- **Certifies**

the **net proceeds of taxes and duties**.


Meaning of Net Proceeds:


**Tax revenue – Cost of collection**


The CAG's certificate is **final**.


---


# 11. Role with Public Accounts Committee


CAG acts as:


### “Guide, Friend and Philosopher”


to the **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** of Parliament.


PAC examines CAG reports.


---


# 12. Compilation of Accounts of States


CAG compiles and maintains accounts of:


➡ **State Governments**


But after **1976 reform**:


CAG **no longer compiles Central Government accounts**.


---


# 10. Types of CAG Reports


The CAG submits **three audit reports** to the President:


### 1. Audit Report on Appropriation Accounts


Shows:


➡ Comparison between:

- **Actual expenditure**
- **Expenditure approved by Parliament**

Purpose:


Check whether spending stayed within **budget authorization**.


---


### 2. Audit Report on Finance Accounts


Shows:

- Annual **receipts**
- Annual **disbursements**

of the **Union Government**.


---


### 3. Audit Report on Public Undertakings


Covers:


➡ **Government enterprises**


---


### What Happens Next?


1️⃣ Reports submitted to **President**


2️⃣ President lays them before **Parliament**


3️⃣ **Public Accounts Committee examines them**


PAC then submits its report to Parliament.


---


# 11. Role of the CAG


The CAG ensures:


➡ **Financial accountability of the Executive to Parliament**.


The **Council of Ministers** is accountable to Parliament through **CAG audit reports**.


Therefore:


➡ **CAG is an agent of Parliament**.


He is **responsible only to Parliament**.


---


# 12. Scope of Audit


CAG has **more freedom in auditing expenditure** than in auditing receipts.


### Expenditure Audit


CAG can:

- Decide scope of audit
- Frame audit manuals and codes

---


### Receipts Audit


CAG must follow:


➡ Rules approved by the **Executive Government**.


---


# 13. Types of Audit


## 1. Legal and Regulatory Audit


Mandatory audit.


Purpose:


Check whether money spent:

- Was **legally available**
- Was **used for the authorized purpose**
- Conformed to **rules and authority**

---


## 2. Propriety Audit


Discretionary audit.


Checks:

- **Wisdom**
- **Faithfulness**
- **Economy**

of government expenditure.


Example:

- Wasteful spending
- Extravagant expenditure

---


# 14. Limitation on CAG


### Secret Service Expenditure


CAG cannot ask details of:


➡ **Secret service expenditure**


Instead:


He must accept a **certificate from competent authority** confirming the spending.


---


# 15. Comptroller vs Auditor Role


Although the Constitution calls him:


➡ **Comptroller and Auditor General**


In practice he works mostly as:


➡ **Auditor General only**.


Reason:


CAG does **not control withdrawal of money** from Consolidated Fund.


Many departments can issue **cheques directly**.


CAG audits **after expenditure occurs**.


---


# Comparison with Britain

# 16. CAG and Public Corporations


The CAG’s role in public corporations falls into **three categories**.


---


# Category 1 — Direct Audit by CAG


Corporations audited **fully by CAG**.


Examples:

- Damodar Valley Corporation
- Oil and Natural Gas Commission
- Air India
- Indian Airlines Corporation

---


# Category 2 — Private Audit + CAG Oversight


Corporations audited by:


➡ **Private auditors appointed by Government**


But:


➡ **CAG may conduct supplementary audit**


Examples:

- Central Warehousing Corporation
- Industrial Finance Corporation

---


# Category 3 — Completely Private Audit


Audit done **entirely by private auditors**.


CAG has **no role**.


Examples:

- Life Insurance Corporation (LIC)
- Reserve Bank of India
- State Bank of India
- Food Corporation of India

These corporations submit reports **directly to Parliament**.


---


# 17. CAG and Government Companies


Government companies are:

- Audited by **private auditors**
- Appointed by **Government on advice of CAG**

CAG may also conduct:

- **Test audit**
- **Supplementary audit**

---


# 18. Audit Board


Established in **1968**.


Purpose:


To assist the CAG in auditing **specialised enterprises**.


Examples:

- Engineering
- Iron & steel
- Chemical industries

Recommendation Source:


➡ **Administrative Reforms Commission**


Structure:

- Chairman
- Two members

Appointed by **CAG**.

# 19. Appleby’s Criticism of CAG


Paul H. Appleby criticized the Indian audit system in his reports:

1. **Public Administration in India (1953)**
2. **Reexamination of India's Administrative System (1956)**

He even suggested **abolishing the office of CAG**.


---


# Appleby’s Arguments


### 1. Colonial Legacy


CAG system is a **remnant of colonial rule**.


---


### 2. Repressive Effect


Audit creates:


➡ Fear among administrators


which reduces **decision-making ability**.


---


### 3. Parliament Overestimates Audit


Parliament places **too much importance on auditing**.


---


### 4. Auditors Don't Understand Administration


Auditors are not experts in **administration or governance**.


---


### 5. Limited Usefulness


Audit is:


➡ A **narrow and pedestrian function**.


---


### 6. Department Officers Know More


A **Deputy Secretary in a department** understands departmental issues better than auditors.
