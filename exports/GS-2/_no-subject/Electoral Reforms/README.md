
# ELECTORAL REFORMS : OVERVIEW


Electoral reforms refer to reforms introduced in:

- Electoral system
- Election machinery
- Election process
- Political funding
- Candidate eligibility/disqualification
- Transparency in elections

### Objectives

- Free & fair elections
- Reduce criminalization of politics
- Control money power
- Increase voter participation
- Ensure transparency
- Strengthen democracy

---

# CLASSIFICATION OF ELECTORAL REFORMS


Electoral reforms are studied under 4 heads:

1. Electoral reforms before 1996
2. Electoral reforms of 1996
3. Electoral reforms after 1996
4. Electoral reforms since 2010

---


# ELECTORAL REFORMS BEFORE 1996


# 1. LOWERING OF VOTING AGE


## Constitutional Amendment

- 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988

## Change


Voting age reduced:

- From 21 years → 18 years

## Applicable For

- Lok Sabha elections
- State Assembly elections

## Objective

- Give youth representation
- Increase political participation

### Important Prelims Fact


Voting age reduced by:


21→18 (61th CA → 1988)


---


# 2. DEPUTATION TO ELECTION COMMISSION (1989)


### Provision


Officers/staff involved in:

- Preparation
- Revision
- Correction of electoral rolls

are deemed to be on deputation to Election Commission.


### Effect


During deputation they work under:

- Control
- Superintendence
- Discipline

of Election Commission.


### Importance

- Strengthened independence of ECI.

---


# 3. INCREASE IN NUMBER OF PROPOSERS (1989)


## Applicable To

- Rajya Sabha elections
- Legislative Council elections

## New Rule


Proposers required:

- 10% of electors OR
- 10 electors
(whichever is less)

## Objective

- Prevent non-serious candidates

---


# 4. INTRODUCTION OF ELECTRONIC VOTING MACHINES (EVMs)


## Provision Made

- 1989

## First Experimental Use

- 1998
- Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Delhi Assembly Elections

## First Full State Use

- Goa Assembly Election, 1999

## Advantages

- Faster counting
- Reduced invalid votes
- Reduced booth capturing
- Environment-friendly

---


# 5. BOOTH CAPTURING MADE GROUND FOR ADJOURNMENT/COUNTERMANDING


## Provision Introduced

- 1989

## Booth Capturing Includes:


(i) Seizing polling station and forcing officials to surrender:

- Ballot papers
- Voting machines

 (ii) Allowing only supporters of one party to vote


(iii) Threatening/preventing voters from voting


(iv) Seizing counting centre


---


# 6. ELECTOR PHOTO IDENTITY CARD (EPIC)


## Decision Taken

- 1993 by Election Commission

## Purpose

- Prevent bogus voting
- Prevent impersonation

## Basis

- Electoral Roll

## Importance

- Simplified electoral process
- Increased authenticity

---


# ELECTORAL REFORMS OF 1996


Implemented mainly on basis of:

- Dinesh Goswami Committee recommendations

## Law

- Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 1996

---


# 1. LISTING OF NAMES OF CANDIDATES


Candidates classified into 3 categories:

1. Recognized political parties
2. Registered unrecognized parties
3. Independent candidates

### Order on ballot paper:

- Same sequence maintained

### Within category:

- Alphabetical order

---


# 2. DISQUALIFICATION UNDER PREVENTION OF INSULTS TO NATIONAL HONOUR ACT, 1971


Person convicted for following offences disqualified for 6 years:

1. Insulting National Flag
2. Insulting Constitution
3. Preventing National Anthem

---


# 3. PROHIBITION ON SALE OF LIQUOR


## Ban Period


48 hours before polling ends.


## Applicable Places

- Shops
- Hotels
- Public/private places

## Purpose

- Prevent voter bribery/influence

---


# 4. NUMBER OF PROPOSERS


## For Independent Candidates

- 10 proposers needed

## For Recognized Party Candidate

- Only 1 proposer

## Objective

- Discourage non-serious candidates

---


# 5. DEATH OF A CANDIDATE


## Earlier


Election countermanded if candidate died before poll.


## Now


Election not cancelled.


### Exception


If deceased belonged to recognized political party:

- Party may nominate another candidate within 7 days.

---


# 6. TIME LIMIT FOR BY-ELECTIONS


## Rule


By-election within:


6 months


## Exceptions


(i) Remaining tenure less than 1 year


(ii) ECI + Central Government certify difficulty


---


# 7. PAID HOLIDAY ON POLLING DAY


Applicable to:

- Employees
- Daily wage workers

### Exception


If absence causes:

- Danger
- Substantial loss

---


# 8. RESTRICTION ON CONTESTING SEATS


Candidate can contest:

- Maximum 2 constituencies

Applicable to:

- Lok Sabha
- Assembly
- Rajya Sabha
- Legislative Council

---


# 9. PROHIBITION OF ARMS


Entering polling station area with arms:

- Cognizable offence

### Exceptions

- Returning officer
- Presiding officer
- Police officers
- Security personnel

---


# 10. CAMPAIGN PERIOD REDUCED


Minimum gap between:

- Last withdrawal date
- Polling date

Reduced:


20 days→14 days


---

Staff can be taken from:

- Local authorities

- Universities

Certain classes of persons allowed:

- Voting through postal ballot

### Determined by:

- Election Commission + Government

# 4. PROXY VOTING (2003)


Applicable to:

- Armed forces
- Service voters

### Requirement


Appointment of proxy in prescribed format.


---


# 5. DECLARATION OF CRIMINAL ANTECEDENTS & ASSETS (2003)


Candidates must disclose:


## (i) Criminal record

- Conviction
- Acquittal
- Imprisonment
- Fine

## (ii) Pending criminal cases


If:

- Punishment ≥ 2 years
- Charges framed

## (iii) Assets

- Movable
- Immovable
- Bank balance

of:

- Candidate
- Spouse
- Dependents

## (iv) Liabilities

- Government dues
- Financial institution dues

## (v) Educational qualifications


### Important


False affidavit = Electoral offence


---


# 6. RAJYA SABHA ELECTION REFORMS (2003)


## (A) Domicile Requirement Removed


### Earlier


Candidate must be elector in same state.


### Now


Can be elector from:

- Any parliamentary constituency in India

---


## (B) OPEN BALLOT SYSTEM INTRODUCED


### Earlier


Secret ballot


### Now


Open ballot


### Objective

- Prevent cross voting
- Reduce money power

---


# 7. CAMPAIGN TRAVEL EXPENSE EXEMPTION


Travel expenses of star campaigners:

- Not included in candidate expenditure

---


# 8. FREE SUPPLY OF ELECTORAL ROLLS


Government supplies free:

- Electoral rolls
- Prescribed materials

to recognized political parties.


---


# 9. POLITICAL DONATIONS (2003)


Political parties can accept donations from:

- Individuals
- Companies

### Exception

- Government companies not allowed

---


## Reporting Requirement


Donation above:


₹20000₹20000₹20000


must be reported to ECI.

# 10. TIME ALLOCATION ON ELECTRONIC MEDIA


ECI allocates equitable time on:

- Cable TV

Past performance of political party

1. Indian citizen

# 8. NOTA (NONE OF THE ABOVE)


## Introduced


2013


## Based On


Supreme Court directions


## Purpose


Right not to vote while maintaining secrecy.


---


## Important Point


Even if NOTA gets highest votes:

- Candidate with highest valid votes wins.

# 9. VVPAT (VOTER VERIFIABLE PAPER AUDIT TRAIL)


## Meaning


Independent verification system attached to EVM.


## Features


Slip shows:

- Serial number
- Candidate name
- Symbol

for:


7 seconds


then falls into sealed box.


---


## Importance

- Transparency
- Auditability
- Verification

---


## Supreme Court Observation


VVPAT is:

- “Indispensable requirement of free and fair elections”

---


## First Use

- Noksen Assembly Constituency, Nagaland (2013)

---


# 10. PERSONS IN JAIL/POLICE CUSTODY CAN CONTEST


## Supreme Court Case


Chief Election Commissioner vs Jan Chaukidar (2013)


### Court Initially Held


Person without voting right cannot contest.


---


## Parliament Response


Representation of People (Amendment & Validation) Act, 2013


### Effect


Persons in:

- Jail
- Police custody

can contest elections.


---


# 11. IMMEDIATE DISQUALIFICATION OF CONVICTED MPs/MLAs


## Supreme Court Case


Lily Thomas vs Union of India


## Judgement


Convicted MPs/MLAs immediately disqualified.


## Struck Down


Section 8(4) of Representation of People Act, 1951


### Earlier


3 months given for appeal.


### Now


Immediate disqualification.


---

- Same names
- Similar names

### Important


No photo ≠ rejection of nomination

Maximum:


7.5%

Mandatory disclosure in profit-loss account.

# 15. ELECTORAL BONDS (2018)


## Objective

- Clean political funding
- Transparency

---


## Features


(i) Bearer banking instrument


(ii) No buyer/payee name


(iii)Can be purchased by:

- Indian citizens
- Indian entities

(iv) Eligible political party:

- Must secure at least:1%
votes in last Lok Sabha/Assembly election

(v) Can be encashed only through authorized bank account.


---


## Denominations

- ₹1,000
- ₹10,000
- ₹1 lakh
- ₹10 lakh
- ₹1 crore

---


## Confidentiality


Buyer identity confidential except:

- Court order
- Criminal investigation
