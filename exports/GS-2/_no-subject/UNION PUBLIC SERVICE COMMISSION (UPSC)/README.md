
# UNION PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION (UPSC)


_(Articles 315–323, Part XIV of the Constitution)_


## 1. Introduction

- The **Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)** is the **central recruiting agency of India**.
- It is an **independent constitutional body**.
- It has been **directly created by the Constitution of India**.

### Constitutional Provisions

- Articles **315–323** in **Part XIV** deal with:
    - Composition
    - Appointment
    - Removal
    - Independence
    - Powers
    - Functions of UPSC.

📌 Therefore UPSC derives its authority **directly from the Constitution**, not from a law of Parliament.


---


# 2. COMPOSITION OF UPSC


## Structure


The UPSC consists of:

- **Chairman**
- **Other Members**

They are **appointed by the President of India**.


The **Constitution does NOT fix the number of members**.


➡ The **President determines the strength** of the commission.


### Usual Strength


Generally:

- **9 to 11 members including the chairman**

---


## Qualification of Members


The Constitution **does not prescribe detailed qualifications**.


However, it mandates:

- **At least half of the members must have held office for 10 years** under:
    - Government of India, OR
    - State Government.

This ensures **administrative experience in the Commission**.


---


## Conditions of Service

- Determined by the **President of India**.
- Applies to:
    - Chairman
    - Members.

---


# 3. TENURE


Chairman and members hold office:

- **6 years**, OR
- Until **65 years of age**

➡ **Whichever is earlier**


---


## Resignation


They can resign anytime by addressing their resignation to:


➡ **President of India**


---


# 4. ACTING CHAIRMAN


The **President may appoint a member as Acting Chairman**.


### Situations when Acting Chairman is appointed


1️⃣ When **office of chairman becomes vacant**


2️⃣ When chairman **cannot perform duties due to absence or other reason**


The acting chairman works:

- Until a **new chairman is appointed**, OR
- Until the **existing chairman resumes duties**.

---


# 5. REMOVAL OF UPSC CHAIRMAN OR MEMBERS


The **President of India can remove them from office**.


### Grounds of Removal


### 1. Insolvency


If the person is **declared insolvent (bankrupt)**.


---


### 2. Paid Employment


If during tenure he engages in **paid employment outside official duties**.


---


### 3. Infirmity


If the President believes the person is **unfit due to mental or physical infirmity**.


---


# 6. REMOVAL FOR MISBEHAVIOUR


For **misbehaviour**, a special procedure is followed.


### Procedure


1️⃣ President refers the case to the **Supreme Court**.


2️⃣ Supreme Court conducts **enquiry**.


3️⃣ If the **Supreme Court advises removal**, the President removes the member.


⚠️ The **advice of the Supreme Court is binding on the President**.


---


## Suspension


During enquiry:


➡ President may **suspend the chairman or member**.


---


# 7. MISBEHAVIOUR DEFINED


A member is guilty of misbehaviour if he:


1️⃣ Is **concerned or interested in any contract or agreement with the Government of India or a State Government**


2️⃣ Participates in **profit from such contract**


3️⃣ Receives benefit **other than as a member of an incorporated company**.


---


# 8. INDEPENDENCE OF UPSC


The Constitution provides several safeguards to ensure the **independent and impartial functioning** of UPSC.


---


## 1. Security of Tenure

- Members can be removed **only in the manner specified in the Constitution**.

Thus they cannot be removed arbitrarily.


---


## 2. Service Conditions Protection

- Conditions of service **cannot be changed to their disadvantage after appointment**.

---


## 3. Charged Expenditure

- Salaries
- Allowances
- Pensions

are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India**.


➡ Therefore they are **not subject to vote of Parliament**.


---


## 4. Restriction on Future Employment


### Chairman


After retirement:


❌ Cannot hold **any office under Government of India or State Government**.


---


### Member


After retirement:


✔ Can become:

- **Chairman of UPSC**
- **Chairman of State PSC**

❌ Cannot hold any **other government employment**.


---


## 5. No Reappointment


Chairman or member **cannot be reappointed after completing their first term**.


---


# 9. FUNCTIONS OF UPSC


The UPSC performs several important functions related to **civil services recruitment and personnel management**.


---


## 1. Conducting Examinations


UPSC conducts examinations for:

- **All India Services**
- **Central Services**
- **Public services of Union Territories**

Examples:

- IAS
- IPS
- IFS
- Civil Services Exam

---


## 2. Joint Recruitment


UPSC assists **two or more states** in:

- Framing
- Operating

schemes for **joint recruitment** when **special qualifications are required**.


---


## 3. Serving State Needs


UPSC may serve the **needs of a state**:

- On request of **State Governor**
- With **approval of President**.

---


## 4. Consultation on Personnel Matters


UPSC is consulted on various **personnel management matters**.


### (i) Recruitment Methods


Methods of recruitment to:

- Civil services
- Civil posts.

---


### (ii) Appointment Principles


Principles for:

- Appointment
- Promotion
- Transfer between services.

---


### (iii) Suitability of Candidates


UPSC evaluates suitability for:

- Appointments
- Promotions
- Transfers
- Deputations

Departments recommend candidates and UPSC **ratifies them**.


---


### (iv) Disciplinary Matters


UPSC is consulted in disciplinary cases against civil servants.


These include:

- **Censure** (severe disapproval)
- **Withholding increments**
- **Withholding promotions**
- **Recovery of financial loss**
- **Reduction to lower rank (Demotion)**
- **Compulsory retirement**
- **Removal from service**
- **Dismissal from service**

---


### (v) Legal Expense Claims


Claims for **reimbursement of legal expenses** incurred by a civil servant while defending a case related to official duties.


---


### (vi) Injury Pension


Claims regarding **pension for injuries sustained while serving government**.


---


### (vii) Temporary Appointments


Matters related to:

- Temporary appointments exceeding **one year**
- Regularisation of such appointments.

---


### (viii) Extension of Service


Cases related to:

- Extension of service
- Re-employment of retired civil servants.

---


### (ix) Other Personnel Matters


Any additional personnel management issues referred to UPSC.


---


# ⚖ Supreme Court Observations


The **Supreme Court has ruled**:

- Failure to consult UPSC **does not invalidate government decisions**.

➡ Hence consultation is **directory, not mandatory**.


Also:

- Selection by UPSC **does not give an automatic right to appointment**.

Government must still act **fairly and without arbitrariness**.


---


# 10. ADDITIONAL FUNCTIONS OF UPSC


Apart from the functions already mentioned, the **Parliament can confer additional functions on UPSC**.


### Extension of Jurisdiction


Through a **law made by Parliament**, the UPSC can also handle:

- Personnel matters of
    - **Local authorities**
    - **Statutory bodies**
    - **Corporate bodies**
    - **Public institutions**

Thus, the **jurisdiction of UPSC can be extended** by Parliament.


---


# 11. REPORT OF UPSC


The UPSC is required to submit a **report of its work every year**.


### Procedure


1️⃣ UPSC submits the **annual report to the President of India**


2️⃣ The **President lays the report before both Houses of Parliament**


3️⃣ Along with the report, the President presents a **memorandum explaining**:

- Cases where **government did not accept UPSC advice**
- **Reasons for non-acceptance**

---


### Important Rule


If the government rejects UPSC advice:


➡ The decision must be approved by the **Appointments Committee of the Union Cabinet (ACC)**.


❌ **Individual ministries or departments cannot reject UPSC advice independently.**


This ensures **executive accountability before Parliament**.


---


# 12. LIMITATIONS OF UPSC


Certain matters are **kept outside the functional jurisdiction of UPSC**.


In other words, **consultation with UPSC is not required** in the following cases:


---


## 1. Reservation Matters


UPSC is **not consulted**:

- While making **reservation of appointments or posts for backward classes**

---


## 2. Claims of SC/ST


UPSC is **not consulted** when:

- Considering **claims of Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes** in appointments.

---


## 3. Certain High Posts


UPSC is **not consulted for selection to**:

- Chairmanship of **commissions**
- Membership of **commissions or tribunals**
- **Highest diplomatic posts**
- Majority of **Group C and Group D services**

---


## 4. Temporary Appointments


UPSC consultation is not required for:

- Temporary appointments
- Officiating appointments

➡ Provided the person **does not hold the post for more than one year**.


---


# 13. POWER OF PRESIDENT TO EXCLUDE MATTERS


The **President of India can exclude certain posts or matters** from UPSC consultation.


### Constitutional Provision


The President can make **regulations specifying matters where UPSC consultation is not necessary**.


These regulations apply to:

- **All India Services**
- **Central services**
- **Central posts**

---


### Parliamentary Control


Such regulations must be:

- **Laid before both Houses of Parliament**
- For **at least 14 days**

Parliament can:

- **Amend**
- **Modify**
- **Repeal**

these regulations.


These are known as:


### UPSC (Exemption from Consultation) Regulations


---


# 14. ROLE OF UPSC


The Constitution visualises UPSC as the:


## “Watchdog of Merit System”


Meaning:


UPSC ensures **merit-based recruitment in civil services**.


---


### UPSC Recruitment Scope


UPSC recruits for:

- **All India Services**
- **Central Services Group A**
- **Central Services Group B**

---


### Advisory Role


UPSC also **advises the government** on:

- Promotion matters
- Disciplinary cases
- Recruitment procedures

However, consultation happens **only when the government refers the matter**.


---


# 15. LIMITATION OF ROLE


The role of UPSC is **limited in two ways**.


---


## 1. Limited Jurisdiction


UPSC does **not deal with certain personnel matters** such as:

- Classification of services
- Pay scales
- Service conditions
- Cadre management
- Training of civil servants

---


### Responsible Authority


These matters are handled by:


## Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT)


DoPT is part of:


### Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions


---


### Three Departments in this Ministry


1️⃣ Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT)


2️⃣ Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances


3️⃣ Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare


---


Thus:

- **UPSC → Recruitment agency**
- **DoPT → Personnel management agency**

---


## 2. Advisory Nature


UPSC recommendations are:


❗ **Only advisory**


They are **not binding on the government**.


The Union Government may:


✔ Accept the advice


❌ Reject the advice


However, if advice is rejected:


➡ Government must explain the reason to **Parliament**.


This creates **parliamentary accountability**.


---


# 16. UPSC AND CENTRAL VIGILANCE COMMISSION (CVC)


The **Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)** was established in:


### 1964


Initially created by **executive resolution**.


Later it received **statutory status in 2003**.


---


## Impact on UPSC


CVC’s emergence affected UPSC’s role in **disciplinary matters**.


This is because:

- Both **UPSC and CVC are consulted in disciplinary cases**.

---


### Problem


Sometimes:

- **UPSC advice and CVC advice may conflict**

---


### Which Body Has More Authority?


UPSC has an **institutional advantage** because:

- UPSC is a **constitutional body**
- CVC was originally created by **executive resolution**

Hence UPSC enjoys **greater constitutional status**.

# 19. IMPORTANT SUPREME COURT OBSERVATION


### Consultation with UPSC


The Supreme Court held:

- Consultation with UPSC is **not mandatory**
- It is **directory in nature**

Therefore:

- Failure to consult UPSC **does not invalidate government decisions**.

- Candidate **does not acquire a right to appointment**

Government must still ensure **fair and non-arbitrary decisions**.
