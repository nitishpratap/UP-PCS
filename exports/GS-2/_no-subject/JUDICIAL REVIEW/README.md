# ORIGIN, MEANING AND TYPES OF JUDICIAL REVIEW


---


## 1. Origin of Judicial Review

- The doctrine of **Judicial Review originated in the United States of America**.
- It was first established in the famous case:

### Marbury v. Madison (1803)

- Decided by **Chief Justice John Marshall** of the **US Supreme Court**.
- This case established that **courts have the power to declare laws unconstitutional**.

### In India

- Judicial Review is **not created by the courts**.
- It is **explicitly provided by the Constitution itself**.

Both the following courts exercise Judicial Review:

1. **Supreme Court**
2. **High Courts**

### Basic Structure Doctrine


The **Supreme Court declared Judicial Review as a Basic Feature of the Constitution**.


Implication:

- Parliament **cannot abolish or curtail Judicial Review**
- Even through **constitutional amendment**

---


# 2. Meaning of Judicial Review


### Definition


**Judicial Review** is the power of the judiciary to examine the **constitutional validity of:**

1. Legislative enactments
2. Executive orders

of both:

- **Central Government**
- **State Governments**

If any law or executive action:

- **Violates the Constitution**
- **Exceeds authority**
- **Violates Fundamental Rights**

Then the court can declare it:

- **Illegal**
- **Unconstitutional**
- **Invalid**
- **Null and Void**

Such law **cannot be enforced by the government**.


---


# 3. Classification of Judicial Review


Justice **Syed Shah Mohamed Quadri** classified Judicial Review into **three categories**:


### 1. Judicial Review of Constitutional Amendments


Courts examine whether a constitutional amendment:

- Violates the **Basic Structure of the Constitution**

Example:

- **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**

---


### 2. Judicial Review of Legislation


Courts review laws made by:

- Parliament
- State Legislatures
- Subordinate legislation (rules, regulations, etc.)

If unconstitutional → declared invalid.


---


### 3. Judicial Review of Administrative Actions


Courts examine the validity of actions taken by:

- Union Government
- State Government
- Public authorities

If they exceed their power → courts can invalidate them.


---


# 4. Important Cases Where Judicial Review Was Used


The Supreme Court has exercised Judicial Review in several landmark cases:

| --------------------------- | ---- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Golaknath Case              | 1967 | Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights |

# SCOPE OF JUDICIAL REVIEW


The **constitutional validity** of a **law or executive order** can be challenged in the **Supreme Court or High Court** on **three grounds**.


## Grounds of Judicial Review


### 1. Violation of Fundamental Rights


A law can be declared unconstitutional if it:

- Violates **Fundamental Rights (Part III)**.

Example:

- If a law violates **Article 14 (Equality)** or **Article 19 (Freedom)**.

Then courts can **invalidate the law**.


---


### 2. Lack of Legislative Competence


A law can be invalid if:

- The authority that made the law **did not have power to make it**.

Example:

- A **State Legislature** making a law on a **Union List subject**.

Because legislative powers are divided under:

- **Union List**
- **State List**
- **Concurrent List**

---


### 3. Repugnancy to Constitutional Provisions


A law may be declared invalid if:

- It is **inconsistent with any provision of the Constitution**.

Example:

- A law violating constitutional principles or provisions.

If yes → law invalid.


---


## 2. Direct Impact Test


Courts examine:

- **Actual effect of law**
- Not just the **form of the amendment**

If impact damages **basic structure** → law invalid.


---


# Important Conclusion of the Court


Even if a law is placed in **Ninth Schedule**:

- It **cannot destroy Basic Structure**.

If it does → courts will invalidate it.


---


# Other Key Conclusions of I.R. Coelho Case


### 1


A law that **abridges Fundamental Rights** may violate basic structure.


If it does → courts can invalidate it.


---


### 2


Each constitutional amendment must be judged **individually**.


Its **actual impact on Fundamental Rights** must be examined.


---


### 3


All amendments **after 24 April 1973** adding laws to Ninth Schedule are **subject to judicial review**.


---


### 4


Protection of Ninth Schedule is **not absolute**.


Courts examine:

- Nature of violation
- Extent of violation

Using:

- Rights Test
- Essence of Rights Test

---


### 5


If a Ninth Schedule law was **already upheld by the Supreme Court earlier**, it cannot be challenged again.


But:


If a law declared unconstitutional is later placed in Ninth Schedule **after 1973**, it can still be challenged.


---


### 6


Past actions already completed under such laws **cannot be reopened**.


This protects legal certainty.
