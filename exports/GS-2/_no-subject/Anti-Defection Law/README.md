
# Anti-Defection Law (दल-बदल विरोधी कानून)


---


# INTRODUCTION

- The **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985** added the **Tenth Schedule** to the Constitution.
- Popularly known as the **Anti-Defection Law**.
- Objective: To prevent **political defections** motivated by:
    - Office/lure of power
    - Monetary benefits
    - Political opportunism

## Constitutional Changes


The amendment changed **4 Articles**:


| Article     | Subject                                |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Article 101 | Vacation of seats in Parliament        |
| Article 102 | Disqualification of MPs                |
| Article 190 | Vacation of seats in State Legislature |
| Article 191 | Disqualification of MLAs/MLCs          |


---


# WHY WAS THE LAW NEEDED?


## Background


During the 1960s–70s:

- Frequent defections destabilized governments.
- Legislators changed parties for:
    - Ministerial posts
    - Money
    - Political gains

### Famous Phrase

- **“Aya Ram, Gaya Ram”**
    - Coined after repeated defections by Haryana MLA **Gaya Lal**.

## Main Objective


To ensure:

- Political stability
- Party discipline
- Ethical politics
- Stable governments

---


# TENTH SCHEDULE : MAIN PROVISIONS


# 1. DISQUALIFICATION ON GROUND OF DEFECTION


## A. Members Belonging to Political Parties


A member is disqualified if:


### (i) Voluntarily gives up membership of party

- Formal resignation is NOT necessary.
- Conduct may indicate voluntary giving up.

### Supreme Court View


Even without resignation:

- Publicly opposing party
- Joining rallies of another party
- Anti-party activities

can amount to voluntarily giving up membership.


---


### (ii) Votes or abstains contrary to party whip


If member:

- Votes against party direction (whip), OR
- Abstains from voting despite whip

→ Disqualification applies.


## Exception


No disqualification if:

- Prior permission obtained, OR
- Party condones action within **15 days**.

---


# PARTY WHIP


## Meaning

- Official direction issued by political party to members regarding voting in House.

## Importance


Ensures:

- Party discipline
- Stability of government
- Passage of important bills

## Types of Whips


| Type            | Meaning                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- |
| One-line whip   | Inform members                  |
| Two-line whip   | Presence requested              |
| Three-line whip | Mandatory attendance and voting |


Violation of three-line whip may attract disqualification.


---


# B. Independent Members

- An independent member is elected without party support.

## Disqualification


If independent member joins any political party after election:


→ Disqualified.


---


# C. Nominated Members


A nominated member may join political party:

- Within **6 months** from taking seat.

After 6 months:

- Joining any party → Disqualification.

---


# EXCEPTIONS TO DISQUALIFICATION


The law provides two exceptions:


---


# 1. MERGER EXCEPTION


Disqualification does NOT apply if:

- Political party merges with another party, AND
- At least **2/3rd members** agree to merger.

## Important

- Valid merger → No disqualification.

---


# 2. PRESIDING OFFICER EXCEPTION


Applicable to:

- Speaker
- Deputy Speaker
- Chairman
- Deputy Chairman

## Provision


If elected as presiding officer:

- Member may resign from party.
- Can rejoin party after leaving office.

## Reason


To maintain:

- Neutrality
- Dignity
- Impartiality of office

---


# SPLIT PROVISION REMOVED


## Earlier Provision


Originally:

- Split by **1/3rd members** protected from disqualification.

## Problem


Led to:

- Mass defections
- Political instability

## 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003

- Deleted split provision.
- Now ONLY merger protection remains.

### Important Prelims Fact


❌ Split = NO protection now


✅ Merger (2/3rd members) = Protection


---


# DECIDING AUTHORITY


## Authority

- Presiding officer of House:
    - Speaker (Lok Sabha/Assembly)
    - Chairman (Rajya Sabha/Legislative Council)

decides disqualification cases.


---


# TIME LIMIT

- Tenth Schedule does NOT prescribe time limit.
- This causes delays and political misuse.

---


# JUDICIAL REVIEW


## Original Position

- Presiding officer’s decision was final.
- Courts barred from interference.

---


# KIHOTO HOLLOHAN CASE (1992)


Kihoto Hollohan vs Zachillhu


## Supreme Court Held


### 1. Judicial review allowed


Speaker acts as:

- Tribunal/quasi-judicial authority.

Hence:

- Decisions subject to judicial review.

---


### 2. Courts can intervene on grounds of:

- Mala fide
- Perversity
- Constitutional violation
- Natural justice violation

---


### 3. But Court also held:


Giving power to Speaker is NOT unconstitutional merely because of political bias possibility.


---


# RULE-MAKING POWER


## Presiding officer can make rules regarding:

- Procedure for complaints
- Inquiry process
- Adjudication

## Such rules must be laid before House for:

- 30 days

House may:

- Approve
- Modify
- Reject

---


# PROCEDURE IN DEFECTION CASES


## Step-wise Process


### Step 1


Complaint filed by House member.


### Step 2


Speaker gives accused member opportunity to explain.


### Step 3


Matter may be referred to Privileges Committee.


### Step 4


Speaker gives final decision.


## Important Point

- Defection does NOT cause automatic disqualification.
- Formal decision necessary.

---


# OBJECTIVES OF ANTI-DEFECTION LAW


The law aims to:

- Prevent unethical defections
- Strengthen parliamentary democracy
- Promote stable governments
- Reduce corruption
- Maintain party discipline

---


# ADVANTAGES OF THE LAW


## 1. Political Stability

- Prevents frequent collapse of governments.

---


## 2. Strengthens Party System

- Maintains discipline among legislators.

---


## 3. Reduces Corruption

- Prevents “horse trading”.
- Reduces expenditure on repeated elections.

---


## 4. Constitutional Recognition to Political Parties

- First constitutional recognition of political parties.

---


# CRITICISMS OF THE LAW


# 1. No Difference Between Dissent and Defection

- Honest disagreement also punished.
- Reduces:
    - Freedom of speech
    - Freedom of conscience
    - Legislative independence

## Criticism


Encourages:

- Party dictatorship
- High-command culture

---


# 2. Legalized Group Defection Earlier


Before 91st Amendment:

- Wholesale defections protected.
- Individual defections punished.

Hence criticism:

> “Retail defections banned, wholesale defections legalized.”

---


# 3. No Action Against Outside Activities


Law applies mainly to:

- Voting inside legislature.

Not sufficient for:

- Anti-party activities outside House.

---


# 4. Discrimination Between Independent and Nominated Members


| Member Type        | Joining Party              |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Independent Member | Immediate disqualification |
| Nominated Member   | Allowed within 6 months    |


Criticized as illogical.


---


# 5. Speaker as Deciding Authority


Criticism:

- Speaker may act politically.
- May delay decisions strategically.

Also:

- Speaker may lack judicial expertise.

---


# IMPORTANT COMMITTEES & RECOMMENDATIONS


# DINESH GOSWAMI COMMITTEE (1990)


Recommended:

- Removal of split exception.

---


# LAW COMMISSION (170th Report, 1999)


Suggested:

- Strengthening anti-defection law.

---


# NATIONAL COMMISSION TO REVIEW WORKING OF CONSTITUTION (NCRWC), 2002


Recommended:

- Delete split provision.
- Debar defectors from ministerial office.
- Limit size of ministry.

---


# 91ST CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT ACT, 2003


## Objectives

- Strengthen anti-defection law
- Reduce jumbo ministries
- Prevent political corruption

---


# MAJOR PROVISIONS


# 1. Ceiling on Central Council of Ministers


Total ministers including PM:


≤ **15% of Lok Sabha strength**


---


# 2. Defectors Disqualified from Becoming Ministers


A disqualified MP:

- Cannot become minister.

---


# 3. Ceiling on State Council of Ministers


Total ministers including CM:


≤ **15% of Legislative Assembly strength**


BUT:

- Minimum = 12 ministers

---


# 4. Defector MLA Also Barred from Ministership


Disqualified legislator:

- Cannot become minister.

---


# 5. Debarred from Remunerative Political Posts


Defector cannot hold:

- Political office with salary/remuneration from public funds.

---


# 6. Split Exception Deleted

- One-third split protection removed.
- Only merger exception retained.

# ONE-LINER REVISION

- Anti-defection law seeks to curb political defections.
- Independent member joining party → disqualified.
- Nominated member may join party within 6 months.
- Speaker acts as tribunal in defection cases.
- Judicial review applicable in anti-defection matters.
- Split protection abolished in 2003.
- Merger protection still valid.
- Tenth Schedule deals with anti-defection law.
