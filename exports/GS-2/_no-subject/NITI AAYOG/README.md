
# PART 1 — NITI AAYOG


## 1. Establishment of NITI Aayog

- On **13 August 2014**, the **Modi Government abolished the 65-year-old Planning Commission**.
- It was replaced by **NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)**.
- **Established:** **1 January 2015**

### Nature of NITI Aayog


Important for prelims.


NITI Aayog is:


| Type                          | Explanation                                          |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Non-constitutional body**   | Not created by the Constitution                      |
| **Non-statutory body**        | Not created by an Act of Parliament                  |
| **Extra-constitutional body** | Created by **Executive Resolution of Union Cabinet** |


Same as Planning Commission.


📌 Key exam point:


**NITI Aayog was created by Executive Resolution of Government of India.**


---


## 2. Role of NITI Aayog


NITI Aayog is the **premier policy Think Tank of Government of India**.


It provides:


1️⃣ **Directional policy inputs**


2️⃣ **Strategic and long-term policy advice**


3️⃣ **Technical advice to Centre and States**


Functions as:

- Policy Think Tank
- Knowledge hub
- Strategic planning institution

---


## 3. Major Change from Planning Commission


Earlier system:


**Planning Commission model**

- Centralised
- Top-down planning
- One-way flow of policy from Centre to States

New system:


**NITI Aayog model**

- Cooperative federalism
- Bottom-up approach
- Partnership with states

Key shift:


| Planning Commission        | NITI Aayog                  |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Centralised planning       | Cooperative federalism      |
| Top-down                   | Bottom-up                   |
| Command economy approach   | Market oriented development |
| One-size-fits-all planning | State-specific development  |


---


# 4. Rationale for Replacing Planning Commission


Government argued that **India has undergone major transformation in 60 years**.


Changes occurred in:

- Political system
- Economy
- Society
- Technology
- Demography

Hence new institution required.


Government stated:

> India needs a body that better serves the **needs and aspirations of the people**.

---


## Foundations of NITI Aayog


The new institution is built on three foundations.


### 1. Empowered Role of States

- States become **equal partners in development**
- Based on **Cooperative Federalism**

### 2. Knowledge Hub


NITI Aayog acts as:

- repository of best practices
- policy think tank
- strategic advisory institution

### 3. Collaborative Platform


Functions:

- monitor development progress
- identify gaps
- coordinate ministries
- facilitate policy implementation

---


## Statement by Finance Minister Arun Jaitley


He stated:


Planning Commission had become **redundant** because:


1️⃣ It suited **command economy**, not modern economy


2️⃣ India has **diverse states with different needs**


3️⃣ One-size-fits-all planning is obsolete


4️⃣ India needs **competitive global economy**


---


## Development Approach of NITI Aayog


NITI Aayog follows **Indian model of development**.


Key idea:

> No single model from outside can be transplanted into India.

Development strategy should be:


**“Bharatiya approach to development.”**

## Structure


### 1. Chairperson


Chairperson = **Prime Minister of India**


---


### 2. Governing Council


Members include:

- Chief Ministers of all States
- Chief Ministers of UTs with legislature:
    - Delhi
    - Puducherry
- Lt. Governors of other UTs

Role:


Highest decision making body.


---


### 3. Regional Councils


Purpose:


Address issues affecting **multiple states or regions**.


Features:

- Temporary
- Formed for specific tenure
- Convened by Prime Minister

Members:

- Chief Ministers of concerned states
- Lt. Governors of UTs

Chair:

- PM or nominee

---


### 4. Special Invitees


These are:

- Experts
- Specialists
- Practitioners

Nominated by:


**Prime Minister**


Purpose:


Provide domain knowledge.

---


## 1. Research Wing


Role:

- Develop in-house expertise
- Policy research
- Sectoral analysis

Members include:

- domain experts
- scholars
- specialists

Purpose:


Act as **dedicated policy think tank**.


---


## 2. Consultancy Wing


Function:


Provides **marketplace of expertise and funding**.


It:

- connects government with solution providers

- acts as **matchmaker**

This allows it to focus on:

- priority areas
- policy guidance
- quality control.

---


## 3. Team India Wing


Composition:


Representatives from:

- all states
- all ministries

Purpose:


Permanent platform for **national collaboration**.


Functions:


1️⃣ Ensures each state/ministry has voice in NITI Aayog


2️⃣ Provides communication channel between states and NITI

# 8. GUIDING PRINCIPLES OF NITI AAYOG


While performing its functions, NITI Aayog follows several guiding principles.


---


## 1. Antyodaya


Concept taken from **Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay**.


Meaning:

- Upliftment of the **poorest and most marginalised sections**.

Focus:

- Poor
- Marginalised
- Downtrodden sections of society

---


## 2. Inclusion


Goal:


Empower vulnerable groups and reduce inequalities based on:

- Gender
- Region
- Religion
- Caste
- Class

---


## 3. Village-Centric Development


Villages must be integrated into the development process.


Reason:


Villages represent the **cultural and economic foundation of India**.


---


## 4. Demographic Dividend


India's greatest asset = **its population**.


Focus areas:

- Education
- Skill development
- Employment generation
- Livelihood opportunities

---


## 5. People's Participation


Development must become **people-driven**.


Important aspects:

- Citizen participation
- Public engagement
- Participatory governance

---


## 6. Governance


Governance should be:

- Open
- Transparent
- Accountable
- Proactive
- Purposeful

Important shift:


**Outlay → Output → Outcome**


Meaning:


| Stage   | Meaning           |
| ------- | ----------------- |
| Outlay  | Budget allocation |
| Output  | Implementation    |
| Outcome | Real results      |


---


## 7. Sustainability


Development must maintain:

- environmental protection
- ecological balance

This aligns with India's **traditional respect for nature**.


---

# 10. CRITICISM OF NITI AAYOG


Opposition parties criticised the replacement of Planning Commission.


Major criticisms include:


---


## 1. “Just Renaming the Planning Commission”


Opposition leaders argued that:


The change from **Planning Commission to NITI Aayog** was only **cosmetic**.


Congress leader **Manish Tewari** said:


The government only changed the **nomenclature**, not the substance.


---


## 2. Policy Influence of Corporates


Critics feared:


Corporate interests may dominate policy-making.


Concern:


NITI Aayog may promote **corporate-led policy decisions**.


---


## 3. CPI(M) Criticism


CPI(M) leader **Sitaram Yechury** called NITI Aayog:


**“Aniti aur Durniti”**


Meaning:

- No policy
- Bad policy

---


## 4. Risk of Discrimination Against States


Concerns raised that:


Finance Ministry could dominate policy decisions and create **Centre bias**.


---


## 5. End of Planning


Veteran CPI leader **Gurudas Dasgupta** argued:


Dissolution of Planning Commission may lead to:

- unregulated economy
- lack of national planning

# 12. ERSTWHILE PLANNING COMMISSION


## Establishment


Established in **March 1950**.


Created by:


**Executive Resolution of Government of India**


Recommendation came from:


**Advisory Planning Board (1946)**


Chairman of board:


**K. C. Neogi**


---


### Nature


Planning Commission was:


| Type                | Status |
| ------------------- | ------ |
| Constitutional body | ❌ No   |
| Statutory body      | ❌ No   |
| Executive body      | ❌ No   |
| Advisory body       | ✔ Yes  |


It was the **supreme organ for planning economic development in India**.


---


# 13. FUNCTIONS OF PLANNING COMMISSION


Major functions:


---


### 1. Resource Assessment


Assess:

- material resources
- capital resources
- human resources

---


### 2. Formulate Development Plans


Prepare national plans for:


efficient use of resources.


---


### 3. Determine Priorities


Identify:

- development priorities
- stages of implementation

---


### 4. Identify Obstacles to Development


Find factors slowing economic growth.


---


### 5. Create Administrative Machinery


Recommend structures needed to implement plans.


---


### 6. Monitor Implementation


Evaluate progress of development plans.


Recommend adjustments when necessary.


---


### 7. Advise Government


Provide advice to:

- Central government
- State governments

---


# 14. ADDITIONAL RESPONSIBILITIES

1️⃣ Public cooperation in national development


2️⃣ Area development programmes


3️⃣ Perspective planning


4️⃣ Institute of Applied Manpower Research

**National Informatics Centre (NIC)** was also under it.

---


### Important Note


Planning Commission had **no executive powers**.

Decision implementation was done by:

### 2. Deputy Chairman

# 19. ABOLITION OF NATIONAL DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL (NDC)


On **1 January 2016**, it was reported that the **Modi Government may abolish the National Development Council (NDC)**.


Proposal:

- Transfer powers of NDC to **Governing Council of NITI Aayog**

However:

- **No official resolution was passed (till August 2016)**.

Important fact:


The **last meeting of NDC** was held on:


📌 **27 December 2012**


Purpose:


Approve the **12th Five-Year Plan (2012–2017)**.


---


# 20. ESTABLISHMENT OF NDC


The **National Development Council (NDC)** was established:


📌 **August 1952**


Created by:


**Executive Resolution of Government of India**


Recommendation came from:


**First Five-Year Plan**


Nature:


| Type                | Status |
| ------------------- | ------ |
| Constitutional body | ❌ No   |
| Statutory body      | ❌ No   |
| Executive body      | ❌ No   |
| Advisory body       | ✔ Yes  |


Thus NDC, like Planning Commission, was **neither constitutional nor statutory**.


---


# 21. COMPOSITION OF NDC


The National Development Council included the following members.


### 1. Chairman


**Prime Minister of India**


---


### 2. Union Cabinet Ministers


Since **1967**, **all cabinet ministers** became members.


Earlier:


Only selected ministers such as:

- Home
- Finance
- Defence
- External Affairs

---


### 3. Chief Ministers of States


All Chief Ministers of Indian states were members.


---


### 4. Administrators / Chief Ministers of Union Territories


Representatives of UTs.


---


### 5. Members of Planning Commission


Members of Planning Commission were also part of NDC.


---


### Secretary of NDC


Secretary of Planning Commission acted as:


**Secretary of NDC**


Planning Commission provided:

- administrative support
- logistical support

---

NDC was created to achieve the following objectives.


---


## 1. Ensure State Cooperation


Secure cooperation of states in **implementation of national plans**.


---


## 2. Mobilise National Resources


Strengthen and mobilise:

- financial resources
- administrative capacity

---


## 3. Promote Common Economic Policies


Ensure coordination in:

- economic policy

---


## 4. Balanced Regional Development


Promote **balanced and rapid development** of all regions of India.


---

To achieve its objectives, NDC performed several functions.


---


## 1. Prescribe Guidelines for National Plan

---


## 2. Examine National Plan

---


## 3. Assess Resources

---


## 5. Review Plan Implementation

Suggest measures to achieve:

- targets

# 24. PROCESS OF APPROVAL OF FIVE-YEAR PLAN


Important for exam questions.


The approval process involved several steps.


---


### Step 1


Planning Commission prepares **Draft Five-Year Plan**.


---


### Step 2


Draft plan submitted to **Union Cabinet**.


---


### Step 3


After Cabinet approval, plan submitted to **NDC**.


Purpose:


NDC gives its **acceptance**.


---


### Step 4


Plan presented before **Parliament**.


---


### Step 5


After Parliament approval:


Plan becomes **official national plan**.


Published in **Official Gazette**.


---


# 25. POSITION OF NDC


NDC was the **highest body below Parliament for planning matters**.


However:

- Its recommendations were **not legally binding**.

NDC served as:

- forum for Centre-State coordination
- advisory body in development planning

NDC was expected to meet:


📌 **At least twice every year**.


---


# 26. ROLE OF NDC IN FEDERAL SYSTEM


The NDC played an important role in:


### 1. Centre-State Coordination


It acted as a bridge between:

- Central Government
- State Governments
- Planning Commission

---


### 2. National Policy Discussion


It served as a forum for discussing:

- national development issues
- economic policies

---


### 3. Shared Responsibility


Enabled **shared responsibility for development planning** in a federal system.


---


# 27. CRITICAL EVALUATION OF NDC


Two opposite views exist regarding NDC.


---


## View 1: Powerful Institution


Some experts described it as:


**“Super Cabinet”**


Reason:

- large and powerful membership
- national level influence

---


## View 2: Rubber Stamp


Others considered NDC a **rubber stamp**.


Reason:


Many policy decisions were already taken by:

- Central Government

NDC simply approved them.


---


### Reason for Rubber Stamp Perception


For many years:

- same party ruled Centre and most states

Therefore:


little opposition during planning discussions.


Later:


rise of **regional parties** strengthened federal character.


---


# 28. EXPERT OPINIONS ON NDC


Several scholars and administrators commented on NDC.


---


## 1. M. Brecher


He described NDC as:


**“Supreme administrative and advisory body on planning.”**


He said:


NDC policy directives were generally **approved by the Cabinet**.


He also observed:


NDC reduced Planning Commission to **research arm**.


---


## 2. H. M. Patel


Former Finance Minister.


He argued:


NDC was **superior to Planning Commission** because of its composition.


Its recommendations were effectively **policy decisions**.


---


## 3. K. Santhanam


Constitutional expert.


He described NDC as:


**“Super Cabinet of the Indian Federation.”**


It functioned like:

- joint cabinet of Centre and States.

---


## 4. A. P. Jain


Former Union Food Minister.


He criticised NDC.


According to him:


NDC sometimes:

- approved policy targets
- without consulting relevant ministries.

He argued:


NDC should **debate and advise**, but final decisions must remain with cabinets.


---
