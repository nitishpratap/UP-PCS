
## 1. Constitutional Background


The **Constitution of India** provides only the **framework of elections**, while **detailed provisions are made by Parliament through laws**.


Certain constitutional articles specify limits but **leave the actual details to legislation**.


### Important Constitutional Articles


| Article     | Provision                                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Article 81  | Maximum number of seats in Lok Sabha                    |
| Article 170 | Maximum number of seats in State Legislative Assemblies |
| Article 171 | Composition of Legislative Councils                     |


These articles:

- Fix **maximum/minimum limits**
- Provide **broad principles**
- Leave **actual allocation of seats to Parliament**

Therefore Parliament enacted the **Representation of the People Act, 1950**.


---


# 2. Representation of the People Act, 1950


## Purpose of the Act


The **Representation of the People Act, 1950** was enacted to provide for:

1. Allocation of seats in **Lok Sabha**
2. Allocation of seats in **State Legislative Assemblies**
3. Allocation of seats in **State Legislative Councils**
4. **Delimitation of constituencies**
5. **Preparation of electoral rolls**
6. **Qualifications and disqualifications of voters**

In simple terms:


**RPA 1950 deals with**

- **Who can vote**
- **Where elections are held**
- **How seats are distributed**

---


# 3. Population Basis for Seat Allocation


While allocating seats:


The **population of each state as on 1 March 1950** was taken into account.


This population was used to determine:

- Number of **Lok Sabha seats**
- Number of **State Assembly seats**

---


# 4. Delimitation of Constituencies


The Act gave powers to the **President** to delimit constituencies.


### Delimitation means


**Drawing boundaries of electoral constituencies.**


The President performs delimitation:

- **After consulting the Election Commission**

Delimitation applies to:

1. Lok Sabha constituencies
2. State Legislative Assembly constituencies
3. Legislative Council constituencies (where applicable)

---


# 5. Electoral Rolls


The Act also provides for:


### Registration of voters for

1. Parliamentary constituencies
2. Assembly constituencies
3. Legislative Council constituencies

It also specifies:

- **Qualifications for voter registration**
- **Disqualifications for voter registration**

---


# 6. Special Provision for Displaced Persons


A **special relaxation of residence requirement** was provided.


Applicable to:


Persons who migrated to India **from Pakistan before 25 July 1949**.


Purpose:


To ensure they could **register as voters even without normal residence proof**.


---


# 7. Preparation of Electoral Rolls


The Act includes provisions for:

1. Preparation of electoral rolls
2. Validity period of rolls
3. Revision of electoral rolls
4. Correction of rolls in special cases

Electoral rolls contain:


**Names of eligible voters in each constituency.**


---


# 8. Validation of Earlier Electoral Roll Work


Before this Act was passed:


The **Constituent Assembly Secretariat had already begun preparing electoral rolls**.


Therefore the Act included a provision to:


**Validate these earlier actions legally.**


---

- Articles **81 & 170** fix **maximum seats** in Parliament and Assemblies.
- **Article 171** deals with Legislative Councils.

# Representation of the People Act, 1951 (RPA 1951)


## 1. Background


The **Representation of the People Act, 1950** did not contain provisions regarding the **actual conduct of elections**.


It only dealt with:

- Allocation of seats
- Delimitation of constituencies
- Preparation of electoral rolls
- Qualification of voters

Therefore, a second law was enacted.


➡ **Representation of the People Act, 1951**.


---


# 2. Purpose of Representation of the People Act, 1951


The Act was enacted to regulate the **entire electoral process**.


It provides provisions for:

1. Qualifications and disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures
2. Notification of elections
3. Administrative machinery for elections
4. Registration of political parties
5. Conduct of elections
6. Election disputes
7. Corrupt practices and electoral offences

Thus:


### RPA 1951 deals with


**How elections are conducted and how candidates contest elections**


---


# 3. Major Provisions of RPA 1951


Broadly the Act contains provisions relating to the following electoral matters:


### 1. Qualifications and Disqualifications for Membership


The Act lays down:

- Qualifications for becoming **Member of Parliament**
- Qualifications for becoming **Member of State Legislature**

It also specifies conditions under which a person can be **disqualified from contesting elections**.


---


### 2. Notification of General Elections


The Act specifies the procedure for:

- Notification of **general elections**
- Notification of **by-elections**

Election notification is issued:


➡ By the **President (for Parliament)**


➡ By the **Governor (for State Legislatures)**


---


### 3. Administrative Machinery for Elections


The Act provides for the machinery required to conduct elections.


Important officials include:

- Chief Electoral Officer
- District Election Officer
- Returning Officer
- Presiding Officer
- Polling Officer

These officers are responsible for conducting elections in their jurisdictions.


---


### 4. Registration of Political Parties


The Act provides for **registration of political parties with the Election Commission**.


Political parties must apply to the **Election Commission of India (ECI)** for registration.


This provision was inserted through **Section 29A**.


Requirements include:

- Party constitution
- Objectives
- Office bearers
- Membership details

---


### 5. Conduct of Elections


This is one of the largest parts of the Act.


It regulates:

- Nomination of candidates
- Scrutiny of nominations
- Withdrawal of candidature
- Polling procedure
- Counting of votes
- Declaration of results

---


### 6. Free Supply of Materials to Recognized Parties


Certain materials are supplied free to candidates of recognized political parties.


Examples include:

- Electoral rolls
- Forms and notices required for election

---


### 7. Election Disputes


The Act provides for settlement of **election disputes**.


Important provisions include:

- Election petitions
- Adjudication by High Courts

If a candidate challenges the election result, they must file an **election petition in the High Court**.


---


### 8. Corrupt Practices and Electoral Offences


The Act defines **corrupt practices during elections**.


Examples include:

- Bribery
- Undue influence
- Booth capturing
- False statements about candidates
- Excessive election expenditure

Such practices may lead to:

- Disqualification
- Cancellation of election

---

# 5. Corrupt Practices (Important for Prelims)


Some important corrupt practices include:


### 1. Bribery


Offering money or gifts to influence voters.


### 2. Undue Influence


Threatening voters to vote in a particular way.


### 3. Appeal on Religion or Caste


Seeking votes based on:

- Religion
- Caste
- Community
- Language

### 4. False Statements


Publishing false information about a candidate's character or conduct.


### 5. Excessive Election Expenditure


Spending more than the permitted election expenditure.


### 6. Booth Capturing


Taking control of a polling booth illegally.


---


# 6. Election Offences


Examples include:

- Illegal voting
- Destruction of ballot papers
- Impersonation
- Disorderly conduct near polling stations

---


# 7. Election Petitions


Election disputes are resolved through **election petitions**.


Key points:

- Filed in **High Court**
- Must be filed within **45 days of election result**
- Court may:
    - Uphold election
    - Declare election void
    - Declare another candidate elected

---


# 8. Bye-Elections


The Act also regulates **bye-elections**.


A bye-election occurs when a seat becomes vacant due to:

- Death of a member
- Resignation
- Disqualification

---

# Delimitation Act, 2002


## 1. Constitutional Basis of Delimitation


The Constitution provides for **readjustment of constituencies after every census**.


Important Articles:


| Article     | Provision                                                 |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Article 82  | Readjustment of Lok Sabha seats after every census        |
| Article 170 | Readjustment of State Legislative Assembly constituencies |
| Article 330 | Reservation of seats for SC/ST in Lok Sabha               |
| Article 332 | Reservation of seats for SC/ST in State Assemblies        |


These articles allow Parliament to make laws for **delimitation of constituencies**.


---


# 2. Meaning of Delimitation


**Delimitation** means:

> The process of fixing or redrawing the boundaries of electoral constituencies.

It ensures that:

- Each constituency has **roughly equal population**
- Representation remains **fair and balanced**

---


# 3. Need for Delimitation


The existing constituencies were based on the **1971 Census**.


Over time several issues emerged:

1. Population growth varied across regions.
2. Migration occurred from **rural to urban areas**.
3. Some constituencies became **much larger than others**.

This created **unequal representation**.


Therefore the **Delimitation Act, 2002** was enacted.


---


# 4. Objective of Delimitation Act, 2002


The Act was enacted to:

1. Establish a **Delimitation Commission**
2. Redraw boundaries of:
    - Lok Sabha constituencies
    - State Assembly constituencies
3. Correct distortions caused by uneven population growth
4. Refix the **number of SC/ST reserved seats**

---


# 5. Basis of Delimitation


The Delimitation under this Act was based on:


➡ **2001 Census**


However:

- **Total number of seats remained based on 1971 census**

This means:


| Aspect                       | Basis       |
| ---------------------------- | ----------- |
| Total number of seats        | 1971 Census |
| Boundaries of constituencies | 2001 Census |


---


# 6. Reservation of Seats for SC/ST


The Act provided for **refixing of reserved seats** for:

- Scheduled Castes
- Scheduled Tribes

This was also done on the basis of **2001 census data**.


However:

- The **total number of seats was not increased**.

---


# 7. Delimitation Commission


The Act provided for creation of a **Delimitation Commission**.


### Main function


To carry out delimitation of:

- Parliamentary constituencies
- Assembly constituencies

---


# 8. Time Limit for the Commission


The Act required that the **Delimitation Commission complete its work by 31 July 2008**.


Originally the Act provided a **two-year period**, but it was later extended.


---


# 9. Application of Delimitation


The new delimitation becomes applicable:

1. From the **next general election**
2. And also for **bye-elections after that election**

Thus once the final order is published:


➡ It automatically applies to future elections.


---

---


# 11. Important Provisions


### 1. Constitution of Delimitation Commission


The Commission is set up to:

- Readjust number of seats
- Redraw boundaries of constituencies

---


### 2. Associate Members


Associate members help the Commission.


They usually include:

- Members of Parliament
- Members of State Legislative Assemblies

From the concerned state.


---


### 3. Powers of the Commission


The Commission has powers to:

- Determine boundaries
- Allocate seats
- Publish orders

Once published:


➡ **Orders cannot be challenged in court.**


(This is a very important exam fact.)


---


### 4. Publication of Orders


After delimitation is completed:

- Orders are published in the **Gazette of India**
- They become **final and legally binding**

---

# 1. Other Acts Relating to Elections


Apart from the **Representation of the People Acts (1950 & 1951)** and the **Delimitation Act**, several other laws regulate the electoral system in India.


---


# 1. Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1959


## Purpose


This Act specifies **which offices of profit will NOT disqualify a person from being a Member of Parliament**.


### Key Idea


The Constitution prohibits MPs from holding **offices of profit under the government**, because it may influence their independence.


However, some offices are declared **not to cause disqualification** under this Act.


### Example


Certain advisory or honorary government positions may not lead to disqualification.


### Important Fact


This Act repealed three earlier Acts:

1. Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1950
2. Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1951
3. Prevention of Disqualification (Parliament and Part C States Legislatures) Act, 1953

---


# 2. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes Orders (Amendment) Act, 1976


## Purpose


This Act deals with:

1. Inclusion of certain castes and tribes in SC/ST lists
2. Exclusion of certain castes and tribes from these lists

The Act helps in **adjusting representation in Parliament and State Assemblies**.


This ensures that **reservation of seats remains accurate according to the updated SC/ST lists**.


---


# 3. Government of Union Territories Act, 1963


This Act provides:

- Legislative Assemblies in some **Union Territories**
- Electoral procedures for those assemblies.

It regulates elections for **UT legislatures**.


---


# 4. Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi Act, 1991


This Act provides the **legislative framework for Delhi**.


It deals with:

- Delhi Legislative Assembly
- Election procedures
- Administrative powers

---


# 5. Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952


This Act regulates elections for:

- **President of India**
- **Vice-President of India**

It provides procedures for:

1. Conduct of presidential elections
2. Conduct of vice-presidential elections
3. Electoral college voting procedures

The Act has been amended in:

- 1974
- 1977
- 1997

---


# 2. Rules Relating to Elections


Rules are detailed procedures issued under the election laws.


---


# 1. Registration of Electors Rules, 1960


These rules provide for:

- Preparation of electoral rolls
- Publication of electoral rolls
- Revision of electoral rolls

These rules ensure that **eligible citizens are registered as voters**.


Earlier similar rules existed in:

- 1950
- 1956

Both were known as:


**Representation of the People (Preparation of Electoral Rolls) Rules**


---


# 2. Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961


These rules regulate the **actual process of conducting elections**.


They ensure **free and fair elections**.


They cover procedures such as:

- Nomination
- Polling
- Counting of votes
- Declaration of results

Earlier similar rules existed in:

- 1951
- 1956

These were called:


**Representation of the People (Conduct of Elections and Election Petitions) Rules**


---


# 3. Prohibition of Simultaneous Membership Rules, 1950


These rules prevent a person from holding **membership in multiple legislatures simultaneously**.


Example:


A person cannot be both:

- Member of Parliament
- Member of State Legislature

at the same time.


---


# 4. Anti-Defection Related Rules (1985)


Two sets of rules regulate disqualification due to defection.


### Members of Lok Sabha (Disqualification on Ground of Defection) Rules, 1985


### Members of Rajya Sabha (Disqualification on Ground of Defection) Rules, 1985


These rules implement the **Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law)**.


---


# 5. Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Rules, 1974


These rules provide **detailed procedures for conducting presidential and vice-presidential elections**.


They replaced earlier rules of **1952**.


---


# 6. Asset Declaration Rules, 2004


Members of Parliament must declare their **assets and liabilities**.


Two sets of rules were introduced:

1. Members of Lok Sabha (Declaration of Assets and Liabilities) Rules, 2004
2. Members of Rajya Sabha (Declaration of Assets and Liabilities) Rules, 2004

---


# 3. Orders Relating to Elections


Orders are administrative directions issued by the **Election Commission of India**.


---


# 1. Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968


This is one of the **most important election orders**.


It deals with:

1. Specification of election symbols
2. Reservation of symbols for political parties
3. Allotment of symbols to candidates
4. Recognition of political parties

Thus this order governs:

- **Party symbols**
- **National and State party recognition**

---


# 2. Registration of Political Parties (Furnishing of Additional Particulars) Order, 1992


This order requires political parties to provide **additional information while registering with the Election Commission**.


It applies to:


Associations or bodies of citizens seeking registration as political parties.
