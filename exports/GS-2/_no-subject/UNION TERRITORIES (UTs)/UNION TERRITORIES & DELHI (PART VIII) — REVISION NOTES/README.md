
Based on Constitutional Provisions under **Articles 239–241**


---


# 1. TERRITORY OF INDIA (ARTICLE 1)


## Territory of India Includes:

1. Territories of States
2. Union Territories
3. Territories acquired by Government of India

➡ These together form the **“Union of India”**.


---


## Important Constitutional Expression


### India = “Union of States”


NOT “Federation of States”


### Why Important?

- Indicates **indestructible Union**
- States do not have right to secede

---

| -------------------- | --------------------------- |

⚠ Current Structure: **28 States + 8 UTs** (after J&K Reorganisation).


---


# 3. STATES vs UNION TERRITORIES


| Feature                 | States                           | Union Territories                     |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Nature                  | Federal units                    | Centrally administered                |
| Relation with Centre    | Power sharing                    | Direct control of Centre              |
| Autonomy                | Constitutional autonomy          | Limited autonomy                      |
| Executive Head          | Governor                         | Administrator/LG                      |
| Constitutional Position | Part of federalism               | Departure from pure federalism        |
| Legislative Power       | Parliament limited on State List | Parliament can legislate on all lists |


---


# 4. EVOLUTION OF UNION TERRITORIES


## During British Rule

- 1874 → Certain areas declared **Scheduled Districts**
- Later became → **Chief Commissioner’s Provinces**

---


## After Independence


Constitution classified states into:


| Category | Description                  |
| -------- | ---------------------------- |
| Part A   | Former Governor’s provinces  |
| Part B   | Former princely states       |
| Part C   | Chief Commissioner provinces |
| Part D   | Andaman & Nicobar            |


Most present UTs came from **Part C & D states**.

# 12. ADMINISTRATION OF UNION TERRITORIES


## Constitutional Position


UTs are administered by:


➡ **President of India**


➡ Through an **Administrator appointed by him**


---


## Important Difference


| State                          | Union Territory                    |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Governor = Constitutional head | Administrator = Agent of President |


Thus UTs remain under **direct Union control**.


---


# 13. DESIGNATION OF ADMINISTRATOR


President decides designation.


Possible titles:

- Lieutenant Governor
- Administrator
- Chief Commissioner

---


## Present Designations


### Lieutenant Governor

- Delhi
- Puducherry
- Andaman & Nicobar Islands

### Administrator

- Chandigarh
- Lakshadweep
- Dadra & Nagar Haveli
- Daman & Diu

---


# 14. GOVERNOR AS ADMINISTRATOR OF UT


Constitution permits:


➡ Governor of adjoining state to act as UT Administrator.


### Important Point


While acting as Administrator:

- Governor acts **independently of State Council of Ministers**.

Example:

- Punjab Governor = Administrator of Chandigarh

---


# 15. UTs WITH LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY

| Delhi      | 1992  |


Remaining UTs are directly administered by Centre.


---


# 16. LEGISLATIVE POWERS OVER UTs


## Parliament


Can legislate on:

- Union List
- State List
- Concurrent List

➡ Strong central control over UTs.


---


## Puducherry Assembly


Can legislate on:

- State List
- Concurrent List

BUT:


➡ Parliament can override.


---


## Delhi Assembly


Can legislate on:

- State List
- Concurrent List

### EXCEPT:

1. Public Order
2. Police
3. Land

These remain under Union Government.


---


# 17. PRESIDENT’S REGULATION MAKING POWER (ARTICLE 240)


President can make regulations for:

- Andaman & Nicobar
- Lakshadweep
- Dadra & Nagar Haveli
- Daman & Diu

---


## Puducherry Special Case


President can make regulations ONLY when:

- Assembly suspended OR
- Assembly dissolved

---


## Importance of Regulation


President’s regulation:

- Has force of Parliamentary law
- Can repeal/amend Parliamentary Act applicable to UT

🔥 Very Important Prelims Point


---

# 20. SPECIAL PROVISIONS FOR DELHI (ARTICLE 239AA)


Granted by:


## 69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991


### Major Features

1. Delhi renamed as NCT of Delhi
2. Legislative Assembly created
3. Council of Ministers created
4. Administrator designated as Lieutenant Governor

---


# 21. EARLIER STRUCTURE OF DELHI


Before 69th Amendment:

- Metropolitan Council
- Executive Council

Had limited powers.


---

| Feature      | Detail                       |

| Strength     | 70 members                   |

---


# 23. DELHI COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


## Size


Maximum:


➡ 10% of Assembly strength


Assembly = 70


Hence:


➡ Maximum Ministers = 7


Includes:

- Chief Minister
- 6 Ministers

---


# 24. APPOINTMENT OF MINISTERS IN DELHI


| Position        | Appointed By           |
| --------------- | ---------------------- |
| Chief Minister  | President              |
| Other Ministers | President on CM advice |


### Tenure


➡ During pleasure of President


---


# 25. RESPONSIBILITY OF COUNCIL


Council of Ministers is:


➡ Collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly.


---


# 26. ROLE OF LIEUTENANT GOVERNOR


LG:

- Administrator of Delhi
- Acts on aid & advice of Council

BUT:


➡ Can act in discretion in some matters.


---


# 27. DIFFERENCE OF OPINION (LG vs CoM)


Procedure:

1. Matter referred to President
2. President’s decision final
3. LG bound by President’s decision

---


# 28. FAILURE OF CONSTITUTIONAL MACHINERY (ARTICLE 239AB)


If governance cannot continue constitutionally:


➡ President may suspend provisions.


Comparable to:


➡ Article 356 (President’s Rule)


Can be imposed:

- On LG report OR
- Otherwise

---


# 29. ORDINANCE MAKING POWER (ARTICLE 239B)


LG can promulgate ordinance when:


➡ Assembly not in session.


### Ordinance Validity

- Same force as Assembly Act
- Must be approved within 6 weeks of reassembly

---


## LG Cannot Promulgate Ordinance When:

1. Assembly dissolved
2. Assembly suspended

---


## Important Point


Prior permission of President required:

- To promulgate ordinance
- To withdraw ordinance

# ULTRA-IMPORTANT PRELIMS FACTS 🚨

1. UT administration → **Articles 239–241**
2. UT administrator → **Agent of President**
3. UTs with legislature → **Delhi & Puducherry**
4. Delhi Assembly cannot legislate on:
    - Public Order
    - Police
    - Land
5. Delhi Assembly strength → **70**
6. Delhi Council of Ministers → **Maximum 7**
7. Delhi HC established → **1966**
8. Delhi special status → **69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991**
9. President’s regulations = force of Parliamentary law
10. Parliament can legislate for UTs on all 3 lists
