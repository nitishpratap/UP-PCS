# 1. TERRITORY OF INDIA (ARTICLE 1)


## Territory of India Includes:

1. Territories of States
2. Union Territories
3. Territories acquired by Government of India

➡ These together form the **“Union of India”**.


---


## Important Constitutional Expression


### India = “Union of States”


NOT “Federation of States”


### Why Important?

- Indicates **indestructible Union**
- States do not have right to secede

# 1. Territory of India (Article 1)


According to **Article 1 of the Constitution**, the territory of India consists of three categories:

1. **Territories of the States**
2. **Union Territories**
3. **Territories that may be acquired by the Government of India**

These together form the **“Union of India”**.


### Key Point for Exam


India is described as a **Union of States**, not a federation of states.


---


# 2. Present Composition of India

- **States** → 28
- **Union Territories** → 8
- **Acquired territories** → None

_(Note: Today India has 28 states and 8 UTs after J&K reorganisation.)_


---


# 3. Difference Between States and Union Territories


### States

- Members of the **federal system**
- Share power with the **Central Government**
- Have **constitutional autonomy**

### Union Territories

- Under **direct control of the Central Government**
- Also called **“Centrally Administered Territories”**

Thus, the existence of UTs is considered a **departure from pure federalism** in India.


---


# 4. Historical Evolution of Union Territories


## British Period


In **1874**, certain regions were declared:


**Scheduled Districts**


Later they became:


**Chief Commissioner’s Provinces**


---


## After Independence


The Constitution initially divided states into:

1. **Part A States**
2. **Part B States**
3. **Part C States**
4. **Part D States**

Many present UTs came under **Part C and Part D states**.

Union Territories were formally created by:


### Two major laws

1. **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**

These reforms reorganised Indian states mainly on **linguistic basis**.

# 1. Constitutional Provisions for Administration


The **administration of Union Territories** is governed by:


### Articles 239 to 241


These provisions are included in **Part VIII of the Constitution**.


Even though all UTs belong to one constitutional category, **their administrative systems are not uniform**.


---


# 2. Administrator of Union Territories


Every Union Territory is administered by:


### The President of India


However, the President exercises this authority through an **administrator appointed by him**.


Thus:


**President → Administrator → UT Administration**


---


## Status of Administrator


The **administrator is an agent of the President**, not a constitutional head like a Governor.


Key point:


| State                           | Union Territory                     |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Governor is constitutional head | Administrator is agent of President |


Therefore, UT administration is **directly controlled by the Union Government**.


---


# 3. Designation of UT Administrator


The **President decides the designation** of the administrator.


Possible titles include:

- Lieutenant Governor
- Chief Commissioner
- Administrator

---


## Present Designations


### Lieutenant Governor

- Delhi
- Puducherry
- Andaman and Nicobar Islands

### Administrator

- Chandigarh
- Dadra and Nagar Haveli
- Daman and Diu
- Lakshadweep

---


# 4. Governor Acting as UT Administrator


The Constitution also allows:


👉 The **Governor of a state** to be appointed as **administrator of an adjoining UT**.


Important feature:


In such cases, the Governor:

- **Acts independently of the State Council of Ministers**
- Performs the role **separately as UT administrator**

This ensures **direct central control** over UT administration.


---


# 5. UTs with Legislative Assembly


Only **two Union Territories** have:

- Legislative Assembly
- Council of Ministers

These are:

1. **Puducherry (since 1963)**
2. **Delhi (since 1992)**

---


# 6. UTs Without Legislative Assembly


The remaining **five UTs do not have elected legislatures**.


These include:

- Andaman and Nicobar Islands
- Chandigarh
- Lakshadweep
- Dadra and Nagar Haveli
- Daman and Diu

These UTs are **directly administered by the President through administrators**.


---


# 7. Central Control Over UTs


Even when UTs have legislatures (Delhi and Puducherry), **Parliament retains supreme authority**.


Meaning:


The creation of a legislature **does not reduce the power of Parliament**.


---


# 8. Legislative Powers Over Union Territories


## Parliament’s Powers


Parliament can make laws for UTs on:

1. **Union List**
2. **State List**
3. **Concurrent List**

This is different from states where Parliament normally **cannot legislate on the State List**.


Thus, UTs remain **under strong central legislative control**.


---


# 9. Legislative Powers of Puducherry Assembly


The Legislative Assembly of Puducherry can make laws on:

- State List
- Concurrent List

However:


Parliament can **override these laws**.


---


# 10. Legislative Powers of Delhi Assembly


Delhi Assembly can legislate on:

- State List
- Concurrent List

But **three subjects are excluded** from Delhi Assembly’s jurisdiction:

1. Public Order
2. Police
3. Land

These subjects remain under **Union Government control**.


---


# 11. President’s Power to Make Regulations


The President has the power to make **regulations for peace, progress and good government** of certain UTs.


This power applies to:

- Andaman and Nicobar Islands
- Lakshadweep
- Dadra and Nagar Haveli
- Daman and Diu

---


## Puducherry Special Case


For Puducherry:


The President can make regulations **only when**:

- Legislative Assembly is **suspended**
- Legislative Assembly is **dissolved**

---


## Legal Status of President’s Regulations


A regulation made by the President:

- Has the **same force as an Act of Parliament**
- Can **repeal or amend an Act of Parliament** applicable to the UT.

This is an **extremely important exam point**.


---


# 12. High Courts for Union Territories


The Constitution allows Parliament to:

1. Establish a **separate High Court for a UT**, or
2. Place a UT under the **jurisdiction of an existing State High Court**.

# 1. Special Provisions for Delhi


Delhi enjoys a **special constitutional status** among Union Territories.


This status was granted by:


### **69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991**


This amendment:

1. Gave Delhi **special status**
2. Renamed Delhi as **National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi**
3. Created a **Legislative Assembly**
4. Created a **Council of Ministers**
5. Designated the Administrator as **Lieutenant Governor**

---


# 2. Earlier Governance Structure of Delhi


Before the 69th Amendment:


Delhi had:

- **Metropolitan Council**
- **Executive Council**

These bodies had **limited powers**.


After the amendment, Delhi received **partial state-like governance**.


---


# 3. Legislative Assembly of Delhi


### Strength of Assembly


The Legislative Assembly consists of:


**70 members**


These members are:

- **Directly elected by the people**

The elections are conducted by:


**Election Commission of India**.


---


# 4. Legislative Powers of Delhi Assembly


The Delhi Assembly can legislate on:

1. **State List**
2. **Concurrent List**

But **three subjects are excluded** from its jurisdiction.


### Subjects excluded

1. Public Order
2. Police
3. Land

These subjects remain under **Union Government control**.


---


# 5. Supremacy of Parliament


Even in matters where Delhi Assembly legislates:


### Parliament has overriding power


If there is **conflict between the two laws**:


➡ **Parliament’s law prevails**


---


# 6. Council of Ministers in Delhi


Delhi also has a **Council of Ministers**.


### Size of Council


The strength is fixed at:


**10% of the total assembly strength**


Since the assembly has **70 members**, the council can have:


**7 ministers**


These include:

- 1 Chief Minister
- 6 other Ministers

---


# 7. Appointment of Ministers


### Chief Minister


Appointed by:


**President of India**


(Not by the Lieutenant Governor)


---


### Other Ministers


Appointed by:


**President on the advice of the Chief Minister**


---


### Tenure of Ministers


The ministers hold office during the:


**Pleasure of the President**


---


# 8. Responsibility of Council of Ministers


The Council of Ministers is:


**Collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly**.


This is similar to the system in the **states and the Union government**.


---


# 9. Role of the Lieutenant Governor


The **Lieutenant Governor (LG)** is the **administrator of Delhi**.


The Council of Ministers:


👉 **Aid and advise the Lieutenant Governor**


However, the LG can act in **discretionary matters**.


---


# 10. Difference of Opinion (LG vs Ministers)


If there is a **disagreement between the LG and Council of Ministers**:

1. The matter is referred to the **President**
2. The **President’s decision becomes final**

The LG must **act according to the President’s decision**.


---


# 11. Failure of Constitutional Machinery in Delhi


If governance cannot be carried out according to the constitutional provisions:


### The President can suspend the provisions.


This is similar to **President’s Rule in States**.


This provision resembles:


### **Article 356**


The President can impose rule:

- On the report of the **Lieutenant Governor**
- Or **otherwise**.

---


# 12. Ordinance Making Power of Lieutenant Governor


The Lieutenant Governor can **promulgate ordinances** when:


➡ The Legislative Assembly is **not in session**.


### Ordinance Status


An ordinance has the **same force as an Act of the Assembly**.


---


### Approval Requirement


The ordinance must be approved within:


**6 weeks of reassembly of the legislature**


Otherwise it **ceases to operate**.


---


### Withdrawal of Ordinance


The Lieutenant Governor can:


➡ **Withdraw the ordinance at any time**


---


### Important Restrictions


The LG **cannot promulgate ordinances when**:

1. Assembly is **dissolved**
2. Assembly is **suspended**

Also:


➡ **Prior permission of the President is required** to promulgate or withdraw an ordinance.
