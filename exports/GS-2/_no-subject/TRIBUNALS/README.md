
# TRIBUNALS

# 1. Introduction to Tribunals


### Constitutional Position

- The **original Constitution of India did NOT contain provisions related to tribunals**.
- The **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** added provisions regarding tribunals.

### New Part Added

- **Part XIV-A** was inserted in the Constitution.

### Title of Part XIV-A


**“Tribunals”**


### Articles Included


| Article          | Subject                     |
| ---------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Article 323A** | Administrative Tribunals    |
| **Article 323B** | Tribunals for other matters |


Thus, the Constitution formally recognised the **tribunal system** after the **42nd Amendment (1976)**.


---


# 2. Administrative Tribunals (Article 323A)


### Meaning


Administrative tribunals are **special courts established to deal with service matters of government employees**.


### Constitutional Provision


**Article 323A empowers Parliament** to establish administrative tribunals.


### Purpose


To adjudicate disputes related to:

- Recruitment
- Conditions of service

of persons appointed in **public services**.


### Covered Employees


Administrative tribunals can handle disputes related to employees of:

- Central Government
- State Governments
- Local Bodies
- Public Corporations
- Other Public Authorities

---


### Important Feature


Article 323A enables Parliament to:

> Remove service matter disputes from **Civil Courts and High Courts** and transfer them to **Administrative Tribunals**.

Thus tribunals act as **specialised adjudicatory bodies**.


---


# 3. Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985


To implement **Article 323A**, Parliament enacted the:


### Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985


This Act provides for:

1. **Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)**
2. **State Administrative Tribunals (SAT)**

### Objective

- Provide **speedy justice**
- Provide **inexpensive justice**
- Reduce burden on **courts**

This marked a **major reform in service dispute resolution**.


---


# 4. Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)


### Establishment

- Established in **1985** under the Administrative Tribunals Act.

### Principal Bench

- **Delhi**

### Additional Benches

- Located in different states.

### Present Structure

- **17 regular benches**

Distribution:

- **15 benches** at **principal seats of High Courts**
- **2 benches** at:
    - Jaipur
    - Lucknow

These benches also hold **circuit sittings** at other locations.


---


# 5. Jurisdiction of CAT


CAT exercises **Original Jurisdiction**.


Meaning:


Cases **start directly in CAT**, not in lower courts.


### Matters Covered

1. Recruitment
2. All service matters

### Employees Covered


CAT jurisdiction applies to:

- All India Services
- Central Civil Services
- Civil posts under Union Government
- Civilian employees of Defence Services

---


### Employees NOT Covered


CAT **does NOT cover**:

1. Members of **Defence Forces**
2. Officers and servants of the **Supreme Court**
3. **Secretarial staff of Parliament**

These disputes go to regular courts.


---


# 6. Composition of CAT


CAT is a **multi-member tribunal**.


### Members include:

- Chairman
- Members

---


### Earlier Structure


Originally CAT had:

- Chairman
- Vice-Chairmen
- Members

---


### After Amendment (2006)


The **Administrative Tribunals Act was amended in 2006**.


Changes:

- **Vice-Chairman post removed**
- Members given **status of High Court judges**

---


### Sanctioned Strength (2016)


| Position | Number |
| -------- | ------ |
| Chairman | 1      |
| Members  | 65     |


Members come from:

- **Judicial stream**
- **Administrative stream**

---


# 7. Appointment of CAT Members


### Appointing Authority


**President of India**


---


### Selection Process


Members are appointed based on recommendations of a:


**High Powered Selection Committee**


Chairperson:

- **A sitting judge of the Supreme Court**

This judge is nominated by:

- **Chief Justice of India**

---


### Final Approval


After:

- **Concurrence of Chief Justice of India**

Appointments are approved by:


**Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC)**.


---


# 8. Tenure of CAT Members


| Position | Maximum Age | Term    |
| -------- | ----------- | ------- |
| Chairman | 65 years    | 5 years |
| Members  | 62 years    | 5 years |


Whichever occurs earlier applies.


Example:


If member reaches **62 years before completing 5 years**, tenure ends.


---


# 9. Procedure of CAT


CAT **does not strictly follow** the:


**Civil Procedure Code (CPC), 1908**


Instead it follows:


### Principles of Natural Justice


Meaning:

- Fair hearing
- No bias
- Reasoned decisions

This ensures:

- Flexible procedures
- Faster disposal of cases.

---


# 10. Filing a Case in CAT


### Application Fee


Very nominal fee:


**₹50 only**


### Representation


The applicant can:

- Appear **personally**, OR
- Appear **through a lawyer**

Thus CAT makes justice **cheap and accessible**.


---


# 11. Appeals Against CAT Orders


### Original Provision


Initially:

- Appeal allowed **only in Supreme Court**
- **High Courts had no jurisdiction**

---


### Landmark Case


### L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)


Supreme Court declared:


The exclusion of High Court jurisdiction **unconstitutional**.


Reason:


**Judicial Review is part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution**.


---


### Current Position


Now appeals against CAT orders go to:


**Division Bench of the concerned High Court**


Thus:


CAT → High Court → Supreme Court


A person **cannot directly approach Supreme Court without first going to High Court**.


---


# 12. State Administrative Tribunals (SAT)


### Legal Basis


The **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985** empowers the **Central Government** to establish **State Administrative Tribunals (SATs)**.


But there is a condition.


### Condition for Establishment


SATs can be established **only on request of the concerned State Government**.


Thus:


State request → Central Government establishes tribunal.

# 16. Jurisdiction of SAT


Just like CAT.


SAT exercises **original jurisdiction** over:

- Recruitment
- Service matters

of **State Government employees**.


Thus:


| Tribunal | Employees Covered            |
| -------- | ---------------------------- |
| CAT      | Central Government employees |
| SAT      | State Government employees   |


---


# 17. Appointment of SAT Members


### Appointing Authority


**President of India**


But consultation is required with:


**Governor of the concerned State**.


---


# 18. Joint Administrative Tribunal (JAT)


The **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985** also allows establishment of a:


### Joint Administrative Tribunal


Meaning:


A single tribunal serving **two or more states**.


---


### Jurisdiction


A JAT exercises **all powers and jurisdiction** of administrative tribunals for those states.


---


### Appointment of Members


Appointed by:


**President of India**


After consultation with:


**Governors of the concerned states**.


---


# 19. Tribunals for Other Matters (Article 323B)


Apart from service matters, tribunals can also be created for **other specialised disputes**.


This is provided under:


### Article 323B


---


### Who Can Establish Tribunals under Article 323B?


Both:

- **Parliament**
- **State Legislatures**

can establish tribunals for matters within their **legislative competence**.


---


# 20. Subjects for Tribunals under Article 323B


Tribunals can be established for disputes related to:

1. **Taxation**
2. **Foreign exchange**
3. **Import and export**
4. **Industrial and labour matters**
5. **Land reforms**
6. **Ceiling on urban property**
7. **Elections to Parliament and State Legislatures**
8. **Foodstuffs**
9. **Rent and tenancy rights**

---


### Important Fact


The subject **“Rent and Tenancy Rights”** was added by:


**75th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1993**.


---


# 21. Differences Between Article 323A and Article 323B


This is an **important exam question**.


| Basis                    | Article 323A                            | Article 323B                    |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Subject                  | Public service matters                  | Other specialised matters       |
| Who can create tribunals | Only Parliament                         | Parliament + State Legislatures |
| Tribunal Structure       | Only one tribunal for Centre and states | Hierarchy of tribunals possible |


---


### Explanation


### 1️⃣ Subject


**Article 323A**


Deals with:

- Recruitment
- Service conditions
- Government employees

**Article 323B**


Deals with:

- Taxation
- Elections
- Labour disputes
- Land reforms
- Rent control etc.

---


### 2️⃣ Authority to Establish


**Article 323A**


Only **Parliament** can create tribunals.


**Article 323B**


Both:

- Parliament
- State legislatures

can create tribunals.


---


### 3️⃣ Tribunal Structure


**Article 323A**

- Only one tribunal allowed.
- No hierarchy.

Example:


CAT for the Centre.


---


**Article 323B**

- A **hierarchy of tribunals** can be created.

Example:


Lower tribunal → appellate tribunal.


---


# 22. Judicial Review of Tribunals


Another **very important constitutional development**.


### Earlier Position


Originally the Constitution allowed:


Exclusion of:

- **High Courts**
- **Supreme Court**

from tribunal matters.


---


### Supreme Court Judgement


### L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)


The Supreme Court declared that:


Certain provisions excluding jurisdiction of courts are **unconstitutional**.


---


### Clauses Declared Unconstitutional

1. **Clause 2(d) of Article 323A**
2. **Clause 3(d) of Article 323B**

---


### Reason


Because:


**Judicial Review is part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution.**


Thus it cannot be removed.


---


### Present Legal Position


Orders of tribunals can now be challenged in:


**High Courts**


and then in


**Supreme Court**.
