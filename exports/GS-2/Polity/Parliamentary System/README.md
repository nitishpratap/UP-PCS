# 2. Concept Notes (For Mains)


## Parliamentary System of Government


India follows a **Parliamentary Executive System** where the **executive is responsible to the legislature**.


### Constitutional Basis


| Level | Articles            |
| ----- | ------------------- |
| Union | Article **74–75**   |
| State | Article **163–164** |


The system is based on **fusion of powers between executive and legislature**.


---


# Classification of Democratic Governments


| Type                     | Key Feature                          |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Parliamentary System** | Executive responsible to Legislature |
| **Presidential System**  | Executive independent of Legislature |

# Major Features of Parliamentary System


## 1. Dual Executive


| Type                  | Meaning        |
| --------------------- | -------------- |
| **Nominal Executive** | President      |
| **Real Executive**    | Prime Minister |


President acts **on advice of Council of Ministers**.


---


## 2. Majority Party Rule


Government is formed by the **party having majority in Lok Sabha**.


Steps:

1. President appoints **Prime Minister**
2. PM advises appointment of **Council of Ministers**

If no majority → **Coalition Government**


---


## 3. Collective Responsibility (Core Principle)


**Article 75**

> Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to **Lok Sabha**.

Meaning:

- Ministers **stand together**
- Ministers **fall together**

Government can be removed by:


**Vote of No Confidence**


---


## 4. Political Homogeneity


Ministers usually belong to **same political party**.


In coalition:

- Consensus among parties required.

---


## 5. Double Membership


Ministers belong to:

- **Executive**
- **Legislature**

Non-MP can be minister but must become MP within **6 months**.


---


## 6. Leadership of Prime Minister


Prime Minister is the **central authority**.


| Role                           | Function                    |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Leader of Council of Ministers | Heads executive             |
| Leader of Parliament           | Controls legislative agenda |
| Leader of ruling party         | Guides party policy         |


Hence system often called **Prime Ministerial Government**.


---


## 7. Dissolution of Lok Sabha


President may dissolve **Lok Sabha** **on advice of Prime Minister**.


This allows **early elections**.


---


## 8. Secrecy of Cabinet


Ministers must maintain **confidentiality of cabinet discussions**.


Before assuming office they take:


**Oath of Secrecy (administered by President)**.

# Presidential System (Concept Overview)


Example: **United States**


### Key Features


| Feature              | Description                                   |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Single Executive     | President = Head of State + Government        |
| Fixed Tenure         | 4 years                                       |
| Non-responsibility   | Executive not responsible to legislature      |
| No dissolution       | Legislature cannot be dissolved               |
| Separation of powers | Executive, legislature, judiciary independent |


Removal method:


**Impeachment**

# Merits of Parliamentary System


| Merit                  | Explanation                          |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Harmony of organs      | Executive part of legislature        |
| Responsible government | Ministers accountable to parliament  |
| Prevents despotism     | Power not concentrated in one person |
| Alternative government | Opposition can form government       |
| Wide representation    | Ministers from various regions       |


---


# Demerits of Parliamentary System


| Demerit                   | Explanation                                  |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Unstable government       | Depends on majority support                  |
| Lack of policy continuity | Governments change frequently                |
| Cabinet dictatorship      | Strong majority leads to executive dominance |
| No separation of powers   | Fusion of powers                             |
| Government by amateurs    | Ministers may lack expertise                 |

# Parliamentary vs Presidential System


| Feature                  | Parliamentary                | Presidential         |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Executive type           | Dual executive               | Single executive     |
| Executive responsibility | Responsible to legislature   | Not responsible      |
| Tenure                   | Not fixed                    | Fixed                |
| Membership               | Double membership            | Single membership    |
| Dissolution              | Lower house can be dissolved | Legislature fixed    |
| Powers                   | Fusion of powers             | Separation of powers |

# Why India Adopted Parliamentary System


## 1. Historical Familiarity


India had experience with parliamentary institutions during **British rule**.


View of **K.M. Munshi**:


India already developed **parliamentary constitutional traditions**.


---


## 2. Preference for Responsibility over Stability


Argument of **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar**:


| System        | Strength       |
| ------------- | -------------- |
| Presidential  | Stability      |
| Parliamentary | Responsibility |


India preferred **responsible government**.


---


## 3. Avoiding Executive-Legislative Conflict


Presidential system often creates **conflict between executive and legislature**.


Example: **US President vs Congress**


---


## 4. Diversity of Indian Society


India is highly diverse:

- Religion
- Language
- Region
- Culture

Parliamentary system allows **wide representation in executive**.

# 1. Parliamentary System of Government


The **Constitution of India provides a Parliamentary form of government at both levels:**


| Level      | Articles              |
| ---------- | --------------------- |
| **Centre** | **Article 74 & 75**   |
| **States** | **Article 163 & 164** |


Thus, India follows the **Parliamentary Executive System**.

# 2. Classification of Democratic Governments


Modern democratic governments are broadly classified into **two types** based on the relationship between **Executive and Legislature**:


| Type                     | Description                                 |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Parliamentary System** | Executive is **responsible to Legislature** |
| **Presidential System**  | Executive is **independent of Legislature** |


### Parliamentary System


Executive remains in office **only as long as it enjoys the confidence of legislature**.


### Presidential System


Executive **does not depend on legislature for survival**.

## 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976

**42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**


(Also called the **“Mini Constitution”** because it made very large changes to the Constitution.)


### 1. Preamble Changed


Three new words were added to the Preamble:

- **Socialist**
- **Secular**
- **Integrity**

So the Preamble became:


**“Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic”**


---


### 2. Fundamental Duties Added

- **Part IVA** was added to the Constitution.
- **Article 51A** introduced **10 Fundamental Duties** for citizens.

Example duties:

- Respect the Constitution and national symbols
- Protect environment
- Promote harmony
- Safeguard public property

---


### 3. Directive Principles Strengthened


DPSPs were given **more importance over Fundamental Rights** in some cases.


New DPSPs added:

- **Article 39A** – Free legal aid
- **Article 43A** – Worker participation in management
- **Article 48A** – Protection of environment

---


### 4. Parliament’s Power Increased

- Parliament’s power to amend the Constitution was strengthened.
- **Judicial review of constitutional amendments was limited**.

---


### 5. Tenure of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies Increased

- Tenure increased from **5 years → 6 years**.

    (Later reversed by **44th Amendment**.)


---


### 6. Tribunal System Introduced

- **Part XIV-A** added.
- Created **Administrative Tribunals** for service matters.

---


### 7. Curbed Power of Judiciary

- Reduced powers of **Supreme Court and High Courts**.
- Some constitutional amendments were made **non-justiciable**.

---


### 8. Changes in Centre–State Relations

- Made the **Central Government stronger** compared to states.

---


### 9. Added Subjects to Concurrent List


Subjects moved from **State List → Concurrent List**:

- Education
- Forests
- Weights & Measures
- Protection of wild animals and birds

---


### 10. Constitutional Amendment During Emergency


This amendment was passed during **The Emergency in India** under the government of **Indira Gandhi**.

## 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978

## 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978


The **44th Constitutional Amendment Act** was passed by the **Janata Party government** mainly to **reverse many changes made by the 42nd Amendment** and **restore democratic safeguards after the Emergency (1975–77)**.


---


### 1. Right to Property Removed as Fundamental Right

- **Article 31** (Right to Property) was **removed from Fundamental Rights**.
- It was made a **legal right under Article 300A**.

➡ Citizens can still own property, but it is **not a Fundamental Right anymore**.


---


### 2. Protection Against Misuse of Emergency


Changes were made in **Article 352**.


Important safeguards added:

- **“Internal disturbance” replaced with “Armed rebellion.”**
- Emergency declaration now requires **written advice of the Cabinet**.
- Parliament must approve Emergency within **1 month**.

---


### 3. Lok Sabha and State Assembly Tenure Restored


The 42nd Amendment increased tenure to **6 years**.


44th Amendment restored it back to:

- **5 years** for **Article 83**
- **5 years** for **Article 172**

---


### 4. Protection of Fundamental Rights During Emergency


The amendment ensured that certain rights **cannot be suspended even during Emergency**.


Rights protected:

- **Article 20**
- **Article 21**

➡ Even during Emergency, **Right to Life and Personal Liberty cannot be suspended**.


---


### 5. Safeguards for Preventive Detention


New protections added:

- Advisory board must review detention cases.
- Maximum detention without review **reduced from 3 months → 2 months**.

---


### 6. President’s Power Clarified


Under **Article 74**:

- President can **once send advice back for reconsideration**.
- After reconsideration, President must accept the advice.

---


### 7. Strengthening Civil Liberties


Many provisions that limited **judicial review** in the 42nd Amendment were removed.


This restored the authority of:

- **Supreme Court of India**
- **High Courts**

---


✅ **UPPCS / UPSC Prelims Quick Points**


Remember **“PAPER”**:

- **P** – Property removed from Fundamental Rights
- **A** – Armed Rebellion replaced Internal Disturbance
- **P** – Protection of Articles **20 & 21** during Emergency
- **E** – Emergency safeguards added
- **R** – Restoration of **5-year tenure**

# 1. Features of Presidential Government


The **American Constitution** provides for the **Presidential form of government**.


The following are its major features.


---


# 1. Single Executive


In a presidential system:


**President = Head of State + Head of Government**


Thus there is **no dual executive**.


Example:


**USA President**


Roles:


| Role               | Meaning         |
| ------------------ | --------------- |
| Head of State      | Ceremonial role |
| Head of Government | Runs executive  |


This is **different from India**, where:

- President = Head of State
- Prime Minister = Head of Government

---


# 2. Fixed Tenure


The President is elected for a **fixed tenure of four years**.


Election method:


### Electoral College


Important point:


President **cannot be removed by legislature easily**.


Only removal method:


### Impeachment


Ground:

> **Grave unconstitutional act**

Body conducting impeachment:


**US Congress**


---


# 3. Cabinet is Advisory


The President governs with help of:


### Cabinet or Kitchen Cabinet


---


### Cabinet


Consists of:


**Departmental Secretaries**


Characteristics:

- Non-elected officials
- Experts in their departments

---


### Kitchen Cabinet


A smaller informal group of advisors.


Important characteristics:

- Personal advisors of President
- Not part of formal government structure

---


### Key Feature


Cabinet members:

- **Appointed by President**
- **Responsible only to President**
- **Removable by President anytime**

---


# 4. Non-responsibility to Legislature


The President and secretaries:

- Are **not responsible to Congress**

Meaning:


They **cannot be removed by Congress through vote of no confidence**.


---


### Also


They:

- Cannot be members of Congress
- Do not attend Congress sessions

This shows **clear separation of powers**.


---


# 5. No Dissolution of Legislature


The President **cannot dissolve the lower house**.


Lower House of Congress:


### House of Representatives


Thus legislature has **fixed tenure independent of executive**.


---


# 6. Separation of Powers


Presidential system is based on:


### Doctrine of Separation of Powers


Three organs function independently.


| Organ       | Function        |
| ----------- | --------------- |
| Legislature | Makes laws      |
| Executive   | Executes laws   |
| Judiciary   | Interprets laws |


Thus:

- No fusion of powers
- Each organ independent

---


# MERITS OF PARLIAMENTARY SYSTEM


The parliamentary system has several advantages.


---


# 1. Harmony Between Legislature and Executive


In parliamentary system:

- Executive is part of legislature.

Thus there is:

- Cooperation
- Coordination
- Less conflict

This creates **smooth functioning of government**.


---


# 2. Responsible Government


Parliamentary system ensures:


### Accountability of executive


Ministers are responsible to Parliament.


---


### Parliamentary Control Tools


Parliament controls executive through:


| Device               | Purpose                |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Question Hour        | Ask questions          |
| Discussions          | Debate policies        |
| Adjournment Motion   | Discuss urgent matters |
| No Confidence Motion | Remove government      |


Thus government remains **accountable to people through Parliament**.


---


# 3. Prevents Despotism


Executive power is **not concentrated in one person**.


Instead it is exercised by:


### Council of Ministers


This dispersal of power prevents:

- Dictatorship
- Autocracy

Also:


Government can be removed through **no confidence motion**.


---


# 4. Ready Alternative Government


If ruling party loses majority:


Head of State may invite **opposition party** to form government.


Thus:


New government can be formed **without fresh elections**.


---


### Famous Statement


Dr. Ivor Jennings said:

> **“Leader of the opposition is the alternative Prime Minister.”**

---


# 5. Wide Representation


Executive consists of **many ministers**.


Prime Minister can select ministers from:

- Different regions
- Different communities
- Different groups

This ensures:


### Inclusive representation


---


# DEMERITS OF PARLIAMENTARY SYSTEM


Despite advantages, it has several weaknesses.


---


# 1. Unstable Government


Government survival depends on:


### Majority support in legislature


If majority is lost:


Government collapses.


---


### Causes of instability

- No confidence motion
- Political defections
- Coalition politics

---


### Examples of unstable governments in India


| Prime Minister  | Government |
| --------------- | ---------- |
| Morarji Desai   | 1977–1979  |
| Charan Singh    | 1979       |
| V P Singh       | 1989–1990  |
| Chandra Shekhar | 1990–1991  |
| Deve Gowda      | 1996–1997  |
| I K Gujral      | 1997–1998  |


---


# 2. No Continuity of Policies


Government may fall anytime.


Thus long-term policies become difficult.


---


### Example


In **1977**:


Janata Government under **Morarji Desai** reversed many policies of previous **Congress government**.


Similarly:


When Congress returned in **1980**, it reversed several Janata policies.


Thus policies lack continuity.


---


# 3. Dictatorship of the Cabinet


When ruling party has **absolute majority**, Cabinet may become very powerful.


Political thinker:


### H J Laski


Said:

> Parliamentary system may lead to **tyranny of executive**.

---


Another thinker:


### Ramsay Muir


Described it as:

> **“Dictatorship of the Cabinet.”**

---


### Example in India


Strong executive dominance during:

- **Indira Gandhi era**
- **Rajiv Gandhi era**

---


# 4. Against Separation of Powers


Parliamentary system does not follow strict separation of powers.


Instead it has:


### Fusion of Powers


Legislature and Executive are interconnected.


---


### Famous observation


Political thinker **Walter Bagehot** said:

> Cabinet is a **hyphen that joins executive and legislature.**

Meaning:


Cabinet connects the two organs.


---


# 5. Government by Amateurs


Ministers are:

- Political leaders
- Not necessarily subject experts

Prime Minister must select ministers from **members of Parliament**.


Thus:


External experts cannot usually become ministers.


---


### Also ministers spend time in:

- Parliamentary debates
- Party meetings
- Cabinet discussions

Thus administrative efficiency may suffer.

# 1. Reasons for Adopting Parliamentary System in India


During the **Constituent Assembly debates**, some members proposed adopting the **US Presidential System**.


Example:


**K.T. Shah** supported the presidential system.


However, the framers finally adopted the **British Parliamentary System**.


The reasons were as follows.


---


# 1. Familiarity with the System


India had experience with the parliamentary system during **British rule**.


For many decades before independence:

- Some degree of **responsible government** had already been introduced.

Thus the Constitution makers were **familiar with this system**.


---


### View of K.M. Munshi


K.M. Munshi said:

> “For the last thirty or forty years, some kind of responsibility has been introduced in the governance of this country. Our constitutional traditions have become parliamentary.”

Thus he argued that India should **continue with the parliamentary system instead of experimenting with a new system**.


---


# 2. Preference for Responsibility over Stability


Dr **B.R. Ambedkar** gave the most famous argument.


He said a democratic executive must satisfy two conditions:

1. **Stability**
2. **Responsibility**

But it is difficult for a system to achieve both equally.


---


### Comparison given by Ambedkar


| System                           | Strength            |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| **American Presidential System** | More stability      |
| **British Parliamentary System** | More responsibility |


---


### Ambedkar’s Conclusion


The Draft Constitution preferred:

> **More responsibility rather than more stability**

Thus India adopted the **Parliamentary Executive**.


---


# 3. Avoiding Executive–Legislative Conflicts


In a **Presidential system**, the executive and legislature are separate.


This often leads to **conflicts between them**.


Example:


Frequent conflicts between **US President and Congress**.


---


### Concern of Constitution makers


India was a **newly independent democracy**.


The framers believed that such conflicts could harm national development.


Thus they preferred a system with:

- Cooperation
- Coordination

Hence they adopted the **parliamentary system**.


---


# 4. Nature of Indian Society


India is one of the **most diverse societies in the world**.


Characteristics of Indian society:

- Religious diversity
- Linguistic diversity
- Regional diversity
- Social diversity

---


### Advantage of Parliamentary System


It allows **wide representation** in government.


Prime Minister can include ministers from:

- Different regions
- Different communities
- Different interest groups

Thus parliamentary system promotes:


### National unity and integration


---


# Debate on Presidential System in India


Since the **1970s**, there has been debate on whether India should shift to a **presidential system**.


To examine this issue:


### Swaran Singh Committee (1975)


was appointed by the **Congress government**.


---


### Recommendation of Swaran Singh Committee


The committee concluded:

> Parliamentary system has been functioning well in India.

Thus:


**No need to replace it with presidential system.**


---


# Distinction Between Indian and British Parliamentary System


India’s parliamentary system is **inspired by Britain**, but it is **not identical**.


There are several important differences.


---


# 1. Republican vs Monarchical System


### India


India has a **Republican system**.


Head of State:


### President


Characteristics:

- **Elected**
- Fixed tenure

---


### Britain


Britain has a **Monarchical system**.


Head of State:


### King or Queen


Characteristics:

- **Hereditary**
- Position passes through royal family

---


# 2. Sovereignty of Parliament


### Britain


British Parliament is **supreme**.


It can:

- Make any law
- Amend any law
- Override courts

Thus Britain follows **Parliamentary Sovereignty**.


---


### India


Indian Parliament is **not supreme**.


Its powers are limited due to:

1. **Written Constitution**
2. **Federal system**
3. **Judicial Review**
4. **Fundamental Rights**

Thus Constitution is **supreme in India**.


---


# 3. Prime Minister’s Membership


### Britain


Prime Minister **must be a member of House of Commons** (Lower House).


---


### India


Prime Minister can be member of:

- **Lok Sabha**, or
- **Rajya Sabha**

---


### Examples


Three Prime Ministers who were Rajya Sabha members:

1. **Indira Gandhi (1966)**
2. **H.D. Deve Gowda (1996)**
3. **Manmohan Singh (2004)**

---


# 4. Appointment of Ministers


### Britain


Ministers must be **members of Parliament**.


---


### India


A person **not a member of Parliament** can also be appointed minister.


But condition:


He must become MP within:


### **6 months**


Otherwise he loses the ministerial post.


---


# 5. Legal Responsibility of Ministers


### Britain


Ministers have **legal responsibility**.


They must:

- **Countersign official acts of the Head of State**

---


### India


India does **not follow legal responsibility**.


Ministers are **not required to countersign President’s acts**.


---


# 6. Shadow Cabinet


This is an **important British institution**.


### Meaning


The opposition party forms a **shadow cabinet**.


Purpose:

- Monitor government policies
- Prepare for future governance

Each opposition leader acts as:


**“Shadow Minister”**


for a particular department.


---


### India


India **does not have shadow cabinet system**.
