# 1. Constitutional Provision


### Article 244


Provides a **special administrative system for certain tribal areas**.


Two categories exist:


| Area Type       | Schedule           | States Covered                                       |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Scheduled Areas | **Fifth Schedule** | All states except Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram |
| Tribal Areas    | **Sixth Schedule** | Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram                   |


Thus:

- **Fifth Schedule → Scheduled Areas**
- **Sixth Schedule → Tribal Areas (North-East)**

These areas receive **special protection because tribal communities are socially and economically backward**.


---


# 2. Why Special Administration for Tribal Areas


Scheduled Areas are treated differently because:

1. Tribal communities are **socially and economically backward**.
2. They have **distinct culture and traditions**.
3. They require **special protection of land and resources**.
4. They need **special administrative attention from the Centre**.

Therefore:

- Normal administrative system **does not fully apply**
- **Central Government has greater responsibility** for their welfare.

---


# 3. Administration of Scheduled Areas


_(Fifth Schedule)_


The Fifth Schedule deals with:

- **Administration and control of Scheduled Areas**
- **Welfare of Scheduled Tribes**

Applicable to **all states except**:

- Assam
- Meghalaya
- Tripura
- Mizoram

These four states are governed by **Sixth Schedule**.


---


# 4. Features of Administration under Fifth Schedule


## 1. Declaration of Scheduled Areas


The **President has the power to declare any area as a Scheduled Area**.


President can:

- Increase the area
- Decrease the area
- Alter boundaries
- Rescind the status
- Redesignate an area

This is done **in consultation with the Governor of the state**.


📌 **UPPCS Important**


President controls **creation and alteration** of Scheduled Areas. → Article 244(1)


---


## 2. Executive Powers of State and Centre


### State Government

- Executive power of the state **extends to Scheduled Areas**.

### Governor


Governor has **special responsibility**.


Governor must:

- Submit **annual report to the President**
- Or whenever President demands it

### Central Government

- Can **give directions to states** regarding administration of Scheduled Areas.

📌 **Exam Trap**


Governor reports **to the President**, not to the State Legislature.


---


## 3. Tribes Advisory Council (TAC)


Every state having **Scheduled Areas must establish a TAC**.


### Purpose


To **advise on welfare and advancement of Scheduled Tribes**.


### Composition


| Feature       | Detail                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------- |
| Total Members | 20                        |
| ST members    | **3/4th must be ST MLAs** |
| Remaining     | Other members             |


A similar council **may also be created in states having Scheduled Tribes but no Scheduled Areas** if **President directs**.


---


## 4. Law Applicable to Scheduled Areas


The **Governor has special legislative powers**.


Governor can direct that:

1. **Act of Parliament may not apply**
2. **Act of State Legislature may not apply**
3. Law may apply **with modifications**

Governor can also **make regulations for peace and good governance** of Scheduled Areas.


These regulations may include:


### Land Protection

- Restrict transfer of tribal land
- Prohibit land transfer between tribes and non-tribes

### Land Allocation

- Regulate allotment of land to tribal people

### Money Lending

- Regulate money lending to tribal communities

Governor’s regulations may:

- **Amend existing laws**
- **Repeal laws**

But such regulations require **President’s assent**.


📌 **Important**


Governor’s regulation power is **very strong** in Scheduled Areas.


---

- Administration of Scheduled Areas

# Administration of Tribal Areas (Sixth Schedule)


---


# 1. Constitutional Provision


The **Sixth Schedule** of the Constitution provides **special provisions for administration of tribal areas**.


It applies to **four North-Eastern states**:

1. Assam
2. Meghalaya
3. Tripura
4. Mizoram

These areas are called **Tribal Areas**.


---


# 2. Reason for Special Provisions


The Constitution treats these four states differently because:

1. Tribal communities in these states **have not assimilated with mainstream society**.
2. They maintain **distinct culture, customs and civilisation**.
3. Their regions historically remained **anthropological specimens**.
4. Therefore, the Constitution provides them **a large degree of autonomy and self-government**.

Thus the **Sixth Schedule gives autonomy to tribal communities**.


---


# 3. Features of Administration under Sixth Schedule


---


# 1. Autonomous Districts


The tribal areas of the four states are divided into **Autonomous Districts**.


Important points:

- They function with **self-government institutions**.
- However, they **remain under the executive authority of the state government**.

Thus autonomy exists **within the state structure**.


---


# 2. Powers of Governor


The **Governor has strong powers over Autonomous Districts**.


Governor can:

- Increase district area
- Decrease district area
- Change boundaries
- Change names
- Reorganize districts

Thus **Governor controls territorial organisation**.


---


# 3. Autonomous Regions


If multiple tribes live within a district:

- Governor can divide the district into **Autonomous Regions**.

Thus structure becomes:


State → Autonomous District → Autonomous Region


---


# 4. District Council


Each Autonomous District has a **District Council**.


### Composition


| Feature               | Details         |
| --------------------- | --------------- |
| Total Members         | 30              |
| Nominated by Governor | 4               |
| Elected Members       | 26              |
| Election Basis        | Adult franchise |


### Tenure


| Member Type       | Tenure                      |
| ----------------- | --------------------------- |
| Elected Members   | 5 years                     |
| Nominated Members | During pleasure of Governor |


District Councils function as **local legislative bodies**.


---


# 5. Regional Councils


Each **Autonomous Region** has its own **Regional Council**.


These councils:

- Administer the region
- Exercise legislative and administrative powers.

---


# 6. Legislative Powers of Councils


District and Regional Councils can make laws on certain subjects.


### Subjects include

1. Land
2. Forests
3. Canal water
4. Shifting cultivation
5. Village administration
6. Property inheritance
7. Marriage
8. Divorce
9. Social customs

However:


All laws require **assent of the Governor**.


---


# 7. Judicial Powers


District and Regional Councils may establish:

- Village courts
- Village councils

These courts handle cases involving **tribal people**.


### Functions

- Trial of civil and criminal disputes among tribes
- Hearing appeals from village courts

The **Governor determines the jurisdiction of the High Court** over these cases.


---


# 8. Development Functions


District Councils can manage development institutions such as:

1. Primary schools
2. Dispensaries
3. Markets
4. Ferries
5. Fisheries
6. Roads

They may also regulate:

- **Money-lending**
- **Trading by non-tribals**

But regulations require **Governor’s approval**.


---


# 9. Financial Powers


District and Regional Councils have taxation powers.


They can:

- Assess and collect **land revenue**
- Impose specified **taxes**.

---


# 10. Applicability of Laws


Acts of:

- Parliament
- State Legislature

may:

1. **Not apply** to autonomous districts
2. Apply **with modifications and exceptions**.

Authority varies by state:


### Assam


Governor decides applicability of both:

- Parliamentary laws
- State laws

### Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram


| Law Type        | Authority |
| --------------- | --------- |
| Parliament Laws | President |
| State Laws      | Governor  |


---


# 11. Commission on Autonomous Districts


The **Governor can appoint a commission** to examine administration of autonomous districts or regions.


The commission studies:

- Administration
- Governance problems
- Development issues

Based on the report:


Governor may **dissolve a district or regional council**.

# 4. Constitutional Philosophy Behind Special Protection


The Constitution gives **special protection to tribal communities** because:

1. They have **distinct culture and customs**.
2. They require **protection of land rights**.
3. They are **economically weaker**.
4. They require **self-governance mechanisms**.

Thus the Constitution follows the principle of:


### “Protective Discrimination”


This concept appears in many places:

- Scheduled Areas
- Reservation
- Tribal autonomy

---


# 5. Governor’s Special Powers (Exam Trap)


Governor plays a **central role in both Fifth and Sixth Schedules**.


### Under Fifth Schedule


Governor can:

1. Modify application of laws
2. Make regulations
3. Protect tribal land
4. Restrict moneylending
5. Submit report to President

---


### Under Sixth Schedule


Governor can:

1. Create autonomous districts
2. Alter boundaries
3. Divide districts into regions
4. Approve council laws
5. Appoint commissions
6. Dissolve councils

Thus **Governor is key authority in tribal governance**.


---


# 6. Self-Government in Sixth Schedule


The Sixth Schedule creates **mini-legislatures for tribal areas**.


These councils have powers over:


### Legislative Functions

- Land
- Forests
- Social customs
- Village administration
- Marriage
- Divorce
- Property inheritance

### Judicial Functions

- Village courts
- Tribal dispute settlement

### Executive Functions

- Schools
- Roads
- Markets
- Fisheries

### Financial Functions

- Tax collection
- Land revenue

Thus Sixth Schedule creates **local self-rule for tribes**.
