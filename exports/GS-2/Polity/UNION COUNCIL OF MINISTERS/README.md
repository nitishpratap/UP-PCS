President may:


➡ **Ask Council of Ministers to reconsider advice**

➡ **President must accept advice**


### Judicial Review

- **Ministerial advice cannot be questioned in court**

Purpose:

- Confidentiality
- Executive independence

# CENTRAL COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


## 1. Introduction


India follows a **Parliamentary System of Government** modeled on the **British system**.


In this system:

- **President → Nominal Executive**
- **Council of Ministers headed by Prime Minister → Real Executive Authority**

Thus, the **Council of Ministers exercises the real executive power in India’s politico-administrative system**.


The principles of the parliamentary system are **not explained in detail in the Constitution** but are mainly covered in:


These Articles form the **constitutional framework of the Council of Ministers**.


| **Article**    | **Subject**                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Article 74** | Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head aids and advises the President; the President acts according to this advice.                                                     |
| **Article 75** | Prime Minister appointed by the President; other ministers appointed on PM’s advice; Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Lok Sabha; minister must become MP within 6 months. |
| **Article 77** | All executive actions of the Government of India are taken in the name of the President; President makes rules for government business allocation among ministers.                        |
| **Article 78** | Prime Minister communicates decisions of the Council of Ministers to the President and provides information when required.                                                                |
| **Article 88** | Ministers and the Attorney General can speak and participate in both Houses of Parliament but can vote only if they are members of that House.                                            |


---


# 2. Constitutional Provisions


## Article 74 – Council of Ministers to Aid and Advise President


### Provision


There shall be a **Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head** to **aid and advise the President**.


The President shall **act in accordance with such advice**.


### Important Provision


The President can:

- **Ask the Council of Ministers to reconsider its advice**

But after reconsideration:

- **President must act according to the advice given again**

### Judicial Review


The **advice given by ministers to the President cannot be questioned in any court**.


This ensures:

- Confidentiality
- Trust between President and Council of Ministers.

---


## Constitutional Amendments Related to Article 74


### 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976


Made **advice of Council of Ministers binding on the President**.


### 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978


Added a proviso:


President can **ask for reconsideration of advice once**.


But after reconsideration:

- **President must accept it.**

---


# 3. Article 75 – Other Provisions as to Ministers


Article 75 deals with:

- Appointment
- Tenure
- Responsibility
- Qualification
- Oath
- Salary of Ministers

### Key Provisions


### 1. Appointment

- **Prime Minister → appointed by President**
- **Other Ministers → appointed by President on advice of PM**

Meaning:


President **cannot appoint ministers on his own**.


---


### 2. Maximum Size of Council of Ministers


The total number of ministers **including PM**:


**Cannot exceed 15% of total strength of Lok Sabha**


Introduced by:


**91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003**


Purpose:

- Prevent oversized ministries
- Reduce political corruption.

---


### 3. Disqualification due to Defection


A Member of Parliament **disqualified under anti-defection law**:


Cannot be appointed as **minister**.


Also added by:


**91st Amendment Act, 2003**


---


### 4. Tenure


Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President**.


But practically:


President acts on **advice of Prime Minister**.


Thus:


PM effectively controls tenure of ministers.


---


### 5. Collective Responsibility


Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to Lok Sabha**.


This is the **foundation of Parliamentary Government**.


Meaning:


If Lok Sabha passes **No Confidence Motion**


→ Entire Council of Ministers must resign.


---


### 6. Oath


Before entering office:


The **President administers oath of office and secrecy** to ministers.


---


### 7. Membership Requirement


A minister must become a **member of Parliament within 6 months**.


If not:


He **ceases to be a minister**.


---


### 8. Salaries


Salary and allowances of ministers are **determined by Parliament**.


---


# 4. Article 77 – Conduct of Government Business


Article 77 regulates the **executive actions of the Government of India**.


### Key Provisions


1️⃣ All executive actions of Government of India are taken **in the name of the President**.


2️⃣ Orders issued in the name of President must be **authenticated according to rules made by President**.


3️⃣ President makes rules for:

- **Transaction of government business**
- **Allocation of work among ministers**

---


# 5. Article 78 – Duties of the Prime Minister


Prime Minister acts as the **link between President and Council of Ministers**.


The PM has following duties:


### 1. Inform the President


PM must communicate to the President:

- Decisions of Council of Ministers
- Legislative proposals

### 2. Provide Information


PM must provide any information regarding:

- Administration of Union affairs
- Legislative proposals

If requested by President.


### 3. Refer Matters to Council of Ministers


If President requires:


A decision taken by an individual minister must be **placed before the Council of Ministers**.


---


# 6. Article 88 – Rights of Ministers in Parliament


Every minister has the right to:

- Speak in **either House of Parliament**
- Participate in **joint sittings**
- Participate in **Parliamentary committees**

But:


They **can vote only in the House where they are members**.


Example:


If a minister is from **Rajya Sabha**:

- Can speak in **Lok Sabha**
- But **cannot vote there**.

---


# 7. Nature of Advice Given by Ministers


Article 74 makes it clear:


President exercises powers **on the advice of Council of Ministers**.


This advice is:


✔ Binding


✔ Confidential


✔ Not subject to judicial inquiry.


---


## Supreme Court Judgments


### 1971 Judgment


Supreme Court held:


Even after **dissolution of Lok Sabha**, the **Council of Ministers continues in office**.


Reason:


Article 74 makes the existence of Council of Ministers **mandatory**.


President **cannot exercise executive power without advice**.


---


### 1974 Judgment


Court clarified:


When Constitution mentions:


**“Satisfaction of the President”**


It actually means:


**Satisfaction of Council of Ministers**.


Thus:


President acts on **ministerial advice**.


---


# 8. Appointment of Ministers


### Appointment Process


1️⃣ President appoints **Prime Minister**.


2️⃣ Other ministers are appointed **on advice of PM**.


Thus:


Real power of minister selection lies with **Prime Minister**.


---


### Membership Requirement


Usually ministers are:

- Members of **Lok Sabha**
- Members of **Rajya Sabha**

But a **non-member can also become minister**.


Condition:


Must become **MP within 6 months**.


Otherwise:


He **ceases to be minister**.


---


### Participation in Parliament


A minister can:

- Speak in both Houses
- Participate in debates

But:


Can **vote only in the House he belongs to**.


---


# 9. Oath of Ministers


Administered by:


**President of India**


Two types:


### 1. Oath of Office


Minister swears:

- Faith to **Constitution of India**
- Uphold **sovereignty and integrity of India**
- Perform duties **faithfully**
- Work **without fear or favour**

---


### 2. Oath of Secrecy


Minister promises:


Not to reveal any matter related to:

- Government decisions
- Cabinet discussions

Except when required for official duties.


---


# 10. Deputy Prime Minister Issue (Important Case)

Argument:


Constitution does **not mention Deputy PM**.


### Supreme Court Decision


Court held:


The term **Deputy Prime Minister is only descriptive**.


It **does not give extra constitutional powers**.


Thus the oath was **valid**.

# 12. Responsibility of Ministers


The Constitution provides **three types of ministerial responsibility**:


1️⃣ Collective Responsibility


2️⃣ Individual Responsibility


3️⃣ No Legal Responsibility


---


# 12.1 Collective Responsibility


### Constitutional Basis


**Article 75(3)**

> The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

---


### Meaning


All ministers are **jointly responsible to Lok Sabha for all actions of government**.


They function like a **team**.


Thus:


**They sink or swim together.**


---


### Consequences


If **Lok Sabha passes a No-Confidence Motion**:


➡ Entire Council of Ministers must resign.


Even ministers from **Rajya Sabha must resign**.


Because responsibility is **towards Lok Sabha only**.


---


### Alternative Option


Instead of resigning, Council of Ministers may advise President to:


➡ **Dissolve Lok Sabha**


and call for **fresh elections**.


However:


President **may refuse** if government has clearly lost majority.


---


### Cabinet Solidarity


Collective responsibility also means:

- Cabinet decisions bind all ministers.
- Ministers must support decisions **inside and outside Parliament**.

If a minister disagrees:


➡ He must **resign**.


---


### Historical Examples of Resignations


1️⃣ **Dr B. R. Ambedkar**


Resigned due to disagreement over **Hindu Code Bill (1953)**.


2️⃣ **C. D. Deshmukh**


Resigned over **State Reorganisation policy**.


3️⃣ **Arif Mohammad Khan**


Resigned due to opposition to **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986**.


---


# 12.2 Individual Responsibility


### Constitutional Basis


**Article 75**


Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President**.


---


### Actual Meaning


President acts **on advice of Prime Minister**.


Thus:


➡ **Prime Minister can remove any minister**


Methods:

1. Ask minister to **resign**
2. Advise President to **dismiss the minister**

---


### Importance


This power allows the PM to:

- Maintain **discipline in Cabinet**
- Enforce **collective responsibility**

---


### Dr B. R. Ambedkar’s Observation


Ambedkar said:

> Collective responsibility can be achieved only through the authority of the Prime Minister to appoint and dismiss ministers.

---


# 12.3 No Legal Responsibility


This concept differs from **British system**.


---


### In Britain


Every order of the King must be **countersigned by a minister**.


If illegal:


➡ Minister is legally responsible in court.


This follows the principle:


**“The King can do no wrong.”**


---


### In India


There is **no system of legal responsibility**.


Reasons:

1. President’s orders do **not require countersignature of ministers**.
2. Courts **cannot inquire into ministerial advice**.

Thus:


Ministers are **politically responsible**, not legally responsible.


---


# 13. Composition of the Council of Ministers


The Council of Ministers consists of **three categories**:


1️⃣ Cabinet Ministers


2️⃣ Ministers of State


3️⃣ Deputy Ministers


Difference lies in:

- Rank
- Salary
- Political importance
- Responsibilities

At the top stands:


**Prime Minister – supreme governing authority of the country**


---


# 13.1 Cabinet Ministers


They head **important ministries**, such as:

- Home
- Defence
- Finance
- External Affairs

### Key Features


✔ Members of **Cabinet**


✔ Attend **Cabinet meetings**


✔ Take **major policy decisions**


✔ Control key departments


Their decisions influence the **entire government machinery**.


---


# 13.2 Ministers of State


They are **second category ministers**.


Two types exist:


### 1️⃣ Ministers of State with Independent Charge


They:

- Head a ministry themselves
- Work independently
- Perform functions similar to Cabinet ministers

But:


They **do not attend Cabinet meetings normally**.


They may attend **only when invited**.


---


### 2️⃣ Ministers of State Attached to Cabinet Ministers


They:

- Assist Cabinet ministers
- Handle specific departmental work
- Work under supervision of Cabinet ministers.

---


# 13.3 Deputy Ministers


They are **third category ministers**.


Characteristics:

- No independent department
- Assist Cabinet ministers or Ministers of State
- Perform administrative and parliamentary duties

They **do not attend Cabinet meetings**.


---


# 13.4 Parliamentary Secretaries


Another category sometimes existed:


**Parliamentary Secretaries**


Features:

- Assist ministers in **parliamentary work**
- No department
- Not significant executive authority

Since **1967**, this post has rarely been used.


---


# 13.5 Deputy Prime Minister


Sometimes government appoints a:


**Deputy Prime Minister**


Important points:


✔ Not mentioned in Constitution


✔ Created mainly for **political reasons**


✔ No special constitutional powers.

# 14. Council of Ministers vs Cabinet


Many people use both terms interchangeably.


But there is an **important difference**.


---


## Council of Ministers vs Cabinet


| Council of Ministers                  | Cabinet                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Large body (60–70 ministers)          | Small body (15–20 ministers)              |
| Includes all categories of ministers  | Includes only Cabinet Ministers           |
| Does not meet as a body               | Meets frequently                          |
| Has no collective functions           | Performs collective decision-making       |
| Powers in theory                      | Powers in practice                        |
| Implements decisions                  | Takes policy decisions                    |
| Constitutional body                   | Mentioned in Article 352 (44th Amendment) |
| Collectively responsible to Lok Sabha | Enforces that responsibility              |

# 15. Role of Cabinet


Cabinet is the **real policy-making body of government**.


### Major Functions


1️⃣ **Highest decision-making authority**


2️⃣ **Policy formulation**


3️⃣ **Supreme executive authority**


4️⃣ **Chief coordinator of administration**


5️⃣ **Advisory body to President**


6️⃣ **Crisis manager (war, emergency)**


7️⃣ Handles **major legislative and financial matters**


8️⃣ Controls **important appointments**


Examples:

- Constitutional authorities
- Senior secretariat officials

9️⃣ Handles **foreign policy and international relations**

**“Dictatorship of the Cabinet.”**

➡ It can control legislation and administration.


This description is considered **applicable to India also**.

# 18. Kitchen Cabinet


Apart from official Cabinet, a smaller informal group exists.


This is called:


**Kitchen Cabinet / Inner Cabinet**


---


### Composition


Usually includes:

- Prime Minister
- 2–4 trusted ministers
- Sometimes trusted friends or advisors.

---


### Purpose


It helps PM in:

- Discussing confidential matters
- Making quick decisions
- Maintaining secrecy.

---


### Advantages


1️⃣ Smaller body → faster decisions


2️⃣ Meets more frequently


3️⃣ Maintains secrecy in political matters

# Quick Revision Points (UPPCS Prelims)


1️⃣ Real executive = **Council of Ministers**


2️⃣ Head of Council of Ministers = **Prime Minister**


3️⃣ Maximum size = **15% of Lok Sabha strength**


4️⃣ Introduced by = **91st Amendment Act 2003**


5️⃣ Collective responsibility = **Lok Sabha**


6️⃣ Minister must become MP within = **6 months**


7️⃣ Advice binding on President = **42nd Amendment**


8️⃣ President can ask reconsideration = **44th Amendment**


9️⃣ Cabinet defined in Constitution = **Article 352**


🔟 Kitchen Cabinet = **Extra-constitutional body**
