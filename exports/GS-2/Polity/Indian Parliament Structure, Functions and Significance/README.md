- Weak voter-representative link

- No by-elections

# PARLIAMENT (भारतीय संसद)


---


# 1. Parliament of India


The **Parliament** is the **legislative organ of the Union Government**.


India follows the **Parliamentary Form of Government** (Westminster Model).


Because of this system, Parliament occupies a **central and supreme position in Indian democracy**.


### Constitutional Provisions


Parliament is dealt with under:


**Articles 79 – 122 (Part V of Constitution)**


These articles deal with:

- Organisation
- Composition
- Duration
- Officers
- Procedures
- Privileges
- Powers

---


# 2. Organisation of Parliament


According to the Constitution, **Parliament consists of three components**

1. **President of India**
2. **Rajya Sabha (Council of States)**
3. **Lok Sabha (House of the People)**

So Parliament =


**President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha**


### Hindi Names (Adopted in 1954)


| English Name      | Hindi Name  |
| ----------------- | ----------- |
| Council of States | Rajya Sabha |
| House of People   | Lok Sabha   |


---


# 3. Nature of the Two Houses


### Rajya Sabha


Also called:

- Upper House
- Second Chamber
- House of Elders

Represents:


**States and Union Territories**


---


### Lok Sabha


Also called:

- Lower House
- First Chamber
- Popular House

Represents:


**People of India**


---


# 4. Role of President in Parliament


Although the **President is not a member of Parliament**, he is still an **integral part of Parliament**.


Reason:


A bill passed by both Houses **cannot become law without the President’s assent**.


### Parliamentary Functions of President


The President performs several parliamentary functions:

1. Summons Parliament
2. Prorogues Parliament
3. Dissolves Lok Sabha
4. Addresses both Houses
5. Issues ordinances when Parliament is not in session
6. Assent to Bills

---


# 5. Indian vs British vs American Model


### Britain


Parliament consists of:

- Crown (King/Queen)
- House of Lords
- House of Commons

Concept:


**Crown-in-Parliament**


---


### India


Similar concept:


**President-in-Parliament**


Executive and legislature are **interdependent**.


---


### United States


Legislature:


**Congress**


Components:

- Senate
- House of Representatives

Important difference:


The **US President is NOT part of the legislature**.


Reason:


USA follows **Presidential System** where **separation of powers** exists.

# 7. Representation of States in Rajya Sabha


Members are **indirectly elected**.


### Electors


Elected members of:


**State Legislative Assemblies**


### Election Method


**Proportional Representation**


By


**Single Transferable Vote (STV)**


---


### Basis of Representation


Seats allocated based on:


**Population**


Therefore:


Large states → more seats


Small states → fewer seats


Example:


| State         | Seats |
| ------------- | ----- |
| Uttar Pradesh | 31    |
| Tripura       | 1     |


---


### Comparison with USA


In USA Senate:


Each state gets **equal representation**.


Example:

- 50 states
- 100 senators
- 2 per state

Irrespective of population.


---


# 8. Representation of Union Territories in Rajya Sabha


Members are elected **indirectly**.


Electors:


**Electoral College specially constituted**


Election method:


**Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote**


---


### Union Territories with Representation


Only **2 UTs have Rajya Sabha members**

1. Delhi
2. Puducherry

Reason:


Other UTs have **very small populations**.


---


# 9. Nominated Members of Rajya Sabha


The President nominates:


**12 members**


They are chosen from people with **special knowledge or practical experience in**

1. Art
2. Literature
3. Science
4. Social Service

Purpose:


To include **eminent personalities without elections**.


Important:


**US Senate has NO nominated members.**

# SYSTEM OF ELECTIONS TO LOK SABHA


The Constitution provides a **specific electoral system** for electing members to the **Lok Sabha**.


The main aspects are:

1. Territorial Constituencies
2. Readjustment after Census
3. Reservation of Seats for SC/ST
4. Territorial Representation System

---


# 1. Territorial Constituencies


For conducting elections to the Lok Sabha:


**Each state is divided into territorial constituencies.**


From each constituency:


**One representative is elected.**


---


## Constitutional Provisions


The Constitution lays down **two principles for representation**.


### Provision 1


Each state is allotted seats in Lok Sabha in such a way that:


**Ratio between number of seats and population remains same for all states.**


However this rule **does not apply to states with population below 6 million.**


---


### Provision 2


Each state is divided into constituencies such that:


**Ratio between population of constituency and number of seats remains same throughout the state.**


---


## Result


This ensures **uniformity of representation** in two ways:

1. Between **different states**
2. Between **constituencies within a state**

---


### Meaning of Population


Population means:


Population according to the **latest census whose figures are officially published**.


---


# 2. Readjustment After Each Census (Delimitation)


After every census:


Two things must be readjusted:

1. **Allocation of seats among states**
2. **Division of states into territorial constituencies**

---


## Authority


Parliament determines:

- the authority
- the method of readjustment

---


## Delimitation Commission Acts


For this purpose Parliament enacted:


| Year | Act                         |
| ---- | --------------------------- |
| 1952 | Delimitation Commission Act |
| 1962 | Delimitation Commission Act |
| 1972 | Delimitation Commission Act |
| 2002 | Delimitation Commission Act |


---


# 3. Freeze on Seat Readjustment


### 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976)


This amendment:


**Froze the allocation of Lok Sabha seats to states.**


Basis:


**1971 Census**


Freeze duration:


Until **year 2000**


Purpose:


Encourage **population control measures**.


---


### 84th Constitutional Amendment Act (2001)


Extended freeze for **25 more years**.


New deadline:


**2026**


Thus:


Seat allocation still based on **1971 population**.


---


### Additional Provision (84th Amendment)


Allowed **readjustment of constituencies** based on **1991 Census**.


But:


Number of seats **remains unchanged**.


---


### 87th Constitutional Amendment Act (2003)


Changed census basis from:


**1991 Census → 2001 Census**


Used for:


Delimitation of constituencies.


But again:


Number of seats **remained unchanged**.


---


# 4. Reservation of Seats for SC and ST


The Constitution abolished:


**Communal representation**


But provides:


**Reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.**


---


## Basis


Reservation is based on:


**Population proportion of SC/ST.**


---


### Important Rule


Even though seats are reserved:


**All voters of the constituency vote.**


Meaning:


There is **no separate electorate**.


---


### Contesting Rules


A person belonging to:

- SC/ST can contest **general seat**
- But general category **cannot contest reserved seat**

---


# Duration of Reservation


Originally reservation was meant for:


**10 years (till 1960)**


But it has been extended **repeatedly**.


Latest extension in text:


**95th Constitutional Amendment Act (2009)**


Extended reservation until:


**2020**


---


### Refixing Reserved Seats


84th Amendment Act (2001)


Allowed refixing of reserved seats based on:


**1991 Census**


---


### Later Change


87th Amendment Act (2003)


Changed basis to:


**2001 Census**


---


# 5. Proportional Representation Not Adopted for Lok Sabha


The Constitution **did not adopt proportional representation** for Lok Sabha elections.


Instead it adopted:


**Territorial Representation System**


---


# Territorial Representation System


Under this system:

- Country divided into **constituencies**
- Each constituency elects **one representative**

Thus:


**Single Member Constituency**


---


## Election Result


The candidate with **majority votes wins.**


This is known as:


**Simple Majority System**


Also called:


**First Past the Post System (FPTP)**


---


## Limitation


This system **does not represent the entire electorate properly**.


Reason:


Minorities may not get representation.


---


# Proportional Representation System


Under this system:


Representation is given **in proportion to population**.


Even smaller groups get representation.


---


## Types of Proportional Representation

1. **Single Transferable Vote System**
2. **List System**

---


## Where PR System is Used in India


India uses **Single Transferable Vote** for:

1. Rajya Sabha elections
2. State Legislative Council elections
3. Presidential election
4. Vice-Presidential election

---


# Why Proportional Representation Not Used for Lok Sabha


Two main reasons:


### 1. Complexity


It would be **difficult for voters to understand**.


Especially considering **low literacy levels at the time of Constitution making.**


---


### 2. Political Instability


PR system may create:


**Too many political parties**


This leads to:


**Unstable governments**


Which is unsuitable for **parliamentary system**.


---


# Additional Demerits of Proportional Representation

1. Very expensive system
2. No scope for by-elections
3. Weak contact between voters and representatives
4. Promotes minority group interests
5. Increases importance of political parties over voters

---

**Permanent House**

# MEMBERSHIP OF PARLIAMENT


The Constitution and laws made by Parliament lay down rules regarding:

- Qualifications
- Disqualifications
- Vacating of seats
- Oath
- Salaries and allowances

---


# 1. Qualifications for Membership of Parliament


The Constitution prescribes the following qualifications for a person to become a **Member of Parliament (MP)**.


### 1. Citizenship


The person must be a **citizen of India**.


---


### 2. Oath or Affirmation


The person must:

- Make and subscribe an **oath or affirmation**
- Before a person authorized by the **Election Commission**

In the oath, the person swears:

1. To bear **true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India**
2. To **uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India**

---


### 3. Minimum Age


| House       | Minimum Age |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Rajya Sabha | 30 years    |
| Lok Sabha   | 25 years    |


---


### 4. Other Qualifications


The candidate must possess **other qualifications prescribed by Parliament.**


---


# Additional Qualifications under Representation of the People Act, 1951


Parliament added extra qualifications through this Act.


### 1. Must be an Elector


The person must be **registered as a voter in a parliamentary constituency**.


Earlier rule:


Candidate contesting Rajya Sabha election had to be **an elector of that particular state**.


This rule was removed in **2003**.


In **2006**, the **Supreme Court upheld this change.**


---


### 2. Qualification for Reserved Seats


If a person wants to contest from:

- **SC reserved seat → must belong to Scheduled Caste**
- **ST reserved seat → must belong to Scheduled Tribe**

However:


SC/ST candidates **can also contest general seats.**


---


# 2. Disqualifications for Membership


The Constitution lists conditions under which a person **cannot become a Member of Parliament**.


---


## Constitutional Disqualifications


A person shall be disqualified if:


### 1. Office of Profit


If he holds **any office of profit under Union or State Government**.


Exception:

- Ministers
- Offices exempted by Parliament

---


### 2. Unsound Mind


If declared **mentally unsound by a court**.


---


### 3. Insolvency


If he is an **undischarged insolvent (दिवालिया)** .


---


### 4. Citizenship Issues


If he:

- is not a citizen of India
- has voluntarily acquired citizenship of a foreign country
- acknowledges allegiance to a foreign state

---


### 5. Disqualification under Parliamentary Law


If disqualified under any law made by Parliament.


---


# Additional Disqualifications under Representation of People Act, 1951


Parliament added more disqualifications.


A person is disqualified if he:

1. Is guilty of **election offences or corrupt practices**
2. Has been **convicted and sentenced to imprisonment for 2 years or more**

Exception:


Preventive detention **is not a disqualification**.

1. Fails to **submit election expense account within time**
2. Has interest in **government contracts**
3. Is **director or managing agent** of a government corporation with **25% government share**
4. Has been **dismissed from government service for corruption or disloyalty**
5. Has been convicted for **promoting enmity between groups**
6. Has been punished for social crimes like:
    - Untouchability
    - Dowry
    - Sati

---


## Authority Deciding Disqualification


If question arises whether an MP is disqualified:


Decision is taken by:


**President of India**


But:


President must **seek opinion of Election Commission**.


The President is **bound by this advice**.


---


# 3. Disqualification on Ground of Defection


The Constitution also provides disqualification under:


**Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law).**


---


## When does Defection occur?


A member becomes disqualified if:


### 1. Voluntarily gives up party membership


If he leaves the political party on whose ticket he was elected.


---


### 2. Violates Party Whip


If he:

- votes against party direction
- abstains from voting contrary to party instructions

---


### 3. Independent Member Joins Party


If an **independent member joins any political party** after election.


---


### 4. Nominated Member Joins Party


If a nominated member joins a political party **after six months of nomination.**


---


## Authority to Decide Defection


The decision is taken by:


| House       | Authority |
| ----------- | --------- |
| Lok Sabha   | Speaker   |
| Rajya Sabha | Chairman  |


Not the President.


---


### Supreme Court Judgment (1992)


The Supreme Court ruled that:


Speaker/Chairman's decision is **subject to judicial review**.


---


# 4. Vacating of Seats


An MP's seat becomes vacant in several situations.


---


# 1. Double Membership


A person cannot be:

- Member of **both Houses of Parliament simultaneously**

---


## If elected to both Houses


The member must choose **within 10 days**.


If he fails:


Seat in **Rajya Sabha becomes vacant**.


---


## If member of one House elected to another


His seat in **first House becomes vacant**.


---


## If elected to two seats in same House


He must choose **one seat**.


Otherwise:


**Both seats become vacant.**


---


## Parliament and State Legislature


A person cannot be member of:

- Parliament
- State Legislature

At the same time.


If elected to both:


He must resign from **state legislature within 14 days**.


Otherwise:


Seat in **Parliament becomes vacant**.


---


# 2. Disqualification


If a member becomes subject to any **constitutional disqualification**, his seat becomes vacant.


This includes **defection under Tenth Schedule**.


---


# 3. Resignation


A member may resign:


| House       | Resignation Given To |
| ----------- | -------------------- |
| Lok Sabha   | Speaker              |
| Rajya Sabha | Chairman             |


Seat becomes vacant **after resignation is accepted**.


However:


Speaker/Chairman may reject resignation if it is **not voluntary or genuine**.


---


# 4. Absence from House


If a member is absent for **60 days continuously** without permission:


House may declare the seat **vacant**.


---


## Exceptions


Days are not counted if House is:

- prorogued
- adjourned for more than **4 days**

---


# 5. Other Situations


Seat becomes vacant if:

1. Election is **declared void by court**
2. Member is **expelled by the House**
3. Member becomes **President or Vice-President**
4. Member becomes **Governor of a State**

---


## Election of Disqualified Person


If a disqualified person is elected:


The **High Court can declare election void**.


Appeal can be made to the **Supreme Court**.


---


# 5. Oath or Affirmation


Before taking seat in Parliament, every MP must take oath before:


**President or person appointed by him.**


---


## Oath Includes


The MP promises:

1. True faith and allegiance to Constitution
2. Uphold sovereignty and integrity of India
3. Faithfully discharge duties

---


## If Oath Not Taken


Member cannot:

- vote
- participate in proceedings
- claim parliamentary privileges

---


## Penalty


If a person sits or votes without qualification:


Fine:


**₹500 per day**


---


# 6. Salaries and Allowances of MPs


Members receive salaries and allowances decided by:


**Parliament**


---


## Law


Parliament passed:


**Salaries, Allowances and Pension of Members of Parliament Act, 1954**


---


## 2010 Salary Revision


| Allowance                | Amount        |
| ------------------------ | ------------- |

| Constituency allowance   | ₹45,000/month |
| Daily allowance          | ₹2,000/day    |
| Office expense allowance | ₹45,000/month |


---


## Pension


Since **1976**, MPs receive **pension based on years of service**.


---


## Additional Facilities


Members also receive:

- Free accommodation
- Travelling facilities
- Telephone
- Medical facilities
- Vehicle advance

---


# Salaries of Presiding Officers


The salaries of:

- Speaker of Lok Sabha
- Chairman of Rajya Sabha

are:


**Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India**


Therefore:


**Not subject to annual vote of Parliament.**


---


## Salaries and Allowances of Officers of Parliament Act, 1953


Under this Act:


Chairman of Rajya Sabha salary:


**₹1.25 lakh per month**


Other officers:

- Speaker
- Deputy Speaker
- Deputy Chairman

Receive salaries equivalent to MPs.

# PRESIDING OFFICERS OF PARLIAMENT


Each House of Parliament has its **own presiding officers**.


| House       | Presiding Officer | Deputy          |
| ----------- | ----------------- | --------------- |
| Lok Sabha   | Speaker           | Deputy Speaker  |
| Rajya Sabha | Chairman          | Deputy Chairman |


Additionally both Houses have:

- Panel of chairpersons/vice-chairpersons.

---


# SPEAKER OF LOK SABHA


The **Speaker** is the **presiding officer of Lok Sabha**.


He is considered:

- Head of the Lok Sabha
- Guardian of powers and privileges of the House

---


# Election of Speaker


The Speaker is:


**Elected by the Lok Sabha from among its members.**


Election happens:


**Soon after the first sitting of a newly elected Lok Sabha.**


The **President fixes the date of election**.


---


# Vacancy of Speaker’s Office


If the office becomes vacant:


Lok Sabha elects **another member** as Speaker.


---


# Tenure of Speaker


Normally the Speaker remains in office:


**During the life of Lok Sabha**


However he may vacate earlier if:

1. He **ceases to be member of Lok Sabha**
2. He **resigns to Deputy Speaker**
3. He is **removed by resolution of Lok Sabha**

---


# Removal of Speaker


Speaker can be removed by:


**Resolution passed by majority of all members of Lok Sabha**


(Absolute Majority)


Conditions:

- **14 days notice required**

---


## When Removal Motion is Under Consideration


Speaker:

- Cannot preside over the House
- Can participate in debate
- Can vote in the **first instance**

But cannot use **casting vote**.


---


# Speaker After Dissolution


Important exam point:


When Lok Sabha is dissolved:


**Speaker does NOT vacate office.**


He continues until:


**New Lok Sabha meets.**


---


# Role, Powers and Functions of Speaker


Speaker is:

- Head of Lok Sabha
- Representative of the House
- Guardian of privileges

His decisions on parliamentary matters are **final**.


---


## Sources of Speaker’s Powers


Speaker derives powers from:

1. Constitution of India
2. Rules of Procedure of Lok Sabha
3. Parliamentary conventions

---


# Major Powers of Speaker


### 1. Maintain Order


Speaker maintains:

- order
- decorum
- discipline

in the House.


---


### 2. Final Interpreter


Speaker interprets:

- Constitution
- Rules of Procedure
- Parliamentary precedents

within the House.


---


### 3. Quorum


If quorum not present:


Speaker may:

- adjourn House
- suspend meeting

Quorum = **1/10 of total strength of House**


---


### 4. Casting Vote


Speaker:

- Does not vote initially
- Votes only in case of tie

This is called:


**Casting Vote**


Purpose:


Resolve deadlock.


---


### 5. Joint Sitting


Speaker presides over:


**Joint sitting of both Houses**


Joint sitting is called by:


**President**


Purpose:


Resolve deadlock on bills.


---


### 6. Secret Sitting


Speaker may allow **secret sitting** on request of Leader of House.


In secret sitting:


No outsiders allowed.


---


### 7. Money Bill Certification


Speaker decides whether a bill is:


**Money Bill or not**


His decision is **final**.


He certifies the bill before sending to Rajya Sabha and President.


---


### 8. Anti-Defection Decision


Speaker decides disqualification under:


**Tenth Schedule (Defection Law)**


However:


Decision subject to **judicial review (1992 Supreme Court ruling)**.


---


### 9. Chairman of Parliamentary Bodies


Speaker acts as:

- Chairman of Indian Parliamentary Group
- Chairman of Conference of Presiding Officers

---


### 10. Parliamentary Committees


Speaker:

- appoints chairmen of Lok Sabha committees
- supervises committee functioning

Speaker himself heads:

1. Business Advisory Committee
2. Rules Committee
3. General Purpose Committee

---


# Independence of Speaker


To ensure neutrality several provisions exist.


---


## 1. Security of Tenure


Speaker can be removed only by:


**Absolute majority of Lok Sabha**


Not ordinary majority.


Removal motion requires support of **50 members**.


---


## 2. Salary Protection


Salary is:


**Charged on Consolidated Fund of India**


Therefore not voted annually.


---


## 3. Conduct Cannot Be Criticised


Speaker's conduct cannot be discussed except on:


**Substantive motion**


---


## 4. Court Cannot Interfere


Speaker’s decisions regarding **procedural matters** are **outside court jurisdiction**.


---


## 5. Voting Rule


Speaker votes **only in case of tie**, maintaining neutrality.


---


## 6. Rank in Order of Precedence


Speaker holds **7th rank**, same as **Chief Justice of India**.


Above all cabinet ministers except:

- Prime Minister
- Deputy Prime Minister

---


# Political Neutrality


In **Britain**:


Speaker resigns from political party.


In **India**:


Speaker usually **remains member of party**, though expected to be impartial.


---


# DEPUTY SPEAKER OF LOK SABHA


Deputy Speaker is also elected by:


**Lok Sabha from among its members.**


Election occurs **after Speaker election**.


Date fixed by:


**Speaker**


---


# Tenure of Deputy Speaker


Normally holds office **during life of Lok Sabha**.


Vacates office if:

1. Ceases to be member
2. Resigns to Speaker
3. Removed by resolution

Removal requires:

- Majority of all members
- **14 days notice**

---


# Functions of Deputy Speaker


Deputy Speaker:

1. Performs duties of Speaker when office vacant
2. Presides when Speaker absent
3. Presides over joint sitting if Speaker absent

In these situations he has **same powers as Speaker**.


---


## Important Point


Deputy Speaker **is not subordinate to Speaker**.


He is **directly responsible to Lok Sabha**.


---


## Special Privilege


If Deputy Speaker becomes member of a committee:


He automatically becomes **chairman of that committee**.


---


## Voting Rule


When presiding:

- Cannot vote initially
- Can use **casting vote in tie**

---


## Political Convention


Earlier:


Speaker and Deputy Speaker both from ruling party.


Since **11th Lok Sabha**:


Convention developed:

- Speaker → ruling party
- Deputy Speaker → opposition party

---


# PANEL OF CHAIRPERSONS (LOK SABHA)


Under Lok Sabha rules:


Speaker nominates **panel of up to 10 chairpersons**.


---


## Role


They preside over House when:

- Speaker absent
- Deputy Speaker absent

They enjoy **same powers as Speaker while presiding**.


---


## Limitation


They cannot preside when:


Office of **Speaker or Deputy Speaker is vacant**.


In that case:


President appoints member to perform duties.


---


# SPEAKER PRO TEM


When new Lok Sabha is formed:


Old Speaker vacates office **before first meeting**.


So President appoints:


**Speaker Pro Tem**


Usually:


**Senior-most member of Lok Sabha**


---


## Functions


Speaker Pro Tem:

1. Administers oath to new members
2. Presides over first sitting
3. Conducts election of Speaker

---


## Duration


Temporary office:


Exists **only for few days**.


Ends when **new Speaker elected**.


---


# CHAIRMAN OF RAJYA SABHA


The **Chairman of Rajya Sabha** is:


**Vice-President of India**


He is **ex-officio Chairman**.


---


## Special Situation


If Vice-President acts as **President**, he does not perform Chairman duties.


---


# Removal


Chairman can be removed only if:


**Vice-President removed from office**.


---


# Powers


Chairman’s powers similar to **Speaker of Lok Sabha**.


However Speaker has two extra powers:

1. Deciding **Money Bill**
2. Presiding over **Joint Sitting**

---


# Voting Rule


Chairman:

- Not a member of Rajya Sabha
- Does not vote initially
- Can use **casting vote in tie**

---


## Removal Motion Situation


If resolution to remove Vice-President is under consideration:


Chairman cannot preside.


But he can:

- participate
- speak

However he **cannot vote**.


---


# DEPUTY CHAIRMAN OF RAJYA SABHA


Deputy Chairman is:


**Elected by Rajya Sabha from among its members**.


---


## Vacancy


When office vacant:


Rajya Sabha elects new Deputy Chairman.


---


## Removal


Deputy Chairman vacates office if:

1. Ceases to be member
2. Resigns to Chairman
3. Removed by resolution

Resolution requires:

- majority of all members
- **14 days notice**

---


## Functions


Deputy Chairman performs duties when:

1. Chairman office vacant
2. Vice-President acting as President
3. Chairman absent

In such cases he exercises **all powers of Chairman**.


---


## Important Point


Deputy Chairman **is not subordinate to Chairman**.


He is **directly responsible to Rajya Sabha**.


---


# PANEL OF VICE-CHAIRPERSONS (RAJYA SABHA)


Chairman nominates **panel of vice-chairpersons**.


---


## Role


They preside over House when:

- Chairman absent
- Deputy Chairman absent

---


## Limitation


They cannot preside when:


Office of **Chairman or Deputy Chairman vacant**.


Then:


President appoints a member to perform duties.


---


# SECRETARIAT OF PARLIAMENT

---


## Recruitment and Service Conditions


Determined by:


**Parliament**


---


## Head of Secretariat

**Secretary-General**

# LEADERS IN PARLIAMENT


Certain leaders play important roles in conducting parliamentary business.


Major parliamentary leaders include:

1. Leader of the House
2. Leader of the Opposition
3. Whip

---


# 1. Leader of the House


According to the **Rules of Lok Sabha**, the **Leader of the House** is:

- **Prime Minister**, if he is a member of Lok Sabha.

If Prime Minister is **not a member of Lok Sabha**, then:


A **minister who is a Lok Sabha member** is nominated by the Prime Minister to act as **Leader of the House**.


---


## Leader of the House in Rajya Sabha


In Rajya Sabha:


Leader of the House is:

- a **minister**
- who is a **member of Rajya Sabha**
- nominated by the **Prime Minister**

---


## Role


Leader of the House:

- influences conduct of business in the House
- coordinates legislative agenda
- manages parliamentary schedule

He may also **nominate a Deputy Leader of the House**.


---


## US Equivalent


In USA this position is similar to:


**Majority Leader**


---


# 2. Leader of the Opposition


Every House of Parliament has:


**Leader of the Opposition**


---


## Recognition


Leader of the Opposition is:


Leader of the **largest opposition party** with:


**at least one-tenth of total strength of House**


---


## Role


Leader of Opposition:

1. Provides **constructive criticism of government**
2. Presents **alternative policies**
3. Represents opposition viewpoint

---


## Legal Recognition


Leader of Opposition received **statutory recognition in 1977**.


They receive:

- salary
- allowances
- facilities

equal to a **Cabinet Minister**.


---


## Historical Note


First official Leader of Opposition recognized in:


**1969**


---


## US Equivalent


In USA this role is similar to:


**Minority Leader**


---


# Shadow Cabinet (British System)


In Britain there exists:


**Shadow Cabinet**


It is formed by the **Opposition Party**.


Purpose:

- Prepare alternative government
- Shadow each minister of ruling cabinet

Thus the **Leader of Opposition** is often described as:


**“Alternative Prime Minister”**


This phrase was used by **Ivor Jennings**.


---


# 3. Whip


The office of **Whip** is based on:


**Parliamentary conventions**


It is **not mentioned in:**

- Constitution
- Rules of House
- Parliamentary statutes

---


## Role of Whip


Each political party appoints its own whip.


The whip acts as:


**Assistant floor leader**


---


## Functions


Whip ensures:

1. Attendance of party members
2. Party discipline
3. Support for party decisions
4. Proper voting behaviour

Members are expected to **follow whip directions**.


Failure may lead to **disciplinary action**.


---


# SESSIONS OF PARLIAMENT


Parliament meets periodically in **sessions**.


---


# 1. Summoning


The **President summons each House** from time to time.


---


## Maximum Gap


The gap between two sessions cannot exceed:


**Six months**


Thus Parliament must meet **at least twice a year**.


---


## Typical Sessions of Parliament


Usually three sessions are held each year.


| Session         | Period              |
| --------------- | ------------------- |
| Budget Session  | February – May      |
| Monsoon Session | July – September    |
| Winter Session  | November – December |


---


# Meaning of Session


A **session** is the period between:


**First sitting of a House → Prorogation or Dissolution**


---


## Daily Sitting


During session the House meets **daily to conduct business**.


---


# Recess


The period between:


**Prorogation of a House and its next session**


is called:


**Recess**


---


# ADJOURNMENT


A session consists of **many meetings**.


Each day normally has:


| Sitting           | Time         |
| ----------------- | ------------ |
| Morning sitting   | 11 AM – 1 PM |
| Afternoon sitting | 2 PM – 6 PM  |


---


## Meaning of Adjournment


Adjournment means:


**Suspension of work of a sitting for a specified time**


This may be:

- hours
- days
- weeks

---


# ADJOURNMENT SINE DIE


Adjournment sine die means:


**Termination of sitting without fixing next meeting date**


---


## Authority


Both adjournment and adjournment sine die are done by:


**Presiding Officer**


(Speaker or Chairman)


---


# PROROGATION


After House is adjourned sine die:


The **President issues notification of prorogation**.


---


## Meaning


Prorogation means:


**Termination of a session of Parliament**


---


## Authority


Prorogation is done by:


**President of India**


Even while House is still in session.


---


# Adjournment vs Prorogation


| Adjournment               | Prorogation          |
| ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Ends sitting only         | Ends session         |
| Done by presiding officer | Done by President    |
| Bills remain pending      | Bills remain pending |
| Notices remain valid      | Most notices lapse   |


---


# DISSOLUTION


Rajya Sabha is **permanent**.


So **only Lok Sabha can be dissolved**.


---


## Meaning


Dissolution means:


**End of life of Lok Sabha**


New Lok Sabha formed after **general elections**.


---


# Types of Dissolution


### 1. Automatic Dissolution


Occurs when:


**5-year term expires**


(or extended term during emergency ends)


---


### 2. Early Dissolution


President may dissolve Lok Sabha **before completion of term**.


Once dissolved:


**It cannot be revoked.**


---


# Effect of Dissolution on Bills


When Lok Sabha dissolves:


Many pending matters **lapse**.


---


## Bills That Lapse

1. Bill pending in Lok Sabha
2. Bill passed by Lok Sabha but pending in Rajya Sabha

---


## Bills That Do NOT Lapse

1. Bill pending in Rajya Sabha but not passed by Lok Sabha
2. Bill passed by both Houses but pending President assent
3. Bill returned by President for reconsideration
4. Bill pending where **joint sitting already notified**

---


# QUORUM


Quorum is:


**Minimum number of members required to conduct business**


---


## Constitutional Rule


Quorum = **1/10 of total membership**


---


## Numbers


| House       | Minimum Members |
| ----------- | --------------- |
| Lok Sabha   | 55              |
| Rajya Sabha | 25              |


---


## If Quorum Not Present


Presiding officer may:

- adjourn House
- suspend meeting

---


# VOTING IN PARLIAMENT


All matters are decided by:


**Majority of members present and voting**


Presiding officer **does not vote initially**.


He votes only in case of tie (**casting vote**).


---


## Matters Requiring Special Majority


Certain matters require **special majority**, such as:

- Constitutional amendment
- Impeachment of President
- Removal of presiding officers

---


# Voting Procedure in Lok Sabha


### Step 1 – Voice Vote


Speaker asks members:


Those in favour → say **Aye**


Those against → say **No**


---


### Step 2 – Speaker's Opinion


Speaker announces which side has majority.


---


### Step 3 – Challenge


If decision challenged:


Lobby cleared.


After **3 minutes 30 seconds**, question asked again.


---


### Step 4 – Division


If still challenged, voting occurs by:

1. Automatic vote recorder
2. Aye/No slips
3. Members entering lobbies

---


### Step 5 – Rising Vote


Speaker may ask members to **stand in place** to count votes.


In this case **names are not recorded**.


---


# LANGUAGE IN PARLIAMENT


Languages allowed:

1. **Hindi**
2. **English**

However presiding officer may allow a member to speak in **mother tongue**.


Simultaneous translation arrangements exist.


---


## English Continuation


English was supposed to stop in **1965**.


But:


**Official Languages Act 1963**


allowed English to continue along with Hindi.


---


# RIGHTS OF MINISTERS AND ATTORNEY GENERAL


Apart from MPs, the following may participate in Parliament:

1. **Ministers**
2. **Attorney General of India**

They can:

- speak
- participate

But **cannot vote**.


---


## Special Situations


### 1. Minister from Other House


Minister from Lok Sabha may participate in **Rajya Sabha** proceedings and vice versa.


---


### 2. Minister Without Membership


A person can remain minister **for 6 months without being MP**.


During this time he may participate in proceedings.


---


# LAME DUCK SESSION


Lame Duck Session refers to:


**Last session of existing Lok Sabha after new Lok Sabha has been elected.**


Members who **fail to get re-elected** are called:


**Lame Ducks**


---


# DEVICES OF PARLIAMENTARY PROCEEDINGS


To ensure accountability of the government and proper functioning of the legislature, Parliament uses several **procedural devices**.


Important devices include:

1. Question Hour
2. Zero Hour
3. Motions
4. Closure Motion
5. Privilege Motion
6. Calling Attention Motion
7. Adjournment Motion
8. No Confidence Motion
9. Censure Motion
10. Motion of Thanks
11. No-Day-Yet-Named Motion
12. Point of Order
13. Half-an-Hour Discussion
14. Short Duration Discussion
15. Special Mention
16. Resolutions

---


# QUESTION HOUR


The **first hour of every parliamentary sitting** is called **Question Hour**.


During this time:

- Members ask questions
- Ministers give answers

Purpose:


Ensure **government accountability**.


---


# Types of Questions


### 1. Starred Question


Marked by:


**Asterisk (*)**


Answer:


**Oral answer**


Members can ask **supplementary questions**.


---


### 2. Unstarred Question


Answer:


**Written answer**


Supplementary questions **not allowed**.


---


### 3. Short Notice Question


Asked with notice of **less than 10 days**.


Answer:


**Oral answer**


---


# Questions to Private Members


Questions can also be asked to **private members**.


Conditions:


When subject relates to:

- bill
- resolution
- parliamentary business handled by that member.

---


# Colour Coding of Questions


| Question Type           | Colour     |
| ----------------------- | ---------- |
| Starred                 | Green      |
| Unstarred               | White      |
| Short notice            | Light pink |
| Private member question | Yellow     |


---


# ZERO HOUR


Zero Hour is **not mentioned in parliamentary rules**.


It is an **informal device**.


---


## Timing


Starts immediately after **Question Hour**.


Ends when **regular agenda begins**.


---


## Purpose


Allows members to raise **urgent public matters without prior notice**.


---


## Origin


Zero Hour began in:


**1962**


It is considered an **Indian innovation in parliamentary procedure**.


---


# MOTIONS


A **motion** is a proposal made by a member for discussion in the House.


No discussion can occur without **motion approval by presiding officer**.


The House expresses decisions by **accepting or rejecting motions**.


---


# Types of Motions


Three main types:

1. Substantive Motion
2. Substitute Motion
3. Subsidiary Motion

---


# 1. Substantive Motion


Independent proposal dealing with **very important matters**.


Examples:

- impeachment of President
- removal of Chief Election Commissioner

---


# 2. Substitute Motion


Moved **instead of original motion**.


If adopted:


It **replaces the original motion**.


---


# 3. Subsidiary Motion


Has **no independent meaning**.


Depends on original motion.


Types:


### (a) Ancillary Motion


Regular procedural motion used for conducting business.


---


### (b) Superseding Motion


Moved during debate to **replace the discussion**.


---


### (c) Amendment


Proposes change to **part of original motion**.


---


# CLOSURE MOTION


Purpose:


To **end debate quickly**.


If House approves:


Debate stops and motion is **put to vote immediately**.


---


# Types of Closure Motions


### 1. Simple Closure


Member moves that **matter be put to vote**.


---


### 2. Closure by Compartments


Bill divided into sections.


Each section debated and voted separately.


---


### 3. Kangaroo Closure


Only **important clauses discussed**.


Other clauses passed without debate.


---


### 4. Guillotine Closure


Undiscussed clauses **put to vote automatically due to lack of time**.


---


# PRIVILEGE MOTION


Moved when a member believes that a **minister violated parliamentary privileges**.


Example:

- withholding facts
- giving incorrect information

Purpose:


**Censure the minister**.


---


# CALLING ATTENTION MOTION


Introduced to call attention of minister to **urgent public matter**.


Minister must give **authoritative statement**.


---


## Special Points

- Introduced in **1954**
- **Indian innovation**

Unlike Zero Hour:


It **is mentioned in Rules of Procedure**.


---


# ADJOURNMENT MOTION


Used to discuss a **definite matter of urgent public importance**.


---


## Requirements


Needs support of:


**50 members**


---


## Characteristics

- interrupts normal business
- implies **censure of government**

Therefore:


**Rajya Sabha cannot use adjournment motion.**


---


## Duration of Discussion


Must last **at least 2 hours 30 minutes**.


---


# Restrictions on Adjournment Motion


It must:

1. Be definite and factual
2. Raise only one issue
3. Relate to recent occurrence
4. Not raise privilege question
5. Not repeat discussion already held
6. Not involve matter under court
7. Not raise issue possible through another motion

---


# NO CONFIDENCE MOTION


Based on **Article 75**


Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to Lok Sabha**.


---


## Meaning


Government remains in office **only if it has majority support in Lok Sabha**.


---


## Procedure


Lok Sabha can remove government by **passing No-Confidence Motion**.


Requires support of **50 members**.


---


# CENSURE MOTION


Moved to **criticize government policies or actions**.


---


# Difference: Censure vs No Confidence


| Censure Motion                                      | No Confidence Motion                     |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Reasons must be stated                              | Reasons not required                     |
| Can target individual minister or entire government | Only against entire Council of Ministers |
| Government need not resign if passed                | Government must resign if passed         |


---


# MOTION OF THANKS


At the beginning of:

1. First session after general election
2. First session of each year

The **President addresses Parliament**.


---


## Content


President outlines:

- government policies
- future programmes

---


## Parliamentary Procedure


After the address:


Both Houses discuss it through:


**Motion of Thanks**


---


## Importance


If Motion of Thanks **fails in Lok Sabha**:


It implies **defeat of government**.


---


# NO-DAY-YET-NAMED MOTION


A motion:

- admitted by Speaker
- but **date not fixed for discussion**

Later:


Speaker assigns date after consultation with **Leader of House or Business Advisory Committee**.


---


# POINT OF ORDER


Raised when:


Proceedings **violate parliamentary rules**.


---


## Conditions


Must relate to:

- interpretation of rules
- enforcement of rules
- constitutional provisions governing House procedure

---


## Characteristics

- suspends House proceedings
- **no debate allowed**

Often used by **opposition members**.


---


# HALF-AN-HOUR DISCUSSION


Used to discuss matter of **public importance needing clarification**.


Speaker may allot:


**3 days per week**


No motion or voting occurs.


---


# SHORT DURATION DISCUSSION


Also called:


**Two-Hour Discussion**


Time limit:


**Maximum 2 hours**


---


## Features

- matter of urgent public importance
- no formal motion
- no voting

Introduced in:


**1953**


---


# SPECIAL MENTION


Used to raise matters **not covered under other procedures**.


---


## House-wise Terminology


| House       | Name                  |
| ----------- | --------------------- |
| Rajya Sabha | Special Mention       |
| Lok Sabha   | Notice under Rule 377 |


---


# RESOLUTIONS


Members move resolutions to draw attention of House or Government to **public issues**.


---


# Types of Resolutions


### 1. Private Member Resolution


Moved by **private member (non-minister)**.


Discussed on **alternate Fridays afternoon**.


---


### 2. Government Resolution


Moved by **minister**.


Can be taken up **Monday to Thursday**.


---


### 3. Statutory Resolution


Moved under **constitutional or statutory provisions**.


May be moved by **minister or private member**.


---


# Motions vs Resolutions


Important difference:

- **All resolutions are motions**
- But **all motions are not resolutions**

Also:


Resolutions **must be voted upon**.

# LEGISLATIVE PROCEDURE IN PARLIAMENT


A **bill is a proposal for legislation**.


When passed and enacted:


It becomes an **Act or Law**.


---


# Types of Bills


Bills are classified into:

1. Ordinary Bills
2. Money Bills
3. Financial Bills
4. Constitutional Amendment Bills

---


# Public Bill vs Private Bill


| Public Bill                            | Private Bill         |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Introduced by minister                 | Introduced by MP     |
| Reflects government policy             | Reflects member view |
| Higher chance of passing               | Low chance           |
| Rejection may lead to govt resignation | No effect on govt    |
| 7 days notice required                 | 1 month notice       |
| Drafted by government department       | Drafted by member    |


---


# STAGES OF ORDINARY BILL


Ordinary bill passes through **five stages**.


---


# 1. First Reading


Bill introduced in either House.


Procedure:

- Member asks permission
- Title and objectives read

No discussion occurs.


Later bill is **published in Gazette of India**.


---


# 2. Second Reading


Most important stage.


Includes three sub-stages.


---


## (a) General Discussion


Members discuss **principles of bill**.


Details not discussed.


House may:

1. take bill into consideration
2. refer to select committee
3. refer to joint committee
4. circulate for public opinion

---


## (b) Committee Stage


Select committee examines bill **clause by clause**.


Can propose amendments.


Then submits report.


---


## (c) Consideration Stage


House debates **each clause separately**.


Members can move amendments.


Amendments approved become part of bill.


---


# 3. Third Reading


Final stage in House.


Debate limited to:


**acceptance or rejection of bill**


No amendments allowed.


If majority supports:


Bill is **passed by House**.


Then sent to **second House**.


---


# BILL IN THE SECOND HOUSE


After an ordinary bill is **passed in the first House**, it is sent to the **second House**.


In the second House, the bill again goes through **three readings**.


The second House has **four options**:


### 1. Pass the Bill Without Amendments


If the second House passes the bill **without amendments**, it is sent to the **President for assent**.


The bill becomes **law after presidential assent**.


---


### 2. Pass the Bill With Amendments


If the second House passes the bill **with amendments**, the bill returns to the **first House**.


Now two situations arise:

- If the first House **accepts the amendments → bill passed**
- If the first House **rejects amendments → deadlock occurs**

---


### 3. Reject the Bill


If the second House **rejects the bill**, a **deadlock arises**.


---


### 4. Take No Action


If the second House **does not take action for six months**, it also results in a **deadlock**.


---


# DEADLOCK BETWEEN HOUSES


Deadlock occurs when disagreement arises between Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha regarding a bill.


Deadlock situations include:

1. Second House **rejects the bill**
2. Houses **disagree on amendments**
3. Second House **does not act for six months**

---


# JOINT SITTING OF PARLIAMENT


To resolve deadlock, the Constitution provides for **Joint Sitting of both Houses**.


---


## Constitutional Provision


Joint sitting is provided under:


**Article 108**


---


## Who Summons Joint Sitting?


**President of India**


---


## Who Presides?


Presiding order:

1. **Speaker of Lok Sabha**
2. **Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha**
3. **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha**

---


## Decision in Joint Sitting


Bill is passed if **majority of members present and voting support it**.


Since Lok Sabha has **more members**, it usually dominates joint sitting.


---


## Situations Where Joint Sitting is NOT Allowed


Joint sitting is **not permitted for**:

1. **Money Bills**
2. **Constitution Amendment Bills**

---


## Historical Examples of Joint Sitting


Joint sittings have been held very rarely.


Examples include:

1. **Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961)**
2. **Banking Service Commission Bill (1978)**
3. **Prevention of Terrorism Bill (2002)**

---


# MONEY BILLS


Money Bills deal **exclusively with financial matters**.


---


## Constitutional Provision


Defined under:


**Article 110**


---


## Matters Included in Money Bill


A bill is a Money Bill if it deals with:

1. Imposition, abolition or regulation of taxes
2. Borrowing of money by the government
3. Custody of Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund
4. Appropriation of money from Consolidated Fund
5. Declaration of expenditure as charged expenditure
6. Receipt or custody of public money

---


## Certification of Money Bill


The **Speaker of Lok Sabha decides whether a bill is a Money Bill**.


Speaker's decision is **final**.


---


# Procedure of Money Bill


Money Bills can be introduced **only in Lok Sabha**.


They **cannot be introduced in Rajya Sabha**.


---


## President’s Recommendation


Money Bill can be introduced only with **prior recommendation of President**.


---


## Role of Rajya Sabha


After Lok Sabha passes the Money Bill, it is sent to Rajya Sabha.


Rajya Sabha has **very limited powers**.


It can:

- discuss the bill
- recommend amendments

But it **cannot amend or reject the bill**.


---


## Time Limit


Rajya Sabha must return the bill within:


**14 days**


---


### Two Possible Situations


**1. Rajya Sabha returns the bill with recommendations**


Lok Sabha may:

- accept recommendations
- reject recommendations

Decision of Lok Sabha is **final**.


---


**2. Rajya Sabha does not return bill within 14 days**


The bill is **considered passed automatically**.


---


## Final Step


After passage, the bill is sent to **President for assent**.


President **cannot return Money Bill for reconsideration**.


---


# FINANCIAL BILLS


Financial Bills also deal with **financial matters**, but they are **different from Money Bills**.


---


## Types of Financial Bills


Two types:

1. **Financial Bill (Category I)**
2. **Financial Bill (Category II)**

---


# Financial Bill (Category I)


This bill contains:

- matters of **Money Bill**
- plus **other matters**

---


## Features

- Can be introduced **only in Lok Sabha**
- Requires **President’s recommendation**

But unlike Money Bill:

- Rajya Sabha has **full powers**

Meaning it can:

- reject
- amend
- delay

---


# Financial Bill (Category II)


This bill:


Contains **financial provisions but not included in Article 110**.


---


## Features

- Can be introduced in **either House**
- Does **not require President recommendation before introduction**

But recommendation required **before passing**.


---


# ORDINARY BILL VS MONEY BILL


| Feature               | Ordinary Bill | Money Bill     |
| --------------------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| Introduction          | Either House  | Only Lok Sabha |
| Rajya Sabha powers    | Equal powers  | Limited powers |
| Joint sitting         | Allowed       | Not allowed    |
| Speaker certification | Not required  | Required       |


---


# POSITION OF RAJYA SABHA IN LEGISLATION


Rajya Sabha has **almost equal powers with Lok Sabha** regarding ordinary legislation.


However there are **important differences**.


---


# Areas Where Lok Sabha is Stronger


Lok Sabha has greater powers in:

1. **Money Bills**
2. **Financial control**
3. **Council of Ministers responsibility**

Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible only to Lok Sabha**.


---


# Areas Where Rajya Sabha is Stronger


Rajya Sabha has special powers under:


### Article 249


Rajya Sabha can authorize Parliament to legislate on **State List subjects**.


Requires:


**2/3 majority of members present and voting**.


---


### Article 312


Rajya Sabha can create **All India Services**.


Again requires **special majority**.


---
