
## FEDERAL SYSTEM –REVISION NOTES

# 1. Types of Government Structure


Political systems are classified based on the **relationship between national and regional governments**.


## 1. Unitary Government

- **All powers vested in the national government**
- Regional units derive authority from the centre
- Centre can **create or abolish regional governments**

### Examples

- Britain
- France
- Japan
- China
- Italy
- Norway
- Sweden
- Spain

---


## 2. Federal Government


A **federal system** divides powers between:

- **National Government**
- **Regional Governments**

Both derive authority **directly from the Constitution**.


### Levels of Government


| Level          | Name                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| National Level | Union / Federal / Central Government |
| Regional Level | State / Provincial Government        |


### Examples

- USA
- Switzerland
- Australia
- Canada
- Russia
- Brazil
- Argentina

---


# 2. Federal vs Unitary Government


| Federal Government                | Unitary Government               |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Dual Government (Centre + States) | Single Government                |
| Written Constitution              | Written or Unwritten             |
| Constitutional division of powers | No constitutional division       |
| Constitution is supreme           | Constitution may not be supreme  |
| Rigid Constitution                | Flexible or rigid                |
| Independent judiciary             | Judiciary may not be independent |
| Usually bicameral legislature     | Unicameral or bicameral          |


---


# 3. Meaning of Federation


The term **Federation** comes from the Latin word:


**Foedus = Treaty / Agreement**


Meaning:

> A political system formed through an agreement among states.

---


### Names of Federal Units


| Country     | Unit Name |
| ----------- | --------- |
| USA         | States    |
| Switzerland | Cantons   |
| Canada      | Provinces |
| Russia      | Republics |


---


# 4. Formation of Federation


Federations are formed in **two ways**.


---


## 1. Coming Together Federation (Integration)


Independent states unite to form a federation.


### Example


**USA**


Reasons:

- Military weakness
- Economic weakness

---


## 2. Holding Together Federation (Disintegration)


A **large unitary state converts into federation** by granting autonomy to provinces.


### Example


**Canada**


India follows this model.


---


# 5. Oldest Federation


## USA

- Constitution adopted: **1787**
- After **American Revolution (1775–1783)**
- Original states: **13**
- Present states: **50**

USA is considered the **model federation**.


---


## Canadian Federation

- Formed: **1867**
- Original provinces: **4**
- Present provinces: **10**

---


# 6. Federal System in India


The **Indian Constitution establishes a federal system**.


### Reasons for adopting federalism

1. Large geographical size
2. Socio-cultural diversity
3. Need for efficient administration
4. Need to maintain national unity

---


# 7. Why Constitution Uses “Union of States”


The word **federation is not used** in the Constitution.


### Article 1

> “India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.”

### Explanation – Dr. B. R. Ambedkar


The term **Union of States** was used because:

1. Indian federation **not formed by agreement of states**
2. **States have no right to secede**

Thus India is an:

> **Indestructible Union of destructible states**

---


# 8. Model of Indian Federalism


Indian federalism is based on the **Canadian model** rather than the American model.


---


## American Model

- Weak centre
- Strong states

---


## Canadian Model

- Strong centre
- Provinces comparatively weaker

India follows **Canadian-style federalism**.


---


# 9. Federal Features of Indian Constitution


---


# 1. Dual Polity


Two levels of government:

1. **Union Government**
2. **State Governments**

Both derive authority from the **Constitution**.


### Examples of Subjects


**Union List**

- Defence
- Foreign affairs
- Currency
- Communication

**State List**

- Public order
- Agriculture
- Health
- Local government

---


# 2. Written Constitution


Indian Constitution is:

- **Written**
- **Lengthiest Constitution in the world**

### Original Constitution (1950)

- **395 Articles**
- **22 Parts**
- **8 Schedules**

### Present Constitution

- **465 Articles**
- **25 Parts**
- **12 Schedules**

---


# 3. Division of Powers


Provided under **Seventh Schedule**.


### Three Lists


| List            | Subjects            |
| --------------- | ------------------- |
| Union List      | 100 (Originally 97) |
| State List      | 61 (Originally 66)  |
| Concurrent List | 52 (Originally 47)  |


---


### Concurrent List Rule


Both Centre and States can legislate.


If conflict arises:

> **Central law prevails**

---


### Residuary Powers


Subjects not listed anywhere.


In India → **Given to the Centre**


---


# 4. Supremacy of Constitution


The Constitution is the **supreme law of the land**.


All laws made by:

- Parliament
- State Legislatures

must conform to the Constitution.


Invalid laws can be struck down through **Judicial Review**.


---


# 5. Rigid Constitution


Certain amendments require:

1. **Special majority in Parliament**
2. **Ratification by half of the states**

### Special Majority

- Majority of total membership
- 2/3 of members present and voting

---


# 6. Independent Judiciary


Headed by the **Supreme Court of India**.


### Functions

- Protect supremacy of Constitution
- Resolve disputes:
    - Centre vs States
    - State vs State

### Safeguards

- Security of tenure
- Fixed service conditions
- Protection from executive interference

---


# 7. Bicameral Legislature


Indian Parliament has two houses.


| House       | Representation |
| ----------- | -------------- |
| Lok Sabha   | People         |
| Rajya Sabha | States         |


### Role of Rajya Sabha

- Represents states
- Protects state interests
- Maintains federal balance

---


# UNITARY FEATURES OF INDIAN CONSTITUTION


Despite federal features, the Constitution contains **strong unitary features** making the **Centre dominant**.


---


# 1. Strong Centre


Reasons:

1. **Union List larger than State List**
2. Important subjects under Union control
3. Centre overrides Concurrent List
4. Residuary powers with Centre

---


# 2. States Not Indestructible


Parliament can:

- Alter boundaries
- Change name
- Change area of states

Done through **simple majority**.


Thus India is:

> **Indestructible Union of destructible states**

---


# 3. Single Constitution


Most federations have **separate state constitutions**.


India has **one Constitution for both Centre and states**.


### Exception


Earlier **Jammu & Kashmir had its own Constitution** due to **Article 370**.


---


# 4. Flexible Constitution


Most amendments require:

- **Simple majority** or
- **Special majority**

States **cannot initiate constitutional amendments**.


Only **Parliament can initiate** them.


---


# 5. Unequal Representation of States


In **Rajya Sabha**, representation is based on **population**.


Thus membership varies from **1 to 31 per state**.


### USA


Each state gets **2 seats in Senate**.


---


# 6. Emergency Provisions


Three types:


| Emergency           | Article |
| ------------------- | ------- |
| National Emergency  | 352     |
| President’s Rule    | 356     |
| Financial Emergency | 360     |


During emergency:

> Federal system becomes **unitary**.

---


# 7. Single Citizenship


India has **only Indian citizenship**.


No separate state citizenship.


Countries like **USA, Switzerland, Australia** have **dual citizenship**.


---


# 8. Integrated Judiciary


Single judicial system:

- Supreme Court
- High Courts
- Subordinate courts

USA has **dual judiciary**.


---


# 9. All-India Services


Three types of services:

1. Central services
2. State services
3. **All-India services**

Examples:

- IAS
- IPS
- IFS

Recruitment and control largely by **Centre**.


---


# 10. Integrated Audit Machinery


The **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)** audits:

- Union accounts
- State accounts

Appointed by **President of India**.


States have **no role in appointment or removal**.


---


# 11. Parliament’s Authority Over State List


Parliament can legislate on **State List** if:

> Rajya Sabha passes a resolution in national interest.

---


# 12. Appointment of Governor


Governor:

- Appointed by **President**
- Holds office during **President’s pleasure**
- Acts as **agent of Centre**

---


# 13. Integrated Election Machinery


The **Election Commission of India** conducts elections for:

- Parliament
- State legislatures

Appointed by **President**.


---


# 14. Veto Over State Bills


Governor may reserve state bills for **President’s consideration**.


President may:

- Approve
- Withhold assent

President’s veto is **absolute**.

# 1. Concept of Federal System


Political scientists classify governments into two types based on the relationship between **national government and regional governments**:

1. **Unitary Government**
2. **Federal Government**

---


## 1.1 Unitary Government


In a **Unitary Government**, all powers are vested in the **national government**.


Regional governments (if they exist):

- derive authority from the national government
- can be created or abolished by the centre

### Examples

- Britain
- France
- Japan
- China
- Italy
- Belgium
- Norway
- Sweden
- Spain

---


## 1.2 Federal Government


In a **Federal Government**, powers are divided between:

- National Government
- Regional Governments

The **division of powers is done by the Constitution itself**, and both governments operate independently within their jurisdiction.


### Names of governments in a federation


| Level               | Name                                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| National Government | Federal / Central / Union Government |
| Regional Government | State / Provincial Government        |


### Examples

- USA
- Switzerland
- Australia
- Canada
- Russia
- Brazil
- Argentina

---


# 2. Comparison: Federal vs Unitary Government


| Federal Government                           | Unitary Government                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Dual Government (Central + State)            | Single Government                          |
| Written Constitution                         | Constitution may be written or unwritten   |
| Division of Powers between centre and states | No division of powers                      |
| Supremacy of Constitution                    | Constitution may or may not be supreme     |
| Rigid Constitution                           | May be rigid or flexible                   |
| Independent Judiciary                        | Judiciary may or may not be independent    |
| Bicameral Legislature                        | Legislature may be bicameral or unicameral |


---

# UNITARY FEATURES OF INDIAN CONSTITUTION


Although the Constitution has federal features, it also contains several **unitary (non-federal) features**.


These provisions make the **Centre stronger than the states**.


---


# 1. Strong Centre


The **division of powers favours the Centre**.


Reasons:


### 1️⃣ Union List is larger than State List

- Union List: **100 subjects**
- State List: **61 subjects**

---


### 2️⃣ Important subjects are in Union List


Examples:

- Defence
- Foreign Affairs
- Currency
- Railways
- Communication

---


### 3️⃣ Centre has overriding power over Concurrent List


Both Centre and States can legislate, but:

> **Central law prevails in case of conflict.**

---


### 4️⃣ Residuary powers belong to Centre


Subjects not mentioned in any list go to **Centre**.


⚠️ In the **USA**, residuary powers belong to **states**.


---


# 2. States Are Not Indestructible


In many federations, states have **territorial integrity**.


But in India:

> Parliament can change the **area, boundaries or name of any state**.

This can be done by:

- **Simple majority**
- **Unilateral action of Parliament**

Therefore Indian federation is described as:

> **“An indestructible Union of destructible states.”**

---


### Comparison with USA


USA is described as:

> **“An indestructible Union of indestructible states.”**

---


# 3. Single Constitution


In many federations:

- States have **separate constitutions**

Example:

- USA

---


In India:

- **States do not have separate constitutions**

The **Constitution of India governs both Centre and states**.


---


### Exception


Earlier **Jammu & Kashmir had its own constitution**.


(This was due to **Article 370**.)


---


# 4. Flexibility of Constitution


The Constitution of India is **less rigid than other federations**.


Most provisions can be amended by:


1️⃣ **Simple majority** OR


2️⃣ **Special majority**


Only some provisions require **state ratification**.


---


Another difference from USA:


In India:

> Only **Parliament can initiate constitutional amendment**.

States cannot propose amendments.


---


# 5. No Equality of State Representation


In the **Rajya Sabha**, representation of states is based on:

> **Population**

Therefore membership varies:


**1 to 31 members per state**


---


### Example


Large states → more seats


Small states → fewer seats


---


### Comparison with USA


In the US Senate:

- Each state gets **equal representation**
- **2 members per state**

This protects **smaller states**.


---


# 6. Emergency Provisions


The Constitution provides **three types of emergencies**:


1️⃣ **National Emergency (Article 352)**


2️⃣ **State Emergency / President’s Rule (Article 356)**


3️⃣ **Financial Emergency (Article 360)**


---


During emergency:

- **Centre becomes very powerful**
- States come under **central control**

This converts the **federal system into a unitary one**.


⚠️ Such transformation without amendment is **unique to India**.


---


# 7. Single Citizenship


India has **single citizenship**.


There is only:

> **Indian Citizenship**

There is **no separate state citizenship**.


---


### Other federations


Countries like:

- USA
- Switzerland
- Australia

have **dual citizenship**:

1. National citizenship
2. State citizenship

---


# 8. Integrated Judiciary


India has **one unified judicial system**.


Structure:

- Supreme Court
- High Courts
- Subordinate courts

---


This system enforces:

- **Central laws**
- **State laws**

---


### USA


USA has **dual judicial system**:

- Federal courts
- State courts

---


# 9. All-India Services


In the USA:

- Federal and State governments have **separate services**.

In India:


We have:


1️⃣ Central services


2️⃣ State services


3️⃣ **All-India services**


Examples:

- IAS
- IPS
- IFS

---


### Features

- Recruitment by **Centre**
- Training by **Centre**
- Control by **Centre**

These services serve **both Centre and states**.


---


# 10. Integrated Audit Machinery


The **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)** audits:

- Central government accounts
- State government accounts

---


### Appointment


Appointed by:

> **President of India**

States have **no role in appointment or removal**.


Thus financial autonomy of states is restricted.


---


### USA


The American Comptroller-General **does not audit state accounts**.


---


# 11. Parliament’s Authority Over State List


Even subjects in the **State List** are not completely exclusive.


Parliament can legislate on State List if:

> **Rajya Sabha passes a resolution in national interest.**

Thus Parliament’s power can be **extended without constitutional amendment**.


---


# 12. Appointment of Governor


The **Governor is appointed by the President**.


Features:

- Head of the state
- Holds office **during pleasure of President**
- Acts as **agent of Centre**

Through the governor, the Centre can **exercise influence over states**.


---


### USA


In the USA:


Governors are **directly elected by people**.


---


# 13. Integrated Election Machinery


The **Election Commission of India** conducts elections for:

- Parliament
- State legislatures

---


### Appointment


Members are appointed by:

> **President**

States have **no role in appointment or removal**.


---


### USA


USA has **separate election machinery** for federal and state elections.


---


# 14. Veto Over State Bills


The Governor may reserve certain state bills for the **President’s consideration**.


The President may:

- Give assent
- Withhold assent

---


Important point:

> The President has **absolute veto over such state bills**.

Even if the state legislature passes the bill again.


---


### USA & Australia


States are **more autonomous**.


There is **no such reservation for President**.

# CRITICAL EVALUATION OF INDIAN FEDERALISM


Indian Constitution contains both:

- **Federal features**
- **Unitary features**

But the **balance tilts towards the Centre**.


This led many scholars to question its federal nature.


---


# Views of Constitutional Experts


---


# 1. K.C. Wheare


Described Indian Constitution as:

> **Quasi-Federal**

Meaning:

> “A unitary state with subsidiary federal features.”

---


# 2. K. Santhanam


He argued that **two factors increased centralisation**:


1️⃣ Financial dominance of Centre


2️⃣ Planning Commission controlling development


He said:

> India has practically functioned as a **unitary state**.

---


# Scholars Supporting Federal Nature


---


# 3. Paul Appleby


Described Indian system as:

> **Extremely federal**

---


# 4. Morris Jones


Called Indian federalism:

> **Bargaining Federalism**

Meaning:


Centre and states negotiate power and resources.


---


# 5. Ivor Jennings


Described it as:

> **Federation with strong centralising tendency**

He believed the Constitution protects:

- national unity
- national growth

---


# 6. Alexandrowicz


Described India as:

> **Sui Generis**

Meaning:

> Unique in character.

---


# 7. Granville Austin


Described Indian federalism as:

> **Cooperative Federalism**

Meaning:


Centre and states **work together**.


---


# Ambedkar’s View on Federalism


Dr. B.R. Ambedkar said in the **Constituent Assembly**:


The Constitution is **federal because it establishes dual polity**.


Both:

- Centre
- States

# Supreme Court on Federalism


## S.R. Bommai Case (1994)


The Supreme Court held that:

> **Federalism is a basic feature of the Constitution.**

---


Important observations:

- States are **not agents of the Centre**
- States have **independent constitutional existence**
- Within their sphere, **states are supreme**

---


Even though Centre has more powers:


This does **not destroy federal character**.


---


# Nature of Indian Federalism


Indian federalism is a **compromise between two needs**:


### 1️⃣ State autonomy


States enjoy power in their own sphere.


---


### 2️⃣ National unity


Centre becomes powerful during emergencies.


---


# Federal Trends in Indian Political System


The functioning of Indian politics shows **federal spirit**.


Examples:


---


### 1️⃣ Territorial disputes between states


Example:

- Maharashtra vs Karnataka over **Belgaum**

---


### 2️⃣ River water disputes


Example:

- Karnataka vs Tamil Nadu over **Cauvery water**

---


### 3️⃣ Rise of regional parties


Examples:

- Andhra Pradesh
- Tamil Nadu

---


### 4️⃣ Creation of new states


Examples:

- Mizoram
- Jharkhand

---


### 5️⃣ Demand for financial grants from Centre


States often depend on **central assistance**.


---


### 6️⃣ Assertion of state autonomy


States resist excessive **central interference**.


---


### 7️⃣ Judicial control on misuse of Article 356


The Supreme Court imposed **procedural limitations** in **Bommai case**.
