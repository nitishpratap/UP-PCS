
## EMERGENCY PROVISIONS (Articles 352–360) - Revision

---


Emergency provisions are contained in **Part XVIII of the Constitution (Articles 352–360)**.


### Purpose


Enable the **Union Government to deal with abnormal situations** and protect:

- Sovereignty
- Unity & integrity of India
- Security of the nation
- Democratic political system
- Constitution

### Key Feature


India’s Constitution can function as:


| Situation        | Nature of System |
| ---------------- | ---------------- |
| Normal times     | Federal          |
| During emergency | Unitary          |


**Dr. B. R. Ambedkar:** Constitution can function as **both federal and unitary depending on circumstances**.


---


# TYPES OF EMERGENCY


| Type                | Article | Popular Name              |
| ------------------- | ------- | ------------------------- |
| National Emergency  | 352     | Proclamation of Emergency |
| State Emergency     | 356     | President’s Rule          |
| Financial Emergency | 360     | Financial Emergency       |


---


# NATIONAL EMERGENCY (Article 352)


## Grounds


President can declare emergency when **security of India or any part is threatened by**:

1. **War**
2. **External Aggression**
3. **Armed Rebellion**

Emergency may be declared **even before the occurrence if danger is imminent**.


---


## Types of National Emergency


| Ground                    | Type               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------ |
| War / External Aggression | External Emergency |
| Armed Rebellion           | Internal Emergency |


---


## Important Amendments


### 42nd Amendment Act 1976


Allowed **emergency for a specific part of India**.


### 44th Amendment Act 1978


Replaced **“Internal Disturbance” → “Armed Rebellion”**.


Reason: _Internal disturbance was vague and prone to misuse._


Example: **1975 Emergency declared on internal disturbance**.


---


# CABINET RECOMMENDATION RULE


President can declare emergency **only after written recommendation of the Cabinet**.


Introduced by **44th Amendment (1978)**.


Reason: During **1975 Emergency**, PM **Indira Gandhi advised the President without Cabinet approval**.


---


# JUDICIAL REVIEW


### 38th Amendment (1975)


Emergency proclamation **not subject to judicial review**.


### 44th Amendment (1978)


Removed this protection.


Now **Supreme Court can review emergency declaration**.


### Important Case


**Minerva Mills Case (1980)**


Court can invalidate emergency if:

- Mala fide
- Based on irrelevant facts
- Absurd or perverse

---


# PARLIAMENTARY APPROVAL


Emergency must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament within 1 month**.


Earlier: **2 months**


Changed by **44th Amendment Act**.


### Special Situation


If **Lok Sabha is dissolved**:


Emergency continues until **30 days after first sitting of new Lok Sabha**, provided **Rajya Sabha approves**.


---


# DURATION OF NATIONAL EMERGENCY


After approval:

- Valid for **6 months**
- Can be **extended indefinitely**
- Requires **parliamentary approval every 6 months**

---


# SPECIAL MAJORITY REQUIREMENT


Approval requires:

1. Majority of **total membership of the House**
2. **Two-thirds of members present and voting**

Earlier: **Simple majority**


Changed by **44th Amendment**.


---


# REVOCATION OF NATIONAL EMERGENCY


## 1. By President


President may **revoke emergency anytime** through proclamation.


No parliamentary approval required.


---


## 2. By Lok Sabha Resolution


If **Lok Sabha passes resolution disapproving emergency**, President **must revoke it**.


Introduced by **44th Amendment**.


### Special Safeguard


If **1/10 of Lok Sabha members give written notice**, a **special sitting must be held within 14 days**.


---


## Approval vs Disapproval


| Feature   | Approval         | Disapproval     |
| --------- | ---------------- | --------------- |
| Passed by | Both Houses      | Lok Sabha       |
| Majority  | Special majority | Simple majority |


---


# EFFECTS OF NATIONAL EMERGENCY


Three areas affected:

1. **Centre–State Relations**
2. **Lok Sabha & State Assemblies**
3. **Fundamental Rights**

---


# EFFECT ON CENTRE–STATE RELATIONS


Federal structure becomes **unitary**.


### Executive Power


Centre can **direct states on any matter**.


State governments **continue but under central control**.


---


### Legislative Power


Parliament can legislate on **State List subjects**.


State legislatures continue but become **subordinate to Parliament**.


Validity of such laws:


➡ Valid **6 months after emergency ends**.


---


### Ordinances


President may issue **ordinances on State List subjects**.


---


### Financial Powers


President may **modify distribution of revenues between Centre and states**.


Valid until **end of financial year in which emergency ends**.


---


# EFFECT ON LEGISLATURE


### Lok Sabha


Term may be extended:

- **1 year at a time**
- Not beyond **6 months after emergency ends**

Example:


**5th Lok Sabha (1971-1977)** extended during emergency.


---


### State Assemblies


Term may also be extended:

- **1 year at a time**
- Maximum **6 months after emergency ends**

---


# EFFECT ON FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS


Governed by:

- **Article 358**
- **Article 359**

---


# ARTICLE 358


Automatic suspension of **Article 19** during National Emergency.


After **44th Amendment**:

- Applies **only during external emergency**
- Protection only for **laws related to emergency**

After emergency ends:

- Article 19 revives
- But actions taken during emergency remain valid.

---


# ARTICLE 359


President may suspend **right to move courts for enforcement of FR**.


Important:

- Rights themselves **not suspended**
- Only **enforcement suspended**

President specifies:

- Which FR affected
- Duration
- Area

---


## Safeguard by 44th Amendment


Enforcement **cannot be suspended for**:

- **Article 20**
- **Article 21**

---


# ARTICLE 358 vs 359


| Feature        | Article 358      | Article 359        |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Applies to     | Article 19 only  | Any FR specified   |
| Nature         | Automatic        | Presidential order |
| Emergency type | External only    | Both types         |
| Area           | Entire country   | Whole/part         |
| Duration       | Entire emergency | As specified       |

# PRESIDENT’S RULE (Article 356)


Also called:

- **State Emergency**
- **Constitutional Emergency**

But Constitution **does not use the word emergency for Article 356**.


---


# CONSTITUTIONAL BASIS


### Article 355


Duty of Union:

- Protect states from external aggression
- Ensure governance according to Constitution

---


# GROUNDS FOR PRESIDENT’S RULE


### Article 356


When **constitutional machinery in state fails**.


President may act:

- On **Governor’s report**
- Or **otherwise**

---


### Article 365


If state **fails to comply with Union directions**.


---


# PARLIAMENTARY APPROVAL


Must be approved within **2 months** by both Houses.


If Lok Sabha dissolved:


Valid until **30 days after first sitting of new Lok Sabha**.


---


# DURATION


After approval:

- **6 months**

Maximum duration:


**3 years**


Extension every **6 months**.


---


# EXTENSION BEYOND 1 YEAR (44th Amendment)


Two conditions required:

1. National Emergency in operation
2. Election Commission certifies elections cannot be held

---


# MAJORITY REQUIRED


Approval or extension requires **simple majority**.


---


# CONSEQUENCES OF PRESIDENT’S RULE


President may:

1. Take over state government functions
2. Exercise powers of governor
3. Authorise Parliament to legislate for state

---


### Administrative Changes

- State **Council of Ministers dismissed**
- Governor administers state on behalf of President
- Assembly **suspended or dissolved**

---


### Legislative Changes


Parliament may:

- Make laws for state
- Delegate law-making to President
- Authorise expenditure from state consolidated fund

---


### Important Limitation


President **cannot assume powers of High Court**.


---


# USE OF ARTICLE 356


Used **100+ times since 1950**.


First imposed:


**Punjab (1951)**.


---


# IMPORTANT CASE


## S. R. Bommai vs Union of India (1994)


Most important judgment on **Article 356**.


### Key Principles

1. President’s Rule **subject to judicial review**
2. Must be based on **relevant material**
3. Court can **restore dismissed government**
4. **Floor test mandatory**
5. Assembly dissolution only **after parliamentary approval**
6. **Secularism is basic feature**
7. Article 356 only for **exceptional situations**

---


# PROPER USE OF ARTICLE 356


According to **Sarkaria Commission & Bommai case**


Examples:

- Hung assembly
- No party willing to form government
- Government resigns
- State ignores constitutional directions
- Internal subversion
- Administrative breakdown

---


# IMPROPER USE

- Governor dismisses government without floor test
- Loss in Lok Sabha election
- Political misuse
- Maladministration or corruption
- No prior warning to state government

---


# FINANCIAL EMERGENCY (Article 360)


Declared when **financial stability or credit of India is threatened**.


---


# PARLIAMENTARY APPROVAL


Must be approved within **2 months**.


---


# DURATION


Continues **indefinitely until revoked**.


---


# MAJORITY REQUIRED


**Simple majority**.


---


# EFFECTS


President may:

1. Direct states to observe financial discipline
2. Reduce salaries of government employees
3. Reserve money bills for President

---


### Salary Reduction


May reduce salaries of:

- Union employees
- State employees
- Supreme Court judges
- High Court judges

---


### Important Fact


**No Financial Emergency has been declared in India yet.**


Even during **1991 economic crisis**, it was not used.

## 1. Emergency Provisions (Articles 352–360)


Emergency provisions are contained in **Part XVIII of the Constitution (Articles 352–360).**


### Purpose


These provisions enable the **Central Government to deal with abnormal situations** and protect:

- Sovereignty
- Unity
- Integrity
- Security of India
- Democratic political system
- Constitution

During emergency, the **Centre becomes very powerful** and **states come under its control**, converting the federal system into a **unitary system without constitutional amendment**.


### Unique Feature


India’s Constitution can function as:


| Situation    | System  |
| ------------ | ------- |
| Normal times | Federal |
| Emergency    | Unitary |


### Ambedkar’s Observation


Dr. **B.R. Ambedkar** said:

> The Indian Constitution can function both as federal and unitary depending on circumstances.

This flexibility is a **unique feature** of the Indian Constitution.


---


# 2. TYPES OF EMERGENCY


The Constitution provides **three types of emergencies**:


| Type                | Article     | Popular Name              |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------- |
| National Emergency  | Article 352 | Proclamation of Emergency |
| State Emergency     | Article 356 | President’s Rule          |
| Financial Emergency | Article 360 | Financial Emergency       |


---


# 3. NATIONAL EMERGENCY (Article 352)


### Grounds for Declaration


The **President can declare National Emergency** when the **security of India or part of it is threatened by**:

1. War
2. External aggression
3. Armed rebellion

### Important Point


Emergency can be declared even **before the actual occurrence** of these situations if there is **imminent danger**.


---


## Types of National Emergency


| Ground                     | Type               |
| -------------------------- | ------------------ |
| War or External Aggression | External Emergency |
| Armed Rebellion            | Internal Emergency |


---


## Emergency for Entire Country or Part


The **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976)** allowed the President to declare emergency **only for a specific part of India.**


---


## Change of Ground (Important Amendment)


Originally the Constitution used the phrase:


**“Internal Disturbance”**


The **44th Amendment Act 1978 replaced it with “Armed Rebellion”** because internal disturbance was too vague.


### Important Example


The **1975 Emergency** was declared on the ground of **internal disturbance**.


After the amendment, this is **no longer possible**.


---


# 4. Cabinet Recommendation Requirement


The **President can declare emergency only after receiving written recommendation from the Cabinet.**


This means:


Emergency **cannot be declared only on the advice of the Prime Minister**.


### Why this rule?


During the **1975 Emergency**, PM **Indira Gandhi advised the President without consulting the Cabinet.**


To prevent misuse, the **44th Amendment Act 1978 introduced this safeguard.**


---


# 5. Judicial Review of Emergency


### 38th Amendment Act (1975)


Made emergency declaration **immune from judicial review**.


### 44th Amendment Act (1978)


Removed this protection.


Now the **Supreme Court can review emergency proclamation.**


---


### Important Case


**Minerva Mills Case (1980)**


The Supreme Court ruled that emergency declaration can be challenged if:

- Mala fide
- Based on irrelevant facts
- Absurd or perverse

---


# 6. Parliamentary Approval of Emergency


### Approval Period


Emergency must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament within 1 month.**


Earlier the period was **2 months**.


The **44th Amendment Act (1978) reduced it to 1 month.**


---


## Special Situation (Lok Sabha Dissolved)


If **Lok Sabha is dissolved** during this period:


Emergency continues until:


**30 days after the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha**


Provided **Rajya Sabha approves it**.


---


# 7. Duration of National Emergency


After approval:


Emergency remains in force for **6 months**.


It can be **extended indefinitely**, but every **6 months Parliament must approve it again**.


Before **44th Amendment**, once approved it could continue indefinitely.


---


# 8. Special Majority Requirement


Approval of emergency requires **special majority**:


1️⃣ Majority of **total membership of the House**


AND


2️⃣ **Two-thirds of members present and voting**


Before **44th Amendment**, only **simple majority** was required.

# 9. Revocation of National Emergency


A **National Emergency can be revoked in two ways**.


### 1️⃣ By President


The **President can revoke emergency anytime by issuing another proclamation**.


No parliamentary approval is required.


---


### 2️⃣ By Lok Sabha Resolution


The **President must revoke emergency** if **Lok Sabha passes a resolution disapproving its continuation**.


This safeguard was introduced by the **44th Amendment Act (1978)**.


Before this amendment:

- Only the **President could revoke emergency**
- Lok Sabha had **no role**

---


### Special Provision (44th Amendment)


If **1/10th members of Lok Sabha** give written notice:

- A **special sitting must be held within 14 days**
- To discuss **disapproval of emergency**

---


## Difference Between Approval and Disapproval Resolution


| Feature   | Approval         | Disapproval     |
| --------- | ---------------- | --------------- |
| Passed by | Both Houses      | Lok Sabha only  |
| Majority  | Special Majority | Simple Majority |


---


# 10. Effects of National Emergency


A National Emergency has **wide-ranging consequences**.


They can be divided into **three categories**:


1️⃣ Effect on **Centre–State relations**


2️⃣ Effect on **life of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies**


3️⃣ Effect on **Fundamental Rights**


---


# 11. Effect on Centre–State Relations


During emergency the **federal structure becomes unitary**.


These effects are seen in **three areas**:

- Executive
- Legislative
- Financial

---


# (A) Executive Powers


During National Emergency:


The **executive power of the Centre extends to directing states on any matter**.


Normally:


Centre can direct states only on **specific matters**.


But during emergency:


➡ Centre can give directions on **any matter**.


However:

- State governments **are not suspended**
- But they come under **complete control of the Centre**

---


# (B) Legislative Powers


During emergency:


**Parliament can make laws on State List subjects.**


Normally:

- Parliament → Union List
- State → State List

But during emergency:


➡ Parliament gets **power over State List also**.


However:

- State legislatures **are not suspended**
- But their power becomes **subordinate to Parliament**

---


### Validity of Laws


Laws made by Parliament on State subjects during emergency:


Remain valid only **6 months after emergency ends**.


---


### Ordinances


During emergency:


If Parliament is not in session:


➡ **President can issue ordinances even on State List subjects**


---


### Additional Provision (42nd Amendment 1976)


The executive and legislative effects apply:

- Not only to the state where emergency is declared
- But **also to other states**

---


# (C) Financial Powers


During emergency:


The **President can modify the distribution of revenues between Centre and States**.


This means:

- Transfer of funds from Centre to States can be **reduced or cancelled**

This modification continues:


➡ Until the end of the **financial year** in which emergency ends.


The President’s order must be **placed before Parliament**.


---


# 12. Effect on Lok Sabha and State Assemblies


During emergency:


### Lok Sabha Term Extension


The **life of Lok Sabha can be extended beyond 5 years**.


Extension rule:

- **1 year at a time**

But:


It cannot extend **beyond 6 months after emergency ends**.


Example:


**5th Lok Sabha (1971–1977)**


Term extended **twice during emergency**.


---


### State Legislative Assemblies


State assemblies can also be extended:

- **1 year at a time**
- Maximum **6 months after emergency ends**

---


# 13. Effect on Fundamental Rights


Effects on FR are governed by:

- **Article 358**
- **Article 359**

---


# ARTICLE 358


## Suspension of Article 19


Article 358 states:


When National Emergency is declared:


➡ **Fundamental Rights under Article 19 are automatically suspended**


No separate order required.


---


### Meaning


During emergency:


State can:

- Make laws violating Article 19
- Take executive actions violating Article 19

These **cannot be challenged in court**.


---


### After Emergency Ends


Article 19 **revives automatically**.


However:


Actions taken during emergency **cannot be challenged later**.


---


### Restrictions by 44th Amendment


The **44th Amendment Act (1978)** limited Article 358:


1️⃣ Article 19 suspension applies **only during external emergency**


(not during armed rebellion)


2️⃣ Protection applies only to **laws related to emergency**


---


# ARTICLE 359


## Suspension of Enforcement of FR


Article 359 allows the **President to suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of Fundamental Rights**.


Important:


Rights themselves **are not suspended**.


Only **enforcement is suspended**.


---


### Example


Citizens may still have the right.


But they **cannot go to court to enforce it**.


---


### Presidential Order


President specifies:

- Which FR will be affected
- Duration
- Whole country or part

The order must be **placed before Parliament**.


---


### 44th Amendment Safeguard


President **cannot suspend enforcement of**:

- **Article 20** (Protection in respect of conviction)
- **Article 21** (Right to life and personal liberty)

These remain enforceable even during emergency.


---


# VERY IMPORTANT COMPARISON


# Article 358 vs Article 359


| Feature         | Article 358                        | Article 359                          |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Applies to      | Only Article 19                    | Any FR specified                     |
| Nature          | Automatic suspension               | Presidential order required          |
| Rights affected | Article 19 only                    | Any FR except 20 & 21                |
| Emergency type  | Only External Emergency            | Both External & Internal             |
| Duration        | Entire emergency period            | Period specified by President        |
| Area            | Entire country                     | Whole or part of India               |
| State powers    | Can make laws violating Article 19 | Can make laws violating specified FR |


---


# Similarity Between Articles 358 & 359


Both provide immunity only to:

- Laws **related to emergency**
- Executive actions **under those laws**

| Article suspending enforcement of FR      | 359                   |

# 14. National Emergencies Declared in India


National Emergency has been proclaimed **three times in India**.


| Year | Reason               |
| ---- | -------------------- |
| 1962 | Chinese aggression   |
| 1971 | War with Pakistan    |
| 1975 | Internal disturbance |


---


## First Emergency (1962)


Declared in **October 1962** due to **Chinese aggression in NEFA**.


NEFA = **North-East Frontier Agency (now Arunachal Pradesh)**.


It continued until **January 1968**.


Important fact:


Because it was already in force, **no new emergency was declared during the 1965 India-Pakistan war**.


---


## Second Emergency (1971)


Declared in **December 1971**.


Reason:


**Pakistan attack on India during Bangladesh Liberation War**.


---


## Third Emergency (1975)


Declared in **June 1975**.


Reason:


**Internal disturbance**.


Government argued that some people were inciting police and armed forces against their duties.


This emergency became **highly controversial** and led to criticism of misuse of emergency powers.


---


## Aftermath of 1975 Emergency


In **1977 Lok Sabha elections**:

- Congress lost
- **Janata Party came to power**

The government appointed **Shah Commission** to investigate the emergency.


The Commission **did not justify the declaration of emergency**.


As a result:


➡ **44th Constitutional Amendment Act 1978** introduced safeguards against misuse.


---


# PRESIDENT’S RULE


# Article 356


President’s Rule is imposed when **constitutional machinery in a state fails**.


It is also called:

- **State Emergency**
- **Constitutional Emergency**

However the Constitution itself **does not use the word emergency for Article 356**.


---


# Constitutional Basis


## Article 355


Duty of the Union:

> Protect states against external aggression and internal disturbance and ensure governance according to Constitution.

This duty allows the Centre to impose **President’s Rule under Article 356**.


---


# Grounds of President’s Rule


President’s Rule can be proclaimed on two grounds.


### 1️⃣ Article 356


President is satisfied that:


**Government of the state cannot be carried on according to the Constitution**.


President may act:

- On **Governor’s report**
- Or **otherwise** (without governor’s report)

---


### 2️⃣ Article 365


If a state **fails to comply with directions from the Centre**, President may conclude that constitutional machinery has failed.


---


# Parliamentary Approval


President’s Rule must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament within 2 months**.


Special situation:


If Lok Sabha is dissolved:


The proclamation continues until **30 days after the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha**, provided Rajya Sabha has approved it.


---


# Duration


After approval:


President’s Rule continues for **6 months**.


It can be extended **every 6 months**.


Maximum period = **3 years**.


---


# Extension Beyond 1 Year (44th Amendment)


President’s Rule can be extended beyond **1 year only if two conditions are satisfied**.


### Condition 1


National Emergency must be in operation.


### Condition 2


Election Commission certifies that elections cannot be held.


---


# Majority Required


Approval or extension of President’s Rule requires **simple majority** in Parliament.


---


# Revocation


President’s Rule can be **revoked anytime by President**.


No parliamentary approval required.


---


# Consequences of President’s Rule


When President’s Rule is imposed:


### President can

1. Take over functions of state government
2. Exercise powers of governor or other authorities
3. Declare that Parliament will exercise legislative powers

---


# Administrative Changes


During President’s Rule:

- State **Council of Ministers is dismissed**
- Governor runs administration on behalf of President
- State Assembly may be **suspended or dissolved**

---


# Legislative Changes


If state legislature is suspended:


Parliament can:

1. Make laws for the state
2. Delegate law-making power to President
3. Authorise expenditure from State Consolidated Fund
4. Promulgate ordinances

---


# Important Limitation


President **cannot assume powers of High Court**.


The constitutional position of High Court **remains unchanged**.


---


# Use of Article 356


Since **1950**, President’s Rule has been imposed **more than 100 times**.


First time imposed in **Punjab in 1951**.


Many times it was criticized for **political misuse**.


---


# Important Historical Examples


### 1977


Janata Party government dismissed **9 Congress-ruled states**.


### 1980


Congress returned to power and dismissed **9 opposition-ruled states**.


### 1992


President’s Rule imposed in:

- Madhya Pradesh
- Himachal Pradesh
- Rajasthan

Reason: failure to enforce ban on religious organisations.


---


# Bommai Case (1994)


Case: **S. R. Bommai vs Union of India (1994)**


This is the **most important case regarding Article 356**.


The Supreme Court laid down important principles.


---


## Key Judgements


1️⃣ President’s Rule is **subject to judicial review**.


2️⃣ President must rely on **relevant material**.


3️⃣ Court can strike down proclamation if:

- Malafide
- Based on irrelevant grounds

4️⃣ Burden of proof lies on **Central Government**.


5️⃣ Court can **restore dismissed government** if proclamation invalid.


6️⃣ Assembly should be **dissolved only after parliamentary approval**.


7️⃣ **Secularism is a basic feature of Constitution**.


8️⃣ Majority must be tested on **floor of the House**.


9️⃣ New central government **cannot dismiss state governments of other parties**.


10️⃣ Article 356 should be used **only in exceptional situations**.


---


# Proper Use of Article 356


According to **Sarkaria Commission and Bommai case**.


President’s Rule is proper when:

1. Hung assembly
2. Majority party refuses to form government
3. Government resigns and no alternative exists
4. State ignores constitutional directions
5. Internal subversion
6. Physical breakdown of administration

---


# Improper Use of Article 356


It is improper when:

1. Governor dismisses government without floor test
2. Governor makes personal assessment of majority
3. Party loses Lok Sabha election but still has state majority
4. Internal disturbances not amounting to breakdown
5. Maladministration or corruption allegations
6. No prior warning to state government
7. Used for intra-party disputes

---


# FINANCIAL EMERGENCY


# Article 360


President may declare Financial Emergency if:


**Financial stability or credit of India is threatened**.


---


# Parliamentary Approval


Must be approved within **2 months by Parliament**.


If Lok Sabha dissolved:


It continues until **30 days after new Lok Sabha meets**.


---


# Duration


Financial Emergency continues **indefinitely until revoked**.


No maximum time limit.


---


# Majority Required


Approval requires **simple majority** in Parliament.


---


# Effects of Financial Emergency


Centre gains control over **financial matters of states**.


President can:

1. Direct states to follow financial discipline
2. Reduce salaries of state employees
3. Reserve money bills for President’s consideration

---


### Salaries Reduction


President may reduce salaries of:

- Union government employees
- State employees
- Judges of Supreme Court
- Judges of High Courts

---


# Important Fact


**No Financial Emergency has been declared in India so far.**


Even during **1991 economic crisis**, it was not declared.


---


# CRITICISM OF EMERGENCY PROVISIONS


Some Constituent Assembly members criticised emergency provisions.


Main criticisms:

1. Federal structure will be destroyed
2. Centre will become all powerful
3. President may become dictator
4. Financial autonomy of states will disappear
5. Fundamental rights will become meaningless

---


# Supporters’ Arguments


Some members supported emergency provisions.


### Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar


Called them:


**“Life-breath of the Constitution.”**


### Mahabir Tyagi


Said they act as a:


**“Safety valve.”**

# FINAL PRELIMS REVISION POINTS


### National Emergency

- Article **352**
- 3 grounds: war, external aggression, armed rebellion
- Approval within **1 month**
- Special majority required
- Duration **6 months (renewable)**

---


### President’s Rule

- Article **356**
- Approval within **2 months**
- Duration **6 months**
- Maximum **3 years**

---


### Financial Emergency

- Article **360**
- Approval within **2 months**
- No maximum duration
- Never declared
