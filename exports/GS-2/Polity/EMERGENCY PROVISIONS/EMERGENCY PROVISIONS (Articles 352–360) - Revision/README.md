
---


Emergency provisions are contained in **Part XVIII of the Constitution (Articles 352–360)**.


### Purpose


Enable the **Union Government to deal with abnormal situations** and protect:

- Sovereignty
- Unity & integrity of India
- Security of the nation
- Democratic political system
- Constitution

### Key Feature


India’s Constitution can function as:


| Situation        | Nature of System |
| ---------------- | ---------------- |
| Normal times     | Federal          |
| During emergency | Unitary          |


**Dr. B. R. Ambedkar:** Constitution can function as **both federal and unitary depending on circumstances**.


---


# TYPES OF EMERGENCY


| Type                | Article | Popular Name              |
| ------------------- | ------- | ------------------------- |
| National Emergency  | 352     | Proclamation of Emergency |
| State Emergency     | 356     | President’s Rule          |
| Financial Emergency | 360     | Financial Emergency       |


---


# NATIONAL EMERGENCY (Article 352)


## Grounds


President can declare emergency when **security of India or any part is threatened by**:

1. **War**
2. **External Aggression**
3. **Armed Rebellion**

Emergency may be declared **even before the occurrence if danger is imminent**.


---


## Types of National Emergency


| Ground                    | Type               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------ |
| War / External Aggression | External Emergency |
| Armed Rebellion           | Internal Emergency |


---


## Important Amendments


### 42nd Amendment Act 1976


Allowed **emergency for a specific part of India**.


### 44th Amendment Act 1978


Replaced **“Internal Disturbance” → “Armed Rebellion”**.


Reason: _Internal disturbance was vague and prone to misuse._


Example: **1975 Emergency declared on internal disturbance**.


---


# CABINET RECOMMENDATION RULE


President can declare emergency **only after written recommendation of the Cabinet**.


Introduced by **44th Amendment (1978)**.


Reason: During **1975 Emergency**, PM **Indira Gandhi advised the President without Cabinet approval**.


---


# JUDICIAL REVIEW


### 38th Amendment (1975)


Emergency proclamation **not subject to judicial review**.


### 44th Amendment (1978)


Removed this protection.


Now **Supreme Court can review emergency declaration**.


### Important Case


**Minerva Mills Case (1980)**


Court can invalidate emergency if:

- Mala fide
- Based on irrelevant facts
- Absurd or perverse

---


# PARLIAMENTARY APPROVAL


Emergency must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament within 1 month**.


Earlier: **2 months**


Changed by **44th Amendment Act**.


### Special Situation


If **Lok Sabha is dissolved**:


Emergency continues until **30 days after first sitting of new Lok Sabha**, provided **Rajya Sabha approves**.


---


# DURATION OF NATIONAL EMERGENCY


After approval:

- Valid for **6 months**
- Can be **extended indefinitely**
- Requires **parliamentary approval every 6 months**

---


# SPECIAL MAJORITY REQUIREMENT


Approval requires:

1. Majority of **total membership of the House**
2. **Two-thirds of members present and voting**

Earlier: **Simple majority**


Changed by **44th Amendment**.


---


# REVOCATION OF NATIONAL EMERGENCY


## 1. By President


President may **revoke emergency anytime** through proclamation.


No parliamentary approval required.


---


## 2. By Lok Sabha Resolution


If **Lok Sabha passes resolution disapproving emergency**, President **must revoke it**.


Introduced by **44th Amendment**.


### Special Safeguard


If **1/10 of Lok Sabha members give written notice**, a **special sitting must be held within 14 days**.


---


## Approval vs Disapproval


| Feature   | Approval         | Disapproval     |
| --------- | ---------------- | --------------- |
| Passed by | Both Houses      | Lok Sabha       |
| Majority  | Special majority | Simple majority |


---


# EFFECTS OF NATIONAL EMERGENCY


Three areas affected:

1. **Centre–State Relations**
2. **Lok Sabha & State Assemblies**
3. **Fundamental Rights**

---


# EFFECT ON CENTRE–STATE RELATIONS


Federal structure becomes **unitary**.


### Executive Power


Centre can **direct states on any matter**.


State governments **continue but under central control**.


---


### Legislative Power


Parliament can legislate on **State List subjects**.


State legislatures continue but become **subordinate to Parliament**.


Validity of such laws:


➡ Valid **6 months after emergency ends**.


---


### Ordinances


President may issue **ordinances on State List subjects**.


---


### Financial Powers


President may **modify distribution of revenues between Centre and states**.


Valid until **end of financial year in which emergency ends**.


---


# EFFECT ON LEGISLATURE


### Lok Sabha


Term may be extended:

- **1 year at a time**
- Not beyond **6 months after emergency ends**

Example:


**5th Lok Sabha (1971-1977)** extended during emergency.


---


### State Assemblies


Term may also be extended:

- **1 year at a time**
- Maximum **6 months after emergency ends**

---


# EFFECT ON FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS


Governed by:

- **Article 358**
- **Article 359**

---


# ARTICLE 358


Automatic suspension of **Article 19** during National Emergency.


After **44th Amendment**:

- Applies **only during external emergency**
- Protection only for **laws related to emergency**

After emergency ends:

- Article 19 revives
- But actions taken during emergency remain valid.

---


# ARTICLE 359


President may suspend **right to move courts for enforcement of FR**.


Important:

- Rights themselves **not suspended**
- Only **enforcement suspended**

President specifies:

- Which FR affected
- Duration
- Area

---


## Safeguard by 44th Amendment


Enforcement **cannot be suspended for**:

- **Article 20**
- **Article 21**

---


# ARTICLE 358 vs 359


| Feature        | Article 358      | Article 359        |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Applies to     | Article 19 only  | Any FR specified   |
| Nature         | Automatic        | Presidential order |
| Emergency type | External only    | Both types         |
| Area           | Entire country   | Whole/part         |
| Duration       | Entire emergency | As specified       |

# NATIONAL EMERGENCIES IN INDIA


| Year | Reason               |
| ---- | -------------------- |
| 1962 | Chinese aggression   |
| 1971 | Indo-Pakistan War    |
| 1975 | Internal disturbance |


---


### 1962 Emergency


Declared during **Chinese aggression in NEFA (now Arunachal Pradesh)**.


Continued till **January 1968**.


---


### 1971 Emergency


Declared during **Bangladesh Liberation War**.


---


### 1975 Emergency


Declared due to **internal disturbance**.


Highly controversial.


Aftermath:

- **1977 elections → Congress defeated**
- **Janata government formed**
- **Shah Commission appointed**

Led to **44th Constitutional Amendment safeguards**.

# PRESIDENT’S RULE (Article 356)


Also called:

- **State Emergency**
- **Constitutional Emergency**

But Constitution **does not use the word emergency for Article 356**.


---


# CONSTITUTIONAL BASIS


### Article 355


Duty of Union:

- Protect states from external aggression
- Ensure governance according to Constitution

---


# GROUNDS FOR PRESIDENT’S RULE


### Article 356


When **constitutional machinery in state fails**.


President may act:

- On **Governor’s report**
- Or **otherwise**

---


### Article 365


If state **fails to comply with Union directions**.


---


# PARLIAMENTARY APPROVAL


Must be approved within **2 months** by both Houses.


If Lok Sabha dissolved:


Valid until **30 days after first sitting of new Lok Sabha**.


---


# DURATION


After approval:

- **6 months**

Maximum duration:


**3 years**


Extension every **6 months**.


---


# EXTENSION BEYOND 1 YEAR (44th Amendment)


Two conditions required:

1. National Emergency in operation
2. Election Commission certifies elections cannot be held

---


# MAJORITY REQUIRED


Approval or extension requires **simple majority**.


---


# CONSEQUENCES OF PRESIDENT’S RULE


President may:

1. Take over state government functions
2. Exercise powers of governor
3. Authorise Parliament to legislate for state

---


### Administrative Changes

- State **Council of Ministers dismissed**
- Governor administers state on behalf of President
- Assembly **suspended or dissolved**

---


### Legislative Changes


Parliament may:

- Make laws for state
- Delegate law-making to President
- Authorise expenditure from state consolidated fund

---


### Important Limitation


President **cannot assume powers of High Court**.


---


# USE OF ARTICLE 356


Used **100+ times since 1950**.


First imposed:


**Punjab (1951)**.


---


# IMPORTANT CASE


## S. R. Bommai vs Union of India (1994)


Most important judgment on **Article 356**.


### Key Principles

1. President’s Rule **subject to judicial review**
2. Must be based on **relevant material**
3. Court can **restore dismissed government**
4. **Floor test mandatory**
5. Assembly dissolution only **after parliamentary approval**
6. **Secularism is basic feature**
7. Article 356 only for **exceptional situations**

---


# PROPER USE OF ARTICLE 356


According to **Sarkaria Commission & Bommai case**


Examples:

- Hung assembly
- No party willing to form government
- Government resigns
- State ignores constitutional directions
- Internal subversion
- Administrative breakdown

---


# IMPROPER USE

- Governor dismisses government without floor test
- Loss in Lok Sabha election
- Political misuse
- Maladministration or corruption
- No prior warning to state government

---


# FINANCIAL EMERGENCY (Article 360)


Declared when **financial stability or credit of India is threatened**.


---


# PARLIAMENTARY APPROVAL


Must be approved within **2 months**.


---


# DURATION


Continues **indefinitely until revoked**.


---


# MAJORITY REQUIRED


**Simple majority**.


---


# EFFECTS


President may:

1. Direct states to observe financial discipline
2. Reduce salaries of government employees
3. Reserve money bills for President

---


### Salary Reduction


May reduce salaries of:

- Union employees
- State employees
- Supreme Court judges
- High Court judges

---


### Important Fact


**No Financial Emergency has been declared in India yet.**


Even during **1991 economic crisis**, it was not used.

- Duration: **6 months**

- Approval: **2 months**

- Article **360**

- Duration: **Unlimited**
- **Never declared**
