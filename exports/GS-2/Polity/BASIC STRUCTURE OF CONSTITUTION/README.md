
# BASIC STRUCTURE OF THE CONSTITUTION


---


# PART 1 – EMERGENCE OF BASIC STRUCTURE DOCTRINE


## 1. Background


One of the most important constitutional questions in India was:

> **Can Parliament amend Fundamental Rights under Article 368?**

This question was decided through several Supreme Court cases.


---


# 2. Shankari Prasad Case (1951)


### Issue


Constitutional validity of the **First Constitutional Amendment Act (1951)** was challenged.


The amendment **curtailed the Right to Property**.


### Supreme Court Judgement


The Supreme Court held:

1. **Parliament has the power to amend Fundamental Rights.**
2. Article **368 gives Parliament power to amend any part of the Constitution**.
3. **Article 13 does not apply to Constitutional Amendments.**

### Reason


The Court said:

- Article 13 uses the word **“law”**
- But **constitutional amendment is not ordinary law**

Therefore:


**Fundamental Rights can be amended.**


---


# 3. Golak Nath Case (1967)


This case **reversed the judgement of Shankari Prasad case.**


### Issue


Constitutional validity of the **17th Constitutional Amendment Act (1964)**.


This amendment placed certain **state land reform laws into the Ninth Schedule.**


### Supreme Court Judgement


The Court ruled:

1. **Fundamental Rights are transcendental and immutable.**
2. Parliament **cannot amend Fundamental Rights.**
3. **Constitutional Amendment is also a “law” under Article 13.**

Therefore:


Any amendment violating Fundamental Rights would be **void**.


### Result


Parliament **lost power to amend Fundamental Rights.**


---


# 4. Parliament’s Reaction → 24th Constitutional Amendment Act (1971)


Parliament disagreed with the Golak Nath judgement.


So it passed the **24th Amendment Act (1971).**


### Changes Made


It amended:


### Article 13


Declared that:

> Constitutional Amendment is **not law under Article 13**

### Article 368


Declared:

> Parliament has power to **amend any part of the Constitution including Fundamental Rights.**

Thus Parliament restored its power.


---


# 5. Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)


This is the **most important constitutional case in Indian history**.


It is also called:

> **Fundamental Rights Case**

### Issue


Validity of the **24th Amendment Act (1971)**.


### Supreme Court Judgement


The Court held:

1. Parliament **can amend Fundamental Rights.**
2. **24th Amendment is valid.**

BUT the Court introduced a new doctrine:


# BASIC STRUCTURE DOCTRINE


The Court ruled:

> Parliament cannot destroy or alter the **basic structure of the Constitution.**

Meaning:


Parliament’s amending power is **not unlimited**.


---


## Meaning of Basic Structure Doctrine


Parliament can amend:


✔ Any part of Constitution


✔ Fundamental Rights


But cannot:


❌ Destroy basic features of Constitution


---


# Important Principle


Article 368 gives Parliament **constituent power**, but this power is **limited**.


---


# DEVELOPMENT OF BASIC STRUCTURE


---


# 6. Indira Nehru Gandhi Case (1975)


### Background


The **39th Constitutional Amendment Act (1975)** stated:


Election disputes of:

- Prime Minister
- Speaker of Lok Sabha

cannot be decided by courts.


### Supreme Court Decision


The Court **struck down this provision**.


Reason:


It violated **basic structure of the Constitution.**


### Why?


Because it destroyed:

- Free and fair elections
- Judicial review

Both are **basic features of Constitution**.


---


# 7. Parliament’s Reaction → 42nd Amendment Act (1976)


Parliament again tried to limit judicial power.


The amendment declared:

1. **No limitation on Parliament’s amending power**
2. No constitutional amendment can be **challenged in court**
3. Not even for violation of **Fundamental Rights**

This attempted to make Parliament **supreme over the Constitution.**


---


# 8. Minerva Mills Case (1980)


The Supreme Court **struck down parts of the 42nd Amendment**.


### Court’s Reasoning


The Court said:


Parliament has **limited amending power**.


Parliament cannot convert:

> limited power → unlimited power.

### Important Statement by Court

> The donee of limited power cannot convert it into unlimited power.

---


## Principle Established


Two important basic features were confirmed:

1. **Judicial Review**
2. **Limited amending power of Parliament**

---


# 9. Waman Rao Case (1981)


The Supreme Court further clarified the doctrine.


### Decision


Basic Structure Doctrine will apply only to:

> Amendments made **after 24 April 1973**

Why?


Because this was the date of **Kesavananda Bharati judgement.**


---


# ELEMENTS OF BASIC STRUCTURE


The Supreme Court **has never given an exact list**.


However, from different judgements several **basic features have been identified**.


---


# Major Elements of Basic Structure


### 1. Supremacy of the Constitution


The Constitution is **the highest law of the land**.


---


### 2. Sovereign Democratic Republican Nature


India is:

- Sovereign
- Democratic
- Republic

---


### 3. Secular Character of Constitution


State has **no official religion**.


---


### 4. Separation of Powers


Power divided between:

1. Legislature
2. Executive
3. Judiciary

---


### 5. Federal Character of Constitution


Power shared between:

- Union
- States

---


### 6. Unity and Integrity of Nation


Ensures national unity.


---


### 7. Welfare State


Goal of **social and economic justice**.


---


### 8. Judicial Review


Courts can review:

- Laws
- Constitutional amendments

---


### 9. Freedom and Dignity of Individual


Protection of **civil liberties.**


---


### 10. Parliamentary System


Executive accountable to Legislature.


---


### 11. Rule of Law


Government of **laws, not individuals.**


---


### 12. Harmony Between FR and DPSP


Balance between:

- Fundamental Rights
- Directive Principles of State Policy

---


### 13. Principle of Equality


Equality before law.


---


### 14. Free and Fair Elections


Essential for democracy.


---


### 15. Independence of Judiciary


Judiciary must remain autonomous.


---


### 16. Limited Power of Parliament


Parliament’s amendment power is **not absolute**.


---


### 17. Effective Access to Justice


Citizens must be able to approach courts.


---


### 18. Essence of Fundamental Rights


Core principles behind Fundamental Rights cannot be destroyed.


---


### 19. Powers of Supreme Court


Under Articles:

- Article 32
- Article 136
- Article 141
- Article 142

---


### 20. Powers of High Courts


Under Articles:

- Article 226
- Article 227

---
