
## STATE COUNCIL OF MINISTERS (राज्य मंत्रिपरिषद)

---


# 1. CONSTITUTIONAL BASIS


India follows **Parliamentary System of Government** at both:


| Level | Nominal Executive | Real Executive                    |
| ----- | ----------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Union | President         | Council of Ministers headed by PM |
| State | Governor          | Council of Ministers headed by CM |


Thus, **real executive authority of State = Council of Ministers led by Chief Minister**.


The **state politico-administrative system functions through the Council of Ministers.**


Structure is **similar to Union Council of Ministers**.


---


# IMPORTANT CONSTITUTIONAL ARTICLES


| Article | Provision                                        |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **163** | Council of Ministers to aid and advise Governor  |
| **164** | Appointment, tenure, responsibility of ministers |
| **166** | Conduct of state government business             |
| **167** | Duties of Chief Minister                         |
| **177** | Rights of Ministers in State Legislature         |


---


# 2. ARTICLE 163 — COUNCIL OF MINISTERS TO AID AND ADVISE GOVERNOR


## Provision


There shall be a **Council of Ministers with Chief Minister as head** to **aid and advise the Governor** in exercising his functions.


### Exception


Governor may act **in discretion** in certain matters.


---


## Key Points


### Aid and Advice Principle


Governor normally acts on:


**Aid and advice of Council of Ministers**


---


### Governor’s Discretion


If dispute arises whether matter is discretionary:

- **Governor's decision is final**
- Validity of action **cannot be challenged**

---


### Confidentiality of Advice


Courts **cannot inquire into advice given by ministers to Governor**.


Purpose:


→ Maintain **confidentiality of executive functioning**


---


# 3. ARTICLE 164 — PROVISIONS RELATED TO MINISTERS


Article deals with:

- Appointment
- Tenure
- Responsibility
- Qualification
- Oath
- Salary

---


# 4. APPOINTMENT OF MINISTERS


### Chief Minister


Appointed by **Governor**


---


### Other Ministers


Appointed by **Governor on advice of Chief Minister**


Meaning:


Governor **cannot appoint ministers independently**


---


# SPECIAL PROVISION — TRIBAL WELFARE MINISTER


A **Tribal Welfare Minister is mandatory in:**

- Chhattisgarh
- Jharkhand
- Madhya Pradesh
- Odisha

This minister may also handle:

- SC Welfare
- OBC Welfare
- Other responsibilities

---


## 94th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2006


Earlier states:

- Bihar
- Madhya Pradesh
- Odisha

Changes:


| Removed | Added        |
| ------- | ------------ |
| Bihar   | Chhattisgarh |
|         | Jharkhand    |


Reason:

- Bihar **has no Scheduled Areas**
- ST population **very small**

---


# 5. SIZE OF COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


## 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003


| Rule         | Provision                                |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Maximum size | **15% of Legislative Assembly strength** |
| Minimum size | **12 ministers**                         |


Includes **Chief Minister**.


---


# 6. ANTI-DEFECTION PROVISION


Also added by **91st Amendment Act, 2003**


A legislator **disqualified for defection**:


❌ **Cannot be appointed as minister**


---


# 7. TENURE OF MINISTERS


Ministers hold office:


**“During the pleasure of the Governor.”**


But in practice:


Governor acts **on advice of Chief Minister**.


---


# 8. MEMBERSHIP OF LEGISLATURE


Minister must be:


**Member of State Legislature**


But non-member can be appointed.


Condition:


Must become member within **6 months**


Otherwise:


→ **Ceases to be minister**


---


# 9. OATH OF MINISTERS


Administered by **Governor**


Two Oaths:


| Oath            | Purpose                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------ |
| Oath of Office  | Loyalty to Constitution  |
| Oath of Secrecy | Maintain confidentiality |


---


## Oath of Office Includes


Minister swears to:

- Bear true faith to **Constitution**
- Uphold **sovereignty & integrity of India**
- Faithfully discharge duties
- Do justice **without fear or favour**

---


## Oath of Secrecy


Minister promises:

- Not disclose confidential matters
- Except when required for official duties

---


# 10. SALARY AND ALLOWANCES


Determined by **State Legislature**


Includes:

- Salary
- Allowances
- Free accommodation
- Travel allowance
- Medical facilities
- Sumptuary allowance

---


# 11. ARTICLE 166 — CONDUCT OF STATE GOVERNMENT BUSINESS


All executive actions of state government are taken:


**In the name of the Governor**


---


## Authentication of Orders


Orders issued in Governor's name:

- Must be **authenticated as per rules**
- Once authenticated → **cannot be challenged**

---


## Business Rules


Governor makes rules for:

- Transaction of government business
- Allocation of business among ministers

Except where Governor acts **in discretion**.


---


# 12. ARTICLE 167 — DUTIES OF CHIEF MINISTER


Chief Minister must:


### 1️⃣ Inform Governor


Communicate:

- Decisions of Council of Ministers
- Legislative proposals

---


### 2️⃣ Provide Information


Provide information about:

- Administration of state affairs
- Proposed legislation

---


### 3️⃣ Submit Matters to Council


If Governor asks:


CM must place before Council any matter:

- Decided by minister
- But not considered by Council

---


# 13. ARTICLE 177 — RIGHTS OF MINISTERS IN STATE LEGISLATURE


Every minister has right to:


✔ Speak in Legislative Assembly


✔ Speak in Legislative Council


✔ Participate in proceedings


✔ Participate in committees


BUT


❌ Cannot vote unless **member of that House**


---


# 14. NATURE OF ADVICE — SUPREME COURT OBSERVATIONS


## 1971 Judgment


Council of Ministers **must always exist** to advise Governor.


Even if:

- Assembly dissolved
- Ministry resigns

Existing ministry continues until successor assumes office.


---


## 1974 Judgment


Except discretionary matters:


Governor **must act on aid and advice of Council of Ministers**.


Where Constitution mentions:


**“Governor’s satisfaction”**


It means:


**Satisfaction of Council of Ministers**


Not personal satisfaction.


---


# 15. RESPONSIBILITY OF MINISTERS


Three types:


| Type                      | Meaning                              |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Collective Responsibility | Council responsible to Assembly      |
| Individual Responsibility | Minister removable individually      |
| No Legal Responsibility   | Advice cannot be questioned in court |


---


# 15.1 COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY


Established by **Article 164**


Council of Ministers collectively responsible to:


**Legislative Assembly**


Core principle of **Parliamentary System**


---


## Meaning


All ministers jointly responsible for:

- Acts of commission
- Acts of omission

Phrase:


**“They swim together and sink together.”**


---


## No-Confidence Motion


If Assembly passes:


**No-Confidence Motion**


→ Entire Council must resign.


Includes ministers from **Legislative Council**.


---


## Alternative Option


Council may advise Governor to:


**Dissolve Legislative Assembly**


Reason:


House no longer represents electorate.


Then:


→ **Fresh elections**


But Governor may **refuse** if government lost majority.


---


## Cabinet Solidarity


Cabinet decisions binding on **all ministers**.


Ministers must:

- Support decisions
- Defend them in legislature
- Defend them publicly

If minister cannot support decision:


→ **Must resign**


---


# 15.2 INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBILITY


Also under **Article 164**


Ministers hold office during:


**Pleasure of Governor**


Meaning:


Minister can be removed individually.


---


### Real Practice


Governor removes minister **only on CM's advice**.


CM may:


1️⃣ Ask minister to resign


2️⃣ Advise Governor to dismiss him


Purpose:


Maintain **discipline and unity** in council.


---


# 15.3 NO LEGAL RESPONSIBILITY


Indian Constitution does **not provide legal responsibility of ministers**.


Meaning:


Governor's orders **do not require countersignature of ministers**.


Also:


Courts **cannot examine ministerial advice to Governor**.


Thus advice remains **confidential**.


---


# 16. COMPOSITION OF COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


Constitution **does not specify:**

- Exact size
- Ranking of ministers

Determined by **Chief Minister**.


---


## Categories of Ministers


| Category           | Position         |
| ------------------ | ---------------- |
| Cabinet Ministers  | Senior ministers |
| Ministers of State | Junior ministers |
| Deputy Ministers   | Junior-most      |


Top authority:


**Chief Minister**


---


# 16.1 CABINET MINISTERS


Most important ministers.


Head major departments such as:

- Home
- Finance
- Agriculture
- Education
- Health
- Revenue

Role:

- Members of **Cabinet**
- Attend **Cabinet meetings**
- Participate in **major policy decisions**

---


# 16.2 MINISTERS OF STATE


Junior ministers.


Two roles:


### Independent Charge


Head department independently.


Example:


Minister of State for Tourism.


---


### Attached to Cabinet Minister


Assist cabinet minister.


Example:


Minister of State in Finance.


---


## Cabinet Membership


Ministers of State:


❌ Not members of Cabinet


Attend meetings **only when invited**.


---


# 16.3 DEPUTY MINISTERS


Junior-most ministers.


Characteristics:

- No independent charge
- Attached to Cabinet Ministers

Assist in:

- Administration
- Political work
- Legislative work

Do not attend **Cabinet meetings**.


---


# 16.4 DEPUTY CHIEF MINISTER


Post **not mentioned in Constitution**.


Created for:

- Coalition balance
- Political reasons
- Regional representation

---


# 17. CABINET


Cabinet = **Inner core of Council of Ministers**


Consists only of:


**Cabinet Ministers**


---


## Importance


Cabinet is:


**Real centre of power in state government**


---


# FUNCTIONS OF CABINET


## 1️⃣ Highest Decision-Making Authority


Top decision-making body in state government.


---


## 2️⃣ Policy Formulation


Decides:

- Government policies
- Development programmes
- Administrative strategies

---


## 3️⃣ Supreme Executive Authority


Supervises entire **state administration**.


---


## 4️⃣ Coordination of Administration


Coordinates:

- Ministries
- Departments
- Government activities

---


## 5️⃣ Advisory Body to Governor


Advises Governor on:

- Administration
- Legislation
- Policy decisions

---


## 6️⃣ Crisis Management


Handles:

- Political crises
- Law & order emergencies
- Natural disasters
- Administrative breakdowns

---


## 7️⃣ Legislative and Financial Role


Handles:

- Legislative proposals
- Budget
- Financial policies

---


## 8️⃣ Control Over Higher Appointments


Influences appointment of:

- Constitutional authorities
- Senior secretariat officials

---


# 18. CABINET COMMITTEES


Cabinet functions through **Cabinet Committees**.


---


## Types


| Type                | Nature    |
| ------------------- | --------- |
| Standing Committees | Permanent |
| Ad-hoc Committees   | Temporary |


---


## Formation


Created by **Chief Minister**.


CM decides:

- Number
- Name
- Composition

---


## Functions


Cabinet Committees:

- Examine issues in detail
- Prepare proposals for Cabinet
- Sometimes take decisions

But Cabinet can **review decisions**.


---


# 19. VERY IMPORTANT PRELIMS FACT


The term **“Ministry”** is used only at **Union level**.


In **States**:


Government is divided into **Departments**, not Ministries.

# STATE COUNCIL OF MINISTERS (राज्य मंत्रिपरिषद)


---


# 1. INTRODUCTION


India follows a **Parliamentary System of Government** both at the **Union and State level**.


At the **State level**:

- The **Governor** is the **constitutional head (nominal executive)**.
- The **Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister** is the **real executive authority**.

Thus, the **politico-administrative system of a state is run by the Council of Ministers**.


The **structure and functioning of the State Council of Ministers is similar to the Union Council of Ministers**.


The Constitution **does not describe the parliamentary system in detail**, but provides broad provisions through certain Articles.


Important Articles:


| Article | Subject                                          |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **163** | Council of Ministers to aid and advise Governor  |
| **164** | Appointment, tenure, responsibility of ministers |
| **166** | Conduct of business of state government          |
| **167** | Duties of Chief Minister                         |
| **177** | Rights of Ministers in State Legislature         |


These Articles give the **constitutional framework for the State Council of Ministers**.


---


# 2. ARTICLE 163 — COUNCIL OF MINISTERS TO AID AND ADVISE GOVERNOR


## Provision


There shall be a **Council of Ministers with the Chief Minister as the head** to **aid and advise the Governor** in exercising his functions.


Exception:


The Governor may act **in his discretion** in certain matters.


---


## Important Points


### 1. Aid and Advice Principle

- The **Governor normally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers**.
- The **Chief Minister heads the Council of Ministers**.

### 2. Governor’s Discretion


If a question arises whether a matter falls under the **Governor’s discretion**:

- **Governor’s decision is final.**
- The **validity of his actions cannot be challenged** on this ground.

### 3. Advice of Ministers Cannot Be Questioned


The **courts cannot inquire into the advice given by ministers to the Governor**.


This maintains **confidentiality between the Governor and the Council of Ministers**.


---


# 3. ARTICLE 164 — OTHER PROVISIONS RELATED TO MINISTERS


This Article deals with:

- Appointment
- Tenure
- Responsibility
- Qualifications
- Oath
- Salary

---


# 4. APPOINTMENT OF MINISTERS


### Chief Minister

- **Appointed by the Governor**

### Other Ministers

- **Appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister**

Meaning:


The **Governor cannot appoint ministers independently**.


---


## Special Provision (Tribal Welfare Minister)


There must be a **Minister in charge of Tribal Welfare** in:

- **Chhattisgarh**
- **Jharkhand**
- **Madhya Pradesh**
- **Odisha**

This minister may also handle:

- Scheduled Castes welfare
- Backward Classes welfare
- Other responsibilities

---


## Important Amendment


### 94th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2006


Before the amendment the provision applied to:

- Bihar
- Madhya Pradesh
- Odisha

Changes:

- **Bihar was removed** from the provision.
- **Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand were added**.

Reason:

- Bihar **does not have Scheduled Areas**
- **Scheduled Tribe population is very small**

---


# 5. SIZE OF COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


### 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003


The **total number of ministers including Chief Minister**:

- **Maximum:** 15% of total strength of Legislative Assembly

Minimum:

- **Not less than 12 ministers**

---


# 6. ANTI-DEFECTION PROVISION


Also introduced by **91st Amendment Act 2003**.


A legislator who is:

- **Disqualified on grounds of defection**

Cannot be:

- Appointed as a **minister**

---


# 7. TENURE OF MINISTERS


Ministers **hold office during the pleasure of the Governor**.


However:


In reality, the Governor acts **on the advice of the Chief Minister**.


---


# 8. COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY


The **Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly**.


Meaning:

- Ministers function **as a team**
- They **stand or fall together**

If the **Assembly passes a No-Confidence Motion**:

- **Entire Council of Ministers must resign**

Including:

- Ministers from the **Legislative Council**

---


# 9. MEMBERSHIP OF LEGISLATURE


A minister must be a **member of the State Legislature**.


But:


A person who is **not a member** can also be appointed minister.


Condition:


He must become a member **within 6 months**.


Otherwise:


He **ceases to be a minister**.


---


# 10. OATH OF MINISTERS


Before assuming office:


The **Governor administers two oaths**:


1️⃣ Oath of Office


2️⃣ Oath of Secrecy


---


## Oath of Office


The minister swears to:

1. Bear true faith and allegiance to the **Constitution of India**
2. Uphold the **sovereignty and integrity of India**
3. Faithfully discharge duties
4. Do justice to all people **without fear or favour**

---


## Oath of Secrecy


The minister promises:

- Not to reveal confidential matters
- Except when required for official duties

---


# 11. SALARY AND ALLOWANCES OF MINISTERS


Determined by the **State Legislature**.


Ministers receive:

- Salary
- Allowances
- Free accommodation
- Travelling allowance
- Medical facilities
- Sumptuary allowance (depending on rank)

---


# 12. ARTICLE 166 — CONDUCT OF BUSINESS OF STATE GOVERNMENT


All **executive actions of the state government are taken in the name of the Governor**.


---


## Authentication of Orders


Orders issued in the name of the Governor:

- Must be **authenticated according to rules made by the Governor**

Once authenticated:

- **Validity cannot be challenged**.

---


## Business Rules


The Governor makes rules for:

- **Convenient transaction of government business**
- **Allocation of business among ministers**

Except where the Governor acts in **discretion**.


---


# 13. ARTICLE 167 — DUTIES OF THE CHIEF MINISTER


The Chief Minister must:


### 1. Inform Governor


Communicate to the Governor:

- Decisions of the Council of Ministers
- Legislative proposals

### 2. Provide Information


Furnish information regarding:

- Administration of state affairs
- Proposed legislation

### 3. Submit Matters to Council


If the Governor asks:


The CM must place before the Council of Ministers any matter decided by a minister but **not considered by the Council**.


---


# 14. ARTICLE 177 — RIGHTS OF MINISTERS IN STATE LEGISLATURE


Every minister has the right to:

- Speak in the Legislative Assembly
- Speak in the Legislative Council (if exists)
- Participate in proceedings
- Participate in committees

But:


❌ **Cannot vote unless he is a member of that House**


---


# 15. NATURE OF ADVICE BY MINISTERS


The **Council of Ministers advises the Governor**.


Except where the Governor acts **in discretion**.


---


## Supreme Court Observations


### 1971 Judgment


The **Council of Ministers must always exist** to advise the Governor.


Even if:

- Legislative Assembly is dissolved
- Council of Ministers resigns

The **existing ministry continues until a successor takes office**.


---


### 1974 Judgment


Except in discretionary matters:


The **Governor must act on aid and advice of the Council of Ministers**.


Meaning:

- Governor cannot act independently
- Governor cannot act against advice

Where Constitution requires **Governor’s satisfaction**:


It means:


**Satisfaction of Council of Ministers**, not personal satisfaction of Governor.


---


# 16. RESPONSIBILITY OF MINISTERS


Responsibility of ministers in a parliamentary system exists in **three forms**:

1. **Collective Responsibility**
2. **Individual Responsibility**
3. **No Legal Responsibility**

---


# 16.1 COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY


**Article 164** establishes the principle of **collective responsibility**.


The **Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly of the State.**


This is the **core principle of the Parliamentary System of Government**.


---


## Meaning


All ministers are **jointly responsible for all actions of the government**.


This includes:

- Acts of commission
- Acts of omission

They **function as a team**.


Often expressed as:


**“They swim together and sink together.”**


---


## No Confidence Motion


If the **Legislative Assembly passes a No-Confidence Motion**:

- Entire **Council of Ministers must resign**

This includes:

- Ministers from **Legislative Council**

Even if they were not directly responsible.


---


## Alternative Option


Instead of resigning, the Council of Ministers may:


Advise the **Governor to dissolve the Legislative Assembly**.


Reason given:


The House **no longer represents the will of the electorate**.


Then:

- **Fresh elections** are held.

However:


The **Governor may refuse this advice** if the ministry has **already lost majority support**.


---


## Cabinet Solidarity


Collective responsibility also means **cabinet solidarity**.


Cabinet decisions are binding on **all ministers**.


Even if a minister **disagreed during cabinet meetings**.


Every minister must:

- Support cabinet decisions
- Defend them inside legislature
- Defend them outside legislature

---


## If Minister Disagrees


If a minister:

- Disagrees with a cabinet decision
- Cannot defend it publicly

Then:


**He must resign.**


Many ministers in India have resigned in the past due to such disagreements.


---


# 16.2 INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBILITY


Article 164 also provides **individual responsibility**.


It states:


Ministers **hold office during the pleasure of the Governor**.


---


## Meaning


A minister can be **removed individually**.


Even when:

- The Council of Ministers enjoys majority support.

---


## Real Position


In practice:


The Governor removes a minister **only on the advice of the Chief Minister**.


---


## Role of Chief Minister


If the Chief Minister:

- Is dissatisfied with a minister
- Or has differences with him

The CM may:

1. Ask the minister to **resign**
2. Advise the Governor to **dismiss the minister**

This power allows the CM to **maintain discipline and unity in the council of ministers**.


---


# 16.3 NO LEGAL RESPONSIBILITY


Unlike some parliamentary systems:


The Indian Constitution **does not provide legal responsibility of ministers**.


Meaning:


A **Governor's order for public acts does not require countersignature of a minister**.


Also:


Courts **cannot inquire into the advice given by ministers to the Governor**.


Thus:


The **advice remains confidential**.


---


# 17. COMPOSITION OF COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


The Constitution **does not specify**:

- Exact size of the Council of Ministers
- Ranking of ministers

These are decided by the **Chief Minister** based on:

- Political requirements
- Administrative needs
- Circumstances of the time

---


## Categories of Ministers


Like the Union Government, states also have **three categories of ministers**:

1. **Cabinet Ministers**
2. **Ministers of State**
3. **Deputy Ministers**

The difference lies in:

- Rank
- Salary
- Political importance

At the top of all stands:


**Chief Minister — Supreme authority in the State Government**


---


# 17.1 CABINET MINISTERS


These are the **most important ministers**.


They:

- Head **major departments**

Examples:

- Home
- Finance
- Education
- Agriculture
- Health
- Revenue

---


## Role


Cabinet Ministers:

- Are **members of the Cabinet**
- Attend **cabinet meetings**
- Participate in **major policy decisions**

Their responsibilities extend across the **entire government system**.


---


# 17.2 MINISTERS OF STATE


They are **junior ministers**.


They can function in two ways:


### 1. Independent Charge


A Minister of State may:

- Head a department independently.

Example:


Minister of State for Tourism.


---


### 2. Attached to Cabinet Minister


They may assist a Cabinet Minister.


Example:


Minister of State in Finance Ministry.


---


## Cabinet Membership


Ministers of State:

- **Are not members of the Cabinet**

They attend cabinet meetings **only when invited**.


Usually when matters related to their department are discussed.


---


# 17.3 DEPUTY MINISTERS


They are **junior-most ministers**.


Characteristics:

- No independent charge
- Attached to Cabinet Ministers

Their role is to assist Cabinet Ministers in:

- Administrative work
- Political duties
- Parliamentary work

---


## Cabinet Participation


Deputy Ministers:

- **Do not attend Cabinet meetings**

---


# 17.4 DEPUTY CHIEF MINISTER


Sometimes a state appoints a:


**Deputy Chief Minister**


However:


This post **is not mentioned in the Constitution**.


It is created mainly for:

- Political reasons
- Coalition balance
- Regional representation

---


# 18. CABINET


The **Cabinet is a smaller and more powerful body within the Council of Ministers**.


It consists **only of Cabinet Ministers**.


---


## Importance


The Cabinet is:


**The real centre of power in the state government.**


---


# FUNCTIONS OF THE CABINET


The Cabinet performs several key roles.


---


## 1. Highest Decision-Making Authority


It is the **highest decision-making body** in the **politico-administrative system of the state**.


All major decisions originate here.


---


## 2. Policy Formulation


The Cabinet is the **chief policy-formulating body** of the state government.


It decides:

- Government policies
- Development programmes
- Administrative strategies

---


## 3. Supreme Executive Authority


The Cabinet acts as the **supreme executive authority**.


It supervises and directs:

- Entire administration of the state.

---


## 4. Coordination of Administration


It coordinates:

- Different departments
- Activities of various ministries

Ensuring **smooth governance**.


---


## 5. Advisory Body to the Governor


The Cabinet advises the **Governor** on:

- Administration
- Legislation
- Policy decisions

---


## 6. Crisis Management


The Cabinet acts as the **chief crisis manager**.


It handles situations like:

- Political crises
- Law and order emergencies
- Natural disasters
- Administrative breakdowns

---


## 7. Legislative and Financial Matters


The Cabinet handles major:

- Legislative proposals
- Financial policies
- Budget decisions

---


## 8. Control Over Higher Appointments


The Cabinet exercises control over important appointments such as:

- Constitutional authorities
- Senior secretariat officials

---


# 19. CABINET COMMITTEES


The Cabinet functions through **smaller groups called Cabinet Committees**.


---


## Types


Two types of Cabinet Committees exist:


### 1. Standing Committees

- Permanent in nature

### 2. Ad Hoc Committees

- Temporary
- Created for specific issues

---


## Formation


Cabinet Committees are:


Created by the **Chief Minister**.


Their:

- Number
- Name
- Composition

may change depending on:

- Political conditions
- Administrative needs

---


## Functions


Cabinet Committees:

- Examine issues in detail
- Prepare proposals for Cabinet
- Sometimes take decisions themselves

However:


The **Cabinet can review their decisions**.
