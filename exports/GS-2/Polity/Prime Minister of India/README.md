
## PRIME MINISTER (प्रधानमंत्री)

---


# 1. POSITION OF PRIME MINISTER IN INDIAN POLITY


### Parliamentary System


India follows **Parliamentary Form of Government**.


| Authority      | Type                            | Role               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| President      | **Nominal Executive (De Jure)** | Head of State      |
| Prime Minister | **Real Executive (De Facto)**   | Head of Government |


**Meaning**


| Term                   | Meaning                           |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **De Jure Executive**  | Constitutional / formal authority |
| **De Facto Executive** | Actual authority exercising power |


✔ **President → Head of State**


✔ **Prime Minister → Head of Government**


➡ **Prime Minister is the most powerful functionary in Indian political system.**


**Dr B. R. Ambedkar**

> If any functionary under our Constitution is to be compared with the US President, it is the **Prime Minister and not the President.**

---


# 2. APPOINTMENT OF PRIME MINISTER


### Constitutional Provision


**Article 75**

> Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President.

However:


⚠ Constitution **does not specify detailed procedure**


➡ **Constitutional conventions followed**


---


## Normal Situation (Clear Majority)


When **one party has majority in Lok Sabha**


➡ President appoints **leader of majority party** as Prime Minister.


Example


| PM            | Year       |
| ------------- | ---------- |
| Narendra Modi | 2014, 2019 |


---


## Hung Parliament (No Clear Majority)


When **no party has majority**


➡ President uses **discretion**


Usually appoints:


➡ Leader of **largest party / coalition**


Then asks PM to:


➡ **Prove majority in Lok Sabha**


Usually through **Confidence Vote within about one month**


---


### Example


| Event        | Detail                           |
| ------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Year         | 1979                             |
| President    | Neelam Sanjiva Reddy             |
| PM Appointed | Charan Singh                     |
| Reason       | Fall of Morarji Desai government |


---


## Special Situation: Sudden Death of PM


Example


| Event     | Detail                     |
| --------- | -------------------------- |
| Year      | 1984                       |
| PM        | Indira Gandhi assassinated |
| President | Zail Singh                 |
| Appointed | Rajiv Gandhi               |


⚠ This **bypassed caretaker PM convention**


Later:


✔ Congress Parliamentary Party **elected Rajiv Gandhi unanimously**


---


## When Ruling Party Elects New Leader


If ruling party elects new leader after PM death/resignation


➡ President **must appoint that person**


❗ President **has no discretion**


---


# Majority Proof Rule (Important Case)


### Delhi High Court Judgment (1980)


Constitution **does NOT require majority proof before appointment**


✔ Correct sequence:

1. President appoints PM
2. PM proves majority in Lok Sabha **within reasonable time**

---


### Examples


| Prime Minister      | Year |
| ------------------- | ---- |
| Charan Singh        | 1979 |
| V. P. Singh         | 1989 |
| Chandra Shekhar     | 1990 |
| P. V. Narasimha Rao | 1991 |
| A. B. Vajpayee      | 1996 |
| H. D. Deve Gowda    | 1996 |
| I. K. Gujral        | 1997 |
| A. B. Vajpayee      | 1998 |


---


# PM WITHOUT BEING MEMBER OF PARLIAMENT


### Supreme Court Judgment (1997)


A person **not member of Parliament can be appointed PM**.


Condition:


➡ Must become member of **either House within 6 months**


Otherwise:


➡ **Ceases to be Prime Minister**


---


# PRIME MINISTER FROM RAJYA SABHA


Indian Constitution allows PM from **either House**.


Examples


| Prime Minister          | House       |
| ----------------------- | ----------- |
| Indira Gandhi (1966)    | Rajya Sabha |
| H. D. Deve Gowda (1996) | Rajya Sabha |
| Manmohan Singh (2004)   | Rajya Sabha |


---


### Comparison with UK


| Country | Rule                                        |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| UK      | PM must be from **House of Commons**        |
| India   | PM can be from **Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha** |


---


# 3. OATH, TERM AND SALARY


---


# OATH


Before entering office


➡ **President administers oath**


Two oaths:


1️⃣ **Oath of Office**


2️⃣ **Oath of Secrecy**


---


## Oath of Office


PM swears:

1. True faith and allegiance to **Constitution**
2. Uphold **sovereignty and integrity of India**
3. Faithfully discharge duties
4. Do justice **without fear or favour**

---


## Oath of Secrecy


PM promises:


➡ Not to reveal **confidential matters**


Except when required for **official duties**


---


# TERM OF PRIME MINISTER


Constitution **does not fix tenure**.


PM holds office:


➡ **During pleasure of President**


But in practice:


➡ Depends on **majority support in Lok Sabha**


If majority lost:

- PM resigns

    OR

- President dismisses PM

---


# SALARY AND ALLOWANCES


Determined by **Parliament**


Prime Minister receives:

- Salary of **Member of Parliament**
- **Sumptuary allowance**
- **Free official residence**
- **Travelling allowance**
- **Medical facilities**

Example


2001


Sumptuary allowance increased


₹1500 → ₹3000


---


# 4. POWERS AND FUNCTIONS OF PRIME MINISTER


Four major areas:


1️⃣ Powers relating to **Council of Ministers**


2️⃣ Powers relating to **President**


3️⃣ Powers relating to **Parliament**


4️⃣ **Other powers and functions**


---


# I. POWERS IN RELATION TO COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


PM is **Head of Council of Ministers**


---


## 1. Appointment of Ministers


PM **recommends ministers**


➡ President appoints ministers **on PM's advice**


Thus:


✔ PM determines **composition of Council of Ministers**


---


## 2. Allocation of Portfolios


PM:

- Allocates ministries
- Can reshuffle portfolios anytime

Examples


Finance / Defence / Home / External Affairs


---


## 3. Removal of Ministers


PM may:

- Ask minister to **resign**

    OR

- Advise President to **dismiss minister**

Reasons

- Policy differences
- Misconduct
- Political crisis

---


## 4. Chairman of Council Meetings


PM:

- Presides over meetings
- Controls agenda
- Influences decisions

---


## 5. Coordination Role


PM:

- Guides
- Directs
- Controls
- Coordinates

activities of all ministers.


Ensures **policy unity + administrative coordination**


---


## 6. Collapse of Council of Ministers


If **Prime Minister resigns**


➡ Entire **Council of Ministers collapses**


Reason


PM is **head of Council of Ministers**


But


If **any other minister resigns**


➡ Only that minister’s office becomes vacant


---


# II. POWERS IN RELATION TO PRESIDENT


PM acts as **principal link between President and Council of Ministers**


---


## 1. Channel of Communication


PM communicates to President:

- Decisions of Council of Ministers
- Administration of Union
- Legislative proposals

---


## 2. Providing Information


President may request information regarding

- Administration
- Legislative proposals

PM **must provide it**


---


## 3. Placing Matters Before Council


If President requires


PM must place before Council of Ministers:


➡ Decisions taken by an **individual minister**


not yet considered by cabinet


---


## 4. Advice on Key Appointments


PM advises President on appointment of:

- **Attorney General of India**
- **CAG**
- **UPSC Chairman & Members**
- **Election Commissioners**
- **Finance Commission Chairman & Members**

---


# III. POWERS IN RELATION TO PARLIAMENT


PM is **Leader of Lok Sabha**


---


## 1. Summoning and Proroguing Parliament


PM advises President regarding:

- Summoning sessions
- Proroguing sessions

---


## 2. Dissolution of Lok Sabha


PM may recommend:


➡ **Dissolution of Lok Sabha**


before completion of **5-year term**


---


## 3. Announcement of Government Policies


PM:

- Announces government policies
- Represents government in debates

---


# IV. OTHER POWERS AND FUNCTIONS


---


## Chairman of Important Bodies


PM chairs:

- **Planning Commission** (now **NITI Aayog**)
- **National Development Council**
- **National Integration Council**
- **Inter-State Council**
- **National Water Resources Council**

---


## Foreign Policy Role


PM plays major role in

- Diplomacy
- Strategic relations
- International negotiations

---


## Chief Spokesperson


PM explains government policies to:

- Parliament
- Public
- Media

---


## Crisis Manager


During crises:

- War
- Economic crisis
- National emergency

PM acts as **national crisis manager**


---


## Leader of Nation


PM:

- Interacts with society
- Receives grievances
- Provides national leadership

---


## Leader of Ruling Party


PM leads **party in power**


Influences:

- Government policy
- Parliamentary strategy
- Party decisions

---


## Political Head of Civil Services


PM is considered **political head of civil services**

# PRIME MINISTER (प्रधानमंत्री)


---


# POSITION OF PRIME MINISTER IN INDIAN POLITY


### Parliamentary System Structure


India follows a **Parliamentary System of Government**.


Two executive authorities exist:


| Authority      | Type                            | Role               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| President      | **Nominal Executive (De Jure)** | Head of State      |
| Prime Minister | **Real Executive (De Facto)**   | Head of Government |


✔ **Meaning**

- **De jure executive** → Constitutional head (formal powers)
- **De facto executive** → Real authority exercising power

Thus:


**President = Head of State**


**Prime Minister = Head of Government**


The **Prime Minister is the most powerful functionary in the political system of India.**


Dr. **B. R. Ambedkar** said:

> “If any functionary under our Constitution is to be compared with the US President, he is the Prime Minister and not the President.”

---


# PART 2 — APPOINTMENT OF PRIME MINISTER


### Constitutional Provision


**Article 75**

> “The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President.”

However, the Constitution **does not provide a detailed procedure** for selection.


Therefore **constitutional conventions** are followed.


---


# NORMAL SITUATION (CLEAR MAJORITY)


When one party has a **clear majority in Lok Sabha**:


➡ President appoints **leader of majority party** as Prime Minister.


Example:

- **Narendra Modi – 2014, 2019**

---


# WHEN NO PARTY HAS MAJORITY (HUNG PARLIAMENT)


When no party has majority:


President uses **discretion (विवेक)**.


Usually he appoints:


➡ Leader of **largest party or coalition**


Then asks him to **prove majority in Lok Sabha**.


Usually **confidence vote must be obtained within about a month.**


---


### Example


1979


President **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy**


Appointed **Charan Singh** as Prime Minister


After fall of **Morarji Desai Government**.


---


# ANOTHER SPECIAL SITUATION


When **Prime Minister dies suddenly** and there is **no obvious successor**.


Example:


1984


Prime Minister **Indira Gandhi assassinated**


President **Zail Singh**


Appointed **Rajiv Gandhi** as Prime Minister.


This ignored the usual practice of appointing a **caretaker Prime Minister first**.


Later:


Congress Parliamentary Party **elected Rajiv Gandhi unanimously**.


---


# WHEN RULING PARTY ELECTS NEW LEADER


If ruling party **elects a new leader after PM's death/resignation**


➡ President **must appoint that person** as Prime Minister.


President has **no discretion**.


---


# MAJORITY PROOF RULE (IMPORTANT CASE)


### Delhi High Court Judgement (1980)


The Constitution **does NOT require majority proof before appointment**.


Process allowed:


1️⃣ President appoints Prime Minister


2️⃣ Then PM proves majority in Lok Sabha within **reasonable period**


---


### Examples of such appointments


| Prime Minister      | Year |
| ------------------- | ---- |
| Charan Singh        | 1979 |
| V. P. Singh         | 1989 |
| Chandra Shekhar     | 1990 |
| P. V. Narasimha Rao | 1991 |
| A. B. Vajpayee      | 1996 |
| H. D. Deve Gowda    | 1996 |
| I. K. Gujral        | 1997 |
| A. B. Vajpayee      | 1998 |


---


# PRIME MINISTER WITHOUT BEING MP


### Supreme Court Judgment (1997)


A person **who is not a member of Parliament can be appointed PM**.


But he must become **member of either House within 6 months**.


Otherwise:


➡ He **ceases to be Prime Minister**.


---


# PRIME MINISTER FROM RAJYA SABHA


The Constitution allows PM to be **member of either House**.


Examples:


| Prime Minister          | House       |
| ----------------------- | ----------- |
| Indira Gandhi (1966)    | Rajya Sabha |
| H. D. Deve Gowda (1996) | Rajya Sabha |
| Manmohan Singh (2004)   | Rajya Sabha |


---


### Comparison with Britain


In **United Kingdom**


Prime Minister **must be member of House of Commons (Lower House).**


India allows **both Houses**.


---

## OATH


Before assuming office:


➡ **President administers oath**.


Two oaths:


1️⃣ Oath of Office


2️⃣ Oath of Secrecy


---


# OATH OF OFFICE (IMPORTANT)


Prime Minister swears:


1️⃣ To bear **true faith and allegiance to the Constitution**


2️⃣ To **uphold sovereignty and integrity of India**


3️⃣ To **faithfully discharge duties**


4️⃣ To **do justice without fear or favour**


---


# OATH OF SECRECY


Prime Minister promises:


➡ Not to reveal **any confidential matter**


Except when required for **official duties**.


---


# TERM OF PRIME MINISTER


The Constitution does **not fix tenure**.


PM holds office:


➡ **During pleasure of President**


But this **does not mean arbitrary removal**.


Actual rule:


✔ PM must enjoy **majority support in Lok Sabha**.


If majority lost:


➡ PM must resign


OR


➡ President may dismiss him.


---


# SALARY AND ALLOWANCES


Determined by **Parliament**.


Prime Minister receives:

- Salary of **Member of Parliament**
- **Sumptuary allowance**
- **Free official residence**
- **Travelling allowance**
- **Medical facilities**

Example:


In **2001**


Sumptuary allowance increased:


₹1500 → ₹3000

# PRIME MINISTER — POWERS AND FUNCTIONS


The **powers and functions of the Prime Minister** can be studied under **four major heads**:

1. **In relation to the Council of Ministers**
2. **In relation to the President**
3. **In relation to the Parliament**
4. **Other powers and functions**

---


# 1. POWERS IN RELATION TO COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


The Prime Minister is the **head of the Union Council of Ministers**.


Therefore he exercises **very significant authority over the ministers**.


Major powers include:


---


## 1. Recommendation of Ministers


The Prime Minister **recommends persons to be appointed as ministers**.


➡ The President appoints ministers **only on the advice of the Prime Minister**.


Thus:


**Prime Minister decides the composition of the Council of Ministers.**


---


## 2. Allocation of Portfolios


The Prime Minister:

- **Allocates portfolios (departments)** to ministers.
- Can **change portfolios anytime**.

This is known as:


**Portfolio allocation and reshuffling.**


Example:

- Finance
- Defence
- Home Affairs
- External Affairs

---


## 3. Power to Remove Ministers


The Prime Minister can:


✔ Ask a minister to **resign**


OR


✔ Advise the President to **dismiss the minister**


This usually happens when:

- Difference of opinion
- Misconduct
- Political crisis

---


## 4. Chairman of Council of Ministers Meetings


The Prime Minister:

- **Presides over meetings** of the Council of Ministers
- **Controls agenda and discussions**
- **Influences decisions**

---


## 5. Coordination Role


The Prime Minister:

- **Guides**
- **Directs**
- **Controls**
- **Coordinates**

the activities of all ministers.


Thus PM ensures **policy unity and administrative coordination**.


---


## 6. Collapse of Council of Ministers


If Prime Minister **resigns**:


➡ Entire **Council of Ministers automatically dissolves**.


Reason:


PM is the **head of the Council of Ministers**.


Therefore ministers **cannot continue without PM**.


---


### Important Concept


If **any other minister resigns**


➡ Only **that minister’s position becomes vacant**


The government **continues normally**.


But if **Prime Minister resigns**


➡ **Entire Council of Ministers collapses**.


---


# 2. POWERS IN RELATION TO THE PRESIDENT


Prime Minister acts as the **principal link between the President and the Council of Ministers.**


---


## 1. Channel of Communication


The Prime Minister communicates to the President:

1. Decisions of the **Council of Ministers**
2. Matters related to **administration of the Union**
3. **Legislative proposals**

---


## 2. Providing Information


The President may ask for information.


Prime Minister must provide information regarding:

- Administration of the Union
- Legislative proposals

---


## 3. Placing Matters Before Council of Ministers


If the President requires:


The Prime Minister must place before the Council of Ministers:


➡ Any decision taken by a minister **individually**


that has **not been considered by the Council of Ministers.**


---


## 4. Advice on Key Appointments


The Prime Minister advises the President on appointment of major officials such as:

- **Attorney General of India**
- **Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG)**
- **Chairman and Members of UPSC**
- **Election Commissioners**
- **Finance Commission Chairman and Members**

---


# 3. POWERS IN RELATION TO PARLIAMENT


The Prime Minister is the **leader of the Lok Sabha (Lower House)**.


Therefore he has major powers in parliamentary functioning.


---


## 1. Summoning and Proroguing Parliament


Prime Minister advises the President regarding:

- **Summoning sessions of Parliament**
- **Proroguing sessions**

---


## 2. Dissolution of Lok Sabha


The Prime Minister can recommend to the President:


➡ **Dissolution of the Lok Sabha**


This can happen:

- Before completion of the **5-year term**

---


## 3. Announcement of Government Policies


Prime Minister:


✔ Announces **government policies in Parliament**


✔ Represents **government position in debates**


---


# 4. OTHER POWERS AND FUNCTIONS


Apart from the above major roles, the Prime Minister performs several additional functions.


---


## 1. Chairman of Important Bodies


Prime Minister is chairman of:

- **Planning Commission** (now replaced by **NITI Aayog**)
- **National Development Council**
- **National Integration Council**
- **Inter-State Council**
- **National Water Resources Council**

---


## 2. Foreign Policy Role


Prime Minister plays a **major role in shaping foreign policy**.


Examples:

- International diplomacy
- Strategic relations
- Global negotiations

---


## 3. Chief Spokesperson of Government


Prime Minister acts as:


➡ **Chief spokesperson of the Union Government**


Explains government policies to:

- Parliament
- Public
- Media

---


## 4. Crisis Manager


During **national crises or emergencies**, Prime Minister acts as:


➡ **Political crisis manager**


Example situations:

- War
- Economic crisis
- National emergency

---


## 5. Leader of the Nation


Prime Minister:

- Meets different sections of society

### “Primus inter pares”

**First among equals**


Explanation:

- All ministers are constitutionally equal.
- But the Prime Minister is **the leader and most powerful among them**.

### “Keystone of the Cabinet Arch”


Meaning:


➡ PM holds the **cabinet structure together**.

# RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN PRESIDENT AND PRIME MINISTER


The Constitution clearly defines the relationship between the two through several Articles.


---


# ARTICLE 74


Article 74 states:


There shall be a **Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head** to **aid and advise the President**.


The President must **act according to this advice**.


However:


The President may ask the Council of Ministers to **reconsider the advice once**.


After reconsideration:


➡ President **must accept the advice**.


---


# ARTICLE 75


This article deals with **appointment and responsibility of ministers**.


Key provisions:


### 1. Appointment

- Prime Minister is appointed by the **President**.
- Other ministers are appointed by the President **on advice of the Prime Minister**.

---


### 2. Pleasure of the President


Ministers hold office **during pleasure of the President**.


But in practice:


➡ Pleasure of the President = **Confidence of Lok Sabha**


---


### 3. Collective Responsibility


Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha**.


Meaning:


If **Lok Sabha passes no-confidence motion**


➡ Entire Council of Ministers must resign.


---


# ARTICLE 78


Article 78 defines the **duties of Prime Minister towards the President**.


PM must:


### 1. Communicate decisions


Communicate all **Cabinet decisions** related to:

- Administration
- Legislation

---


### 2. Provide information


Provide information regarding:

- Administration of Union
- Legislative proposals

---


### 3. Submit matters to Council


If the President requires:


PM must place before the Council of Ministers any decision taken by an individual minister.


---
