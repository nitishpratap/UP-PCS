# 1. CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK


### Key Articles:

- Article 124 → Appointment of Supreme Court judges
- Article 217 → Appointment of High Court judges
- Article 222 → Transfer of High Court judges
- Article 74 → President acts on aid and advice of Council of Ministers

---


## Core Issue:


The Constitution uses the term **“consultation”**, not “concurrence”.


This ambiguity led to judicial interpretation through a series of cases.


---


# 2. FIRST JUDGES CASE (1981)


### Case:


S.P. Gupta vs Union of India


---


## Background:


Conflict between Executive and Judiciary regarding appointments and transfers.


---


## Judgment:

- “Consultation” does not mean “concurrence”
- Executive is not bound by the opinion of the Chief Justice of India

---


## Result:

- Executive primacy in judicial appointments

---


## Exam Line:


First Judges Case established executive dominance in judicial appointments.


---


# 3. SECOND JUDGES CASE (1993)


### Case:


Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association vs Union of India


---


## Significance:


Reversed the First Judges Case.


---


## Judgment:

- “Consultation” means “concurrence”
- Opinion of CJI is binding

---


## Collegium System Introduced:

- CJI + 2 senior-most Supreme Court judges

---


## Result:

- Judicial primacy established

---


## Exam Line:


Second Judges Case created the Collegium system and gave primacy to the judiciary.


---


# 4. THIRD JUDGES CASE (1998)


### Case:


In Re Special Reference 1 of 1998


---


## Background:


President sought clarification under Article 143.


---


## Judgment:


### Collegium Expanded:


### Supreme Court:

- CJI + 4 senior-most judges

### High Court:

- CJI + 2 senior-most judges

---


## Key Rules:

- Decisions taken collectively
- If 2 judges disagree, recommendation fails

---


## Result:


Collegium system formalized and strengthened.


---


# 5. FINAL COLLEGIUM STRUCTURE


---


## Supreme Court Appointments:

- CJI + 4 senior-most judges

---


## High Court Appointments:


### Step 1: High Court Collegium

- Chief Justice of High Court
- 2 senior-most High Court judges

### Step 2: Supreme Court Collegium

- CJI + 2 senior-most Supreme Court judges

---


## Transfer of Judges:

- Initiated by CJI
- Consults 4 senior-most Supreme Court judges

---


## Key Feature:


Judiciary has primacy in appointments.


---


# 6. CRITICISM OF COLLEGIUM


---


## Lack of Transparency:

- No formal selection criteria
- No disclosure of reasons

---


## Nepotism:

- Perception of “judges appointing judges”

---


## Lack of Accountability:

- Not answerable to Parliament or public

---


## Delays:

- Many vacancies in courts

---


## Lack of Diversity:

- Limited representation of women and marginalized groups

---


# 7. NJAC (2014)


---


## Legal Basis:

- 99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014
- NJAC Act, 2014

---


## Composition:

- CJI
- 2 senior-most Supreme Court judges
- Law Minister
- 2 eminent persons

---


## Special Provision:


Any 2 members could veto a recommendation.


---


## Objective:

- Improve transparency
- Increase accountability
- Reduce judicial monopoly

---


# 8. FOURTH JUDGES CASE (2015)


### Case:


Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association vs Union of India


---


## Judgment:

- NJAC declared unconstitutional (4:1 majority)
- Collegium system restored

---


## Key Reason:


### Based on:


Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala


---


## Core Argument:


Judicial independence is part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution.


---


## Why NJAC was invalid:

- Executive involvement through Law Minister
- Inclusion of eminent persons with possible political influence
- Veto provision could block judicial decisions

---


## Conclusion of Court:


Judicial independence must be protected.


---


# 9. POST-2015 POSITION

- Collegium system continues
- Supreme Court suggested reforms through Memorandum of Procedure (MoP)
- Debate on reform versus replacement continues

---


# 10. EVOLUTION SUMMARY


| Year | Case               | Outcome            |
| ---- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| 1981 | First Judges Case  | Executive primacy  |
| 1993 | Second Judges Case | Judicial primacy   |
| 1998 | Third Judges Case  | Collegium expanded |
| 2014 | NJAC introduced    | Reform attempt     |
| 2015 | NJAC Case          | Collegium restored |


---


# 11. PRELIMS FACTS

- Collegium system is not mentioned in the Constitution
- It is created through judicial decisions
- NJAC was struck down in 2015
- Decision based on Basic Structure Doctrine
- Article 143 used in Third Judges Case

---


# 12. MAINS ANSWER STRUCTURE


---


## Introduction:


Judicial appointments in India have evolved through judicial interpretation of constitutional provisions.


---


## Body:

- First Judges Case: Executive dominance
- Second Judges Case: Judicial primacy and Collegium creation
- Third Judges Case: Expansion of Collegium
- NJAC: Attempt to reform
- NJAC struck down: Protection of Basic Structure

---


## Analysis:


### Strengths:

- Ensures judicial independence

### Weaknesses:

- Lack of transparency and accountability

---


## Conclusion:


There is a need to reform the Collegium system to improve transparency while preserving judicial independence.
