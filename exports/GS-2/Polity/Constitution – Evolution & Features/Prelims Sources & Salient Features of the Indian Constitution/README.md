# 1. SOURCES OF THE INDIAN CONSTITUTION


### Core Idea (Mains)


Indian Constitution borrowed features from many constitutions but **adapted them to Indian conditions**.


Dr B.R. Ambedkar said the constitution was framed after **“ransacking all known constitutions of the world.”**


---


## MOST IMPORTANT SOURCE


### Government of India Act 1935


Most provisions of the Constitution came from this Act.


Borrowed features:

- Federal scheme
- Office of Governor
- Public Service Commissions
- Emergency provisions
- Administrative structure
- Division of powers

About **half of the Constitution resembles this Act.**


---


## SOURCES TABLE (PRELIMS FAVORITE)


| Country / Act                | Borrowed Features                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Government of India Act 1935 | Federal scheme, PSC, governors, judiciary, emergency                                |
| Britain                      | Parliamentary system, rule of law, cabinet system, bicameralism, single citizenship |
| USA                          | Fundamental Rights, judicial review, impeachment, Vice-President                    |
| Ireland                      | DPSP, nomination to Rajya Sabha                                                     |
| Canada                       | Federation with strong centre, residuary powers                                     |
| Australia                    | Concurrent list, joint sitting                                                      |
| Germany                      | Suspension of FR during emergency                                                   |
| USSR                         | Fundamental Duties, ideals of justice                                               |
| France                       | Liberty, Equality, Fraternity                                                       |
| South Africa                 | Constitutional amendment procedure                                                  |
| Japan                        | Procedure established by law                                                        |

# 2. SALIENT FEATURES OF THE CONSTITUTION


---


# 2.1 Lengthiest Written Constitution


India has the **longest written constitution in the world**.


### Structure


| Year            | Articles | Parts | Schedules |
| --------------- | -------- | ----- | --------- |
| Original (1950) | 395      | 22    | 8         |
| Present         | ~465–470 | 25    | 12        |


Changes through amendments:

- ~20 Articles deleted
- ~90 Articles added
- 4 Parts added
- 4 Schedules added

---

| Part | Subject               |
| ---- | --------------------- |
| IVA  | Fundamental Duties    |
| IXA  | Municipalities        |
| IXB  | Cooperative Societies |
| XIVA | Tribunals             |


---


## Newly Added Schedules


| Schedule | Amendment      |
| -------- | -------------- |
| 9th      | 1st Amendment  |

| 12th     | 74th Amendment |


---


## Reasons for Large Size

- Vast geographical diversity
- Influence of **GOI Act 1935**
- Single constitution for centre & states
- Detailed administrative provisions
- Drafting dominated by lawyers/jurists

---


# 2.2 Blend of Rigidity & Flexibility


Indian Constitution is **neither rigid nor flexible**.


### Types of Amendments


| Type                                  | Requirement        | Example                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Simple Majority                       | Ordinary law       | Citizenship, state formation  |
| Special Majority                      | Art 368            | Most amendments               |
| Special Majority + State Ratification | Federal provisions | President election, SC powers |


Special majority =

- 2/3 members present & voting
- More than 50% total membership

---


# 2.3 Federal System with Unitary Bias


India = **Federal + strong centre**.


---


### Federal Features

- Two governments
- Written constitution
- Division of powers
- Supremacy of constitution
- Independent judiciary
- Bicameral legislature

---


### Unitary Features

- Strong centre
- Single constitution
- Single citizenship
- Integrated judiciary
- Governor appointed by centre
- All India Services
- Emergency provisions

---


## Article 1


India described as **“Union of States”**


Meaning:

1. Federation not formed by agreement of states
2. States cannot secede

---

# 2.4 Parliamentary System


India adopted **British Parliamentary System**.


Also called:

- Westminster Model
- Cabinet Government
- Responsible Government

---


### Features

- Nominal Executive → President/Governor
- Real Executive → PM/CM
- Collective responsibility
- Majority party rule
- Ministers from legislature
- PM leadership
- Lower House can be dissolved

---


### India vs Britain


| India                    | Britain              |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- |
| Parliament not sovereign | Parliament sovereign |
| Elected head             | Hereditary monarch   |


Political scientists call it **Prime Ministerial Government**.


---


# 2.5 Synthesis of Parliamentary Sovereignty & Judicial Supremacy


| Principle                 | Country |
| ------------------------- | ------- |
| Parliamentary sovereignty | Britain |
| Judicial supremacy        | USA     |


India adopted **middle path**.

- Parliament can amend Constitution
- Supreme Court can review laws

---


### Article 21 Concept


India follows


**Procedure Established by Law**


Instead of


**Due Process of Law (USA)**


---


# 2.6 Integrated & Independent Judiciary


India has **single integrated judiciary**.


### Structure


Supreme Court


↓


High Courts


↓


Subordinate Courts


---


### Functions of Supreme Court

- Federal court
- Highest appellate court
- Guardian of constitution
- Protector of fundamental rights

---


### Safeguards of Judicial Independence

- Security of tenure
- Fixed service conditions
- Salaries from Consolidated Fund
- No discussion on judges in legislature
- Contempt powers
- Separation of judiciary & executive

---


# 2.7 Fundamental Rights


Located in **Part III (Articles 12–35)**.


Originally **7 rights → now 6 rights**.


Reason:


**44th Amendment (1978)** removed **Right to Property**.


Now **Article 300A (legal right)**.


---


### Six Fundamental Rights


| Right                   | Articles |
| ----------------------- | -------- |
| Equality                | 14–18    |
| Freedom                 | 19–22    |
| Against exploitation    | 23–24    |
| Freedom of religion     | 25–28    |
| Cultural & educational  | 29–30    |
| Constitutional remedies | 32       |


---


### Heart & Soul of Constitution


**Article 32**


— Dr B.R. Ambedkar


---


### Writs

- Habeas Corpus
- Mandamus
- Prohibition
- Certiorari
- Quo Warranto

---


### Suspension during Emergency


FR suspended except:

- Article 20
- Article 21

---


# 2.8 Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)


Located in **Part IV (Articles 36–51)**.


Inspired by **Ireland Constitution**.


Aim → **Welfare State**.


Nature:


| Feature                   | Meaning                   |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Non-justiciable           | Not enforceable by courts |
| Fundamental in governance | State must apply them     |


---


### Classification

- Socialistic
- Gandhian
- Liberal-Intellectual

---


### Important Case


**Minerva Mills Case (1980)**


FR + DPSP must remain **balanced**.


---


# 2.9 Fundamental Duties


Located in **Part IVA (Article 51A)**.


Added by:


**42nd Amendment (1976)**


Committee:


**Swaran Singh Committee**.


---


### Additional Duty


**86th Amendment (2002)**


Parents must provide education.


---


### Total Duties


**11 Duties**


Nature:

- Non-justiciable
- Moral obligation

---


# 2.10 Secular State


India is **secular**.


Meaning:


State **treats all religions equally**.


Word **Secular added in Preamble by 42nd Amendment (1976)**.


---

---


# 2.11 Universal Adult Franchise


Every citizen **18+ can vote**.


Originally voting age = **21**.


Reduced by:


**61st Amendment (1988)**.


---


# 2.12 Single Citizenship


India → **single citizenship**


USA → **dual citizenship**


Purpose:

- National unity
- National integration

# BASIC STRUCTURE DOCTRINE


### Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)


Supreme Court held:


Parliament **cannot alter the basic structure of the Constitution** even under **Article 368**.
