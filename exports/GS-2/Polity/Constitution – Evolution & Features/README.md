
## Prelims : Sources & Salient Features of the Indian Constitution

---


# 1. SOURCES OF THE INDIAN CONSTITUTION


### Core Idea (Mains)


Indian Constitution borrowed features from many constitutions but **adapted them to Indian conditions**.


Dr B.R. Ambedkar said the constitution was framed after **“ransacking all known constitutions of the world.”**


---


## MOST IMPORTANT SOURCE


### Government of India Act 1935


Most provisions of the Constitution came from this Act.


Borrowed features:

- Federal scheme
- Office of Governor
- Public Service Commissions
- Emergency provisions
- Administrative structure
- Division of powers

About **half of the Constitution resembles this Act.**


---


## SOURCES TABLE (PRELIMS FAVORITE)


| Country / Act                | Borrowed Features                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Government of India Act 1935 | Federal scheme, PSC, governors, judiciary, emergency                                |
| Britain                      | Parliamentary system, rule of law, cabinet system, bicameralism, single citizenship |
| USA                          | Fundamental Rights, judicial review, impeachment, Vice-President                    |
| Ireland                      | DPSP, nomination to Rajya Sabha                                                     |
| Canada                       | Federation with strong centre, residuary powers                                     |
| Australia                    | Concurrent list, joint sitting                                                      |
| Germany                      | Suspension of FR during emergency                                                   |
| USSR                         | Fundamental Duties, ideals of justice                                               |
| France                       | Liberty, Equality, Fraternity                                                       |
| South Africa                 | Constitutional amendment procedure                                                  |
| Japan                        | Procedure established by law                                                        |

# 2.2 Blend of Rigidity & Flexibility


Indian Constitution is **neither rigid nor flexible**.


### Types of Amendments


| Type                                  | Requirement        | Example                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Simple Majority                       | Ordinary law       | Citizenship, state formation  |

| Special Majority + State Ratification | Federal provisions | President election, SC powers |


Special majority =

- 2/3 members present & voting
- More than 50% total membership

---


# 1. Introduction


The **Indian Constitution** is unique in both **contents and spirit**. Although many provisions are borrowed from other constitutions, it possesses several distinctive features.


Important points:

- Originally adopted in **1949**.
- Many features have changed due to amendments, especially:
    - **7th Amendment (1956)**
    - **42nd Amendment (1976)** – called **Mini Constitution**
    - **44th Amendment (1978)**
    - **73rd Amendment (1992)**
    - **74th Amendment (1992)**
    - **97th Amendment (2011)**

### Basic Structure Doctrine

- In **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**, the Supreme Court held that:

    **Parliament cannot alter the “Basic Structure” of the Constitution even through amendment under Article 368.**

# 7. Synthesis of Parliamentary Sovereignty and Judicial Supremacy


Two constitutional principles influenced the Indian system:


| Principle                 | Country |
| ------------------------- | ------- |
| Parliamentary Sovereignty | Britain |
| Judicial Supremacy        | USA     |


India adopted a **balanced synthesis of both**.


### Parliamentary Sovereignty (British Model)


Parliament is supreme and can make or unmake any law.


### Judicial Supremacy (American Model)


The Supreme Court can declare laws unconstitutional.


### Indian Position


India adopted **a middle path**.

- Parliament can **amend the Constitution**.
- The **Supreme Court can review laws** and declare them unconstitutional.

### Reason for Limited Judicial Review


The Indian Constitution uses:


**“Procedure Established by Law” (Article 21)**


Instead of the American concept:


**“Due Process of Law”**


Thus judicial review in India is **narrower than in the USA**.


---


# 8. Integrated and Independent Judiciary


India has a **single integrated judicial system**.


### Structure of Judiciary


Hierarchy of courts:


1️⃣ Supreme Court


2️⃣ High Courts


3️⃣ District Courts


4️⃣ Subordinate Courts


Unlike the USA:

- In the USA → Federal and State courts are separate.
- In India → **One unified judiciary** enforces both central and state laws.

---


### Role of Supreme Court


The Supreme Court acts as:

- Federal Court
- Highest Court of Appeal
- Guardian of Fundamental Rights
- Guardian of Constitution

---


### Provisions Ensuring Judicial Independence


1️⃣ Security of tenure of judges


2️⃣ Fixed service conditions


3️⃣ Salaries charged on **Consolidated Fund of India**


4️⃣ No discussion on conduct of judges in legislature


5️⃣ Judges cannot practice after retirement


6️⃣ Supreme Court can punish for contempt


7️⃣ Separation of judiciary from executive

# Criticism of the Constitution


---


## 1. Borrowed Constitution


Critics called it:

- Bag of borrowings
- Patchwork constitution

Dr Ambedkar replied:


Borrowing is natural since constitutional ideas are universal.


---


## 2. Carbon Copy of Government of India Act 1935


Many provisions resemble the 1935 Act.


Critics said it is just an amended version of the Act.


Ambedkar defended it saying:


Borrowing administrative provisions was necessary.


---


## 3. Un-Indian or Anti-Indian


Critics argued it ignored Indian traditions.


Example criticism:


K. Hanumanthaiya said:

> Instead of Indian music we hear an English band.

---


## 4. Un-Gandhian Constitution


Critics said:

- Gandhi favoured **village republics**
- Constitution focused on **centralized governance**

---


## 5. Elephantine Size


Critics said the Constitution is **too lengthy and detailed**.


H.V. Kamath compared it with an **elephant**.


---


## 6. Paradise of Lawyers


Sir Ivor Jennings called it:


**“A lawyer’s paradise.”**


Because:

- Complex legal language
- Too many provisions
- Large scope for litigation

# 2. Schedules of the Constitution


There are **12 Schedules** in the Constitution.


---


## First Schedule


Contains:

- Names of **States**
- Names of **Union Territories**
- Their territorial jurisdiction

Articles: **1 and 4**


---


## Second Schedule


Contains provisions relating to:


Salaries and allowances of:

- President
- Governors
- Speaker and Deputy Speaker
- Chairman and Deputy Chairman
- Judges of Supreme Court
- Judges of High Courts
- CAG

---


## Third Schedule


Contains **Forms of Oaths and Affirmations** for:

- Ministers
- MPs
- MLAs
- Judges
- CAG

---


## Fourth Schedule


Deals with:


**Allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha**


Articles: **4 and 80**


---


## Fifth Schedule


Deals with:


Administration and control of **Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes**


Article: **244**


---


## Sixth Schedule


Deals with:


Administration of **Tribal Areas** in:

- Assam
- Meghalaya
- Tripura
- Mizoram

Articles: **244 and 275**


---


## Seventh Schedule


Division of powers between **Centre and States**.


Three lists:


| List            | Subjects     |
| --------------- | ------------ |
| Union List      | 100 subjects |
| State List      | 61 subjects  |
| Concurrent List | 52 subjects  |


Article: **246**

## Ninth Schedule


Added by **1st Amendment (1951)**.


Purpose:


To protect certain laws from **judicial review**.


Mainly land reform laws.


Important case:


**I.R. Coelho Case (2007)**


Laws added after **24 April 1973** can be judicially reviewed.

**102 and 191**
