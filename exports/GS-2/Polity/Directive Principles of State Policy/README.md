# 1. Directive Principles of State Policy – Introduction


### Constitutional Location

- DPSP are mentioned in **Part IV of the Indian Constitution**
- Covered under **Articles 36 – 51**

However:


| Article       | Subject                     |
| ------------- | --------------------------- |
| Article 36    | Definition of State         |
| Article 37    | Nature of DPSP              |
| Article 38–51 | Actual Directive Principles |


---


### Origin of DPSP


The concept was borrowed from:


| Source                        | Details                             |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Irish Constitution (1937)** | Direct inspiration                  |
| **Spanish Constitution**      | Irish Constitution copied from here |


Dr. **B.R. Ambedkar** called DPSP a **“novel feature of the Constitution.”**


Political scientist **Granville Austin** described:

> Fundamental Rights + Directive Principles = **Conscience of the Constitution**

---


# 2. Features of Directive Principles


## 1. Meaning of Directive Principles


The phrase **Directive Principles of State Policy** refers to:

- **Ideals**
- **Guidelines**
- **Instructions**

which the **State must follow while making laws and policies.**


They are **constitutional recommendations** to:

- Legislature
- Executive
- Administrative authorities

---


## Meaning of "State" in DPSP


According to **Article 36**:


The term **State** has the same meaning as in **Part III (Fundamental Rights)**.


Thus it includes:

- Central Government
- State Governments
- Parliament
- State Legislatures
- Local authorities
- Other public authorities

---


## 2. Similar to Instrument of Instructions


DPSP resemble the **Instrument of Instructions** under:


**Government of India Act, 1935**


These instructions were issued to:

- Governor General
- Provincial Governors

Dr B.R. Ambedkar explained:

> DPSP are like the Instrument of Instructions but issued to **Legislature and Executive**.

---


## 3. Aim: Welfare State


DPSP provide a **comprehensive social, economic and political programme** for a democratic state.


Their objective:

- Achieve **Justice**
- Ensure **Liberty**
- Promote **Equality**
- Establish **Fraternity**

These ideals come from the **Preamble of the Constitution**.


---


### Welfare State vs Police State


| Welfare State                       | Police State                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| State works for welfare of citizens | State focuses mainly on law & order |
| Social justice priority             | Minimal state role                  |
| Economic democracy                  | Individualistic state               |


DPSP aim to establish **a Welfare State**.


---


## 4. Non-Justiciable Nature


DPSP are **non-justiciable**.


Meaning:

- They **cannot be enforced by courts**.
- Citizens **cannot approach courts** if DPSP are violated.

However:


### Article 37 says


Directive Principles are:

> **Fundamental in the governance of the country**

Therefore:

- State **must apply them in making laws**.

---


## 5. Role in Judicial Review


Even though DPSP are not enforceable, they help courts.


The **Supreme Court has ruled**:


If a law is made to implement DPSP, the court may consider it **reasonable** under:

- **Article 14 (Equality before law)**
- **Article 19 (Freedom rights)**

Thus DPSP can **protect laws from being declared unconstitutional.**


---


# 3. Classification of Directive Principles


The Constitution **does not classify DPSP**.


However, scholars classify them into **three groups**:


1️⃣ **Socialistic Principles**


2️⃣ **Gandhian Principles**


3️⃣ **Liberal–Intellectual Principles**


---


# 4. Socialistic Principles


These reflect the **ideology of socialism**.


Their objective:

- Establish **economic and social justice**
- Build a **democratic socialist state**
- Reduce **inequality**

---


## Article 38


State must:

- Promote **welfare of people**
- Establish a social order based on:

✔ Social Justice


✔ Economic Justice


✔ Political Justice


Also requires:

- **Minimisation of inequalities** in:
    - income
    - status
    - facilities
    - opportunities

_(Second provision added by_ _**44th Constitutional Amendment 1978**__)_


---


## Article 39


State must secure:


### (a) Adequate Livelihood


All citizens should have **adequate means of livelihood**.


---


### (b) Distribution of Resources


Material resources of the community should be distributed to:


➡ Serve **common good**


---


### (c) Prevention of Wealth Concentration


Economic system must not result in:


➡ **Concentration of wealth**


---


### (d) Equal Pay


State should ensure:


➡ **Equal pay for equal work for men and women**


---


### (e) Protection of Workers


Health and strength of:

- workers
- men
- women
- children

should not be abused.


---


### (f) Protection of Children


Children must receive:

- Opportunities for **healthy development**
- Protection from **exploitation**

_(Modified by_ _**42nd Amendment 1976**__)_


---


## Article 39A


State must:

- Promote **equal justice**
- Provide **free legal aid to the poor**

Added by:


**42nd Constitutional Amendment 1976**


---


## Article 41


State must ensure:

- Right to **work**
- Right to **education**
- Right to **public assistance**

in cases of:

- unemployment
- old age
- sickness
- disability

---


## Article 42


State must provide:

- **Just and humane working conditions**
- **Maternity relief**

---


## Article 43


State must ensure:

- **Living wage for workers**
- **Decent standard of life**
- **Social and cultural opportunities**

### Important Concept


| Type of Wage | Meaning                               |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Minimum Wage | Bare necessities                      |
| Fair Wage    | Between minimum & living wage         |
| Living Wage  | Includes education, health, insurance |


---


## Article 43A


State must ensure:


➡ **Participation of workers in management of industries**


Added by:


**42nd Amendment (1976)**


---


## Article 47


State must:

- Raise **nutrition level**
- Improve **standard of living**
- Improve **public health**

---


# 5. Gandhian Principles


These principles are based on the **ideology of Mahatma Gandhi**.


They represent Gandhi's **programme of reconstruction** during the **Indian national movement**.


The Constitution includes these principles to **fulfil Gandhi’s vision for India**.


---


## Article 40 – Village Panchayats


State shall:

- Organise **village panchayats**
- Give them **necessary powers and authority**

Objective:


➡ Make them **units of self-government**


This reflects Gandhi’s idea of **Gram Swaraj**.


---


## Article 43 – Cottage Industries


State shall:

- Promote **cottage industries**
- On **individual or cooperative basis**
- Particularly in **rural areas**

Purpose:

- Rural employment
- Decentralised economy

---


## Article 43B – Co-operative Societies


State shall promote:

- **Voluntary formation**
- **Autonomous functioning**
- **Democratic control**
- **Professional management**

of **co-operative societies**.


Added by:


**97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011**


---


## Article 46 – Protection of Weaker Sections


State shall promote:

- Educational interests
- Economic interests

of:

- **Scheduled Castes (SCs)**
- **Scheduled Tribes (STs)**
- **Other weaker sections**

Also protects them from:

- **Social injustice**
- **Exploitation**

---


## Article 47 – Prohibition of Intoxicating Drinks


State shall:

- Prohibit consumption of
    - intoxicating drinks
    - drugs harmful to health

---


## Article 48 – Protection of Cattle


State shall:

- Prohibit slaughter of
    - cows
    - calves
    - milch cattle
    - draught cattle
- Improve their breeds.

---


# 6. Liberal–Intellectual Principles


These principles represent the **liberal ideology**.


They focus on:

- modern governance
- intellectual development
- international relations
- scientific progress

---


## Article 44 – Uniform Civil Code (UCC)


State shall attempt to secure:


➡ **Uniform Civil Code for all citizens**


This means:

- Same civil laws for all religions regarding:
    - marriage
    - divorce
    - inheritance
    - adoption

---


## Article 45 – Early Childhood Care


State shall provide:

- **Early childhood care**
- **Education**

for all children **below 6 years of age**


---


### Amendment


Originally:

- Free and compulsory education **up to 14 years**

Changed by:


**86th Amendment Act, 2002**


Now:

- Education (6-14 years) became **Fundamental Right under Article 21A**

Article 45 now focuses on **children below 6 years**.


---


## Article 48 – Agriculture and Animal Husbandry


State shall:

- Organise agriculture on **modern and scientific lines**
- Develop **animal husbandry**

---


## Article 48A – Protection of Environment


State shall:

- Protect environment
- Improve environment
- Safeguard:
    - forests
    - wildlife

Added by:


**42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**


---


## Article 49 – Protection of Monuments


State shall protect:

- Monuments
- Historical places
- Artistic objects

declared of **national importance**.


---


## Article 50 – Separation of Judiciary and Executive


State shall ensure:


➡ Separation of

- Judiciary
- Executive

in **public services of the state**.


Purpose:

- Maintain **judicial independence**.

---


## Article 51 – International Peace


State shall promote:

1. International peace and security
2. Just and honourable relations between nations
3. Respect for international law
4. Settlement of disputes through **arbitration**

# 8. Sanction Behind Directive Principles


The idea was suggested by:


### Sir B. N. Rau


(Constitutional Advisor)


He recommended dividing rights into:


| Category               | Nature                  |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Justiciable rights     | Legally enforceable     |
| Non-justiciable rights | Not legally enforceable |


Thus:


| Part     | Rights               |
| -------- | -------------------- |
| Part III | Fundamental Rights   |
| Part IV  | Directive Principles |


---


## Article 37 – Importance of DPSP


Article 37 states:

> Directive Principles are **fundamental in the governance of the country**.

Thus:

- State has **duty to apply them** in law making.

---


## Real Force Behind DPSP


Although not legally enforceable, they operate through:


➡ **Political pressure and public opinion**


---


### Alladi Krishna Swamy Ayyar stated

> No government responsible to the people can ignore DPSP.

---


### Dr B.R. Ambedkar stated


If a government ignores DPSP:


➡ It will have to answer before the **electorate during elections**.


---


# Why DPSP Were Made Non-Justiciable


Constitution makers had practical reasons:


### 1. Lack of Financial Resources


India did not have sufficient resources to implement them immediately.


---


### 2. Socio-economic Backwardness


India had:

- poverty
- illiteracy
- inequality
- backward infrastructure

---


### 3. Flexibility Needed


Government needed freedom to decide:

- **time**
- **priority**
- **method**

of implementing DPSP.


---


Thus:


Constitution makers relied on:


➡ **Awakened public opinion** rather than courts.


---


# 9. Criticism of Directive Principles


Directive Principles have been criticised by members of the **Constituent Assembly** and constitutional experts on several grounds.


---


# 1. No Legal Force


The most common criticism is that **DPSP are non-justiciable**.


This means:

- Courts **cannot enforce them**
- Citizens **cannot claim them as rights**

### Criticism by Scholars


| Scholar                 | Comment                                                         |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **K.T. Shah**           | Called them **“pious superfluities”**                           |
|                         | Compared them to **a cheque payable when resources permit**     |
| **Nasiruddin**          | Said they are like **New Year resolutions broken on January 2** |
| **T.T. Krishnamachari** | Called them **“a veritable dustbin of sentiments”**             |
| **K.C. Wheare**         | Called them **“manifesto of aims and aspirations”**             |
| **Sir Ivor Jennings**   | Called them **“pious aspirations”**                             |


---


# 2. Illogical Arrangement


Critics argue that DPSP are **not logically organised**.


**N. Srinivasan** said:

- They mix **important issues** with **less important ones**
- They combine **modern ideas** with **traditional ideas**

---


# 3. Conservative Nature


Sir **Ivor Jennings** criticised DPSP saying:

- They are based on **19th century British ideas**
- They reflect **Fabian Socialism**

He remarked:

> “The ghosts of Sydney Webb and Beatrice Webb stalk through the pages.”

He argued they may become **outdated in future**.


---


# 4. Constitutional Conflict


**K. Santhanam** argued DPSP may cause conflicts between authorities.


Possible conflicts:

1. **Centre vs States**
2. **President vs Prime Minister**
3. **Governor vs Chief Minister**

Example:


If Parliament passes a law violating DPSP:

- President could theoretically reject it.

Similarly:

- Centre may direct states to implement DPSP.

---

---

They form the **background for government action**.

# 11. Conflict Between Fundamental Rights and DPSP


Because:

- **Fundamental Rights are justiciable**
- **Directive Principles are non-justiciable**

a constitutional conflict emerged.


---


# Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951)


The Supreme Court ruled:


➡ If there is a conflict:


**Fundamental Rights will prevail over DPSP.**


The Court said:

- DPSP must **conform to Fundamental Rights**.

---


### Result


Parliament enacted amendments:

1. **1st Amendment Act (1951)**
2. **4th Amendment Act (1955)**
3. **17th Amendment Act (1964)**

These amendments were intended to implement some DPSP.


---


# Golaknath Case (1967)


The Supreme Court ruled:


➡ Parliament **cannot amend Fundamental Rights**.


Thus:

- FR became **sacrosanct**.

This prevented implementing some DPSP.


---


# Parliament’s Reaction


Parliament passed:

1. **24th Amendment Act (1971)**
2. **25th Amendment Act (1971)**

---


# Article 31C (Inserted by 25th Amendment)


It stated:


Laws implementing **Article 39(b) and 39(c)** cannot be challenged for violating:

- Article 14 (Equality)
- Article 19 (Freedom)
- Article 31 (Right to property)

It also said courts **cannot question such laws**.


---


# Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)


Supreme Court judgement:

- **Second part of Article 31C invalid**
- Because **Judicial Review is part of Basic Structure**

But:

- First provision of Article 31C **was upheld**.

---


# 42nd Amendment Act (1976)


Extended protection of Article 31C.


Now:


➡ Any law implementing **any Directive Principle** would override Fundamental Rights under Articles:

- 14
- 19
- 31

Thus DPSP were given **supremacy**.


---


# Minerva Mills Case (1980)


Supreme Court struck down the **42nd amendment expansion**.


Court said:

> Constitution is based on **balance between FR and DPSP**.

Important observation:


FR and DPSP are like:

> **Two wheels of a chariot**

Destroying balance would destroy **Basic Structure**.


---


# Present Position


Today:

- **Fundamental Rights have supremacy**
- DPSP still guide government policies.

However:


Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights **if Basic Structure is not destroyed**.


---


# 12. Distinction Between Fundamental Rights and DPSP


| Fundamental Rights                  | Directive Principles                         |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Negative obligations                | Positive obligations                         |
| Justiciable                         | Non-justiciable                              |
| Establish political democracy       | Establish social and economic democracy      |
| Legal sanction                      | Moral and political sanction                 |
| Promote individual welfare          | Promote community welfare                    |
| Automatically enforceable           | Need legislation                             |
| Courts invalidate laws violating FR | Courts cannot invalidate laws violating DPSP |
