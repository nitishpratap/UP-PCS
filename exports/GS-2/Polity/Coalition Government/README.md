
## Meaning of Coalition Government

- The term **Coalition** is derived from the Latin word **“Coalitio”** meaning **“to grow together.”**
- Politically, coalition means an **alliance of distinct political parties**.

### Definition


When several political parties join together to:

- form a government,
- exercise political power,
- and work on the basis of a **common agreed programme/agenda**,

it is called a **Coalition Government**.


---


# Why Coalition Governments are Formed


Coalition governments generally arise when:

- No single political party secures a majority in the legislature.
- Multiple parties combine to cross the majority mark.
- Parties agree on a **common minimum programme (CMP)**.

### Important Point


Coalition politics is mainly a feature of:

- **Multi-party systems**
- Parliamentary democracies

---


# Definitions by Scholars


### According to Robertson


Coalitions arise when:

- no party gets majority,
- and two or more parties combine on a common programme with minimum compromises.

### According to Ogg


Coalition is:

> A cooperative arrangement under which distinct political parties unite to form a government.

### According to N.C. Sahni


Coalition is:

- a phenomenon of multi-party democracy,
- where several minority groups unite,
- sink differences,
- and form majority government.

# Features of Coalition Government


(Important for Prelims + Mains)


## 1. Formed for Reward or Benefit

- Parties join for:
    - political power,
    - policy influence,
    - ministerial positions,
    - electoral gains.

---


## 2. Presence of Minimum Two Parties

- Coalition cannot exist with one party.
- At least two political groups are necessary.

---


## 3. Temporary Alliance

- Coalition is based on:
    - temporary interests,
    - practical adjustments,
    - not permanent ideological unity.

---


## 4. Dynamic in Nature

- Coalition partners may:
    - join,
    - withdraw,
    - split,
    - or realign.

Thus coalition politics is highly fluid and changing.


---


## 5. Based on Compromise

- Rigid ideology is diluted.
- Partners adjust their agendas for survival of government.

### Key Theme:

> “Compromise is the soul of coalition politics.”

---


## 6. Works on Common Minimum Programme (CMP)

- Policies are based on minimum agreed agenda.
- Coalition policies may not fully satisfy all parties.

---


## 7. Pragmatism over Ideology

- Practicality becomes more important than ideology.
- Political adjustments dominate governance.

---


## 8. Main Objective: Capture Power

- Coalition adjustments are often aimed at forming government and retaining power.

---


# Types of Coalition


## 1. Pre-Poll Coalition


Alliance formed before elections.


### Advantages

- Common manifesto
- Joint campaigning
- Clear alternative before voters

---


## 2. Post-Poll Coalition


Alliance formed after elections.


### Purpose

- Sharing political power
- Achieving majority after fractured mandate

---

- 1952
- 1957
- 1962
- 1967

Congress obtained majority and formed government alone.


---


## Important Events


### 1969 Congress Split


Despite split:

- Indira Gandhi continued with outside support of:
    - CPI
    - DMK
    - other parties.

---


## 1971 Election

- Congress again formed single-party government.

---


## Beginning of Coalition Era

- In **1977**, Congress was defeated badly.
- Since then coalition politics became common at Centre.

# Merits of Coalition Government


# 1. Accommodation of Diverse Interests

- Different social, cultural and regional interests get representation.

---


# 2. More Representative Government


India is:

- multilingual,
- multicultural,
- multi-religious,
- multi-caste society.

Coalition governments better reflect:

- public opinion,
- regional aspirations,
- diversity of India.

---


# 3. Consensus-Based Politics


Policies require agreement among partners.


Thus:

- discussion,
- negotiation,
- consensus become important.

---


# 4. Strengthens Federalism


Coalition governments are:

- more sensitive to states,
- more responsive to regional demands.

Hence coalition politics strengthens:


## Cooperative Federalism


---


# 5. Prevents Authoritarianism

- Reduces domination of one party.
- Decision-making becomes balanced.
- Prevents despotic tendencies.

---


# Demerits of Coalition Government


# 1. Political Instability

- Internal conflicts may collapse government.
- Withdrawal of support can topple ministry.

### Example

- Several coalition governments in 1990s fell early.

---


# 2. Weakening of Prime Minister’s Authority


Prime Minister must:

- consult coalition partners,
- negotiate continuously.

### Important Term


Critics call coalition leaders:

- “Super Prime Ministers”
- “Ultra Prime Ministers”

because coalition partners influence decisions heavily.


---


# 3. Rise of ‘Super Cabinet’


Coordination/Steering Committees of coalition partners:

- sometimes overshadow Cabinet.

Thus:

- Cabinet authority weakens.

---


# 4. Small Parties Become Kingmakers


Small regional parties may:

- demand extra ministries,
- pressure government,
- gain disproportionate influence.

### Important Term


## “Kingmaker”


---


# 5. Regionalism in National Politics


Regional parties may:

- pressure central government,
- threaten withdrawal,
- seek regional concessions.

---


# 6. Jumbo Council of Ministers


Large ministries are formed to satisfy coalition partners.


### Example


Vajpayee ministry (1999):

- 70+ ministers,
- called “Jumbo Ministry.”

### Problems

- Portfolio distribution issues
- Coordination difficulties

---


# 7. Weak Collective Responsibility


Coalition partners often:

- blame each other,
- avoid accountability,
- escape responsibility for failures.

This weakens:

- Collective Responsibility,
- Individual Responsibility.
