# PREAMBLE OF THE CONSTITUTION


## 1. Meaning of Preamble

- The **Preamble** is the **introduction or preface of the Constitution**.
- It contains the **summary and essence of the Constitution**.
- It explains the **philosophy and objectives** of the Constitution.

### Important Quote

- **N.A. Palkhivala** called the Preamble the **“Identity Card of the Constitution.”**

### Global Origin

- The **USA Constitution** was the first constitution to start with a Preamble.
- Many countries including **India adopted this practice**.

---


# 2. Source of the Indian Preamble


The Preamble of India is based on:


**Objectives Resolution**

- Drafted and moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- Presented on **13 December 1946**
- Adopted by the Constituent Assembly on **22 January 1947**

This resolution laid down the **philosophy and guiding principles of the Constitution**.


---


# 3. Amendment to the Preamble


The Preamble has been amended **only once**.


### 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976


Added three words:


| Added Word | Location                         |
| ---------- | -------------------------------- |
| Socialist  | Between Sovereign and Secular    |
| Secular    | Between Socialist and Democratic |
| Integrity  | After Unity                      |


Thus the description became:


**Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic**


---


# 4. Text of the Preamble


The present Preamble reads:

> “We, the people of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic** and to secure to all its citizens:
>
> **Justice** — social, economic and political
>
>
> **Liberty** — of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship
>
>
> **Equality** — of status and opportunity
>
>
> and to promote among them all **Fraternity** assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation
>
>
> In our Constituent Assembly this **26th day of November 1949** do hereby adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution.”
>
>

# 7. Sovereign


### Meaning


The word **Sovereign** means:

- India is **independent**
- It is **not a colony or dependency of any other country**
- No authority is above India.

Thus India has **complete freedom in internal and external affairs**.


### Important Facts

- India became **sovereign republic on 26 January 1950**.
- Earlier political status:

| Period      | Status                   |
| ----------- | ------------------------ |
| Before 1947 | Colony of British Empire |
| 1947–1950   | Dominion                 |
| After 1950  | Sovereign Republic       |


### Sovereignty and International Organisations


India remains sovereign despite:

- Membership of **Commonwealth of Nations**
- Membership of **United Nations (UNO)**

These memberships **do not limit India’s sovereignty**.


### Power of a Sovereign State


India can:

- Acquire foreign territory
- Cede territory to another state

---


# 8. Socialist


The term **Socialist** was added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**.


However, socialist principles already existed in the Constitution through **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)**.


### Meaning of Indian Socialism


India follows **Democratic Socialism**, not **Communist Socialism**.


| Democratic Socialism            | Communist Socialism             |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Mixed economy                   | State ownership of all property |
| Both public and private sectors | Only state control              |
| Democratic political system     | Authoritarian system            |


India’s socialism is considered a **blend of Marxism and Gandhism**, leaning more toward **Gandhian socialism**.


### Goals of Indian Socialism

- End poverty
- Remove inequality
- Remove ignorance and disease
- Provide equal opportunities

### Impact of 1991 Economic Reforms


The policies of:

- Liberalisation
- Privatisation
- Globalisation

have **somewhat diluted the socialist character of the Indian state**.


---


# 9. Secular


The word **Secular** was also added by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976**.


However, secular principles were already present in the Constitution through:


**Articles 25–28 (Freedom of Religion)**.


### Meaning of Secularism in India


Indian secularism means:


**Equal respect and protection for all religions by the state.**


The state:

- Does not have an official religion
- Treats all religions equally

This is called **Positive Secularism**.


### Types of State Based on Religion


| Type             | Meaning                 | Example               |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| Atheistic State  | Opposes religion        | Some communist states |
| Theocratic State | Official state religion | Pakistan, Bangladesh  |
| Secular State    | Neutral to religion     | India, USA            |


---

# 11. Republic

| Type         | Description                              |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Monarchy** | Head of state is hereditary (King/Queen) |
| **Republic** | Head of state is elected                 |


### Meaning of Republic


The word **Republic** in the Preamble means:

- The **head of the state is elected**, not hereditary.
- In India the head of state is the **President**.

### President of India

# 17. Is Preamble Part of the Constitution?


This issue was decided by the **Supreme Court**.


---


## Berubari Union Case (1960)


Supreme Court held:

- Preamble is **not part of the Constitution**
- It is only **a key to understand the Constitution**.

---


## Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)


Supreme Court reversed earlier judgement.


It held:

- **Preamble is part of the Constitution**
- Constitution must be interpreted **in light of the Preamble**.

---


## LIC of India Case (1995)


Supreme Court again confirmed:


**Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution.**


---


# 18. Important Points About the Preamble

1. Preamble **is part of the Constitution**.
2. It is **not a source of power** for legislature.
3. It **does not impose limitations** on legislative powers.
4. It is **non-justiciable**.

Meaning:


Its provisions **cannot be enforced in court**.


---


# 19. Amendability of the Preamble


The question arose in **Kesavananda Bharati case (1973).**


### Supreme Court Judgment

- Preamble **can be amended under Article 368**.
- However, **basic structure cannot be altered**.

---


### Basic Structure Principle


Parliament **cannot destroy basic features** of the Constitution.


Examples:

- Sovereignty
- Democracy
- Secularism
- Republic

---


### Amendment of Preamble


Only one amendment so far:


**42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**

## 1. Why Preamble Was Placed at the End


Although it appears **at the beginning of the Constitution**, it was **adopted last**.


Reason:


The **Constituent Assembly wanted to ensure that the Preamble matches the Constitution adopted.**


While presenting it, the **President of the Constituent Assembly said:**

> “The question is that the Preamble stands part of the Constitution.”

Then the motion was **approved by the Assembly**.


This supports the later Supreme Court view that **Preamble is part of the Constitution**.


---


# 2. Two Important Legal Points About the Preamble


The Supreme Court clarified two things:


### 1. Preamble is **not a source of power**


It **does not give powers** to:

- Parliament
- Legislature
- Government

### 2. Preamble is **not a limitation on powers**


It **does not restrict powers of legislature**.


It only **guides interpretation** of the Constitution.


---


# 3. Non-Justiciable Nature


The Preamble is **non-justiciable**.


Meaning:

- Citizens **cannot go to court** to enforce Preamble provisions.

Example:


You cannot file a case saying:

> "Equality mentioned in Preamble is violated"

Instead you must rely on **Fundamental Rights articles**.


---


# 4. Important Historical Facts


### Membership of UNO


India became a member of **United Nations in 1945**.


This **does not reduce India’s sovereignty**.


---


### Commonwealth Membership


India remained a member of the **Commonwealth of Nations**, but this:

- does **not affect sovereignty**
- is a **voluntary association**.

Pandit Nehru explained:

> Membership is based on free will and can be terminated anytime.
