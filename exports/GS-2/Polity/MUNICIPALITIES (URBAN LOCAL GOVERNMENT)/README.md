# 1. Urban Local Government – Meaning


Urban Local Government means **governance of urban areas by elected representatives of the people**.


Key Points:

- It administers **urban areas**.
- The jurisdiction is **limited to a demarcated urban area** defined by the **State Government**.
- It is a form of **local self-government at the urban level**.

### Constitutional Position

- **Local Government** is included in the **State List (7th Schedule)**.
- Therefore **states have primary responsibility** for urban local bodies.

---


# 2. Types of Urban Local Governments in India


There are **8 types of Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)**.

1. Municipal Corporation
2. Municipality
3. Notified Area Committee
4. Town Area Committee
5. Cantonment Board
6. Township
7. Port Trust
8. Special Purpose Agency

---


# 3. Ministries Handling Urban Local Government


At the **Central Level**, urban local government is handled by:


### 1. Ministry of Urban Development

- Created as a **separate ministry in 1985**.

### 2. Ministry of Defence

- Responsible for **Cantonment Boards**.

### 3. Ministry of Home Affairs

- Responsible for **Union Territories**.

---


# 4. Evolution of Urban Local Bodies in India (History)


Urban local government developed mainly during **British Rule**.


### Important Historical Events


### 1. 1687–88

- **First Municipal Corporation in India**
- Established at **Madras**.

### 2. 1726


Municipal corporations established at:

- **Bombay**
- **Calcutta**

### 3. 1870 – Lord Mayo’s Resolution


Focus:

- **Financial decentralisation**
- Development of **local self-government institutions**

### 4. 1882 – Lord Ripon’s Resolution


Very important for exams.


Key points:

- Called **Magna Carta of Local Self-Government**
- Lord Ripon is known as:

👉 **Father of Local Self Government in India**


Major objective:

- Development of **local self-government institutions**

### 5. 1907 – Royal Commission on Decentralisation

- Chairman: **Hobhouse**
- Submitted report in **1909**

### 6. Government of India Act 1919

- Introduced **Dyarchy in Provinces**
- **Local Self Government became a transferred subject**
- Administered by **Indian Ministers**

### 7. Cantonments Act 1924

- Passed by **Central Legislature**
- Regulated **Cantonment Boards**

### 8. Government of India Act 1935

- Introduced **Provincial Autonomy**
- **Local self-government became a Provincial Subject**

# 7. 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992


This amendment **constitutionalised urban local government in India**.


### Important Constitutional Changes

1. Added **Part IX-A** to the Constitution
2. Title: **The Municipalities**
3. Articles: **243P – 243ZG**
4. Added **Twelfth Schedule**
5. Twelfth Schedule contains **18 subjects of municipalities**

Purpose:

- Strengthen **urban local government**
- Ensure **effective functioning**
- Provide **constitutional protection**

Key Impact:

- States are **constitutionally obligated** to create municipalities according to the Act.

---


# 8. Objectives of the 74th Amendment


The amendment aimed to:

1. Strengthen **urban local governments**
2. Provide **democratic decentralisation**
3. Improve **urban governance**
4. Promote **local planning and development**
5. Ensure **participation of people in urban governance**

# 1. Types of Municipalities


The **74th Constitutional Amendment Act (1992)** provides for **three types of municipalities in every state**.


### 1. Nagar Panchayat


Created for a **transitional area**.


Meaning:

- Area **in transition from rural to urban**

Example:

- Growing villages becoming towns

---


### 2. Municipal Council


Created for a **smaller urban area**.


Examples:

- Medium towns

---


### 3. Municipal Corporation


Created for a **larger urban area**.


Examples:

- Big cities such as

    Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bengaluru etc.


---


### Determination of Urban Area


The **Governor of the State** decides whether an area will be:

- Transitional
- Smaller urban
- Larger urban

Based on factors like:

1. Population
2. Population density
3. Revenue generated for local administration
4. Percentage of non-agricultural employment
5. Economic importance
6. Other factors considered appropriate

---


# 2. Composition of Municipalities


### Election of Members

- All members of a municipality are **directly elected by the people**.

### Electoral Units

- The municipal area is divided into **territorial constituencies called wards**.

People elect **ward councillors**.


---


### Election of Chairperson


The **State Legislature** determines:

- Method of election of **Chairperson/Mayor**

---


### Representation of Other Persons


The State Legislature may allow representation of:


### 1. Experts in Municipal Administration

- Persons having **special knowledge or experience**
- **No voting rights**

---


### 2. Members of Lok Sabha


If their constituency includes the municipal area.


---


### 3. Members of State Legislative Assembly


If their constituency includes the municipal area.


---


### 4. Members of Rajya Sabha and Legislative Council


If they are **registered voters in the municipal area**


---


### 5. Chairpersons of Committees


(except Ward Committees)


---


# 3. Ward Committees


Ward Committees exist in **large cities**.


### Condition


Ward Committees must be created in municipalities having:


👉 **Population ≥ 3 lakh**


---


### Composition


Determined by **State Legislature**.


The state decides:

1. Composition of the committee
2. Territorial area
3. Method of filling seats

---


States can also create **additional committees besides ward committees**.


Purpose:

- Improve **local participation**
- Better **urban governance**

---


# 4. Reservation of Seats


The Constitution provides **mandatory reservation**.


### Reservation for SC and ST


Seats reserved **in proportion to their population in the municipal area**.


---


### Reservation for Women


At least:


👉 **1/3rd of total seats**


Includes:

- Seats reserved for **SC/ST women**

---


### Reservation for Chairpersons


State Legislature may reserve **Chairperson posts for:**

- SC
- ST
- Women

---


### Reservation for Backward Classes


States may also provide reservation for:


👉 **Backward Classes (OBCs)**


---


# 5. Duration of Municipalities


The term of every municipality is:


👉 **5 Years**


---


### Early Dissolution


Municipality may be **dissolved before 5 years**.


---


### Elections After Dissolution


Fresh elections must be held:


1️⃣ **Before expiry of 5 years**


OR


2️⃣ **Within 6 months after dissolution**


---


### Exception


If the remaining term is **less than 6 months**, elections are **not required**.


---


### Reconstituted Municipality


If a municipality is dissolved early and reconstituted:

- It **does not get a fresh 5-year term**

Instead it continues only for:


👉 **Remaining period of the original term**


---


# 6. Disqualification for Membership


A person can be disqualified if:


### 1. Disqualified under State Legislative Election Laws


OR


### 2. Disqualified under a law made by the State Legislature


---


### Age Condition


Normally minimum age = **25 years**


However:


A person **cannot be disqualified only because he is under 25 years if he has completed 21 years**.


Thus:


👉 Minimum age for municipality membership = **21 years**


---


### Authority for Disqualification


Questions regarding disqualification are decided by:


👉 Authority determined by the **State Legislature**


---


# 7. State Election Commission (SEC)


The **State Election Commission** conducts elections to municipalities.


### Powers of SEC


It has control over:

1. Preparation of **electoral rolls**
2. Conduct of **municipal elections**

---


### Legal Provision


State Legislature can make laws regarding:

- Election procedures
- Electoral matters

---


### Importance


Ensures:

- **Free and fair municipal elections**
- Independence from state government

# 1. Powers and Functions of Municipalities


The **State Legislature** determines the powers and authority of municipalities.


Purpose:

- Enable them to function as **institutions of self-government**.

The powers may include:


### 1. Preparation of Plans


Municipalities prepare plans for:

- **Economic development**
- **Social justice**

---


### 2. Implementation of Schemes


Municipalities implement schemes relating to:

- Economic development
- Social justice

These schemes relate to the **18 subjects listed in the Twelfth Schedule**.


---


# 2. Municipal Finances


The **State Legislature** determines financial powers of municipalities.


It may authorize municipalities to:


### 1. Levy Taxes


Municipalities may levy and collect:

- Taxes
- Duties
- Tolls
- Fees

---


### 2. Assignment of Taxes


State Government may assign certain taxes to municipalities.


---


### 3. Grants-in-Aid


Municipalities receive **grants from the Consolidated Fund of the State**.


---


### 4. Municipal Funds


State Legislature may create **municipal funds** for:

- Depositing municipal revenue
- Managing expenditure

---


# 3. Finance Commission (for Municipalities)


The **State Finance Commission**, which is constituted for Panchayats, also reviews **municipal finances every five years**.


### Role


It reviews the **financial position of municipalities**.


---


### Recommendations to the Governor


The Commission recommends:


### 1. Principles of Revenue Distribution


Between:

- State Government
- Municipalities

Includes revenue from:

- Taxes
- Duties
- Tolls
- Fees

---


### 2. Taxes Assigned to Municipalities


Which taxes should be **assigned to municipalities**.


---


### 3. Grants-in-Aid


Grants to municipalities from the **Consolidated Fund of the State**.


---


### 4. Measures to Improve Finances


Suggestions to **improve municipal financial health**.


---


### 5. Other Financial Matters


Any matter referred by the **Governor**.


---


### Presentation to State Legislature


The Governor must place before the **State Legislature**:

- Finance Commission recommendations
- Action taken report

---


### Role of Central Finance Commission


The **Central Finance Commission** also suggests measures to:


👉 Increase resources of states to support municipalities.


---


# 4. Audit of Municipal Accounts


The **State Legislature** can make laws regarding:

- Maintenance of municipal accounts
- Auditing of accounts

Purpose:

- Ensure **financial accountability**
- Prevent misuse of funds

---


# 5. Application to Union Territories


The **President of India** may apply provisions of the 74th Amendment to:


👉 **Union Territories**


However:


He may apply them with **modifications or exceptions**.


---


# 6. Exempted Areas


The provisions of the **74th Constitutional Amendment** do **not apply** to:


### 1. Scheduled Areas


### 2. Tribal Areas


These areas are governed under special provisions of the Constitution.


---


### Additional Exception


The Act does not affect the powers of:


👉 **Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council (West Bengal)**

# 7. District Planning Committee (DPC)


Every state must establish a **District Planning Committee**.


### Purpose


To prepare a **district development plan**.


---


### Functions

1. Consolidate plans prepared by:
    - Panchayats
    - Municipalities
2. Prepare a **draft development plan for the district**.

---


### Composition


State Legislature decides:

- Composition
- Election method
- Functions
- Election of chairperson

---


### Representation Rule


At least:


👉 **4/5 of members must be elected**


They are elected by:

- Members of district panchayats
- Members of municipalities

---


### Representation Formula


Members are chosen **in proportion to:**


👉 Ratio of **rural and urban population in the district**


---


### Role of Chairperson


The Chairperson forwards the **development plan to the State Government**.


---


### Factors Considered in Planning


While preparing the plan, DPC must consider:

1. Common interests of Panchayats and Municipalities

Examples:

- Spatial planning
- Water resource sharing
- Infrastructure development
- Environmental conservation

---

1. Available resources

Includes:

- Financial resources
- Other resources

---

1. Consultation

DPC consults institutions and organizations specified by the **Governor**.


---


# 8. Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC)


Every **metropolitan area** must have a Metropolitan Planning Committee.


### Metropolitan Area


Defined as an area with:


👉 **Population of 10 lakh or more**


Consisting of:

- Two or more municipalities
- Panchayats
- Other contiguous areas

---


### Functions of MPC


To prepare a **draft development plan for the metropolitan area**.


---


### Composition


State Legislature decides:

1. Composition
2. Election method
3. Representation of:
    - Central Government
    - State Government
    - Other organisations
4. Functions
5. Election of chairperson

---


### Representation Rule


At least:


👉 **2/3 of members must be elected**


They are elected by:

- Municipal members
- Panchayat chairpersons

---


### Representation Formula


Members are chosen in proportion to:


👉 Population of municipalities and panchayats in the metropolitan area.


---


### Role of Chairperson


The Chairperson forwards the **development plan to the State Government**.


---


### Factors Considered in Planning


While preparing the development plan, MPC must consider:

1. Plans prepared by municipalities and panchayats
2. Shared interests such as:
    - Spatial planning
    - Resource sharing
    - Infrastructure development
    - Environmental conservation
3. Objectives of Central and State governments
4. Likely investments by government agencies
5. Available financial resources
6. Consultation with institutions specified by the Governor

# Types of Urban Local Governments in India


India has **eight types of urban local bodies** for urban administration.

1. Municipal Corporation
2. Municipality
3. Notified Area Committee
4. Town Area Committee
5. Cantonment Board
6. Township
7. Port Trust
8. Special Purpose Agency

---


# 1. Municipal Corporation


Municipal Corporations are established for **large cities**.


Examples:

- Delhi
- Mumbai
- Kolkata
- Hyderabad
- Bengaluru

---


## Establishment


Municipal corporations are created:

- In **States** → by **State Legislature Acts**
- In **Union Territories** → by **Acts of Parliament**

States may have:

- One common act for all corporations
OR
- Separate act for each corporation

---


## Authorities of Municipal Corporation


A municipal corporation has **three authorities**:


### 1. Council


### 2. Standing Committees


### 3. Municipal Commissioner


---


## 1. Council


The **Council** is the:


👉 **Legislative and deliberative wing**


Composition:

- Councillors elected by people
- Some nominated members with expertise

Reservation of seats for:

- SC
- ST
- Women

These follow provisions of the **74th Amendment**.


---


## Mayor


The council is headed by the:


👉 **Mayor**


Assisted by:


👉 **Deputy Mayor**


Features:

- Usually elected for **1-year renewable term**
- Mainly an **ornamental and ceremonial head**

Functions:

- Presides over council meetings

---


## 2. Standing Committees


Created because the council is **too large**.


They deal with areas like:

- Public works
- Education
- Health
- Taxation
- Finance

They take **decisions within their domain**.


---


## 3. Municipal Commissioner


The **Municipal Commissioner** is the:


👉 **Chief Executive Authority**


Responsibilities:

- Implements decisions of:
    - Council
    - Standing committees

Appointment:

- Appointed by **State Government**
- Usually an **IAS officer**

---


# 2. Municipality


Municipalities govern **smaller towns and cities**.


Also known as:

- Municipal Council
- Municipal Committee
- Municipal Board
- Borough Municipality
- City Municipality

---


## Establishment


Same as municipal corporations:

- States → State legislature acts
- UTs → Parliament acts

---


## Authorities of Municipality


Like corporations, municipalities also have:

1. Council
2. Standing Committees
3. Chief Executive Officer

---


## Council


The council is the **legislative wing**.


Members:

- Councillors directly elected by people.

---


## President / Chairman


The council is headed by:


👉 **President or Chairman**


Assisted by:


👉 Vice-President / Vice-Chairman


Unlike mayor, the chairman:

- Plays a **significant executive role**
- Is the **pivot of municipal administration**

---


## Standing Committees


Handle subjects like:

- Public works
- Taxation
- Health
- Finance

---


## Chief Executive Officer


Responsible for:


👉 **Day-to-day administration**


Appointment:

- By **State Government**

---


# 3. Notified Area Committee


Created for two types of areas:

1. Fast-developing towns due to **industrialisation**
2. Towns **not yet eligible to become municipalities**

---


## Establishment


Created through:


👉 **Government Gazette Notification**


Hence called **Notified Area Committee**.


---


## Nature

- Functions within **State Municipal Act**
- Only certain provisions apply.

---


## Composition


All members including the chairman are:


👉 **Nominated by the State Government**


Thus:

- Not an elected body
- Not a statutory body

---


# 4. Town Area Committee


Created for **small towns**.


It is a:


👉 **Semi-municipal authority**


---


## Functions


Limited civic functions like:

- Drainage
- Roads
- Street lighting
- Conservancy

---


## Creation


Created by:


👉 **Separate State Legislature Act**


---


## Composition


May be:

- Fully elected
- Fully nominated
- Partly elected and partly nominated

---


# 5. Cantonment Board


Created for **civilian population in cantonment areas**.


A cantonment area is where:


👉 **Military forces are permanently stationed**


---


## Law


Established under:


👉 **Cantonments Act 2006**


This Act replaced:


👉 **Cantonments Act 1924**


---


## Administrative Control


Unlike other urban bodies:


Cantonment Boards are controlled by:


👉 **Ministry of Defence (Central Government)**


---


## Number


There were **62 cantonment boards** in India (as per data in text).


---


## Classification of Cantonment Boards


| Category     | Civil Population |
| ------------ | ---------------- |
| Category I   | Above 50,000     |
| Category II  | 10,000 – 50,000  |
| Category III | 2,500 – 10,000   |
| Category IV  | Below 2,500      |


---


## Composition


Members are:

- Partly elected
- Partly nominated

Term:

- Elected members → **5 years**

---


## President of Board


The president is:


👉 **Military officer commanding the station**


---


## Vice-President

- Elected by elected members
- Term = **5 years**

---


## Executive Officer


Appointed by:


👉 **President of India**


Responsible for:

- Implementing board decisions

---


# 6. Township


Townships are created by:


👉 **Large Public Sector Enterprises**


Purpose:

- Provide civic amenities to employees.

Example:


Townships near:

- Steel plants
- Power plants

---


## Administration


The enterprise appoints:


👉 **Town Administrator**


He is assisted by:

- Engineers
- Technical staff

---


## Nature

- No elected members
- Part of **bureaucratic structure**

---


# 7. Port Trust


Created in **port cities** such as:

- Mumbai
- Kolkata
- Chennai

---


## Objectives

1. Manage and protect ports
2. Provide civic amenities

---


## Creation


Created by:


👉 **Act of Parliament**


---


## Composition


Members are:

- Elected
- Nominated

Chairman is:


👉 **Government official**


---


# 8. Special Purpose Agencies


These are **function-based urban agencies**.


Also called:

- Single purpose agencies
- Uni-purpose agencies
- Functional local bodies

---


## Examples

1. Town Improvement Trusts
2. Urban Development Authorities
3. Water Supply and Sewerage Boards
4. Housing Boards
5. Pollution Control Boards
6. Electricity Supply Boards
7. City Transport Boards

---


## Nature


These agencies:

- Are **statutory bodies**
- Function **independently of municipalities**

Thus they are **not subordinate to municipal bodies**.


---


# Municipal Personnel Systems


There are **three types of personnel systems** in municipalities.


---


## 1. Separate Personnel System


Each municipality:

- Appoints its own staff
- Controls its own staff

Employees:

- Cannot be transferred to other municipalities

This is the **most common system**.


Advantages:

- Promotes **local autonomy**
- Ensures **loyalty to local body**

---


## 2. Unified Personnel System


Under this system:


👉 State Government controls municipal personnel.


Features:

- State-wide municipal service cadre
- Employees transferable between municipalities

States using this system include:

- Andhra Pradesh
- Tamil Nadu
- Uttar Pradesh
- Rajasthan
- Madhya Pradesh

---


## 3. Integrated Personnel System


Under this system:


👉 Municipal personnel are part of **state civil services**.


Thus employees can be transferred between:

- Municipalities
- State government departments

States using this system include:

- Odisha
- Bihar
- Karnataka
- Punjab
- Haryana

---

# Municipal Revenue


Urban local bodies have **five sources of income**.


---


# 1. Tax Revenue


Major taxes include:

- Property tax
- Entertainment tax
- Advertisement tax
- Professional tax
- Water tax
- Animal tax
- Lighting tax
- Pilgrim tax
- Market tax
- Toll on bridges
- Octroi (abolished in many states)

Most important tax:


👉 **Property Tax**


Municipal bodies also impose **cesses**, such as:

- Library cess
- Education cess
- Beggary cess

---


# 2. Non-Tax Revenue


Includes:

- Rent on municipal properties
- Fees
- Fines
- Royalties
- Interest
- Profits and dividends
- User charges

Examples of user charges:

- Water charges
- Sewerage charges
- Sanitation charges

---


# 3. Grants


Given by:

- Central Government
- State Governments

Purpose:

- Development programs
- Infrastructure projects

---


# 4. Devolution


Funds transferred by **State Government** based on:


👉 **State Finance Commission recommendations**


---


# 5. Loans


Municipalities can borrow from:

- State government
- Financial institutions

But only with **state government approval**.

👉 **Central Council of Local Self-Government**

👉 **Central Council of Local Government**

Advisory body on **local government matters**.

## Composition


Members include:

---

1. Consider policy matters
2. Recommend legislation

5. Recommend central financial assistance
6. Review work done by local bodies
