# EVOLUTION OF PANCHAYATI RAJ


## BALWANT RAI MEHTA COMMITTEE (1957)


| Aspect           | Details                                 |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Appointed        | January 1957                            |
| Purpose          | Examine **CDP (1952)** & **NES (1953)** |
| Report Submitted | November 1957                           |
| Chairman         | **Balwant Rai G. Mehta**                |
| Key Idea         | **Democratic Decentralisation**         |


## Major Recommendations


### 3-Tier Structure


| Level    | Institution      |
| -------- | ---------------- |
| Village  | Gram Panchayat   |
| Block    | Panchayat Samiti |
| District | Zila Parishad    |


### Other Recommendations

- Gram Panchayat → **Direct election**
- Panchayat Samiti & Zila Parishad → **Indirect election**
- Panchayat Samiti → **Executive body**
- Zila Parishad → **Advisory + supervisory body**
- District Collector → Chairman of Zila Parishad
- Transfer planning & development functions
- Adequate financial resources
- Genuine devolution of powers

## Implementation


| Aspect         | Details                             |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Accepted by    | National Development Council (1958) |
| First State    | Rajasthan                           |
| Launch Date    | 2 October 1959                      |
| Place          | Nagaur                              |
| Inaugurated by | Jawaharlal Nehru                    |
| Second State   | Andhra Pradesh                      |


## State Variations


| State       | Structure |
| ----------- | --------- |
| Rajasthan   | 3-tier    |
| Tamil Nadu  | 2-tier    |
| West Bengal | 4-tier    |


### Powerful Institution


| Model                      | Dominant Institution |
| -------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Rajasthan & Andhra Pradesh | Panchayat Samiti     |
| Maharashtra & Gujarat      | Zila Parishad        |


### Nyaya Panchayats

- Minor civil & criminal disputes settlement.

# ASHOK MEHTA COMMITTEE (1977)


| Aspect          | Details               |
| --------------- | --------------------- |
| Appointed       | December 1977         |
| Government      | Janata Government     |

| Recommendations | 132                   |
| Goal            | Revive Panchayati Raj |

| Topic                 | Recommendation                               |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Structure             | **2-tier system**                            |
| Levels                | Zila Parishad + Mandal Panchayat             |
| Mandal Panchayat      | Group of villages (15,000–20,000 population) |
| Main Unit             | District                                     |
| Zila Parishad         | Executive + district planning body           |
| Political Parties     | Participation in Panchayat elections         |

# 1. PANCHAYATI RAJ – INTRODUCTION


**Meaning**

- Panchayati Raj refers to the **system of rural local self-government in India**.
- It aims to **establish democracy at the grassroots level** and promote **rural development**.

**Constitutional Status**

- Constitutionalised by **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992**
- Implemented on **24 April 1993**.

**Purpose**

- Democratic decentralisation
- Participation of people in governance
- Rural development administration

---


# 2. EVOLUTION OF PANCHAYATI RAJ


The Panchayati Raj system evolved through **various committees and reforms after independence**.


---


# 3. BALWANT RAI MEHTA COMMITTEE (1957)


### Background

- Appointed in **January 1957**
- Purpose: Examine functioning of:
    - **Community Development Programme (1952)**
    - **National Extension Service (1953)**

Chairman → **Balwant Rai G. Mehta**


Report submitted → **November 1957**


---


## Key Concept Introduced


**Democratic Decentralisation**


This later became known as **Panchayati Raj System**.


---


## Major Recommendations


### 1. Three-Tier Panchayati Raj System


| Level    | Institution      |
| -------- | ---------------- |
| Village  | Gram Panchayat   |
| Block    | Panchayat Samiti |
| District | Zila Parishad    |


These tiers should be **linked through indirect elections**.


---


### 2. Election System

- **Gram Panchayat → Direct elections**
- **Panchayat Samiti & Zila Parishad → Indirect elections**

---


### 3. Planning and Development

- All **planning and development activities** should be transferred to Panchayati Raj institutions.

---


### 4. Roles of Institutions


| Body             | Role                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Panchayat Samiti | Executive body                              |
| Zila Parishad    | Advisory, coordinating and supervisory body |


---


### 5. District Collector

- Should be **Chairman of Zila Parishad**

---


### 6. Devolution of Power

- Genuine transfer of **powers and responsibilities**

---


### 7. Financial Resources

- Adequate financial resources should be provided.

---


### 8. Future Devolution

- System should allow **future decentralisation of authority**

---


## Implementation


Accepted by **National Development Council (1958)**.


However, states were allowed to adopt **different patterns** depending on local conditions.


---


## First State to Implement


**Rajasthan**

- Launched on **2 October 1959**
- In **Nagaur district**
- Inaugurated by **Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru**.

Second state → **Andhra Pradesh (1959)**


---


## Variations Across States


Different states adopted **different models**.


| State       | Structure |
| ----------- | --------- |
| Rajasthan   | 3 tier    |
| Tamil Nadu  | 2 tier    |
| West Bengal | 4 tier    |


---


### Power Distribution Differences


| Model                    | Powerful Body    |
| ------------------------ | ---------------- |
| Rajasthan–Andhra Pradesh | Panchayat Samiti |
| Maharashtra–Gujarat      | Zila Parishad    |


Reason:

# 1. SIGNIFICANCE OF THE 73rd CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT ACT (1992)


The **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** gave **constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions**.


## Major Constitutional Changes


The Act introduced:

1. **Part IX of the Constitution**
2. **Articles 243 to 243-O**
3. **Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)**

---


## Implementation

- Passed by Parliament → **December 1992**
- Presidential assent → **20 April 1993**
- Came into force → **24 April 1993**

---


## Connection with Directive Principles


The Act implemented **Article 40 (DPSP)** which states:

> The State shall organise village panchayats and endow them with powers to function as units of self-government.

---


## Importance


The amendment transformed Indian democracy from:


**Representative democracy → Participatory democracy**


Meaning:

- People directly participate in governance at **grassroot level**.

---


# 2. COMPULSORY AND VOLUNTARY PROVISIONS


The provisions of the Act are divided into **two categories**.


---


# A. COMPULSORY (MANDATORY) PROVISIONS


These **must be included** in state laws.

1. Organisation of **Gram Sabha** in villages.
2. Establishment of **three-tier Panchayati Raj system**.
3. **Direct elections** to all seats in Panchayats.
4. **Indirect election of chairperson** at intermediate and district levels.
5. **Minimum age for contesting elections → 21 years**
6. Reservation of seats for **SCs and STs**.
7. Reservation of **1/3 seats for women**.
8. **Five-year tenure** of Panchayats.
9. Creation of **State Election Commission**.
10. Creation of **State Finance Commission every 5 years**.

---


# B. VOLUNTARY (OPTIONAL) PROVISIONS


States **may or may not include** these.

1. Representation of **MPs and MLAs** in Panchayats.
2. Reservation for **Backward Classes (OBC)**.
3. Giving Panchayats **self-government powers**.
4. Assigning **economic development and social justice functions**.
5. Granting **financial powers (tax collection)**.

---


# 3. SALIENT FEATURES OF THE 73rd AMENDMENT


---


# 1. GRAM SABHA


### Definition


Gram Sabha is the **foundation of Panchayati Raj system**.


It consists of:

- All **registered voters of the village**

Thus, it is a **village assembly**.


---


### Functions


The **state legislature decides** its powers and functions.


Typical functions include:

- Approving development plans
- Social audit of schemes
- Monitoring Panchayat functioning

---


# 2. THREE-TIER PANCHAYATI RAJ SYSTEM


The Act establishes **three levels of Panchayats** in every state.


| Level        | Institution      |
| ------------ | ---------------- |
| Village      | Gram Panchayat   |
| Intermediate | Panchayat Samiti |
| District     | Zila Parishad    |


However:


States with population **less than 20 lakh** may **not create intermediate level**.


---


# 3. ELECTION OF MEMBERS AND CHAIRPERSONS


### Members


Members at **all three levels** are:


✔ **Directly elected by people**


---


### Chairpersons


| Level                  | Election Method                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Village Panchayat      | As decided by State Legislature |
| Intermediate Panchayat | Indirect election               |
| District Panchayat     | Indirect election               |


Indirect election means:

- Elected members choose the chairperson.

---


# 4. RESERVATION OF SEATS


The Act provides reservation in Panchayats.


---


## Reservation for SC and ST

- Seats reserved **in proportion to population**.

Applicable at **all three levels**.


---


## Reservation for Women

- **Minimum 1/3 seats reserved for women**

Includes seats reserved for:

- SC women
- ST women

---


### Reservation of Chairperson Posts


At least **1/3 of chairperson posts reserved for women**.


---


## Reservation for Backward Classes


States are **authorised** to provide reservation for:


**Other Backward Classes (OBC)**.


---


# 5. DURATION OF PANCHAYATS


The tenure of Panchayats is:


**5 years**.


---


### Early Dissolution


Panchayat can be dissolved **before 5 years**.


But elections must be held:


**within 6 months**.


---


### Exception


If the remaining tenure is **less than 6 months**, elections are not required.


---


### Reconstituted Panchayat


If a Panchayat is dissolved early:


The new Panchayat will serve **only the remaining term**, not a full 5 years.


---


# 6. DISQUALIFICATIONS


A person can be disqualified if:

1. Disqualified under **state legislative election law**
2. Disqualified under **state law**

---


### Age Requirement


Minimum age for Panchayat election:


**21 years**


(Not 25 like MP/MLA).


---


### Authority for Disqualification


The authority is decided by:


**State Legislature**


---


# 7. STATE ELECTION COMMISSION


The Act created a **State Election Commission (SEC)**.


---


### Responsibilities


SEC supervises:

- Preparation of electoral rolls
- Conduct of Panchayat elections

---


### Appointment


State Election Commissioner is appointed by:


**Governor**


---


### Removal


He can be removed **only like a High Court Judge**.


Meaning:

- Only by **President on recommendation of Parliament**

Thus ensuring **independence**.


---


# 8. POWERS AND FUNCTIONS OF PANCHAYATS


The **State Legislature may empower Panchayats**.


Their responsibilities include:

1. **Preparation of plans for economic development**
2. **Social justice programs**
3. **Implementation of schemes**

These include schemes related to the **29 subjects in the Eleventh Schedule**.


---


# 9. FINANCES OF PANCHAYATS


State legislatures may give Panchayats powers to:

1. **Levy taxes**
2. **Collect duties**
3. **Collect tolls**
4. **Collect fees**

---


States may also:

- Assign state taxes to Panchayats
- Provide **grants-in-aid**
- Create **Panchayat funds**

---


# 10. STATE FINANCE COMMISSION


Every **5 years**, the Governor must appoint:


**State Finance Commission**.


---


### Functions


It recommends:

1. Distribution of tax revenue between **State and Panchayats**
2. Taxes that Panchayats may collect
3. Grants-in-aid to Panchayats
4. Measures to improve Panchayat finances

---


### Report


Governor places the report before:


**State Legislature**


---


### Role of Central Finance Commission


Central Finance Commission also suggests:


Measures to **increase State funds to support Panchayats**.


---


# 11. AUDIT OF ACCOUNTS


State legislature can make provisions for:

- Maintenance of accounts
- Audit of Panchayat accounts

---


# 12. APPLICATION TO UNION TERRITORIES


President may apply Panchayat provisions to **Union Territories** with modifications.


---


# 13. EXEMPTED STATES AND AREAS


The Act does **not apply to certain areas**.


States excluded:

- Nagaland
- Meghalaya
- Mizoram
- Jammu & Kashmir (before reorganisation)

---


Other excluded areas:

1. **Scheduled Areas**
2. **Tribal Areas**
3. **Hill areas of Manipur**
4. **Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council area**

However, Parliament may extend the provisions later.


---


# 14. CONTINUANCE OF EXISTING PANCHAYATS


State laws regarding Panchayats could continue for **one year** after the Act.


Thus states had **1 year to implement the new system** after **24 April 1993**.


Most states passed new Panchayat laws during **1993–94**.


---


# 15. BAR ON COURT INTERFERENCE


Courts cannot interfere in:

1. Delimitation of constituencies
2. Allocation of seats

Election disputes can be raised only through:


**Election petitions**

# 3. PESA ACT 1996


_(Extension of Panchayats to Scheduled Areas)_


The **73rd Amendment does not apply to Fifth Schedule areas**.


Therefore Parliament passed:


**Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996**


Commonly called:


**PESA Act**


---


## States with Fifth Schedule Areas

1. Andhra Pradesh
2. Telangana
3. Chhattisgarh
4. Gujarat
5. Himachal Pradesh
6. Jharkhand
7. Madhya Pradesh
8. Maharashtra
9. Odisha
10. Rajasthan

---


# OBJECTIVES OF PESA ACT

1. Extend Panchayati Raj provisions to Scheduled Areas.
2. Provide **self-governance to tribal communities**.
3. Promote **participatory democracy at village level**.
4. Preserve **tribal traditions and customs**.
5. Develop administrative systems compatible with tribal practices.
6. Empower Panchayats according to tribal needs.
7. Prevent higher Panchayats from dominating Gram Sabha.

---


# FEATURES OF PESA ACT


---


## 1. Customary Law Recognition


State Panchayat laws must respect:

- Tribal customs
- Social practices
- Traditional management of resources.

---


## 2. Definition of Village


Village may consist of:

- Habitation
- Group of habitations
- Hamlet

Managing affairs according to **customary traditions**.


---


## 3. Gram Sabha


Every village has a **Gram Sabha consisting of all registered voters**.


---


## 4. Powers of Gram Sabha


Gram Sabha protects:

- Tribal traditions
- Cultural identity
- Community resources
- Traditional dispute resolution systems.

---


## 5. Development Role


Gram Sabha must:

- Approve development plans
- Identify beneficiaries of poverty alleviation schemes.

---


## 6. Certification of Funds


Panchayat must obtain **Gram Sabha certification** for utilisation of funds.


---


## 7. Reservation


In Scheduled Areas:

- Reservation proportional to population.
- **ST reservation not less than 50%**.
- All chairperson posts reserved for **STs**.

---


## 8. Nomination of ST Members


State government may nominate STs without representation.


But maximum nomination:


**10% of total members**.


---


## 9. Land Acquisition Consultation


Gram Sabha must be consulted before:

- Land acquisition
- Rehabilitation of displaced persons.

---


## 10. Control over Natural Resources


Panchayats manage:

- Minor water bodies
- Local natural resources.

---


## 11. Mining Rights


Gram Sabha recommendation is required for:

- Mining leases
- Prospecting licences.

---


## 12. Additional Powers


Gram Sabha or Panchayats have powers to:

- Regulate liquor consumption
- Own minor forest produce
- Prevent tribal land alienation
- Manage village markets
- Control money lending
- Control institutions in social sectors
- Control local development plans.

---


# 4. FINANCES OF PANCHAYATI RAJ


According to the **Second Administrative Reforms Commission**, Panchayats receive funds from several sources.


---


## Sources of Revenue

1. **Grants from Union Government**

    Based on Central Finance Commission recommendations.

2. **State Government devolution**

    Based on State Finance Commission recommendations.

3. **Loans and grants from State Government**
4. **Funds under centrally sponsored schemes**
5. **Internal resource generation**

---


## Internal Revenue Sources


Village Panchayats may collect taxes such as:

- Property tax
- Land tax
- Profession tax
- Vehicle tax
- Entertainment tax
- License fees
- Market fees
- Water tax
- Lighting tax
- Sanitation tax
- Education cess
- Fair and festival tax.

# 5. FINANCIAL PROBLEMS OF PANCHAYATS


Major problems observed:


---


## 1. Weak Internal Revenue


Panchayats generate **very little internal revenue**.


---


## 2. Dependence on Government Grants


Most Panchayats rely heavily on:

- State Government funds
- Central Government grants.

---


## 3. Scheme-Based Funding


Most funds are tied to specific schemes.


This limits **local flexibility**.


---


## 4. Limited Fiscal Devolution


State governments often hesitate to transfer funds.


---


## 5. Responsibility Without Resources


Panchayats have responsibilities but **insufficient financial resources**.


---


# 6. REASONS FOR INEFFECTIVE PERFORMANCE OF PANCHAYATI RAJ


Even after constitutional status, performance has been **unsatisfactory**.


---


## 1. Lack of Devolution of 3Fs


States have not adequately transferred:

- **Functions**
- **Funds**
- **Functionaries**

---


## 2. Bureaucratic Control


Panchayats remain dependent on:

- Block-level bureaucrats
- Government approvals.

---


## 3. Tied Funds


Funds are often restricted to specific schemes.


---


## 4. Excessive Dependency


Heavy reliance on government funding reduces local accountability.


---


## 5. Reluctance to Levy Taxes


Local leaders avoid taxing their own communities.


---


## 6. Weak Gram Sabha


Many states have not clearly defined powers of Gram Sabha.


---


## 7. Parallel Bodies


Creation of development missions and agencies bypass Panchayats.


---


## 8. Poor Infrastructure


Problems include:

- Lack of Panchayat offices
- Lack of staff
- Lack of computers.

---


## 9. Lack of Training


Many elected representatives are:

- Semi-literate
- Not trained in administration.
