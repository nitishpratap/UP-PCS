# 3. MEANING OF JUDICIAL ACTIVISM


### Core Idea


**Judicial Activism = proactive role of judiciary to enforce constitutional governance and protect rights.**


**In simple terms**

> Judiciary compels **Legislature + Executive** to perform constitutional duties.

### Key Functions

- Protection of **citizens’ rights**
- Promotion of **justice**
- Enforcement of **constitutional governance**

### Other Name


**Judicial Dynamism**


### Opposite Concept


**Judicial Restraint**


Meaning → Judiciary **limits interference** in executive and legislative functions.


---


# 4. SCHOLARLY DEFINITIONS


### Definition 1 (Legal Scholars)


Judicial activism =

> exercise of judicial power that **departs from strict precedent** to promote **progressive social policies**.

**Key characteristics**

- Departure from precedent
- Progressive policy orientation
- Social engineering by courts
- Possible intrusion into legislative/executive domains

---


### Definition 2 (Merriam-Webster Dictionary of Law)


Judicial activism =

> judiciary **protects or expands individual rights** through decisions beyond established precedent or legislative intent.

**Key elements**

- Expansion of individual rights
- Independent judicial interpretation
- May go beyond precedent

# 11. JUDICIAL ACTIVISM vs JUDICIAL RESTRAINT


| Judicial Activism                             | Judicial Restraint                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |

| Expands rights creatively                     | Strict constitutional interpretation |
| Intervenes in policy matters                  | Avoids policy matters                |
| Progressive interpretation                    | Conservative interpretation          |


---


# 12. MEANING OF JUDICIAL RESTRAINT


Judicial restraint = **self-limitation of judicial power**.


### Core Principles

- Courts **interpret law**, not create it.
- Law-making belongs to **Legislature + Executive**.
- Judges must avoid **policy-making**.
- Decisions must reflect **original intent of constitution framers**.

# 1. Origin of Judicial Activism

- The concept of **Judicial Activism originated and developed in the United States of America (USA).**
- The term **“Judicial Activism”** was **first coined in 1947**.

**Person who coined the term**

- **Arthur Schlesinger Jr.**
    - American historian and educator.
    - Used the term in his article **“The Supreme Court: 1947”** published in **Fortune Magazine**.

---


# 2. Judicial Activism in India


In **India**, judicial activism started developing in the **mid-1970s**.


### Judges who laid the foundations

1. **Justice V. R. Krishna Iyer**
2. **Justice P. N. Bhagwati**
3. **Justice O. Chinnappa Reddy**
4. **Justice D. A. Desai**

These judges played a key role in expanding judicial intervention in governance and protecting citizens’ rights.


---


# 3. Meaning of Judicial Activism


### Definition


Judicial Activism refers to the **proactive role played by the judiciary** in:

- Protection of **citizens’ rights**
- Promotion of **justice in society**
- Ensuring **constitutional governance**

In simple words:

> Judicial activism means the **assertive role of the judiciary to compel the legislature and executive to perform their constitutional duties.**

---


### Another name


Judicial Activism is also called


**Judicial Dynamism**


because courts actively interpret laws to address social problems.


---


### Opposite concept


The opposite of judicial activism is:


**Judicial Restraint**


Judicial restraint means **self-control exercised by the judiciary** and minimal interference with executive and legislative functions.


---


# 4. Scholarly Definitions of Judicial Activism


### Definition 1


According to legal scholars:


Judicial activism is

> “A way of exercising judicial power that motivates judges to depart from normally practised strict adherence to judicial precedent in favour of progressive and new social policies.”

Characteristics of this definition:

- Departure from strict precedent
- Adoption of progressive social policies
- Courts engage in **social engineering**
- Possible intrusion into **legislative and executive matters**

---


### Definition 2


According to **Merriam-Webster’s Dictionary of Law**


Judicial activism is

> “The practice in the judiciary of protecting or expanding individual rights through decisions that depart from established precedent or are independent of constitutional or legislative intent.”

Key points:

- Protection of individual rights
- Expansion of rights
- Decisions may go beyond existing precedent

---


# 5. Relationship Between Judicial Activism and PIL


The concept of **Public Interest Litigation (PIL)** is closely connected to judicial activism.


### Key Points

- Judicial activism of the **Supreme Court** led to the **rise of PIL**.
- PIL is considered the **most popular manifestation of judicial activism**.
- Judicial activism **expanded access to justice** through PIL.

Thus,


**Judicial Activism → Rise of PIL**


---


# 6. Justification of Judicial Activism


Legal scholar **Dr. B. L. Wadehra** explained the reasons for judicial activism.


### Major reasons


---


## 1. Collapse of Responsible Government


Sometimes:

- Legislature fails to perform its duties
- Executive fails to implement laws

This results in:

- Loss of public confidence in
    - Constitution
    - Democracy

Therefore the judiciary steps in.


---


## 2. Protection of Citizens’ Rights


Citizens look to the **judiciary** for protection of:

- Rights
- Freedoms
- Justice

This creates **tremendous pressure on courts** to intervene for public welfare.


---


## 3. Judicial Enthusiasm


Judges often want to participate in **social reforms**.


This leads to:

- Encouragement of **Public Interest Litigation**
- Liberal interpretation of **Locus Standi**

### Locus Standi


Meaning:


The right of a person to approach the court.


Judicial activism **relaxed this rule**, allowing:

- NGOs
- Social activists
- Public spirited citizens

to file petitions.


---


## 4. Legislative Vacuum


Sometimes the legislature:

- Has **not made laws** in certain areas.

In such situations:

- Courts perform **judicial legislation**
- Courts fill the legal gap to meet changing social needs.

---


## 5. Constitutional Provisions Allow Judicial Activism


The **Indian Constitution itself gives scope** to the judiciary to play an active role.


Examples include:

- Judicial review
- Protection of fundamental rights
- Constitutional interpretation

These powers allow courts to intervene when necessary.


---


# 7. Subhash Kashyap's Explanation of Judicial Activism


Political thinker **Subhash C. Kashyap** explained situations where judicial intervention becomes necessary.


### Situations requiring judicial activism


---


## 1. Failure of Legislature


When the **legislature fails to perform its responsibilities**, courts may intervene.


---


## 2. Hung Legislature


When there is a **hung legislature**:

- Government becomes weak
- Political instability occurs
- Government focuses on survival rather than governance

Thus courts intervene.


---


## 3. Political Fear of Decisions


Sometimes rulers avoid **difficult decisions** because:

- They fear losing power
- They fear upsetting certain groups

In such cases:

- Issues are sent to courts as legal disputes.

---


## 4. Failure to Protect Basic Rights


If the legislature and executive fail to protect:

- Right to life
- Healthy environment
- Decent living conditions
- Honest administration

The judiciary steps in.


---


## 5. Misuse of Power by Government


Courts may intervene when:

- Authoritarian governments misuse the legal system.

Example:

- During the **Emergency period (1975–77)**.

---


## 6. Judicial Populism


Sometimes courts themselves become influenced by:

- Media publicity
- Popular opinion
- Desire for public recognition

This may also encourage judicial activism.

# 11. Judicial Activism vs Judicial Restraint


Judicial Activism and Judicial Restraint represent **two alternative judicial philosophies**.


These concepts developed mainly in the **United States**, but are also relevant in India.


| Judicial Activism                                               | Judicial Restraint                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Judiciary plays an **active role** in governance.               | Judiciary **limits its role** and avoids interfering with legislature and executive. |
| Courts may expand rights and interpret laws creatively.         | Courts strictly interpret law according to constitutional intent.                    |
| Courts may intervene in administrative and legislative matters. | Courts avoid policy matters and leave them to elected branches.                      |
| Encourages **progressive interpretation of law**.               | Encourages **strict adherence to constitutional limits**.                            |


---


# 12. Meaning of Judicial Restraint


Judicial restraint means that **judges should limit the exercise of their power**.


Supporters of judicial restraint argue that:

- The role of judges is **only to interpret the law**, not to make it.
- Law-making powers belong to:
    - **Legislature**
    - **Executive**

Therefore courts should avoid:

- Creating policy
- Influencing governance.

---


### Key Principle


Judges should **not allow their personal political values or policy preferences** to influence judicial decisions.


The principle also argues that courts should follow the **“original intent”** of the framers of the constitution.


Meaning:


Judges should interpret the constitution **according to what the framers intended**, rather than changing its meaning according to contemporary social needs.


---


# 13. Assumptions of Judicial Restraint (USA Doctrine)


In the United States, judicial restraint is based on **six major assumptions**.


---


## 1. Court is Undemocratic in Nature


Courts are considered **undemocratic** because:

- Judges are **not elected by people**
- They may not represent popular will.

Therefore courts should defer to **democratic branches of government** (legislature and executive).


---


## 2. Questionable Origin of Judicial Review


The power of **judicial review** is extremely strong.


However:

- It is **not explicitly granted in the US Constitution**.

Therefore courts should exercise this power cautiously.


_(Note: This assumption does not fully apply to India because judicial review is constitutionally recognised.)_


---


## 3. Doctrine of Separation of Powers


Government powers are divided among three organs:

1. Legislature
2. Executive
3. Judiciary

Judicial restraint ensures that courts **do not interfere in the domain of other organs**.


---


## 4. Federalism


Federalism divides powers between:

- National government
- State governments

Therefore courts should show **deference to state governments and officials**.


---


## 5. Pragmatic Institutional Limitations


Courts depend on:

- Legislature for **resources and jurisdiction**
- Public acceptance for **authority**

Therefore courts should avoid excessive interference in governance.


---


## 6. Aristocratic Notion of Judiciary


Courts are institutions of:

- Law
- Reason
- Judgement

Whereas politics involves:

- Power
- Influence

Therefore courts should **not descend too deeply into political matters**.


---


### Applicability to India


Most of these assumptions apply to **Indian judicial philosophy as well**, except the assumption relating to judicial review.


In India:

- Judicial review is **explicitly provided in the Constitution**.

---


# 14. Supreme Court Observations on Judicial Restraint (2007)


In **December 2007**, the **Supreme Court of India emphasised the importance of judicial restraint**.


The court warned judges **not to overstep constitutional limits**.


---


## Key Observations of the Supreme Court


---


### 1. Judges Should Not Perform Executive or Legislative Functions


The Supreme Court said:


Judges are increasingly attempting to perform **executive or legislative functions**, which is unconstitutional.


Judicial activism must not become **judicial overreach**.


---


### 2. Judges Must Know Their Limits


The court observed:


Judges must show **modesty and humility**.


They should not behave like **“emperors running the government.”**


---


### 3. Warning of Montesquieu


The Supreme Court referred to **Montesquieu’s book “The Spirit of Laws.”**


Montesquieu warned:


If separation of powers is not maintained between:

- Legislature
- Executive
- Judiciary

then **liberty would be destroyed**.


The court stated that this warning is **highly relevant to Indian judiciary today**.


---


### 4. Judicial Activism Should Not Become Judicial Adventurism


The court warned that:


Judicial activism should not turn into **judicial adventurism**.


Meaning:


Courts should avoid unnecessary experimentation in governance.


Judicial decisions must remain within **historically validated restraints**.


---


### 5. Courts Should Respect Administrative Expertise


Administrative authorities possess **specialised expertise** in governance.


Courts do not always have that expertise.


Therefore courts should avoid embarrassing administrative authorities unnecessarily.


---


### 6. Judiciary Also Has Weaknesses


The court noted that:


If people criticize legislature or executive for inefficiency, the same criticism can also apply to the judiciary.


Example:


Some court cases remain pending **for more than 50 years**.


---


### 7. People Should Correct Political Failures Through Elections


If legislature or executive is ineffective:


The remedy lies in:

- Elections
- Democratic participation
- Peaceful demonstrations.

Courts should **not replace democratic institutions**.


---


### 8. Judiciary Lacks Resources for Governance


Courts do not possess:

- Administrative machinery
- Policy-making institutions

Therefore judiciary cannot perform functions of government.


---


### 9. Judicial Restraint Protects Separation of Powers


Judicial restraint promotes:

- Balance of power among state organs
- Respect among institutions

It protects judicial independence in two ways:

1. Recognises equality of legislature and executive.
2. Minimises unnecessary interference between branches.
