# NATIONAL HUMAN RIGHTS COMMISSION (NHRC)


## 1. Establishment of NHRC


The **National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)** is a **statutory body (वैधानिक निकाय)**, not a constitutional body. It was established in **1993** under the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**.


The Act was later **amended in 2006**. 


### Nature of NHRC

- **Statutory body**
- Created by **Parliamentary legislation**
- Works as the **watchdog of human rights in India**

---


## Meaning of Human Rights (as per the Act)


Human rights include rights related to:

- **Life**
- **Liberty**
- **Equality**
- **Dignity of the individual**

These rights must be:

1. **Guaranteed by the Constitution**, OR
2. **Contained in international covenants**, AND
3. **Enforceable by courts in India**

---


## International Covenants Included


The term **International Covenants** refers to:


1️⃣ **International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)**


2️⃣ **International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR)**


Adopted by the **UN General Assembly on 16 December 1966**.


India acceded to these covenants on:


📅 **10 April 1979**


---


# 2. Objectives of Establishing NHRC


The NHRC was established with the following objectives:


### 1. Strengthening Institutional Mechanism


To strengthen institutions dealing with **human rights protection**.


### 2. Independent Investigation of Violations


To investigate allegations of **human rights violations independently of the government**.


### 3. Supporting Existing Efforts


To **complement and strengthen existing efforts** for protection of human rights.


---


# 3. Composition of NHRC


NHRC is a **multi-member body**.


## Structure


| Position | Number |
| -------- | ------ |
| Chairman | 1      |
| Members  | 4      |


Thus total **5 full-time members**.


---


## Qualifications of Members


### Chairman


Must be a:


➡ **Former Chief Justice of India**


---


### Other Members


1️⃣ One **serving or retired judge of Supreme Court**


2️⃣ One **serving or retired Chief Justice of High Court**


3️⃣ Two persons with **knowledge or practical experience in human rights**


---


## Ex-Officio Members


NHRC also includes **four ex-officio members**:


| Commission                         | Chairperson becomes NHRC member |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| National Commission for Minorities | Yes                             |
| National Commission for SCs        | Yes                             |
| National Commission for STs        | Yes                             |
| National Commission for Women      | Yes                             |


Thus NHRC also indirectly coordinates with these commissions.


---


# 4. Appointment of Chairman and Members


All members are appointed by:


👑 **President of India**


But the President appoints them **on the recommendation of a committee**.


---


## Selection Committee


The committee consists of **6 members**:


| Member                             | Position |
| ---------------------------------- | -------- |
| Prime Minister                     | Chairman |
| Speaker of Lok Sabha               | Member   |
| Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha     | Member   |
| Leader of Opposition (Lok Sabha)   | Member   |
| Leader of Opposition (Rajya Sabha) | Member   |
| Union Home Minister                | Member   |


---


### Important Rule


If a **sitting Supreme Court judge** or **sitting Chief Justice of High Court** is to be appointed:


➡ Consultation with the **Chief Justice of India** is necessary.


---


# 5. Tenure of Chairman and Members


The chairman and members hold office:


🕒 **5 years OR until 70 years of age**


(whichever occurs earlier)


---


## Post-retirement Restriction


After completing their tenure:


🚫 They are **not eligible for further employment** under:

- Central Government
- State Government

Purpose:


To ensure **independence and impartiality**.


---


# 6. Removal of Chairman or Members


The **President can remove them** under certain conditions.


---


## Removal WITHOUT Supreme Court Inquiry


President can remove directly if:


1️⃣ **Insolvent (bankrupt)**


2️⃣ Engages in **paid employment outside official duties**


3️⃣ **Infirmity of mind or body**


4️⃣ Declared **unsound mind by court**


5️⃣ **Convicted and sentenced to imprisonment**


---


## Removal WITH Supreme Court Inquiry


For:

- **Proved misbehaviour**
- **Incapacity**

Procedure:


1️⃣ President refers the matter to **Supreme Court**


2️⃣ Supreme Court conducts **inquiry**


3️⃣ If SC confirms removal grounds


4️⃣ President removes the member


---


# 7. Salary and Service Conditions

- Determined by **Central Government**
- But **cannot be altered to their disadvantage after appointment**

Purpose:


➡ Protect **independence of the commission**


---


# Why These Provisions Exist


All these safeguards ensure:

- **Autonomy**
- **Independence**
- **Impartial functioning**

of the NHRC.

# 1. FUNCTIONS OF NHRC


The **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** assigns several functions to NHRC.


These functions mainly aim to **protect, promote and monitor human rights in India**.


---


## 1. Inquiry into Human Rights Violations


NHRC can **inquire into violations of human rights** or negligence in preventing such violations by a **public servant**.


The inquiry can begin in three ways:


1️⃣ **Suo motu** (on its own initiative)


2️⃣ **On a petition filed by a victim or any person**


3️⃣ **On an order from a court**


This means NHRC does not always need a formal complaint.


---


## 2. Intervention in Court Proceedings


NHRC may **intervene in court proceedings** involving allegations of human rights violations.


This can happen only when:


➡ The court **permits the intervention**.


This allows NHRC to assist courts in protecting human rights.


---


## 3. Inspection of Jails and Detention Centres


NHRC can:

- Visit **jails**
- Visit **detention centres**

Purpose:

- Study **living conditions of prisoners**
- Make **recommendations for improvement**

This power helps ensure **humane treatment of prisoners**.


---


## 4. Review of Constitutional and Legal Safeguards


NHRC reviews:

- **Constitutional safeguards**
- **Legal safeguards**

that protect human rights.


After review it may recommend:


➡ **Measures for effective implementation**


Example:

- Reviewing laws protecting women or children.

---


## 5. Review of Factors Affecting Human Rights


NHRC examines factors that **prevent people from enjoying their human rights**.


Such factors include:

- **Terrorism**
- **Insurgency**
- **Violence**

After studying these issues, the commission recommends **remedial measures**.


---


## 6. Study of International Human Rights Instruments


NHRC studies:

- **International treaties**
- **International conventions**
- **Other global instruments related to human rights**

Then it recommends how these can be **implemented effectively in India**.


---


## 7. Promotion of Human Rights Research


NHRC undertakes and promotes **research activities** related to human rights.


This helps:

- Develop better policies
- Improve protection of rights

---


## 8. Promotion of Human Rights Literacy


NHRC spreads awareness among people about:

- Human rights
- Safeguards available for protection

Methods include:

- Educational programs
- Publications
- Training sessions

---


## 9. Encouragement of NGOs


NHRC encourages the work of **Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs)** working in the field of human rights.


NGOs often provide:

- Information about violations
- Ground-level data
- Victim assistance

---


## 10. Any Other Necessary Functions


NHRC may perform **any other function necessary for promoting human rights**.


This clause gives flexibility to the commission.


---


# 2. WORKING OF THE COMMISSION


---


## Headquarters


The headquarters of NHRC is located at:


📍 **New Delhi**


However, it may establish **regional offices anywhere in India**.


---


## Power to Regulate Procedure


NHRC has the power to:


➡ **Regulate its own procedure**


This means it can decide how its investigations and hearings are conducted.


---


## Powers of a Civil Court


While conducting inquiries, NHRC has powers similar to a **civil court**.


These powers include:

- Summoning witnesses
- Requiring discovery of documents
- Receiving evidence
- Requisitioning public records

Because of this:


➡ Proceedings of NHRC have a **judicial character**.


---


## Power to Seek Reports


NHRC may request information from:

- **Central Government**
- **State Governments**
- **Any subordinate authority**

These authorities must provide the required information.


---


## Investigation Machinery


NHRC has its own **investigating staff**.


This staff investigates complaints related to human rights violations.


---


## Assistance from Government Agencies


NHRC can also utilise services of:

- Officers of **Central Government**
- Officers of **State Governments**
- **Investigation agencies**

This ensures proper investigation.


---


## Cooperation with NGOs


NHRC has developed cooperation with **NGOs**.


These NGOs often provide:

- First-hand information
- Ground reports
- Victim assistance

This improves investigation quality.


---


# 3. TIME LIMIT FOR INQUIRY


NHRC cannot investigate cases after a certain time limit.


⏳ **Maximum time limit: 1 year**


Meaning:


NHRC cannot inquire into a matter **after one year from the date of violation**.


This is an important limitation.


---


# 4. ACTIONS AFTER INQUIRY


After completing an inquiry, NHRC can take several steps.


---


## 1. Recommendation for Compensation


NHRC may recommend the government to:


➡ Pay **compensation or damages to the victim**.


---


## 2. Recommendation for Prosecution


NHRC may recommend:


➡ **Prosecution or disciplinary action against the guilty public servant**.


---


## 3. Recommendation for Interim Relief


NHRC may recommend:


➡ Immediate **interim relief to the victim**.


This helps victims get quick support.


---


## 4. Approach Higher Judiciary


NHRC may approach:

- **Supreme Court**
- **High Court**

for appropriate:

- Directions
- Orders
- Writs

This strengthens enforcement of human rights.


---


# 5. ROLE OF NHRC


Although NHRC has many powers, its role is mainly **recommendatory**.


This means:


❗ NHRC **cannot punish violators**.


❗ NHRC **cannot directly award compensation**.


Instead, it **recommends actions to the government**.


---


## Nature of NHRC Recommendations


NHRC recommendations are:


➡ **Not legally binding**


However:


The concerned government must inform NHRC within:


⏳ **1 month**


about the **action taken on its recommendations**.


---


## Practical Importance


Even though recommendations are not binding:

- Governments generally take them seriously.
- NHRC has **moral and institutional authority**.

A former NHRC member stated that governments **cannot easily ignore its recommendations**.


---


# 6. LIMITATION REGARDING ARMED FORCES


NHRC has **limited jurisdiction** over human rights violations by **armed forces**.


In such cases:


NHRC cannot directly investigate.


Instead it may:


1️⃣ **Seek a report from the Central Government**


2️⃣ After examining the report, it may make **recommendations**


---


## Government Response Time


The **Central Government must inform NHRC** about action taken within:


⏳ **3 months**


---


# 7. REPORTS OF THE COMMISSION


NHRC submits:

- **Annual Reports**
- **Special Reports**

to:

- **Central Government**
- **State Governments concerned**

---


## Placement Before Legislature


These reports are laid before:


| Government         | Legislature       |
| ------------------ | ----------------- |
| Central Government | Parliament        |
| State Government   | State Legislature |


They are presented along with:

- **Memorandum of action taken**
- **Reasons for non-acceptance of recommendations**

# 1. PERFORMANCE OF THE COMMISSION


Over time, the **NHRC has taken up many important human rights issues**. These issues cover **social justice, women’s rights, child rights, labour rights, police reforms, and disaster relief**.


Below are the major areas addressed by NHRC.


---


# A. Labour and Social Justice Issues


### 1. Abolition of Bonded Labour


NHRC has taken steps to eliminate **bonded labour**, where workers are forced to work to repay debts.


### 2. Abolition of Child Labour


NHRC has worked toward preventing **child labour** and ensuring children’s rights.


### 3. Preventing Employment of Children by Government Servants


NHRC recommended **amendments in service rules** so that government employees cannot employ children as domestic workers.


---


# B. Women’s Rights Issues


### 1. Rehabilitation of Destitute Women in Vrindavan


NHRC addressed issues of **abandoned and destitute women**, especially widows living in Vrindavan.


### 2. Combating Sexual Harassment at Workplace


NHRC took initiatives to prevent **sexual harassment of women at workplaces**.


### 3. Harassment of Women Passengers in Trains


NHRC addressed complaints regarding **harassment of women passengers in trains**.


### 4. Trafficking in Women and Children


NHRC prepared manuals for **gender sensitisation of the judiciary** to deal with trafficking cases.


---


# C. Child Rights Issues


NHRC addressed several issues related to children.


Important initiatives include:


1️⃣ **Protocols to the Convention on the Rights of the Child**


2️⃣ **Guidebook for media on sexual violence against children**


3️⃣ **Prevention of sex tourism and trafficking**


These efforts aim to improve **child protection mechanisms**.


---


# D. Health and Human Rights


NHRC has taken up several health-related issues.


Important issues include:


### 1. Maternal Anemia


NHRC highlighted the **health rights of pregnant women**.


### 2. Issues Related to Right to Health


NHRC worked on improving access to healthcare.


### 3. Rights of Persons with HIV/AIDS


NHRC addressed **discrimination against people living with HIV/AIDS**.


---


# E. Issues Related to Vulnerable Groups


NHRC has taken steps to protect the rights of vulnerable communities.


These include:


### 1. Dalit Rights


NHRC addressed **atrocities against Scheduled Castes (Dalits)**.


### 2. Denotified and Nomadic Tribes


NHRC studied problems faced by **Denotified and Nomadic Tribes**.


### 3. Rights of Persons with Disabilities


NHRC addressed **human rights of disabled persons**.


---


# F. Manual Scavenging


NHRC took initiatives to eliminate **manual scavenging**, a degrading practice.


---


# G. Right to Food


NHRC worked on issues related to **food security and hunger**, emphasising the **right to food**.


---


# H. Disaster Relief Monitoring


NHRC monitored relief measures after major disasters.


Examples include:


### 1. 1999 Orissa Cyclone


NHRC monitored relief efforts for victims.


### 2. 2001 Gujarat Earthquake


NHRC supervised relief measures to ensure proper assistance.


---


# I. Law and Order / Police Reforms


NHRC has taken many initiatives to improve **policing and law enforcement**.


Important measures include:


### 1. Guidelines to Prevent Misuse of Arrest Powers


NHRC issued guidelines to prevent **arbitrary arrests by police**.


### 2. Human Rights Cells in Police Headquarters


NHRC recommended establishment of **human rights cells in police headquarters**.


### 3. Preventing Custodial Deaths and Torture


NHRC took steps to reduce:

- Custodial deaths
- Custodial rape
- Torture by police

# J. Prison and Mental Health Institutions


NHRC examined the functioning of:

- **Mental hospitals in Ranchi, Agra, and Gwalior**
- **Government Protective Home for Women in Agra**

The aim was to improve **living conditions and treatment of inmates**.

# K. Legislative and Policy Review


NHRC also reviews laws affecting human rights.


Examples include:


1️⃣ Review of **Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929**


2️⃣ Review of **terrorism-related laws** such as:

- Terrorist and Disruptive Activities (Prevention) Act (TADA)
- Draft Prevention of Terrorism Bill (2000)

3️⃣ Discussion on adoption of a **Refugee Law in India**

# 2. HUMAN RIGHTS (AMENDMENT) ACT, 2006


The **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** was amended in **2006**.


The amendment introduced several important reforms.


---


# Major Amendments


## 1. Reduction in Members of State Human Rights Commissions


Members of **State Human Rights Commissions (SHRC)** were reduced:


From:


➡ **5 members**


To:


➡ **3 members**


---


## 2. Change in Eligibility Conditions


The amendment changed **eligibility conditions for members of SHRCs**.


---


## 3. Strengthening Investigation Machinery


The amendment strengthened the **investigative powers of human rights commissions**.


---


## 4. Compensation Recommendation During Inquiry


The commissions were empowered to **recommend compensation even during the inquiry process**, not only after completion.


---


## 5. Surprise Jail Visits


NHRC can visit **jails without prior intimation to state governments**.


This increases transparency.


---


## 6. Stronger Evidence Recording Procedures


Procedures for **recording witness evidence** were strengthened.


---


## 7. Distinction Between Chairperson and Members


The amendment clarified that:


➡ Chairperson and members are **distinct positions**.


---


## 8. Transfer of Complaints to SHRC


NHRC can transfer complaints to the **State Human Rights Commission**.


This reduces workload and ensures faster resolution.


---


## 9. Resignation Procedure


Resignations must be addressed to:


| Commission                 | Resignation to        |
| -------------------------- | --------------------- |
| NHRC Chairperson or Member | President of India    |
| SHRC Chairperson or Member | Governor of the State |


---


## 10. Selection Committee Decisions Valid


Even if **a member of the selection committee is absent**, the selection decision **remains valid**.


---


## 11. SC and ST Commission Chairpersons Included


Chairpersons of:

- National Commission for SCs
- National Commission for STs

are deemed to be **members of NHRC**.


---


## 12. International Covenants


Central Government can notify **future international human rights conventions** to be covered under the Act.


---


# 3. IMPORTANT NOTES FROM THE CHAPTER


---


## Origin of the Act


Before the Act was passed:


📅 **28 September 1993**


President issued **Protection of Human Rights Ordinance**.


Later:


📅 **8 January 1994**


The **Protection of Human Rights Act** received presidential assent.


But the Act was applied **retrospectively from 28 September 1993**.


---


# 4. Committee Recommendation


The **A.M. Ahmadi Committee** recommended that:


NHRC should be allowed to **investigate cases even after 1 year** if there are valid reasons for delay.


---


# 5. Armed Forces Definition


Under the Act, **armed forces include**:

- Army
- Navy
- Air Force
- Other armed forces of the Union

The Ahmadi Committee recommended limiting this definition to **only Army, Navy, and Air Force**.
