
## VICE-PRESIDENT OF INDIA (UPPCS GS-2 Polity Revision Notes)

---


## 1. Constitutional Position

- The **Vice-President of India** holds the **second highest constitutional office**.
- Ranked **next to the President** in the **Warrant of Precedence**.
- Office inspired by the **Vice-President of the United States**, but functions differ.

---


# 2. Election of Vice-President


### Nature of Election

- **Indirect election**

### Electoral College


Consists of **all members of Parliament**:

- Elected members of **Lok Sabha**
- Nominated members of **Lok Sabha**
- Elected members of **Rajya Sabha**
- Nominated members of **Rajya Sabha**

Thus **both elected and nominated MPs vote**.


---


# 3. Difference: Election of President vs Vice-President


| Feature | President        | Vice-President          |
| ------- | ---------------- | ----------------------- |
| MPs     | Only elected MPs | Elected + nominated MPs |
| MLAs    | Participate      | Do not participate      |
| Nature  | Federal election | Parliamentary election  |


### Explanation (Dr. B. R. Ambedkar)

- **President represents Union + States → MLAs participate**
- **Vice-President mainly presides over Rajya Sabha → MPs only**

---


# 4. Method of Election


Election is conducted by:

- **Proportional Representation System**
- **Single Transferable Vote**
- **Secret Ballot**

Purpose → ensures **fair political representation in Parliament**.


---


# 5. Qualifications


A candidate must:

1. Be a **citizen of India**
2. Be **at least 35 years old**
3. Be **qualified for election as a member of Rajya Sabha**
4. **Not hold office of profit** under:
    - Union Government
    - State Government
    - Local authority
    - Public authority

---


# 6. Office of Profit Exceptions


The following **are not considered office of profit**:

- President
- Vice-President
- Governor
- Union Ministers
- State Ministers

---


# 7. Nomination Requirements


To contest the election:

- **20 proposers**
- **20 seconders**

(All must be **MPs**)


### Security Deposit

- **₹15,000 deposited in RBI**

Deposit may be **forfeited if required votes not secured**.


---


# 8. Oath or Affirmation


Before assuming office, the Vice-President takes an oath to:

- Bear **true faith and allegiance to the Constitution**
- **Faithfully discharge duties**

### Administered by

- **President of India**
- or **person appointed by President**

---


# 9. Conditions of Office


### 1. Not a Member of Legislature


Vice-President **cannot be a member of**:

- Parliament
- State Legislature

If an MP becomes Vice-President → **seat automatically vacates**.


### 2. No Office of Profit


Cannot hold **any other office of profit**.


---


# 10. Term of Office

- **5 years** from date of entering office.

### Key Points

- May **resign anytime** (to President)
- Can be **removed before term**
- **Eligible for re-election any number of times**

### Continuity Rule


Vice-President continues **until successor assumes office**.


---


# 11. Removal of Vice-President


Vice-President **is NOT impeached**.


### Removal Procedure

1. **Resolution passed in Rajya Sabha**
2. Must be passed by **Absolute Majority**

    (majority of total membership)

3. **Lok Sabha must agree to it**

### Notice Requirement

- **14 days advance notice required**

### Important Feature

- **No grounds specified in Constitution** for removal.

---


# 12. Vacancy in Office


Vacancy may occur due to:

- Expiry of term
- Resignation
- Removal
- Death
- Disqualification
- Election declared void by Supreme Court

---


# 13. Filling the Vacancy


### 1. Vacancy due to Expiry


Election held **before expiry of term**.


### 2. Vacancy due to Death / Resignation / Removal


Election held **as soon as possible**.


New Vice-President gets **full 5-year term** from date of office.


---


# 14. Powers and Functions of Vice-President


The functions of the Vice-President are **two-fold**.


---


# 1. Ex-Officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha


The **primary function** of the Vice-President is to act as the **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**.


### Powers

- Presides over **Rajya Sabha proceedings**
- Maintains **order and discipline**
- Allows **debates and discussions**
- Decides **points of order**
- Supervises **functioning of the House**

### Voting Power

- **No vote in the first instance**
- Can cast a **casting vote in case of tie**

### Comparison

- Similar to the **Speaker of Lok Sabha**, but presides over **Rajya Sabha**.

---


# 2. Acting President of India


The Vice-President **acts as President** in case of vacancy in the office of President.


### Vacancy may arise due to:

- Death
- Resignation
- Removal
- Otherwise (invalid election etc.)

### Duration


Vice-President can act as President for **maximum 6 months**.


Within **6 months a new President must be elected**.


---


# 3. When President is Temporarily Unable to Work


Vice-President **discharges the functions of President** if the President is unable to perform duties due to:

- Illness
- Absence
- Any other reason

Vice-President performs the duties **until the President resumes office**.


---


# 4. When Vice-President Acts as President


During this period:

- Vice-President **does not perform Rajya Sabha Chairman duties**

Instead:


➡ **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha presides over the House**.


---


# 15. Indian vs American Vice-President


| Feature    | India                          | USA                |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Succession | Acts as President temporarily  | Becomes President  |
| Duration   | Until new President elected    | For remaining term |
| Role       | Mainly Chairman of Rajya Sabha | Executive role     |


Thus:

- **India → temporary acting role**
- **USA → full Presidential succession**

---


# 16. Importance of Vice-President


The Constitution assigns **limited executive powers** to the Vice-President.


Some scholars describe the office as:


**“His Superfluous Highness.”**


However, the position ensures:

- **Continuity of government**
- **Stability of constitutional system**
- **Smooth functioning of Parliament**

---


# 17. Emoluments (Salary & Allowances)


The Constitution **does not fix a salary for the Vice-President** in that capacity.


Instead he receives salary as:


➡ **Chairman of Rajya Sabha**


### Salary


In **2008**, salary increased from:


**₹40,000 → ₹1.25 lakh per month**


### Other Facilities


Vice-President receives:

- Daily allowance
- Free furnished residence
- Medical facilities
- Travel allowances
- Staff and other privileges

---


# Salary When Acting as President


When acting as President:

- Vice-President **does not receive Rajya Sabha Chairman salary**

Instead receives:


➡ **Salary and allowances of the President of India**.

The **Vice-President of India** occupies the **second highest constitutional office** in the country.


He is ranked **next to the President in the official warrant of precedence**.


The office of the Vice-President in India is **modelled on the lines of the American Vice-President**.


However, there are **important differences between the Indian and American systems**, which are discussed later.


---


# 1. Election of Vice-President


The Vice-President is **not elected directly by the people**.


Instead, he is elected through an **indirect election**.


### Electoral College


The Vice-President is elected by an **electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament**.


Electoral college includes:

- Elected members of **Lok Sabha**
- Nominated members of **Lok Sabha**
- Elected members of **Rajya Sabha**
- Nominated members of **Rajya Sabha**

Thus **both elected and nominated members of Parliament participate** in the election of the Vice-President.


---


# 2. Difference Between Electoral College of President and Vice-President


| Feature               | President                | Vice-President                 |
| --------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Members of Parliament | Only elected MPs         | Both elected and nominated MPs |
| State Legislature     | Elected MLAs participate | No participation               |
| Nature                | Federal election         | Parliamentary election         |


### Explanation by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar


Dr. Ambedkar explained the difference:

- The **President is head of the Union and States**, so **state legislatures must participate**.
- The **Vice-President mainly presides over Rajya Sabha**, so **state legislatures are not required**.

---


# 3. Method of Election


The Vice-President is elected using the **same method as the President**.


### Election System


The election follows:


**1. Proportional Representation System**


**2. Single Transferable Vote**


**3. Secret Ballot**


This ensures **fair representation of different political parties in Parliament**.


---


# 4. Qualifications of Vice-President


A person must satisfy the following qualifications:

1. Must be a **citizen of India**
2. Must have **completed 35 years of age**
3. Must be **qualified for election as a member of Rajya Sabha**
4. Must **not hold any office of profit** under:
    - Union Government
    - State Government
    - Local authority
    - Public authority

---


# 5. Office of Profit Exception


The following persons are **not considered to hold an office of profit**, and hence can contest:

- Sitting **President**
- Sitting **Vice-President**
- **Governor of a State**
- **Union Ministers**
- **State Ministers**

---


# 6. Nomination Requirements


To contest the Vice-President election:


A candidate must have:

- **20 proposers**
- **20 seconders**

All proposers and seconders must be **members of the electoral college (MPs)**.


### Security Deposit


Candidate must deposit:


**₹15,000 in the Reserve Bank of India**


If the candidate fails to obtain the required votes, the **deposit may be forfeited**.


---


# 7. Oath or Affirmation


Before entering office, the Vice-President must take an **oath or affirmation**.


In the oath he swears:

1. **True faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India**
2. **Faithfully discharge the duties of his office**

### Who administers the oath?


The oath is administered by:


**The President of India**


or


**Any person appointed by the President**


---


# 8. Conditions of Office


The Constitution lays down **two conditions**.


### Condition 1


The Vice-President **must not be a member of**:

- Parliament
- State Legislature

If a member of Parliament is elected as Vice-President:


➡ His seat becomes **vacant from the date he assumes office**.


### Condition 2


He must **not hold any other office of profit**.


---


# 9. Term of Office


The Vice-President holds office for **five years** from the **date he enters office**.


### Important Points

- The Vice-President **may resign anytime**.
- Resignation letter is addressed to the **President of India**.
- He **can be removed before completion of term**.
- He **can be re-elected any number of times**.

### Continuation in Office


Even after the completion of the 5-year term:


➡ The Vice-President **continues in office until his successor assumes office**.


This ensures **continuity in constitutional functioning**.


---


# 10. Removal of Vice-President


Unlike the President, the Vice-President **is not impeached**.


### Removal Procedure


The Vice-President can be removed by:

1. **Resolution passed in Rajya Sabha**
2. Must be passed by **Absolute Majority**

Absolute Majority = **Majority of total membership of the House**

1. The resolution must be **agreed to by Lok Sabha**.

### Notice Requirement


Before introducing the resolution:


➡ **14 days advance notice must be given**


### Important Feature


The Constitution **does not mention any grounds for removal**.


Thus, removal can be done **without stating specific charges**.


---


# 11. Vacancy in the Office of Vice-President


A vacancy may arise in the following situations:

1. **Expiry of tenure (5 years)**
2. **Resignation**
3. **Removal**
4. **Death**
5. **Disqualification**
6. **Election declared void** by Supreme Court

---


# 12. Filling the Vacancy


### Case 1: Vacancy due to Expiry of Term


Election must be held:


➡ **Before the expiry of the term**


This ensures **no constitutional vacuum**.


---


### Case 2: Vacancy due to Death / Resignation / Removal


Election must be held:


➡ **As soon as possible**


The newly elected Vice-President:


➡ Gets **full 5-year term** from the date of assumption of office.


---


# 13. Election Disputes


All disputes regarding the election of Vice-President are decided by the:


### Supreme Court of India


The **decision of the Supreme Court is final**.


---


### Important Constitutional Principle


The election **cannot be challenged on the ground that the electoral college was incomplete**.


Meaning:


➡ Even if some seats in Parliament are vacant, the election **remains valid**.


---


### If Election Declared Void


If the Supreme Court declares the election void:


➡ All acts performed by the Vice-President **before the declaration remain valid**.


This principle ensures **constitutional stability**.


---


# 14. Powers and Functions of Vice-President


The functions of the Vice-President are **two-fold**.


---


# (1) Ex-Officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha


The Vice-President is the **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**.


This is his **primary function**.


### Powers as Chairman


His powers are similar to the **Speaker of Lok Sabha**, such as:

- Presiding over Rajya Sabha meetings
- Maintaining order in the House
- Allowing debates
- Deciding points of order
- Supervising proceedings

However:


➡ He **cannot vote in the first instance**.


He can **only cast a casting vote in case of tie**.


---


### Comparative Feature


This role resembles the **American Vice-President**, who acts as the **Chairman of the Senate**.


---


# (2) Acting President of India


The Vice-President **acts as President** when the office of President becomes vacant.


Vacancy may arise due to:

- Death
- Resignation
- Removal
- Otherwise (e.g., invalid election)

---


### Duration of Acting President


The Vice-President can act as President for **maximum six months**.


Within **six months**, a **new President must be elected**.


---


# When President is Temporarily Unable to Work


If the President cannot perform duties due to:

- Illness
- Absence
- Any other reason

➡ Vice-President **discharges the functions of President** until the President resumes office.


---


# During Acting Presidency


When the Vice-President performs the role of President:

- He **does not perform duties of Rajya Sabha Chairman**.

Instead:


➡ **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha performs those duties**.


---


# 15. Indian vs American Vice-President


Although the Indian Vice-President is inspired by the American system, there is a key difference.


| Feature    | India                         | USA                |
| ---------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Succession | Acts as President temporarily | Becomes President  |
| Duration   | Until new President elected   | For remaining term |
| Role       | Mainly Rajya Sabha Chairman   | Executive role     |


Thus:


➡ In India, the Vice-President **only acts as President temporarily**.


➡ In the USA, the Vice-President **becomes President for the remaining term**.


---

Therefore some scholars call the Vice-President:


**“His Superfluous Highness.”**


However, the office is important because it ensures:


➡ **Political continuity of the Indian State**
