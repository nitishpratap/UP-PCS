
# SUBORDINATE COURTS (STATE JUDICIARY)


## Meaning

- The **State Judiciary** consists of:
    - **High Court**
    - **Hierarchy of Subordinate Courts (Lower Courts)**
- Subordinate courts are called so because they are **subordinate to the High Court of the State**.
- These courts function:
    - **Below the High Court**
    - At **district and lower levels**.

---


# CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISIONS


The Constitution regulates subordinate courts through:


**Articles 233 – 237 (Part VI)**


These provisions ensure:

- **Organization of subordinate courts**
- **Independence of judiciary from executive**

---


# 1. Appointment of District Judges (Article 233)


### Authority


The **Governor of the State** appoints:

- District Judges
- Posts them
- Promotes them

But the Governor acts **in consultation with the High Court**.


### Qualifications for District Judge


A person must:

1. **Not be in service** of Central or State Government.
2. Must have been an **advocate or pleader for at least 7 years**.
3. Must be **recommended by the High Court**.

---


# 2. Appointment of Other Judges (Article 234)


Judges **below the rank of District Judge** are appointed by:


**Governor of the State**


After consultation with:

1. **State Public Service Commission**
2. **High Court**

### Recruitment Process (Practical System)


Usually:

- **State Public Service Commission conducts competitive exam** for recruitment to judicial service.

---


# 3. Control Over Subordinate Courts (Article 235)


The **High Court exercises control over subordinate judiciary**.


This includes control over:

- Posting
- Promotion
- Grant of leave
- Discipline

Applicable to:

- Persons belonging to **judicial service of the state**
- Holding posts **below the rank of District Judge**.

---


# 4. Interpretation of Terms (Article 236)


### Meaning of “District Judge”


The term **District Judge includes many judicial posts**, such as:

- Judge of City Civil Court
- Additional District Judge
- Joint District Judge
- Assistant District Judge
- Chief Judge of Small Cause Court
- Chief Presidency Magistrate
- Additional Chief Presidency Magistrate
- Sessions Judge
- Additional Sessions Judge
- Assistant Sessions Judge

### Meaning of “Judicial Service”


Judicial Service means:

> A service consisting exclusively of persons intended to fill the post of **district judge and other civil judicial posts inferior to it**.

---


# 5. Application of these Provisions to Magistrates (Article 237)


The **Governor may direct** that these constitutional provisions shall apply to:

- Any **class or classes of magistrates** in the state.

---


# STRUCTURE OF SUBORDINATE JUDICIARY


The **structure, jurisdiction and nomenclature** of subordinate courts are decided by **states**.


Hence:

- Slight variations exist between states.

But broadly there are **three tiers of courts below the High Court**.


---


# 1️⃣ DISTRICT & SESSIONS COURT (TOP LEVEL)


The **District Judge** is the **highest judicial authority in the district**.


### Jurisdiction


The District Judge exercises:

- **Original Jurisdiction**
- **Appellate Jurisdiction**

In both:

- **Civil cases**
- **Criminal cases**

### Dual Role


| When dealing with | Called         |
| ----------------- | -------------- |
| Civil cases       | District Judge |
| Criminal cases    | Sessions Judge |


---


### Powers of District Judge

1. Judicial powers
2. Administrative powers
3. Supervisory powers over all subordinate courts in district.

Appeals against decisions of district judge go to:


➡ **High Court**.


---


### Criminal Powers of Sessions Judge


A Sessions Judge can award:

- Any sentence
- Life imprisonment
- Death penalty

However:


⚠ **Death sentence must be confirmed by the High Court**, even if there is no appeal.


---


# 2️⃣ SUBORDINATE LEVEL COURTS


Below District Court there are two parallel streams:


| Civil Side                 | Criminal Side             |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Court of Subordinate Judge | Chief Judicial Magistrate |


---


## Subordinate Judge (Civil Court)

- Deals with **civil cases**
- Has **unlimited pecuniary jurisdiction** (can hear civil suits of any financial value).

Other names:

- Civil Judge (Senior Division)
- Civil Judge Class I

Sometimes they may also be given **powers of Assistant Sessions Judge**.


---


## Chief Judicial Magistrate (CJM)


Deals with **criminal cases** punishable with:


➡ **Imprisonment up to 7 years**.


---


# 3️⃣ LOWEST LEVEL COURTS


## On Civil Side


### Court of Munsiff

- Deals with **civil cases of small pecuniary value**
- Has **limited jurisdiction**.

Also known as:

- Civil Judge (Junior Division)
- Civil Judge Class II.

---


## On Criminal Side


### Judicial Magistrate


Deals with criminal cases punishable with:


➡ **Imprisonment up to 3 years**.


---


# SPECIAL COURTS IN SOME AREAS


## 1. Metropolitan Courts


In metropolitan cities:


Civil side:

- **City Civil Courts**

Criminal side:

- **Metropolitan Magistrates**.

---


## 2. Small Causes Courts


Established in some states and presidency towns.


They deal with:

- **Civil cases of small value**
- Decided through **summary procedure**

Their decisions are **final**.


However:


➡ **High Court has power of revision**.


---


## 3. Panchayat Courts


Some states have **village level courts** for petty disputes.


They are called:

- Nyaya Panchayat
- Gram Kutchery
- Adalati Panchayat
- Panchayat Adalat.

---

# NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY (NALSA)


## Constitutional Basis


### Article 39A (Directive Principles of State Policy)


The Constitution directs the State to:

- Provide **free legal aid** to poor and weaker sections.
- Ensure **justice for all**.

This article ensures that:

> Opportunities for securing justice are **not denied because of economic or other disabilities**.

---


## Other Constitutional Provisions Supporting Legal Aid


### Article 14

- Guarantees **equality before law**.

### Article 22(1)

- Guarantees **right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner**.

Together these provisions ensure a **fair legal system with equal opportunity for justice**.


---


# LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITIES ACT, 1987


To implement Article 39A, Parliament enacted:


➡ **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**


The Act came into force on:


📅 **9 November 1995**


Purpose:

- Establish a **nationwide network of legal aid institutions**
- Provide **free and competent legal services** to weaker sections.

---


# NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY (NALSA)


NALSA was established under the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**.


### Main Role


NALSA:

- Monitors implementation of legal aid programs.
- Lays down **policies and principles** for legal services.
- Ensures availability of **free legal aid across India**.

---


# ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE OF LEGAL AID SYSTEM


## 1️⃣ National Level


### National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)


Role:

- Policy formulation
- Monitoring and evaluation

---


## 2️⃣ State Level


### State Legal Services Authority (SLSA)


Established in **every state**.


Functions:

- Implement NALSA policies
- Provide legal services at state level.

---


## 3️⃣ High Court Level


### High Court Legal Services Committee


Role:

- Implement legal aid programs related to **High Courts**.

---


## 4️⃣ District Level


### District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)


Functions:

- Provide legal aid at district level.
- Conduct **Lok Adalats**.

---


## 5️⃣ Taluk Level


### Taluk Legal Services Committees


Purpose:

- Provide legal services at **sub-district level**.

---


## 6️⃣ Supreme Court Level


### Supreme Court Legal Services Committee


Purpose:

- Implement legal aid programs related to **Supreme Court cases**.

---


# FUNCTIONS OF LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITIES


The following functions are performed regularly:


### 1️⃣ Free Legal Services


Provide **free and competent legal services** to eligible persons.


### 2️⃣ Lok Adalats


Organize **Lok Adalats for amicable settlement of disputes**.


### 3️⃣ Legal Awareness


Conduct **legal awareness camps in rural areas**.


---


# WHAT DOES FREE LEGAL SERVICE INCLUDE?


Free legal services include:

1. Payment of **court fees and process fees**.
2. Payment of **all legal expenses** related to proceedings.
3. Providing **lawyers for legal proceedings**.
4. Obtaining **certified copies of orders and documents**.
5. Preparation of **appeals and paper books**.
6. **Printing and translation of documents**.

---


# PERSONS ELIGIBLE FOR FREE LEGAL SERVICES


The following categories are eligible:


1️⃣ **Women and children**


2️⃣ **SC/ST members**


3️⃣ **Industrial workmen**


4️⃣ **Victims of disasters**

- Mass disaster
- Violence
- Flood
- Drought
- Earthquake
- Industrial disaster

5️⃣ **Disabled persons**


6️⃣ **Persons in custody**


7️⃣ Persons with **annual income below ₹1 lakh**


(In Supreme Court Legal Services Committee limit = **₹1.25 lakh**)


8️⃣ **Victims of trafficking or begar**

# LOK ADALATS


## Meaning


Lok Adalat is a forum where:

- Cases **pending in court**
- Cases at **pre-litigation stage**

are **settled through compromise or amicable settlement**.


---


## Explanation by Supreme Court


The Supreme Court described Lok Adalat as:

- An **ancient Indian dispute resolution system**
- Still valid today.

Meaning:

> Lok Adalat = **People’s Court**

It is based on:


➡ **Gandhian principles**.


---


# Lok Adalat as ADR Mechanism


Lok Adalat is a part of:


➡ **ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution)** system.


Purpose:

- Provide **quick justice**
- Provide **cheap justice**
- Reduce burden of courts.

Because regular courts are:

- Slow
- Expensive
- Procedurally complex.

---


# Key Characteristics of Lok Adalat


1️⃣ Settlement through **negotiation and conciliation**.


2️⃣ Use of:

- Common sense
- Persuasive methods
- Human approach.

3️⃣ No winners or losers.


4️⃣ Parties settle disputes **amicably**.


5️⃣ Justice is **speedy and inexpensive**.


---


# STATUTORY STATUS OF LOK ADALATS


First Lok Adalat (post-independence):


📍 **Gujarat — 1982**


Initially:

- Lok Adalats had **no statutory backing**.

Due to popularity:


➡ **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**


gave **statutory status to Lok Adalats**.


---


# ORGANIZATION OF LOK ADALATS


Lok Adalats may be organized by:


1️⃣ State Legal Services Authority


2️⃣ District Legal Services Authority


3️⃣ Supreme Court Legal Services Committee


4️⃣ High Court Legal Services Committee


5️⃣ Taluk Legal Services Committee


---


# COMPOSITION OF LOK ADALAT


A Lok Adalat generally consists of:


1️⃣ **Judicial officer (Chairman)**


2️⃣ **Lawyer**


3️⃣ **Social worker**


---


# JURISDICTION OF LOK ADALAT


Lok Adalat can settle disputes relating to:


1️⃣ **Cases pending before courts**


2️⃣ **Disputes not yet filed in court (pre-litigation)**.


---


# TYPES OF CASES TAKEN BY LOK ADALAT


Examples include:

- Matrimonial disputes
- Family disputes
- Compoundable criminal offences
- Land acquisition cases
- Labour disputes
- Workmen compensation cases
- Bank recovery cases
- Pension disputes
- Housing board disputes
- Consumer disputes
- Electricity disputes
- Telephone bill disputes
- Municipal tax cases
- Disputes with telecom companies.

---


# CASES NOT ALLOWED


Lok Adalat **cannot deal with**:


➡ **Non-compoundable criminal offences**.


---


# REFERENCE OF CASE TO LOK ADALAT


A case may be referred if:


1️⃣ Both parties agree.


2️⃣ One party applies to court.


3️⃣ Court itself considers the case appropriate.


For **pre-litigation disputes**:

- Parties can directly apply to Lok Adalat.

---


# POWERS OF LOK ADALAT


Lok Adalat has powers similar to **Civil Court under CPC**:


1️⃣ Summoning witnesses


2️⃣ Enforcing attendance


3️⃣ Examining witnesses under oath


4️⃣ Discovery and production of documents


5️⃣ Receiving evidence on affidavits


6️⃣ Requisitioning public records.


---


### Legal Status of Proceedings

- Lok Adalat proceedings are **judicial proceedings** under **IPC**.
- Lok Adalat is deemed a **Civil Court for purposes of CrPC**.

---


# AWARD OF LOK ADALAT


Important facts for exams:


1️⃣ Award is **deemed decree of Civil Court**.


2️⃣ Award is **final and binding**.


3️⃣ **No appeal lies** against Lok Adalat award.


---


# BENEFITS OF LOK ADALAT


According to Supreme Court:


1️⃣ **No court fees**


If fees already paid → **refunded**.


2️⃣ **Speedy settlement**


3️⃣ **Flexible procedures**


4️⃣ No strict application of:

- Civil Procedure Code
- Evidence Act

5️⃣ Parties can interact with judges directly.


6️⃣ Award is final → avoids delay.


---


# ADVANTAGES OF ADR (LAW COMMISSION)


Law Commission summarized benefits of ADR:


1️⃣ Less expensive


2️⃣ Less time consuming


3️⃣ Free from technical procedures


4️⃣ Parties freely discuss disputes


5️⃣ No winner or loser — relationship preserved.


---


# PART 3 — PERMANENT LOK ADALATS


## Background


The **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** was amended in **2002** to establish:


➡ **Permanent Lok Adalats (PLAs)**


Purpose:

- To deal with disputes relating to **public utility services**.

---


# Reasons for Establishment


The following reasons led to the creation of Permanent Lok Adalats:


### 1. Implementation of Legal Aid


The **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** aims to:

- Provide **free legal services** to weaker sections.
- Ensure **equal opportunity in securing justice**.

---


### 2. Success of Lok Adalat System


Lok Adalats proved successful in:

- Resolving disputes through **conciliation**
- Providing **quick and inexpensive justice**.

---


### 3. Limitation of Existing Lok Adalats


The major drawback was:

- Lok Adalats could function **only when parties reached compromise**.

If compromise failed:

- The case returned to the court.

This caused **delay in justice**.


Therefore:


➡ Permanent Lok Adalats were given power to **decide cases on merits if settlement fails**.


---


### 4. Public Utility Service Disputes


Disputes related to public services require **urgent settlement**, such as:

- MTNL
- Delhi Vidyut Board
- Other service providers

Hence:

- Permanent Lok Adalats help resolve disputes **before litigation stage**.

This reduces burden on regular courts.


---


# FEATURES OF PERMANENT LOK ADALATS


### 1. Composition


Permanent Lok Adalat consists of:


1️⃣ **Chairman**

- Must be:
    - District Judge
    - Additional District Judge
    - Or a judicial officer higher than District Judge.

2️⃣ **Two other members**

- Must have **adequate experience in public utility services**.

---


### 2. Jurisdiction


Permanent Lok Adalat deals with disputes related to **public utility services**, such as:

- Transport services (air, road, water)
- Postal services
- Telegraph services
- Telephone services
- Electricity supply
- Water supply
- Public sanitation
- Hospitals or dispensaries
- Insurance services.

---


### 3. Pecuniary Jurisdiction


Maximum jurisdiction:


➡ **Up to ₹10 lakh**


However:


➡ **Central Government may increase this limit**.


---


### 4. Matters Not Covered


Permanent Lok Adalat **cannot deal with:**


➡ **Non-compoundable criminal offences**.


---


### 5. Pre-Litigation Mechanism


Before approaching court:

- A party can apply to **Permanent Lok Adalat**.

Once the application is filed:


⚠ The parties **cannot approach any court for the same dispute**.


---


### 6. Procedure


Step-1: PLA examines if **settlement is possible**


Step-2: If yes → proposes settlement terms.


Step-3: If parties agree → **PLA issues award**


Step-4: If parties disagree → **PLA decides case on merits**.


---


### 7. Finality of Award

- Award is **final and binding**
- Decision taken by **majority of members**.

---


# FAMILY COURTS


## Establishment


Family Courts were established under:


➡ **Family Courts Act, 1984**


Purpose:

- Promote **conciliation**
- Ensure **speedy settlement of family disputes**.

---


# Reasons for Establishment


### 1. Demand from Society


Women's organizations and social groups demanded:

- Special courts for family disputes
- Focus on **conciliation instead of litigation**.

---


### 2. Law Commission Recommendation


The **59th Law Commission Report (1974)** recommended:

- Courts should adopt a **different approach in family disputes**.

The CPC was amended in **1976** to allow special procedure.


But courts continued using **traditional adversarial approach**.


Hence the need for **Family Courts**.


---


# Objectives of Family Courts


1️⃣ Establish **specialized courts for family matters**


2️⃣ Promote **conciliation between parties**


3️⃣ Provide **cheap and quick remedy**


4️⃣ Maintain **informal and flexible proceedings**.


---


# FEATURES OF FAMILY COURTS


### 1. Establishment Authority


Family Courts are established by:


➡ **State Governments**


in consultation with:


➡ **High Courts**.


---


### 2. Mandatory Establishment


Family Courts must be established in:


➡ Cities or towns with **population above 1 million**.


---


### 3. Optional Establishment


State Governments may establish Family Courts in:


➡ Any other area if necessary.


---


# Jurisdiction of Family Courts


Family Courts deal with matters related to:


### 1️⃣ Matrimonial Relief


Includes:

- Nullity of marriage
- Judicial separation
- Divorce
- Restitution of conjugal rights
- Validity of marriage
- Matrimonial status.

---


### 2️⃣ Property Disputes Between Spouses

- Property of husband and wife.

---


### 3️⃣ Legitimacy of Person

- Declaration regarding legitimacy.

---


### 4️⃣ Guardianship and Custody

- Custody of minors.

---


### 5️⃣ Maintenance Cases

- Maintenance of:
    - Wife
    - Children
    - Parents.

---


# Procedure in Family Courts


### 1. Reconciliation First


Family Court must first try to:


➡ **Reconcile the parties**


before starting the trial.


---


### 2. Informal Proceedings


During reconciliation:

- Proceedings are **informal**
- Strict legal procedures **do not apply**.

---


### 3. Assistance of Experts


Family Courts may use:

- Social welfare agencies
- Counsellors
- Medical experts
- Welfare experts.

---


### 4. Legal Representation


Parties are **not entitled as a matter of right** to have lawyers.


However:


Court may appoint a legal expert as **amicus curiae** if necessary.


---


### 5. Simplified Evidence Rules


Evidence and procedure rules are **simplified**.


---


### 6. Appeal


Only **one appeal** is allowed:


➡ Appeal lies to **High Court**.


---

# GRAM NYAYALAYAS


## Establishment


Established under:


➡ **Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008**


Purpose:

- Provide **justice at grassroots level**
- Ensure **justice reaches citizens at their doorstep**.

---


# Reasons for Establishment


### 1. Access to Justice


Despite reforms, **poor and disadvantaged people still face difficulty accessing courts**.


Article **39A** directs the State to:

- Provide equal access to justice.

---


### 2. Judicial Reforms


Government introduced several measures:

- Simplification of procedural laws
- ADR mechanisms
- Lok Adalats.

Gram Nyayalayas strengthen these efforts.


---


### 3. Law Commission Recommendation


The **114th Law Commission Report** recommended:


➡ Establishment of Gram Nyayalayas.


---


### 4. Rural Justice


Goal:

- Provide **speedy and affordable justice in rural areas**.

---


# FEATURES OF GRAM NYAYALAYAS


### 1. Nature of Court


Gram Nyayalaya is:


➡ **Court of Judicial Magistrate First Class**


Presiding officer:


➡ **Nyayadhikari**


Appointed by:


➡ **State Government in consultation with High Court**.


---


### 2. Area of Jurisdiction


Established for:

- Every **intermediate Panchayat**
- Or group of contiguous Panchayats.

---


### 3. Status of Nyayadhikari


Nyayadhikari:

- Is a **judicial officer**
- Has powers of **First Class Magistrate**.

---


### 4. Mobile Court


Gram Nyayalaya acts as:


➡ **Mobile Court**


It travels to villages to hear cases.


---


### 5. Civil and Criminal Jurisdiction


Gram Nyayalaya exercises powers of:

- Civil Court
- Criminal Court.

---


### 6. Types of Cases


Gram Nyayalaya hears cases listed in:

- **First Schedule**
- **Second Schedule** of the Act.

---


### 7. Amendment Powers


Both:

- **Central Government**
- **State Governments**

can amend schedules.


---


### 8. Criminal Procedure


Criminal trials follow:


➡ **Summary Procedure**


---


### 9. Civil Procedure


Special procedure provided under the Act.


---


### 10. Conciliation


Court must attempt:


➡ **Settlement through conciliation**.


Conciliators may be appointed.


---


### 11. Judgment Status


Orders of Gram Nyayalaya are treated as:


➡ **Decree of Civil Court**.


Execution follows **summary procedure**.


---


### 12. Evidence Rules


Gram Nyayalaya:

- Not bound by **Indian Evidence Act 1872**
- Guided by **principles of natural justice**.

---


### 13. Appeals


Criminal cases:


➡ Appeal to **Court of Session**


Civil cases:


➡ Appeal to **District Court**


Both appeals must be disposed within:


➡ **6 months**.


---


### 14. Plea Bargaining


Accused may apply for:


➡ **Plea bargaining**.


---

# PROBLEMS IN IMPLEMENTATION


Issues affecting Gram Nyayalayas include:


1️⃣ Reluctance of police officials


2️⃣ Lukewarm response from lawyers


3️⃣ Lack of notaries and stamp vendors


4️⃣ Concurrent jurisdiction with regular courts.

- Chief Justices of High Courts
- Chief Ministers of States

➡ Establish Gram Nyayalayas mainly in **Talukas without regular courts**.
