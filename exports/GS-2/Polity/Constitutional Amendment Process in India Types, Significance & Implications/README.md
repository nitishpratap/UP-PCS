
# Amendment of the Constitution (Article 368)


## 1. Meaning and Nature of Constitutional Amendment


The **Constitution of India provides for its amendment** to adjust itself to **changing conditions and needs of society**.


Unlike some constitutions, the amendment process in India is **neither extremely flexible nor extremely rigid**.

- **Britain** → Very flexible (simple law can change constitution)
- **USA** → Very rigid (very difficult amendment process)
- **India** → **Combination of flexibility and rigidity**

Thus, the Indian Constitution is described as **a synthesis of rigidity and flexibility**.


---


# Article 368 – Power and Procedure of Amendment


**Article 368 (Part XX)** deals with:

1. **Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution**
2. **Procedure for amendment**

Parliament can amend the Constitution by:

- **Addition**
- **Variation**
- **Repeal**

However, Parliament **cannot amend the Basic Structure of the Constitution**.


This principle was established by the Supreme Court in:

- **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**.

---


# 2. Procedure for Amendment of the Constitution


The **procedure under Article 368** consists of several steps.


### Step 1: Introduction of the Bill

- Amendment can be initiated **only by introducing a bill in Parliament**.
- It **cannot be introduced in state legislatures**.

### Step 2: Who can introduce the Bill


The bill may be introduced by:

- **A Minister**, or
- **A Private Member**

No **prior permission of the President** is required.


---


### Step 3: Special Majority in Parliament


The bill must be passed in **each House separately** with a **Special Majority**.


Special majority means:

1. **Majority of the total membership of the House**, and
2. **Two-thirds majority of members present and voting**.

Meaning of **Total Membership**:

- Includes **all members of the House**
- Even if there are **vacancies or absentees**.

---


### Step 4: Separate Passage in Both Houses

- Each House must **pass the bill separately**.
- If there is disagreement between the Houses:

**No joint sitting is allowed** for Constitutional Amendment Bills.


---


### Step 5: Ratification by States (If Required)


If the amendment relates to **federal provisions**, then:

- It must be ratified by **half of the State Legislatures**.
- Ratification is done by **simple majority**.

Meaning:

- Majority of **members present and voting**.

Important points:

- If remaining states do not take action → it **does not matter**
- Once **half the states approve**, requirement is complete
- **No time limit** for state ratification

---


### Step 6: President’s Assent


After passing by Parliament and ratification by states (if required):


The bill is presented to the **President**.


The President:

- **Must give assent**
- **Cannot withhold assent**
- **Cannot return the bill** for reconsideration.

Important Amendment:

- **24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971**
- Made **President’s assent mandatory**.

---


### Step 7: Amendment Becomes Law


After the President gives assent:

- Bill becomes a **Constitutional Amendment Act**
- Constitution stands **amended accordingly**.

---


# 3. Types of Constitutional Amendments


Though Article 368 mentions two types, **the Constitution actually allows three types** of amendments.


Thus amendments occur through:


1️⃣ **Simple Majority of Parliament**


2️⃣ **Special Majority of Parliament**


3️⃣ **Special Majority + Ratification by States**


---


# 4. Amendment by Simple Majority of Parliament


Some provisions can be amended by **simple majority of Parliament**.


This process is **similar to passing an ordinary law**.


Important:


These amendments are **not considered constitutional amendments under Article 368**.


---


## Provisions Amended by Simple Majority


### 1. Creation and alteration of states

- Admission or establishment of new states
- Formation of new states
- Alteration of boundaries
- Change of names of states

---


### 2. Legislative Councils in States

- Creation or abolition of **Legislative Councils**

---


### 3. Second Schedule


Deals with:

- Emoluments
- Allowances
- Privileges

Of:

- President
- Governors
- Speakers
- Judges etc.

---


### 4. Parliament related matters

- Quorum in Parliament
- Salaries and allowances of MPs
- Rules of procedure in Parliament
- Parliamentary privileges

---


### 5. Language matters

- Use of **English language in Parliament**
- Use of **official language**

---


### 6. Supreme Court matters

- Number of **Puisne Judges** in the Supreme Court
- Conferment of **additional jurisdiction** to the Supreme Court

---


### 7. Citizenship


Matters related to:

- Acquisition of citizenship
- Termination of citizenship

---


### 8. Electoral matters

- Elections to Parliament and State Legislatures
- Delimitation of constituencies

---


### 9. Union Territories


Provisions related to **Union Territories**


---


### 10. Fifth Schedule


Administration of:

- **Scheduled Areas**
- **Scheduled Tribes**

---


### 11. Sixth Schedule


Administration of **Tribal Areas**.


---


# 5. Amendment by Special Majority of Parliament


Most provisions of the Constitution are amended through **Special Majority of Parliament**.


Meaning:

- Majority of **total membership**
- Plus **two-thirds majority of members present and voting**.

---


## Provisions amended by Special Majority


Includes:

1. **Fundamental Rights**
2. **Directive Principles of State Policy**
3. All other provisions not included in other categories.

---


# 6. Amendment by Special Majority + State Ratification


Certain provisions related to **federal structure** require:

- Special majority in Parliament
- Plus ratification by **half of state legislatures**.

---


## Provisions requiring State Ratification


### 1. Election of the President

- Method of election

### 2. Executive powers

- Extent of **executive power of Union and States**

### 3. Judiciary

- **Supreme Court**
- **High Courts**

### 4. Legislative powers

- Distribution of powers between **Union and States**

### 5. Seventh Schedule

- Union List
- State List
- Concurrent List

### 6. Representation of states

- Representation in **Parliament**

### 7. Amendment procedure itself

- **Article 368**.

---


# 7. Criticism of the Amendment Procedure


Scholars and critics have pointed out several weaknesses.


---


## 1. No Special Constitutional Body


Unlike USA:

- India does not have **Constitutional Convention** or **Constitutional Assembly** for amendments.

Amendment power lies mainly with **Parliament**.


---


## 2. States cannot initiate amendments


In India:

- Only **Parliament can initiate amendments**.

Exception:


States can pass resolution requesting:

- Creation or abolition of **Legislative Councils**.

But Parliament may:

- Accept
- Reject
- Ignore the request.

---


## 3. Parliament dominates amendment process


Most provisions can be amended by:

- **Simple majority**
- **Special majority**

States’ consent required only in **few cases**.


In USA → **3/4 states required**.


---


## 4. No time limit for state ratification


Constitution does not specify:

- Time limit for ratification
- Whether states can withdraw approval later.

---


## 5. No joint sitting


If both Houses disagree:

- **Joint sitting not allowed**

But joint sitting exists for **ordinary bills**.


---


## 6. Similar to legislative process


Except for **special majority**, amendment process is similar to passing ordinary legislation.


---


## 7. Provisions are sketchy


The amendment provisions are **not very detailed**.


Hence, many issues go to **judiciary interpretation**.


---


# 8. Evaluation of the Amendment Process


Despite criticism, scholars consider the amendment process **balanced and effective**.


### K.C. Wheare


The process **balances flexibility and rigidity**.


---


### Jawaharlal Nehru


In the Constituent Assembly:


A constitution must be **flexible enough for national growth** and changing needs.


---


### Dr B.R. Ambedkar


He stated:


The Constitution avoids extreme rigidity and provides **a flexible amendment procedure**.


---


### Granville Austin


He called the amendment process:


**One of the most ably conceived aspects of the Constitution**.


---


# 9. Key Case Law


### Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)


Supreme Court held:

- Parliament **can amend the Constitution**
- But **cannot alter the Basic Structure**.

Examples of Basic Structure:

- Supremacy of Constitution
- Rule of law
- Judicial review
- Separation of powers
- Federalism

---


# 10. Important Constitutional Amendment


### 24th Constitutional Amendment Act (1971)


Provisions:

- **Restored Parliament’s power to amend Fundamental Rights**
    - It declared that **Parliament has the power to amend any part of the Constitution**, including **Part III (Fundamental Rights)**.
- **Amended Article 368**
    - Clearly stated that **Parliament can amend the Constitution using its constituent power**.
- **Made Presidential assent mandatory**
    - The **President cannot refuse assent** to a Constitutional Amendment Bill passed by Parliament.
- **Amended Article 13**
    - Added that **“law” under Article 13 does not include Constitutional Amendments**, meaning amendments cannot be challenged for violating Fundamental Rights.
- **Strengthened Parliament after Golaknath judgment**
    - Ensured Parliament could amend the Constitution even if the amendment affects Fundamental Rights.
