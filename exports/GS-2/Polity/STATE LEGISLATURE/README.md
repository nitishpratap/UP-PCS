# 1. ORGANISATION OF STATE LEGISLATURE


There is **no uniform structure** of state legislatures in India.


Two types exist:


### 1️⃣ Unicameral Legislature


Only **one house**


Structure:


```plain text
Governor + Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)
```


Most states follow this system.


---


### 2️⃣ Bicameral Legislature


Two houses


Structure:


```plain text
Governor
│
├─ Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) → Lower House
└─ Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) → Upper House
```


---


## States with Legislative Council (Bicameral)


At present only **7 states** have bicameral legislature:

1. Andhra Pradesh
2. Telangana
3. Uttar Pradesh
4. Bihar
5. Maharashtra
6. Karnataka
7. Jammu & Kashmir (earlier under its own constitution)

---


## Why Legislative Council was criticised?


In the **Constituent Assembly**, many members opposed it because:

1. Not representative of people
2. Delays legislation
3. Expensive institution

Therefore Constitution allowed **flexibility**.


---


# Creation and Abolition of Legislative Council


The Constitution allows states to create or abolish it.


### Procedure


Step 1


State Legislative Assembly passes a **special resolution**


Special Majority required:


```plain text
Majority of total membership
+
2/3 of members present and voting
```


Step 2


Parliament passes a law to create or abolish the council.


Important:


This **is NOT a constitutional amendment** under Article 368.


It is passed by **simple majority**.


---


# Examples of Creation and Abolition


### Andhra Pradesh

- Council created → **1957**
- Abolished → **1985**
- Revived → **2007**

---


### Tamil Nadu

- Council abolished → **1986**
- Resolution to revive → **2010**
- Later resolution to abolish again → **2011**

---


### Punjab and West Bengal


Councils abolished in **1969**

# DURATION OF LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY (VIDHAN SABHA)


## Nature


Like the **Lok Sabha**, the Legislative Assembly is **not a continuing chamber**.


---


## Normal Term


```plain text
5 years
```


The term begins from:


```plain text
Date of its first meeting after the general elections
```


After five years:


```plain text
Automatic dissolution occurs
```


---


## Early Dissolution


The **Governor** can dissolve the Assembly **before 5 years**.


Reason:

- Loss of majority
- Political instability
- Fresh elections required

---


## Extension During National Emergency


During **National Emergency (Article 352)**:


Parliament can extend Assembly term.


Extension rule:


```plain text
1 year at a time
```


But there is a limit.


Maximum extension:


```plain text
Only up to 6 months after the emergency ends
```


Meaning:


Assembly must be re-elected **within 6 months after emergency ends**.


---


# DURATION OF LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL (VIDHAN PARISHAD)


The Legislative Council works like **Rajya Sabha**.


It is:


```plain text
Permanent body
```

Rule:


```plain text
1/3 members retire every 2 years
```


Term of a member:


```plain text
6 years
```


---


## Filling Vacancies


When members retire:

- New elections are conducted
- Governor nominates new members

Retiring members can:



If a person sits or votes without qualification:


Penalty:


```plain text
₹500 per day
```


Applies when:


1️⃣ Oath not taken


2️⃣ Person is disqualified


3️⃣ Person prohibited by law


---


# Salaries of Members


Members receive:


```plain text
Salary and allowances
```


Decided by:


```plain text
State Legislature
```


---


# 6. VACATION OF SEATS


A member's seat becomes vacant under several situations.


---


# 1️⃣ Double Membership


A person **cannot be member of both houses** of state legislature simultaneously.


If elected to both:


He must choose one.


Otherwise:


One seat becomes vacant.


---


# 2️⃣ Disqualification


If member becomes disqualified under any law.


Seat automatically becomes vacant.


---


# 3️⃣ Resignation


Member may resign by writing to:


| House                | Resignation to |
| -------------------- | -------------- |
| Legislative Assembly | Speaker        |
| Legislative Council  | Chairman       |


Seat becomes vacant **after acceptance**.


Speaker/Chairman may reject resignation if:


```plain text
Not voluntary or genuine
```


---


# 4️⃣ Absence


If member is absent:


```plain text
60 days without permission
```


House may declare seat vacant.


---


# 5️⃣ Other Situations


Seat becomes vacant if:


1️⃣ Election declared void by court


2️⃣ Member expelled by House


3️⃣ Member elected as President or Vice President


4️⃣ Member appointed Governor of a state


---


# 7. PRESIDING OFFICERS OF STATE LEGISLATURE


Each House of the State Legislature has its **own presiding officers**.


| House                | Presiding Officers         |
| -------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Legislative Assembly | Speaker & Deputy Speaker   |
| Legislative Council  | Chairman & Deputy Chairman |


In addition:


| House                | Panel                  |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Legislative Assembly | Panel of Chairmen      |
| Legislative Council  | Panel of Vice-Chairmen |


These officers regulate the functioning of the legislature.

# SPEAKER OF LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY


The **Speaker** is the most important officer of the Legislative Assembly.


---


## Election


The Speaker is:


```plain text
Elected by the Legislative Assembly
```


From:


```plain text
Among its own members
```


---


## Tenure


Normally the Speaker remains in office:


```plain text
During the life of the Assembly
```


However, he may vacate office earlier.


---


## Speaker Vacates Office When


### 1️⃣ Ceases to be Member


If he is no longer a member of the Assembly.


---


### 2️⃣ Resignation


He can resign by writing to:


```plain text
Deputy Speaker
```


---


### 3️⃣ Removal


Speaker may be removed by:


```plain text
Resolution passed by majority of all the then members of the Assembly
```


Important condition:


```plain text
14 days advance notice required
```


---


# POWERS AND DUTIES OF SPEAKER


The Speaker has extensive powers.


---


## 1️⃣ Maintains Order


Speaker maintains:

- Order
- Decorum
- Discipline

During Assembly proceedings.


This is **his primary responsibility**.


His decision is **final** in this matter.


---


## 2️⃣ Final Interpreter


Speaker interprets:

1. Constitution provisions
2. Rules of procedure
3. Legislative precedents

Within the Assembly.


---


## 3️⃣ Adjournment Due to Lack of Quorum


If quorum is not present:


Speaker may:


```plain text
Adjourn Assembly
or
Suspend meeting
```


---


## 4️⃣ Casting Vote


Speaker does **not vote initially**.


But if votes are equal:


Speaker exercises:


```plain text
Casting vote
```


---


## 5️⃣ Secret Sitting


Speaker may allow:


```plain text
Secret sitting of the House
```


If requested by:


```plain text
Leader of the House
```


---


## 6️⃣ Decides Money Bill


Speaker decides whether a bill is a:


```plain text
Money Bill
```


His decision is:


```plain text
Final
```


---


## 7️⃣ Decides Defection Cases


Speaker decides disqualification cases under:


```plain text
Tenth Schedule
```


(anti-defection law)


---


## 8️⃣ Committee Powers


Speaker:

- Appoints chairmen of Assembly committees
- Supervises committee functioning

Speaker is chairman of:

1. Business Advisory Committee
2. Rules Committee
3. General Purpose Committee

---


# DEPUTY SPEAKER OF ASSEMBLY


Deputy Speaker is also:


```plain text
Elected by Legislative Assembly
```


From its members.


Election takes place **after election of Speaker**.

## Tenure


Usually holds office:

```plain text
During life of Assembly
```


---


## Deputy Speaker Vacates Office


Same conditions as Speaker:

1. Ceases to be member
2. Resignation to Speaker

Again:

```plain text
14 days notice required
```

---


## Functions


Deputy Speaker performs Speaker's duties:


1️⃣ When Speaker's office is vacant


2️⃣ When Speaker is absent from sitting


During this time:


Deputy Speaker has:


```plain text
All powers of Speaker
```


---


# PANEL OF CHAIRMEN


Speaker nominates a **Panel of Chairmen**.


Purpose:


To preside over Assembly when:

- Speaker absent
- Deputy Speaker absent

Panel member presiding has **same powers as Speaker**.


They hold office until **new panel is nominated**.


---


# CHAIRMAN OF LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL

---


## Election


Chairman is:

```plain text
Elected by the Legislative Council
```

From:

```plain text
Among its own members
```

---


## Chairman Vacates Office


1️⃣ Ceases to be member

Condition:

```plain text
14 days notice required
```

## Powers of Chairman

```plain text
Decision on Money Bill
```

```plain text
Fixed by State Legislature
```

```plain text
14 days
```


---


## Functions


Deputy Chairman performs duties:


1️⃣ When Chairman's office vacant


2️⃣ When Chairman absent


In both situations:


Deputy Chairman has **same powers as Chairman**.

# PANEL OF VICE-CHAIRMEN

- Chairman absent

# 8. SESSIONS OF STATE LEGISLATURE


The working of the legislature is divided into **sessions**.


---


# SUMMONING


The power to summon the legislature lies with:


```plain text
Governor
```


Governor summons:


```plain text
Each House of the Legislature
```


---


## Maximum Gap Between Sessions


The Constitution states:


```plain text
Maximum gap = 6 months
```


Meaning:


Legislature must meet **at least twice a year**.


---


## Session Meaning


A **session** consists of:


```plain text
Many sittings of the House
```


---


# ADJOURNMENT


Adjournment means:


```plain text
Suspension of sitting for a specified time
```


It may be for:

- Hours
- Days
- Weeks

---


# ADJOURNMENT SINE DIE


Meaning:


```plain text
Termination of sitting for an indefinite period
```


Power lies with:


```plain text
Presiding officer
```


(Speaker or Chairman)


---


# PROROGATION


After adjournment sine die:


Governor issues notification for:


```plain text
Prorogation
```


Meaning:


```plain text
Termination of session
```


Difference:


| Term        | Meaning      |
| ----------- | ------------ |
| Adjournment | Ends sitting |
| Prorogation | Ends session |


Governor may also prorogue **while House is in session**.


---


# DISSOLUTION


Dissolution applies only to:


```plain text
Legislative Assembly
```


Because:


Council is a **permanent house**.


---


## Meaning


Dissolution means:


```plain text
End of life of Assembly
```


New Assembly is formed after **general elections**.


---


# EFFECT OF DISSOLUTION ON BILLS


Important for exams.


### Bills that Lapse


1️⃣ Bill pending in Assembly lapses


2️⃣ Bill passed by Assembly but pending in Council lapses


---


### Bills that DO NOT Lapse


3️⃣ Bill pending in Council but not passed by Assembly does not lapse


4️⃣ Bill passed by both Houses but pending Governor/President assent does not lapse


5️⃣ Bill returned by President for reconsideration does not lapse


---


# QUORUM


Quorum means:


```plain text
Minimum number of members required to conduct business
```


Rule:


```plain text
1/10 of total membership
or
10 members
whichever is greater
```


Includes:


```plain text
Presiding officer
```


---


## If Quorum Not Present


Presiding officer must:


```plain text
Adjourn House
or
Suspend meeting
```


Until quorum is achieved.


---


# VOTING IN HOUSE


Decisions are taken by:


```plain text
Majority of members present and voting
```


Presiding officer:


```plain text
Does not vote initially
```


But may use:


```plain text
Casting vote in case of tie
```


---


## Special Majority Required In Some Cases


Examples:

- Removal of Speaker
- Removal of Chairman

---


# LANGUAGE IN STATE LEGISLATURE


Languages allowed:


1️⃣ Official language of the state


2️⃣ Hindi


3️⃣ English


---


## Exception


Presiding officer may allow member to speak in:


```plain text
Mother tongue
```


---


## Continuation of English


Legislature may decide whether English should continue after:


```plain text
15 years from Constitution (1965)
```


Special cases:


| States                                        | Limit    |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Tripura | 25 years |
| Arunachal Pradesh, Goa, Mizoram               | 40 years |


---


# RIGHTS OF MINISTERS & ADVOCATE GENERAL


Even if they are not members of the House:


They can:


```plain text
Speak
Participate in proceedings
Participate in committees
```


But they **cannot vote**.


---


## Two Important Reasons


1️⃣ A minister can participate in proceedings of a House **of which he is not a member**.


2️⃣ A minister who is **not member of either House** can still participate in both Houses.


However:


A minister must become member of legislature within:


```plain text
6 months
```


Otherwise he must resign.

# 9. LEGISLATIVE PROCEDURE IN STATE LEGISLATURE


Legislative procedure deals with **how a bill becomes a law**.


Two main types of bills:


| Type           | Meaning             |
| -------------- | ------------------- |
| Ordinary Bills | Non-financial bills |
| Money Bills    | Financial matters   |


---


# ORDINARY BILLS


---


# INTRODUCTION OF ORDINARY BILL


An ordinary bill can be introduced in:


```plain text
Either House of the State Legislature
```


(Only in bicameral states).


---


## Who can introduce?


Two possibilities:


1️⃣ Minister


2️⃣ Private member (non-minister)


---


# STAGES OF ORDINARY BILL


The bill passes through **three stages**.


### 1️⃣ First Reading


Bill is introduced.


Purpose:

- Formal introduction
- No detailed discussion

---


### 2️⃣ Second Reading


This is the **most important stage**.


Activities include:

- Detailed discussion
- Clause-by-clause consideration
- Amendments proposed

---


### 3️⃣ Third Reading


Final discussion and voting.


If passed:


```plain text
Bill is transmitted to the second House
```


---


# PASSAGE IN SECOND HOUSE


The second House also goes through:

- First Reading
- Second Reading
- Third Reading

---


# POSSIBLE ACTIONS BY LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL


When a bill comes from the Assembly, the Council has **four options**.


---


### 1️⃣ Pass the Bill


Council may pass the bill **without amendment**.


Result:


```plain text
Bill goes to Governor
```


---


### 2️⃣ Pass with Amendments


Council may return the bill with amendments.


Assembly may:

- Accept amendments
- Reject amendments

---


### 3️⃣ Reject the Bill


Council may reject the bill completely.


---


### 4️⃣ Keep the Bill Pending


Council may **take no action**.


---


# WHAT HAPPENS IF COUNCIL DELAYS BILL


If:

- Council rejects bill
- Council suggests unacceptable amendments
- Council does not act for **3 months**

Then:


Assembly can **pass the bill again**.


---


# SECOND TIME PASSAGE


If Assembly passes the bill again and sends it to Council:


Council has **1 month**.


If within one month Council:

- Rejects bill
- Suggests unacceptable amendments
- Takes no action

Then:


```plain text
Bill is deemed passed by Legislature
```


In the form passed by Assembly the second time.


---


# MAXIMUM DELAY BY COUNCIL


The Council can delay a bill only:


```plain text
3 months + 1 month = 4 months
```


Thus Assembly has **ultimate authority**.


---


# NO JOINT SITTING


Unlike Parliament:


```plain text
No provision for joint sitting in State Legislature
```


Deadlock resolved by **Assembly supremacy**.


---


# SPECIAL CASE


If a bill originates in the Council and the Assembly rejects it:


```plain text
Bill ends (becomes dead)
```


This further shows Assembly dominance.


---


# GOVERNOR'S ASSENT


After passing by Legislature:


Bill goes to **Governor**.


Governor has **four options**.


---


# OPTION 1 — GIVE ASSENT


Governor signs bill.


Result:


```plain text
Bill becomes an Act
```


---


# OPTION 2 — WITHHOLD ASSENT


Governor refuses assent.


Result:


```plain text
Bill ends
```


---


# OPTION 3 — RETURN BILL


Governor may return bill for reconsideration.


Assembly can:

- Pass again with amendments
- Pass again without amendments

If bill returned again to Governor:


```plain text
Governor must give assent
```


This power is called:


```plain text
Suspensive veto
```


---


# OPTION 4 — RESERVE FOR PRESIDENT


Governor may reserve the bill for:


```plain text
President's consideration
```


---


# PRESIDENT'S ASSENT


If bill reserved for President:


President has **three options**.


1️⃣ Give assent


2️⃣ Withhold assent


3️⃣ Return bill for reconsideration


---


# RECONSIDERATION RULE


If President returns bill:


Legislature must reconsider within:


```plain text
6 months
```


After reconsideration bill goes again to President.


Important:


```plain text
Constitution does not require President to give assent
```


Even after reconsideration.


---


# MONEY BILLS


Money Bills deal with **financial matters**.


Special procedure exists.


---


# INTRODUCTION OF MONEY BILL


Money Bill can be introduced:


```plain text
Only in Legislative Assembly
```


Not in Legislative Council.


---


# GOVERNOR'S RECOMMENDATION


Money Bill requires:


```plain text
Prior recommendation of Governor
```


---


# WHO CAN INTRODUCE


Money Bill can be introduced only by:


```plain text
Minister
```


Private members **cannot introduce Money Bills**.


---


# AFTER PASSAGE IN ASSEMBLY


Once Assembly passes Money Bill:


Bill is sent to **Legislative Council**.


---


# POWERS OF LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL


Council has **very limited powers**.


Council **cannot**:

- Reject bill
- Amend bill

Council can only:


```plain text
Make recommendations
```


---


# TIME LIMIT


Council must return the bill within:


```plain text
14 days
```


---


# ACTION BY ASSEMBLY


Assembly may:


1️⃣ Accept recommendations


2️⃣ Reject recommendations


---


# IF RECOMMENDATIONS ACCEPTED


Bill passes in **modified form**.


---


# IF RECOMMENDATIONS REJECTED


Bill passes in **original form**.


---


# IF COUNCIL DOES NOT RETURN BILL


If Council does not return bill within **14 days**:


```plain text
Bill deemed passed automatically
```


---


# ROLE OF GOVERNOR IN MONEY BILL


Governor may:


1️⃣ Give assent


2️⃣ Withhold assent


3️⃣ Reserve for President


Governor **cannot return money bill**.


---


# PRESIDENT'S ROLE


If money bill reserved for President:


President may:

- Give assent
- Withhold assent

President **cannot return the money bill**.


---


# IMPORTANT EXAM SUMMARY


### Assembly vs Council (Money Bill)


| Power                | Assembly | Council |
| -------------------- | -------- | ------- |
| Introduce Money Bill | Yes      | No      |
| Amend Bill           | Yes      | No      |
| Reject Bill          | Yes      | No      |
| Delay Bill           | —        | 14 days |


Thus Assembly dominates financial matters.


---


# 10. POSITION OF LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL


The constitutional position of the **Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)** is weaker compared to the **Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)**.


Its status can be understood from **two perspectives**:


1️⃣ Areas where Council is **equal to Assembly**


2️⃣ Areas where Council is **inferior to Assembly**


---


# A. SPHERES WHERE COUNCIL IS EQUAL TO ASSEMBLY


In some matters both houses have **almost equal powers**.


---


## 1. Ordinary Bills


Both Houses participate in:

- Introduction
- Discussion
- Passage

However:


If disagreement occurs, **Assembly's decision prevails**.


---


## 2. Approval of Ordinances


Both Houses must approve:


```plain text
Ordinances issued by Governor
```


---


## 3. Selection of Ministers


Ministers including **Chief Minister** can be members of:


```plain text
Either House
```


But:


Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible only to Assembly**.


---


## 4. Reports of Constitutional Bodies


Both Houses examine reports of:

- State Finance Commission
- State Public Service Commission
- Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)

---


## 5. Enlargement of State Public Service Commission Jurisdiction


Both Houses participate in legislation related to:


```plain text
Expansion of State PSC jurisdiction
```


---


# B. SPHERES WHERE COUNCIL IS INFERIOR TO ASSEMBLY


The Assembly clearly dominates the Council in many areas.


---


# 1. Money Bills


Money Bills can be introduced only in:


```plain text
Legislative Assembly
```


Council cannot introduce them.


---


# 2. Limited Powers over Money Bills


Council cannot:

- Reject
- Amend

It can only **recommend changes**.


---


# 3. Assembly Final Authority


Assembly may:

- Accept recommendations
- Reject recommendations

In both cases bill is considered passed.


---


# 4. Speaker Decides Money Bill


Final authority to decide whether a bill is Money Bill lies with:


```plain text
Speaker of Legislative Assembly
```


---


# 5. Ordinary Bill Supremacy


Assembly ultimately decides ordinary bills.


Council can delay bill only:


```plain text
Maximum 4 months
```


(3 months + 1 month)


---


# 6. Budget Powers


Council may:


```plain text
Discuss budget
```


But it cannot:


```plain text
Vote on demands for grants
```


This power belongs only to Assembly.


---


# 7. No Confidence Motion


Council **cannot remove government**.


Because:


Council of Ministers is responsible only to:


```plain text
Legislative Assembly
```


---


# 8. Bill Originating in Council


If a bill originates in Council and Assembly rejects it:


```plain text
Bill becomes dead
```


---


# 9. Election of President


Council members do **not participate** in:


```plain text
Election of President
```


---


# 10. Election of Rajya Sabha Members


Council has **no role** in electing:


```plain text
State representatives to Rajya Sabha
```


---


# 11. Constitutional Amendment Ratification


Council has **no effective role** in ratifying constitutional amendments.


Assembly's will **prevails**.


---


# 12. Existence Depends on Assembly


Council exists only if:


```plain text
Assembly recommends it
```


Parliament may abolish Council based on Assembly resolution.


---


# COMPARISON WITH RAJYA SABHA


Legislative Council is **weaker than Rajya Sabha**.


Reasons include:


---


## 1. Federal Role


Rajya Sabha represents:


```plain text
States in the Union
```


It protects federal balance.


Council does not perform such function.


---


## 2. Composition


Rajya Sabha is **homogeneous**.


It consists mainly of **elected representatives of states**.


Council is **heterogeneous**.


Members come from different groups:

- Teachers
- Graduates
- Local bodies
- Nominees

This weakens its authority.


---


## 3. Democratic Principle


Assembly represents the **direct will of people**.


Therefore Council must yield to Assembly.


This principle is derived from:


```plain text
British parliamentary system
```


Where:


House of Commons dominates House of Lords.


---


# CRITICISM OF LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL


Critics describe Council as:

- **Secondary chamber**
- **Costly ornamental luxury**
- **White elephant**

Criticism states:


It is often used to accommodate **defeated politicians**.


---


# ARGUMENTS IN FAVOR OF COUNCIL


Supporters argue that Council is useful because:


---


## 1. Revising Chamber


Council checks:


```plain text
Hasty and ill-considered legislation
```


---


## 2. Representation of Experts


Council allows entry of:

- Professionals
- Intellectuals
- Experts

Who may not contest elections.


Governor nominates **1/6 members** for this purpose.


---


# 11. PRIVILEGES OF STATE LEGISLATURE


Privileges are **special rights and immunities**.


They ensure:


```plain text
Independence and effectiveness of Legislature
```


Without privileges:


Legislature cannot maintain its **authority, dignity and honour**.


---


# WHO ENJOYS THESE PRIVILEGES


Privileges apply to:

- Houses of Legislature
- Committees
- Members
- Ministers
- Advocate General

But privileges **do not apply to Governor**.


---


# TYPES OF PRIVILEGES


Privileges are divided into two categories:


1️⃣ Collective privileges


2️⃣ Individual privileges


---


# COLLECTIVE PRIVILEGES


These belong to **each House collectively**.


---


## 1. Right to Publish Proceedings


House may publish:

- Reports
- Debates
- Proceedings

It may also **prohibit publication** by outsiders.


---


## 2. Secret Sitting


House may:


```plain text
Exclude strangers
```


And conduct **secret sittings**.


---


## 3. Rule-Making Power


House can make rules regarding:


```plain text
Procedure and conduct of business
```


---


## 4. Punishment for Contempt


House may punish members or outsiders for:

- Breach of privilege
- Contempt of House

Punishment may include:

- Reprimand
- Admonition
- Imprisonment
- Suspension
- Expulsion

---


## 5. Information of Arrest


House has right to receive **immediate information** if a member is:

- Arrested
- Detained
- Convicted
- Imprisoned
- Released

---


## 6. Inquiry Powers


House may:

- Conduct inquiries
- Summon witnesses
- Demand documents

---


## 7. Courts Cannot Question Proceedings


Courts cannot inquire into:


```plain text
Legislative proceedings
```


---


## 8. Arrest Restriction


No person may be arrested within the **House premises** without permission of presiding officer.


---


# INDIVIDUAL PRIVILEGES


These belong to **members individually**.


---


# 1. Freedom from Arrest


Members cannot be arrested:


```plain text
During session
+
40 days before session
+
40 days after session
```


But this applies only in:


```plain text
Civil cases
```


Not applicable to:

- Criminal cases
- Preventive detention

---


# 2. Freedom of Speech


Members enjoy freedom of speech in legislature.


They cannot be prosecuted in court for:


```plain text
Anything said or vote given in legislature
```


However this freedom is subject to:

- Constitution
- Rules of legislature

---


## Important Restriction


Under **Article 211**:


Discussion about conduct of judges of:

- Supreme Court
- High Court

is **not allowed**.


---


# 3. Exemption from Jury Service


Members may refuse to appear in court as witness when legislature is in session.
