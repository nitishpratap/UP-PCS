# 1. DEMAND FOR A CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY


## Origin of the Idea

- The idea of a **Constituent Assembly for India** was first proposed in **1934** by **M. N. Roy** (pioneer of the communist movement in India).

## Congress Demand

- In **1935**, the **Indian National Congress (INC)** officially demanded a **Constituent Assembly to frame the Constitution of India**.

## Nehru’s Declaration (1938)

- **Jawaharlal Nehru** declared that:
    > The Constitution of free India must be framed **without outside interference** by a **Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise**.

## British Acceptance


The British Government gradually accepted the demand.


### 1. August Offer (1940)

- For the first time, the British accepted the **idea of a Constituent Assembly in principle**.

### 2. Cripps Mission (1942)

- **Sir Stafford Cripps** proposed a **draft constitution after World War II**.

However:

- The proposal was **rejected by the Muslim League** because:
    - They demanded **two separate Constituent Assemblies**
    - They wanted **Pakistan**.

### 3. Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)

- The British Government sent the **Cabinet Mission** to India.

Members:

- Lord Pethick Lawrence
- Sir Stafford Cripps
- A. V. Alexander

The Cabinet Mission:

- **Rejected the idea of two Constituent Assemblies**
- Proposed **one Constituent Assembly for India**.

This plan was **accepted**, and the **Constituent Assembly was formed in 1946**.


---


# 2. COMPOSITION OF THE CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY


The **Constituent Assembly was constituted in November 1946** under the **Cabinet Mission Plan**.


## Total Strength


| Category                     | Seats   |
| ---------------------------- | ------- |
| British Indian Provinces     | 292     |
| Princely States              | 93      |
| Chief Commissioner Provinces | 4       |
| **Total**                    | **389** |


---


## Provincial Distribution


### British Provinces (292 seats)


From **11 Governors’ provinces**:

- Madras
- Bombay
- United Provinces
- Bihar
- Central Provinces
- Orissa
- Punjab
- NWFP
- Sindh
- Bengal
- Assam

### Chief Commissioner Provinces (4 seats)

- Delhi
- Ajmer–Merwara
- Coorg
- British Baluchistan

Each province had **1 representative**.


---


## Seat Allocation Principle


Seats were distributed based on **population**.


Rule:

- **1 seat for approximately 1 million population**.

---


## Community Representation


Seats in provinces were divided among **3 communities**:

1. Muslims
2. Sikhs
3. General (Hindus and others)

Allocation was based on **population proportion**.


---


## Election Method


Members were **indirectly elected**.


### Election Process

- Provincial legislative assemblies elected members.
- Community members elected their representatives.

Voting method:


**Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (STV)**.


---


## Representation of Princely States

- Representatives were **nominated by the rulers of the princely states**.

Thus the Constituent Assembly was:


✔ **Partly elected**


✔ **Partly nominated**


---


## Franchise System


Members of provincial assemblies were elected using **limited franchise**.


Based on:

- Tax
- Property
- Education

This system came from **Government of India Act 1935**.


Therefore:


❗ The Constituent Assembly was **not elected by universal adult franchise**.

# 6. OBJECTIVES RESOLUTION


One of the **most important steps in constitution making**.


## Introduction


📅 **13 December 1946**


**Jawaharlal Nehru** moved the **Objectives Resolution** in the Constituent Assembly.


---


## Importance


The Objectives Resolution:

- Laid down the **philosophy of the Constitution**
- Defined the **fundamental principles of governance**
- Became the **basis of the Preamble** of the Constitution.

---


## Adoption


📅 **22 January 1947**


The Objectives Resolution was **unanimously adopted** by the Assembly.


---


## Main Points of Objectives Resolution


### 1️⃣ India to be Independent Sovereign Republic


The resolution declared that:

- India would become an **Independent Sovereign Republic**.

---


### 2️⃣ India to be a Union


The following territories would form the Union:

- British India
- Indian states
- Other territories willing to join India.

---


### 3️⃣ Autonomy of Units

- Territories would have **autonomous status**.
- They would have **residuary powers** except powers given to the Union.

_(Note: Later Constitution gave residuary powers to Union.)_


---


### 4️⃣ Source of Authority


All powers of government would be derived from:


➡ **The people of India**.


---


### 5️⃣ Fundamental Rights


The Constitution would guarantee:

- Justice (social, economic, political)
- Equality
- Freedom of:
    - Thought
    - Expression
    - Belief
    - Faith
    - Worship
    - Vocation
    - Association
    - Action.

---


### 6️⃣ Safeguards for Weaker Sections


Adequate safeguards would be provided for:

- Minorities
- Backward classes
- Tribal areas
- Depressed classes.

---


### 7️⃣ Territorial Integrity


The integrity of India’s territory would be protected on:

- Land
- Sea
- Air.

---


### 8️⃣ Promotion of World Peace


India would contribute to:

- World peace
- Welfare of mankind.

---


## Exam Fact


✔ **Modified Objectives Resolution became the Preamble of the Constitution.**

# Effects of Indian Independence Act 1947


The Act made **three important changes**.


---


# 1️⃣ Constituent Assembly Became Sovereign


The Assembly became a **fully sovereign body**.


It could:

- Frame any Constitution
- Modify or repeal British laws related to India.

---


# 2️⃣ Assembly Became Legislative Body


The Assembly performed **two functions**:


1️⃣ Constitution making


2️⃣ Making ordinary laws


Thus it became the:


➡ **First Parliament of Free India (Dominion Legislature)**.

# 15. CRITICISM OF THE CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY


Several critics raised objections regarding the functioning and composition of the Constituent Assembly.


---


# 1. Not a Representative Body


Critics argued that the Assembly was **not fully representative**.


Reason:

- Members were **not directly elected by the people**.
- Elections were held by **Provincial Legislative Assemblies**.

Thus:


❗ It was **not elected on the basis of universal adult franchise**.


---


# 2. Not a Sovereign Body


Some critics believed the Assembly lacked sovereignty.


Reason:

- It was **created under British Government proposals**.
- Assembly meetings were held **with British approval**.

However, after **Indian Independence Act 1947**, the Assembly became **fully sovereign**.


---


# 3. Time Consuming


Critics said the Assembly took **too long to draft the Constitution**.


Comparison:


| Country            | Time taken     |
| ------------------ | -------------- |
| USA Constitution   | 4 months       |
| India Constitution | Nearly 3 years |


A member named **Naziruddin Ahmed** criticized the Drafting Committee by calling it:


➡ **“Drifting Committee”**.


---


# 4. Dominated by Congress


Critics argued that the Assembly was dominated by the **Indian National Congress**.


British constitutional expert **Granville Austin** remarked:

> “The Constituent Assembly was a one-party body in an essentially one-party country.”

---


# 5. Dominated by Lawyers and Politicians


Critics also argued that the Assembly had too many:

- Lawyers
- Politicians

And fewer representatives from:

- Labour
- Farmers
- Other social groups.

They believed this led to:

- A **lengthy Constitution**
- **Complex legal language**.

---


# 6. Dominated by Hindus


Some critics claimed that the Assembly was dominated by Hindus.


Examples:


**Lord Viscount Simon** described it as:


➡ “A body of Hindus”


**Winston Churchill** stated that the Assembly represented:


➡ “Only one major community of India.”
