# CLASSIFICATION OF SERVICES


---


# 1. Public Services in India


Public services (also called **Civil Services or Government Services**) are classified into **three categories**:


1️⃣ **All-India Services**


2️⃣ **Central Services**


3️⃣ **State Services**


These services form the **administrative machinery of the government**.


---


# 2. All-India Services (AIS)


## Meaning


All-India Services are **common to both the Central Government and State Governments**.


Officers of these services:

- Hold **top administrative positions**
- Serve **both Centre and States**
- Work **by rotation**

Thus they form the **link between Union and State administration**.


---


# 3. Present All-India Services


Currently **three All-India Services exist**

1. **Indian Administrative Service (IAS)**
2. **Indian Police Service (IPS)**
3. **Indian Forest Service (IFS)**

---


# 4. Historical Background


| Year     | Change                                         |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **1947** | Indian Civil Service (ICS) replaced by **IAS** |
| **1947** | Indian Police (IP) replaced by **IPS**         |
| **1966** | **Indian Forest Service (IFS)** created        |


---


# 5. All-India Services Act, 1951


The **All-India Services Act, 1951** provides that:


The **Central Government**, in consultation with **State Governments**, can make rules regarding:

- Recruitment
- Service conditions

---


# 6. Recruitment and Cadre System


Important features:


### Recruitment

- Conducted by **Central Government**
- Through **UPSC**

### Training

- Done by **Central Government**

### Cadre Allocation

- Officers are assigned to **State Cadres**
- Example: UP cadre, AGMUT cadre etc.

### Deputation


Officers:

- Work in **State Government**
- Can be sent to **Central Government on deputation**
- After tenure → return to **State Cadre**

This is called the **Tenure System**.


---


# 7. Structure of All-India Services


Important point for exams:


Even though officers are divided into **state cadres**, each service is still **one single national service**.


Features:


✔ Common rights


✔ Same status


✔ Uniform pay scale


✔ Same service rules across India


---


# 8. Salary and Pension


Important fact often asked in exams:


👉 **Salaries and pensions of AIS officers are paid by the State Governments.**


---


# 9. Control Over All-India Services


Control is **shared between Centre and States**.


| Authority              | Role              |
| ---------------------- | ----------------- |
| **State Government**   | Immediate control |
| **Central Government** | Ultimate control  |


Important rule:


👉 **Disciplinary action against AIS officers can be taken only by the Central Government**


---

# PART 5 — ARTICLE 310 (TENURE OF OFFICE)


---


# 1. Doctrine of Pleasure


Article 310 introduces the **Doctrine of Pleasure**.


According to this doctrine:


Government servants hold office **during the pleasure of the executive authority**.


---


# 2. For Union Services


Members of:

- Defence services
- Civil services of the Centre
- All-India services
- Persons holding civil or military posts under the Centre

hold office **during the pleasure of the President**.


---


# 3. For State Services


Members of:

- Civil services of a state
- Persons holding civil posts under the state

hold office **during the pleasure of the Governor**.


---


# 4. Exception to Doctrine of Pleasure


There is an **exception to the doctrine of pleasure**.


The **President or Governor** may provide **compensation** to a person if:


1️⃣ The post is **abolished before expiry of contractual period**


OR


2️⃣ The person is required to **vacate the post for reasons not related to misconduct**


---


# 5. Condition for Such Contract


This contract can be made **only with a new entrant**.


It **cannot be made with a person already serving in**:

- Defence services
- Civil services of the Centre
- All-India services
- State civil services

---


# 6. Meaning of Civil Post


A **civil post** means:

> An appointment or employment on the **civil side of administration**, as opposed to the **military side**.

# PART 6 — ARTICLE 311 (SAFEGUARDS TO CIVIL SERVANTS)


Article 311 provides **constitutional protection to civil servants** against **arbitrary dismissal, removal, or reduction in rank**.


It acts as a **limitation on the Doctrine of Pleasure (Article 310)**.


---


# 1. Purpose of Article 311


The main purpose is:


✔ Protect civil servants from **arbitrary action by the executive**


✔ Ensure **fair administrative procedure**


---


# 2. Two Constitutional Safeguards


Article 311 provides **two safeguards** to civil servants.


---


# Safeguard 1


A civil servant **cannot be dismissed or removed** by an authority **lower than the appointing authority**.


Example:


If an officer is appointed by **Governor**, he **cannot be dismissed by a lower officer**.


---


# Safeguard 2


A civil servant cannot be:

- dismissed
- removed
- reduced in rank

without **an inquiry**.


During this inquiry:


✔ The officer must be **informed of the charges**


✔ Must be given **reasonable opportunity to defend himself**


---


# 3. Who Gets Protection Under Article 311


These safeguards apply only to:


1️⃣ Members of **civil services of the Union**


2️⃣ Members of **civil services of a State**


3️⃣ Members of **All-India services**


4️⃣ Persons holding **civil posts under Union or State**


---


# 4. Who Does NOT Get Protection


The safeguards **do not apply to**:


❌ Members of **defence services**


❌ Persons holding **military posts**


---


# 5. Meaning of “Reasonable Opportunity of Being Heard”


The **Supreme Court clarified** what this means.


It includes:


### 1️⃣ Opportunity to know the charges


The officer must know:

- the charges against him
- the allegations forming the basis of those charges

### 2️⃣ Opportunity to defend


The officer must be able to:

- cross-examine witnesses
- produce his own witnesses

### 3️⃣ Inquiry report must be supplied


The disciplinary authority must give the civil servant:


✔ a **copy of the inquiry officer’s report**


✔ opportunity to give **comments or observations**


---


# 6. Meaning of Reduction in Rank


Reduction in rank means:


👉 **Demotion from a higher post to a lower post as a penalty.**


---


# 7. Difference Between Dismissal and Removal


This is an important exam point.


| Punishment    | Meaning                                                          |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dismissal** | Disqualifies the person from future government employment        |
| **Removal**   | Does NOT disqualify the person from future government employment |


---


# 8. Exceptions to the Inquiry Rule


Normally inquiry is mandatory.


However, **inquiry is NOT required in three situations**.


---


## Exception 1 — Criminal Conviction


If a civil servant is punished because:


👉 He has been **convicted in a criminal case**


then inquiry is not required.


---


## Exception 2 — Inquiry Not Practicable


If the disciplinary authority is satisfied that:


👉 It is **not reasonably practicable to hold inquiry**


Then inquiry can be skipped.


Important condition:


The reasons must be **recorded in writing**.


---


## Exception 3 — Security of the State


If the:

- **President** (for Union services)
- **Governor** (for State services)

is satisfied that:


👉 Inquiry is **not in the interest of the security of the State**


then inquiry may be avoided.


---


# 9. Position Before 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976)


Earlier, civil servants were given **two opportunities**:


1️⃣ During **inquiry stage**


2️⃣ During **punishment stage**


Meaning:


After inquiry, the officer could **make representation against proposed punishment**.


---


# 10. Change by 42nd Amendment Act (1976)


The **42nd Amendment abolished the second opportunity**.


Now:


After inquiry,


✔ punishment can be imposed


❌ without giving separate opportunity to represent against penalty.


---


# Present Position


Today:


If after inquiry it is proposed to impose punishment like:

- dismissal
- removal
- reduction in rank

Then the punishment can be imposed **based on the evidence in inquiry**, without giving additional opportunity.

# PART 7 — ARTICLE 312, 312A, 313, 314


(All-India Services & Other Constitutional Provisions)


---


# 1. Article 312 — All-India Services


Article 312 deals with the **creation and regulation of All-India Services**.


---


# 1. Power to Create New All-India Services


Parliament can create **new All-India Services**.


But **one condition must be fulfilled**.


👉 **Rajya Sabha must pass a resolution** stating that creation of such service is **necessary or expedient in national interest**.


---


# 2. Special Majority Requirement


The Rajya Sabha resolution must be passed by:


⭐ **Two-thirds of members present and voting**


---


# 3. Why This Power is Given to Rajya Sabha


Rajya Sabha represents the **states in the federal system**.


Therefore this power protects **state interests** when creating services that operate under both **Union and States**.


---


# 4. Regulation of Recruitment and Service Conditions


After creation of an All-India Service:


👉 **Parliament regulates recruitment and service conditions**


through law.


Example:


⭐ **All-India Services Act, 1951**


---


# 5. Services Existing at the Beginning of Constitution


The following services were **already functioning when the Constitution came into force (26 January 1950)**:


1️⃣ Indian Administrative Service (IAS)


2️⃣ Indian Police Service (IPS)


These were **deemed to be created by Parliament under Article 312**.


---


# 6. All-India Judicial Service


Article 312 also allows creation of:


⭐ **All-India Judicial Service (AIJS)**


Important rule:


This service **cannot include any post below the rank of District Judge**.


---


# 7. Constitutional Status of AIJS Law


A law creating **All-India Judicial Service**:


👉 **Will NOT be treated as a constitutional amendment under Article 368**


---


# 8. 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976)


The **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** added provision for **All-India Judicial Service**.


However:


❗ **No such service has been created yet.**


---


# 9. Proposed Additional All-India Services (1963)


In **1963**, a provision was made for creating three additional services:

1. Indian Forest Service
2. Indian Medical and Health Service
3. Indian Service of Engineers

---


# 10. Which One Was Actually Created


Out of these:


✔ **Indian Forest Service (IFS)** was created in **1966**


❌ The other two services have **not been created**.


---


# 2. Article 312A


Article 312A was inserted by:


⭐ **28th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1972**


---


## Purpose


It gives **Parliament the power to vary or revoke service conditions** of officers who were:


👉 Appointed to **civil service of the Crown in India before 1950**


This mainly affected **ICS officers serving before independence**.


---


# 3. Article 313 — Transitional Provisions


Article 313 deals with **continuity of existing laws**.


It states:


Until new laws are made,


👉 **All service laws existing before 1950 will continue to operate**.


---


# 4. Article 314 (Now Repealed)


Article 314 originally provided:


👉 **Protection to officers of certain services who served before independence**


---


# Repeal of Article 314


This article was **removed by the 28th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1972**.


---
