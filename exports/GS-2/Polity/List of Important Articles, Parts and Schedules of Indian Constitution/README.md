## **How many Articles are there in the Indian Constitution?**


The Indian Constitution is the longest written constitution of any sovereign country in the world. When it was originally adopted in 1949, it consisted of 395 Articles divided into 22 Parts and 8 Schedules. However, due to numerous amendments over the years, the total count has increased substantially. The Constitution now consists of approximately 448 Articles organized into 25 Parts and 12 Schedules. Despite the new additions,

## **Important Articles of the Indian Constitution Under the 25 Parts**


Indian Constitution has Important parts that deal with rights and duties, governance and formation of our nation. Both sections are of unique importance to law and administration. UPSC exams also have a direct tendency to question these parts.


## **Part 1: The Union and its Territory (Articles 1–4)**


This part defines India as a “Union of States” and lays down the framework regarding the territory of India. It empowers Parliament to admit, establish, alter, or reorganize states, including changing boundaries and names. It provides the constitutional basis for state reorganization and territorial adjustments.


| **Article** | **Provision**                                                                                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Article 1   | India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States; defines territory.                                                     |
| Article 2   | Parliament may admit or establish new States.                                                                             |
| Article 3   | Parliament may form new States; alter areas, boundaries, or names.                                                        |
| Article 4   | Laws made under Articles 2 & 3 may amend First & Fourth Schedules; not deemed constitutional amendment under Article 368. |


## **Part 2: Citizenship (Articles 5–11)**


This part deals with provisions relating to citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution. It outlines who was considered a citizen of India in 1950 and empowers Parliament to regulate citizenship by law. The detailed framework for acquisition and termination of citizenship is governed by subsequent legislation.


| **Article** | **Provision**                                                               |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Article 5   | Citizenship at the commencement of Constitution.                            |
| Article 6   | Citizenship of persons migrated from Pakistan.                              |
| Article 7   | Citizenship of migrants to Pakistan returning to India.                     |
| Article 8   | Citizenship of persons of Indian origin residing abroad.                    |
| Article 9   | Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship terminates Indian citizenship. |
| Article 10  | Continuance of rights of citizenship.                                       |
| Article 11  | Parliament empowered to regulate citizenship by law.                        |


## **Part 3: Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)**


This part guarantees essential civil liberties to individuals, including the Right to Equality, Freedom, Protection against Exploitation, Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, and the Right to Constitutional Remedies. These rights are justiciable and enforceable by courts, forming the core of India’s democratic framework.

## **Part 4A: Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)**


Inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, this part lists the moral obligations of citizens. It includes duties such as respecting the Constitution, promoting harmony, protecting the environment, and safeguarding public property. Though not directly enforceable, they serve as ethical standards for responsible citizenship.


## **Part 5: The Union (Articles 52–151)**


**Part V of the Constitution of India (Articles 52–151)** deals with the **Union Government**, i.e., the central executive, legislature, and judiciary of India. It establishes the constitutional framework for the functioning of the Union authority.It is broadly divided into the following chapters:

The President of India is provided under **Part V, Chapter I (Articles 52–62)** of the Constitution, which establishes the office, method of election, qualifications, tenure, oath, and impeachment procedure. ## **Part XXII – Short Title, Commencement, Authoritative Text in Hindi and Repeals (Articles 393–395)**


Part XXII contains the concluding provisions of the Constitution. It formally declares the name of the Constitution, specifies its commencement, provides for the authoritative Hindi text, and repeals prior constitutional enactments such as the Government of India Act, 1935. These Articles mark the legal closure and formal operationalization of the Constitution.

| Article 394A | Authoritative text of the Constitution in the Hindi language                                                       |
| Article 395  | Repeals (Indian Independence Act, 1947 and Government of India Act, 1935 repealed)                                 |
