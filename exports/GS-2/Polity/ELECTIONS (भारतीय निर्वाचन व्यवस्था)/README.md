
# ELECTIONS (भारतीय निर्वाचन व्यवस्था)


---


# 1. Constitutional Provisions of Electoral System


Articles **324–329 (Part XV)** deal with elections in India.


## Article 324 – Election Commission


The Constitution provides for an **independent Election Commission** to ensure **free and fair elections**.


Powers vested in Election Commission:

- Superintendence of elections
- Direction of elections
- Control of elections

Applies to elections of:

- Parliament
- State Legislatures
- President
- Vice-President

### Composition


Currently consists of:

- **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)**
- **Two Election Commissioners**

All are appointed by the **President of India**.


---


## Single Electoral Roll


The Constitution provides:

- **One general electoral roll for every territorial constituency**

Implication:

- Abolishes **communal representation**
- Abolishes **separate electorates**

These systems had contributed to **Partition of India**.


---


## Equality in Electoral Franchise


No person can be disqualified for inclusion in electoral roll on grounds of:

- Religion
- Race
- Caste
- Sex

Also:

- No special electoral rolls based on these grounds.

This ensures **equality in voting rights**.


---


## Adult Franchise (Universal Adult Suffrage)


Elections to:

- **Lok Sabha**
- **State Legislative Assemblies**

are conducted on the basis of **adult franchise**.


### Eligibility for Voting


A person must:

- Be a **citizen of India**
- Be **18 years or above**

Provided he/she is not disqualified due to:

- Non-residence
- Unsound mind
- Crime
- Corrupt or illegal electoral practices

### Constitutional Amendment


**61st Constitutional Amendment Act (1988)**

- Reduced voting age from **21 → 18 years**
- Effective from **28 March 1989**

---


## Power of Parliament (Article 327)


Parliament can make laws regarding:

- Elections to Parliament
- Elections to State Legislatures

Matters include:

- Preparation of electoral rolls
- Delimitation of constituencies
- Conduct of elections

---


## Power of State Legislatures (Article 328)


State legislatures can make laws regarding elections to state legislatures.


However:

- Only for matters **not covered by Parliament**
- Cannot override Parliamentary laws
- Can only **supplement them**

---


## Delimitation Laws Not Challengeable


The Constitution declares:

- Laws relating to **delimitation of constituencies**
- Allotment of seats

**Cannot be challenged in courts.**


Thus:


Orders of **Delimitation Commission are final**.


---


## Election Petitions (Article 329)


Elections to:

- Parliament
- State legislatures

cannot be challenged except through an **election petition**.


Since **1966**

- Election petitions are tried by **High Courts**.

Appeal lies to:

- **Supreme Court**

---


## Article 323B – Election Tribunals


Allows legislature to establish **Election Tribunals** for election disputes.


Provision:

- Courts’ jurisdiction can be excluded except **Supreme Court SLP**.

### Supreme Court Judgement


Case: **L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)**


Held:

- Clause excluding High Court jurisdiction **unconstitutional**.

Hence:

- Appeals must lie to **High Courts**.

---


# 2. Election Machinery


## Election Commission of India (ECI)


Under **Article 324**.


Functions:

- Conduct elections to:
    - Lok Sabha
    - State Legislative Assemblies

Composition:

- Chief Election Commissioner
- Two Election Commissioners

Appointed by:

- **President of India**

---


# Election Officials


## 1. Chief Electoral Officer (CEO)


Responsible for:

- Supervising election work in **State / UT**

Appointed by:

- Election Commission

From:

- Officers of State Government

---


## 2. District Election Officer (DEO)


Responsible for:

- Supervising elections at **district level**

Appointed by:

- Election Commission

From:

- State Government officers

Works under:

- Chief Electoral Officer

---


## 3. Returning Officer (RO)


Responsible for:

- Conducting elections in a **constituency**

Appointed by:

- Election Commission

Usually from:

- Government or local authority officials

### Assistant Returning Officers


Appointed to assist the RO.


---


## 4. Electoral Registration Officer (ERO)


Responsible for:

- Preparation of **electoral rolls**

Appointed by:

- Election Commission

Assistant EROs help in:

- Revision
- Updating electoral rolls

---


## 5. Presiding Officer


Responsible for:

- Conduct of polling at **polling station**

Appointed by:

- District Election Officer

With help of:

- Polling Officers

---


# Election Observers


Election Commission appoints observers to ensure fair elections.


Types:


---


## 1. General Observers


Responsibilities:

- Monitor entire election process
- Ensure free and fair elections

---


## 2. Expenditure Observers


Monitor:

- Election expenditure by candidates

Purpose:

- Prevent bribery or inducement to voters

---


## 3. Police Observers


Usually **IPS officers**.


Responsibilities:

- Monitor law and order
- Deployment of security forces
- Coordination between civil and police administration

---


## 4. Awareness Observers


Introduced during:


**16th Lok Sabha Election (2014)**


Role:

- Promote voter awareness
- Increase voter turnout
- Monitor media aspects including **Paid News**

---


## 5. Micro Observers


Deployed at:

- Sensitive polling stations

Responsibilities:

- Observe polling from mock poll to sealing of EVM
- Report irregularities to General Observers

Usually selected from:

- Central Government / PSU officials

---


## 6. Assistant Expenditure Observers


Responsibilities:

- Videograph election rallies
- Monitor election spending
- Address complaints of malpractice

---


# 3. Election Process


---


# Time of Elections


Elections to:

- Lok Sabha
- State Assemblies

Must be held every **5 years** unless dissolved earlier.


President may dissolve Lok Sabha if:

- Government loses confidence
- No alternative government possible

---


# Schedule of Elections


Election Commission announces:

- Election schedule
- Polling dates

Important rule:


Maximum **6 months gap** between:

- Last session of dissolved Lok Sabha
- First session of new Lok Sabha

---


## Model Code of Conduct (MCC)


Comes into force immediately after election schedule announcement.


Purpose:

- Ensure fair elections
- Prevent misuse of government machinery

Model Code was agreed upon by political parties in **1968**.


First effectively implemented in **1991**.


---


# Nomination Process


Steps:

1. Notification issued
2. Candidates file nominations
3. Scrutiny of nominations by Returning Officer
4. Candidates can withdraw within **2 days**
5. Campaign begins

Campaign period:

- Minimum **two weeks**

---


# Election Campaign


Political parties:

- Promote candidates
- Issue manifestos
- Conduct rallies and meetings
- Distribute pamphlets and posters

Campaign officially ends:


**48 hours before polling**


---


# Polling Days


Polling may be conducted in **multiple phases** due to:

- Large electorate
- Security concerns

---


# Ballot Papers and Symbols


Returning Officer prepares:

- List of candidates
- Ballot papers

Ballot includes:

- Candidate names
- Party symbols

Recognised parties receive **reserved symbols**.


---


# Voting Procedure


Voting is conducted through:


**Secret Ballot**


Polling stations usually located in:

- Schools
- Community halls

Election Commission ensures:

- Polling station within **2 km of voters**
- Maximum **1500 voters per polling station**

Polling station remains open for **at least 8 hours**.


---


# Voting Process


Steps:

1. Voter identity verified using **electoral roll**
2. Voter receives ballot paper
3. Marks vote with rubber stamp on candidate symbol
4. Ballot inserted into ballot box

---


# Electronic Voting Machines (EVM)


Used instead of ballot papers since **1998**.


By **2004 Lok Sabha elections**

- Entire election conducted using **EVMs**

More than **1 million EVMs used**.


---


# Advantages of EVM

1. Eliminates invalid votes
2. Faster counting
3. Saves paper
4. Reduces printing cost

---


# Supervising Elections


Election Commission appoints:

- Observers
- Expenditure monitors

Purpose:

- Ensure fairness
- Monitor campaign expenditure

---


# Counting of Votes


Counting is conducted under supervision of:

- Returning Officer
- Election Observers

Winner:


Candidate who receives **maximum votes**.


---


# Electoral System Used


India uses:


**First Past the Post System (FPTP)**


Meaning:

- Candidate with highest votes wins
- No need for absolute majority

Used for:

- Lok Sabha elections
- State Assembly elections

---


# Media Coverage


Media allowed to cover elections to promote transparency.


They receive:

- Special passes for polling stations
- Access to counting halls

However:

- Must maintain secrecy of vote.

---


# Election Petitions


Any:

- Candidate
- Voter

can file election petition in case of malpractice.


Tried by:

- **High Court**

If petition is upheld:

- Election may be **cancelled**
- Fresh election conducted.

---
