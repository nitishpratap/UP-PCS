**Article:** 52


**Position**

- Head of the Indian State
- **First Citizen of India**
- Symbol of **unity, integrity and solidarity**
- **Nominal executive**

**Real executive:**


➡ Council of Ministers headed by **Prime Minister**

# 3. Election of President


President is **indirectly elected**.


### Electoral College


Includes:


✔ Elected MPs of **Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha**


✔ Elected MLAs of **State Assemblies**


✔ Elected MLAs of **Delhi & Puducherry**


### Not Included


❌ Nominated MPs


❌ Nominated MLAs


❌ Members of Legislative Councils


❌ Nominated members of UT assemblies


---


# 4. Principles of Election


Two principles maintained:


### 1. Uniformity of representation of states


Votes proportional to population.


### 2. Parity between Union and States


Total votes of MPs = Total votes of MLAs.


---


# 5. Value of Votes


### Value of Vote of MLA


[


\text{Value of MLA vote} =


\frac{\text{Population of State}}{\text{Total elected MLAs} \times 1000}


]


Population used → **1971 Census**


Reason → **84th Constitutional Amendment (2001)**


Population freeze until **2026 census**.


---


### Value of Vote of MP


[


\text{Value of MP vote} =


\frac{\text{Total value of MLA votes}}{\text{Total elected MPs}}


]


---


# 6. Method of Election


System → **Proportional Representation**


Method → **Single Transferable Vote**


Ballot → **Secret Ballot**


Voting → **Preference voting (1,2,3,4)**


---


# 7. Winning Quota


[


\text{Quota} =


\frac{\text{Total valid votes}}{(\text{Seats}+1)} + 1


]


Seats = **1**


---


# 8. Counting Process


Step 1


First preference votes counted.


Step 2


If quota not reached


→ lowest candidate eliminated.


Step 3


Second preference votes transferred.


Process continues until quota achieved.


---


# 9. Disputes in Presidential Election


Decided by → **Supreme Court**


Important:


✔ Decision of SC **final**


✔ Election cannot be challenged due to **vacancy in Electoral College**


✔ If election declared void → President’s actions remain valid.


---


# 10. Why Indirect Election?


Reasons:


### 1. Parliamentary System


President is **nominal executive**.


### 2. Cost and Time


Direct election would be expensive.


### 3. Federal Balance


If Parliament alone elected → ruling party dominance.


Hence **states included**.

# PRESIDENT: QUALIFICATION, TERM & IMPEACHMENT


# 11. Qualifications


Article **58**


A person must:


✔ Citizen of India


✔ Minimum **35 years**


✔ Qualified for **Lok Sabha membership**


✔ Not hold **office of profit**


---


### Not considered Office of Profit


Allowed:

- President
- Vice-President
- Governor
- Minister

---


# 12. Nomination Requirements


Candidate must have:


✔ **50 proposers**


✔ **50 seconders**


(All electors)


Security deposit → **₹15,000**


Deposit forfeited if:


Candidate gets **less than 1/6 votes**.


---


# 13. Oath of President


Article **60**


Administered by:


➡ **Chief Justice of India**


If absent → **Senior-most Supreme Court judge**


President swears:

- Faithfully execute office
- Preserve Constitution
- Serve people.

---


# 14. Conditions of Office


President:


❌ Cannot be member of Parliament or State Legislature


❌ Cannot hold office of profit


If elected as MP/MLA → seat automatically vacated.


Residence → **Rashtrapati Bhavan**


Salary decided by → **Parliament**


Salary **cannot be reduced during tenure**.


---


# 15. Salary and Facilities


Current salary (after revision):


₹ **5 lakh per month**


Former Presidents receive:

- Pension (50% salary)
- Residence
- Car
- Medical care
- Staff
- Travel facilities

---


# 16. Privileges and Immunities


President enjoys **legal immunity**.


### Criminal cases


❌ Cannot be prosecuted.


### Arrest


❌ Cannot be arrested.


### Civil cases


Allowed only after:


➡ **2 months notice**


---


# 17. Term of President


Article **56**


Term → **5 years**


Starts from date of **entering office**.


Important:


President continues until **successor assumes office**.


Purpose → Avoid **constitutional vacuum**.


---


# Resignation


Addressed to:


➡ **Vice-President**


---


# Re-Election


Unlimited times allowed.


Example:


**Dr Rajendra Prasad**

- Only President elected **twice**.

---


# 18. Impeachment of President


Article **61**


Ground:


➡ **Violation of the Constitution**


Term **not defined in Constitution**.


---


## Impeachment Procedure


### Step 1 — Initiation


Either House of Parliament.


### Step 2 — Notice


Signed by **1/4 members**


**14 days notice** to President.


### Step 3 — First House Vote


Passed by **2/3 of total membership**.


### Step 4 — Investigation


Second House investigates.


President may:

- Appear
- Be represented.

### Step 5 — Removal


Second House passes resolution by **2/3 majority**.


---


### Nature


➡ **Quasi-Judicial**


---


### Important Exam Facts


✔ Nominated MPs participate in impeachment


✔ State MLAs **do not participate**


Fact:


➡ **No President has ever been impeached**.

# POWERS OF PRESIDENT (MOST IMPORTANT FOR EXAMS)


# 1. Executive Powers


Article **53**


Executive power vested in President but exercised through **Council of Ministers**.


### Key Powers


President appoints:

- Prime Minister
- Council of Ministers
- Attorney General
- CAG
- Election Commissioners
- UPSC Chairman
- Governors
- Finance Commission

Other powers:

- Administration of Union Territories
- Creation of Inter-State Council
- Declaring Scheduled Areas
- Seeking information from PM.

---


# 2. Legislative Powers


President is **integral part of Parliament**.


### Functions


✔ Summons Parliament


✔ Prorogues sessions


✔ Dissolves Lok Sabha


✔ Addresses Parliament


✔ Sends messages to Houses


✔ Nominates **12 members to Rajya Sabha**


Fields:

- Literature
- Science
- Art
- Social service

(Anglo-Indian nomination removed by **104th Amendment 2020**)


---


# Assent to Bills


President may:

1. Give assent
2. Withhold assent
3. Return bill (except Money Bill)

If passed again → President **must assent**.


---


# Ordinance Power


Article **123**


President can issue **Ordinance** when Parliament not in session.


Equivalent to **law of Parliament**.


Maximum life:


➡ **6 months + 6 weeks**


---


### Important Case


**D.C. Wadhwa Case (1987)**


Repromulgation of ordinances → **Unconstitutional**.


---


# 3. Financial Powers


President:


✔ Recommends **Money Bills**


✔ Causes **Union Budget** to be laid before Parliament


✔ Controls **Contingency Fund of India**


✔ Appoints **Finance Commission every 5 years**.


---


# 4. Judicial Powers


President appoints:

- CJI
- Supreme Court judges
- High Court judges

---


### Advisory Power


Article **143**


President may seek **Supreme Court advice**.


Advice → **Not binding**.


---


### Pardoning Power


Article **72**


President may grant:

1. Pardon
2. Commutation
3. Remission
4. Respite
5. Reprieve

Applicable in:


✔ Court martial cases


✔ Union law offences


✔ Death sentence


---


# 5. Military Powers


President is:


➡ **Supreme Commander of Armed Forces**


Appoints:

- Army Chief
- Navy Chief
- Air Force Chief

---


# 6. Diplomatic Powers


President:

- Sends ambassadors
- Receives diplomats
- Concludes treaties

(Treaties require **Parliament approval**)


---


# 7. Emergency Powers


President can proclaim:

1. **National Emergency** — Article 352
2. **President’s Rule** — Articles 356 & 365
3. **Financial Emergency** — Article 360

---


# 8. Veto Powers


Article **111**


Types:


| Veto            | Meaning     |
| --------------- | ----------- |
| Absolute veto   | Reject bill |
| Suspensive veto | Return bill |
| Pocket veto     | No action   |


India does **not have qualified veto**.


---


### Famous Example


1986


President **Giani Zail Singh**


Used **pocket veto** on:


**Indian Post Office Amendment Bill**

# 9. Constitutional Position


India follows **Parliamentary System**.


President = **Nominal Executive**


Real power:


➡ **Prime Minister + Council of Ministers**


---


### Key Amendments


**42nd Amendment (1976)**


President bound by cabinet advice.


**44th Amendment (1978)**


President may return advice **once for reconsideration**.


---


# 1. SUPREME COURT JUDGMENTS RELATED TO PRESIDENT


### Ram Jawaya Kapoor v State of Punjab (1955)


Supreme Court held:

- India follows a **Parliamentary Executive System**.
- President is **constitutional head**, not the real executive.
- Real executive power lies with **Council of Ministers**.

---


### Shamsher Singh v State of Punjab (1974)


One of the **most important cases**.


Supreme Court ruled:

- President must act **only on aid and advice of Council of Ministers**.
- President is **formal head of the executive**.
- Real authority lies with **Cabinet**.

---


### Maru Ram v Union of India (1980)


Related to **pardoning power**.


Court held:

- Pardoning power is exercised on **aid and advice of Council of Ministers**.
- President cannot act **independently**.

---


### Kehar Singh v Union of India (1989)


Court clarified scope of **mercy petition**.


Important points:

- President can **re-examine evidence of the case**.
- No requirement to give **reasons for decision**.
- Judicial review possible only in **limited circumstances**.

---


### Epuru Sudhakar v Govt. of Andhra Pradesh (2006)


Supreme Court held:


Pardoning power can be **judicially reviewed** if decision is:

- Mala fide
- Arbitrary
- Based on irrelevant considerations.

---


# 2. IMPORTANT JUDGMENT ON ORDINANCE POWER


### Krishna Kumar Singh v State of Bihar (2017)


Supreme Court ruled:

- **Re-promulgation of ordinances is unconstitutional**.
- Ordinance power is **not a parallel legislative power**.
- Ordinances must be used **only for genuine urgency**.

This strengthened the earlier ruling in:


### D.C. Wadhwa Case (1987)


Which criticized **repeated ordinance re-promulgation in Bihar**.


---


# 3. MODES OF PRESIDENTIAL DISCRETION


Normally President acts on **aid and advice of Council of Ministers**, but limited discretion exists.


### 1. Situational Discretion


Occurs in **political situations**.


Examples:

- Appointment of PM in **hung parliament**
- Dismissal of government losing majority
- Dissolution of Lok Sabha when government loses confidence.

---


### 2. Constitutional Discretion


Allowed directly by Constitution.


Example:


Under **44th Amendment (1978)**:


President can **return cabinet advice once for reconsideration**.


After reconsideration → President **must accept advice**.


---


# 4. PRESIDENT AND CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT BILLS


Originally President could **veto amendment bills**.


However:


### 24th Constitutional Amendment Act (1971)


Made it **mandatory** for President to give assent to a **Constitutional Amendment Bill**.


Thus:


❌ President **cannot veto** a Constitutional Amendment Bill.


---


# 5. IMPORTANT FEATURES OF POCKET VETO


Pocket veto means:


➡ President **neither signs nor rejects nor returns the bill**.


Reason:

- Constitution **does not prescribe time limit** for Presidential assent.

---


### Famous Example


**1986**


President **Giani Zail Singh**


Used pocket veto on:


➡ **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill**


Concern:

- Bill threatened **freedom of press**.

Government later **dropped the bill**.


---


# 6. JUDICIAL REVIEW OF PRESIDENTIAL POWERS


Though President has constitutional powers, **courts can review decisions** in limited situations.


Judicial review allowed if decision is:

- **Mala fide**
- **Arbitrary**
- Based on **irrelevant considerations**
- Violating **constitutional provisions**

Example areas reviewed by courts:

- **Pardoning power**
- **Ordinance power**
- **Emergency proclamations**

---


# 7. ADDITIONAL EXAM FACTS


✔ President's election disputes decided by **Supreme Court (Article 71)**


✔ President continues in office **until successor assumes charge** to avoid **constitutional vacuum**


✔ President can be **re-elected unlimited times**


✔ **No President of India has been impeached so far**


✔ President is an **integral part of Parliament**


✔ Ordinance issued by President has **same force as law passed by Parliament**


✔ Maximum life of ordinance = **6 months + 6 weeks**

# 8. QUICK MAINS ANALYTICAL POINT


### Why President is called "Nominal Executive"


Reasons:

- Executive power exercised by **Council of Ministers**
- President bound by **cabinet advice (Article 74)**
- Ministers responsible to **Lok Sabha (Article 75)**

Thus:


President **represents the state but does not rule the government**.
