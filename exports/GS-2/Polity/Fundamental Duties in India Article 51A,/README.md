# Concept Notes (for Mains)


## 1. Meaning of Fundamental Duties


Fundamental Duties are the **moral and civic obligations of citizens towards the nation**, ensuring that **rights are balanced with responsibilities**.


They encourage:

- **Patriotism**
- **National integration**
- **Responsible citizenship**

---


# 2. Origin of Fundamental Duties


| Aspect  | Explanation                               |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Source  | Borrowed from **USSR Constitution**       |
| Reason  | To balance **rights with duties**         |
| Context | Introduced during **Emergency (1975-77)** |


Most democratic constitutions **do not explicitly list duties**.


---


# 3. Swaran Singh Committee (1976)


**Purpose**

- To recommend inclusion of duties in the Constitution.

**Key Recommendations**


| Recommendation                                    | Status   |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Separate chapter for duties                       | Accepted |
| Punishment for violating duties                   | Rejected |
| Duty to pay taxes                                 | Rejected |
| Laws enforcing duties immune from court challenge | Rejected |


---


# 4. Features of Fundamental Duties


| Feature                                 | Explanation                                       |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Combination of Moral & Civic Duties** | Reflect ethical values and civic responsibilities |
| **Codification of Indian values**       | Reflect traditions and culture                    |
| **Applicable only to citizens**         | Unlike some Fundamental Rights                    |
| **Non-justiciable**                     | Courts cannot directly enforce them               |


However, **Parliament can make laws to enforce them**.


---


# 5. Significance of Fundamental Duties


### 1. Reminder to Citizens


Encourages citizens to perform duties towards **nation and society**.


### 2. Promote National Unity


Discourages:

- Communalism
- Violence
- Anti-national activities

### 3. Promote Discipline & Patriotism


Encourages active participation in **national development**.


### 4. Aid in Constitutional Interpretation


Courts may consider laws implementing duties as **reasonable restrictions**.


---


# 6. Criticism of Fundamental Duties


| Criticism       | Explanation                                                     |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Not exhaustive  | Important duties like **voting and paying taxes** missing       |
| Vague language  | Terms like **scientific temper**, **composite culture** unclear |
| Non-justiciable | No direct legal enforcement                                     |
| Wrong placement | Should have been after **Fundamental Rights (Part III)**        |


---


# 7. Political Response


Even after the Emergency:

- **43rd Amendment (1977)** and **44th Amendment (1978)** removed several provisions of the **42nd Amendment**.
- **Fundamental Duties were retained**, showing **broad political acceptance**.

# 1. INTRODUCTION


Fundamental Duties are the **moral and civic obligations of citizens towards the nation**.


### Important Facts

- **Original Constitution (1950)** contained **Fundamental Rights only**, not Fundamental Duties.
- The framers believed duties were **inherent in Indian culture and society** and did not need explicit mention.
- However, the **duties of the State** were included in the Constitution through **Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV)**.

Later, Fundamental Duties were introduced.


### Timeline


| Year     | Development                                   |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **1950** | Constitution enforced (No Fundamental Duties) |
| **1976** | Fundamental Duties added                      |
| **2002** | One more duty added                           |


Thus today **Total Fundamental Duties = 11**.


---


# 2. ORIGIN OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES


The concept of Fundamental Duties in India is **borrowed from the Constitution of the USSR (Soviet Union)**.


### International Comparison


| Country   | Fundamental Duties |
| --------- | ------------------ |
| USA       | No list            |
| Canada    | No list            |
| France    | No list            |
| Germany   | No list            |
| Australia | No list            |
| Japan     | Contains duties    |
| USSR      | Contains duties    |


Most **democratic constitutions do not list duties explicitly**.


Socialist countries like **USSR placed equal importance on rights and duties**.


USSR principle:

> Exercise of rights is inseparable from performance of duties.

---


# 3. SWARAN SINGH COMMITTEE (1976)


### Background


During the **Internal Emergency (1975-1977)**, the government felt that citizens should be reminded about their duties.


Therefore, the **Congress Government established the Swaran Singh Committee in 1976**.


### Objective


To recommend **inclusion of Fundamental Duties in the Constitution**.


---


## Recommendations of the Committee


The committee recommended:


1️⃣ A **separate chapter on Fundamental Duties** should be added.


2️⃣ Citizens should realize that **along with rights they have duties**.


3️⃣ Initially **8 Fundamental Duties** were recommended.


---


## What the Government Did


The Government accepted the recommendation and passed:


### 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976)


Key features:

- Added **Part IVA** in the Constitution
- Introduced **Article 51A**
- Included **10 Fundamental Duties**

Thus:


| Part         | Content            |
| ------------ | ------------------ |
| **Part IVA** | Fundamental Duties |
| **Article**  | Article 51A        |


---


## Historical Claim


The Congress Government declared:

> Not including Fundamental Duties earlier was a **historical mistake**.

---


# 4. RECOMMENDATIONS NOT ACCEPTED


Some recommendations of the **Swaran Singh Committee were rejected**.


### 1. Punishment for violation


Committee recommended:

- Parliament should have power to impose **penalty for non-performance of duties**.

But **this was not included in the Constitution**.


---


### 2. Laws enforcing duties should not be challenged in court


Committee suggested:

- Such laws should **not be challenged for violation of Fundamental Rights**.

This suggestion was **also rejected**.


---


### 3. Duty to Pay Taxes


Committee proposed:

> Paying taxes should be a Fundamental Duty.

This was **not accepted**.


Thus **Paying taxes is NOT a Fundamental Duty in the Constitution**.


---


# 5. LIST OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES (ARTICLE 51A)


Article 51A states:

> It shall be the duty of every citizen of India:

---


## (a) Respect Constitution


To:

- Abide by the Constitution
- Respect its ideals and institutions
- Respect the **National Flag**
- Respect the **National Anthem**

---


## (b) Follow freedom struggle ideals


To cherish and follow:

- Noble ideals of **India’s freedom struggle**

---


## (c) Protect sovereignty


To uphold and protect:

- **Sovereignty**
- **Unity**
- **Integrity of India**

---


## (d) Defend the country


To:

- Defend the nation
- Render **national service when called upon**

---


## (e) Promote harmony


To promote:

- Harmony
- Spirit of **common brotherhood**

Beyond:

- Religion
- Language
- Region
- Sectional differences

Also includes:


➡ Renouncing practices **derogatory to dignity of women**


---


## (f) Preserve cultural heritage


To:

- Value and preserve **rich heritage of India’s composite culture**

---


## (g) Protect environment


To protect and improve:

- Forests
- Lakes
- Rivers
- Wildlife

Also:


➡ Show compassion for **living creatures**


---


## (h) Develop scientific temper


To promote:

- Scientific temper
- Humanism
- Spirit of inquiry
- Reform

---


## (i) Safeguard public property


To:

- Protect public property
- Abjure (avoid) violence

---


## (j) Strive (कोशिश करना) for excellence


To strive for excellence in:

- Individual activity
- Collective activity

So that the nation rises to:


➡ Higher levels of achievement


---


## (k) Education of children


To provide opportunities for **education to children aged 6-14 years**.


Added by:


### 86th Constitutional Amendment Act (2002)


This duty applies to:

- Parent or guardian.

# 6. FEATURES OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES


Important characteristics of Fundamental Duties:


---


## 1. Combination of Moral and Civic Duties


Fundamental Duties include **both moral and civic obligations**.


### Moral Duties


Based on **ethical values and traditions**.


Examples:

- Cherishing noble ideals of the freedom struggle
- Preserving composite culture
- Developing scientific temper

### Civic Duties


Related to **citizenship responsibilities toward the State**.


Examples:

- Respecting the Constitution
- Respecting the National Flag and Anthem
- Safeguarding public property

---


## 2. Codification of Indian Values


The duties represent values rooted in:

- Indian traditions
- Mythology
- Religions
- Cultural practices

Thus they are **essentially a codification of tasks integral to the Indian way of life**.


---


## 3. Applicable Only to Citizens


Unlike Fundamental Rights, which sometimes apply to **both citizens and foreigners**, Fundamental Duties apply **only to citizens of India**.


Examples of rights available to **all persons**:

- Article 14
- Article 20
- Article 21
- Article 21A
- Article 22
- Article 23
- Article 24
- Articles 25–28

But **Fundamental Duties are only for citizens**.


---


## 4. Non-Justiciable Nature


Fundamental Duties are **non-justiciable**, meaning:

- Courts **cannot directly enforce them**
- No **direct legal sanction** exists for violation

However:


➡ **Parliament can make laws to enforce them.**


Thus they are similar to **Directive Principles of State Policy** in this aspect.


---


# 7. CRITICISM OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES


Fundamental Duties have been criticized by several constitutional experts.


---


## 1. List is Not Exhaustive


The duties list does not include some important duties like:

- Voting in elections
- Paying taxes
- Family planning

The **Swaran Singh Committee recommended including the duty to pay taxes**, but it was not adopted.


---


## 2. Vague and Ambiguous Terms


Some duties are difficult for ordinary citizens to understand.


Examples:

- Noble ideals
- Composite culture
- Scientific temper

Different people may interpret them differently.


---


## 3. Non-Justiciable Character


Critics argue that since duties:

- Cannot be enforced by courts
- Have no punishment

They become merely a **code of moral precepts**.


---


## 4. Unnecessary Addition


Some critics argued that citizens would perform these duties **even if they were not included in the Constitution**.


Thus they considered the inclusion **superfluous**.


---


## 5. Wrong Placement in Constitution


Fundamental Duties were placed in **Part IVA**, after Directive Principles.


Critics believe they should have been placed **after Part III (Fundamental Rights)** so that both rights and duties would have equal importance.


---


# 8. SIGNIFICANCE OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES


Despite criticism, Fundamental Duties have significant importance.


---


## 1. Reminder to Citizens


They remind citizens that:

> Enjoying rights also involves performing duties.

Citizens must fulfil responsibilities toward:

- Nation
- Society
- Fellow citizens

---


## 2. Warning Against Anti-National Activities


They discourage actions such as:

- Burning the National Flag
- Destroying public property
- Promoting communal hatred

---


## 3. Promote Discipline and Patriotism


They encourage citizens to:

- Participate in national development
- Maintain discipline
- Work toward national goals

Thus citizens become **active participants rather than passive spectators**.


---


## 4. Help Courts in Constitutional Interpretation


The Supreme Court has ruled that:


If a law promotes Fundamental Duties, courts may consider it **reasonable**.


Important ruling:


### Supreme Court Judgement (1992)


While determining constitutionality of a law:


If the law seeks to implement a Fundamental Duty, it may be considered **reasonable under Articles 14 and 19**.


Thus duties indirectly influence judicial decisions.


---


## 5. Parliament Can Enforce Them


Even though duties are non-justiciable:


➡ Parliament can make laws imposing **penalties or punishments** for violating them.


---


# 9. JUSTIFICATION BY GOVERNMENT


### H. R. Gokhale (Law Minister)


He justified Fundamental Duties saying:


Some citizens were **not respecting the legal order**, particularly before the Emergency.


The duties would have a **sobering effect on anti-national activities**.


---


### Indira Gandhi (Prime Minister)


She argued that Fundamental Duties:

- Do not suppress rights
- Instead create **democratic balance**

Citizens should be as conscious of **duties as they are of rights**.


---


# 10. POLITICAL RESPONSE


The Opposition strongly opposed the inclusion of Fundamental Duties.


However, later developments showed **broad political acceptance**.


After the Emergency:


### Janata Government (Morarji Desai)


The government:

- Reversed many provisions of the **42nd Amendment**
- Passed the **43rd Amendment (1977)** and **44th Amendment (1978)**

But **did NOT remove Fundamental Duties**.


This indicates a **national consensus** regarding their necessity.


Later:


➡ **86th Amendment (2002)** added one more duty.

# 11. VERMA COMMITTEE (1999)

To identify **existing laws that enforce Fundamental Duties**.

# 12. LAWS IMPLEMENTING FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES


---


## 1. Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act (1971)


Prevents disrespect toward:

- Constitution
- National Flag
- National Anthem

---


## 2. Criminal Laws


Various criminal laws punish:

- Promotion of enmity between groups based on
    - Religion
    - Language
    - Race
    - Place of birth

---


## 3. Protection of Civil Rights Act (1955)


Provides punishment for offences related to:

- Caste discrimination
- Religious discrimination

Originally called:


➡ **Untouchability (Offences) Act**


---


## 4. Indian Penal Code (IPC)


IPC punishes acts that harm:

- National integration
- Unity of the country

---


## 5. Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (1967)


Allows the government to declare:


➡ Communal organizations as **unlawful associations**.


---


## 6. Representation of the People Act (1951)


Disqualifies MPs or MLAs for:

- Promoting communal hatred
- Seeking votes on religious grounds

---


## 7. Wildlife Protection Act (1972)


Protects:

- Rare species
- Endangered animals

---


## 8. Forest Conservation Act (1980)


Prevents:

- Indiscriminate deforestation
- Misuse of forest land

---


# 13. IMPORTANT CRITICS


---


## D. D. Chawla


He argued that duties should be written in **clear language**.


He criticized vague phrases like:

- Noble ideals
- Composite culture
- Scientific temper

---


## C. K. Daphtary (Former Attorney General)


He said:

> Most citizens are law-abiding; therefore, it was unnecessary to list duties.

---


## A. K. Sen


He criticized duties saying that:

- A democratic state should rely on **people's cooperation**
- Not act like a **school master forcing duties on citizens**.
