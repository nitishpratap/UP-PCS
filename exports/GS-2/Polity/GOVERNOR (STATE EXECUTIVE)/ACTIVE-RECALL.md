### Block A — State Executive & Constitutional Position

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1.</strong> [RECALL] Which Part and Articles of the Constitution deal with the State Executive? List all four components.</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

- **Part VI**; **Articles 153–167**
- Components: (1) **Governor**, (2) **Chief Minister**, (3) **Council of Ministers**, (4) **Advocate General**

**Key point:** There is **no Vice-Governor** (unlike Vice-President at Centre).

</details>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2.</strong> [RECALL] Why is the Governor called the nominal executive? Who holds real executive power?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

- Governor = **nominal / titular / constitutional head** (like President at Centre)
- **Real executive power** = **Council of Ministers headed by Chief Minister**
- System = **parliamentary** at state level

</details>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3.</strong> [RECALL] What are the two roles of the Governor in the federal system?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

1. **Constitutional head of State** — acts on **aid & advice** of CoM (Art. 163)
2. **Agent of the Centre** — represents **Union Government** in the state

</details>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4.</strong> [RECALL] Why was direct election of the Governor rejected? Which international model was adopted?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Rejected because:** conflict with parliamentary system & CM; nominal head; partisan/regional risks; Centre–State control issues; dual leadership.

**Model:** **Canadian** (Centre appoints) ✓ | **American** (direct election) ✗

</details>
</details>

- **Criminal proceedings** — not during tenure
- **Arrest/imprisonment** — not during tenure

<details>
<summary><strong>Q12.</strong> [RECALL] Under which Article is the oath taken? What three pledges? Who administers the oath?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

- **Art. 159**
- Pledges: faithfully execute office; **preserve/protect/defend Constitution**; serve **people of state**
- Administered by: **Chief Justice of HC** (or senior-most HC judge)

</details>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q13.</strong> [RECALL] What is the term of the Governor? Can the President remove without reasons? What happens after 5 years if no successor?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

- Resignation → **President**
- President **need not give reasons** for removal (B.P. Singhal: not arbitrary/mala fide)
- After 5 years: **continues until successor takes charge** (no vacuum)
- President may **transfer** or **reappoint**; controversies: **1989** (V.P. Singh), **1991** (14 removed by Rao)

</details>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

- Example: **Chief Justice of HC** may discharge Governor's functions

### Block F — Veto, Ordinance & Financial Powers

<details>
<summary><strong>Q25.</strong> [RECALL] Name the four veto powers of the Governor. What are Money Bill options for Governor vs President?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

| Veto | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Absolute | Withhold assent |
| Suspensive | Return for reconsideration |
| Pocket | No action (no time limit) |
| Reserved | Send to President |

**Money Bill:** Governor = assent / withhold / **reserve** — **cannot return** (same as President on return).

**Governor alone** can **reserve** bill for President (President cannot reserve state bills).

If bill **reserved for President** and repassed after President returns → President **NOT bound** to assent.

</details>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q26.</strong> [RECALL] Under Article 213, when can the Governor issue an ordinance? Validity period? When are President's instructions required?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

- When legislature **not in session**; immediate action; **Governor satisfied**
- Valid **6 weeks** after legislature **reassembly**; Governor may **withdraw anytime**
- **President's instructions** if: prior Presidential sanction needed | bill would be reserved | law invalid without President's assent

</details>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q27.</strong> [RECALL] List five financial powers of the Governor.</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

1. **Budget** (Annual Financial Statement) before legislature
2. **Money Bill** — prior recommendation of Governor
3. **Demand for Grants** — only on Governor's recommendation
4. **Contingency Fund** — advances for unforeseen expenses
5. **State Finance Commission** — every **5 years** (Panchayats/Municipalities)

</details>
</details>

### Block G — Judicial, Discretion, Special Responsibilities & Supplement

<details>
<summary><strong>Q28.</strong> [RECALL] Article 161 scope? Death sentence trap? Constitutional vs situational discretion? Special responsibility states? Key cases & commissions?</summary>

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Art. 161:** Pardon, reprieve, respite, remission, suspension, commutation — **state law offences** only. No court-martial.

**Death sentence:** Governor **cannot pardon**; can suspend/commute/remit; only **President pardons** death (Art. 72).

**Judicial role:** consulted on HC judges; appoints district judges (with HC) & judicial service (HC + SPSC).

**Constitutional discretion:** reserve bill, recommend **Art. 356**, UT administrator, tribal council royalty (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram), seek info from CM.

**Situational discretion:** hung assembly, CM death, loss of majority, dissolve assembly.

**Special responsibilities:** Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Sikkim, Arunachal, Karnataka (**98th CAA 2012**).

**Supplement:**
- **Shamsher Singh (1974):** real power with CoM; Art. **163(2)** discretion
- **SR Bommai (1994):** floor test; judicial review of Art. 356
- **B.P. Singhal (2010):** removal not arbitrary/mala fide
- **Sarkaria (1988) / Punchhi (2010):** CM consultation, floor test, depoliticise
- **Art. 174:** max **6 months** between sessions
- **Art. 164:** ministers' oath before Governor; non-MLA minister → elected within **6 months**
- **Governor vs LG:** Part VI Art. 155 vs Part VIII Art. 239

</details>
</details>
