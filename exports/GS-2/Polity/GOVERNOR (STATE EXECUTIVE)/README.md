
## STATE EXECUTIVE – GOVERNOR - Revision

---


# 1. STATE EXECUTIVE – BASIC STRUCTURE


**Part VI of Constitution**


| Feature           | Details                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Articles          | **153 – 167**                           |
| System            | **Parliamentary system at State level** |
| Nominal Executive | **Governor**                            |
| Real Executive    | **Council of Ministers headed by CM**   |


### Composition of State Executive

1. Governor
2. Chief Minister
3. Council of Ministers
4. Advocate General

❗ **No Vice-Governor (unlike Vice-President at Centre)**


---


# 2. GOVERNOR – CONSTITUTIONAL POSITION


| Feature    | Explanation                                 |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Status     | **Chief executive head of State**           |
| Nature     | **Nominal / Titular / Constitutional head** |
| Real power | **Council of Ministers led by CM**          |
| Model      | Similar to **President at Union level**     |


---


# 3. DUAL ROLE OF GOVERNOR


| Role                    | Explanation                                       |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Constitutional Head** | Works on **aid & advice of Council of Ministers** |
| **Agent of Centre**     | Represents **Union Government in State**          |


➡ Important for **Indian federal structure**


---


# 4. ONE GOVERNOR FOR MULTIPLE STATES


| Provision | Details                                               |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Amendment | **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**            |
| Provision | Same person can be **Governor of two or more states** |


Used frequently for **additional charge**.


---


# 5. APPOINTMENT OF GOVERNOR


| Feature     | Provision                                    |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Article     | **155**                                      |
| Appointment | **President appoints**                       |
| Method      | **Warrant under hand and seal of President** |
| Nature      | Governor is **nominee of Union Government**  |


---


# 6. SUPREME COURT VIEW (1979)


Governor is:


✔ **Independent constitutional authority**


❌ **Not an employee of Central Government**


---


# 7. WHY GOVERNOR IS NOT ELECTED


The **Constituent Assembly rejected direct election**.


### Reasons

1. Against **parliamentary system**
2. **Conflict with CM**
3. Governor is **nominal head**
4. Election becomes **political contest**
5. Governor may become **partisan**
6. Encourages **regionalism**
7. Centre loses **control over states**
8. **Dual leadership conflict**
9. CM may install **loyal candidate**

---


# 8. MODELS CONSIDERED


| Model              | System                       | Decision   |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- | ---------- |
| **American Model** | Governor elected             | ❌ Rejected |
| **Canadian Model** | Governor appointed by Centre | ✔ Adopted  |


India follows **Canadian model**.


---


# 9. QUALIFICATIONS OF GOVERNOR


**Article 157**


A person must:

1. **Citizen of India**
2. **Minimum age: 35 years**

---


# 10. CONVENTIONS (UNWRITTEN RULES)


### 1. Outsider Governor


Governor **should not belong to the same state**


Purpose → avoid **local politics**


---


### 2. Consultation with Chief Minister


President should **consult CM before appointment**


⚠ Often violated.


---


# 11. CONDITIONS OF GOVERNOR OFFICE


**Article 158**


| Condition              | Provision                     |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Legislature membership | Cannot be MP or MLA           |
| If appointed           | Seat automatically vacated    |
| Office of profit       | Cannot hold                   |
| Residence              | **Raj Bhavan (rent free)**    |
| Salary                 | Determined by **Parliament**  |
| Salary reduction       | **Not allowed during tenure** |


---


### Salary Revision


| Year              | Amount               |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Earlier           | ₹36,000              |
| **2008 revision** | **₹1.10 lakh/month** |


---


### Governor of Multiple States


Salary **shared by states** (decided by President).


---


# 12. PRIVILEGES & IMMUNITIES


Same as **President**


| Immunity             | Explanation                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Official acts        | Cannot be legally questioned   |
| Criminal proceedings | **Not allowed during tenure**  |
| Arrest               | Not possible                   |
| Civil cases          | Only after **2 months notice** |


---


# 13. OATH OF GOVERNOR


**Article 159**


Governor swears to:

1. **Faithfully execute office**
2. **Preserve, protect & defend Constitution**
3. **Serve people of state**

| Administered by |                                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Primary         | **Chief Justice of High Court** |
| Alternative     | Senior-most HC judge            |


---


# 14. TERM OF GOVERNOR


**Article 156**


| Feature  | Details                   |
| -------- | ------------------------- |
| Tenure   | **5 years**               |
| Reality  | **Not fixed**             |
| Doctrine | **Pleasure of President** |


President can remove **anytime**.


---


### Resignation


Governor resigns to **President**.


---


### No Security of Tenure


SC:


President **not required to give reasons**.


---

# 16. TRANSFER & REAPPOINTMENT


President may:


✔ Transfer Governor


✔ Reappoint Governor


---


# 17. CONTINUATION AFTER TERM


Governor continues **until successor takes charge**


Purpose → avoid **constitutional vacuum**

# 18. VACANCY IN OFFICE


If Governor dies / office vacant:

❌ No **diplomatic powers**


❌ No **military powers**


❌ No **emergency powers** (except recommending **Article 356**)

### Disqualification Decision


Governor decides disqualification of MLAs


BUT:


✔ Based on **Election Commission opinion**

| Option   | Result            |
| -------- | ----------------- |
| Assent   | Bill becomes law  |
| Withhold | Bill rejected     |
| Return   | Reconsideration   |
| Reserve  | Sent to President |


---


### Mandatory Reservation


When bill **endangers High Court powers**


---


### Optional Reservation


Governor may reserve if bill:

- Against Constitution
- Against DPSP
- Against national interest
- Of grave importance
- Property acquisition (Art 31A)

# ORDINANCE POWER


**Article 213**


Governor can issue **ordinance when legislature not in session**.


Conditions:

- Immediate action needed
- Governor satisfaction

---


### Validity


Ordinance must be approved within:


✔ **6 weeks of legislature reassembly**


---


### Governor May


✔ Withdraw ordinance anytime.


---


### PRESIDENT'S INSTRUCTIONS REQUIRED IF

1. Bill requires **Presidential sanction**
2. Bill should be **reserved for President**
3. Law invalid without **President's assent**

# 1. STATE EXECUTIVE (OVERVIEW)


The **Constitution of India provides the same parliamentary system in states as at the Centre.**


This means:

- **Nominal Executive → Governor**
- **Real Executive → Council of Ministers headed by Chief Minister**

The provisions related to **state executive are contained in Part VI of the Constitution.**


### Articles Covered


**Articles 153 – 167**


These articles deal with:

- Governor
- Chief Minister
- Council of Ministers
- Advocate General

### Composition of State Executive


State executive consists of:

1. Governor
2. Chief Minister
3. Council of Ministers
4. Advocate General

Important point:


❌ There is **no office of Vice-Governor** like the **Vice-President at the Centre.**


---


# 2. GOVERNOR – CONSTITUTIONAL POSITION


The **Governor is the chief executive head of the state.**


However:


Like the **President**, the governor is only a:

- **Nominal executive**
- **Titular head**
- **Constitutional head**

The **real executive power lies with the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister.**


---


# 3. DUAL ROLE OF GOVERNOR


The office of governor performs **two roles**.


### 1. Constitutional Head of State


He acts on **aid and advice of Council of Ministers**.


### 2. Agent of the Centre


He represents the **Union Government in the State**.


This dual role is important for **Indian federal system**.


---


# 4. ONE GOVERNOR FOR MULTIPLE STATES


Normally:


✔ One governor → One state


However:


The **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956** made it possible to:


✔ Appoint **same person as governor of two or more states**


This is frequently used in India.


Example (general concept for exams):

- Same governor may hold **additional charge** of another state.

---


# 5. APPOINTMENT OF GOVERNOR


The governor is **not elected**.


He is **appointed by the President**.


### Appointment Method


The governor is appointed:


**By the President by warrant under his hand and seal**


Thus:


Governor is **nominee of the Central Government**.


---


# 6. SUPREME COURT VIEW (IMPORTANT)


Supreme Court (1979) held:


Governor is **NOT an employee of Central Government**.


Important points:

- It is an **independent constitutional office**
- Not subordinate to the Central Government

---


# 7. WHY GOVERNOR IS NOT ELECTED


Originally, the **Draft Constitution proposed direct election of governor**.


But the **Constituent Assembly rejected it**.


### Reasons for Rejecting Direct Election


### 1. Incompatible with Parliamentary System


Direct election would conflict with the **parliamentary system in states**.


---


### 2. Conflict Between Governor and Chief Minister


Direct election would give **separate mandate**.


This may lead to:


Governor vs Chief Minister power conflict.


---


### 3. Governor is Only Nominal Head


Since governor is **constitutional head**, expensive election is unnecessary.


---


### 4. Election Based on Personal Issues


Governor election may become:

- Personality based
- Political contest

This is **not desirable**.


---


### 5. Governor May Become Partisan


An elected governor may belong to a **political party**.


This would destroy neutrality.


---


### 6. Separatist Tendencies


Direct election may create:

- Regionalism
- Political instability

---


### 7. Centre Control over States


Presidential appointment ensures **central influence over states**.


---


### 8. Leadership Conflict During Elections


Direct election could create **dual leadership issues**.


---


### 9. Chief Minister Influence


Chief Minister may try to elect **loyal candidate**.


Thus governor could become **second-rate party member**.


---


# 8. MODELS CONSIDERED


Two international models were considered:


### American Model


Governor directly elected.


❌ Rejected


---


### Canadian Model


Governor appointed by **Governor General (Centre)**.


✔ Accepted


India adopted **Canadian model**.


---


# 9. QUALIFICATIONS OF GOVERNOR


The Constitution prescribes **two qualifications**.


A person must:

1. Be a **citizen of India**
2. Have **completed 35 years of age**

These are given under **Article 157**.


---


# 10. CONVENTIONS (UNWRITTEN RULES)


Over time, **two conventions developed**.


### 1. Governor Should Be an Outsider


Governor should **not belong to the state where appointed**.


Reason:


✔ Keeps him free from local politics.


---


### 2. Consultation with Chief Minister


While appointing governor:


President should **consult the Chief Minister of the concerned state**.


Purpose:


✔ Smooth functioning of constitutional machinery.


However:


⚠ These conventions have been **violated in some cases**.


---


# 11. CONDITIONS OF GOVERNOR’S OFFICE


_(Article 158)_


The Constitution provides the following conditions:


---


### 1. No Membership of Legislature


Governor **cannot be a member of**:

- Parliament
- State Legislature

If a member is appointed governor:


✔ He automatically vacates his seat.


---


### 2. No Office of Profit


Governor cannot hold:


✔ Any **other office of profit**


---


### 3. Official Residence


Governor is entitled to:


✔ Official residence **Raj Bhavan**


✔ Without paying rent


---


### 4. Salary and Allowances


Governor receives:

- Emoluments
- Allowances
- Privileges

These are determined by **Parliament**.


---


### 5. Governor for Multiple States


If same person is governor of multiple states:


Salary is shared among states.


Distribution decided by **President**.


---


### 6. Salary Cannot Be Reduced


Governor’s emoluments **cannot be reduced during tenure**.


---


### Salary Increase (Important Fact)


In **2008**, Parliament increased salary:


From:


₹36,000


To:


₹1.10 lakh per month


---


# 12. PRIVILEGES AND IMMUNITIES


Like the President, the Governor enjoys **legal immunity**.


### 1. No Legal Liability for Official Acts


Governor cannot be sued for **official actions**.


---


### 2. Criminal Proceedings Not Allowed


During tenure:


❌ No criminal proceedings can be initiated.


---


### 3. No Arrest or Imprisonment


Governor **cannot be arrested or jailed**.


---


### 4. Civil Cases Allowed With Notice


Civil proceedings can be initiated only after:


✔ **Two months notice**


---


# 13. OATH OF GOVERNOR


_(Article 159)_


Before entering office, governor must take **oath or affirmation**.


In the oath, governor promises:

1. To **faithfully execute the office**
2. To **preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and law**
3. To **serve the people of the state**

---


### Who Administers the Oath?


Oath is administered by:


✔ **Chief Justice of the High Court**


If unavailable:


✔ **Senior-most judge of High Court**


---


# 14. TERM OF GOVERNOR


_(Article 156)_


The governor holds office for:


✔ **5 years**


But this is **not guaranteed**.


---


## Doctrine of Pleasure


Governor holds office **during the pleasure of the President**.


This means:


✔ President can remove governor anytime.


---


### Resignation


Governor may resign by writing to:


✔ **President**


---


### No Security of Tenure


Supreme Court held:


Governor does **not have fixed tenure security**.


---


### No Grounds Required for Removal


The Constitution **does not specify grounds** for removal.


Thus President can remove governor **without giving reason**.


---


# 15. POLITICAL CONTROVERSIES (IMPORTANT FOR MAINS)


Change of government often leads to replacement of governors.


Examples mentioned:


### 1989


National Front government (V.P. Singh)


Asked governors appointed by Congress to resign.


---


### 1991


Congress government (P.V. Narasimha Rao)


Removed **14 governors** appointed by previous governments.


---


# 16. TRANSFER AND REAPPOINTMENT


President may:


✔ Transfer governor to another state.


✔ Reappoint governor in same or different state.


---


# 17. CONTINUATION AFTER TERM


Governor continues in office **even after 5 years** until successor takes charge.


Purpose:


✔ Avoid constitutional vacuum.


---


# 18. VACANCY IN GOVERNOR OFFICE


If governor dies or office becomes vacant:


President can make arrangements.


Example:


✔ Chief Justice of High Court may discharge functions temporarily.

# GOVERNOR – POWERS AND FUNCTIONS


The **Governor has powers similar to the President** of India.


However, unlike the President, the Governor **does not possess:**


❌ Diplomatic powers


❌ Military powers


❌ Emergency powers


(Except recommending **President’s Rule**)


---

The powers of the Governor are classified into **four categories**:

1. Executive Powers
2. Legislative Powers
3. Financial Powers
4. Judicial Powers

# 1. EXECUTIVE POWERS OF GOVERNOR


The executive powers of the Governor relate to **administration of the state government**.


---


## 1. Executive Actions in His Name


All executive actions of the **state government are taken in the name of the Governor**. **Article 166**.


Example:


Orders issued by departments are issued **in the name of the Governor**.


---


## 2. Rules for Authentication of Orders


Governor makes rules specifying:

- How orders issued in his name are authenticated.

This ensures **legal validity of government orders**.


---


## 3. Rules for Conduct of Government Business


Governor can make rules regarding:

- **Convenient transaction of government business**
- **Allocation of work among ministers**

---


## 4. Appointment of Chief Minister


Governor appoints:


✔ **Chief Minister**


Usually:


Leader of **majority party in Legislative Assembly**.


---


## 5. Appointment of Council of Ministers


Governor appoints other ministers **on advice of Chief Minister**.


Important:


Ministers hold office **during pleasure of Governor**.


However in practice:


✔ Pleasure means **advice of Chief Minister**.


---


## 6. Tribal Welfare Minister


Governor appoints **Tribal Welfare Minister** in certain states.


States:

- Chhattisgarh
- Jharkhand
- Madhya Pradesh
- Odisha

Important amendment:


**94th Constitutional Amendment Act (2006)**


➡ Bihar was removed from this requirement.


---


## 7. Appointment of Advocate General - Article 165


Governor appoints:


✔ **Advocate General of the State**


Functions:

- Highest law officer of state
- Advises government on legal matters.

Advocate General holds office:


✔ During **pleasure of Governor**


---


## 8. Appointment of State Election Commissioner


Governor appoints:


✔ **State Election Commissioner**


Responsibilities:


Conduct elections for:

- Panchayats
- Municipalities

Removal:


Can only be removed **like a High Court judge**.


Thus:


✔ Governor cannot remove arbitrarily.


---


## 9. Appointment of State Public Service Commission


Governor appoints:


✔ Chairman of State Public Service Commission


✔ Members of State Public Service Commission


However:


Removal power lies with:


✔ **President of India**


---


## 10. Information from Chief Minister - Article 167


Governor can ask Chief Minister to provide information regarding:

- Administration of state
- Legislative proposals

This ensures **constitutional accountability**.


---


## 11. Reconsideration by Council of Ministers


Governor can require the Chief Minister:


To place before Council of Ministers any decision taken by **individual minister**.


Purpose:


✔ Collective responsibility.


---


## 12. Recommendation of President’s Rule


Governor can recommend to President:


✔ **Imposition of President’s Rule (Article 356)**.


During President’s Rule:


Governor acts as **agent of President**.


---


## 13. Chancellor of State Universities


In most states:


Governor acts as:


✔ **Chancellor of universities**


Functions include:

- Appointment of Vice-Chancellors
- Supervisory role in universities.

---


# 2. LEGISLATIVE POWERS OF GOVERNOR


Governor is **an integral part of the state legislature**.


This means legislature consists of:

- Governor
- Legislative Assembly
- Legislative Council (if present)

---


## 1. Summoning the Legislature


Governor can:


✔ Summon the legislature.


Meaning:


Call legislature to meet.


---


## 2. Proroguing Legislature


Governor can:


✔ Prorogue the legislature


Meaning:


End the session of legislature.


---


## 3. Dissolution of Legislative Assembly


Governor can:


✔ Dissolve the Legislative Assembly.


However:


Usually done on advice of **Chief Minister**.


---


## 4. Special Address


Governor addresses legislature:


1️⃣ First session after **general elections**


2️⃣ First session of **each year**


Purpose:


Explain **government policies and programs**.


---


## 5. Sending Messages to Legislature


Governor may send messages regarding:

- Pending bills
- Legislative matters

---


## 6. Appointment of Presiding Officer in Certain Situations


Governor can appoint a member to preside over proceedings when:


### Assembly


If both:

- Speaker
- Deputy Speaker

are vacant.


---


### Legislative Council


If both:

- Chairman
- Deputy Chairman

are vacant.


---


## 7. Nomination to Legislative Council


Governor nominates **1/6th of members** of Legislative Council.


From persons having special knowledge in:

- Literature
- Science
- Art
- Cooperative movement
- Social service

---


## 8. Nomination of Anglo-Indian Member


Governor can nominate:


✔ **One Anglo-Indian member to Legislative Assembly**


(if community not adequately represented).


(Note: this provision was removed by **104th Amendment**, but Laxmikanth chapter still mentions original provision.)


---


## 9. Disqualification of Legislators


Governor decides questions of:


✔ **Disqualification of members of state legislature**


But:


Decision is taken **after consulting Election Commission**.


---


## 10. Assent to Bills


After a bill is passed by the state legislature, it is sent to the Governor.


Governor has **four options**.


### 1. Give Assent


Bill becomes **law**.


---


### 2. Withhold Assent


Bill **does not become law**.


---


### 3. Return Bill for Reconsideration


Applicable only to:


✔ Ordinary bills


❌ Not Money Bills


If legislature passes bill again:


Governor **must give assent**.


---


### 4. Reserve Bill for President


Governor may reserve bill for **President’s consideration**.


---


## Cases Where Reservation Is Mandatory


Reservation is compulsory when:


✔ Bill **endangers position of High Court**


---


## Situations Where Governor May Reserve Bill


Governor may reserve bill if it is:


1️⃣ Against Constitution (Ultra vires)


2️⃣ Against Directive Principles of State Policy


3️⃣ Against national interest


4️⃣ Of grave national importance


5️⃣ Related to compulsory acquisition of property (Article 31A)


---


## 11. Ordinance Making Power - Article 213


Governor can issue **ordinances**.


Condition:


Legislature **not in session**.


Ordinance has:


✔ Same force as law.


---


### Validity of Ordinance


Must be approved by legislature within:


✔ **6 weeks of reassembly**


Governor may also:


✔ Withdraw ordinance anytime.


This is one of the **most important legislative powers**.


---


## 12. Reports Laid Before Legislature


Governor lays the following reports before legislature:


1️⃣ State Finance Commission report


2️⃣ State Public Service Commission report


3️⃣ CAG report on state accounts


---

Governor plays important role in **state finances**.


---


## 1. State Budget


Governor ensures:


✔ **Annual Financial Statement (Budget)** is laid before legislature.

## 2. Introduction of Money Bill


Money bill can be introduced only with:

No demand for grant can be made except:

Governor can make advances from:


✔ **State Contingency Fund**


Purpose:


Meet **unforeseen expenses**.


---


## 5. Finance Commission for Local Bodies


Governor constitutes:

Every:


✔ **5 years**


Purpose:


Review financial position of:

- Panchayats

# 4. JUDICIAL POWERS OF GOVERNOR - 161


Governor has limited judicial powers.


---


## 1. Pardoning Power


Governor can grant:

- Pardon
- Reprieve
- Respite
- Remission
- Suspension
- Commutation

For offences related to:


✔ **State laws**


---


## 2. Appointment of High Court Judges (Consultation)


President appoints High Court judges.


But:


Governor of the state is **consulted** during appointment.


---


## 3. Appointment of District Judges


Governor appoints:


✔ District Judges


In consultation with:


✔ High Court.


---


## 4. Appointment of Judicial Officers


Governor appoints persons to **state judicial service** (other than district judges).


Consultation with:

- High Court
- State Public Service Commission

# 1. VETO POWERS OF GOVERNOR


After a bill is passed by the **state legislature**, it is sent to the **Governor** for assent.


The Governor has **four veto options**.


---


# TYPES OF VETO OF GOVERNOR


### 1. Absolute Veto


Governor **withholds assent** to the bill.


Result:


✔ Bill **does not become law**


Used mainly when:

- Bill passed by previous government
- Private member bill

---


### 2. Suspensive Veto


Governor returns the bill for reconsideration.


Important:


✔ Applicable only to **ordinary bills**


If legislature passes the bill again:


Governor **must give assent**.


---


### 3. Pocket Veto


Governor **does not take any action** on the bill.


Since Constitution **does not prescribe time limit**, bill remains pending.


---


### 4. Reserved Veto


Governor reserves bill for **President's consideration**.


Then President decides fate of bill.


---


# COMPARISON: PRESIDENT VS GOVERNOR VETO


---


# WITH RESPECT TO ORDINARY BILLS


### PRESIDENT


After bill is passed by Parliament:


President has **three options**


1️⃣ Give assent → Bill becomes law


2️⃣ Withhold assent → Bill rejected


3️⃣ Return bill for reconsideration


If Parliament passes bill again:


✔ President **must give assent**


Thus President has **Suspensive Veto only**.


---


### GOVERNOR


Governor has **four options**


1️⃣ Give assent


2️⃣ Withhold assent


3️⃣ Return bill for reconsideration


4️⃣ Reserve bill for President


Important difference:


✔ Governor can **reserve bill for President**


---


# WHEN BILL RESERVED FOR PRESIDENT


If Governor sends bill to President:


Governor has **no further role**.


President has three options:


1️⃣ Give assent → Bill becomes law


2️⃣ Withhold assent → Bill rejected


3️⃣ Return bill for reconsideration


Important:


If legislature passes bill again:


President **is NOT bound** to give assent.


---


# MONEY BILL VETO


---


### PRESIDENT


For money bill:


President has **two options**


1️⃣ Give assent


2️⃣ Withhold assent


President **cannot return money bill**.


---


### GOVERNOR


Governor has **three options**


1️⃣ Give assent


2️⃣ Withhold assent


3️⃣ Reserve for President


Governor **cannot return money bill**.


---


# IMPORTANT EXAM TRAP


President → cannot return money bill.


Governor → cannot return money bill but **can reserve it for President**.


---


# 2. ORDINANCE MAKING POWER


Ordinance is a **temporary law** issued when legislature is not in session.


---


# GOVERNOR ORDINANCE POWER


Governor can promulgate ordinance when:


✔ Legislature **not in session**


Conditions:

- Immediate action necessary
- Satisfaction of Governor

Ordinance has **same force as law**.


---


# VALIDITY OF ORDINANCE


Ordinance must be approved within:


✔ **6 weeks of legislature reassembly**


Otherwise:


✔ Ordinance **ceases to operate**


---


# WITHDRAWAL OF ORDINANCE


Governor may withdraw ordinance **anytime**.


---


# IMPORTANT POINT


Governor **cannot issue ordinance in certain cases without President’s instructions**.


---


# CASES WHERE PRESIDENT'S INSTRUCTIONS REQUIRED


Governor must obtain President's instructions if:


### 1. Bill Requires Prior Presidential Sanction


Example:


Certain constitutional provisions.


---


### 2. Bill Would Be Reserved for President


If governor thinks such bill should be reserved.


---


### 3. Law Would Be Invalid Without President’s Assent


Example:


If subject affects **Union powers**.


---


# COMPARISON: PRESIDENT VS GOVERNOR ORDINANCE


| Feature                    | President                   | Governor                         |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Legislature not in session | Yes                         | Yes                              |
| Satisfaction required      | Yes                         | Yes                              |
| Scope                      | Parliament subjects         | State legislature subjects       |
| Force                      | Same as Act of Parliament   | Same as Act of State Legislature |
| Withdrawal                 | Allowed                     | Allowed                          |
| Advice                     | PM and Council of Ministers | CM and Council of Ministers      |
| Time limit                 | 6 weeks                     | 6 weeks                          |


---


# 3. PARDONING POWER


Both President and Governor have power to **grant clemency**.


Types include:


1️⃣ Pardon


2️⃣ Reprieve


3️⃣ Respite


4️⃣ Remission


5️⃣ Suspension


6️⃣ Commutation


---


# PRESIDENT VS GOVERNOR PARDONING POWER


| Feature             | President    | Governor      |
| ------------------- | ------------ | ------------- |
| Laws covered        | Central laws | State laws    |
| Death sentence      | Can pardon   | Cannot pardon |
| Court martial cases | Yes          | No            |


---


# IMPORTANT POINT


Governor **cannot pardon death sentence**.


But he can:


✔ Suspend


✔ Remit


✔ Commute


Death sentence.


Only **President can pardon death sentence**.


---


# 4. CONSTITUTIONAL POSITION OF GOVERNOR


India follows **parliamentary system in states**.


Thus:


Governor = **Nominal executive**


Real executive = **Council of Ministers headed by Chief Minister**


---


# IMPORTANT ARTICLES


### Article 154


Executive power of state vested in **Governor**.


---


### Article 163


Council of Ministers **aid and advise Governor**.


Exception:


Governor may act **in discretion**.


---


### Article 164


Council of Ministers **collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly**.


This forms basis of **parliamentary government in states**.


---


# GOVERNOR VS PRESIDENT (IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE)


### 1. Discretionary Power


Governor:


✔ Can act in **discretion (the freedom and power to make decisions by yourself)**


President:


❌ No discretionary power.


---


### 2. Binding Advice


After **42nd Amendment (1976)**:


President **must follow ministerial advice**.


But Constitution **does not explicitly bind Governor**.


---


# 5. DISCRETIONARY POWERS OF GOVERNOR


Governor can act **without advice of Council of Ministers** in certain cases.


---


# CONSTITUTIONAL DISCRETION


These are explicitly mentioned in Constitution.


---


### 1. Reserving Bill for President


Governor may reserve bill for **President’s consideration**.


---


### 2. Recommendation of President’s Rule


Governor may recommend:


✔ **Article 356 (President’s Rule)**.


---


### 3. Administrator of Union Territory


If given additional charge.


---


### 4. Determining Royalty to Tribal District Councils


Applicable to:

- Assam
- Meghalaya
- Tripura
- Mizoram

Governor decides royalty from mineral exploration.


---


### 5. Seeking Information from Chief Minister


Governor may ask information on:

- Administration
- Legislation

---


# 6. SITUATIONAL DISCRETION


These arise due to **political situations**.


---


### 1. Appointment of Chief Minister


When:


✔ No party has majority.


---


### 2. Death of Chief Minister


Governor selects **successor** if leadership unclear.


---


### 3. Dismissal of Council of Ministers


If government **loses majority**.


---


### 4. Dissolution of Assembly


If ministry loses majority.


---

Governor performs certain responsibilities **as directed by President**.


In these matters:
