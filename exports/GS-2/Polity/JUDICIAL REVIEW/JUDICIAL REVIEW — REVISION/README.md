# 1. ORIGIN OF JUDICIAL REVIEW


### Origin


| Feature       | Details                                      |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Country       | **United States of America**                 |
| Landmark Case | **Marbury v. Madison (1803)**                |
| Judge         | **Chief Justice John Marshall**              |
| Principle     | Courts can **declare laws unconstitutional** |


---


### Judicial Review in India


| Feature           | Details                             |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Source            | **Provided by Constitution itself** |
| Courts exercising | **Supreme Court + High Courts**     |
| Nature            | **Explicit constitutional power**   |
| Status            | **Basic Structure of Constitution** |


➡ Declared in **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**


Therefore Parliament **cannot abolish or curtail judicial review even through amendment**.


---


# 2. MEANING OF JUDICIAL REVIEW


### Definition


**Judicial Review = Power of judiciary to examine constitutional validity of**


| Examined Acts          | Authority                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Legislative enactments | Parliament + State Legislatures |
| Executive actions      | Central + State governments     |


---


### Court can declare law

- **Unconstitutional**
- **Illegal**
- **Invalid**
- **Null and Void**

Such law **cannot be enforced**.


---


# 3. CLASSIFICATION OF JUDICIAL REVIEW


Classification by **Justice Syed Shah Mohamed Quadri**


| Type                            | Meaning                                 | Example                        |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **1 Constitutional Amendments** | Check violation of **Basic Structure**  | **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** |
| **2 Legislative Acts**          | Laws of Parliament / State legislatures | Invalid if unconstitutional    |
| **3 Administrative Actions**    | Actions of government authorities       | Ultra vires actions invalid    |

# 8. JUDICIAL REVIEW — INDIA VS USA


### India


Follows:


### **Procedure Established by Law**


Courts examine:

- Whether law is **within authority**
- Whether **constitutional procedure followed**

Courts **do not examine**

- Reasonableness
- Policy wisdom

---


### USA


Follows:


### **Due Process of Law**


Courts examine:

1. Procedural fairness
2. Substantive validity
3. Reasonableness

Therefore **judicial review is broader**.


---


# DIFFERENCE — DUE PROCESS vs PROCEDURE ESTABLISHED


| Feature           | Due Process                | Procedure Established |
| ----------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Origin            | USA                        | UK / India            |
| Judicial power    | Wide                       | Limited               |
| Courts check      | Procedure + Reasonableness | Only legality         |
| Judicial activism | High                       | Moderate              |


---


# CRITICISM IN USA


Due Process powers make US Supreme Court:

- **Third Chamber of Legislature**
- **Super Legislature**
- **Arbiter of Social Policy**

---


# INDIAN SYSTEM — MIDDLE PATH


India adopts **synthesis of two systems**


| From USA           | From UK                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Judicial Review    | Parliamentary sovereignty |
| Judicial supremacy | Legislative primacy       |


---


# LIMITATIONS ON PARLIAMENTARY SOVEREIGNTY IN INDIA


Indian Parliament **not fully sovereign**.


Limited by:

1. Written Constitution
2. Federal system
3. Division of powers
4. Fundamental Rights
5. Judicial Review

➡ **Constitution is supreme**

# IMPORTANT CASE — I.R. COELHO CASE (2007)


Major ruling:


**Ninth Schedule laws not completely immune from judicial review**


---


### Key Principle


Judicial Review = **Basic Structure**


Therefore Parliament **cannot bypass courts by putting laws in Ninth Schedule**


---


# CUT OFF DATE


### **24 April 1973**


Date of **Kesavananda Bharati judgement**


---


### Rule


| Laws Added               | Judicial Review            |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| **Before 24 April 1973** | Protected                  |
| **After 24 April 1973**  | Subject to judicial review |


Especially if violating:

- **Article 14**
- **Article 19**
- **Article 21**

---


# TESTS INTRODUCED BY COURT


### 1 Rights Test


Court examines whether law destroys **Fundamental Rights forming part of Basic Structure**


---


### 2 Direct Impact Test


Court examines **actual impact of law**, not just form.


---


# IMPORTANT CONCLUSIONS OF I.R. COELHO


1️⃣ Law violating **basic structure** can be invalid even if in Ninth Schedule


2️⃣ Each amendment judged **individually**


3️⃣ Amendments after **24 April 1973** subject to review


4️⃣ Ninth Schedule protection **not absolute**


5️⃣ If law already upheld earlier → cannot be reopened


6️⃣ Past actions already completed **cannot be reopened**
