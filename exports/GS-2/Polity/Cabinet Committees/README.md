# INTRODUCTION & FEATURES OF CABINET COMMITTEES


## Meaning of Cabinet Committees


Cabinet Committees are **smaller groups of ministers formed from the Council of Ministers** to assist the Cabinet in the efficient conduct of government business.


They perform functions such as:

- **Policy formulation**
- **Coordination between ministries**
- **Decision making**
- **Reducing the workload of the Cabinet**

These committees help the Cabinet to **focus on major policy matters**, while routine and specialised issues are handled by committees.


---


# Constitutional Status


Cabinet Committees are **extra-constitutional bodies**.


Meaning:

- They are **not mentioned in the Constitution of India**.
- They are **created under the Rules of Business of the Government of India**.

Thus:


| Aspect                  | Status                |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| Mention in Constitution | ❌ No                  |
| Created under           | Rules of Business     |
| Nature                  | Executive arrangement |


---


# Types of Cabinet Committees


Cabinet Committees are of **two types**:


## 1. Standing Cabinet Committees

- Permanent in nature
- Deal with **regular policy issues**
- Continue for long periods

Examples:

- Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs
- Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs

---


## 2. Ad Hoc Cabinet Committees

- Temporary committees
- Created for **specific issues**
- Dissolved after the task is completed

Example:

- **Emergency Committee set up in 1962 after the Chinese invasion**

---


# Key Features of Cabinet Committees


## 1. Extra-constitutional bodies


They are **not mentioned in the Constitution**, but **Rules of Business provide for their establishment**.


---


## 2. Two types


Cabinet Committees are:

- **Standing (Permanent)**
- **Ad hoc (Temporary)**

Ad hoc committees are created to deal with **special problems** and are **disbanded after completing their task**.


---


## 3. Formed by the Prime Minister

- They are **set up by the Prime Minister**.
- Their **number, name and composition vary from time to time** depending on government requirements.

---


## 4. Membership


Usually **3 to 8 ministers** are members of a cabinet committee.


Members may include:

- Cabinet Ministers
- Sometimes **Ministers of State or non-cabinet ministers**

---


## 5. Composition


Members include:

- Ministers responsible for the subject concerned
- Other senior ministers for coordination purposes

---


## 6. Chairmanship


Cabinet Committees are **mostly headed by the Prime Minister**.


However sometimes:

- **Home Minister**
- **Finance Minister**

may act as chairman.


Important rule:


➡ If the **Prime Minister is a member**, he **always presides over the committee**.


---


## 7. Role in Decision Making


Cabinet Committees:

- **Examine issues in detail**
- **Formulate proposals for the Cabinet**
- Sometimes **take decisions themselves**

However:


➡ The **Cabinet can review their decisions**.


---


## 8. Administrative Importance


Cabinet Committees serve as an **organisational device** to:

- Reduce the heavy workload of the Cabinet
- Allow **in-depth examination of policy issues**
- Improve **coordination among ministries**

They operate on principles of:

- **Division of labour**
- **Delegation of authority**

# Meaning of Groups of Ministers


Apart from Cabinet Committees, the Government of India constituted **Groups of Ministers (GoMs)** to examine specific issues.


Purpose:

- Look into **particular policy issues**
- Improve **coordination among ministries**
- Provide **recommendations to the Cabinet**

Some GoMs were **empowered to take decisions**, while others only **gave recommendations**.


---


# Nature of GoMs


GoMs are:

- **Ad hoc bodies**
- Created for **specific issues**
- Dissolved after completing their work

Members usually include ministers heading the **concerned ministries**.


---


# Importance of GoMs


For many years GoMs were an **important coordination mechanism** between ministries.


They served as:

- A **coordination instrument**
- A platform to address **critical policy problems**

---


# Groups of Ministers in 2013


In **2013**, there were **21 GoMs** functioning.


Examples included GoMs on:

- Water management
- Civil aviation
- Power sector issues
- Coal mining environmental issues
- Land Acquisition Bill
- National Skill Development Authority
- Reforms in Central Public Sector Enterprises
- Reviving BSNL and MTNL
- Administrative reforms
- Media policy

---


# Empowered Groups of Ministers (EGoMs)


Some GoMs were given **decision-making powers**.


These were called:


➡ **Empowered Groups of Ministers (EGoMs)**


Unlike normal GoMs:

- They could **take decisions directly on behalf of the Cabinet**

---


# EGoMs in 2013


In **2013**, there were **6 Empowered Groups of Ministers**.


Examples included EGoMs on:

- Gas pricing
- Ultra Mega Power Projects
- MRTS
- Spectrum auctions
- CPSE share price decisions
- Drought management

---


# Observations of Second Administrative Reforms Commission


The **Second ARC (2005-2009)** reviewed the functioning of GoMs.


Key observations:


### Too many GoMs


Large numbers resulted in:

- Committees **not meeting regularly**
- **Delay in decision making**

---


### Selective use recommended


ARC suggested:

- GoMs should be used **selectively**
- They should be **empowered to take decisions**

---


### Time limits required


ARC recommended:

- **Clear mandate**
- **Time limits for completion of work**

---


# Abolition of GoMs and EGoMs


On **31 May 2014**, the government led by **Prime Minister Narendra Modi abolished all GoMs and EGoMs**.


Purpose:

- Faster decision-making
- Greater accountability of ministries

Pending matters were transferred directly to **ministries and departments**.


---


# Informal Groups of Ministers (2015)


In **April 2015**, reports suggested formation of **informal Groups of Ministers**.


Key points:

- Around **16 informal GoMs**
- Review proposals **before they reach the Cabinet**
- Once cleared, Cabinet approval becomes easier

---


# Leadership of Informal GoMs


Informal Groups of Ministers **do not have a fixed chairman**.

- Their composition is decided by the **Prime Minister depending on the issue**.
- A **senior minister may coordinate discussions**.
- Earlier, **Arun Jaitley informally chaired several such groups**, but this was **not an institutional position**.

---


# Issues Examined by Informal GoMs


Examples:

- Juvenile Justice Act amendments
- Internet governance guidelines
- Child Labour Act amendment
- Bureau of Indian Standards Bill
