
## 1. Meaning of Political Parties


A **political party** is a **voluntary association or organised group of individuals** who:

- Share the **same political views**
- Try to **gain political power through constitutional means**
- Work to **promote national interest**

### Key Points

- Parties act as a **link between people and government**
- They **contest elections**
- They try to **form government**
- They influence **policy making**

---


# 2. Types of Political Parties (Based on Ideology)


Political parties are classified into **four categories** based on ideology.


| Type                 | Meaning                                               |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Reactionary parties  | Support old socio-economic and political institutions |
| Conservative parties | Support **status quo** (existing system)              |
| Liberal parties      | Want **reforms in existing institutions**             |
| Radical parties      | Want **complete change / overthrow existing system**  |


---


## Ideological Spectrum


Political scientists classify parties as:


| Position | Party Type                 |
| -------- | -------------------------- |
| Left     | Radical                    |
| Centre   | Liberal                    |
| Right    | Conservative / Reactionary |


Thus:

- **Leftist parties**
- **Centrist parties**
- **Rightist parties**

---


## Examples in India


| Ideology | Example                  |
| -------- | ------------------------ |
| Leftist  | CPI, CPM                 |
| Centrist | Indian National Congress |
| Rightist | BJP                      |


---


# 3. Party Systems in the World


Three types of **party systems** exist globally.


## 1. One-Party System


Characteristics

- Only **one ruling party exists**
- **Opposition not allowed**

Example

- Former **USSR**
- East European communist states

---


## 2. Two-Party System


Characteristics

- Two major parties dominate
- Power alternates between them

Examples


| Country | Parties                 |
| ------- | ----------------------- |
| USA     | Democratic & Republican |
| Britain | Conservative & Labour   |


---


## 3. Multi-Party System


Characteristics

- **Many political parties exist**
- Often leads to **coalition governments**

Examples

- France
- Switzerland
- Italy

---


# PART 2 — PARTY SYSTEM IN INDIA


India follows a **Multi-Party System**.


The Indian party system has several **distinct characteristics**.


---


# 1. Multi-Party System


India has **a very large number of political parties**.


### Reasons

- Large **geographical size**
- **Social diversity**
- **Universal adult franchise**
- Complex **political processes**

Thus India has **one of the largest numbers of political parties in the world**.


---


### Political Parties in India (2014)


On the eve of **16th Lok Sabha elections (2014)**:


| Type                            | Number |
| ------------------------------- | ------ |
| National parties                | 6      |
| State parties                   | 47     |
| Registered-unrecognised parties | 1593   |


India includes **all types of parties**

- Left parties
- Right parties
- Centrist parties
- Communal parties
- Non-communal parties

### Result


This leads to:

- Hung parliament
- Hung assemblies
- Coalition governments

---


# 2. One-Dominant Party System


Even though India has a multiparty system, **Congress dominated politics for a long time**.


Political scientist **Rajni Kothari** called it:

> **“Congress System” or One-Party Dominance System**

---


### Decline of Congress Dominance


Since **1967**, dominance declined because of:

- Rise of **regional parties**
- Emergence of **other national parties**

Examples


| Party        | Year |
| ------------ | ---- |
| Janata Party | 1977 |
| Janata Dal   | 1989 |
| BJP          | 1991 |


Thus India moved towards **competitive multi-party system**.


---


# 3. Lack of Clear Ideology


Most political parties in India **lack clear ideology**.


Exceptions:

- BJP
- CPI
- CPM

Most parties have **similar policies and programmes**.


Almost every party supports:

- Democracy
- Secularism
- Socialism
- Gandhism

---


### Key Feature


Politics has become:

- **Issue-based**
- **Pragmatic**

Instead of ideological commitment.


Main goal often becomes:

> **Capture of power**

---


# 4. Personality Cult


Many political parties revolve around **a strong leader**.


The **leader becomes more important than the party ideology**.


Example:


| Party    | Leader                             |
| -------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Congress | Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Rajiv Gandhi |
| AIADMK   | M.G. Ramachandran                  |
| TDP      | N.T. Rama Rao                      |


Some parties are even **named after leaders**


Examples

- Biju Janata Dal
- Lok Dal (A)
- Congress (I)

Thus it is often said:

> India has **political personalities rather than political parties**

# 7. Factionalism and Defections


Indian political parties often experience:

- Factionalism
- Defections
- Splits
- Mergers
- Fragmentation
- Polarisation

The main reason:

- **Lust for power**
- **Material considerations**

---


### Major Increase


Defections increased significantly after:

> **Fourth General Elections (1967)**

This led to:

- Political instability
- Disintegration of parties

Examples:

- Two Janata Dals
- Two Congress
- Two TDPs
- Two Communist parties
- Three Akali Dals
- Three Muslim Leagues

---


# 8. Lack of Effective Opposition


A strong opposition is necessary for **healthy parliamentary democracy**.


Opposition functions:

- Checks **autocratic tendencies**
- Provides **alternative government**
- Strengthens democracy

---


### Situation in India


For a long time India lacked a **strong national opposition**.


Problems:

- Opposition parties lack unity
- Conflicting positions
- Weak constructive role

# RECOGNITION OF NATIONAL AND STATE PARTIES


## Role of Election Commission of India (ECI)


The **Election Commission of India** is responsible for:

1. **Registering political parties**
2. **Granting recognition** to parties as:
    - National Party
    - State Party
3. Monitoring **poll performance**
4. Allocating **party symbols**

Political parties in India are therefore divided into:


| Category                        | Meaning                       |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Recognised National Parties     | Recognised across India       |
| Recognised State Parties        | Recognised in specific states |
| Registered-Unrecognised Parties | Registered but not recognised |


---


# Privileges of Recognised Political Parties


Recognition gives parties several **important advantages**.


## 1. Allocation of Party Symbols


Recognised parties receive **reserved symbols**.


Examples:


| Party    | Symbol   |
| -------- | -------- |
| BJP      | Lotus    |
| Congress | Hand     |
| BSP      | Elephant |


Reserved symbols **cannot be used by other parties**.


---


## 2. Broadcast Facilities


Recognised parties get **free broadcast time** on:

- **Doordarshan**
- **All India Radio**

This helps them campaign during elections.


---


## 3. Access to Electoral Rolls


Recognised parties are given **copies of electoral rolls** for election purposes.


---


## 4. Nomination Advantages


Candidates of recognised parties need:


✔ **Only one proposer**


For **independent candidates** and **unrecognised parties**:


✔ **Ten proposers required**


---


## 5. Star Campaigners


Political parties are allowed **star campaigners** during elections.


| Party Type                      | Maximum Star Campaigners |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Recognised Parties              | 40                       |
| Registered-Unrecognised Parties | 20                       |


### Important Rule


Travel expenses of star campaigners:


✔ **Not counted** in candidate’s election expenditure.


This is an important **UPPCS Prelims fact**.


---


# PARTY SYMBOLS


The Election Commission classifies symbols into two types.


## 1. Reserved Symbols


Reserved symbols are **exclusively allotted** to recognised parties.


| Party Type     | Reserved Symbol Validity |
| -------------- | ------------------------ |
| National Party | Entire country           |
| State Party    | Only in that state       |


Example:

- BJP → Lotus (nationwide)
- SP → Bicycle (UP)

---


## 2. Free Symbols


Free symbols are available for:

- Independent candidates
- Registered unrecognised parties

They can choose from the **list provided by the Election Commission**.


---


# Important Terminology


## Recognised Party


A party that fulfills **Election Commission criteria**.


Two types:

1. National Party
2. State Party

---


## Registered-Unrecognised Party


A party that is:


✔ Registered with the Election Commission


❌ But does **not fulfill recognition criteria**


They do **not get special privileges** like recognised parties.

# CONDITIONS FOR RECOGNITION


The **Election Commission of India (ECI)** determines whether a party becomes a:

- **National Party**
- **State Party**

Recognition depends on **electoral performance**.


---


# CONDITIONS FOR RECOGNITION AS A NATIONAL PARTY


A political party is recognised as a **National Party** if **ANY ONE** of the following conditions is fulfilled.


---


## Condition 1: Vote Share + Lok Sabha Seats


A party must:

- Secure **6% of valid votes** in **any 4 or more states**

AND

- Win **at least 4 seats in Lok Sabha**

---


## Condition 2: Lok Sabha Seat Share


A party must:

- Win **2% of total Lok Sabha seats**

AND

- Those members must be elected from **at least 3 states**

### Important Fact


Total Lok Sabha seats = **543**


2% of 543 ≈ **11 seats**


Thus:


✔ A party must win **around 11 seats** from **3 states**


---


## Condition 3: State Party Recognition


A party becomes a **National Party** if:


✔ It is recognised as a **State Party in 4 states**


---


# Summary Table (National Party)


| Condition   | Requirement                       |
| ----------- | --------------------------------- |
| Condition 1 | 6% votes in 4 states + 4 LS seats |
| Condition 2 | 2% LS seats from 3 states         |
| Condition 3 | State party in 4 states           |


---


# CONDITIONS FOR RECOGNITION AS A STATE PARTY


A political party becomes a **State Party** if it fulfills **ANY ONE** of the following **five conditions**.


---


## Condition 1: Vote Share + Assembly Seats


A party must:

- Secure **6% of valid votes** in **state assembly elections**

AND

- Win **2 seats in the State Legislative Assembly**

---


## Condition 2: Vote Share + Lok Sabha Seat


A party must:

- Secure **6% votes in Lok Sabha elections** in the state

AND

- Win **1 Lok Sabha seat from that state**

---


## Condition 3: Assembly Seat Share


A party must:


Win:

- **3% of total seats in state assembly**

OR

- **3 seats**

Whichever is **greater**.


---


## Condition 4: Lok Sabha Seat Ratio


A party must win:

- **1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 seats allotted to the state**

OR fraction thereof.


---


## Condition 5: Vote Share Only


A party must secure:


✔ **8% of total valid votes in the state**


in either:

- Lok Sabha election

    OR

- State Assembly election

### Important Fact


This **8% rule was added in 2011**.


---


# Summary Table (State Party)


| Condition   | Requirement                  |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- |
| Condition 1 | 6% votes + 2 Assembly seats  |
| Condition 2 | 6% votes + 1 LS seat         |
| Condition 3 | 3% Assembly seats or 3 seats |
| Condition 4 | 1 LS seat per 25 seats       |
| Condition 5 | 8% votes                     |


---


# Important Prelims Facts


### 1️⃣ National Party Recognition


A party must satisfy **any one** of **three conditions**.


---


### 2️⃣ State Party Recognition


A party must satisfy **any one** of **five conditions**.


---


### 3️⃣ Authority


Recognition is granted by:


✔ **Election Commission of India**
