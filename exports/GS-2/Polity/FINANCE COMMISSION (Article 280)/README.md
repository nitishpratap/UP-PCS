
# FINANCE COMMISSION (Article 280)


Articles: **280–281**


---


# 1. Finance Commission – Overview


The **Finance Commission** is a **constitutional body** established under **Article 280 of the Constitution of India**.


It is constituted by the **President of India**:

- **Every five years**, or
- **Earlier if necessary**

### Nature of the Body

- **Quasi-judicial body**
- Constitutional authority
- Advisory body

### Core Purpose


To recommend **financial distribution between the Centre and the States**.


Thus, it acts as the:


**“Balancing wheel of fiscal federalism in India.”**


---


# 2. Constitutional Provision


| Article         | Provision                             |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Article 280** | Finance Commission                    |
| **Article 281** | Recommendations of Finance Commission |


---


# 3. Composition of Finance Commission


The Finance Commission consists of:

- **1 Chairman**
- **4 other members**

Thus, total members = **5**


All are **appointed by the President of India**.


### Tenure


They hold office for a **period specified by the President**.


### Reappointment


Members **are eligible for reappointment**.


---


# 4. Qualifications of Members


The Constitution authorizes **Parliament to determine qualifications** and **selection procedure**.


Parliament enacted:


**Finance Commission Act, 1951**


### Chairman Qualification


The Chairman must be:


➡ **A person having experience in public affairs**


---


### Other Four Members


They are selected from among persons having expertise in:


| Category | Qualification                                                            |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1        | Judge of High Court or qualified to become one                           |
| 2        | Person with **specialized knowledge of government finance and accounts** |
| 3        | Person with **wide experience in financial matters and administration**  |
| 4        | Person with **special knowledge of economics**                           |


---


# 5. Functions of Finance Commission


The Finance Commission gives **recommendations to the President** on the following matters:


---


## 1. Distribution of Tax Revenue


It recommends:

- Distribution of **net proceeds of taxes between Centre and States**

This is called:


**Vertical Devolution**


It also recommends:

- Allocation among the states

This is called:


**Horizontal Devolution**


---


## 2. Grants-in-Aid to States


It suggests **principles governing grants-in-aid** to states.


These grants are given from:


➡ **Consolidated Fund of India**


---


## 3. Strengthening Local Governments


The Finance Commission recommends measures to:

- **Augment Consolidated Fund of a State**

Purpose:


To support:

- **Panchayats**
- **Municipalities**

This recommendation is based on **State Finance Commission recommendations**.


### Important Constitutional Note


This function was added by:


**73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992**


(Panchayats)


**74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992**


(Municipalities)


---


## 4. Other Matters


The Finance Commission may also recommend on:


**Any matter referred by the President in the interest of sound finance.**


---


# 6. Historical Function (Till 1960)


Until **1960**, Finance Commission also recommended grants to:

- Assam
- Bihar
- Odisha
- West Bengal

These grants compensated for:


**Loss of share in export duty on jute and jute products.**


### Duration of Grants


These grants were given for:


**10 years from commencement of the Constitution.**


---


# 7. Submission of Report


The Commission submits its report to the:


➡ **President of India**


The President then:

1. **Places the report before both Houses of Parliament**
2. Provides an **explanatory memorandum** describing action taken.

---


# 8. Advisory Nature of Finance Commission


The recommendations of the Finance Commission are:


**Advisory in nature**


They are **NOT binding** on the Government.


Thus:

- Union Government may **accept or reject recommendations**

There is **no constitutional obligation** to implement them.


---


### Important Constitutional Observation


It is **not stated anywhere in the Constitution** that the recommendations are binding.


Thus:


States **cannot legally claim the recommended funds**.


---


# 9. View of Dr. P. V. Rajamannar


Dr **P.V. Rajamannar**, Chairman of the **Fourth Finance Commission**, stated:

> Since the Finance Commission is a constitutional quasi-judicial body, its recommendations should not be rejected unless there are very compelling reasons.

---


# 10. Finance Commission and Fiscal Federalism


The Constitution envisages the Finance Commission as:


### “Balancing wheel of fiscal federalism”


Meaning:


It ensures **fair financial relations between Centre and States**.


---


# 11. Conflict with Planning Commission (Historical)


Earlier, the role of Finance Commission was weakened due to the existence of:


**Planning Commission**


Characteristics of Planning Commission:

- Non-constitutional body
- Non-statutory body
- Created by executive resolution

### Problem


There was **overlap in financial transfers** between:

- Finance Commission
- Planning Commission

This weakened the importance of Finance Commission.


This issue was highlighted by:


➡ **Dr P. V. Rajamannar**


(Chairman of Fourth Finance Commission)
