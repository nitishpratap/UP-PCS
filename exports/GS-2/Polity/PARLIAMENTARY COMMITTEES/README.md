PARLIAMENTARY COMMITTEES


## PARLIAMENTARY COMMITTEES - Revision

---


# 1. MEANING OF PARLIAMENTARY COMMITTEES


Parliament deals with **large volume of legislative, financial and oversight work** → detailed examination by full House not possible.


Therefore **Parliamentary Committees assist Parliament** in:

- detailed scrutiny of **legislation**
- examination of **government expenditure**
- investigation of **issues and policies**
- ensuring **executive accountability**

They **examine issues in detail and submit reports to Parliament**.


---


# 2. CONSTITUTIONAL POSITION


| Feature                 | Details                                           |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Mention in Constitution | Yes (at various places)                           |
| Composition / functions | **NOT specified**                                 |
| Governed by             | **Rules of Procedure of Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha** |


📌 **Exam Trap:** Parliamentary committees are **not detailed in Constitution**.


---


# 3. DEFINITION OF PARLIAMENTARY COMMITTEE


A body is a Parliamentary Committee only if:


1️⃣ **Appointed/elected by House OR nominated by Speaker/Chairman**


2️⃣ Works **under direction of Speaker/Chairman**


3️⃣ **Reports submitted to House or Presiding Officer**


4️⃣ Secretariat provided by **Lok Sabha / Rajya Sabha**


📌 **Consultative Committees are NOT parliamentary committees** (fail above conditions).


---


# 4. CLASSIFICATION OF PARLIAMENTARY COMMITTEES


## (A) STANDING COMMITTEES


Permanent committees.

- constituted **every year**
- function **continuously**

## (B) AD HOC COMMITTEES


Temporary committees.

- created **for specific purpose**
- dissolved after **task completion**

# PUBLIC ACCOUNTS COMMITTEE (PAC)


## Origin


Established **1921** under **Government of India Act 1919**.


---


## Composition


| House       | Members |
| ----------- | ------- |
| Lok Sabha   | 15      |
| Rajya Sabha | 7       |
| Total       | **22**  |


Election → **Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (STV)**


Term → **1 year**


Minister → **Cannot be member**


---


## Chairman


Appointed by **Speaker of Lok Sabha**


Convention:


| Period      | Chairman       |
| ----------- | -------------- |
| Before 1967 | Ruling Party   |
| After 1967  | **Opposition** |


Purpose → independent scrutiny.


---


## Role of CAG


PAC examines **CAG audit reports**.


CAG submits **3 reports to President**:


1️⃣ Audit Report on **Appropriation Accounts**


2️⃣ Audit Report on **Finance Accounts**


3️⃣ Audit Report on **Public Undertakings**


CAG acts as **“Guide, Friend and Philosopher of PAC.”**


---


## Functions


PAC examines:

- **Appropriation Accounts**
- **Finance Accounts**
- accounts of autonomous bodies audited by **CAG**
- audit reports on **government receipts**
- **excess expenditure**
- accounts of **state corporations**

### Meaning


| Account                | Meaning                          |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Appropriation Accounts | Actual vs sanctioned expenditure |
| Finance Accounts       | Annual receipts & expenditure    |


---


## Objective


Checks:

- legality of expenditure
- financial propriety
- efficiency

Detects:

- waste
- corruption
- extravagance
- nugatory expenditure

---


## Limitations


PAC cannot:

- examine **policy matters**
- interfere in **administration**
- **disallow expenditure**
- issue **executive orders**

Work is **post-mortem**.


Recommendations → **advisory only**.


---

---


## Chairman


Appointed by **Speaker**.


Convention → **Ruling party member**.


---


## Objective


Examines **budget estimates**.


Suggests:

- economy
- efficiency
- administrative reforms

Hence called:


**“Continuous Economy Committee.”**


---


## Functions


1️⃣ Suggest **economies in expenditure**


2️⃣ Suggest **alternative policies**


3️⃣ Check **proper utilisation of funds**


4️⃣ Suggest **budget presentation format**


---


## Special Feature


Examines **selected ministries annually**.


All ministries covered **by rotation**.


---


## Limitations

- Cannot question **policy approved by Parliament**
- Only **selected ministries examined**
- No **CAG assistance**
- Recommendations **advisory**

---

Examines:

- reports of **public sector enterprises**

- management of PSUs

- sound business principles

- Day-to-day administration

- **Post-mortem work**
- Members not **technical experts**
- Recommendations **non-binding**

# 1. Meaning of Parliamentary Committees


The **Parliament of India** is a very large body dealing with **complex, varied and voluminous functions**. It does not have sufficient **time, expertise, or resources** to examine every legislative and financial matter in detail.


Therefore, **Parliamentary Committees are created to assist Parliament** in performing its legislative and oversight functions efficiently.


These committees **examine matters in detail**, investigate issues and submit reports to the House.


The **Constitution of India mentions Parliamentary Committees at different places**, but it **does not specify their composition, tenure, or functions**. These details are governed by the **Rules of Procedure of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha**.


---


# 2. Parliamentary Committee – Definition


A **Parliamentary Committee** is one that satisfies the following conditions:

1. It is **appointed or elected by the House**, or **nominated by the Speaker (Lok Sabha) / Chairman (Rajya Sabha)**.
2. It **works under the direction of the Speaker or Chairman**.
3. It **presents its report to the House or to the Speaker/Chairman**.
4. It has a **secretariat provided by Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha**.

📌 **Important Point**


Consultative Committees are **NOT parliamentary committees** because they **do not fulfill the above four conditions**.


---


# 3. Classification of Parliamentary Committees


Parliamentary committees are broadly classified into two categories:


## 1. Standing Committees


These are **permanent committees**.


They are constituted **every year or periodically** and work **continuously**.


## 2. Ad Hoc Committees


These are **temporary committees**.


They are created **for a specific purpose** and **dissolve after completing the assigned task**.

# Important Exam Points (UPPCS)


✔ Parliamentary committees assist Parliament in **detailed scrutiny of legislation and finances**.


✔ They **increase accountability of the executive**.


✔ The **Constitution does not specify details** about committees.


✔ Committees operate according to **Rules of Procedure of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha**.


✔ Committees submit **reports to the House**, but **recommendations are advisory**.

# FINANCIAL COMMITTEES


Financial Committees exercise **parliamentary control over government finances and expenditure**.


They ensure that **public money approved by Parliament is used properly**.


There are **three Financial Committees**:

1. **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)**

# 1. PUBLIC ACCOUNTS COMMITTEE (PAC)


## Origin


The **Public Accounts Committee was first set up in 1921** under the provisions of the **Government of India Act, 1919**.


It has continued to exist even after independence.


---


# Composition


Total Members: **22**

- **15 members from Lok Sabha**
- **7 members from Rajya Sabha**

Members are **elected annually by Parliament** from among its members.


Election is done through:


**Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (STV)**


This ensures **representation of all political parties**.


---


# Term


**1 year**


---


# Eligibility


A **minister cannot be a member** of this committee.


If a member becomes a minister, he/she **ceases to be a member of the committee**.


---


# Chairman


The **Chairman is appointed by the Speaker of Lok Sabha** from among the committee members.


### Important Convention


Before **1967** → Chairman belonged to the **ruling party**


After **1967** → Chairman is always from the **Opposition**


This ensures **independent scrutiny of government expenditure**.


---


# Role of CAG


The committee examines **audit reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)**.


CAG submits **three reports to the President**, which are then laid before Parliament:

1. Audit Report on **Appropriation Accounts**
2. Audit Report on **Finance Accounts**
3. Audit Report on **Public Undertakings**

The **CAG acts as guide, friend and philosopher of the committee**.


---


# Functions of Public Accounts Committee


### 1. Examines Accounts of Government


It examines:

- **Appropriation Accounts**
- **Finance Accounts**
- Any other accounts laid before Lok Sabha

**Appropriation Accounts**


These compare:


Actual Expenditure vs Expenditure approved by Parliament.


**Finance Accounts**


These show:


Annual **receipts and expenditures of Union Government**.


---


### 2. Verifies Proper Use of Money


PAC ensures that:

1. Money was **legally available** for the purpose.
2. Expenditure conforms to **authority granted by Parliament**.
3. **Re-appropriation of funds** followed proper rules.

---


### 3. Examines Public Corporations


PAC examines accounts of:

- State corporations
- Trading concerns
- Manufacturing projects

However, **public undertakings assigned to the Committee on Public Undertakings are excluded**.


---


### 4. Examines Autonomous Bodies


It examines accounts of **autonomous and semi-autonomous bodies** audited by CAG.


---


### 5. Examines Audit Reports on Receipts


PAC studies:

- Audit reports related to **government receipts**
- **Accounts of stores and stock**

---


### 6. Examines Excess Expenditure


If government spends **more than the amount granted by Lok Sabha**, PAC investigates the reason.


---


# Objective of PAC


PAC checks expenditure from two perspectives:

1. **Legality** (whether expenditure was authorised)
2. **Efficiency**

It identifies:

- Waste
- Loss
- Corruption
- Extravagance
- Inefficiency
- Nugatory expenditure

---


# Limitations of PAC


Despite its importance, PAC has certain limitations:

1. It **does not examine policy matters**.
2. It conducts **post-mortem examination** (after expenditure occurs).
3. It cannot interfere in **day-to-day administration**.
4. Its **recommendations are advisory**, not binding.
5. It **cannot disallow expenditure**.
6. It is **not an executive body** and cannot issue orders.

Only **Parliament can take final action**.


---


# 2. ESTIMATES COMMITTEE


## Origin


The **Estimates Committee originated from the financial committee created in 1921**.


After independence, the **first Estimates Committee was constituted in 1950**.


It was formed on the recommendation of **Finance Minister John Mathai**.


---


# Composition


Total Members: **30**


All members are from **Lok Sabha only**.


Rajya Sabha has **no representation**.


Members are elected annually using:


**Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote**


---


# Term


**1 year**


---


# Eligibility


A **minister cannot be a member** of the committee.


---


# Chairman


The **Chairman is appointed by the Speaker from among Lok Sabha members**.


The chairman is **always from the ruling party**.


---


# Objective


The committee examines **budget estimates** and suggests **economies in government expenditure**.


Because of this role, it is known as:


**“Continuous Economy Committee.”**


---


# Functions of Estimates Committee


### 1. Suggest Economies


It recommends ways to achieve:

- Economy in expenditure
- Administrative efficiency
- Organisational improvement

---


### 2. Suggest Alternative Policies


It proposes **alternative policies** to increase efficiency and reduce expenditure.


---


### 3. Examine Whether Money is Properly Used


It checks whether funds are **spent properly within policy limits**.


---


### 4. Suggest Format of Budget


The committee recommends **how budget estimates should be presented to Parliament**.


---


# Special Feature


The committee **does not examine all ministries every year**.


Instead, it examines **selected ministries annually**.


Over time, **all ministries are examined through rotation**.


---


# Limitations of Estimates Committee

1. It examines **estimates after they are voted by Parliament**.
2. It **cannot question policy decisions of Parliament**.
3. Recommendations are **advisory only**.
4. It examines **only selected ministries each year**.
5. It does **not receive assistance from CAG**.
6. Its work is largely **post-mortem**.

---


# 3. COMMITTEE ON PUBLIC UNDERTAKINGS


## Origin


This committee was established in **1964**.


It was created based on the recommendation of the **Krishna Menon Committee**.


---


# Composition


Originally:


15 Members

- 10 Lok Sabha
- 5 Rajya Sabha

Later (1974):


Total **22 Members**

- **15 Lok Sabha**
- **7 Rajya Sabha**

Members are elected annually by **proportional representation using STV**.


---


# Term


**1 year**


---


# Eligibility


A **minister cannot be a member** of the committee.


---


# Chairman


The chairman is **appointed by the Speaker of Lok Sabha**.


The chairman must be **from Lok Sabha members**.


Rajya Sabha members **cannot become chairman**.


---


# Functions


### 1. Examine Reports of Public Undertakings


It studies:

- Reports
- Accounts

of public sector enterprises.


---


### 2. Examine CAG Reports on PSUs


The committee examines **CAG audit reports related to public undertakings**.


---


### 3. Examine Management of PSUs


It ensures that public enterprises operate according to:

- **Sound business principles**
- **Prudent commercial practices**
- **Efficiency and autonomy**

---


### 4. Perform Additional Functions


It may perform functions of PAC and Estimates Committee relating to **public undertakings** if assigned by the Speaker.


---


# Matters NOT Examined


The committee does not investigate:

1. **Major government policy decisions**
2. **Day-to-day administration**
3. Matters handled under **special statutes governing specific PSUs**

---


# Limitations

1. It can examine only **10–12 public undertakings per year**.
2. Its work is **post-mortem in nature**.
3. Members are **not technical experts**, so technical matters are not examined deeply.
4. Recommendations are **not binding**.

---

# 1. Background


Departmentally Related Standing Committees were introduced to **strengthen parliamentary control over the executive**.


They help Parliament **scrutinise legislation, budget, and policies in detail**.


### Establishment

- **1993** → 17 DRSCs created
- **2004** → 7 more committees added

Total committees now = **24**


These committees cover **all ministries and departments of the Union Government**.


---


# 2. Objective of DRSCs


The main objective is to ensure:


**Greater accountability of the Executive (Council of Ministers) to Parliament**, particularly in financial matters.


They also help Parliament:

- Debate the **budget effectively**
- Examine **policies and legislation in detail**

---


# 3. Composition


Each committee has **31 members**.


Breakup:


| House       | Members |
| ----------- | ------- |
| Lok Sabha   | 21      |
| Rajya Sabha | 10      |


---


### Nomination


Members are **nominated**, not elected.

- Lok Sabha members → nominated by **Speaker**
- Rajya Sabha members → nominated by **Chairman**

---


# 4. Eligibility


A **minister cannot be a member** of any Departmental Standing Committee.


If a member becomes a **minister**, he/she automatically **ceases to be a committee member**.


---


# 5. Tenure


Term of office = **1 year**

# 8. Functions of Departmental Standing Committees


Each DRSC performs the following functions.


---


## 1. Examine Demands for Grants


They consider **demands for grants of ministries before voting in Lok Sabha**.


Their report **cannot suggest cut motions**.


---


## 2. Examine Bills


They examine **bills related to their ministries**.


---


## 3. Examine Annual Reports


They analyse **annual reports of ministries and departments**.


---


## 4. Examine Long-Term Policy Documents


They consider **national long-term policy documents presented to Parliament**.


---


# 9. Procedure for Examining Demands for Grants


The process is as follows:

1. Budget is presented in Parliament.
2. General discussion on the budget takes place.
3. Houses are adjourned for a fixed period.
4. Committees examine **demands for grants of ministries**.
5. Committees submit their reports.
6. Parliament considers demands **in light of these reports**.

Separate reports are prepared for **each ministry**.


---


# 10. Procedure for Examining Bills


When a bill is referred to a committee:

1. Committee examines **general principles of the bill**.
2. It analyses **each clause of the bill**.
3. Committee studies **only bills referred to it**.
4. It submits its **report within a fixed time period**.

---


# 11. Limitations of DRSCs


These committees **cannot**:

1. Examine **day-to-day administration** of ministries.
2. Consider matters already handled by **other parliamentary committees**.

Also:


Their recommendations are **advisory**, not binding.


---


# 12. Advantages of the Standing Committee System


The DRSC system has several benefits.


---


### 1. Reduces Party Bias


Committee discussions are **less political** compared to debates in Parliament.


---


### 2. Flexible Procedure


Committees follow **less rigid procedures** than Parliament.


---


### 3. Stronger Executive Accountability


They ensure **detailed and continuous parliamentary control over the executive**.


---


### 4. Better Financial Control


Ministries become **more careful in preparing budget demands**, improving efficiency.


---


### 5. Participation of MPs


More members of Parliament get opportunities to **participate in governance and oversight**.


---


### 6. Expert Consultation


Committees can invite:

- Experts
- Specialists
- Stakeholders
- Public organisations

to provide opinions.


---


### 7. Greater Role for Opposition and Rajya Sabha


These committees enable:

- Opposition parties
- Rajya Sabha members

to exercise **greater financial oversight over the government**.

# COMMITTEES TO SCRUTINISE AND CONTROL


These committees ensure that the **Executive follows parliamentary authority properly**.


They monitor:

- Government promises
- Delegated legislation

# CONSULTATIVE COMMITTEES


Consultative committees are **different from Parliamentary Committees**.


Although they include MPs, they **do not satisfy the conditions required to be parliamentary committees**.


---


# Nature


Consultative committees are attached to **ministries and departments of the Central Government**.


They serve as **forums for informal discussion between ministers and MPs**.


---


# Chairman


The **minister or minister of state of the concerned ministry acts as chairman**.


---


# Purpose


These committees discuss:

- Government policies
- Programmes of ministries
- Implementation of schemes.

They allow MPs to **give suggestions and feedback**.


---


# Formation


Consultative committees are constituted by the **Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs**.


This ministry also:

- decides their composition
- lays down their procedures
- arranges meetings.

---


# Membership


Membership is **voluntary**.


Members join based on:

- their interest
- recommendations of party leaders.

---


## Size of Committee


Maximum Members → **30**


Minimum Members → **10**


---


# Tenure


Consultative committees are normally constituted **after the formation of a new Lok Sabha following general elections**.


These committees **dissolve automatically when Lok Sabha is dissolved**.


They are then **reconstituted with each new Lok Sabha**.


---


# Railway Informal Consultative Committees


Separate informal consultative committees exist for **each Railway Zone**.


Members of Parliament belonging to the **region of that railway zone** are nominated to these committees.


---


## Difference from Ministry Consultative Committees


| Feature  | Railway Committees                 | Ministry Committees              |
| -------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Meetings | Only during parliamentary sessions | During session and inter-session |
| Area     | Railway zones                      | Central ministries               |

# IMPORTANT NOTES (FROM CHAPTER REFERENCES)

1. Ministers **cannot be members of certain committees**, including:
- Financial Committees
- Departmental Standing Committees
- Committee on Empowerment of Women
- Committee on Government Assurances
- Committee on Petitions
- Committee on Subordinate Legislation
- Committee on Welfare of SCs and STs.

---

1. **Joint Committee**

A joint committee consists of members from **both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha**.


---

1. **Railway Convention Committee (1949)**

This was the **first committee constituted after independence**.


Initially its role was to **determine the rate of dividend payable by Railways to General Revenues**.


Later it began examining issues related to **railway finances and operations**.


---

1. **Standing Committee System Objective**

When the committee system was introduced in 1993, Vice-President **K.R. Narayanan** said its purpose was to ensure:

> greater accountability of the government to Parliament through detailed examination of policies and measures.

---

1. **Restructuring of DRSCs (2004)**

Before 2004:

- Committee members = **45**
- Lok Sabha = **30**
- Rajya Sabha = **15**

After restructuring in 2004:

- Total members = **31**
- Lok Sabha = **21**
- Rajya Sabha = **10**
