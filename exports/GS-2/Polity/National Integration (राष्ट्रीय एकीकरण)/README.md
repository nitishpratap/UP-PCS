
# NATIONAL INTEGRATION – MEANING


## Meaning


India is a country of:

- Religious diversity
- Linguistic diversity
- Caste diversity
- Regional diversity
- Tribal diversity
- Cultural diversity

Hence, maintaining **unity in diversity** is essential for:

- Political stability
- Economic development
- Social harmony
- National security
- Democratic success

National Integration means:

> Developing a feeling of unity, solidarity, common identity and loyalty towards the nation above narrow interests.

---


# DEFINITIONS OF NATIONAL INTEGRATION


## 1. Myron Weiner

> “National integration implies avoidance of divisive movements that would balkanise the nation and preference to national/public interest over parochial interests.”

### Key Points

- Opposes divisive tendencies
- National interest above local interests
- Prevents fragmentation (“Balkanisation”)

---


## 2. H.A. Gani

> “National integration is a socio-psychological and educational process through which unity and common citizenship develop.”

### Key Points

- Psychological process
- Emotional unity
- Sense of common citizenship

---


## 3. Dr. S. Radhakrishnan

> National integration is not merely a structure or plan but a consciousness among people.

### Key Points

- Emotional and mental unity important
- Cannot be imposed mechanically
- Requires public awareness

---


## 4. Rasheeduddin Khan

> National integration means “cohesion not fusion, unity not uniformity.”

### Key Interpretation

- Diversity should remain protected
- Integration ≠ Assimilation
- India supports pluralism

---


# ESSENCE OF NATIONAL INTEGRATION


National Integration has:

1. Political dimension
2. Economic dimension
3. Social dimension
4. Cultural dimension
5. Psychological dimension

Thus, integration means:

- Unity amidst diversity
- Shared national identity
- Constitutional patriotism

---


# OBSTACLES TO NATIONAL INTEGRATION


Main obstacles:

1. Regionalism
2. Communalism
3. Casteism
4. Linguism

---


# 1. REGIONALISM (क्षेत्रवाद)


## Meaning


Regionalism means:

> Loyalty to one’s region/state over the nation.

It promotes:

- Sub-nationalism
- Local identity politics

---


## Types


### 1. Regionalism


Preference for state/region over nation.


### 2. Sub-regionalism


Preference for a smaller region within a state.


Example:

- Vidarbha in Maharashtra
- Telangana (before statehood)

---


# Definition


Regionalism is:

> A manifestation of residual elements excluded from national polity.

Meaning:

- People feel neglected
- Leads to political dissatisfaction

---


# FORMS OF REGIONALISM


## 1. Secessionist Demands


Demand to separate from India.


### Examples

- Khalistan movement
- Dravid Nadu demand
- Naga movement
- Mizo movement

---


## 2. Demand for Separate Statehood


### Examples

- Bodoland
- Gorkhaland
- Vidarbha

---


## 3. Demand for Statehood by Union Territories


### Examples

- Delhi
- Puducherry

---


## 4. Interstate Disputes


### Boundary Disputes

- Chandigarh dispute
- Belgaum dispute (Maharashtra–Karnataka)

### River Water Disputes

- Cauvery
- Krishna
- Ravi-Beas

---


## 5. Sons of Soil Theory


### Meaning


Preference to locals in:

- Government jobs
- Private jobs
- Permits/admissions

### Examples

- “Maharashtra for Maharashtrians”
- “Assam for Assamese”

---


# CAUSES OF REGIONALISM

- Uneven economic development
- Linguistic differences
- Cultural differences
- Political mobilisation
- Perceived neglect by Centre
- Regional parties

---


# IMPACT OF REGIONALISM


## Positive

- Protects local culture
- Encourages decentralisation

## Negative

- Threat to national unity
- Separatism
- Interstate conflicts
- Violence

---


# 2. COMMUNALISM (साम्प्रदायिकता)


## Meaning


Communalism means:

> Loyalty to one’s religious community over the nation.

It promotes:

- Religious intolerance
- Conflict among communities

---


# HISTORICAL ROOTS


Communalism was encouraged during British rule through:

- Divide and Rule policy
- Separate electorates

## Important Acts

- Indian Councils Act, 1909
- Government of India Act, 1919
- Government of India Act, 1935

These introduced communal representation.


---


# MANIFESTATIONS OF COMMUNALISM


## 1. Religion-based Political Parties


### Examples

- Akali Dal
- Muslim League

---


## 2. Religious Pressure Groups


### Examples

- Vishwa Hindu Parishad (VHP)
- Jamaat-e-Islami

---


## 3. Communal Riots


### Examples of affected places

- Varanasi
- Mathura
- Hyderabad
- Aligarh
- Amritsar

---


## 4. Disputes over Religious Structures


### Example


Ram Janmabhoomi–Babri Masjid dispute


Important Event:

- Structure demolished on 6 December 1992 by kar sevaks.

---


# CAUSES OF COMMUNALISM

- Religious orthodoxy
- Politicisation of religion
- Vote bank politics
- Role of Pakistan
- Communal media
- Socio-economic backwardness
- Government inaction
- Extremist organisations

---


# EFFECTS OF COMMUNALISM

- Riots and violence
- Loss of life/property
- Threat to secularism
- Weakening of democracy
- Social distrust

---

# OFFICIAL LANGUAGE ISSUE


## Official Languages Act, 1963


Made Hindi Official Language of Union.


This caused:

- Anti-Hindi agitations in South India
- Opposition in West Bengal

---


# GOVERNMENT RESPONSE


Centre assured:

- English would continue as associate official language as long as non-Hindi states desire.

---


# THREE LANGUAGE FORMULA


### Languages:

1. English
2. Hindi
3. Regional language

---


# TAMIL NADU’S POSITION


Tamil Nadu opposed the three-language formula and continued:

- Tamil
- English

Only two languages taught.


(Important Prelims Fact)


---


# RESULT


Hindi could not become:

> “Lingua Franca” of India as envisioned by Constitution makers.

---

# NATIONAL INTEGRATION COUNCIL (NIC) (2025 pre)


## Background


### National Integration Conference – 1961


Held to combat:

- Communalism
- Regionalism
- Linguism
- Casteism
- Narrow-mindedness

Decision:


→ Set up National Integration Council (NIC)


---


# ESTABLISHMENT


| Feature       | Details        |
| ------------- | -------------- |
| Established   | 1961           |
| First Meeting | 1962           |
| Chairman      | Prime Minister |


---


# OBJECTIVES OF NIC

- Promote national integration
- Review communal situations
- Recommend measures for harmony
- Strengthen unity

---


# COMPOSITION OF NIC


Includes:

- Prime Minister
- Union Ministers
- Leader of Opposition (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha)
- Chief Ministers
- Leaders of national/regional parties
- Chairpersons of national commissions
- Journalists
- Public figures
- Women organisation representatives
- Business representatives

---


# MEETINGS

- No fixed interval
- Meetings held as required

### Total Meetings


16 meetings held till now.


---


# 16th MEETING OF NIC


## Date


23 September 2013


No Meeting in NDA Gov


---


# RESOLUTION PASSED


The NIC resolved to:

- Condemn violence
- Strengthen communal harmony
- Resolve disputes legally
- Condemn atrocities on SCs/STs
- Condemn sexual violence
- Ensure women’s freedom and safety
