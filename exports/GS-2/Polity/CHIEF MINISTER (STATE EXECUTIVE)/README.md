
## CHIEF MINISTER (STATE EXECUTIVE) — REVISION

---


# 1. POSITION OF CHIEF MINISTER


### Parliamentary Executive Structure


| Executive Type                  | Meaning                    | Position           |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Nominal Executive (De jure)** | Constitutional head        | **Governor**       |
| **Real Executive (De facto)**   | Actual governing authority | **Chief Minister** |


**Governor → Head of State**


**Chief Minister → Head of Government**


➡ **CM at State = Prime Minister at Union**


### Key Exam Concepts


| Concept            | Meaning             |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| **Governor**       | Constitutional Head |
| **Chief Minister** | Political Head      |


➡ Governor generally acts on **aid and advice of Council of Ministers**.


---


# 2. APPOINTMENT OF CHIEF MINISTER


### Constitutional Provision


**Article 164**


➡ **Governor appoints Chief Minister**


BUT


➡ **Constitution does NOT specify selection procedure**


Thus **constitutional conventions operate**.


---


## Normal Situation (Clear Majority)


Governor appoints:


➡ **Leader of majority party in Legislative Assembly**


Reason:


➡ **Council of Ministers must enjoy confidence of Assembly**


---


## Situation 1: Hung Assembly (No Clear Majority)


Governor exercises **discretion**.


Usually appoints:


➡ **Leader of largest party or coalition**


Then asks him to:


➡ **Prove majority in Assembly within reasonable time (≈1 month)**


### Historical Examples


| Situation                       | State       | Year |
| ------------------------------- | ----------- | ---- |
| Leader of largest party invited | Tamil Nadu  | 1951 |
|                                 | Rajasthan   | 1967 |
|                                 | Haryana     | 1982 |
| Leader of coalition invited     | Punjab      | 1967 |
|                                 | West Bengal | 1970 |
|                                 | Maharashtra | 1978 |


---


## Situation 2: Death of Sitting CM


Governor may exercise **personal judgement temporarily**


BUT normally:


1️⃣ **Ruling party elects new leader**


2️⃣ Governor appoints that person as CM


---


## Majority Proof Rule


❗ Constitution **does NOT require proving majority before appointment**


### Usual Process


1️⃣ Governor appoints CM


2️⃣ CM proves majority on floor of Assembly


### Examples


| Case                | Detail                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Jammu & Kashmir** | Governor **Jagmohan** appointed **G.M. Shah** and asked to prove majority |
| **Andhra Pradesh**  | Governor **Ram Lal** appointed **Bhasker Rao**, later failed majority     |


---


## Non-Legislator as Chief Minister


A person **not member of legislature** can become CM.


Condition:


➡ Must become member **within 6 months**


Otherwise:


➡ **Ceases to hold office**


### Examples


| Leader          | State       |
| --------------- | ----------- |
| **Bansi Lal**   | Haryana     |
| **S.B. Chavan** | Maharashtra |

# 3. OATH, TERM AND SALARY


---


# OATH


Administered by:


➡ **Governor**


Two types:


| Oath Type           | Content                                                |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Oath of Office**  | Faith to Constitution, sovereignty, faithful discharge |
| **Oath of Secrecy** | Maintain confidentiality                               |


---


## Oath of Office Includes


CM swears to:


1️⃣ Bear **true faith and allegiance to Constitution**


2️⃣ Uphold **sovereignty and integrity of India**


3️⃣ **Faithfully discharge duties**


4️⃣ Do **justice to all people without fear or favour**


---


## Oath of Secrecy


CM promises:


➡ Not to reveal **confidential matters** directly or indirectly


➡ Except when required for **official duties**


Format same as **other state ministers**.


---


# TERM OF CHIEF MINISTER


Constitution:


❗ **No fixed tenure**


CM holds office:


➡ **During pleasure of Governor**


BUT this **is not arbitrary**.


### Important Principle


Governor **cannot dismiss CM** if he **enjoys majority in Assembly**.


If CM:


| Situation         | Result               |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Loses majority    | Must resign          |
| Refuses to resign | Governor may dismiss |


---


## Landmark Case


### **S.R. Bommai vs Union of India (1994)**


Judgement:


➡ **Majority must be tested on floor of Assembly**


➡ CM **cannot be dismissed without floor test**


---


# SALARY AND ALLOWANCES


Determined by:


➡ **State Legislature**


CM receives:

- Salary
- Allowances
- Sumptuary allowance
- Free accommodation
- Travelling allowance
- Medical facilities

# 1. POWERS IN RELATION TO COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


CM = **Head of Council of Ministers**


---


## Appointment of Ministers


➡ Governor appoints ministers **on advice of CM**


Meaning:


➡ **CM decides ministerial appointments**


Governor cannot appoint independently.


---


## Allocation of Portfolios


CM assigns **departments to ministers**


Examples:


Home | Finance | Education | Agriculture | Health | Transport


CM may also:


➡ **Reshuffle portfolios**


---


## Removal of Ministers


If conflict arises:


CM may:


➡ Ask minister to **resign**


OR


➡ Advise Governor to **dismiss minister**


Although constitutionally ministers hold office during **pleasure of Governor**, politically they hold office **at pleasure of CM**.


---


## Chairman of Council of Ministers


CM:

- Presides over meetings
- Decides agenda
- Guides policy discussions

➡ Controls **government decision-making**


---


## Coordination Role


CM:

- Guides
- Directs
- Controls
- Coordinates

the work of all ministers.


Ensures **collective functioning of government**.


---


## Collapse of Council of Ministers


If CM:


| Event   | Result                   |
| ------- | ------------------------ |
| Resigns | Entire Council collapses |
| Dies    | Entire Council collapses |


Reason:


➡ CM is **head of ministry**


But resignation of **other ministers only creates vacancy**.

## Principal Communication Channel


CM connects:


➡ **Governor ↔ Council of Ministers**


---

- **Members of State Public Service Commission**

➡ Powers may be affected by **Governor’s discretionary powers**.

Defined mainly through **Articles 163, 164, 167**.


---


# ARTICLE 163


➡ **Council of Ministers with CM as head** to **aid and advise Governor**


Exception:


➡ Governor may act **in discretion in certain matters**


---


# ARTICLE 164

1️⃣ CM appointed by **Governor**

Defines **duties of CM toward Governor**


CM must:


1️⃣ Inform Governor about **decisions of Council of Ministers**


2️⃣ Provide **information requested by Governor**


3️⃣ Submit matters to **Council of Ministers if Governor asks**

# 1. POSITION OF CHIEF MINISTER

- In the **parliamentary system**, two types of executive exist:

| Executive Type                  | Meaning        |
| ------------------------------- | -------------- |
| **Nominal Executive (De jure)** | Governor       |
| **Real Executive (De facto)**   | Chief Minister |

- **Governor** → Head of the State
- **Chief Minister** → Head of the Government

Thus:


**Chief Minister at state level = Prime Minister at the Union level**


This means:

- CM exercises **real executive powers**
- Governor acts mainly on **aid and advice of Council of Ministers**

📌 Important Concept for Exams


**Governor = Constitutional head**


**Chief Minister = Political head**


---


# 2. APPOINTMENT OF CHIEF MINISTER


### Constitutional Provision

- **Article 164** → Chief Minister is appointed by the **Governor**

However, the Constitution **does not prescribe a procedure** for selection.


Thus, **constitutional conventions** are followed.


---

Governor appoints:


➡ **Leader of the majority party in Legislative Assembly**


because:


**Council of Ministers must enjoy majority support in the Assembly**

Governor exercises **discretion**.

➡ **Leader of the largest party or coalition**

➡ **Prove majority in Assembly within a reasonable time (usually within one month)**

- **Tamil Nadu – 1951**
- **Rajasthan – 1967**
- **Haryana – 1982**

Leader of **largest party invited**


Other examples:

- **Punjab – 1967**
- **West Bengal – 1970**
- **Maharashtra – 1978**

Leader of **coalition invited**

# CHIEF MINISTER – POWERS AND FUNCTIONS


The **powers and functions of the Chief Minister** can be studied under **four major heads**:

1. In relation to **Council of Ministers**
2. In relation to **Governor**
3. In relation to **State Legislature**
4. **Other powers and roles**

---


# 1. POWERS IN RELATION TO COUNCIL OF MINISTERS


Chief Minister is the **head of the Council of Ministers**.


Thus he exercises **real executive authority** over the ministry.


---


## 1. Appointment of Ministers


Governor appoints ministers **only on recommendation of the Chief Minister**.


This means:


➡ Chief Minister decides **who becomes minister**.


The governor **cannot appoint ministers independently**.


---


## 2. Allocation of Portfolios


Chief Minister:

- **Allocates departments (portfolios)** to ministers.

Examples of portfolios:

- Home
- Finance
- Education
- Agriculture
- Health
- Transport

He may also:


➡ **Reshuffle portfolios**


Example:


Home minister may be shifted to **finance or education**.


---


## 3. Power to Remove Ministers


If conflict arises between CM and a minister:


Chief Minister can:

- Ask the **minister to resign**

OR

- Advise the **Governor to dismiss him**

Since ministers hold office **during pleasure of Governor**, but **actually at the pleasure of CM**.


---


## 4. Chairman of Council of Ministers Meetings


Chief Minister:

- **Presides over meetings of Council of Ministers**

He also:

- Determines agenda
- Influences decisions
- Leads policy discussions

Thus he controls **decision-making of the government**.


---


## 5. Coordination and Control


Chief Minister:

- **Guides**
- **Directs**
- **Controls**
- **Coordinates**

the work of **all ministers**.


This ensures **collective functioning of government**.


---


## 6. Collapse of Council of Ministers


Chief Minister is **central to existence of ministry**.


If CM:

- **Resigns**
- **Dies**

then:


➡ Entire **Council of Ministers automatically dissolves**


Because:


CM = **Head of Council**


However:


If **any other minister resigns**, it only creates a **vacancy**, not collapse of government.


Source reference:


---


# 2. POWERS IN RELATION TO GOVERNOR


Chief Minister acts as the **main link between Governor and Council of Ministers**.


This role is defined in **Article 167**.


---


## Chief Minister as Communication Channel


CM is the **principal channel of communication** between:


➡ Governor


and


➡ Council of Ministers


---


## Duties of Chief Minister toward Governor


Under **Article 167**, CM must:


### 1. Inform Governor


Communicate to the Governor:

- Decisions of **Council of Ministers**
- Matters related to **administration of the state**
- **Proposals for legislation**

---


### 2. Provide Information


CM must furnish any information about:

- Administration of state
- Legislative proposals

if **Governor asks for it**.


---


### 3. Submit Matters to Council of Ministers


If Governor requires:


CM must place before Council of Ministers any matter:

- decided by a **minister individually**
- but **not considered by Council**

This ensures **collective responsibility**.


---


## Advice on Important Appointments


Chief Minister advises Governor regarding appointment of key officials:


Examples:

- **Advocate General**
- **Chairman of State Public Service Commission**
- **Members of State Public Service Commission**
- **State Election Commissioner**

Thus CM influences **major constitutional appointments at state level**.


---


# 3. POWERS IN RELATION TO STATE LEGISLATURE


Chief Minister is the **leader of the House** (usually Legislative Assembly).


---


## 1. Summoning and Proroguing Legislature


Chief Minister advises Governor regarding:

- **Summoning sessions**
- **Proroguing sessions**

Governor acts based on **CM's advice**.


---


## 2. Dissolution of Legislative Assembly


Chief Minister can recommend:


➡ **Dissolution of Legislative Assembly**


Governor normally accepts this advice.


This may happen when:

- Government loses majority
- Early elections are needed

---


## 3. Announcement of Government Policies


Chief Minister:

- Announces **government policies**
- Makes **major statements**
- Defends government actions

on the **floor of the House**.


---


# 4. OTHER POWERS AND FUNCTIONS


Apart from constitutional roles, CM has several **administrative and political roles**.


---


## 1. Chairman of State Planning Board


Chief Minister serves as:


➡ **Chairman of State Planning Board**

- economic development

---


## 2. Vice-Chairman of Zonal Council (by Rotation)

➡ **Vice-Chairman of Zonal Council**

---


## 3. Member of National Development Institutions


Chief Minister is member of:

- **Inter-State Council**

Both are headed by:


➡ **Prime Minister**


---


## 4. Chief Spokesperson of State Government


Chief Minister represents the **state government publicly**.


He communicates:

- government policies
- government decisions
- political stance

---


## 5. Crisis Manager


During:

- political crisis
- administrative crisis
- emergency situations

Chief Minister acts as:


➡ **Political crisis manager of the state**


---


## 6. Leader of the State


Chief Minister:

- Meets **public delegations**
- Receives **memoranda**
- Addresses **public grievances**

Thus he acts as **political leader of the state**.


---


## 7. Political Head of State Services


Chief Minister is:


➡ **Political head of state administration**


He oversees functioning of:

- civil services
- administration
- governance machinery

# IMPORTANCE OF CHIEF MINISTER


Chief Minister plays a **central role in state administration** because:

- Head of **Council of Ministers**
- Leader of **Legislative Assembly**
- Main advisor to **Governor**
- Head of **executive government**

However:


His powers may be **reduced by discretionary powers of Governor**.
