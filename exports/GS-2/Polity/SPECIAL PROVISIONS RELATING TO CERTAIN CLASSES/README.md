
# SPECIAL PROVISIONS RELATING TO CERTAIN CLASSES


_(Part XVI: Articles 330–342)_


## 1. Rationale of Special Provisions


The Constitution provides **special safeguards for certain disadvantaged communities** in order to achieve the ideals of **Equality and Justice mentioned in the Preamble**.


These provisions relate to:

1. **Scheduled Castes (SCs)**
2. **Scheduled Tribes (STs)**
3. **Backward Classes (BCs / OBCs)**
4. **Anglo-Indians**

These provisions are contained in **Part XVI (Articles 330–342)** of the Constitution.


---


# Types of Special Provisions


The special provisions can be classified into **two broad categories**.


## (A) Permanent vs Temporary Provisions


### Permanent Provisions


These remain permanently in the Constitution.


Examples:

- National Commission for SCs
- National Commission for STs
- Presidential specification of SCs/STs
- Investigation commission for backward classes

### Temporary Provisions


These were initially meant to operate for a **limited time**.


Examples:

- Reservation of seats in legislatures
- Representation of Anglo-Indians

These have been **extended repeatedly through constitutional amendments**.


---


## (B) Protective vs Developmental Provisions


### Protective Provisions


Aim to **protect disadvantaged groups from exploitation and injustice**.


Examples:

- Safeguards in services
- Constitutional commissions
- Welfare schemes for tribal areas

### Developmental Provisions


Aim to **promote socio-economic development**.


Examples:

- Reservation in legislatures
- Reservation in services
- Educational grants

---


# 2. Specification of Classes


## Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes


The Constitution **does not directly list SCs or STs**.


Instead:


The **President specifies them through a notification** for each state and Union Territory.


### Important Points

1. Lists **vary from state to state**.
2. The President issues the notification:
    - **After consulting the Governor** of the state.
3. **Only Parliament can modify the list** (add or remove castes).
4. President **cannot change it later by another notification**.

### Important Presidential Orders


Examples include:

- Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950
- Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950
- Constitution (Scheduled Castes) (Union Territories) Order, 1951
- Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) (Union Territories) Order, 1951

These orders were later modified by:

- **SC & ST Orders (Amendment) Act 1956**
- **SC & ST Orders (Amendment) Act 1976**

---


## Backward Classes (BCs / OBCs)


Unlike SCs and STs:


The Constitution **does not define a specific list of Backward Classes**.


Instead it uses various expressions:


| Article     | Expression Used                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Article 15  | Socially and Educationally Backward Classes |
| Article 16  | Backward Class of Citizens                  |
| Article 46  | Weaker Sections                             |
| Article 340 | Socially and Educationally Backward Classes |


In practice:


**Backward Classes = Other Backward Classes (OBCs)**


because **SCs and STs are already backward classes but treated separately**.


---


## Anglo-Indians


Unlike other groups, the Constitution **explicitly defines Anglo-Indians**.


### Definition


An **Anglo-Indian** is a person:

- whose **father or male ancestor is of European descent**
- but who is **domiciled in India**
- and **born in India**
- from parents habitually residing in India (not temporary residents).

---


# 3. Components of Special Provisions


The Constitution provides **six types of safeguards**.

1. Reservation in legislatures
2. Special representation in legislatures
3. Reservation in services and posts
4. Educational grants
5. National commissions
6. Commissions of investigation

---


# 1. Reservation for SCs/STs and Special Representation for Anglo-Indians


### Reservation in Legislatures


Seats are reserved for:

- **SCs**
- **STs**

in:

- **Lok Sabha**
- **State Legislative Assemblies**

Reservation is based on:


**Population proportion of SCs/STs in the state**.


---


## Representation of Anglo-Indians


### In Lok Sabha


The **President may nominate 2 Anglo-Indians** if the community is not adequately represented.


### In State Assemblies


The **Governor may nominate 1 Anglo-Indian** member.


---


## Why Special Representation Was Needed


Anglo-Indians were:

- **Very small in number**
- **Spread across India**

Hence they could **not win elections easily**.


Therefore nomination provision was created.


---


# Duration of Reservation (Article 334)


Originally:


Reservation in legislatures and Anglo-Indian representation were meant to last **10 years (till 1960)**.


However, the period was extended repeatedly.


| Amendment               | Extension            |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| 8th Amendment Act 1959  | 20 years             |
| 23rd Amendment Act 1969 | 30 years             |
| 45th Amendment Act 1980 | 40 years             |
| 62nd Amendment Act 1989 | 50 years             |
| 79th Amendment Act 1999 | 60 years             |
| 95th Amendment Act 2009 | 70 years (till 2020) |


Reason for extension:


Although progress occurred, **social disadvantages of SCs/STs still existed**.


---


# 2. Claims of SCs and STs to Services and Posts


**Article 335**


The Constitution states that:

> The claims of the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes shall be taken into consideration while making appointments to public services of the Union and the States.

However, this must be done **without compromising the efficiency of administration**.


### Key Idea


Reservation in services should balance two objectives:

1. **Social justice**
2. **Administrative efficiency**

---


## Relaxation in Promotion


The **82nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2000** introduced an important provision.


It allows the government to:

- Relax qualifying marks in examinations

    OR

- Lower the standards of evaluation

for **SC/ST candidates in matters of promotion** in public services of the Union and states.


### Important Exam Point


Article **335 → Reservation in Services**


---


# 3. Special Provisions for Anglo-Indians


_(Services and Educational Grants)_


Before independence, Anglo-Indians had **special reservations in certain services**.


These included:

- Railway services
- Customs services
- Postal services
- Telegraph services

After independence, these benefits were **allowed to continue temporarily** under the Constitution.


However:


They were provided **on a progressive diminution basis**.


Meaning:


The benefits were **gradually reduced over time**.


Finally:


These provisions **ended in 1960**.


---


# Educational Grants for Anglo-Indian Institutions


**Article 337**


Certain **educational institutions run by Anglo-Indians** received **special grants from the Central and State governments**.


These grants were also:

- Gradually reduced
- Ultimately discontinued in **1960**.

---


# 4. National Commissions for SCs and STs


The Constitution provides for **constitutional bodies to safeguard these communities**.


---


## National Commission for Scheduled Castes


**Article 338**


The **President establishes the National Commission for SCs**.


### Functions


The Commission:

1. Investigates constitutional safeguards for SCs
2. Monitors implementation of safeguards
3. Inquires into complaints
4. Participates in planning socio-economic development
5. Submits reports to the President

The **President places these reports before Parliament** along with an **Action Taken Memorandum**.


---


## National Commission for Scheduled Tribes


**Article 338A**


Similarly, the President establishes the **National Commission for STs**.


Functions are similar to those of the SC Commission.


---


## Earlier Arrangement


Originally:


There was **one combined commission for SCs and STs**.


Later:


The **89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** divided it into:

1. National Commission for Scheduled Castes
2. National Commission for Scheduled Tribes

---


## Additional Responsibility of SC Commission


Even after the **89th Amendment**, Article 338 states that the commission can also examine safeguards related to:

- Other Backward Classes (OBCs)
- Anglo-Indian community.

---


# 5. Control of the Union over Scheduled Areas and Welfare of STs


**Article 339**


The President can appoint a **commission to report on**:

- Administration of Scheduled Areas
- Welfare of Scheduled Tribes in states.

### Appointment Rule


The President may appoint such a commission:

- **At any time**
- But **must appoint one after 10 years of the Constitution's commencement**

---


## First Commission on Tribal Welfare


### Dhebar Commission (1960)


Chairman: **U.N. Dhebar**


Purpose:

- Examine administration of scheduled areas
- Study welfare of tribal communities

Report submitted in **1961**.


---


## Second Commission


### Bhuria Commission (2002)


Chairman: **Dilip Singh Bhuria**


Report submitted in **2004**.


---


## Union’s Power


The **Central Government can issue directions to states** regarding:

- Formulation of schemes
- Implementation of welfare programs for STs.

---


# 6. Commission to Investigate Conditions of Backward Classes


**Article 340**


The President may appoint a commission to:

1. Investigate conditions of **socially and educationally backward classes**
2. Recommend measures to improve their condition.

The report must be:

- Submitted to the President
- Placed before Parliament with **Action Taken Memorandum**.

---


# First Backward Classes Commission


### Kaka Kalelkar Commission


Year: **1953**


Chairman: **Kaka Kalelkar**


Report submitted in **1955**.


### Why recommendations were rejected

1. Criteria for backwardness were **too vague**
2. Lack of consensus among members
3. Recommendations considered **impractical**

Hence **no major action was taken**.


---


# Second Backward Classes Commission


### Mandal Commission


Year: **1979**


Chairman: **B.P. Mandal**


Report submitted in **1980**.


Key recommendation:


**27% reservation in government jobs for OBCs**.


---


## Implementation


The recommendations were implemented in **1990 by the V.P. Singh Government**.


This led to the famous **Mandal reservation system**.
