# 6️⃣ REGULATING ACT, 1773


### Significance

- First **British Parliamentary control over Company**
- Recognised **political role of Company**
- Beginning of **central administration**

### Key Provisions


| Provision                  | Details                                   |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Governor-General of Bengal | Created                                   |
| First GG                   | **Lord Warren Hastings**                  |
| Executive Council          | **4 members**                             |
| Presidencies               | **Bombay & Madras subordinate to Bengal** |


### Supreme Court (1774)


Structure:


| Chief Justice | Judges |
| ------------- | ------ |
| 1             | 3      |


First Chief Justice


**Sir Elijah Impey**


### Restrictions on Company Officials


Prohibited:

- Private trade
- Accepting gifts
- Accepting bribes

### Parliamentary Control


Court of Directors must report on:

- Civil affairs
- Military affairs
- Revenue

---


# 7️⃣ ACT OF SETTLEMENT (1781)


Also called


➡ **Amending Act of 1781**


Purpose


➡ Remove defects of **Regulating Act 1773**


Key Point


➡ Supreme Court jurisdiction **limited mainly to Calcutta**


---


# 8️⃣ PITT’S INDIA ACT, 1784


Created **Dual Government (Double Control)**


| Authority          | Function           |
| ------------------ | ------------------ |
| Court of Directors | Commercial affairs |
| Board of Control   | Political affairs  |


Board of Control supervised:

- Civil administration
- Military administration
- Revenue

### Significance

- Company territories called **“British Possessions in India”**
- British Government gained **supreme control**

---


# 9️⃣ CHARTER ACT, 1793


Key provisions:

- Company monopoly extended **20 years**
- Company must pay **£5 lakh annually** to British government
- Senior officials **cannot leave India without permission**

# 11️⃣ CHARTER ACT, 1833


Major step toward **administrative centralisation**


### Governor-General of India


| Before                     | After                         |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Governor-General of Bengal | **Governor-General of India** |


First GG of India


**Lord William Bentinck**


### Legislative Centralisation

- Bombay & Madras **lost legislative powers**
- Power centralized with **Governor-General**

### Other Provisions

- Laws renamed **Regulations → Acts**
- Company **stopped commercial activities**
- Indians **not to be barred from public services** (not implemented)

---


# 12️⃣ CHARTER ACT, 1853


Last Charter Act.


### Key Features


| Provision            | Details                                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Separation of powers | Legislative & Executive separated                      |
| Legislative council  | Expanded with **6 legislative members**                |
| Mini-Parliament      | Legislative council functioned like British Parliament |
| Civil services       | Recruitment through **open competitive exam**          |
| Committee            | **Macaulay Committee (1854)**                          |


### Representation introduced


Members from:

- Bengal
- Bombay
- Madras
- Agra

### Charter without time limit


Earlier charters renewed **every 20 years**


➡ 1853 Act removed time limit


Meaning


➡ Company rule could end anytime


---

- Board of Control
- Court of Directors

Features:

- Member of **British Cabinet**

- **15 members**

### Secretary of State-in-Council

# HISTORICAL BACKGROUND OF THE INDIAN CONSTITUTION


---


## 1. Arrival of British in India

- The **British came to India in 1600** as traders.
- They came through the **East India Company**.

### Charter of 1600

- Queen **Elizabeth I granted a charter** to the East India Company.
- This charter gave the company **exclusive trading rights in India**.

### Expansion of Company Power


Initially:

- The Company performed **only trading functions**.

Later:

- In **1765**, the company obtained **Diwani rights**.

### Diwani Rights


Meaning:

- Right to collect **revenue**
- Authority over **civil justice**

Granted by:

- **Mughal Emperor Shah Alam**

After:

- **Battle of Buxar (1764)**

Regions:

- Bengal
- Bihar
- Orissa

Result:

- Company became a **territorial power**, not just a trader.

---


## 2. End of Company Rule


### Revolt of 1857


Also called:

- First War of Independence
- Sepoy Mutiny

Impact:

- British Crown took control of India.

### 1858

- **British Crown assumed direct control of India**

This system continued until:

- **Indian Independence (15 August 1947)**.

---


## 3. Need for the Indian Constitution


After independence:

- India needed a **written constitution**.

### Idea of Constituent Assembly


First proposed by:

- **M. N. Roy (1934)**

Constituent Assembly formed:

- **1946**

Constitution adopted:

- **26 January 1950**

---

- Parliamentary government
- Rule of law
- Cabinet system
- Bicameral legislature

- Legislative procedures

These evolved through **British Acts**.

# REGULATING ACT, 1773


This was the **first constitutional step** by the British government to control the East India Company.


### Importance

1. First attempt by **British Parliament to regulate Company affairs**.
2. First recognition of **political and administrative role of the Company**.
3. Laid foundation of **central administration in India**.

---


## Features of the Regulating Act 1773


### 1. Governor-General of Bengal Created

- Governor of Bengal was redesignated as **Governor-General of Bengal**.
- Assisted by **Executive Council of 4 members**.

First Governor-General:

- **Lord Warren Hastings**

---


### 2. Subordination of Presidencies


Before this act:

- Bombay, Madras and Bengal were **independent presidencies**.

After the act:

- **Bombay and Madras made subordinate to Bengal**.

Result:

- Beginning of **centralized administration**.

---


### 3. Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)


Structure:

- 1 Chief Justice
- 3 Judges

Purpose:

- Highest judicial authority in British India.

---


### 4. Restrictions on Company Officials


Company servants were prohibited from:

- Private trade
- Accepting gifts
- Accepting bribes from Indians

Purpose:

- Reduce corruption.

---


### 5. British Government Control Increased


Court of Directors had to report to British government about:

- Revenue
- Civil affairs
- Military affairs

---


# ACT OF SETTLEMENT (1781)


Passed to remove defects of Regulating Act 1773.


Also called:

- **Amending Act of 1781**

---


# PITT'S INDIA ACT, 1784


This act created **dual control of India**.


---


## Features


### 1. Separation of Functions


Company functions divided into:

1. Commercial functions
2. Political functions

---


### 2. Double Government System


Two authorities were created:


### (1) Court of Directors

- Managed **commercial affairs**

### (2) Board of Control

- Managed **political affairs**

This system was called:


**Dual Government**


---


### 3. Powers of Board of Control


Board could:

- Supervise civil administration
- Supervise military administration
- Control revenue operations

---


## Significance of Pitt’s India Act

1. Company's territories called **“British possessions in India”** for the first time.
2. British government gained **supreme control over Company administration**.

---


## Charter ACT, 1793

- The act renewed the company’s trade monopoly for a further 20 more years.
- It required the company to pay 5 lakh annually to British gov after meeting all expenses.
- Senior officials could not leave India without permission.
- Governor-General given **power of veto** over councils.
- Commander-in-Chief **not necessarily a council member**.
- Salaries of Board of Control paid from **Indian revenues**.
- Company rule extended for **20 years**.

## Charter Act, 1813

- **End of Trade Monopoly (except tea & China trade)**
    - The monopoly of the East India Company over Indian trade **was abolished**.
    - However, the Company **still retained monopoly over tea trade and trade with China**.
- **Permission for Christian Missionaries**
    - For the **first time, Christian missionaries were allowed to enter India**.
    - They were allowed to **propagate Christianity and promote Western education**.
- **Grant for Education (₹1 Lakh per year)**
    - The British government **allocated ₹1 lakh per year for education in India**.
    - This was the **first official step of the British government toward education in India**.
    - Created debate b/t
    - **Orientalists**
    - **Anglicists**
- **British Crown Control Continued**
    - The Company's rule in India was **extended for another 20 years**, but under **greater control of the British Parliament**.

# CHARTER ACT, 1833


This Act was a **major step toward centralization**.


---


## Features


### 1. Governor-General of India Created


Governor-General of Bengal became:


**Governor-General of India**


Powers:

- Civil powers
- Military powers

First Governor-General of India:

- **Lord William Bentinck**

Result:

- First **central government for entire British India**.

---


### 2. Legislative Power Centralized


Governors of:

- Bombay
- Madras

lost their **legislative powers**.


Legislative power now with:

- **Governor-General of India**

---


### 3. Laws Called Acts


Before this act:

- Laws called **Regulations**

After this act:

- Laws called **Acts**

---


### 4. End of Company’s Commercial Role


East India Company stopped:

- Trading activities

Company became only:

- **Administrative body**

Its territories were held **in trust for the British Crown**.


---


### 5. Attempt to Introduce Open Competition


Act stated:


Indians should **not be barred from public employment**.


But:

- This provision was **not implemented** due to opposition.

---


# CHARTER ACT, 1853


Last of the Charter Acts (1793–1853).


Considered a **major constitutional landmark**.


---


## Features


### 1. Separation of Legislative and Executive Functions


Governor-General's council divided into:

- Executive functions
- Legislative functions

Six new members added called:


**Legislative Councillors**


This body became:


**Indian (Central) Legislative Council**


---


### 2. Mini-Parliament System


Legislative council worked like:

- British Parliament

Legislation now required:

- Special machinery
- Special process

---


### 3. Open Competition for Civil Services


Civil services recruitment introduced through:


**Open competitive exams**


Civil service opened to Indians.


Committee formed:


**Macaulay Committee (1854)**


---


### 4. Company Rule Extended Without Time Limit


Earlier charter acts:

- Specified duration.

But this act:

- Did **not specify time period**.

Meaning:

- Parliament could end Company rule anytime.

---


### 5. Introduction of Local Representation


For the first time:

- **Local representation introduced**

Six legislative members included representatives from:

- Madras
- Bombay
- Bengal
- Agra

---


# END OF COMPANY RULE


Company rule ended after:


### Revolt of 1857


Result:


**Government of India Act 1858**

- British Crown directly ruled India.

- Crown rule started → **1858**

---


# THE CROWN RULE (1858–1947)


The **Revolt of 1857** forced the British government to change its policy toward India.


Result:

- **Company rule ended**
- **British Crown took direct control of India**

This was done through the **Government of India Act, 1858**.


---


# GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1858


Also known as:


**Act for the Good Government of India**


Reason:

- Enacted after **Revolt of 1857**.

Main objective:

- Transfer administration from **East India Company → British Crown**.

---


## Major Provisions


### 1. India to be Governed in the Name of the Crown


India was to be governed:

> “In the name of Her Majesty”

Meaning:

- Direct control of **British monarch**.

---


### 2. Governor-General Became Viceroy


The **Governor-General of India** was redesignated as:


**Viceroy of India**


Meaning:

- Representative of British Crown in India.

First Viceroy:

- **Lord Canning**

---


### 3. End of Double Government


The system created by **Pitt’s India Act (1784)** ended.


Two bodies abolished:

1. **Board of Control**
2. **Court of Directors**

---


### 4. Creation of Secretary of State for India


A new office was created:


**Secretary of State for India**


Features:

- Had **complete authority over Indian administration**
- Member of **British Cabinet**
- Responsible to **British Parliament**

This made Indian administration **fully controlled from Britain**.


---


### 5. Council of India Created


A **15-member Council of India** was established.


Role:

- Assist the Secretary of State.

Characteristics:

- Advisory body
- Secretary of State was **Chairman**

---


### 6. Secretary of State-in-Council


This body was given the status of a **corporate body**.


Meaning:


It could:

- **Sue**
- **Be sued**

Both in:

- India
- England

---


## Important Observation


The Act mainly:

- **Changed control mechanism in Britain**
- Did **not significantly change administration in India**.

---


# INDIAN COUNCILS ACTS


After the revolt, British policy changed.


Earlier policy:

- Exclusion of Indians.

New policy:

- **Association of Indians in administration**

Three Acts passed:

1. **Indian Councils Act 1861**
2. **Indian Councils Act 1892**
3. **Indian Councils Act 1909**

---


# INDIAN COUNCILS ACT, 1861


Very important constitutional landmark.


---


## Features


### 1. Beginning of **legislative representation**, not democracy.


Indians were associated with the **law-making process**.


Provision:

- Viceroy could nominate Indians to legislative council.

Example:


In **1862**, Lord Canning nominated:

- Raja of Benaras
- Maharaja of Patiala
- Sir Dinkar Rao

---


### 2. Beginning of Decentralisation


Legislative powers restored to:

- Bombay Presidency
- Madras Presidency

This reversed the centralization created by:

- **Charter Act 1833**

Later result:

- Provinces gained **internal autonomy in 1937**.

---


### 3. New Legislative Councils Created


Legislative councils established for:


| Province | Year |
| -------- | ---- |
| Bengal   | 1862 |
| NWFP     | 1866 |
| Punjab   | 1897 |


---


### 4. Portfolio System Recognised


Introduced earlier by **Lord Canning (1859)**.


Meaning:


Each member of Viceroy's council:

- Responsible for specific department.

Example:

- Finance
- Home
- Defence

Members could issue **final orders** for their department.


This is the origin of the **modern cabinet system**.


---


### 5. Ordinance Power of Viceroy


Viceroy could issue **ordinances during emergencies**.


Conditions:

- Without legislative council approval.

Validity:

- **6 months**

This power later influenced **Article 123 of Indian Constitution**.


---


# INDIAN COUNCILS ACT, 1892


This act introduced **limited legislative reforms**.


---


## Features


### 1. Increased Legislative Members


Number of **additional (non-official) members increased to 16**.


But:

- **Official majority retained**.

Meaning:

- British officials still controlled decisions.

---


### 2. Legislative Councils Got New Powers


Members could now:

- Discuss **budget**
- Ask **questions to executive**

But they **could not vote on budget**.


Note:


Budget system introduced in India in **1860**.


---


### 3. Indirect Election Introduced


Members were nominated based on recommendations from:

- Provincial legislative councils
- Bengal Chamber of Commerce
- District boards
- Municipalities
- Universities
- Trade associations
- Zamindars
- Chambers of commerce

Important fact:


The word **“election” was not used** in the Act.


It was described as:

> “Nomination on recommendation”.

---


# INDIAN COUNCILS ACT, 1909


(MORLEY–MINTO REFORMS)


Named after:

- **Lord Morley** → Secretary of State for India
- **Lord Minto** → Viceroy of India

---


## Features


### 1. Size of Legislative Councils Increased


Central Legislative Council:


Members increased from


**16 → 60**


Provincial council size:

- Varied across provinces.

---


### 2. Official Majority Retained at Centre


At **Central Legislative Council**:

- Official majority maintained.

At **Provincial Councils**:

- Non-official majority allowed.

---


### 3. Legislative Powers Increased


Members could now:

- Ask **supplementary questions**
- Move **resolutions on budget**
- Participate in discussions

---


### 4. Indians in Executive Councils


Indians were allowed to join **executive councils**.


Example:


**Satyendra Prasad Sinha**

- First Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council
- Law member.

---


### 5. Introduction of Separate Electorates


Most important feature.


Separate electorate introduced for:


**Muslims**


### What is a Separate Electorate?


A **separate electorate** means:

- A **particular community elects its own representatives**.
- **Only voters of that community can vote** for those seats.
- Candidates must also belong to the **same community**.

Impact:

- Beginning of **communal politics in India**.

Because of this:


**Lord Minto called “Father of Communal Electorate”**.


---


### 6. Special Representation


Separate representation given to:

- Chambers of commerce
- Universities
- Zamindars
- Presidency corporations

---

| -------- | ------------------------------- |

| 1861 Act | Indians included in councils    |
| 1892 Act | Budget discussion allowed       |
| 1909 Act | Separate electorate for Muslims |

# Conceptual Flow (Important for Mains)


British reforms followed a pattern:


1️⃣ **1858** → Crown took control


2️⃣ **1861** → Indians included in councils


3️⃣ **1892** → Limited legislative powers


4️⃣ **1909** → Separate electorates introduced

# GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1919


_(Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms)_


Background:


On **20 August 1917**, the British Government declared its policy:

> Gradual development of responsible government in India.

Based on this declaration, the **Government of India Act 1919** was enacted.


It came into force in:


**1921**


Named after:


| Person          | Position                     |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Edwin Montagu   | Secretary of State for India |
| Lord Chelmsford | Viceroy of India             |


---


# Features of Government of India Act 1919


## 1. Division of Subjects (Central vs Provincial)


Subjects divided into:

- **Central subjects**
- **Provincial subjects**

Both legislatures could make laws on their respective subjects.


However:


India still remained a **centralised unitary state**.


---


## 2. Introduction of Dyarchy in Provinces


Provincial subjects divided into two categories:


### (1) Transferred Subjects


Administered by:

- Governor
- Ministers responsible to legislative council

Examples:

- Education
- Local government
- Public health

---


### (2) Reserved Subjects


Administered by:

- Governor
- Executive council

They were **not responsible to legislature**.


Examples:

- Police
- Finance
- Revenue

---


### Meaning of Dyarchy


Dyarchy = **Double Rule**


Origin of term:

- Greek word **Di-arche**

Meaning:

- Dual authority.

However:


This system **failed in practice**.


---


## 3. Bicameral Legislature Introduced


For the first time in India.


Indian Legislative Council replaced by **two houses**:


| House                | Role        |
| -------------------- | ----------- |
| Council of State     | Upper House |
| Legislative Assembly | Lower House |


Most members were **directly elected**.


---


## 4. Indians in Viceroy's Executive Council


Requirement:


Out of 6 members:

- **3 had to be Indians**

(Commander-in-Chief excluded)


---


## 5. Expansion of Communal Representation


Separate electorates extended to:

- Sikhs
- Indian Christians
- Anglo-Indians
- Europeans

Communal politics thus expanded further.


---


## 6. Limited Franchise Introduced


Voting rights were given to only a **small section of population**.


Criteria:

- Property ownership
- Tax payment
- Education level

Thus, democracy was **very limited**.


---


## 7. High Commissioner for India


New office created:


**High Commissioner for India in London**


Some powers of Secretary of State transferred to him.


---


## 8. Public Service Commission


Provision for establishment of:


**Public Service Commission**


Result:


**Central Public Service Commission established in 1926**.


Recommendation came from:


**Lee Commission (1923-24)**.


---


## 9. Separate Provincial Budgets


For the first time:

- Provincial budgets separated from **Central Budget**.

Provincial legislatures got power to pass budgets.


---


## 10. Provision for Statutory Commission


Act provided for a commission after **10 years** to review working.


This led to the creation of:


**Simon Commission (1927)**.


---


# SIMON COMMISSION (1927)


Purpose:


To review working of **Government of India Act 1919**.


Chairman:


**Sir John Simon**


Members:

- Total **7 members**
- All were **British**

This caused widespread protests.


Slogan:


**“Simon Go Back”**


---


## Recommendations of Simon Commission

1. Abolition of **dyarchy**
2. Responsible government in **provinces**
3. Creation of **federation**
4. Continuation of **communal electorates**

Report submitted in:


**1930**


---


## Round Table Conferences


To discuss reforms, **three Round Table Conferences** were held.


Participants:

- British Government
- British India representatives
- Princely states

Based on discussions:


A document called **White Paper on Constitutional Reforms** was prepared.


This became the basis of:


**Government of India Act 1935**.


---


## 🔹 Nehru Report (1928)

- Prepared by **Motilal Nehru**
- Response to **Simon Commission**
- Proposed:
    - Dominion status
    - Fundamental Rights
    - Responsible government
    - Joint electorates

---


# COMMUNAL AWARD (1932)


Announced by:


**Ramsay MacDonald**


(British Prime Minister)


Purpose:


Representation of minorities.


---


## Features


Separate electorates continued for:

- Muslims
- Sikhs
- Indian Christians
- Anglo-Indians
- Europeans

New addition:

- **Depressed classes (Scheduled Castes)**

---


## Reaction of Mahatma Gandhi


Gandhi strongly opposed:


Separate electorates for depressed classes.


Reason:


It would **divide Hindu society**.


He started:


**Fast unto death in Yeravada Jail (Poona)**.


---


# POONA PACT (1932)


Agreement between:

- **Congress leaders**
- **Leaders of depressed classes**

Outcome:

- Separate electorates removed.
- Reserved seats provided for **Scheduled Castes**.

But voting remained under:


**Joint Hindu electorate**.


---


# GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1935


Most important constitutional act before independence.


Features:

- **321 Sections**
- **10 Schedules**

Considered the **second milestone toward responsible government**.


---


# Major Features


## 1. All-India Federation


Proposed federation of:

- British provinces
- Princely states

Three lists introduced:


| List            | Subjects |
| --------------- | -------- |
| Federal List    | 59       |
| Provincial List | 54       |
| Concurrent List | 36       |


Residuary powers given to:


**Viceroy**


However:


Federation **never came into existence**.


Reason:


Princely states did not join.


---


## 2. Provincial Autonomy


Dyarchy in provinces abolished.


Provinces given **autonomous powers**.


Governors required to act on advice of:


**Council of Ministers responsible to legislature**.


Implemented in:


**1937**


Ended in:


**1939**


---


## 3. Dyarchy at Centre


Federal subjects divided into:

- Reserved
- Transferred

But this provision **never came into operation**.


---


## 4. Bicameralism in Provinces


Six provinces became bicameral:


Province


---


Bengal


---


Bombay


---


Madras


---


Bihar


---


Assam


---


United Provinces


---


Each had:

- Legislative Council
- Legislative Assembly

---


## 5. Extension of Communal Representation


Separate electorates given to:

- Scheduled Castes
- Women
- Labour

---


## 6. Abolition of Council of India


Council created in **1858 Act** abolished.


Secretary of State assisted by **advisors** instead.


---


## 7. Franchise Expanded


Voting rights extended to about:


**10% of population**


---


## 8. Reserve Bank of India


Provision for establishment of:


**Reserve Bank of India**


Purpose:


Control currency and credit.


---


## 9. Public Service Commissions


Act provided for:

- Federal Public Service Commission
- Provincial Public Service Commissions
- Joint Public Service Commissions

---


## 10. Federal Court


Provision for **Federal Court of India**


Established in:


**1937**


---

- Offered:
    - Expansion of Viceroy Executive Council
    - War advisory council
    - Post-war constitution making

Rejected by **Congress**.

# INDIAN INDEPENDENCE ACT, 1947


Background:


On **20 February 1947**


British PM **Clement Attlee** announced:


British rule would end by **30 June 1948**.


However political developments led to:


**Partition of India**.


---


## Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)


Proposed:


Partition of India into:

- India
- Pakistan

Accepted by:

- Congress
- Muslim League

---


## Indian Independence Act Passed


Bill introduced:


**4 July 1947**


Royal assent:


**18 July 1947**


Act came into force:


**15 August 1947**


---


# Features of the Act


## 1. End of British Rule


India became:


**Independent and Sovereign State**


on **15 August 1947**.


---


## 2. Partition of India


Two dominions created:

- India
- Pakistan

Both could leave:


**British Commonwealth**


---


## 3. End of Viceroy


Office of **Viceroy abolished**.


Each dominion had:


**Governor-General**


Appointed by:


British King (on advice of dominion cabinet).


---


## 4. Constituent Assembly Powers


Constituent Assemblies could:

- Frame constitution
- Repeal British laws

Even repeal **Independence Act itself**.


---


## 5. Legislative Powers


Constituent Assemblies could legislate until constitution enacted.


No British law after **15 Aug 1947** would apply unless accepted.


---


## 6. Secretary of State for India Abolished


Functions transferred to:


**Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs**


---


## 7. End of British Paramountcy


British control over princely states ended.


Treaties with princely states **lapsed**.


---


## 8. Freedom for Princely States


States could:

- Join India
- Join Pakistan
- Remain independent

---


## 9. Interim Governance


Until constitution framed:


Government run under:


**Government of India Act 1935**


(with modifications).


---


## 10. Royal Veto Removed


British Monarch lost power to:

- Veto bills
- Reserve bills

But **Governor-General retained assent power**.


---


## 11. Constitutional Heads


Governor-General and Governors became:


**Nominal heads**


They acted on advice of:


**Council of Ministers**.


---


## 12. Title Removed


British King dropped title:


**“Emperor of India”**


---


## 13. Civil Services Protection


Civil servants appointed before independence retained:

- Salary
- Benefits
- Service rights

---


# Important Facts About Independence


Power transferred:


**Midnight of 14–15 August 1947**


First Governor-General of India:


**Lord Mountbatten**


First Prime Minister:


**Jawaharlal Nehru**


Constituent Assembly became:


**Dominion Parliament**
