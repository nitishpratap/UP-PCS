## 1. Meaning & Constitutional Basis


The **success of Indian federalism** depends on:


| Dimension                  | Meaning                            |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Centre–State Relations** | Cooperation between Union & States |
| **Inter-State Relations**  | Cooperation among states           |


The **Constitution provides mechanisms to maintain inter-state harmony (comity).**


### Constitutional Mechanisms


| Mechanism                                                 | Article         |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Mutual recognition of acts, records, judicial proceedings | **Art 261**     |
| Inter-State Water Disputes                                | **Art 262**     |
| Inter-State Council                                       | **Art 263**     |
| Inter-State Trade, Commerce & Intercourse                 | **Art 301–307** |


Additional mechanism created by law:


| Body               | Law                                 |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| **Zonal Councils** | **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** |

# 2. INTER-STATE WATER DISPUTES


## Constitutional Provision


### Article 262


Parliament may legislate for:


| Provision                          | Meaning                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Adjudication of water disputes** | Use, distribution, control of waters of **inter-state rivers/river valleys** |
| **Exclusion of jurisdiction**      | **Supreme Court & other courts can be excluded**                             |


---


## Laws Enacted by Parliament (1956)


| Law                                      | Purpose                                        | Key Feature                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **River Boards Act, 1956**               | Regulation & development of inter-state rivers | Boards created **only on request of states** |
| **Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956** | Settlement of disputes                         | **Ad-hoc tribunal created by Centre**        |


### Tribunal Decisions


| Feature         | Meaning                      |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Final           | Binding on states            |
| Judicial review | **SC jurisdiction excluded** |


---


## Why Special Tribunals?


Although **Article 131 gives SC jurisdiction in Centre-State disputes**, water disputes require:

- Technical expertise
- Economic considerations
- Social and political factors
- Public interest issues

Therefore **special tribunals are preferred**.

# 3. INTER-STATE COUNCIL


## Constitutional Provision


### Article 263


The **President may establish an Inter-State Council** if **public interest requires coordination between Centre and States**.


The President determines:

- Duties
- Organisation
- Procedure

---


## Functions (Article 263)


| Function Type              | Role                             |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Inquiry & Advice           | Investigate inter-state disputes |
| Investigation & Discussion | Subjects of common interest      |
| Policy Coordination        | Improve coordination of policies |


Nature → **Advisory (Recommendatory)**


---


## Councils Established Under Article 263


Examples:

- Central Council of Health
- Central Council of Local Government & Urban Development
- Regional Sales Tax Councils
    - Northern
    - Eastern
    - Western
    - Southern

Also established by Acts:

- Central Council of Indian Medicine
- Central Council of Homoeopathy

# 4. MUTUAL RECOGNITION OF ACTS & JUDGMENTS


## Constitutional Provision


### Article 261 — Full Faith and Credit Clause


Ensures **mutual recognition of governmental acts across India**.


---


## Three Components


| Provision              | Meaning                                                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recognition            | Public acts, records & judicial proceedings recognised across India |
| Parliamentary Power    | Parliament determines proof & legal effect                          |
| Execution of Judgments | Civil judgments executable anywhere in India                        |


---


## Meaning of Key Terms


| Term           | Meaning                                        |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Public Acts    | Legislative & executive acts                   |
| Public Records | Registers, official records of public servants |


---


## Important Limitation


| Applies to        | Civil Judgments    |
| ----------------- | ------------------ |
| Not applicable to | Criminal Judgments |


Courts **do not enforce penal laws of other states**.

# 5. INTER-STATE TRADE, COMMERCE & INTERCOURSE


## Constitutional Provisions


| Articles    | Subject                       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| **301–307** | Trade & Commerce within India |


---


## Article 301


**Trade, commerce and intercourse throughout India shall be FREE.**


Objective:

- Economic unity
- Removal of trade barriers
- Free flow of goods & services

Scope applies to:


| Type              | Meaning        |
| ----------------- | -------------- |
| Inter-State Trade | Between states |
| Intra-State Trade | Within states  |


Restrictions before/after borders may violate **Art 301**.


---


# Exceptions to Freedom of Trade


Freedom under Article 301 **is not absolute**.


| Article | Provision                                             |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **302** | Parliament may impose restrictions in public interest |
| **303** | No discrimination between states                      |
| **304** | States may impose restrictions                        |
| **305** | Saving of existing laws & monopolies                  |


---


## Article 302


Parliament may restrict trade **in public interest**.


Example:


**Essential Commodities Act, 1955**


Regulates:

- Petroleum
- Coal
- Iron
- Steel

---


## Article 303


Parliament **cannot give preference to one state over another**.


Exception → **Scarcity of goods**.


---


## Article 304 — Powers of States


### Article 304(a)


States may impose **tax on imported goods** if:


Tax = **same as locally produced goods**


Purpose → Prevent **discriminatory taxation**


---


### Article 304(b)


States may impose **reasonable restrictions** if:


| Condition             | Requirement                       |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Public Interest       | Must be justified                 |
| Presidential sanction | Required before bill introduction |


---


## Article 305


Protects:

- Existing laws
- State monopolies

Government may create monopolies in:

- Trade
- Industry
- Business
- Services

---


## Article 306


Earlier allowed trade restrictions by certain states.


**Repealed.**


---


## Article 307


Parliament may establish an **authority to regulate trade freedom**.


Important fact:


**No such authority has been created yet.**

# 6. ZONAL COUNCILS


## Nature


| Feature | Status                              |
| ------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Type    | **Statutory body**                  |
| Law     | **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** |
| Not     | Constitutional body                 |


Purpose:

- Inter-state cooperation
- National integration
- Policy coordination

---


## Zonal Division of India


| Zone     | Members                                                              | HQ        |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Northern | J&K, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan, Delhi, Chandigarh | New Delhi |
| Central  | Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, MP, Chhattisgarh                         | Allahabad |
| Eastern  | Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha                                | Kolkata   |
| Western  | Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu         | Mumbai    |
| Southern | AP, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Puducherry             | Chennai   |


---


## Factors Used for Creating Zones

- Natural divisions
- River systems
- Communication links
- Cultural affinity
- Linguistic similarity
- Economic development
- Security needs
- Law & order

---


## Composition of Zonal Councils


### Members

- **Union Home Minister**
- **Chief Ministers of states in zone**
- **Two ministers from each state**
- **Administrator of each UT**

---


### Advisory Members (No Voting Rights)

- Planning Commission nominee
- Chief Secretaries
- Development Commissioners

---


## Chairman & Vice Chairman


| Position      | Holder                                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Chairman      | **Union Home Minister**                          |
| Vice Chairman | **Chief Minister of one state (rotates yearly)** |


---


## Functions

- Promote **emotional integration**
- Reduce **regionalism & linguistic tensions**
- Solve problems after **state reorganisation**
- Promote **Centre-State cooperation**
- Exchange policy ideas
- Coordinate development projects
- Maintain political balance

---


## Issues Discussed

- Economic planning
- Social planning
- Linguistic minorities
- Border disputes
- Inter-state transport
- Development projects

Nature → **Advisory & deliberative body**
