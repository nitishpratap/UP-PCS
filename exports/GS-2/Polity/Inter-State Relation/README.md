## 1. Meaning & Constitutional Basis


The **success of Indian federalism** depends on:


| Dimension                  | Meaning                            |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Centre–State Relations** | Cooperation between Union & States |
| **Inter-State Relations**  | Cooperation among states           |


The **Constitution provides mechanisms to maintain inter-state harmony (comity).**


### Constitutional Mechanisms


| Mechanism                                                 | Article         |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Mutual recognition of acts, records, judicial proceedings | **Art 261**     |
| Inter-State Water Disputes                                | **Art 262**     |
| Inter-State Council                                       | **Art 263**     |
| Inter-State Trade, Commerce & Intercourse                 | **Art 301–307** |


Additional mechanism created by law:


| Body               | Law                                 |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| **Zonal Councils** | **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** |

| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |

# 5. INTER-STATE TRADE, COMMERCE & INTERCOURSE


## Constitutional Provisions


| Articles    | Subject                       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| **301–307** | Trade & Commerce within India |


---


## Article 301


**Trade, commerce and intercourse throughout India shall be FREE.**


Objective:

- Economic unity
- Removal of trade barriers
- Free flow of goods & services

Scope applies to:


| Type              | Meaning        |
| ----------------- | -------------- |
| Inter-State Trade | Between states |
| Intra-State Trade | Within states  |


Restrictions before/after borders may violate **Art 301**.


---


# Exceptions to Freedom of Trade


Freedom under Article 301 **is not absolute**.


| Article | Provision                                             |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **302** | Parliament may impose restrictions in public interest |
| **303** | No discrimination between states                      |
| **304** | States may impose restrictions                        |
| **305** | Saving of existing laws & monopolies                  |


---


## Article 302


Parliament may restrict trade **in public interest**.


Example:


**Essential Commodities Act, 1955**


Regulates:

- Petroleum
- Coal
- Iron
- Steel

---


## Article 303


Parliament **cannot give preference to one state over another**.


Exception → **Scarcity of goods**.


---


## Article 304 — Powers of States


### Article 304(a)


States may impose **tax on imported goods** if:


Tax = **same as locally produced goods**


Purpose → Prevent **discriminatory taxation**


---


### Article 304(b)


States may impose **reasonable restrictions** if:


| Condition             | Requirement                       |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Public Interest       | Must be justified                 |
| Presidential sanction | Required before bill introduction |


---


## Article 305


Protects:

- Existing laws
- State monopolies

Government may create monopolies in:

- Trade
- Industry
- Business
- Services

---


## Article 306


Earlier allowed trade restrictions by certain states.


**Repealed.**


---


## Article 307


Parliament may establish an **authority to regulate trade freedom**.


Important fact:


**No such authority has been created yet.**

## 1. Meaning of Inter-State Relations


The **successful functioning of the Indian federal system** depends on:

1. Harmonious relations between **Centre and States**
2. Cooperation between **States themselves**

Therefore, the **Constitution provides several mechanisms to ensure inter-state comity (friendly relations).**


### Constitutional Provisions for Inter-State Relations

1. **Adjudication of Inter-State Water Disputes**
2. **Coordination through Inter-State Council**
3. **Mutual recognition of public acts, records and judicial proceedings**
4. **Freedom of Inter-State Trade, Commerce and Intercourse**

Apart from these constitutional provisions, **Parliament has established Zonal Councils** to promote cooperation between states.


---


# 2. INTER-STATE WATER DISPUTES


## Constitutional Provision


### Article 262


Provides for **adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-state rivers or river valleys**.


It empowers **Parliament to make laws** regarding water disputes.


### Two Important Provisions


1️⃣ Parliament may provide for **adjudication of disputes regarding:**

- Use of waters
- Distribution of waters
- Control of waters

    of **inter-state rivers or river valleys**


2️⃣ Parliament may also provide that:

- **Supreme Court**
- **Any other court**

shall **not have jurisdiction** over such disputes.


---


# Laws Made by Parliament


Under Article 262, Parliament enacted **two laws in 1956**


### 1. River Boards Act, 1956


Purpose:

- Establish **River Boards**
- Regulation and development of **inter-state rivers and river valleys**

Important Point:

- River Board is **established by the Central Government**
- Only **after request from concerned state governments**
- Role: **Advisory**

---


### 2. Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956


Purpose:


To resolve **water disputes between states**.


Key Features:

1. Central Government may establish an **Ad-hoc Tribunal**
2. Tribunal settles disputes between **two or more states**
3. Decision of tribunal is:
- **Final**
- **Binding**
1. **Supreme Court and other courts have no jurisdiction** in such disputes.

---


## Why Extra-Judicial Mechanism is Needed


Even though **Supreme Court has jurisdiction under Article 131**, special tribunals are preferred because:

- Water disputes involve **public interest**
- They involve **economic, social and political issues**
- Private law principles cannot properly resolve such disputes.

Therefore **special tribunals are created**.


---

So far **8 tribunals have been established** by the Central Government.

# 3. INTER-STATE COUNCIL


## Constitutional Provision


### Article 263


Provides for **establishment of Inter-State Council** to improve coordination between:

- States
- Centre and States

The **President may establish such a council** if it appears that **public interest would be served**.


The President also defines:

- Duties
- Organisation
- Procedure

---


# Functions of Inter-State Council


Article 263 specifies three possible duties.


### 1. Inquiry and Advice

- Enquire into disputes between states
- Provide advice

### 2. Investigation and Discussion


Discuss subjects where **Centre and states have common interest**


### 3. Policy Coordination


Make recommendations for **better coordination of policy and action**


---


### Important Concept


The Inter-State Council's function is **advisory**, unlike the **Supreme Court**, which gives **binding judgments**.


---


# Councils Established Under Article 263


Examples include:

1. **Central Council of Health**
2. **Central Council of Local Government and Urban Development**
3. **Regional Sales Tax Councils** for:
- Northern Zone
- Eastern Zone
- Western Zone
- Southern Zone

Additionally:

- **Central Council of Indian Medicine**
- **Central Council of Homoeopathy**

were established by **Acts of Parliament**.


---


# Establishment of Permanent Inter-State Council


### Recommendation


**Sarkaria Commission (1983-87)** on Centre-State relations recommended:

- Creation of a **permanent Inter-State Council**
- Should be called **Inter-Governmental Council**

It recommended assigning duties mentioned in:

- **Article 263(b)**
- **Article 263(c)**

---


## Actual Establishment


The **Inter-State Council was established in 1990** by:


Government of **V. P. Singh**


---


# Composition of Inter-State Council


Members include:

# Nature of the Council


The Inter-State Council is:


✔ **Recommendatory body**


✔ Deals with **Centre-State and Inter-State relations**


✔ Promotes **coordination and cooperation**

# Functions of Inter-State Council

# 4. MUTUAL RECOGNITION OF PUBLIC ACTS, RECORDS AND JUDICIAL PROCEEDINGS


## Constitutional Provision


### Article 261


Article 261 incorporates the **“Full Faith and Credit Clause.”**


Purpose:


Since **each state has jurisdiction only within its territory**, conflicts may arise regarding recognition of:

- Acts
- Records
- Court decisions

Therefore, the Constitution ensures **mutual recognition across India**.


---


## Full Faith and Credit Clause


The clause contains **three provisions**:


### 1. Recognition of Acts and Records


Full faith and credit shall be given throughout India to:

- **Public acts**
- **Public records**
- **Judicial proceedings**

of:

- **Central Government**
- **State Governments**

### Meaning of Key Terms


**Public Acts**


Includes:

- Legislative acts
- Executive acts of government

**Public Records**


Includes:

- Official books
- Registers
- Records prepared by public servants in official duty

---


### 2. Power of Parliament


Parliament may determine:

- **How these acts and records will be proved**
- **What legal effect they will have**

Thus the rule of recognition is **subject to parliamentary legislation**.


---


### 3. Execution of Civil Judgments


Final judgments of **civil courts** in any part of India:


✔ Can be executed anywhere in India


✔ No need to file a **fresh suit**


Important:


This applies only to **civil judgments**.


It **does not apply to criminal judgments**.


Therefore:


Courts of one state **are not required to enforce the penal laws of another state**.


---


# 5. INTER-STATE TRADE, COMMERCE AND INTERCOURSE


## Constitutional Provisions


Articles **301 to 307** (Part XIII of Constitution)


These deal with **trade and commerce within India**.


---


# Article 301


### Freedom of Trade, Commerce and Intercourse


Article 301 declares:

> Trade, commerce and intercourse throughout the territory of India shall be **free**.

---


## Objective


To:

- Break down **trade barriers between states**
- Create **economic unity**
- Promote **free flow of goods and services**

---


## Scope of Freedom


The freedom applies to:

1. **Inter-State trade** (between states)
2. **Intra-State trade** (within a state)

Therefore:


Restrictions imposed at:

- State borders
- Before border
- After border

can violate Article 301.


---


# Exceptions to Article 301


Freedom under Article 301 is **not absolute**.


Restrictions are allowed under **Articles 302–305**.


---


# Article 302


### Parliament's Power


Parliament may impose restrictions on:

- Trade
- Commerce
- Intercourse

within India **in public interest**.


Example:


**Essential Commodities Act, 1955**


Allows the government to control:

- Production
- Supply
- Distribution

of commodities like:

- Petroleum
- Coal
- Iron
- Steel

---


# Article 303


### Restriction on Discrimination


Parliament **cannot**:

- Give preference to one state over another
- Discriminate between states

Exception:


Allowed in case of **scarcity of goods in any part of India**.


---


# Article 304


### Power of States


State legislatures can impose restrictions.


Two provisions exist.


---


## Article 304(a)


States can impose **tax on imported goods** from other states.


But:


The tax must be **equal to tax on locally produced goods**.


Purpose:


Prevent **discriminatory taxation**.


---


## Article 304(b)


State legislature can impose **reasonable restrictions** on trade.


Conditions:

1. Must be **in public interest**
2. Bill must have **previous sanction of President**

Also:


States **cannot discriminate between states**.


---


# Article 305


### Saving of Existing Laws


Article 305 protects:

1. **Existing laws**
2. **Laws creating state monopolies**

Therefore:


Centre or states can create **government monopolies** in:

- Trade
- Industry
- Business
- Services

Even if it restricts free trade.


---


# Article 306


This article earlier allowed certain states to impose trade restrictions.


But it was **repealed**.


---


# Article 307


Parliament may create an **authority** to implement Articles 301–304.


This authority would regulate:

- Freedom of trade
- Restrictions on trade

However:


**No such authority has been established yet**.


---


# 6. ZONAL COUNCILS


## Nature


Zonal Councils are:


✔ **Statutory bodies**


❌ Not constitutional bodies


They were created under:


**States Reorganisation Act, 1956**


---


## Purpose


To promote:

- Inter-state cooperation
- Policy coordination
- National integration

---


## Division of Country into Zones


India was divided into **five zones**:

1. Northern
2. Central
3. Eastern
4. Western
5. Southern

---


## Factors Considered While Forming Zones

1. Natural divisions of the country
2. River systems
3. Communication links
4. Cultural affinity
5. Linguistic similarity
6. Economic development needs
7. Security considerations
8. Law and order requirements

---


# Composition of Zonal Councils


Each council consists of:


### Members

1. **Union Home Minister**
2. **Chief Ministers of states in the zone**
3. **Two other ministers from each state**
4. **Administrator of each Union Territory**

---


### Advisory Members (No Voting Rights)

1. Representative nominated by **Planning Commission**
2. **Chief Secretary of each state**
3. **Development Commissioner of each state**

---


# Chairman and Vice Chairman


**Chairman**


Union **Home Minister**


(common chairman of all five councils)


**Vice-Chairman**


Chief Minister of a state in the zone

- Rotates every **one year**

---


# Functions of Zonal Councils

1. Promote **emotional integration**
2. Reduce **regionalism and linguistic tensions**
3. Remove effects of **state reorganisation**
4. Promote **Centre-State cooperation**
5. Exchange **ideas and policy experiences**
6. Coordinate **major development projects**
7. Maintain **political balance between regions**

---


# Subjects Discussed in Zonal Councils


They discuss issues like:

- Economic planning
- Social planning
- Linguistic minorities
- Border disputes
- Inter-state transport
- Development projects

Important:


They are **advisory and deliberative bodies**.


---
