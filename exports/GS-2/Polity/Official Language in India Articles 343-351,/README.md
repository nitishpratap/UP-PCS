
# OFFICIAL LANGUAGE


---


# 1. Constitutional Provisions on Official Language


The provisions related to the **Official Language of India** are contained in **Part XVII of the Constitution (Articles 343–351)**.


These provisions are divided into **four categories**:

1. Language of the Union
2. Regional Languages
3. Language of the Judiciary and Texts of Laws
4. Special Directives

---


# 2. Language of the Union


The Constitution lays down the following provisions regarding the official language of the Union.


## 1. Official Language

- **Hindi in Devanagari script** is the **official language of the Union**.

### Numerals

- The **numerals for official purposes** shall be the **international form of Indian numerals (1,2,3)**
- NOT the **Devanagari numerals**

---


## 2. Use of English (Transitional Period)


The Constitution provided a **transitional arrangement**.

- English would continue for **15 years after the commencement of the Constitution**
- Period: **1950–1965**

During this period English would be used for:

- Official purposes of Union
- Parliamentary proceedings
- Communication

---


## 3. Use of English after 1965


Even after 1965:

- **Parliament may provide for continued use of English**

This provision led to the **Official Language Act 1963**.


---


## 4. Official Language Commission


The Constitution provides for the appointment of an **Official Language Commission**.


### Appointment


The **President shall appoint the Commission**:

- At the end of **5 years**
- At the end of **10 years**

after the commencement of the Constitution.


### Functions


The Commission makes recommendations regarding:

1. Progressive use of **Hindi**
2. Restrictions on **English**
3. Language for **Union administration**
4. Language for **courts and legislation**
5. Language for **communication between Union and States**

---


## 5. Parliamentary Committee on Official Language


A **Committee of Parliament** examines the report of the Commission.


### Composition

- **30 Members**
    - 20 from **Lok Sabha**
    - 10 from **Rajya Sabha**

### Election


Members are elected by:

- **Proportional Representation**
- **Single Transferable Vote**

The committee submits its views to the **President**.


---

### Report


Submitted in **1956**


---


## Parliamentary Committee on Report


The report was examined by a **Parliamentary Committee in 1957**.


### Chairman

---


## Important Fact


The Constitution envisaged **another commission in 1960**, but **it was never appointed**.


---


# 4. Official Language Act, 1963


Parliament enacted the **Official Language Act in 1963**.


### Purpose


To regulate the **use of Hindi and English** for official purposes.


### Key Provisions

1. **English will continue to be used even after 1965**
2. Both **Hindi and English** can be used for:
    - Official work
    - Parliamentary business
3. The act allows **indefinite use of English** (no time limit)

---


## Amendment of the Act


The Act was amended in **1967**.


### Provision

- Use of **English along with Hindi made compulsory in certain cases**.

---


# 5. Regional Languages


The Constitution **does not specify the official language of states**.


Instead, it gives states freedom.


---


## 1. Official Language of State


A **state legislature may adopt**:

- One or more languages in use in the state

    OR

- Hindi

as the **official language of the state**.


Until such adoption:

- **English continues as official language**

---

---


## Important Fact


States are **not restricted to languages in the Eighth Schedule**.


They can adopt **any language**.


---


# 6. Language for Communication


For communication between:

- Union and states
- One state and another

The language used is **English**.


---


## Exception


Two or more states may agree to use **Hindi**.


Example:

- Rajasthan
- Uttar Pradesh
- Madhya Pradesh
- Bihar

These states communicate in **Hindi**.


---


## Provision under Official Language Act 1963


If communication occurs between:


### Union and Non-Hindi States


→ **English must be used**


### Hindi State → Non-Hindi State


→ Hindi communication must include **English translation**


---


# 7. Recognition of Minority Language in State


If a **substantial proportion of population** demands recognition of a language:


The **President may direct the state** to recognise that language.


Purpose:

- Protect **linguistic minorities**

# 1. Language of the Judiciary and Texts of Laws


The Constitution provides rules regarding **languages used in courts and legislation**.


---


## 1. Language in Supreme Court and High Courts


Until Parliament provides otherwise:


The following shall be **only in English**:

1. **Proceedings in the Supreme Court**
2. **Proceedings in every High Court**
3. **Authoritative texts of:**
    - Bills
    - Acts
    - Ordinances
    - Orders
    - Rules
    - Regulations
    - Bye-laws

These apply at **both Central and State levels**.


---


## 2. Use of Hindi or Other Language in High Court


The **Governor of a State**, with **previous consent of the President**, may allow:

- Hindi

    OR

- Any other official language of the state

for **High Court proceedings**.


However:


Judgements, decrees and orders **must remain in English**.


Unless Parliament provides otherwise.


---


## 3. Language for State Legislation


A **State Legislature may prescribe any language** (other than English) for:

- Bills
- Acts
- Ordinances
- Orders
- Rules
- Regulations
- Bye-laws

BUT:


An **English translation must also be published**.


---


# 2. Provisions under Official Language Act 1963


The **Official Language Act 1963** made additional provisions.


---


## 1. Hindi Authoritative Text


Hindi translations of the following are **authoritative texts**:

- Acts
- Ordinances
- Orders
- Regulations
- Bye-laws

if published under authority of the **President**.


---


## 2. Hindi Translation of Bills


Every bill introduced in **Parliament** must have a **Hindi translation**.


---


## 3. Hindi Translation of State Laws


In certain cases, **state acts or ordinances must also have Hindi translations**.


---


## 4. Language in High Court Judgements


The Act allows:


Governor + President’s consent → Hindi or official language allowed for **High Court judgements**


BUT:


English translation **must be provided**.


---


### States using Hindi in High Courts


Examples:

- Uttar Pradesh
- Madhya Pradesh
- Bihar
- Rajasthan

---


## 5. Language of the Supreme Court


Parliament has **not authorised Hindi in Supreme Court**.


Therefore:

- Supreme Court proceedings are **only in English**.

---


### Important Example


In **1971**, a petitioner tried to argue a **Habeas Corpus petition in Hindi**.


The **Supreme Court cancelled the petition** stating:

- Court language is **English**
- Using Hindi would be **unconstitutional**.

---


# 3. Special Directives


The Constitution provides **special directives related to language policy**.


These directives aim to:

1. Protect **linguistic minorities**
2. Promote **Hindi language development**

---


# 4. Protection of Linguistic Minorities


The Constitution includes several safeguards.


---


## 1. Right to Submit Representation


Any person may submit a **representation (complaint or grievance)**:

- To any **Union or State authority**
- In **any language used in the Union or State**

Therefore:


A representation **cannot be rejected** because it is not in the official language.


---


## 2. Mother-Tongue Education


Every **State and local authority** must provide:


**Adequate facilities for instruction in mother tongue**


for children belonging to **linguistic minorities**


at the **primary stage of education**.


---


### Presidential Power


The **President may issue directions** to states to ensure this.


---


### Important Fact


This provision was added by the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**.


It was based on recommendation of the **States Reorganisation Commission**.


---


## 3. Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities


The **President appoints a Special Officer** for Linguistic Minorities.


### Duties


The officer:

1. Investigates matters relating to **constitutional safeguards for linguistic minorities**
2. Reports to the **President**

---


### Report Procedure


The President:

1. Places the report before **Parliament**
2. Sends it to **state governments**

---


# 5. Development of Hindi Language


The Constitution imposes a **duty on the Union Government**.


The Union must:


Promote **spread and development of Hindi**.


Objective:


Hindi should become the **lingua franca of India's composite culture**.


---


## Enrichment of Hindi


The Union must enrich Hindi by:

1. Assimilating forms and styles of **Hindustani**
2. Incorporating expressions from **Eighth Schedule languages**

---


### Vocabulary Sources


Hindi vocabulary should be drawn:

1. **Primarily from Sanskrit**
2. **Secondarily from other languages**

---

# 9. Committee of Parliament on Official Language


The **Official Language Act 1963** provided for a **Committee of Parliament**.


Purpose:


To review **progress of Hindi usage in official work**.


---


## Establishment


The Committee was established in **1976**.


---


## Composition


Total Members: **30**

- 20 from **Lok Sabha**
- 10 from **Rajya Sabha**

---


## Election Method


Members elected by:


**Proportional Representation using Single Transferable Vote**


---


## Chairman


Chairman is elected by members.


Conventionally:


**Union Home Minister becomes Chairman**.


---


## Functions


The Committee:

1. Reviews progress of **Hindi use in Central Government offices**
2. Submits report to **President**
3. President places report before **Parliament**
4. Report also sent to **State Governments**

---


## Additional Activities


The committee:

- Inspects **Central Government offices**
- Reviews **Hindi implementation**
- Invites experts from:
    - Education
    - Judiciary
    - Government departments

# 10. Classical Language Status


In **2004**, Government of India created a category called:


**Classical Languages**


---


## Criteria


A language must have:

1. **Antiquity of at least 1500–2000 years**
2. Ancient literature considered **valuable heritage**
3. **Original literary tradition**
4. Distinctness from modern language forms
