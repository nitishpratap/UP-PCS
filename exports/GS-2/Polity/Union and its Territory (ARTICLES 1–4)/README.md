
# 🔶 1. UNION OF STATES (Article 1)


## 📌 Key Provisions

- India = **“India, that is Bharat”**
- Described as **Union of States (not Federation)**

---


## 🔍 Why “Union” and not “Federation”? (VERY IMPORTANT)


### Dr. B.R. Ambedkar’s Reasons:

1. **Not formed by agreement among states**

    → Unlike USA

2. **No right to secede**

    → States cannot leave India


👉 Hence:


✅ India = **Indestructible Union of Destructible States**


---


## 📌 Name Controversy in Constituent Assembly

- Some → **“Bharat” (traditional)**
- Others → **“India” (modern)**

    👉 Final compromise: **“India, that is Bharat”**


---


## 📌 Territory of India (Article 1)


### 3 Components:

1. **States**
2. **Union Territories**
3. **Acquired Territories (future)**

---


## ⚠️ Important Concept (Prelims Favorite)


### Difference:

- **Union of India** → Only States
- **Territory of India** → States + UTs + Acquired territories

---


## 📌 Administration

- **States** → Federal system (power sharing)
- **UTs & Acquired Territories** → Directly administered by Centre

---


## 📌 Acquisition of Territory (International Law)


Modes:

- Cession (treaty, gift, purchase, etc.)
- Occupation
- Conquest
- Subjugation

### Examples:

- Dadra & Nagar Haveli
- Goa, Daman & Diu
- Puducherry
- Sikkim

---


## 📌 Special Provisions (VERY IMPORTANT)

- Part XXI → Special states (e.g., Maharashtra, Nagaland, etc.)
- 5th Schedule → Scheduled Areas
- 6th Schedule → Tribal Areas

---


# 🔶 2. ARTICLE 2 vs ARTICLE 3 (SUPER IMPORTANT)


## 📌 Article 2 → External Changes


Parliament can:

- Admit new states
- Establish new states

👉 Applies to **areas NOT part of India**


---


## 📌 Article 3 → Internal Changes


Deals with:

- Formation of new states
- Alteration of existing states

---


# 🔶 3. PARLIAMENT’S POWER (Article 3)


Parliament can:

1. Form new state
2. Increase area
3. Decrease area
4. Alter boundaries
5. Change name

---


## 📌 Conditions (IMPORTANT)

1. **President’s prior recommendation required**
2. **State Legislature must be consulted**

---


## ⚠️ BUT VERY IMPORTANT:

- Parliament **NOT bound** by State’s views
- Can **accept/reject** suggestions
- No need to re-consult on amendments
- For UTs → No consultation needed

---


## 📌 Key Conclusion:


👉 Parliament can **redraw India’s political map**


---


## 🔥 KEY CONCEPT (Mains + Prelims)


### India:


👉 **Indestructible Union of Destructible States**


### USA:


👉 **Indestructible Union of Indestructible States**


---


# 🔶 4. ARTICLE 4 (VERY IMPORTANT)

- Laws under Articles 2 & 3:

    ❌ NOT Constitutional Amendment (Article 368)


    ✅ Passed by **Simple Majority**


---


# 🔶 5. CESSION OF TERRITORY (SUPREME COURT)


## 📌 Berubari Case (1960)

- Parliament **cannot cede territory under Article 3**
- Requires **Constitutional Amendment (Article 368)**

👉 Result:

- **9th Constitutional Amendment Act (1960)**

---


## 📌 1969 SC Judgement

- Boundary settlement ≠ cession
- Can be done by **Executive action**

---


## 📌 100th Constitutional Amendment Act (2015)


India–Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement:

- India gave **111 enclaves**
- Received **51 enclaves**
- Adjusted:
    - Adverse possessions
    - 6.1 km border

👉 Affected states:

- Assam
- West Bengal
- Meghalaya
- Tripura

---

# 🔶 8. FAZL ALI COMMISSION (1953)


### Members:

- Fazl Ali (Chairman)
- K.M. Panikkar
- H.N. Kunzru

---


## 📌 Recommendations:

- Language as basis (BUT not “one language–one state”)

### 4 Factors:

1. National unity
2. Linguistic & cultural homogeneity
3. Economic & administrative factors
4. Welfare of people

---


## 📌 Outcome (1956)

- States Reorganisation Act (1956)
- 7th Constitutional Amendment

👉 Result:

- 14 States
- 6 UTs
- Abolition of Part A, B, C, D

### **Part A States**

- Former **British provinces**
- Governed by **Governor + elected legislature**
- Examples: Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar

### **Part B States**

- Former **Princely States / groups of princely states**
- Governed by **Rajpramukh (ex-ruler)**
- Examples: Hyderabad, Mysore, Jammu & Kashmir

### **Part C States**

- Smaller areas / Chief Commissioner provinces
- Governed by **Chief Commissioner / Lt. Governor**
- Some had limited legislature
- Examples: Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Ajmer

### **Part D States**

- Only **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**
- Governed by **Lt. Governor (central control)**
