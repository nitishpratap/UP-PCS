
# CO-OPERATIVE SOCIETIES


_(97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011)_


## Introduction


The **97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011** granted **constitutional status and protection to co-operative societies** in India.


This amendment aimed to strengthen the **democratic functioning, autonomy, and professional management of cooperative institutions**.


### Major Constitutional Changes Introduced


The amendment made **three major changes in the Constitution**:

1. **Fundamental Right Added**
    - The **right to form co-operative societies** was made a **fundamental right**.
    - Inserted in **Article 19(1)(c)**.
2. **New Directive Principle of State Policy**
    - Added **Article 43B** in Part IV.
    - It directs the state to promote co-operative societies.
3. **New Part Added in the Constitution**
    - **Part IX-B** added titled **“The Co-operative Societies”**.
    - Covers **Articles 243ZH to 243ZT**.

---


# Article 43B (Directive Principle)


Article 43B states that:


The **State shall endeavour to promote**:

- Voluntary formation of co-operative societies
- Autonomous functioning
- Democratic control
- Professional management

This reflects the importance of cooperatives in **economic democracy and inclusive growth**.


---


# PART IX-B: CO-OPERATIVE SOCIETIES


(Articles 243ZH – 243ZT)


Part IX-B provides **constitutional provisions regarding the functioning and governance of cooperative societies**.


---


# 1. Incorporation of Co-operative Societies


**Article 243ZI**


The **State Legislature** can make laws regarding:

- Incorporation
- Regulation
- Winding up of co-operative societies

These laws must follow the following principles:

1. Voluntary formation
2. Democratic member control
3. Member economic participation
4. Autonomous functioning

---


# 2. Board of Co-operative Societies


**Article 243ZJ**


### Number of Directors


The **State Legislature determines the number of directors**.


However:

- Maximum number of directors = **21**.

### Reservation in Board


The state legislature must provide reservation for:


| Category                            | Reservation |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Scheduled Castes / Scheduled Tribes | 1 seat      |
| Women                               | 2 seats     |


This applies to cooperative societies having such members.


---


### Term of Board Members

- The **term of elected members and office bearers = 5 years** from the date of election.

---


### Co-opted Members


State laws may allow **co-option of experts** in areas such as:

- Banking
- Management
- Finance
- Other specialized fields

Rules:

- Maximum **2 co-opted members**
- These are **in addition to 21 directors**

Restrictions:


Co-opted members:

- **Cannot vote in elections**
- **Cannot become office bearers**

---


### Functional Directors


Functional directors are also members of the board.


However:

- They **are not counted within the 21 director limit**.

---


# 3. Election of Board Members


**Article 243ZK**


Important provisions:

- Elections must be conducted **before expiry of the existing board’s term**.
- Newly elected members must assume office **immediately after the old board’s term ends**.

### Election Authority


The responsibility for:

- Preparation of electoral rolls
- Conduct of elections

is vested in a **body designated by the State Legislature**.


---


# 4. Supersession and Suspension of Board


**Article 243ZL**


A cooperative board **cannot be superseded or suspended for more than 6 months**.


### Grounds for Supersession


The board can be superseded if:

1. Persistent default
2. Negligence in duties
3. Acts against interests of society or members
4. Stalemate in board constitution or functioning
5. Election authority fails to conduct elections

---


### Exception


A cooperative society **cannot be superseded if there is no**:

- Government shareholding
- Government loan
- Government financial assistance
- Government guarantee

---


### Administrator


If the board is superseded:

- An **administrator is appointed**.
- Elections must be conducted **within 6 months**.
- Management must be transferred to the **newly elected board**.

---


### Special Case: Cooperative Banks


For cooperative banks (except multi-state cooperative banks):


Maximum supersession period = **1 year**.


---


# 5. Audit of Accounts


**Article 243ZM**


The State Legislature may make provisions for:

- Maintenance of accounts
- Annual auditing

### Audit Rules

1. Audit must occur **once every financial year**.
2. Auditor must be appointed by the **General Body**.
3. Auditor must be selected from a **panel approved by State Government**.

### Time Limit


Audit must be completed **within 6 months after the financial year ends**.


### Apex Cooperative Society


Audit report of **apex cooperative societies must be placed before the State Legislature**.


---


# 6. General Body Meetings


**Article 243ZN**


State laws may require:

- **Annual General Body Meeting (AGM)** of every cooperative society.

Time limit:

- Must be held **within 6 months after the financial year ends**.

---


# 7. Rights of Members


**Article 243ZO**


Members have the **right to access**:

- Books
- Accounts
- Information of the society

State laws may also provide:

- Participation of members in management
- Cooperative education
- Training of members

---


# 8. Filing of Returns


**Article 243ZP**


Every cooperative society must submit **annual returns within 6 months of the financial year closing**.


Returns include:

1. Annual report of activities
2. Audited accounts
3. Plan for disposal of surplus
4. Amendments in by-laws
5. Date of general body meeting
6. Election details
7. Other information required by Registrar

---


# 9. Offences and Penalties


**Article 243ZQ**


State Legislature may define offences and penalties.


Offences include:

1. False returns or false information
2. Disobeying summons or orders
3. Employer not depositing employee deductions within **14 days**
4. Officer refusing to handover society records
5. Corrupt practices during elections

---


# 10. Application to Multi-State Co-operative Societies


**Article 243ZR**


For **multi-state cooperative societies**:


| Term Used         | Replaced By        |
| ----------------- | ------------------ |
| State Legislature | Parliament         |
| State Act         | Central Act        |
| State Government  | Central Government |


---


# 11. Application to Union Territories


**Article 243ZS**


The provisions of Part IX-B apply to **Union Territories**.


However:

- The **President may exempt any UT or part of it** through notification.

---


# 12. Continuance of Existing Laws


**Article 243ZT**


Existing state laws related to cooperatives that are inconsistent with this amendment:

- Continue only **until amended or repealed**
- Or **for one year from commencement**

Whichever is earlier.


---


# Definitions


### Board


The **board of directors or governing body** managing the affairs of a cooperative society.


### Office Bearer


Includes:

- President
- Vice-President
- Chairperson
- Vice-Chairperson
- Secretary
- Treasurer
- Any other elected member.

### Registrar


Registrar refers to:

- **Central Registrar** for multi-state cooperatives
- **State Registrar** for state cooperatives.

---


# Reasons for the 97th Amendment


### 1. Weakness in Cooperative Sector


Despite growth, cooperatives had problems such as:

- Elections postponed indefinitely
- Administrators controlling societies for long periods
- Lack of accountability
- Poor professional management
- Low productivity

Thus reforms were necessary.


---


### 2. State List Subject


Cooperative societies fall under:


**Entry 32 of State List (Seventh Schedule)**.


States had their own laws but performance remained **unsatisfactory**.


Hence constitutional reforms were considered necessary.


---


### 3. Need for Democratic and Professional Management


The amendment aimed to ensure:

- Democratic functioning
- Professional management
- Institutional autonomy
- Accountability to members
- Prevention of external interference.
