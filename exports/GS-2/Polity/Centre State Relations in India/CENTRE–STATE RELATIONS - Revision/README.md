# 1. FEDERAL SYSTEM IN INDIA


**Nature:** Federal system with **strong unitary bias**


**Key Features**


| Feature                   | Explanation             |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Division of powers        | Union vs States         |
| Written Constitution      | Supreme law             |
| Supremacy of Constitution | All organs bound        |
| Independent judiciary     | SC as guardian          |
| Bicameral legislature     | Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha |


⚠ **Important Difference from USA**


India has **Integrated Judicial System**


```plain text
Supreme Court
      ↓
High Courts
      ↓
Subordinate Courts
```

# PART 1 — LEGISLATIVE RELATIONS


### Articles: **245–255**


### Four Aspects


1️⃣ Territorial extent of legislation


2️⃣ Distribution of legislative subjects


3️⃣ Parliamentary legislation in state field


4️⃣ Centre control over state legislation


---


# 1. TERRITORIAL EXTENT OF LEGISLATION


## Parliament


Can legislate for:

- Whole of India
- Any part of India

**Territory of India includes**

- States
- Union Territories
- Territories acquired later

---


## State Legislature


Can legislate for:

- Whole state
- Any part of state

Normally cannot operate outside state.


### Doctrine of Territorial Nexus


State law may apply outside state if **sufficient connection exists**.


Example: Company registered in one state operating in another.


---


## Extra Territorial Legislation


Only **Parliament** can make laws affecting:

- Indian citizens abroad
- Indian property abroad

---


# Restrictions on Parliamentary Territorial Power


| Area            | Authority                                                 |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| UTs             | President regulations                                     |
| Scheduled Areas | Governor may modify laws                                  |
| Tribal Areas    | Governor (Assam), President (Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram) |


President may regulate:

- Andaman & Nicobar
- Lakshadweep
- Dadra & Nagar Haveli
- Daman & Diu

These regulations have **force of parliamentary law**.

# LIST SUPREMACY


Hierarchy


```plain text
Union List > Concurrent List > State List
```


Meaning

- Union vs State → **Union prevails**
- Union vs Concurrent → **Union prevails**
- Concurrent vs State → **Concurrent prevails**

---


# CONFLICT IN CONCURRENT LIST


Rule:


➡ Central law prevails


Exception:


If

- State law reserved for President
- President gives assent

Then


➡ State law prevails **in that state**


BUT


➡ Parliament can override later

# 3. PARLIAMENTARY LEGISLATION IN STATE FIELD


Normally Parliament **cannot legislate on State List**


Allowed in **5 situations**


---


## 1. National Interest Resolution


**Article 249**


Rajya Sabha may allow Parliament to legislate.


Conditions

- **2/3 majority**
- Valid **1 year**
- Can be renewed

Law valid **6 months after expiry**


---


## 2. During National Emergency


**Article 250**


If **Art 352 emergency**


Parliament can legislate on State List.


Law valid **6 months after emergency ends**


---


## 3. When States Request


**Article 252**


Two or more states request Parliament.


Applies to

- Requesting states
- Other states adopting later

States **cannot amend or repeal**


Only Parliament can.


Examples

- Prize Competition Act 1955
- Wildlife Protection Act 1972
- Water Pollution Act 1974
- Urban Land Ceiling Act 1976
- Transplantation of Human Organs Act 1994

---


## 4. International Agreements


**Article 253**


Parliament may legislate to implement:

- Treaties
- Conventions
- International agreements

Examples

- UN Privileges Act 1947
- Geneva Convention Act 1960
- Anti-Hijacking Act 1982

---


## 5. During President’s Rule


**Article 356**


Parliament legislates on State List for that state.


Law continues after President's Rule.


State legislature may later:

- Amend
- Repeal
- Re-enact

---


# 4. CENTRE CONTROL OVER STATE LEGISLATION


### 1. Reservation of Bills


Governor may reserve bill for **President**.


President may exercise **absolute veto**.


---


### 2. Prior Presidential Sanction


Required for certain bills


Example


Bills restricting **freedom of trade and commerce**.


---


### 3. Financial Emergency


**Article 360**


President may require states to reserve:

- Money bills
- Financial bills

---


# FEDERAL SUPREMACY


According to **Sarkaria Commission**


Union supremacy prevents:

- Legal chaos
- Conflicting laws
- Administrative confusion

# DISTRIBUTION OF EXECUTIVE POWERS


General rule:


➡ **Executive power follows legislative power**


---


## Union Executive Power


Extends to

- Union List matters
- International treaties

Applies **throughout India**


---


## State Executive Power


Extends to

- State List subjects

---


## Concurrent List


Normally


➡ Executive power with **states**


Unless Constitution assigns to Centre.


---


# OBLIGATIONS OF STATES


States must:


1️⃣ Ensure compliance with parliamentary laws


2️⃣ Not obstruct Union executive power


---


# ARTICLE 365


If state fails to follow Union directions


➡ President may conclude constitutional breakdown


➡ May lead to **President’s Rule (Art 356)**


---

# MUTUAL DELEGATION OF FUNCTIONS


| Authority  | Delegation                   |
| ---------- | ---------------------------- |
| President  | Union → State (with consent) |
| Governor   | State → Union (with consent) |
| Parliament | May impose duties on states  |


States **cannot impose duties on Centre**.


---


# COOPERATIVE MECHANISMS


### Inter-State River Disputes


Parliament may establish tribunals.


Example: **Cauvery Tribunal**


---


### Inter-State Council


**Article 263**


Established **1990**


Functions

- Investigate disputes
- Discuss policies
- Recommend coordination

Based on **Sarkaria Commission recommendation**


---


### Full Faith and Credit


Public acts and judicial decisions of:

- Union
- States

must be recognised across India.


---


### Inter-State Trade Authority


Parliament may establish authority to ensure trade freedom.


⚠ None created yet.

# TRENDS IN CENTRE–STATE RELATIONS


### Early Phase (1950–1967)

- Congress ruled Centre & states
- Few conflicts

---


### Turning Point: **1967 Elections**


Congress lost **9 states**


Result

- Rise of regional parties
- Demand for state autonomy
- Centre-state conflicts increased
