## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Acid rain** means wet or dry acidic deposition with pH below **5.6**.
2. Normal unpolluted rain is already about **pH 5.6** because dissolved CO₂ forms weak carbonic acid — it is **not** pH 7.
3. **Wet deposition** is acids in rain, snow, or fog. **Dry deposition** is acidic gases and particles settling without rain.
4. **SO₂** oxidises to **H₂SO₄** (sulphuric acid), mainly from coal TPPs, smelters, and refineries.
5. **NOx** oxidises to **HNO₃** (nitric acid), mainly from vehicles and high-temperature combustion.
6. CO₂ forms only weak H₂CO₃ and sets the **5.6 baseline**; it is **not** the main culprit of severe acid rain.
7. On the pH scale, acidic is **<7**, basic is **>7**, neutral is **7**. Each step down multiplies H⁺ by **10**.
8. Freshwater below about **pH 5** stresses fish and can release toxic **Al³⁺**.
9. Acid deposition leaches soil **Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺** and can free toxic aluminium.
10. Conifers are especially sensitive forest receptors. Marble and limestone monuments form a **gypsum crust** (CaSO₄) under sulphuric attack — the Taj story.
11. **Lichens** die in high SO₂ and act as pollution bio-indicators.
12. Prevention tools include **FGD scrubbers** (lime/limestone), catalytic converters, low-sulphur coal, CNG/LPG switches, and **BS-VI** fuel (about **10 ppm** sulphur).
13. The **Gothenburg Protocol (1999)** (गोथेनबर्ग प्रोटोकॉल) under **CLRTAP (1979)** cuts transboundary acidifying emissions.
14. Treaty trap: Gothenburg/CLRTAP = acidifying air pollution; Montreal = ozone; Kyoto (क्योटो) = climate GHGs.
15. The **Taj Trapezium Zone** is about **10,400 km²** around the Taj Mahal (ताज महल), strengthened by Supreme Court orders from **1996**.
16. **Mathura (मथुरा) refinery** is a classic SO₂ source in the Taj Trapezium narrative.
17. The **Singrauli–Sonbhadra (सोनभद्र)** coal TPP belt is a major UP–MP SO₂ source region.
18. UP NCAP (राष्ट्रीय स्वच्छ वायु कार्यक्रम) cities linked to acidifying/urban (नगरीय) air pressure include Kanpur (कानपुर), Ghaziabad, Lucknow (लखनऊ), and Noida.
19. SO₂ is denser than air — a physical property sometimes used in distractors.
20. Acid rain is **not** “rain only”; dry deposition counts too.
21. Secondary pollutants H₂SO₄ and HNO₃ form in the atmosphere from primary SO₂ and NOx.
22. FGD is flue-gas desulphurisation at power-plant chimneys — a mitigation hardware fact, not a treaty name.
23. Calling CO₂ the chief severe acid-rain driver is wrong; SO₂ and NOx are the pair.
24. Montreal Protocol (मॉन्ट्रियल प्रोटोकॉल) does **not** control acid rain. Keep ozone chemistry out of acid-rain treaty options.
25. **Acid rain** is precipitation or atmospheric deposition with **pH below 5.6**, mainly from **sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)** and **nitric acid (HNO₃)** (नाइट्रिक अम्ल).
26. **Normal unpolluted rain** has pH about **5.6**, not 7, because atmospheric **CO₂** forms weak **carbonic acid (H₂CO₃)**.
27. **Wet deposition** carries acids in rain, snow, fog, or dew.
28. **Dry deposition** means **SO₂**, **NO₂**, and sulphate/nitrate particles settle on soil, leaves, and buildings **without rainfall**.
29. **SO₂** and **NOx** are **primary pollutants**. Strong acids form in the atmosphere, so acid rain is a **secondary pollutant** phenomenon.
30. **Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)** is the main source of **sulphuric acid rain**.
31. **Nitrogen oxides (NOx)** form at **high combustion temperatures** when nitrogen in air reacts with oxygen.
32. **CO₂** dissolves as weak carbonic acid and sets the **pH 5.6 baseline**. It is **not** the main driver of severe acid rain.
33. **Coal thermal power plants** are India's largest **SO₂** point sources.
34. **Vehicles** are a major **NOx** source in cities.
35. **SO₂ is heavier than air** and tends to accumulate near ground-level emission sources.
36. **Aquatic ecosystems** fail when lake/river pH drops **below about 5.0** — fish eggs do not hatch and adult fish die.
37. **Aluminium (Al³⁺)** leached from acidified soil is **toxic to fish gills** and plant roots.
38. **Soil acidification** leaches essential **calcium (Ca²⁺)** and **magnesium (Mg²⁺)** and releases toxic **Al³⁺**.
39. **Coniferous forests** (pine, spruce, fir) are **most sensitive** to acid rain and acid fog.
40. **Crops** suffer from direct leaf damage and nutrient loss in soil.
41. **Marble and limestone (CaCO₃)** react with sulphuric acid to form **gypsum (CaSO₄)**, causing yellow crust and pitting.
42. **Metal structures** rust faster under acid deposition.
43. **SO₂ and NOx** cause **respiratory illness** — asthma, bronchitis, and lung irritation from acid aerosols and PM₂.₅.
44. **Lichens** are **bio-indicators of clean air**. They **die in high SO₂** and do **not** thrive in polluted areas.
45. **Flue Gas Desulphurisation (FGD) scrubbers** pass chimney gas through **lime or limestone slurry** to absorb **SO₂** and can remove **90%+** of it.
46. **Coal washing** and **low-sulphur coal** cut sulphur before combustion.
47. **Catalytic converters** in vehicles convert **NO to N₂** and reduce **NOx** that forms nitric acid.
48. **BS-VI norms (2020)** brought **10 ppm sulphur** in petrol and diesel, cutting both SO₂ and NOx precursors.
49. **CNG, LPG, and natural gas** produce **negligible SO₂** and lower NOx than coal.
50. **CPCB (केंद्रीय प्रदूषण नियंत्रण बोर्ड) emission standards** under the Air Act cap SO₂ and NOx from TPPs, cement, steel, and refineries.

51. The main strong acid pathway is **SO₂ → H₂SO₄**. **NOx → HNO₃** is the other major path.
52. Reversed statements that call normal rain alkaline or acid rain alkaline are both false.
53. Acid deposition harms conifers and weathers marble and limestone. The Taj marble → **gypsum** story is the classic Indian fact.
54. The **Gothenburg Protocol (1999)** under **CLRTAP (1979)** targets acidifying air pollutants. **Montreal** targets ozone ODS — different problem.
55. The **Singrauli** thermal belt and industrial NCR/Kanpur (कानपुर) airsheds are Indian acidifying-emission hotspots.
56. SO₂ is denser than air and can travel long distances before deposition.
57. Coal power and some industries are major SO₂ sources. Vehicles and combustion add NOx.
58. Liming lakes is an emergency response, not a substitute for cutting emissions at source.
59. Do not blame acid rain mainly on CO₂ just because normal rain is weakly acidic.
60. Dry deposition can damage materials even on rainless days.
61. Acid mist and acid fog are wet forms even when they are not classic rainfall.
62. Cultural-heritage damage and forest dieback are both valid effect facts alongside aquatic harm.
63. India’s focus pairs chemistry (SO₂/NOx) with Taj protection geography.
64. **Conifers** (pine, spruce) are especially sensitive to acid fog and soil change.
65. **Taj Trapezium Zone (~10,400 km²)** — Supreme Court-driven controls (1996 onward) forced gas switch/relocation of polluting units around Agra.
