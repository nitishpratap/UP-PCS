## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. A **carbon footprint** totals greenhouse gases in **tCO₂e**. Scope **1** is direct, Scope **2** is purchased energy, Scope **3** is the wider value chain.
2. India’s **per capita** carbon footprint is about **2 tCO₂e** — low versus global and USA averages — even though absolute (निरपेक्ष) emissions are high.
3. An **ecological footprint** (पारिस्थितिक पदचिह्न) measures resource demand in **global hectares (gha)**. It is **not** the same unit as tCO₂e. The **Social Cost of Carbon** is the monetary long-term damage of **one tonne of CO₂**.
4. **Net zero** balances remaining emissions with removals. India’s year fact is **2070**. It is not gross zero and not automatically **2050**.
5. NDC-style facts include about **45%** intensity cut, about **50%** non-fossil capacity, and a large forest sink goal.
6. The **Emissions Gap Report** (उत्सर्जन अंतराल रिपोर्ट) is by **UNEP** (संयुक्त राष्ट्र पर्यावरण कार्यक्रम).
7. A **green economy** (UNEP framing) is low-carbon, resource-efficient, and socially inclusive.
8. A **blue economy** is sustainable ocean use linked to **SDG 14**. **MISHTI** mangrove restoration is from the **2023** Budget, **not 2019**.
9. A **circular economy** closes material loops through 3R/9R and EPR. It sits inside the broader green-economy idea and links to **SDG 12**.
10. **Green hydrogen** uses electrolysis powered by renewables. India’s mission (January **2023**) targets about **5 MMT** by **2030**.
11. **Grey hydrogen** reforms natural gas. **Blue hydrogen** adds CCS (सुरक्षा कैबिनेट समिति) to grey pathways. Do not call grey “green.”
12. **Microplastics** are plastic pieces smaller than **5 mm**. They are primary (made small) or secondary (fragmented) and are not fast biodegradable.
13. The **SUP ban** push of **2022** targets single-use plastics that feed microplastic pathways.
14. **Climate refugees** are mostly **internally** displaced. They are **not** a separate legal category under the **1951** Refugee Convention.
15. **Nature-based Solutions** protect, manage, or restore ecosystems (IUCN (आईयूसीएन) framing). They are not geoengineering (भू-इंजीनियरिंग) (cirrus thinning / sulphate aerosols are geoengineering ideas for cooling, not NbS).
16. **LiFE** was an idea at **COP-26 (2021)** and launched in **June 2022** under MoEFCC (पर्यावरण, वन और जलवायु परिवर्तन मंत्रालय) with a long action list. **COP-25 Madrid** is the false trap.
17. Carbon footprint ≠ ecological footprint. Units are **tCO₂e** versus **gha**.
18. Green economy ≠ circular economy as identical synonyms. Circular is the material-loop design inside a wider green agenda.
19. Net zero allows balancing removals. Gross zero means nearly eliminating emissions without relying on offsets.
20. MISHTI is a mangrove initiative. Pairing it with 2019 is wrong.
21. Microplastic size fact is **<5 mm**, not 5 cm.
22. NbS examples include mangroves, wetlands, and urban (नगरीय) forests — ecosystem (पारिस्थितिकी तंत्र) actions, not aerosol injection.
23. LiFE is lifestyle and demand-side behaviour change, not a substitute for the entire NDC package.
24. Blue economy is oceans and coasts. Green hydrogen is energy. Do not merge the two labels.
25. **Scope 1** covers **direct** emissions from owned sources (boilers, fleet fuel).
26. **Scope 2** covers **purchased electricity, heat, or steam**.
27. **Scope 3** covers other indirect emissions (supply chain, product use, travel, waste) — often the largest share and hardest to measure.
28. **Biocapacity** is nature’s regenerative capacity; if footprint exceeds biocapacity, there is an **ecological deficit**.
29. **Earth Overshoot Day** marks when annual demand exceeds that year’s regeneration.
30. **Net zero** means anthropogenic emissions are **balanced by anthropogenic removals** over a period — residual emissions may remain if matched by sinks or removals.
31. **Gross zero** means eliminating virtually all emissions — harder than net zero.
32. **UNEP Emissions Gap Report** measures the gap between pledges and 1.5°C/2°C pathways.
33. **Green finance** (हरित वित्त) (sovereign green bonds, SEBI green-bond rules) funds this pathway — detailed market tools sit with Topic 24.
34. **Blue economy** is sustainable use of ocean and coastal resources for growth and jobs while protecting marine ecosystems.
35. **SDG 14** = **Life Below Water**.
36. **Blue carbon** is carbon stored in mangroves, seagrass, and salt (लवणाध्यक्ष) marshes.
37. **MISHTI** (Mangrove Initiative for Shoreline Habitats and Tangible Income) was announced in **Union Budget 2023–24** to restore mangroves for shoreline protection and income.
38. **Sagarmala** is port-led coastal development; **Deep Ocean Mission** covers deep-sea exploration (Samudrayaan).
39. **Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016** (amended 2022) and related EPR rules make producers responsible for end-of-life collection.
40. **Green hydrogen** is H₂ from **water electrolysis** powered by **renewable electricity**.
41. **Grey hydrogen** from natural-gas reforming dominates today’s supply and is **not** climate-friendly.
42. **Blue hydrogen** is grey production plus **CCS** — a debated transition label.
43. **National Green Hydrogen Mission (January 2023)**, coordinated under **MNRE**, targets about **5 MMT/year by 2030** for refining, ammonia/fertiliser, steel, and heavy transport.
44. **Microplastics** are plastic particles **smaller than 5 mm**.
45. **Primary** types are manufactured small (microbeads, nurdles).
46. **Secondary** types form when larger plastics fragment (bags, nets, tyre wear) — most environmental mass is secondary.
47. **Climate refugees / climate migrants** are people forced to move by climate impacts — cyclones, floods, drought (सूखा), sea-level rise, desertification (मरुस्थलीकरण).
48. **Nature-based Solutions** protect, sustainably manage, or restore ecosystems to address societal challenges (climate, disasters, food, water) while aiding biodiversity and well-being.
49. **MISHTI mangrove restoration** is a flagship Indian NbS example (Budget **2023–24**).
50. **LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment)** nudges individual and community behaviour toward mindful consumption.

## ⏱️ Timeline / Chronology

### Chronological Sequence of Environmental Legislation & Governance in India

```
1972 ──► Wildlife (Protection) Act (WPA) ➔ Apex PA statute, hunting prohibition, statutory schedules
1974 ──► Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act ➔ Established Central & State Pollution Control Boards (CPCB/SPCB)
1980 ──► Forest (Conservation) Act (FCA) ➔ Mandatory prior central approval for diversion of forest land
1981 ──► Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act ➔ Air pollution control; noise included via 1987 amendment
1986 ──► Environment (Protection) Act (EPA) ➔ Post-Bhopal umbrella statute under Art 253; EIA, CRZ, ESZ notifications
1991 ──► Public Liability Insurance Act ➔ Mandatory insurance for handling hazardous substances
2002 ──► Biological Diversity Act (BDA) ➔ 3-tier decentralized framework: NBA (Chennai) -> SBB -> BMC
2006 ──► Scheduled Tribes & Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (FRA) Act ➔ Individual & community forest rights
2010 ──► National Green Tribunal (NGT) Act ➔ Specialized judicial body for environmental disputes under Article 21
```

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

- **4 Biodiversity Hotspots of India**: **H-I-W-S** (Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Sundaland)
- **6 Kyoto Protocol Greenhouse Gases**: **C-M-N-H-P-S** (Carbon dioxide, Methane, Nitrous oxide, Hydrofluorocarbons, Perfluorocarbons, Sulphur hexafluoride; NF3 added later)
- **Trophic Energy Transfer Rule**: **Lindeman's 10% Law (1942)** — Only ~10% of energy is transferred to the next higher trophic level; 90% lost as metabolic heat.
- **WPA Schedules (Post-2022 Amendment)**:
  - **Schedule I**: Animal species enjoying highest level of protection (Blackbuck, Lion-tailed Macaque, Great Indian Bustard)
  - **Schedule II**: Animal species with lesser protection
  - **Schedule III**: Protected Plant species
  - **Schedule IV**: Specimens listed under CITES Appendices
- **Ozone Units**: Measured in **Dobson Units (DU)** using spectrophotometers (1 DU = 0.01 mm thickness at STP; normal ozone layer is ~300 DU; <220 DU is classified as an ozone hole).
- **Montreux Record**: Ramsar sites where ecological character has changed or is changing (In India: **Keoladeo National Park** and **Loktak Lake**; Chilika Lake was removed post-restoration).
