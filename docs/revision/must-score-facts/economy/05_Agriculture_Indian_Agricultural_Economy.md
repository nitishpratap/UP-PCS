1. Agriculture and allied sectors contribute approximately **17%–18% of India's Gross Value Added (GVA)** at current prices, but engage **~45%–47% of the nation's total workforce**.
2. According to the **Agricultural Census of India** (conducted quinquennially since 1970–71), agricultural holdings are classified into 5 categories: **Marginal (< 1.00 ha)**, **Small (1.00–2.00 ha)**, **Semi-Medium (2.00–4.00 ha)**, **Medium (4.00–10.00 ha)**, and **Large (≥ 10.00 ha)**.
3. **Small and Marginal Farmers (SMFs, holding < 2.0 ha)** account for **~86.2% of all operational land holdings** in India, but operate only **~47.3% of the total operated area**.
4. **Marginal holdings (< 1.0 ha)** alone constitute **68.5%** of all agricultural land holdings in India, with an average operational size of just **0.38 hectare**.
5. The all-India average size of operational land holdings has consistently declined from **2.28 hectares in 1970–71** to **1.08 hectares in 2015–16** due to generational land subdivision and population pressure.
6. The state having the **highest average size of operational land holdings** in India is **Nagaland** (~5.06 ha) followed by **Punjab** (~3.62 ha) and **Rajasthan** (~2.73 ha); **Kerala** has among the lowest (~0.18 ha).
7. India is divided into **15 broad Agro-Climatic Zones** by the Planning Commission (1988) based on physiography, climate, soil, and cropping patterns; ICAR under NARP classifies India into **127 Agro-Climatic Zones** (and Uttar Pradesh into **9 zones**).
8. **Kharif crops (खरीफ)** are sown with the onset of the South-West Monsoon in **June–July** and harvested in **September–October**; major crops include **Rice, Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut, Soybean, Tur/Arhar, Urad, and Moong**.
9. **Rabi crops (रबी)** are sown in **October–November** (winter onset) and harvested in **March–April** (spring); major crops include **Wheat, Barley, Gram (चना), Mustard (सरसों), Linseed (अलसी), Peas (मटर), and Potato**.
10. **Zaid crops (जायद)** are short-duration summer crops grown from **March to June** between Rabi harvest and Kharif sowing; predominantly include cucurbitaceous crops: **Watermelon (तरबूज), Muskmelon (खरबूजा), Cucumber (खीरा), Bitter gourd (करेला), Moong, and leafy vegetables/fodder**.
11. India is the **world's largest producer** of **Milk, Pulses, Jute, and Spices**; and the **second-largest producer** of **Rice, Wheat, Sugarcane, Groundnut, Vegetables, Fruits, and Cotton** (after China).
12. India is the **largest exporter of Rice** in the world (accounting for ~40% of global rice trade), but is the **largest importer of Pulses and Vegetable Edible Oils**.
13. **Rice (चावल)** is the **most cultivated crop in India**, occupying the largest gross cropped area (~47 million hectares); **West Bengal** is the largest producer of Rice, while **Punjab** has the highest per-hectare productivity.
14. **Wheat (गेहूं)** is the second most important cereal crop in India, occupying ~31 million hectares; **Uttar Pradesh** is the largest producer of Wheat in terms of total absolute output, while **Punjab** records the highest yield/productivity per hectare.
15. **Shree Anna (श्री अन्न / Millets)** was championed by India, leading the UN to declare **2023 as the International Year of Millets**; **Rajasthan** is the largest producer of **Bajra** (Pearl Millet), while **Karnataka/Maharashtra** lead in **Jowar** (Sorghum).
16. Among pulses, **Gram (चना)** accounts for the largest share (~45%–50% of total pulse production in India); **Madhya Pradesh** is the largest pulse-producing state in India.
17. In oilseeds, **Soybean** and **Groundnut** lead production; **Madhya Pradesh & Maharashtra** lead in Soybean, **Gujarat** leads in Groundnut, and **Rajasthan** leads in Rapeseed & Mustard.
18. **Sugarcane (गन्ना)** is a perennial tropical cash crop; **Uttar Pradesh** is the largest producer of Sugarcane and Sugar in India, but Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu record higher sucrose content and recovery rates due to maritime tropical climate.
19. **Cotton (कपास - White Gold)** is predominantly grown in the black regur soil of the Deccan plateau; **Gujarat** and **Maharashtra** are the top producing states.
20. In Net Irrigated Area across India, the source-wise share is: **Tube-wells & Wells (~62%–64%)**, followed by **Canals (~23%–25%)**, and **Tanks (~3%–4%)**.
21. **Uttar Pradesh** possesses the largest canal network and largest tube-well irrigated area in absolute terms in India; **Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh** lead in **Tank irrigation**.
22. The **Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)** was launched in **2015** with the motto *"Har Khet Ko Pani"* and *"Per Drop More Crop"* (focused on micro-irrigation: drip and sprinkler).
23. The ideal, scientifically balanced consumption ratio of **NPK fertilizers** is **4 : 2 : 1** ($N:P:K$); in high-subsidy states like Punjab and Haryana, the actual consumption ratio has severely skewed to over **8 : 3 : 1**.
24. **Urea** is the ONLY fertilizer whose **Maximum Retail Price (MRP) is statutorily fixed** by the Central Government; the difference between the normative delivered cost and the controlled MRP is paid as subsidy directly to manufacturers.
25. **Neem Coated Urea (NCU)** was made **100% mandatory** for all indigenous and imported agricultural urea in **2015**; it prevents illegal diversion to chemical industries and improves Nitrogen Use Efficiency (NUE) by slowing degradation.
26. The **Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS)** scheme was introduced on **1 April 2010** for non-urea P&K fertilizers (**DAP, MOP, Complex fertilizers**), where government fixes an annual per-kg subsidy based on nutrient content ($N, P, K, S$), while MRPs are decontrolled.
27. India is **100% import-dependent** for **Potassic fertilizers (Muriate of Potash - MOP)** as India has no commercially exploitable potash reserves.
28. The world's first commercial **Nano Liquid Urea** and **Nano DAP** plants were established by **IFFCO** at **Kalol, Gujarat**.
29. **PM-PRANAM** (PM Programme for Restoration, Awareness, Nourishment and Amelioration of Mother Earth) was announced in Union Budget 2023–24 to incentivise states to promote alternative fertilizers and reduce chemical fertilizer usage.
30. **Sikkim** was formally declared India's and the world's **First 100% Organic State** in **January 2016**; **Lakshadweep** is the first 100% Organic Union Territory (2020).
31. **Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF)** was pioneered in India by Padma Shri **Subhash Palekar**; it relies on 4 core pillars: **Jeevamrutha** (microbial culture), **Bijamrutha** (seed treatment), **Acchadana** (mulching), and **Whapasa** (soil moisture aeration).
32. In institutional agricultural credit flow, **Commercial Banks** account for the overwhelming majority (**~75%–78%**), followed by **Regional Rural Banks (~12%–14%)** and **Cooperative Banks (~10%–12%)**.
33. Under the **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** norms of the RBI, domestic scheduled commercial banks and foreign banks with ≥20 branches must allocate **18% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)** or Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure (CEOBE) to the **Agricultural sector** (with **10% specifically sub-targeted to Small and Marginal Farmers**).
34. The **National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD)** was established on **12 July 1982** under the NABARD Act, 1981, upon the recommendations of the **B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD)**, taking over the functions of the ACD and RPCC of RBI and the ARDC.
35. The **Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)** was instituted within NABARD in **1995–96** to finance rural infrastructure projects of State Governments using the PSL shortfall of commercial banks.
36. The **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)** scheme was formulated by NABARD in **August 1998** on the recommendations of the **R.V. Gupta Committee** to provide timely, flexible short-term credit to farmers for crop production, post-harvest expenses, and working capital.
37. In 2018–19, the Government of India extended the **Kisan Credit Card (KCC) facility to Fisheries and Animal Husbandry farmers** to meet their working capital requirements.
38. The **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)** was launched in **February 2016** (effective Kharif 2016), replacing NAIS and MNAIS; it charges a uniform low premium of **2.0% for Kharif crops**, **1.5% for Rabi crops**, and **5.0% for Annual Commercial & Horticultural crops**, with the balance actuarial premium subsidized 50:50 by the Centre and States.
39. The **Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)** was established in **January 1965** (initially as the *Agricultural Prices Commission* until renamed in 1985); it functions as an advisory body under the Ministry of Agriculture.
40. The CACP recommends Minimum Support Prices (MSP) for **22 mandated agricultural crops** (14 Kharif, 6 Rabi, 2 commercial: Jute and Copra) and Fair & Remunerative Price (FRP) for **Sugarcane**; the final decision is taken by the **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)** chaired by the Prime Minister.
41. The **National Commission on Farmers (NCF)**, set up in 2004 under **Prof. M.S. Swaminathan**, recommended in its 5th report (2006) that the MSP should be fixed at least **50% above the comprehensive cost of production (Cost C2 + 50%)**.
42. The Central Government officially calculates the 1.5x MSP benchmark against the **Cost A2 + FL** formula (Paid-out costs + imputed family labor value), whereas farmer unions demand MSP based on the higher **Cost C2** (which also includes imputed land rent and interest on owned capital assets).
43. **Electronic National Agriculture Market (e-NAM)** was launched on **14 April 2016** as a pan-India electronic trading portal networking existing APMC mandis to create a unified national market for agricultural commodities; it is managed by the **Small Farmers' Agri-Business Consortium (SFAC)**.
44. The Central Sector Scheme for **Formation and Promotion of 10,000 Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs)** was launched in **2020** with a total budgetary outlay of ₹6,865 crore to achieve economies of scale for small and marginal farmers.
45. Under the **Model APMC Act 2003** and the **APLM Act 2017**, states are encouraged to allow private wholesale markets, direct purchase from farmers, establishment of e-trading platforms, and exemption of fruits and vegetables from mandi fees.
46. The **APEDA** (Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) was established by an Act of Parliament in **December 1985** under the **Ministry of Commerce and Industry** to promote export of agricultural and processed food products.
47. **MPEDA** (Marine Products Export Development Authority) was constituted in **1972** under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry to promote marine and seafood exports.
48. **Operation Flood (ऑपरेशन फ्लड)** was launched in **1970** by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under **Dr. Verghese Kurien** (Father of White Revolution in India), transforming India from a milk-deficient nation into the world's largest milk producer through the Anand cooperative pattern (AMUL).
49. In the **WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA)**, domestic agricultural subsidies are categorized into three color-coded boxes: **Green Box** (non-trade distorting, allowed without limit, e.g. R&D, pest control, environment), **Blue Box** (direct payments under production-limiting programs, allowed), and **Amber Box** (trade-distorting market price support and input subsidies, subject to reduction commitments).
50. Under WTO Amber Box rules, developing countries like India are entitled to a **de-minimis limit of 10%** of the total value of agricultural production; the **Peace Clause** agreed at the **Bali Ministerial Conference (2013)** protects India's food procurement programs under NFSA against legal challenges by developed nations even if the 10% ceiling is breached.
