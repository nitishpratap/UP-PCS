## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Borrowed Features (UK):** Parliamentary system, Rule of Law (Art. 14), Single Citizenship, Bicameralism, Cabinet System, Prerogative Writs, Office of Speaker, Legislative Procedure.
2. **Borrowed Features (USA):** Fundamental Rights (Part III), Judicial Review (Art. 13), Independence of Judiciary, Impeachment of President (Art. 61), Removal of SC/HC Judges, Post of Vice-President, Preamble phrase ("We the People").
3. **Borrowed Features (Ireland):** Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP, Part IV), Nomination of 12 members to Rajya Sabha, Method of Presidential Election (PR-STV).
4. **Borrowed Features (Canada):** Federation with a Strong Centre, Residuary Powers vested in Centre (Art. 248), Appointment of State Governors by Centre, Advisory Jurisdiction of Supreme Court (Art. 143).
5. **Borrowed Features (Australia):** Concurrent List (7th Schedule), Freedom of Trade, Commerce and Intercourse (Part XIII), Joint Sitting of both Houses of Parliament (Art. 108).
6. **Borrowed Features (USSR/Russia):** Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Art. 51A via 42nd Amd 1976), Ideals of Justice (Social, Economic, Political) in Preamble, Five-Year Plans.
7. **Borrowed Features (Weimar Germany):** Suspension of Fundamental Rights during National Emergency (Art. 359). *(Note: Emergency machinery itself is from GOI Act 1935; suspension of FRs is from Weimar!)*
8. **Borrowed Features (South Africa):** Procedure for Amendment of the Constitution (Art. 368), Election of Members of Rajya Sabha by elected MLAs.
9. **Borrowed Features (Japan & France):** Japan: Procedure Established by Law (Art. 21); France: Ideals of Liberty, Equality, Fraternity in Preamble and Republican character.
10. **Parts Count:** Originally 22 Parts (1950); now **25 Parts** (Parts IVA, IXA, IXB, and XIVA added; Part VII repealed by 7th Amd 1956).
11. **Vital Parts:** Part III (Fundamental Rights, Arts. 12–35), Part IV (DPSP, Arts. 36–51), Part IVA (Duties, Art. 51A), Part IX (Panchayats, 243–243O), Part IXA (Municipalities, 243P–243ZG), Part IXB (Cooperatives, 243ZH–243ZT), Part XI (Union-State relations, 245–263), Part XIVA (Tribunals, 323A–323B), Part XV (Elections, 324–329A), Part XVIII (Emergency, 352–360), Part XX (Amendment, Art. 368).
12. **Schedules (TEARS OF OLD PM):** 1st (Territories), 2nd (Emoluments — President, Governors, Judges, CAG; *PM/Ministers NOT included*), 3rd (Oaths — Ministers, MPs, Judges; *President/VP/Governor oaths are under Arts. 60, 69, 159, NOT 3rd Sch*), 4th (Rajya Sabha seats: UP highest = 31), 5th (Scheduled Areas in 10 states), 6th (Tribal Areas in AMTM: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram; *Manipur is NOT included*).
13. **Schedules 7 to 12:** 7th (Union: 100, State: 61, Concurrent: 52), 8th (22 Languages: Sindhi added by 21st Amd; Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali by 71st Amd; Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali by 92nd Amd; *English and Bhojpuri are NOT in 8th Sch*), 9th (Validation of Acts, added by 1st Amd 1951; Coelho Case 2007: judicial review post 24 April 1973), 10th (Anti-defection, added by 52nd Amd 1985; 91st Amd deleted 1/3rd split), 11th (Panchayats: 29 functional items, 73rd Amd), 12th (Municipalities: 18 functional items, 74th Amd).
14. **Warrant of Precedence (1979 Notification):** Rank 1: President → Rank 2: Vice-President → Rank 3: Prime Minister → Rank 4: Governors (within respective states) → Rank 5: Former Presidents → Rank 6: Chief Justice of India (CJI) and Speaker of Lok Sabha (*Equal Rank!*) → Rank 7: Union Cabinet Ministers, Chief Ministers (within states), Leaders of Opposition (LS & RS) → Rank 7A: Bharat Ratna holders → Rank 9: Supreme Court Judges → Rank 9A: CEC, CAG, UPSC Chairman → Rank 11: Attorney General for India, Cabinet Secretary.
15. **Parliamentary Terms & Hours:** Question Hour: 11:00 AM–12:00 PM (Starred = oral answer + supplementary Qs allowed; Unstarred = written answer, no supplementary Qs); Zero Hour: 12:00 PM onwards (*Indian innovation since 1962, not mentioned in Rules of Procedure*).
16. **Motions in Parliament:** Adjournment Motion: Lok Sabha only, needs 50 members' support, debates urgent public matter ≥ 2.5 hours; No-Confidence Motion: Lok Sabha only, needs 50 members' support, brings down government if passed; Censure Motion: Lok Sabha only, against specific policies; Cut Motions: Disapproval of Policy (reduced to Re 1), Economy Cut (lump sum reduction), Token Cut (reduced by Rs 100).
17. **Quorum:** Minimum 1/10th of total membership of the House (55 in Lok Sabha, 25 in Rajya Sabha) under Article 100(3).
18. **Parliamentary Financial Committees:** Estimates Committee: 30 members (*all Lok Sabha only, largest committee, John Mathai 1950*); Public Accounts Committee (PAC): 22 members (15 LS + 7 RS, set up 1921, Chairman from Opposition by convention since 1967, "friend, philosopher, guide" of CAG); Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU): 22 members (15 LS + 7 RS, Krishna Menon 1964).
19. **Writs (Art. 32 SC vs Art. 226 HC):** Article 32 is a Fundamental Right itself (SC only for FRs); Article 226 is wider (HC for FRs + ordinary legal rights).
20. **Writs Scope:** Habeas Corpus ('To have the body of', against public & private entities for unlawful detention); Mandamus ('We Command', against public officials for non-performance of duty, *cannot be issued against President or Governors*); Prohibition ('To Forbid', preventive against lower courts/tribunals); Certiorari ('To be Certified', preventive and curative against judicial/quasi-judicial/administrative orders); Quo-Warranto ('By what authority', challenges illegal usurpation of substantive public office).
21. **Article 12 & 13:** Art. 12 defines "State" (Union/State govts, Parliament/Legislatures, local bodies, statutory authorities like LIC, ONGC); Art. 13 declares laws inconsistent with FRs void (*Doctrines of Judicial Review, Severability, and Eclipse*).
22. **Equality Articles (14–18):** Art. 14 (Equality before law — UK negative; Equal protection of laws — USA positive); Art. 15 (No discrimination ONLY on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth; 15(4) SC/ST/OBC quota; 15(6) 10% EWS quota); Art. 16 (Equal opportunity in public employment; 16(4) reservation; 16(6) EWS quota); Art. 17 (Abolition of Untouchability — absolute right, Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955); Art. 18 (Abolition of titles; Bharat Ratna/Padma awards are decorations, not titles — *Balaji Raghavan 1996*).
23. **Article 19 (Six Freedoms):** 19(1)(a) Speech & expression (includes press freedom); 19(1)(b) Peaceful assembly without arms; 19(1)(c) Form associations/unions/cooperatives; 19(1)(d) Move freely throughout India; 19(1)(e) Reside & settle in any part; 19(1)(g) Practice any trade/profession. *(Note: 19(1)(f) Right to property was repealed by 44th Amd 1978).*
24. **Article 20 (Three Protections):** 20(1) No ex-post-facto criminal law; 20(2) No double jeopardy; 20(3) No self-incrimination.
25. **Article 21 & 21A:** Art. 21 (Protection of Life & Personal Liberty; Maneka Gandhi 1978 introduced "Due Process of Law"; includes Right to Privacy — *Puttaswamy 2017*); Art. 21A (Right to Education for ages 6–14 inserted by 86th Amd 2002; RTE Act in force 1 April 2010).
26. **Non-Suspendable Rights:** Under Article 359 (44th Amd 1978), Articles 20 and 21 **can never be suspended** even during National Emergency!
27. **Key DPSPs:** Art. 39A (Free Legal Aid, added by 42nd Amd 1976 / NALSA); Art. 40 (Village Panchayats); Art. 44 (Uniform Civil Code — *Uttarakhand first post-independence state to pass UCC in 2024*); Art. 48A (Environment & Wildlife protection, 42nd Amd); Art. 50 (Separation of Judiciary from Executive).
28. **Pardoning Powers (Art. 72 vs Art. 161):** President (Art. 72) can pardon Death Sentences and Court Martial (military) sentences. Governor (Art. 161) cannot pardon death sentences or court-martial sentences.
29. **Ordinance Powers (Art. 123 vs Art. 213):** President (Art. 123) and Governor (Art. 213) can promulgate ordinances only when legislature is in recess; maximum validity: 6 weeks from reassembly (absolute max: 6 months + 6 weeks).
30. **Joint Sitting (Art. 108):** Summoned by President, presided over by Lok Sabha Speaker (simple majority of total members present and voting). *No joint sitting provision exists in State Legislatures!*
31. **Money Bill (Art. 110 vs Art. 199):** Speaker's decision is final. Introduced only in Lower House with prior recommendation of President/Governor. Upper House has only **14 days** to make recommendations.
32. **Constitutional vs Statutory Bodies:** Constitutional: ECI (Art. 324), UPSC/SPSC (Arts. 315–323), Finance Commission (Art. 280), CAG (Art. 148), AG (Art. 76), AdvG (Art. 165), NCSC (Art. 338), NCST (Art. 338A), NCBC (Art. 338B via 102nd Amd), GST Council (Art. 279A), Inter-State Council (Art. 263); Statutory: CVC (2003 Act), NHRC (1993 Act), CIC (2005 Act), Lokpal (2013 Act), NGT (2010 Act); Executive Body: NITI Aayog (Cabinet Resolution 1 Jan 2015).
33. **State PSC Removal Trap:** SPSC Chairman/members are appointed by the **Governor**, but **can be removed ONLY by the President** on grounds of misbehaviour after Supreme Court inquiry under Article 317.
34. **Major Amendments (42nd vs 44th):** 42nd Amd 1976 ('Mini-Constitution'): Added 'Socialist, Secular, Integrity' to Preamble, Part IVA (Duties), Part XIVA (Tribunals), shifted 5 subjects (Education, Forests, Weights/Measures, Wildlife, Administration of Justice) to Concurrent List; 44th Amd 1978: Replaced 'Internal Disturbance' with 'Armed Rebellion' in Art. 352, deleted Right to Property from Part III to Art. 300A legal right, barred suspension of Arts. 20 & 21.
35. **Other High-Yield Amendments:** 52nd (1985, Anti-Defection 10th Schedule); 61st (1988, voting age lowered 21 → 18); 73rd & 74th (1992, Panchayats 11th Sch & Municipalities 12th Sch); 86th (2002, Art. 21A RTE & 11th Duty); 91st (2003, CoM max 15% of Lower House, deleted 1/3rd split); 101st (2016, GST in force 1 July 2017); 102nd (2018, Constitutional status to NCBC Art. 338B); 103rd (2019, 10% EWS quota Arts. 15(6) & 16(6)); 104th (2020, SC/ST quota extended to 2030, Anglo-Indian nomination discontinued); 106th (2023, 33% women quota in LS and Assemblies).
36. **Panchayati Raj Committees Chronology:** Balwant Rai Mehta (1957, 3-tier: Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad; Nagaur, Rajasthan first on 2 Oct 1959) → K. Santhanam (1963, PRI finance) → Ashok Mehta (1977, 2-tier: Mandal Panchayat & Zila Parishad) → G.V.K. Rao (1985, "Grass without roots", DDC) → L.M. Singhvi (1986, first recommended Constitutional Status) → P.K. Thungon (1988, constitutional status, 5-yr term) → V.N. Gadgil (1988, 73rd Amd draft).
37. **Landmark SC Judgments:** Shankari Prasad (1951, Parliament can amend FRs) → Golaknath (1967, FRs transcendental/unamendable) → Kesavananda Bharati (24 April 1973, 13 judges, 7:6 verdict, Basic Structure Doctrine) → Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975, Free & Fair elections in Basic Structure) → Maneka Gandhi (1978, Art. 21 Due Process, Golden Triangle Arts. 14, 19, 21) → Minerva Mills (1980, balance between FR and DPSP) → S.R. Coelho (2007, 9th Schedule post-1973 subject to judicial review) → Puttaswamy (2017, Right to Privacy in Art. 21) → Navtej Johar (2018, Section 377 IPC decriminalized) → Janhit Abhiyan (2022, 103rd Amd 10% EWS quota upheld).
38. **State Reorganisation Timeline:** 1953 (Andhra State first linguistic state, Kurnool capital) → 1956 (Fazl Ali SRC: 14 States + 6 UTs) → 1960 (Bombay split into Maharashtra & Gujarat - 15th state) → 1963 (Nagaland - 16th state) → 1966 (Haryana - 17th state, Shah Commission) → 1971 (Himachal Pradesh - 18th state) → 1972 (Manipur 19th, Tripura 20th, Meghalaya 21st) → 1975 (Sikkim 36th Amd - 22nd state) → 1987 (Mizoram 23rd, Arunachal 24th, Goa 25th state) → 2000 (Chhattisgarh 1 Nov, Uttarakhand 9 Nov, Jharkhand 15 Nov) → 2014 (Telangana 2 June - 29th state) → 2019 (J&K bifurcated into UTs of J&K and Ladakh on 31 Oct; 28 States & 8 UTs) → 2020 (Dadra & Nagar Haveli merged with Daman & Diu).
39. **UP Legislature Fast Facts:** Vidhan Sabha (403 seats: 84 SC, 2 ST); Vidhan Parishad (100 seats, bicameral); Lok Sabha (80 seats: 17 SC, 0 ST); Rajya Sabha (31 seats — highest in India); Allahabad High Court established 1866 at Agra, shifted to Allahabad 1869 (Lucknow Bench created 1948).
40. **Drafting Committee (29 August 1947):** 7 members chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer, Dr. K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter), T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan). Constitutional Adviser: Sir B.N. Rau; Chief Draftsman: S.N. 41. **Center-State Commissions:** ARC-I (1966, Morarji Desai/Hanumanthaiah, Inter-State Council); Rajamannar Committee (1969, Tamil Nadu DMK, urged abolition of IAS/IPS and repeal of Art. 356); Sarkaria Commission (1983–88, Art. 356 as last resort, Inter-State Council set up 1990 under Art. 263); Punchhi Commission (2007–10, localized emergency Art. 355, 5-yr fixed tenure for Governor).
42. **Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII):** Art. 352 (National Emergency: War, External Aggression, Armed Rebellion; approved within 1 month by special majority); Art. 356 (President's Rule: failure of constitutional machinery; approved within 2 months by simple majority; max 3 years with conditions); Art. 360 (Financial Emergency: never invoked).

**Q1.**
The concept of 'Joint Sitting of both Houses of Parliament' and 'Concurrent List' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:

A. Canada
B. Australia
C. United Kingdom
D. United States

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: B** — Both the 'Concurrent List' (Seventh Schedule) and the provision for 'Joint Sitting of both Houses of Parliament' (Article 108) were borrowed from the Australian Constitution.

</details>

**Q2.**
Which of the following pairs of source country and constitutional feature is NOT correctly matched?

A. Ireland — Directive Principles of State Policy
B. Germany (Weimar) — Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency
C. South Africa — Procedure for Amendment of the Constitution
D. Canada — Fundamental Duties

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: D** — Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, Part IVA) were inspired by the USSR (Soviet) Constitution, incorporated by the 42nd Amendment Act 1976. Canada was the source for a Federation with a strong Centre and vesting of Residuary Powers in the Centre (Article 248).

</details>

**Q3.**
With reference to the Parts of the Indian Constitution, consider the following pairs:
1. Part IX — The Panchayats
2. Part IXA — The Municipalities
3. Part XI — Relations between the Union and the States
4. Part XIVA — Tribunals

Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

A. 1, 2 and 3 only
B. 2 and 4 only
C. 1, 3 and 4 only
D. 1, 2, 3 and 4

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: D** — All four pairs are correctly matched. Part IX (Panchayats - 73rd Amd), Part IXA (Municipalities - 74th Amd), Part XI (Union-State relations), and Part XIVA (Tribunals - 42nd Amd).

</details>

**Q4.**
Which language was added to the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution by the 21st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1967?

A. Sindhi
B. Konkani
C. Manipuri
D. Nepali

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A** — Sindhi was added as the 15th language by the 21st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1967. Konkani, Manipuri, and Nepali were added later by the 71st Amendment (1992). Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, and Santhali were added by the 92nd Amendment (2003).

</details>

**Q5.**
In which landmark judgment did a 9-judge bench of the Supreme Court rule that laws included in the Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973 are subject to judicial review if they violate the Basic Structure?

A. Shankari Prasad Case (1951)
B. Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)
C. Minerva Mills Case (1980)
D. I.R. Coelho Case (2007)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: D** — In I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007), a 9-judge bench held that any law placed in the 9th Schedule after the date of the Kesavananda Bharati judgment (24 April 1973) is open to challenge if it violates Fundamental Rights forming part of the basic structure.

</details>

**Q6.**
Under the Sixth Schedule of the Indian Constitution, special provisions for the administration of tribal areas are provided for which group of North-Eastern States?

A. Assam, Nagaland, Manipur, and Mizoram
B. Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram
C. Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Manipur, and Tripura
D. Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, and Nagaland

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: B** — The Sixth Schedule applies to tribal areas in four states: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram (mnemonic: AMTM). Manipur and Nagaland are NOT covered under the Sixth Schedule.

</details>

**Q7.**
In the official Warrant of Precedence (Table of Precedence) of the Government of India, which two dignitaries occupy the SAME rank (Rank 6)?

A. Prime Minister and Former Presidents
B. Chief Justice of India and Speaker of Lok Sabha
C. Cabinet Ministers and Governors of States
D. Supreme Court Judges and Attorney General for India

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: B** — Both the Chief Justice of India (CJI) and the Speaker of the Lok Sabha share equal precedence at Rank 6.

</details>

**Q8.**

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only

</details>

**Q9.**
With reference to Parliamentary Committees, which committee is the LARGEST committee of the Indian Parliament and consists of members drawn EXCLUSIVELY from the Lok Sabha?

A. Public Accounts Committee
B. Estimates Committee
C. Committee on Public Undertakings
D. Business Advisory Committee

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: B** — The Estimates Committee is the largest committee of Parliament, consisting of 30 members, all elected from the Lok Sabha alone (no representation from the Rajya Sabha). It was set up in 1950 on the recommendation of Finance Minister John Mathai.

</details>

**Q10.**
Which prerogative writ can be issued against BOTH public authorities and private individuals?

A. Mandamus
B. Quo-Warranto
C. Habeas Corpus
D. Prohibition

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>
