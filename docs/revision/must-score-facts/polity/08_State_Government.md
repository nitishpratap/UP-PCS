## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Part VI (Articles 152–237)** of the Constitution governs the State Government; deals with the State Executive, State Legislature, and High Courts / Subordinate Courts.
2. The **State Executive** consists of the **Governor**, the **Chief Minister**, the **Council of Ministers**, and the **Advocate-General for the State**; there is no office of Vice-Governor in the States.
3. **Office of the Governor (Article 153):** There shall be a Governor for each State; the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956** added a proviso permitting the appointment of the **same person as Governor for two or more States**.
4. **Appointment of Governor (Article 155):** Appointed by the **President by warrant under his hand and seal**; neither directly elected by the people nor indirectly elected by an electoral college; borrowed from the **Canadian model**.
5. In *Hargovind Pant v. Dr. Raghukul Tilak (1979)*, Supreme Court held that the office of Governor is an **independent constitutional office** and is NOT an employment under the Central Government, nor is it subordinate to the Central Government.
6. **Qualifications of Governor (Article 157):** Must be a citizen of India; must have completed the age of **35 years**.
7. Important Conventions on Governor: (1) Should be an outsider (not belonging to the State to which appointed); (2) Chief Minister of the State should be consulted by the President before appointment (recommended by Sarkaria Commission, though frequently violated).
8. **Conditions of Governor's Office (Article 158):** Shall not be a member of either House of Parliament or State Legislature; shall not hold any other office of profit; emoluments, allowances, and privileges determined by Parliament and cannot be diminished during term; where same person is Governor of two or more States, emoluments are allocated among the States by the President.
9. **Oath of the Governor (Article 159):** Administered by the **Chief Justice of the State High Court** (or senior-most HC Judge available); swears to **"preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law"** (identical oath text to President).
10. **Term of Office (Article 156):** Holds office for a term of **5 years**, but holds office during the **PLEASURE OF THE PRESIDENT**; has no security of tenure and can be removed by the President at any time without assigning any reason (*B.P. Singhal v. Union of India, 2010* held removal cannot be arbitrary, capricious, or on political whim); resigns by writing to the President.
11. **Executive Powers of Governor:** Appoints the **Chief Minister** and other Ministers (on CM's advice), Advocate-General, State Election Commissioner, Chairman and members of SPSC (can appoint, but **CANNOT remove SPSC members** — removed only by President under Art. 317); acts as Chancellor of State Universities.
12. **Legislative Powers of Governor:** Summons, prorogues State Legislature, dissolves Legislative Assembly (Art. 174); addresses Legislature (Art. 175 & 176); nominates **1/6th members of Legislative Council** (Art. 171(5): literature, science, art, cooperative movement, social service — *cooperative movement is unique to Governor*); nominates 1 Anglo-Indian to Assembly under Art. 333 (abolished by 104th Amd 2019).
13. **Assent to Bills (Article 200):** Governor can: (1) Give assent; (2) Withhold assent; (3) Return the Bill for reconsideration (if not Money Bill); (4) **Reserve the Bill for consideration of the President**.
14. **Mandatory Reservation for President:** Governor is **constitutionally required to reserve** any Bill passed by the State Legislature that in his opinion so derogates from the powers of the **High Court** as to endanger its constitutional position (Art. 200 second proviso).
15. **President's Action on Reserved State Bill (Article 201):** President can give assent, withhold assent, or direct Governor to return Bill; if returned, legislature must reconsider within 6 months; if passed again with or without amendment, the **President is NOT BOUND to give assent** (unlike Art. 111 where President must assent to repassed parliamentary bills!).
16. **Ordinance-Making Power of Governor (Article 213):** Promulgated when State Legislature (or either House in bicameral states) is not in session; ceases to operate **6 weeks from reassembly** of legislature; maximum lifespan **6 months + 6 weeks**; requires prior instruction from President in three cases (if Bill required presidential recommendation, or would have been reserved for President, or State Act would be invalid without presidential assent).
17. **Pardoning Power of Governor (Article 161):** Can grant pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions of punishment for offences against laws relating to State executive power; **CANNOT PARDON A DEATH SENTENCE** (even if death penalty is prescribed under State law, only the President has power to pardon death sentences under Art. 72; Governor can only suspend, remit, or commute a death sentence); **CANNOT pardon court-martial (military) sentences**.
18. **Discretionary Powers of Governor (Article 163):** Constitution explicitly recognises the Governor's discretionary powers (unlike the President); Art. 163(1) mandates Council of Ministers to aid and advise Governor except when acting in his discretion; Art. 163(2) provides that the **Governor's decision on whether a matter falls within his discretion is FINAL and cannot be questioned**.
19. **Constitutional Discretion of Governor:** (1) Reservation of Bill for President (Art. 200); (2) Recommending President's Rule (Art. 356); (3) Determining amount payable to autonomous Tribal District Council by Assam/Meghalaya/Tripura/Mizoram (6th Schedule); (4) Seeking information from CM on administration (Art. 167).
20. **Situational Discretion of Governor:** (1) Appointment of CM when no party has clear majority; (2) Dismissal of Council of Ministers that lost majority in Assembly; (3) Dissolution of Legislative Assembly.
21. **Chief Minister (Article 164):** Real executive head of the State; appointed by Governor; leader of majority party in Legislative Assembly; can be a member of either House; non-member can be appointed CM but must become member within **6 months**.
22. **Council of Ministers (Articles 163 & 164):** Aided and advised by CoM; collectively responsible to the **Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)** (Art. 164(2)); ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the Governor** (Art. 164(1)).
23. **Size of State Council of Ministers (91st Amendment, 2003):** Total ministers including CM shall **not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly**, but shall **NOT BE LESS THAN 12 MINISTERS** (Art. 164(1A)).
24. **Advocate-General for the State (Article 165):** Highest law officer of the State; appointed by Governor; must be qualified to be a **Judge of a High Court**; holds office during the **pleasure of the Governor**; right to speak and take part in proceedings of State Legislature without the right to vote (Art. 177).
25. **State Legislature (Part VI, Articles 168–212):** Most States have a unicameral legislature (Legislative Assembly); only **6 States have a bicameral legislature** (Assembly + Council): Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, and Uttar Pradesh.
26. **Creation and Abolition of Legislative Councils (Article 169):** Parliament can abolish or create a Legislative Council if the Legislative Assembly of the State passes a resolution by a **Special Majority** (majority of total membership of Assembly + 2/3rd of members present and voting); the parliamentary law is passed by a **Simple Majority** and is NOT deemed an amendment under Art. 368.
27. **Composition of Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha, Article 170):** Maximum strength **500**, minimum strength **60**; exceptions with smaller assemblies: Sikkim (32), Goa (40), Mizoram (40), Puducherry (30); directly elected by universal adult franchise.
28. **Composition of Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad, Article 171):** Maximum strength is **1/3rd of total strength of Legislative Assembly**; minimum strength is **40**; indirect election and nomination:
    - **1/3rd** elected by local authorities (Municipalities, District Boards);
    - **1/12th** elected by graduates of 3 years' standing;
    - **1/12th** elected by secondary school teachers of 3 years' standing;
    - **1/3rd** elected by MLAs from amongst non-MLAs;
    - **1/6th nominated by Governor** (literature, science, art, cooperative movement, social service).
29. **Legislative Council Tenure (Article 172(2)):** **Permanent body**, NOT subject to dissolution; one-third of its members retire every second year; member's tenure is **6 years**; presided over by **Chairman and Deputy Chairman** elected by Council members from amongst themselves.
30. **Legislative Assembly Tenure (Article 172(1)):** Normal term **5 years** from date of first meeting; can be dissolved earlier by Governor; can be extended during National Emergency by Parliament for **1 year at a time**, up to 6 months after emergency ends.
31. **Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Legislative Assembly (Articles 178–181):** Elected by Assembly members; Speaker resigns to Deputy Speaker; removed by resolution passed by **majority of all the then members (Effective Majority)** with 14 days' notice; casting vote in a tie.
32. **Legislative Powers Comparison: Assembly vs Council:** The Legislative Council is a **weak secondary chamber**; has NO veto power over ordinary bills:
    - On an ordinary bill, Council can delay for a maximum of **3 months in the first instance**;
    - If repassed by Assembly, Council can delay for **1 month in the second instance**;
    - Total maximum delay permissible is **4 MONTHS**; after 4 months, Bill is deemed passed in the form passed by the Assembly!
33. **NO Joint Sitting in State Legislature:** Unlike Parliament (Art. 108), there is **NO PROVISION FOR A JOINT SITTING** of both Houses in State Legislatures to resolve deadlocks on bills.
34. **Money Bills in State Legislature (Article 198):** Introduced ONLY in Legislative Assembly on prior recommendation of Governor; Legislative Council must return it within **14 days**; Assembly can accept or reject Council's recommendations.
35. **Annual Financial Statement of State (Article 202):** Budget of the State laid before the Legislature by the Governor; Demands for Grants voted **ONLY by the Legislative Assembly**; Legislative Council can only discuss.
36. **Uttar Pradesh Legislature Details:** Vidhan Sabha has **403 elective seats** (84 reserved for SC, 2 for ST); Vidhan Parishad has **100 seats** (largest legislative council in India).
37. **Qualifications for State Legislature (Article 173):** Must be a citizen of India; age not less than **25 years for Legislative Assembly** and not less than **30 years for Legislative Council**.
38. **Disqualification on Ground of Defection:** 10th Schedule applies to MLAs and MLCs; decided by the **Speaker of Assembly** or **Chairman of Council**; subject to judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan, 1992*).
39. **Sessions of State Legislature (Article 174):** Governor summons, prorogues, dissolves; maximum gap between two sessions cannot exceed **6 months**; quorum is **10 members or 1/10th of total membership**, whichever is greater (Art. 189(3)).
40. **Immunity of Governor (Article 361):** Not answerable to any court for exercise of official powers; no criminal proceedings can be instituted during term; cannot be arrested or imprisoned during term; civil proceedings require **2 months' prior written notice**.
41. **6 Bicameral States Architecture:** Uttar Pradesh (100 seats), Maharashtra (78), Bihar (75), Karnataka (75), Andhra Pradesh (58), Telangana (40).
42. **Article 169 Mechanism Detail:** Resolution passed by Assembly by special majority; Parliament enacts simple law; creates or dissolves upper house; entirely non-Art. 368 pathway.
43. **Cooperative Movement in Governor's Nomination:** Article 171(5) lists 5 fields for Council nomination: Literature, Science, Art, **Co-operative Movement**, and Social Service. Note: Under Article 80(3) for Rajya Sabha, President nominates from only 4 fields (Cooperative Movement is NOT included!).
44. **Governor's Veto Comparison with President:** Governor has Suspensive Veto, Absolute Veto, and Pocket Veto, plus the unique **Reservation Veto** under Art. 200 to refer Bills to the President.
45. **Presidential Supremacy on Reserved State Bills:** If President returns a reserved state bill and the State Assembly repasses it within 6 months, the President **CAN STILL WITHHOLD ASSENT**. He is never forced to assent to a State Bill.
46. **Governor's Ordinance-Making Guardrails:** The Governor cannot issue ordinances without presidential instructions if the bill would have required presidential assent or would have been reserved for President.
47. **Advocate-General Remuneration:** Remuneration is determined by the **Governor**, NOT by the State Legislature (Art. 165(3)); holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.
48. **Council of Ministers Minimum 12 Rule:** In smaller Assemblies like Goa (40), Sikkim (32), or Mizoram (40), 15% would yield only 5–6 ministers; hence Art. 164(1A) guarantees a floor of **at least 12 ministers** for governance viability.
49. **4-Month Maximum Delay Rule for Vidhan Parishad:** First transit = max 3 months delay; Second transit = max 1 month delay. Thus, the Legislative Council is only a **dilatory chamber**, having no ultimate power to block the Assembly.
50. **Quorum in State Legislature (Article 189(3)):** Minimum quorum is **10 members or 1/10th of total membership of the House, whichever is greater** (unlike Parliament which is strictly 1/10th).
51. **Governor Appointment and Dual Role (Articles 153 to 155):** Article 153 mandates a Governor for each State (7th CAA 1956 permitted same person as Governor for two or more States); appointed by the **President under his hand and seal** (Article 155); acts as constitutional head of state and key federal link between Centre and State.
52. **Tenure and Qualifications of Governor (Articles 156 & 157):** Holds office during the **pleasure of the President** (no security of tenure; normal term 5 years; *B.P. Singhal v. UOI, 2010* ruled pleasure is justiciable against arbitrariness); Qualifications: Citizen of India and completed **35 years of age**; does not hold office of profit; not a member of Parliament or State Legislature.
53. **Governor’s Discretionary Powers (Article 163):** Unlike President, Governor has **constitutional discretionary powers** (Article 163(1)); Article 163(2) provides that if any question arises whether a matter falls within Governor’s discretion, the **decision of the Governor in his discretion shall be final** and cannot be called into question; Examples: reserving a bill for President (Art. 200), recommending President's Rule (Art. 356), selecting CM in hung assembly.
54. **Assent to Bills and Reservation for President (Articles 200 & 201):** Governor has 4 options: assent, withhold assent, return bill (if not Money Bill) for reconsideration, or **reserve bill for consideration of the President**; Reservation is mandatory if bill endangers constitutional position of the High Court; When President reconsiders a reserved state bill (Art. 201), President may direct Governor to return it to legislature; state legislature must reconsider within 6 months, but even if passed again, **President is NOT bound to give assent**.
55. **Governor's Ordinance-Making Power (Article 213):** Promulgated when State Legislature (or either House in bicameral state) is not in session; ceases to operate **6 weeks after reassembly** of legislature; requires President's prior instruction if bill containing same provisions required President's previous sanction.
56. **Governor’s Pardoning Power (Article 161) vs President (Article 72):** Governor can pardon, reprieve, respite, remit, or commute sentences under State laws; Governor **CANNOT pardon death sentences** (can only suspend, remit or commute death penalty); Governor has **NO power over court-martial sentences**.
57. **State Council of Ministers & Chief Minister (Articles 163 & 164):** CM appointed by Governor; other ministers appointed by Governor on CM's advice; Ministers hold office during pleasure of Governor; Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)**; 91st Amendment 2003: total ministers including CM shall not exceed **15% of total MLAs**, and shall **not be less than 12 members**.
58. **Advocate-General for the State (Article 165):** Highest law officer in the State; appointed by Governor; must be qualified to be appointed a **Judge of a High Court**; holds office during the **pleasure of the Governor**; has right to speak and take part in proceedings of State Legislature without right to vote (Article 177).
59. **State Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) Creation / Abolition (Article 169):** Parliament can abolish or create a Legislative Council if the **State Legislative Assembly passes a resolution by a Special Majority** (majority of total membership + 2/3rd members present and voting); Parliament then passes an ordinary bill by simple majority (not an amendment under Art. 368).
60. **6 States with Bicameral Legislature (Vidhan Parishad):** Currently 6 States have Legislative Councils: **Uttar Pradesh (100 seats - highest)**, Bihar (75 seats), Maharashtra (78 seats), Karnataka (75 seats), Andhra Pradesh (58 seats), and Telangana (40 seats).
61. **Composition of State Legislative Council (Article 171):** Maximum strength is **1/3rd of total MLAs of the State**; minimum strength is **40 members**; Election breakdown: (1) **1/3rd elected by Local Authorities** (Municipalities, District Boards); (2) **1/12th elected by Graduates** of 3 years' standing; (3) **1/12th elected by Teachers** of 3 years' secondary standing; (4) **1/3rd elected by MLAs** from non-assembly members; (5) **1/6th nominated by Governor** from literature, science, art, cooperative movement, and social service.
62. **Composition of State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha - Article 170):** Maximum strength **500 members**; minimum strength **60 members** (exceptions with smaller assemblies: Goa 40, Mizoram 40, Sikkim 32, Puducherry 30); Uttar Pradesh has the largest Legislative Assembly in India with **403 elected members**.
63. **Disproportionate Power of Vidhan Sabha over Vidhan Parishad:** In passing ordinary bills, Legislative Council has only dilatory power: can delay bill for max **3 months** in first instance, and **1 month** in second instance (total maximum delay = **4 months**); there is **NO provision for a Joint Sitting** in state legislatures.
64. **Money Bills in State Legislature (Article 198):** Can be introduced ONLY in Vidhan Sabha on Governor's recommendation; Vidhan Parishad cannot reject or amend, must return within **14 days**; Vidhan Sabha may accept or reject recommendations.
65. **Secretariat of State Legislature (Article 187):** Each House of State Legislature has a separate secretarial staff; recruitment and conditions of service regulated by State Legislature by law.

## ⏱️ Timeline / Chronology

```
1950  ── Part VI of Constitution enforces: Governor, Chief Minister, State Legislature established
  │
1956  ── 7th Amendment: Proviso to Art. 153 added (Same person as Governor for 2+ States)
  │
1960  ── Bombay bifurcated into Maharashtra and Gujarat (both started with bicameral councils; Gujarat abolished soon after)
  │
1969  ── Madras renamed Tamil Nadu; West Bengal Legislative Council abolished under Art. 169
  │
1979  ── *Hargovind Pant v. Raghukul Tilak*: Governor's office is an independent constitutional post
  │
1986  ── Tamil Nadu Legislative Council abolished under Art. 169
  │
2000  ── Three new States created: Chhattisgarh, Uttaranchal (renamed Uttarakhand in 2007), Jharkhand
  │
2003  ── 91st Amendment: Capped State Council of Ministers at 15% of SLA (minimum 12 ministers)
  │
2010  ── *B.P. Singhal*: Arbitrary dismissal of Governors by Central Government held unconstitutional
  │
2014  ── Telangana created as 29th State with a 40-member Legislative Council
  │
2019  ── J&K Legislative Council abolished; J&K converted into UT
  │
2019  ── 104th Amendment: Abolished Anglo-Indian nomination to State Assemblies (Art. 333)
  │
2023  ── *State of Punjab*: Supreme Court limits gubernatorial delay in assenting to bills under Art. 200
```
