## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Part XV (Articles 324–329A)** of the Constitution governs the electoral machinery in India; **Article 324** vests the superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of all elections to Parliament and State Legislatures, and to the offices of President and Vice-President, in the **Election Commission of India (ECI)**.
2. **Article 325** mandates that there shall be **one general electoral roll** for every territorial constituency and prohibits discrimination or inclusion/exclusion on grounds **only of religion, race, caste, sex, or any of them**.
3. **Article 326** establishes **Universal Adult Suffrage** for elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies; every citizen of India who is not less than **18 years of age** on the qualifying date has the right to be registered as a voter.
4. Voting Age Lowered: The voting age was reduced from **21 years to 18 years** by the **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988** (enacted under the Rajiv Gandhi government, came into force on **28 March 1989**).
5. **Article 327 & 328:** Article 327 empowers **Parliament** to make provisions by law with respect to all matters relating to elections to Parliament and State Legislatures (including delimitation and electoral rolls); Article 328 gives residual power to State Legislatures subject to parliamentary laws.
6. **Article 329 (Bar on Interference by Courts):** Courts are barred from questioning the validity of any law relating to the delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats under Art. 327/328; **no election can be called in question except by an Election Petition** presented to the High Court under the Representation of the People Act, 1951.
7. **Representation of the People Act, 1950 (RPA 1950):** Primarily deals with: (1) Allocation of seats in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies/Councils; (2) Delimitation of constituencies; (3) Qualifications and disqualifications of voters; (4) Preparation and revision of **Electoral Rolls**.
8. **Representation of the People Act, 1951 (RPA 1951):** Primarily deals with: (1) Actual conduct of elections and bye-elections; (2) Qualifications and disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures; (3) Corrupt practices and electoral offences; (4) Settlement of election disputes (Election Petitions); (5) Registration and regulation of **Political Parties (Section 29A)**.
9. **Prisoners' Right to Vote (Section 62(5) of RPA 1951):** A person confined in a prison (whether under sentence of imprisonment or transportation or lawful custody of police) **CANNOT VOTE** in any election; **Exception**: A person subjected to **Preventive Detention** under any law **IS ENTITLED TO VOTE** via postal ballot.
10. Disqualification on Criminal Conviction (**Section 8 of RPA 1951**): Under Section 8(1) and 8(2), conviction for specified offences (promoting enmity, rape, corruption) triggers automatic disqualification; under **Section 8(3)**, conviction for any other offence with imprisonment of **not less than 2 years** results in disqualification **from the date of conviction and for a further period of 6 years after release**.
11. In ***Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)***, the Supreme Court struck down **Section 8(4) of RPA 1951** (which allowed sitting MPs/MLAs a 3-month protection against immediate disqualification if an appeal was filed) as unconstitutional; convicted sitting legislators are now **immediately disqualified upon conviction**.
12. Candidate Affidavit and Right to Know: In ***Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms - ADR (2002)*** and ***PUCL (2003)***, the SC ruled that voters have a Fundamental Right under **Article 19(1)(a)** to know criminal antecedents, assets, liabilities, and educational qualifications of candidates via mandatory affidavit (**Form 26**).
13. **None of the Above (NOTA):** Introduced in India following the Supreme Court's landmark judgment in ***People’s Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL) v. Union of India (2013)***; NOTA gives voters the right to register a negative vote; however, NOTA votes have only symbolic value (if NOTA gets the highest votes, the candidate with the second highest votes is declared elected, except in certain municipal polls in Maharashtra/Haryana where re-polls were ordered).
14. **Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs):** First used in a bye-election in **Paravur Assembly constituency in Kerala in May 1982**; first used in an entire State election in **Goa in 1999**; used in all parliamentary constituencies across India in the **2004 Lok Sabha General Elections**; manufactured by two PSUs: **Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL)** and **Electronics Corporation of India Limited (ECIL)**; maximum capacity is **2,000 votes** and can record up to **64 candidates** (expandable to 384 with 24 Balloting Units).
15. **Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT):** Introduces a paper slip containing candidate name, symbol, and serial number visible for **7 seconds** before dropping into a sealed box; first used in the **Noksen Assembly constituency in Nagaland in September 2013**; 100% VVPAT deployment implemented in the **2019 Lok Sabha General Elections**; under SC directive in 2019, VVPAT slips of **5 randomly selected polling stations per assembly constituency** are verified against EVM count.
16. **Delimitation Commission:** An independent statutory commission constituted under Delimitation Commission Acts to redraw territorial constituencies based on the latest census; **Delimitation Commission orders have the full force of law and CANNOT BE QUESTIONED IN ANY COURT OF LAW**; Delimitation Commissions have been constituted 4 times: in **1952, 1962, 1972, and 2002** (headed by Justice Kuldip Singh).
17. Constituency Freeze: The **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** froze the total number of seats in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies based on the **1971 Census until the year 2000**; the **84th Amendment Act, 2001** extended this freeze on total seats for another 25 years (until the first census after **2026**); the **87th Amendment Act, 2003** provided that delimitation of boundaries within States be done based on the **2001 Census**.
18. **Model Code of Conduct (MCC):** A set of guidelines agreed upon by political parties to govern conduct during elections; **comes into force immediately upon the announcement of election schedule by the ECI** and remains in operation until the completion of the electoral process; the MCC **DOES NOT have statutory backing** (it is a consensus-based voluntary moral code), but ECI enforces it under its plenary powers under Article 324.
19. Origin of MCC: First introduced in **Kerala Assembly elections in 1960** as a voluntary code; adopted by ECI during 1962 Lok Sabha elections; comprehensive code drafted in 1979.
20. **Electoral Reforms Committees Chronology:** (1) **Tarkunde Committee (1974–75)**: Appointed by J.P. Narayan (Citizens for Democracy); recommended lowering voting age to 18, multi-member ECI, and TV/radio time for parties; (2) **Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990)**: Recommended use of EVMs, rotation of reserved seats, curbing booth capturing; (3) **Vohra Committee (1993)**: Investigated politician-criminal-bureaucrat nexus; (4) **Indrajit Gupta Committee (1998)**: Recommended **State funding of elections** (partial state funding in kind: fuel, paper, microphones); (5) **Law Commission 170th Report (1999)**: Pre-poll alliances, banning candidates contesting two seats; (6) **J.S. Verma Committee (2013)**: Disqualification for sexual offences against women.
21. Contesting from Multiple Seats: Under **Section 33(7) of RPA 1951** (amended in 1996), a candidate can contest an election from a **maximum of TWO constituencies**; the Dinesh Goswami Committee and ECI have recommended restricting it to only one constituency.
22. Security Deposit for Elections: Lok Sabha general candidate = **₹25,000** (₹12,500 for SC/ST); Vidhan Sabha general candidate = **₹10,000** (₹5,000 for SC/ST); forfeited if candidate fails to secure **more than 1/6th (16.67%) of the total valid votes** polled.
23. Campaign Silence Period: Under Section 126 of RPA 1951, all public campaigning, rallies, and media advertisements must end **48 hours before the conclusion of polling**.
24. **Electoral Bonds Scheme Struck Down (2024):** Introduced in Finance Act 2017 to allow anonymous corporate and individual donations to political parties via interest-free bearer bonds issued by SBI; in ***Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (February 2024)***, a unanimous 5-judge Constitution bench headed by CJI D.Y. Chandrachud **struck down the Electoral Bonds Scheme as unconstitutional**, holding that anonymous political funding violates the voter's **Right to Information under Article 19(1)(a)**.
25. Voting Facilities: **Postal Ballot** is available to: Service voters (Armed forces), Special voters (President, Governors), voters on election duty, electors subjected to preventive detention, and (since 2019/2020) senior citizens above **85 years** and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs > 40%).
26. **Election Expenditure Limits:** For Lok Sabha constituencies, maximum limit is **₹95 lakh** in larger States (including UP, Maharashtra, Bihar) and **₹75 lakh** in smaller States/UTs; for Assembly constituencies, maximum limit is **₹40 lakh** in larger States and **₹28 lakh** in smaller States (revised in 2022).
27. Candidate Election Accounts: Every candidate must maintain a separate account of election expenses from nomination date to result declaration; under **Section 78 of RPA 1951**, the account must be lodged with the District Election Officer (DEO) within **30 days of election declaration**; failure to do so results in disqualification for **3 years** under Section 10A.
28. **Tenth Schedule Disqualification Nuances:** A legislator is disqualified under defection if: (a) voluntarily gives up party membership; (b) votes or abstains contrary to party whip without prior permission and not condoned within 15 days; (c) an **independently elected member joins ANY political party**; (d) a **nominated member joins a political party AFTER expiry of 6 months** (joining within 6 months is permitted!).
29. Tenth Schedule Exceptions: (1) **Merger**: Where original party merges with another and **not less than 2/3rd of the members** of the legislature party agree to such merger; (2) **Presiding Officer**: If a member elected as Speaker/Chairman resigns party membership and does not rejoin while holding office.
30. Deciding Authority on Defection: Decided by the **Presiding Officer of the House**; in ***Kihoto Hollohan (1992)***, SC struck down para 7 (barring judicial review), holding that the Presiding Officer acts as a **Tribunal** and his decision is subject to **Judicial Review** on grounds of mala fides, perversity, or violation of natural justice.
31. In ***Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker, Manipur (2020)***, the Supreme Court ruled that Speakers should decide anti-defection petitions within a **reasonable period of 3 MONTHS**, failing which courts can intervene.
32. **NRI Voting Rights:** Under the Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, **2010**, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs holding valid Indian passports) were given voting rights; however, they **must vote in person** at the polling station in their constituency (postal/proxy voting for NRIs is not yet operationalized).
33. **Electoral Trust Scheme (2013):** Created under Section 8(1)(b) of the Companies Act to streamline corporate funding to registered political parties; requires distributing at least **95% of contributions** received in a financial year to political parties; distinct from the struck-down Electoral Bonds Scheme.
34. Exit Polls vs Opinion Polls: Under **Section 126A of RPA 1951**, conducting and publishing **Exit Polls is completely BANNED from the commencement of polling on the first day until half an hour after closing of polling on the final day** across all phases; Opinion Polls are banned only during the 48-hour silence period before each phase.
35. **Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) and District Election Officer (DEO):** The CEO heads election administration in a State, appointed by ECI in consultation with State government; the DEO (normally the District Magistrate/Collector) supervises elections at the district level; Returning Officer (RO) conducts elections for a constituency.

**Q15.**

Which one of the following statements is not correct?

<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A. In case of differences of opinion amongst the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners, the matter is decided by the Law Commission.. At present, there is one Chief Election Commissioner and two other Election Commissioners in the Election Commission of India. All of them have equal powers, and their salary, wages and other benefits are also equal, which are equivalent to the Judges of the Supreme Court of India. The tenure of all Election Commissioners (including the Chief) is 6 years or 65 years of age, whichever is earlier, from the date of their appointment.
