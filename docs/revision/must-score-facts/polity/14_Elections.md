## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Part XV (Articles 324–329)** of the Constitution contains the constitutional provisions relating to elections; establishes an independent machinery to conduct free and fair elections.
2. **Article 324(1):** Vests the **superintendence, direction, and control** of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of all elections to: (1) **Parliament**; (2) **State Legislatures**; (3) **Office of the President**; (4) **Office of the Vice-President** in the **Election Commission of India (ECI)**.
3. Elections to **Panchayats and Municipalities** are NOT conducted by the ECI; they are conducted by the **State Election Commission** under **Articles 243K and 243ZA**.
4. **Article 325 (Single General Electoral Roll):** Declares that there shall be **one general electoral roll for every territorial constituency**; no person is ineligible for inclusion in the electoral roll on grounds **ONLY of religion, race, caste, sex**, or any of them; completely abolishes communal/separate electorates.
5. **Article 326 (Universal Adult Suffrage):** Elections to the House of the People (Lok Sabha) and the Legislative Assembly of every State shall be on the basis of **adult suffrage**; every citizen who is not less than **18 years of age** has the right to be registered as a voter.
6. **Lowering of Voting Age:** The voting age was reduced from **21 years to 18 years** by the **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988** (enacted under Rajiv Gandhi; came into force on **28 March 1989**).
7. **Article 327:** Empowers **Parliament** to make provisions with respect to all matters relating to elections to Parliament and State Legislatures, including preparation of electoral rolls, delimitation of constituencies, and all other matters.
8. **Article 328:** Empowers **State Legislatures** to make provisions with respect to elections to State Legislatures, in so far as provision has not been made by Parliament.
9. **Article 329 (Bar on Interference by Courts in Electoral Matters):**
   - (a) The validity of any law relating to the **delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats** cannot be questioned in any court.
   - (b) No election to Parliament or State Legislature can be called in question **except by an ELECTION PETITION** presented to such authority and in such manner as provided by parliamentary law (RPA 1951).
10. **Election Petitions Trial:** Election petitions questioning parliamentary or assembly elections are tried exclusively by the **State High Court** (jurisdiction transferred from Election Tribunals to High Courts in 1966); an appeal lies as of right to the **Supreme Court**.
11. **Disputes Regarding President and Vice-President (Article 71):** Inquired into and decided **EXCLUSIVELY by the Supreme Court of India**, whose decision is **final**.
12. **Representation of the People Act, 1950 (RPA 1950):** Provides for: (1) Allocation of seats in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies/Councils; (2) Delimitation of constituencies; (3) Qualifications of voters; (4) Preparation of electoral rolls and registration of electors.
13. **Representation of the People Act, 1951 (RPA 1951):** Provides for: (1) Qualifications and disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures; (2) Actual conduct of elections; (3) Corrupt practices and electoral offences; (4) Registration of political parties (Section 29A); (5) Free supply of electoral rolls and broadcast time.
14. **Disqualifications under Section 8 of RPA 1951:**
    - **Section 8(1):** Conviction for specific offences (hate speech, untouchability, rape, hoarding) leads to disqualification from date of conviction and for **6 years after release**.
    - **Section 8(3):** Conviction for ANY other offence resulting in **imprisonment for not less than 2 YEARS** leads to disqualification from the date of conviction and continues for a period of **6 years following release**.
15. ***Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013):*** Supreme Court struck down **Section 8(4) of RPA 1951** as unconstitutional; previously, sitting MPs and MLAs convicted of offences were exempt from immediate disqualification for 3 months or until their appeal was decided; post-*Lily Thomas*, disqualification is **INSTANTANEOUS upon conviction**.
16. **Disqualification on Ground of Corrupt Practices (Section 8A of RPA 1951):** Case of corrupt practice decided by High Court; referred to President; President decides after **obtaining opinion of the Election Commission of India** (maximum disqualification is **6 years**).
17. **Model Code of Conduct (MCC):** Set of guidelines issued by the ECI to regulate political parties and candidates during elections; **first evolved in Kerala in the 1960 Assembly elections**; has **NO STATUTORY BACKING** (not an Act of Parliament); enforced through moral authority, media publicity, and Article 324 powers; comes into effect **immediately upon announcement of the election schedule** by ECI.
18. **Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs):** First used in India on an experimental basis in **1982** in the **Paravur assembly constituency of Kerala**; manufactured by two PSUs: **Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL)**, Bengaluru and **Electronic Corporation of India Limited (ECIL)**, Hyderabad; independent standalone machines with no internet connectivity.
19. **Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT):** Independent system attached to EVMs that prints a paper slip showing the candidate name, symbol, and serial number for **7 SECONDS** before dropping into a sealed box; first used in a bye-election in **Noksen constituency of Nagaland in 2013**; introduced following SC order in *Subramanian Swamy v. ECI (2013)*; 5 EVMs per assembly constituency randomly verified against paper slips.
20. **NOTA (None of the Above):** Introduced in **2013** following Supreme Court judgment in ***People's Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL) v. Union of India*** under Rule 49-O of Conduct of Election Rules; provides secrecy for negative voting; does NOT affect election outcome (even if NOTA gets the highest votes, the candidate with the highest votes among valid candidates is declared elected).
21. **Right to Reject vs Right to Vote:** In *PUCL (2013)*, SC held that the right to vote includes the right not to vote and the right to register a negative vote (NOTA); however, the **Right to Vote is a STATUTORY RIGHT** created by RPA 1951 (Section 62), NOT a Fundamental Right (*Kuldip Nayar, 2006*; *Rajbala, 2015*).
22. **Right to Know Candidate's Background:** In ***Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) v. Union of India (2002)***, Supreme Court ruled that a citizen's right to know the criminal antecedents, assets, liabilities, and educational qualifications of candidates is a **Fundamental Right under Article 19(1)(a)** (Freedom of Speech and Expression).
23. **Security Deposit in Elections:**
    - **Lok Sabha Election:** **₹25,000** for General candidates; **₹12,500** for SC/ST candidates.
    - **State Legislative Assembly Election:** **₹10,000** for General candidates; **₹5,000** for SC/ST candidates.
    - Security deposit is forfeited if a candidate fails to secure **more than ONE-SIXTH (16.67%) of the total valid votes polled** in the constituency.
24. **Candidate Election Expenditure Ceilings (Revised 2022):**
    - **Lok Sabha:** Up to **₹95 lakh** in larger States (including UP, Bihar, Maharashtra); up to **₹75 lakh** in smaller States and UTs.
    - **State Assembly:** Up to **₹40 lakh** in larger States; up to **₹28 lakh** in smaller States and UTs.
25. **Delimitation Commission:** High-powered body constituted by the Central Government under the **Delimitation Commission Act**; orders have the force of law and **CANNOT be challenged in any court**; 4 Delimitation Commissions constituted so far: **1952, 1962, 1972, and 2002** (headed by Justice Kuldip Singh).
26. **Freezes on Delimitation:** The **42nd Amendment (1976)** froze total Lok Sabha seats at the **1971 Census** level until 2000; the **84th Amendment (2001)** extended this seat freeze until the **first census after 2026**; the **87th Amendment (2003)** allowed internal readjustment of constituency boundaries within States based on the **2001 Census**.
27. **Criteria for Recognition as a National Party (Election Symbols Order, 1968):** A political party is recognized as a National Party if it satisfies ANY ONE of three conditions:
    - (1) Secures at least **6% of the valid votes polled in 4 or more States** at a general election to Lok Sabha or Assembly, AND in addition, **wins at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha** from any State(s); OR
    - (2) Wins at least **2% of the total seats in the Lok Sabha (11 seats)**, and these members are elected from **at least 3 different States**; OR
    - (3) Is recognized as a **State Party in at least 4 States**.
28. **Criteria for Recognition as a State Party:** A political party is recognized as a State Party if it satisfies ANY ONE of five conditions:
    - (1) Secures at least **6% of valid votes polled in the State** at an Assembly general election AND wins at least **2 seats in the Assembly**; OR
    - (2) Secures at least **6% of valid votes polled in the State** at a Lok Sabha general election AND wins at least **1 seat in the Lok Sabha** from that State; OR
    - (3) Wins at least **3% of the total seats in the Legislative Assembly** or at least **3 seats**, whichever is more; OR
    - (4) Wins at least **1 seat in Lok Sabha for every 25 seats** or fraction thereof allotted to the State; OR
    - (5) Secures at least **8% of the total valid votes polled in the State** at a general election to Lok Sabha or Assembly (added in 2011; no seats required).
29. **Present National Parties (2024):** Exactly **6 National Parties**:
    - (1) **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)**
    - (2) **Indian National Congress (INC)**
    - (3) **Communist Party of India (Marxist) [CPI(M)]**
    - (4) **Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)**
    - (5) **National People's Party (NPP)** — first party from North-East India to gain national status (2019)
    - (6) **Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)** — recognized in April 2023.
30. Parties that **lost National Party status in April 2023**: All India Trinamool Congress (TMC), Nationalist Congress Party (NCP), and Communist Party of India (CPI).
31. **Electoral Reforms Committees:**
    - **Tarkunde Committee (1974–75):** Recommended lowering voting age to 18; multi-member ECI.
    - **Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990):** Comprehensive report on electoral reforms; recommended state funding of elections in kind; tightening anti-defection.
    - **Indrajit Gupta Committee (1998):** Recommended **State Funding of Elections** (in kind, not cash) to level the playing field.
    - **Law Commission 170th Report (1999):** Recommended banning political parties that do not observe internal democracy.
    - **J.S. Verma Committee (2013):** Recommended criminal law amendments on sexual offences and disqualification of candidates facing serious charges.
32. **Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule, 52nd Amendment 1985):** Disqualification on ground of defection decided by **Speaker of Lok Sabha / Chairman of Rajya Sabha**; in *Kihoto Hollohan (1992)*, SC held Speaker's decision is subject to **Judicial Review**.
33. **Exit Polls Ban:** Section 126A of RPA 1951 prohibits conduct of exit polls and dissemination of their results during the period from the commencement of the first phase of polling until half an hour after closing of the final phase.
34. **Voting Rights of Prisoners (Section 62(5) of RPA 1951):** No person confined in prison (whether convicted or under-trial) or in lawful custody of police has the right to vote; exception: persons subjected to **preventive detention** CAN VOTE by postal ballot.
35. **Free Airtime for Political Parties:** Section 39A of RPA 1951 mandates allocation of equitable sharing of time on public broadcasting media (Doordarshan and All India Radio) to recognized political parties during elections.
36. **Part XV Article Coverage:** Articles 324 to 329A (Art. 329A was repealed by 44th Amd 1978).
37. **Single Electoral Roll Principle:** Article 325 eliminated the legacy of colonial separate electorates (introduced by 1909 and 1919 Acts), ensuring a single unified secular civic identity.
38. **RPA 1950 vs RPA 1951 Distinction:** RPA 1950 is about **allocation of seats and voter registration**; RPA 1951 is about the **conduct of elections, machinery, qualifications, and corrupt practices**.
39. **Lily Thomas Historic Impact:** Prior to 2013, politicians convicted of serious criminal offences used Section 8(4) appeals to retain legislative membership for years; *Lily Thomas* eliminated this buffer completely.
40. **PUCL 2013 Negative Voting Rationale:** Negative voting allows citizens to reject all candidates, signaling dissatisfaction to political parties and encouraging cleaner candidate selection.
41. **7-Second VVPAT Display Rule:** The paper slip is backlit and visible to the voter through a transparent window for exactly 7 seconds before being cut automatically and dropping into the sealed ballot container.
42. **MCC Non-Statutory Justification:** ECI has consistently opposed statutory status for the MCC because statutory enforcement through courts would lead to endless legal delays, whereas election processes require instantaneous executive correction under Article 324.
43. **First Phase of EVM Rollout:** EVMs were introduced nationwide in the **2004 Lok Sabha General Elections** across all polling stations in India for the first time.
44. **1/6th Valid Votes Deposit Forfeiture Threshold:** Candidates must secure more than 16.66% of valid votes polled to recover their security deposit; discourages non-serious candidates.
45. **Delimitation Commission Unchallengeable Status:** Article 329(a) and Section 10 of Delimitation Acts prohibit any court from examining the delimitation orders once notified in the Gazette of India.
46. **84th Amendment 2026 Freeze Logic:** Prevented States with successful population control policies (Southern States) from losing parliamentary representation relative to States with higher population growth (Northern States).
47. **TMC, NCP, CPI Demotion (2023):** ECI withdrew national party status from TMC, NCP, and CPI after reviewing their performance across the 2014 and 2019 Lok Sabha elections and subsequent assembly elections.
48. **Indrajit Gupta State Funding Committee (1998):** Emphasized that state funding should be provided strictly in kind (fuel, paper, microphone facilities, time slots) and restricted to recognized political parties.
49. **Electoral Bonds Judgment Key Rationale (2024):** Supreme Court observed that information about funding of political parties is essential for voters to exercise their freedom of speech and voting choice effectively under Art. 19(1)(a).
50. **Prisoner Franchise Exception:** While undertrials and convicted prisoners cannot vote under Section 62(5) RPA 1951, persons under **Preventive Detention** are explicitly allowed to exercise their vote via postal ballot under the Conduct of Election Rules.

**Q15.**

Which one of the following statements is not correct?

A. In case of differences of opinion amongst the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners, the matter is decided by the Law Commission.
B. The Election Commissioners cannot be removed from their office except on the the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.
C. The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners enjoy equal powers.
D. The term of office of Election Commissioners is six years from the date they assume office or till the day they attain the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Logic:** In a multi-member Commission, decisions are taken by majority because the members have equal powers; the Law Commission does not decide their internal differences.

**Ans: A. In case of differences of opinion amongst the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners, the matter is decided by the Law Commission.. At present, there is one Chief Election Commissioner and two other Election Commissioners in the Election Commission of India. All of them have equal powers, and their salary, wages and other benefits are also equal, which are equivalent to the Judges of the Supreme Court of India. The tenure of all Election Commissioners (including the Chief) is 6 years or 65 years of age, whichever is earlier, from the date of their appointment. According to the provisos of the clause (5) of the Article 324, the Chief Election Commissioner shall not be removed from his office except in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court and any other Election Commissioner or a Regional Commissioner shall not be removed from his office except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner. In the 3-member election commission, although the Chief Election Commissioner acts as the Head, nevertheless due to the powers of all election commissioners being equal, all the issues are decided by the decision of the majority. Hence, statement of option (a) is not correct.**
