## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005:** Enacted to promote transparency, accountability, and contain corruption in public authorities; roots lie in the Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS) grassroots movement led by **Aruna Roy in Rajasthan (1990s)**.
2. **Legislative History of RTI:** Tamil Nadu was the **first State to pass an RTI Act in 1997** (followed by Goa in 1997); Parliament passed the Freedom of Information Act, 2002 (never enforced); the comprehensive **Right to Information Act, 2005** received presidential assent on **15 June 2005** and came into full force on **12 October 2005**.
3. **Constitutional Foundation of RTI:** In ***State of U.P. v. Raj Narain (1975)*** and ***SP Gupta (1981)***, the Supreme Court held that the **Right to Information is an inalienable component of Freedom of Speech and Expression under Article 19(1)(a)** of the Constitution.
4. **Definition of 'Public Authority' (Section 2(h)):** Includes any authority or body established or constituted: (a) by or under the Constitution; (b) by any law made by Parliament or State Legislature; (c) by notification issued by government; and includes **any non-government organization (NGO) substantially financed directly or indirectly by funds provided by the appropriate Government**.
5. **Time Limits under RTI (Section 7):** (1) General request = within **30 days** of application; (2) If application is made to an Assistant Public Information Officer (APIO) = within **35 days**; (3) If information concerns the **LIFE OR LIBERTY OF A PERSON = MUST BE PROVIDED WITHIN 48 HOURS**; (4) Third-party information = within **40 days**.
6. **Fees and Deemed Refusal:** Application fee is ₹10; information is **provided completely FREE OF COST to persons below the poverty line (BPL)**; if a public authority fails to give information within the specified time limit, it is deemed a refusal and information **must be provided FREE OF COST to all applicants** thereafter.
7. **Exemptions from Disclosure (Section 8(1)):** 10 categories exempt from disclosure: sovereignty/integrity of India, security/scientific/economic interests of State, contempt of court, parliamentary privilege, commercial confidence/intellectual property, information received in confidence from foreign government, endanger life/safety of any person, impede investigation/prosecution, Cabinet papers (until decision taken), and unwarranted invasion of privacy.
8. **Public Interest Override (Section 8(2)):** A public authority **may allow access to information** even if it falls under exemptions, if **public interest in disclosure outweighs the harm to protected interests**.
9. **RTI Overriding Effect (Section 22):** The RTI Act has **overriding effect** over the **Official Secrets Act, 1923** and any other law inconsistent with it to the extent of the inconsistency.
10. **Exempted Intelligence & Security Agencies (Section 24 & Second Schedule):** Agencies like IB, RAW, Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI), and CAPFs are exempt from the RTI Act; **Exception**: Information pertaining to **allegations of corruption and human rights violations IS NOT EXEMPT** (human rights violations require approval of Central/State Information Commission and must be provided within **45 days**).
11. **Appeals Structure under RTI:** **First Appeal** lies to the First Appellate Authority (senior officer in the department) within **30 days**; **Second Appeal** lies to the **Central Information Commission (CIC) or State Information Commission (SIC)** within **90 days**; decision of the Commission is binding.
12. **Disaster Management Act, 2005:** Enacted following the 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami; provides an institutional mechanism for drawing up disaster management plans and holistic response; came into force in January 2006.
13. **National Disaster Management Authority - NDMA (Section 3):** Apex body for disaster management in India; **Chairperson = PRIME MINISTER OF INDIA**; consists of other members not exceeding 9, nominated by the Chairperson (one designated Vice-Chairperson with Cabinet Minister rank).
14. **State Disaster Management Authority - SDMA (Section 14):** Apex body at State level; **Chairperson = CHIEF MINISTER OF THE STATE**; consists of other members not exceeding 9 nominated by CM.
15. **District Disaster Management Authority - DDMA (Section 25):** Apex body at district level; **Co-chaired by the District Magistrate / Collector / Deputy Commissioner AND the elected Chairperson of the Zila Parishad** (ex-officio Co-Chairperson).
16. **National Disaster Response Force - NDRF (Section 44):** Specialist multi-skilled force constituted for disaster response; personnel deputed from Central Armed Police Forces (BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB, Assam Rifles); headed by a **Director General (DG)** appointed by Central Government.
17. **Funds under Disaster Management Act:** (1) **National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF)**: Managed by Central Government under Section 46 for meeting emergency expenses; (2) **National Disaster Mitigation Fund (NDMF)**; parallel State Disaster Response Fund (SDRF) and District Disaster Response Fund (DDRF).
18. **Right to Education (RTE) Act, 2009:** Enacted to give statutory effect to **Article 21A**; came into force on **1 April 2010**; guarantees free and compulsory elementary education (Class 1 to 8) to all children of **6 to 14 years**.
19. **Key RTE Provisions:** **Section 12(1)(c)** mandates **at least 25% reservation of seats** in Class 1 (or pre-school) in private unaided schools for children belonging to **economically weaker sections and disadvantaged groups**; prohibits screening tests and capitation fees; pupil-teacher ratio (PTR) fixed at **30:1 for primary** and **35:1 for upper primary**.
20. **RTE Prohibitions:** Completely bans physical punishment, mental harassment, unrecognized schools, private tuition by teachers, and deployment of teachers for non-educational work (except decennial census, disaster relief, and elections).
21. **Whistleblowers Protection Act, 2014:** Enacted to establish a mechanism to receive complaints relating to disclosure of corruption or wilful misuse of power against any public servant, and to provide safeguards against victimization of whistleblowers; designated competent authority is the **Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)**.
22. **Citizen’s Charter (नागरिक अधिकार-पत्र):** Originates from the **Citizen's Charter initiative in the UK (1991)** under Prime Minister John Major; represents a voluntary written declaration by public service providers outlining commitments regarding standards, quality, transparency, choice, accessibility, and grievance redressal.
23. **Citizen's Charter in India:** First adopted at the Chief Ministers' Conference in **May 1997**; coordinated by the **Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG)**; **Citizen's Charters are NOT legally enforceable in courts of law** (voluntary administrative standards).
24. **Sevottam Model (सेवोत्तम मॉडल):** A quality management framework developed by DARPG in 2006 to assess public service delivery; rests on 3 modules: (1) Citizen’s Charter; (2) Public Grievance Redress Mechanism; (3) Service Delivery Capability.
25. **Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS):** Online web-enabled platform developed by NIC and DARPG in 2007; facilitates 24x7 lodging and tracking of grievances against Central and State government departments.
26. **E-Governance Initiatives:** National e-Governance Plan (NeGP) approved in 2006 with 31 Mission Mode Projects; **Digital India Programme** launched on **1 July 2015** with 3 vision areas: (1) Digital Infrastructure as core utility; (2) Governance and Services on Demand; (3) Digital Empowerment of citizens.
27. **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023:** Regulates the processing of digital personal data; establishes the **Data Protection Board of India** as an adjudicatory body; imposes penalties of up to **₹250 crore** for failure to prevent personal data breaches; grounds for processing include consent and 'legitimate uses'.
28. **Section 4 of RTI Act (Proactive Disclosure):** Mandates that every public authority shall maintain all its records catalogued and indexed, and **suo motu publish 17 categories of information** within 120 days of enactment, minimizing the need for citizens to file formal RTI requests.
29. **Lokpal Inquiry and Prosecution Wings:** Under the Lokpal Act 2013, Lokpal has its own **Inquiry Wing** headed by Director of Inquiry and **Prosecution Wing** headed by Director of Prosecution; can utilize any government agency including CBI.
30. **Timeline for Lokpal Inquiries:** Preliminary inquiry must be completed within **90 days** (extendable by 90 days); investigation must be completed within **6 months**; trial before Special Court must be completed within **1 year** (extendable up to 2 years).
31. **Citizen's Charter Bill, 2011:** The Right of Citizens for Time Bound Delivery of Goods and Services and Redressal of their Grievances Bill, 2011 lapsed upon dissolution of 15th Lok Sabha; however, over 20 States (including UP under UP Janhit Guarantee Act, 2011) have enacted their own **Public Service Delivery Guarantee Acts** providing statutory timelines and penalties for delay.
32. **National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013:** Enacted on 5 July 2013; gives legal entitlement to subsidized foodgrains to **75% of rural population and 50% of urban population** (~67% of total population); entitles priority households to **5 kg of foodgrains per person per month** at ₹3/kg rice, ₹2/kg wheat, and ₹1/kg coarse grains; Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) households continue to receive **35 kg per household per month**.
33. **NFSA Women Empowerment & Maternity Benefit:** Section 13 mandates that the **eldest woman of 18 years of age or above** in every eligible household shall be the head of the household for the issue of ration cards; Section 4 guarantees maternity benefit of **not less than ₹6,000** for pregnant and lactating mothers.
34. **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (FRA), 2006:** Enacted by Parliament; nodal agency is the **Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA)**; cut-off date to prove occupation is **13 December 2005**; recognizes individual and community forest rights over land up to a ceiling of **4 hectares**.
35. **Gram Sabha Authority under FRA:** The **Gram Sabha is designated as the competent statutory authority** to initiate the process for determining the nature and extent of individual and community forest rights, receive claims, and pass resolutions.
36. **Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA), 1993:** Enacted under Article 253 to establish NHRC and State HRCs; 2019 Amendment allows a **former Judge of the Supreme Court** (in addition to retired CJI) to be appointed NHRC Chairperson; reduced tenure from 5 years to **3 years (or age 70)**, making members eligible for reappointment.
37. **Consumer Protection Act (CPA), 2019:** Replaced the 1986 Act; established the **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)** with an Investigation Wing headed by Director-General; introduces strict **product liability** against manufacturers/sellers, and regulates misleading advertisements and e-commerce transactions.
38. **Pecuniary Jurisdiction under CPA 2019 (2021 Rules):** District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission hears claims up to **₹50 lakh**; State Commission hears claims between **₹50 lakh and ₹2 crore**; National Commission (NCDRC) hears claims exceeding **₹2 crore**.
39. **Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act (PESA), 1996:** Enacted on the recommendations of the **Dilip Singh Bhuria Committee**; extends Part IX of the Constitution to **Fifth Schedule areas across 10 States**; empowers the Gram Sabha to approve plans, manage minor forest produce (MFP), prevent land alienation, and regulate minor minerals.
40. **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989:** Section 18 bars anticipatory bail under Section 438 CrPC; following the SC ruling in *Subhash Kashinath Mahajan (2018)*, Parliament enacted the **2018 Amendment Act inserting Section 18A**, clarifying that no preliminary inquiry or prior sanction is required before registering an FIR or making an arrest.
41. **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012:** Defines a child as any person **below 18 years of age**; gender-neutral legislation; Section 19 makes reporting of sexual offences mandatory; provides for child-friendly Special Courts; 2019 amendment introduces the death penalty for aggravated penetrative sexual assault.
42. **Prevention of Corruption Act (PCA), 1988:** 2018 Amendment inserted **Section 17A**, requiring prior police approval from the competent authority before conducting any inquiry/investigation into offences by public servants; explicitly criminalized the **giving of a bribe (Section 8)** with up to 7 years imprisonment (bribe giver granted 7-day reporting safe harbour).
43. **Citizenship (Amendment) Act (CAA), 2019:** Amends Citizenship Act 1955 to grant fast-tracked eligibility for citizenship to persecuted religious minorities (**Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, Christians**) from **Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Afghanistan** who entered India on or before **31 December 2014**; reduces residency requirement from 11 to **5 years**.
44. **The Four Labour Codes (2019–2020):** Consolidates 29 central labour enactments into 4 streamlined codes: (1) **Code on Wages, 2019**; (2) **Industrial Relations Code, 2020**; (3) **Social Security Code, 2020**; (4) **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020**.
45. **Code on Wages, 2019:** Subsumes 4 central labour statutes: Payment of Wages Act 1936, Minimum Wages Act 1948, Payment of Bonus Act 1965, and Equal Remuneration Act 1976; universalizes minimum wage coverage to all workers across organized and unorganized sectors and empowers Centre to fix a statutory **National Floor Wage**.
46. **Representation of the People Act (RPA), 1950:** Provides for the allocation of seats in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies, delimitation of constituencies, qualifications of voters, and preparation and periodic revision of electoral rolls.
47. **Representation of the People Act (RPA), 1951:** Provides for the actual conduct of elections, qualifications and disqualifications of MPs and MLAs, corrupt practices and electoral offences, resolution of election disputes via High Court election petitions, and **registration of political parties under Section 29A**.
48. **Mission Karmayogi (NPCSCB):** Approved by Cabinet in September 2020 as the National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building; overseen by the Prime Minister's Public Human Resources Council; operationalized by the **Capacity Building Commission (CBC)** and the **iGOT Karmayogi platform** for anytime-anywhere digital learning.
49. **Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2nd ARC):** Constituted in August 2005 under **Veerappa Moily** (later succeeded by Ramachandran); submitted **15 landmark reports**; Report 1: "Right to Information: Master Key to Good Governance"; Report 4: "Ethics in Governance" (recommended Civil Service Code); Report 12: "Citizen Centric Administration".
50. **Social Audit (सामाजिक अंकेक्षण):** A process wherein the beneficiaries and citizens audit the implementation of public programs and expenditures against official records; institutionalized under **Section 17 of MGNREGA, 2005** to be conducted by the **Gram Sabha**; **Meghalaya** was the first State to pass a dedicated Social Audit law in 2017.
51. **Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005 Timelines:** Normal application response time is **30 days**; if information concerns **life or liberty of a person**, information must be provided within **48 hours**; if application submitted to an Assistant Public Information Officer (APIO), add **5 days** (35 days total); if third party interests involved (Section 11), timeline is **40 days**; failure to decide within specified period deemed refusal.
52. **RTI Act Appeals & Penalties:** First Appeal under Section 19(1) to senior officer in same public authority within **30 days** (decided within 30–45 days); Second Appeal under Section 19(3) to Central/State Information Commission within **90 days**; Central/State IC can impose penalty of **₹250 per day up to maximum ₹25,000** under Section 20(1) on defaulting PIO for malafide delay/denial, and recommend disciplinary action under Section 20(2).
53. **RTI Section 8 Exemptions (High-Yield):** Section 8(1) lists 10 categories of exempted information: (a) Sovereignty and integrity of India, security, strategic/scientific interests; (b) Forbidden by court of law or contempt of court; (c) Breach of parliamentary privilege; (d) Commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property; (e) Fiduciary relationship; (f) Information received in confidence from foreign government; (g) Endanger life or physical safety of any person; (h) Impede investigation or apprehension of offenders; (i) Cabinet papers (exempt during deliberations; open after decision made); (j) Personal information with no public interest.
54. **Citizen's Charter Origin and Evolution in India:** Concept originated in the United Kingdom under Prime Minister **John Major in 1991**; introduced in India at the Chief Ministers' Conference in **May 1997**; Citizen's Charter is a voluntary declaration by public service delivery agencies about their service standards, choice, accessibility, non-discrimination, transparency, and accountability; Charters are **NOT legally enforceable / non-justiciable** in courts of law.
55. **Sevottam Model of Public Service Delivery:** Developed by Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) in 2006; an assessment-improvement framework for public service delivery consisting of 3 modules: (1) Citizen’s Charter, (2) Public Grievance Redress Mechanism, and (3) Service Delivery Capability.
56. **Central Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS):** Online web-enabled system developed by DARPG in 2007 for lodging, tracking, and redressing citizen grievances across central ministries and departments; mapped to single citizen grievance portal with mandated redressal timeline (presently 21–30 days).
57. **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2011 (Enacted 2014):** Provides a statutory mechanism for receiving and investigating complaints relating to corruption or willful misuse of power by public servants and protecting whistleblowers from victimization; Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) designated as the competent authority to receive public interest disclosures; provides penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to ₹30,000 for false or frivolous disclosures.
58. **Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (2018 Amendment Highlights):** Amended in 2018 to align with UN Convention Against Corruption (UNCAC); Section 7 makes both bribe-taking and **bribe-giving a specific penal offence** (bribe-giver punishable with up to 7 years imprisonment unless reported within 7 days); Section 17A introduces mandatory **prior approval of competent authority** before investigating any decision taken by a public servant in discharge of official functions.
59. **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 Inquiry Machinery:** Lokpal can refer complaints to its own Inquiry Wing or to the **Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)**; CBI officers investigating Lokpal cases cannot be transferred without Lokpal's prior approval; Lokpal has powers of search, seizure, and provisional attachment of property under Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA).
60. **Mission Karmayogi (National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building - NPCSCB):** Launched in September 2020 by Union Cabinet; aims to transition civil servants from 'rules-based' to 'roles-based' competency framework; operates through digital learning platform **iGOT Karmayogi** (Integrated Government Online Training); overseen by Capacity Building Commission (CBC).
61. **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) Governance Architecture:** Launched on **1 January 2013** across 20 districts in India; transfers subsidies and welfare entitlements directly into bank accounts of beneficiaries; underpinned by the **JAM Trinity** (Jan Dhan Yojana, Aadhaar, Mobile numbers) to eliminate middlemen, ghost beneficiaries, and administrative leakage.
62. **Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies) Act, 2016:** Enacted as a Money Bill; statutory basis for UIDAI (Unique Identification Authority of India); in *K.S. Puttaswamy (Aadhaar Judgment, 2018)*, 5-judge bench upheld constitutional validity of Aadhaar for welfare subsidies (Section 7), but struck down Section 57 (private companies cannot demand Aadhaar) and requirement for bank accounts and school admissions.
63. **e-Governance National E-Governance Plan (NeGP):** Approved in May 2006 with 27 Mission Mode Projects (MMPs) (expanded to 31 MMPs); foundational core infrastructure comprises: State Data Centres (SDC), State Wide Area Networks (SWAN), and Common Services Centres (CSCs); Digital India programme launched on **1 July 2015** to transform India into a digitally empowered society and knowledge economy.
64. **Gram Nyayalayas Implementation Issues:** Recommended by Law Commission 114th Report and enacted in 2008; aimed at establishing 5,000+ courts; as of 2024, fewer than 300 Gram Nyayalayas are operational across 15 states due to reluctant state funding, lack of police infrastructure, and opposition from local bar associations.
65. **Right to Public Services Legislation in Indian States:** **Madhya Pradesh was the first State in India to enact a Public Service Guarantee Act (Madhya Pradesh Lok Sewaon Ke Pradan Ki Guarantee Adhiniyam, 2010)**; Uttar Pradesh enacted the **Uttar Pradesh Janhit Guarantee Adhiniyam, 2011** guaranteeing time-bound delivery of notified public services with penal fines on defaulting officers.

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

### 1. RTI Response Timelines: "48H - 30D - 35D - 40D - 90D"
- **48 Hours:** Life or Personal Liberty (व्यक्ति के जीवन या स्वतंत्रता का मामला — शीर्ष प्राथमिकता).
- **30 Days:** Normal request to PIO (सामान्य सूचना).
- **35 Days:** If submitted to Assistant PIO (एपीआईओ के माध्यम से).
- **40 Days:** Third-party information consultation (तृतीय पक्ष सूचना).
- **90 Days:** Limitation period to file Second Appeal to CIC/SIC (द्वितीय अपील).

### 2. PESA 10 States: "BIG MO CART" Trap-Proof Mnemonic
Remember: **BIHAR IS NOT IN PESA!** (No Fifth Schedule areas in Bihar or UP).
- **B / A:** Andhra Pradesh (AP)
- **T:** Telangana
- **O:** Odisha
- **M:** Madhya Pradesh & Maharashtra
- **G:** Gujarat
- **R:** Rajasthan
- **C:** Chhattisgarh
- **H / J:** Himachal Pradesh & Jharkhand

### 3. Sevottam Model 3 Pillars: "CAP"
- **C:** **C**itizen's Charter (नागरिक अधिकार पत्र का क्रियान्वयन).
- **A:** **A**ction on Grievances (लोक शिकायत निवारण प्रणाली — CPGRAMS).
- **P:** **P**erformance Capability of Service Delivery (सेवा वितरण क्षमता संवर्धन).

### 4. Four Labour Codes: "W-I-S-O"
- **W:** **W**ages Code, 2019 (Subsumes 4 wage laws; statutory floor wage).
- **I:** **I**ndustrial Relations Code, 2020 (300 worker threshold for closure/layoffs).
- **S:** **S**ocial Security Code, 2020 (Covers gig and platform workers).
- **O:** **O**ccupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020 (Contract labour, safety).
