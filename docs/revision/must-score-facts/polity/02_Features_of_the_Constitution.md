## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The Constitution of India is the **lengthiest written constitution** in the world; originally containing a Preamble, **395 Articles (in 22 Parts), and 8 Schedules** in 1949, it has grown to about **470 Articles (in 25 Parts) and 12 Schedules**.
2. Factors contributing to its vast size: (1) **Geographical diversity and vastness** of the country; (2) **Historical legacy** of the voluminous Government of India Act, 1935; (3) **Single constitution for both Union and States** (except erstwhile J&K); (4) **Dominance of legal luminaries** in the Constituent Assembly.
3. The Indian Constitution is drawn from diverse sources: **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** proudly stated that the Constitution was framed after **"ransacking all the known constitutions of the world"**.
4. The **structural part** is largely derived from the **Government of India Act, 1935** (Federal Scheme, Office of Governor, Judiciary, Public Service Commissions, Emergency provisions, Administrative details).
5. The **philosophical part** (Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles of State Policy) is derived from the **American** and **Irish** Constitutions respectively.
6. The **political part** (principle of Cabinet government and relations between executive and legislature) is largely drawn from the **British Constitution**.
7. Blend of **Rigidity and Flexibility**: The Indian Constitution is neither purely rigid (like USA) nor purely flexible (like UK); some provisions can be amended by a **Simple Majority** of Parliament (outside Art. 368); some require a **Special Majority** (Art. 368); and some require a **Special Majority plus Ratification by half the States** (Art. 368 proviso).
8. **Federal System with Unitary Bias:** The Constitution establishes a federal system of government (two governments, division of powers, written constitution, supremacy of constitution, rigidity of constitution, independent judiciary, bicameralism), but contains powerful unitary/non-federal features (strong Centre, single constitution, single citizenship, flexibility of constitution, integrated judiciary, appointment of State Governor by Centre, All-India Services, Emergency provisions).
9. **Article 1** describes India as a **'Union of States'**, which implies two things: (1) The Indian Federation is **NOT the result of an agreement among the States** (unlike the American Federation); and (2) **No State has the right to secede** from the federation. Hence, India is an **"Indestructible Union of destructible States"**.
10. **Scholarly Characterisations of Indian Federalism:**
    - **K.C. Wheare:** Termed the Indian Constitution **"Quasi-federal"**; a unitary state with subsidiary federal features rather than a federal state with subsidiary unitary features.
    - **Granville Austin:** Described Indian federalism as **"Co-operative Federalism"**.
    - **Morris-Jones:** Characterised it as **"Bargaining Federalism"**.
    - **Sir Ivor Jennings:** Described it as a **"Federation with a strong centralising tendency"**.
    - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Declared that the Constitution is **"Federal in normal times, Unitary in emergency"**.
11. **Parliamentary Form of Government:** Based on the British Westminster model; characterized by: (1) Presence of **nominal and real executives** (President and PM); (2) **Majority party rule**; (3) **Collective responsibility** of the executive to the legislature (Art. 75(3)); (4) **Membership of the ministers in the legislature**; (5) **Leadership of the Prime Minister**; (6) **Dissolution of the lower House** (Lok Sabha).
12. **Differences between Indian and British Parliamentary Systems:** (1) India has a **republican system** with an elected head of State (President), whereas Britain has a **hereditary monarchy**; (2) The British Parliament is **sovereign/supreme**, whereas the Indian Parliament is **limited by a written Constitution, federal division of powers, Fundamental Rights, and Judicial Review**; (3) In Britain, the Prime Minister must strictly be a member of the lower House (House of Commons); in India, the PM can be a member of either House (Indira Gandhi 1966, Deve Gowda 1996, Manmohan Singh 2004 were Rajya Sabha members).
13. **Synthesis of Parliamentary Sovereignty and Judicial Supremacy:** Indian framework harmonizes the British principle of **Parliamentary Sovereignty** and the American principle of **Judicial Supremacy**; the Supreme Court can declare parliamentary laws unconstitutional through its power of **Judicial Review** (Articles 13, 32, 136, 142), while Parliament can amend major portions of the Constitution through its **Constituent Power** (Article 368).
14. **Integrated and Independent Judiciary:** India possesses a **single integrated judicial system** enforcing both Central and State laws (unlike the dual court system in USA); at the apex is the **Supreme Court of India**, below it are **High Courts** at the State level, and below High Courts are **Subordinate Courts**.
15. **Constitutional Safeguards for Judicial Independence:** Security of tenure of judges (removed only by presidential order supported by special majority in both Houses); fixed service conditions; salaries charged on Consolidated Fund; prohibition on discussion of conduct of judges in legislature (Art. 121); ban on post-retirement practice for SC judges (Art. 124(7)); power to punish for contempt (Art. 129 & Art. 215).
16. **Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35):** Meant for promoting the ideal of **Political Democracy**; operate as limitations on executive tyranny and legislative absolutism; justiciable and enforceable through writs under **Article 32** (Supreme Court) and **Article 226** (High Courts).
17. **Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36–51):** Meant for promoting the ideal of **Social and Economic Democracy**; seek to establish a **Welfare State**; non-justiciable (Art. 37) but fundamental in the governance of the country; in *Minerva Mills (1980)*, SC held that the Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the **balance between Part III and Part IV**.
18. **Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A):** Added by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** on recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**; non-justiciable; originally 10 duties; **11th duty** (education of child 6–14 years) added by **86th Amendment Act, 2002**.
19. **Secular State:** The Constitution does not uphold any particular religion as the official religion; the word **'Secular'** was formally added to the Preamble by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976**; Indian secularism is **positive** ("Sarva Dharma Sambhava" — equal respect and protection for all religions), unlike Western negative secularism of complete separation between religion and State.
20. **Constitutional Provisions Embodying Secularism:** Articles 14, 15, 16, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, and 44 (Uniform Civil Code); in *S.R. Bommai (1994)*, SC held **Secularism is a Basic Feature** of the Constitution.
21. **Universal Adult Suffrage (Article 326):** Every citizen who is not less than **18 years of age** has the right to vote without discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or property; voting age was reduced from **21 years to 18 years** by the **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988** (effective 1989).
22. **Single Citizenship:** The Constitution provides for only a **single Indian citizenship** (borrowed from UK); there is NO separate State citizenship (unlike the USA and Switzerland); all citizens enjoy the same civil and political rights throughout India without distinction based on state of origin.
23. **Independent Bodies (Bulwarks of Democracy):** Constitution creates certain independent watchdog institutions:
    - **Election Commission of India (Article 324)**
    - **Comptroller and Auditor General of India (Article 148)**
    - **Union Public Service Commission (Article 315)**
    - **State Public Service Commissions (Article 315)**
24. **Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII, Articles 352–360):** Enable the President to handle extraordinary situations; transforms federal structure into a **unitary system without constitutional amendment** (unique in world constitutional history).
25. **Three-Tier Government Structure:** Originally, the Constitution provided for a dual polity (Union and States); the **73rd and 74th Amendment Acts, 1992** added a **third tier of local government** (Panchayats and Municipalities) with constitutional status, which is not found in any other major federation.
26. **Co-operative Societies (Part IXB):** Given constitutional status by the **97th Amendment Act, 2011**; added Art. 19(1)(c) right to form co-operatives, Art. 43B DPSP, and Articles 243ZH to 243ZT (partially struck down for intra-state co-ops in *Rajendra N. Shah, 2021*).
27. **Major Borrowings — UK:** Parliamentary government, Rule of Law, Legislative procedure, Single Citizenship, Cabinet system, Prerogative Writs, Parliamentary privileges, Bicameralism, Office of the Speaker.
28. **Major Borrowings — USA:** Fundamental Rights, Independence of Judiciary, Judicial Review, Impeachment of President, Removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges, Post of Vice-President.
29. **Major Borrowings — Ireland:** Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV), Nomination of 12 members to Rajya Sabha, Method of Election of the President.
30. **Major Borrowings — Canada:** Federation with a strong Centre, Vesting of **Residuary Powers in the Centre** (Art. 248), Appointment of State Governors by Centre, Advisory Jurisdiction of Supreme Court (Art. 143).
31. **Major Borrowings — Australia:** **Concurrent List** (7th Schedule), Freedom of Trade, Commerce and Intercourse (Part XIII), mechanism of **Joint Sitting** of the two Houses of Parliament (Art. 108).
32. **Major Borrowings — Germany (Weimar):** Suspension of Fundamental Rights during National Emergency (Art. 359).
33. **Major Borrowings — USSR (Russia):** Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A), Ideals of **Justice (Social, Economic, and Political)** in the Preamble.
34. **Major Borrowings — France:** Republic, Ideals of **Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity** in the Preamble.
35. **Major Borrowings — South Africa:** Procedure for **Amendment of the Constitution** (Art. 368), Election of members of the Rajya Sabha.
36. **Major Borrowings — Japan:** **'Procedure Established by Law'** (Article 21).
37. **Government of India Act, 1935 as Core Base:** Supplied ~60% of constitutional text: Federal scheme, Office of Governor, Judiciary, Public Service Commissions, Emergency provisions, and Administrative details.
38. **Critiques of the Indian Constitution Answered:**
    - Critiques called it a "Borrowed Constitution" / "Bag of Borrowings" &rarr; Ambedkar refuted this, noting that borrowing good ideas while adapting them to Indian soil is wisdom, not plagiarism.
    - Critiques called it a "Carbon Copy of 1935 Act" &rarr; 1935 Act had no Fundamental Rights, no adult suffrage, and no independent judiciary; Indian Constitution has all three.
    - Critiques called it "Un-Indian and Anti-Indian" &rarr; It reflects the composite culture and democratic heritage of the freedom movement.
    - Critiques called it an "Elephantine Size" &rarr; Required due to extreme geographic, social, and cultural diversities.
    - Critiques called it a "Paradise for Lawyers" (Sir Ivor Jennings) &rarr; Legal precision was necessary to prevent autocratic encroachment on federal and individual rights.
39. **Preamble as the Soul of the Constitution:** Described by **Pt. Thakur Das Bhargava** as the "most precious part of the Constitution, the soul of the Constitution, the key to the Constitution"; described by **K.M. Munshi** as the **"Political Horoscope"** of the sovereign democratic republic; described by **Sir Ernest Barker** as the "Key-note to the Constitution".
40. **Preamble Amendability (*Kesavananda Bharati, 1973*):** Supreme Court held that the Preamble is an **integral part of the Constitution** and can be amended under Article 368, provided the **Basic Structure is not altered**; amended only once by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** (added 'Socialist', 'Secular', 'Integrity').
41. **Rule of Law (Article 14):** Borrowed from **A.V. Dicey's concept**: (1) Absence of arbitrary power; (2) Equality before the law; (3) Primacy of rights of the individual. In India, the Constitution is the supreme source of law, not judicial decisions.
42. **Separation of Powers vs Checks and Balances:** The Indian Constitution does NOT adopt a rigid separation of powers (like the US presidential system); instead, it adopts a system of **Checks and Balances** where the executive is drawn from and responsible to the legislature, and the judiciary reviews actions of both.
43. **Integrated Judiciary vs Dual System:** The Supreme Court and High Courts form a single integrated judicial pyramid that enforces both Central and State laws (unlike the US dual court system where federal courts enforce federal laws and state courts enforce state laws).
44. **Universal Adult Franchise Transformation:** When adopted in 1950 with universal adult franchise, critics doubted its success in an illiterate population; the first general election (1951–52) proved that adult franchise was the greatest democratic triumph of independent India.
45. **Bulwarks of the Democratic System:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar identified four institutional bulwarks: Election Commission, CAG, UPSC, and State PSCs, without which democratic governance would crumble into autocracy.
46. **Unique Evolution of Local Self-Government:** The addition of Parts IX and IXA through the 73rd and 74th Amendments makes India the only major federation where local self-government institutions have complete, constitutionally protected architecture with mandatory 5-year elections and finance commissions.
47. **Positive vs Negative Secularism:** In *St. Xavier's College (1974)* and *Bommai (1994)*, SC highlighted that Indian secularism does not mean hostility towards religion, but treats all faiths with equal dignity, ensuring minority rights under Articles 29 and 30.
48. **Federalism as Basic Structure:** In *S.R. Bommai (1994)*, the Supreme Court unequivocally ruled that **Federalism is a part of the Basic Structure** of the Constitution; States are not mere vassals or administrative agencies of the Centre.
49. **Harmony between Rights and Directives:** In *Minerva Mills (1980)*, CJI Y.V. Chandrachud observed: "To give absolute primacy to one over the other is to disturb the harmony of the Constitution. Part III and Part IV are like two wheels of a chariot."
50. **Balance of Rigidity and Flexibility Matrix:** Article 368 does not follow a monolithic formula: simple majority provisions ensure daily adaptability, special majority ensures national consensus, and state ratification ensures federal integrity.
51. **Preamble Text Sequence & Landmark Landmark:** The exact preamble sequence of sovereign declarations is: **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC**; 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' were incorporated by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**.
52. **Three Dimensions of Justice:** Justice in the Preamble encompasses **Social, Economic, and Political** dimensions; this noble ideal was directly inspired by the **Russian Revolution of 1917**.
53. **Five Facets of Liberty:** The Preamble guarantees Liberty of **Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith, and Worship**; these ideals of Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity were adopted from the **French Revolution (1789–1799)**.
54. **Two Facets of Equality:** The Preamble secures Equality of **Status** and of **Opportunity**, aiming to eradicate artificial civic, economic, and political distinctions (enforced via Articles 14–18 and Article 325/326).
55. **Preamble Adoption Date in Hindi Calendar:** The English date is **26th November 1949**; the Preamble also records the traditional Hindi calendar date: **Miti Margashirsha Shukla Saptami, Samvat 2006 Vikrami**.
56. **Berubari Union (1960) vs Kesavananda Bharati (1973):** In *Berubari Union case (1960)*, the SC held Preamble is NOT part of the Constitution; in *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)*, a 13-judge bench overruled Berubari, holding the **Preamble IS an integral part of the Constitution** and subject to Article 368 without altering basic structure.
57. **LIC of India Case (1995):** The Supreme Court once again reaffirmed that the **Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution**, though it remains non-justiciable and cannot be directly enforced in a court of law.
58. **Indestructible Union of Destructible States:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar termed the Indian federation an 'Indestructible Union of destructible states' because states have no right to secede (Article 1) and Parliament can alter state boundaries without state consent (Article 3).
59. **Scholarly Characterizations of Indian Federalism:** **K.C. Wheare** classified it as *'Quasi-Federal'*; **Granville Austin** designated it *'Cooperative Federalism'*; **Morris-Jones** termed it *'Bargaining Federalism'*; **Ivor Jennings** described it as a *'Federation with a strong centralizing tendency'*.
60. **Doctrine of Basic Structure Evolution:** First articulated in *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)*; reaffirmed in *Minerva Mills (1980)* (balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs is part of basic structure); *S.R. Bommai (1994)* held secularism and federalism are basic structure components.
61. **Parliamentary Executive - Dual Executive System:** The **President is the de jure (nominal/titular) executive**, while the **Prime Minister is the de facto (real) executive**; Article 75(3) mandates collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers exclusively to the House of the People (Lok Sabha).
62. **Independent vs Integrated Judiciary:** The Indian Constitution establishes an **integrated judicial hierarchy** (Supreme Court at apex, High Courts, Subordinate Courts enforcing both Central and State laws), distinct from the dual court system of the United States.
63. **Universal Adult Suffrage (Article 326):** Adopted from the commencement of the Constitution; the voting age was reduced from 21 years to **18 years** by the **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988** (enforced 28 March 1989).
64. **Single Citizenship Exceptionless Principle:** Unlike dual-citizenship federations (USA, Switzerland), the Indian Constitution provides **only single Indian citizenship** for the entire nation, guaranteeing equal civil and political rights irrespective of state domicile.
65. **Three-Tier Governance Innovation:** Originally bicameral at Centre and States; the **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992)** added constitutional status to rural Panchayats (Part IX) and urban Municipalities (Part IX-A), creating a unique 3-tier federal governance structure found in no other major world constitution.
