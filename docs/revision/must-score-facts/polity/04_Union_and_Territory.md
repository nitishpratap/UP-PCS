## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Part I (Articles 1–4)** of the Constitution is titled 'The Union and its Territory'; defines India's territorial framework and parliamentary reorganization powers.
2. **Article 1(1)** declares: **"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"**; adopts both traditional (Bharat) and modern (India) names; uses the term 'Union' rather than 'Federation'.
3. Reasons for 'Union of States' (**Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**): (1) Indian federation is **not the result of an agreement among States**; (2) States have **no right to secede** from the Union.
4. **Territory of India vs Union of India:** 'Territory of India' is a **wider expression** that includes: (a) Territories of the States; (b) Union Territories; (c) Such other territories as may be acquired by India. 'Union of India' includes **only the States**.
5. **Article 2:** Empowers Parliament to **admit into the Union of India, or establish, new States** that were not part of India previously (e.g., admission of Sikkim, establishment of new external territories).
6. **Article 3:** Empowers Parliament to form new States or alter boundaries of existing States:
   - (a) Form a new State by separation of territory from any State, or by uniting two or more States, or parts of States;
   - (b) Increase the area of any State;
   - (c) Diminish the area of any State;
   - (d) Alter the boundaries of any State;
   - (e) Alter the name of any State.
7. **Procedural Safeguards under Article 3:**
   - (1) Bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament **ONLY on the prior recommendation of the President**;
   - (2) Before recommending, President must **refer the bill to the concerned State Legislature** for expressing its views within a specified period;
   - (3) The President and Parliament are **NOT bound by the views of the State Legislature** and may accept or reject them.
8. **UTs Excluded from Reference:** For Union Territories, no reference needs to be made to the concerned legislature; Parliament can unilaterally alter the area, boundaries, or name of any UT.
9. **Article 4:** Declares that any law made under Article 2 or Article 3 for the admission/establishment or formation of new States is **NOT to be deemed an amendment of the Constitution under Article 368**; such laws can be passed by a **Simple Majority** of Parliament.
10. **Indestructible Union of Destructible States:** The territorial integrity or continued existence of any State is NOT guaranteed by the Constitution; Parliament can redraw the political map of India at will. Hence, India is an "indestructible Union of destructible States" (unlike the USA, which is an "indestructible Union of indestructible States").
11. **Cession of Indian Territory (*Berubari Union Case, 1960*):** Supreme Court held that Parliament's power to diminish state territory under Article 3 **does NOT include the power to cede Indian territory to a foreign State**; cession can be effected ONLY by an **amendment of the Constitution under Article 368** (implemented via the 9th Amendment Act, 1960).
12. **Settlement of Boundary Disputes:** In 1969, Supreme Court ruled that settlement of a boundary dispute between India and another country does **not require a constitutional amendment**; it can be implemented by **executive action** as it does not involve cession of territory.
13. **Integration of Princely States (1947):** Of the 552 princely states situated within Indian geographical boundaries, 549 joined India voluntarily; 3 states initially refused and were integrated later:
    - **Hyderabad:** Integrated by police action (**Operation Polo**, September 1948);
    - **Junagadh:** Integrated through a **Plebiscite** (February 1948);
    - **Kashmir:** Integrated by signing the **Instrument of Accession** (October 1947).
14. **Four-Fold Classification of States in 1950:**
    - **Part A States (9):** Former British provinces governed by elected Governors (Assam, Bihar, Bombay, MP, Madras, Orissa, Punjab, UP, West Bengal).
    - **Part B States (9):** Former princely states governed by Rajpramukhs (Hyderabad, Jammu & Kashmir, Madhya Bharat, Mysore, Patiala and East Punjab States Union [PEPSU], Rajasthan, Saurashtra, Travancore-Cochin, Vindhya Pradesh).
    - **Part C States (10):** Former Chief Commissioners' provinces and small princely states administered centrally (Ajmer, Bhopal, Bilaspur, Cooch-Behar, Coorg, Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Kutch, Manipur, Tripura).
    - **Part D Territory (1):** Andaman and Nicobar Islands (administered by Lt. Governor).
15. **Dhar Commission (June 1948):** Appointed by President of CA, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, under Justice **S.K. Dhar**; recommended reorganization of states on the basis of **administrative convenience** rather than linguistic considerations.
16. **JVP Committee (December 1948):** Formed at the Jaipur Congress session consisting of **Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, and Pattabhi Sitaramayya**; submitted report in April 1949 formally rejecting language as the basis for state reorganization.
17. **Creation of Andhra State (1953):** First linguistic state created on **1 October 1953** by separating Telugu-speaking areas from Madras State; forced by the death of veteran Gandhian **Potti Sreeramulu** after a 56-day hunger strike.
18. **Fazl Ali Commission (States Reorganisation Commission — SRC, 1953):** 3-member commission appointed under Justice **Fazl Ali** (Chairman), with **K.M. Panikkar** and **H.N. Kunzru** as members; submitted report in September 1955.
19. **SRC Recommendations:** Broadly accepted language as the basis of reorganization, but firmly rejected the theory of **"one language, one state"**; recommended preservation of national unity and administrative/economic viability; recommended abolition of the 4-fold classification of States.
20. **States Reorganisation Act, 1956 & 7th Constitutional Amendment:** Abolished Parts A, B, C, D states; created **14 States and 6 Union Territories** on **1 November 1956**.
21. The 14 States created in 1956: Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Bombay, Jammu & Kashmir, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Madras, Mysore, Orissa, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal.
22. The 6 Union Territories created in 1956: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands, Manipur, Tripura.
23. **Bifurcation of Bombay (1960):** Bombay State bifurcated into **Maharashtra** (for Marathi speakers) and **Gujarat** (for Gujarati speakers); Gujarat became the **15th State** of the Indian Union.
24. **Dadra and Nagar Haveli:** Liberated from Portuguese rule in 1954; converted into a Union Territory by the **10th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1961**.
25. **Goa, Daman and Diu:** Liberated from Portuguese rule by police action (**Operation Vijay**, December 1961); constituted as a UT by the **12th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1962**; Goa was conferred full statehood in 1987 (**25th State**).
26. **Puducherry:** Handed over by the French in 1954; administered as acquired territory until 1962, when made a UT by the **14th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1962**.
27. **Nagaland (1963):** Formed by taking out the Naga Hills and Tuensang area from Assam; became the **16th State** of India (13th Amd Act, 1962).
28. **Bifurcation of Punjab (1966):** On recommendation of the **Shah Commission**, Punjab was reorganized into **Punjab** (for Punjabi speakers), **Haryana** (**17th State**, for Hindi speakers), and **Chandigarh** was created as a Union Territory serving as joint capital.
29. **Himachal Pradesh (1971):** Union Territory of Himachal Pradesh elevated to full statehood as the **18th State** of India.
30. **North-Eastern Reorganisation (1972):** Manipur became the **19th State**, Tripura the **20th State**, and Meghalaya the **21st State**; Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh were formed as Union Territories.
31. **Integration of Sikkim (1974–1975):**
    - **35th Amendment Act, 1974:** Conferred the status of **'Associate State'** on Sikkim and inserted Article 2A and the 10th Schedule.
    - **36th Amendment Act, 1975:** Following a referendum, abolished the Chogyal monarchy, repealed Article 2A and 10th Schedule, and admitted Sikkim as the **22nd full-fledged State** of India (Article 371F).
32. **Statehood to Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa (1987):** **Mizoram** became the **23rd State** (53rd Amd), **Arunachal Pradesh** the **24th State** (55th Amd), and **Goa** the **25th State** (56th Amd).
33. **Three New States in 2000:**
    - **Chhattisgarh (26th State):** Carved out of Madhya Pradesh on **1 November 2000**.
    - **Uttarakhand (27th State, originally Uttaranchal):** Carved out of Uttar Pradesh on **9 November 2000**.
    - **Jharkhand (28th State):** Carved out of Bihar on **15 November 2000**.
34. **Telangana (2014):** Carved out of Andhra Pradesh as the **29th State** on **2 June 2014** (Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014).
35. **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation (2019):** Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 bifurcated the State of Jammu and Kashmir into two Union Territories: **UT of Jammu & Kashmir** (with a Legislative Assembly) and **UT of Ladakh** (without an assembly), effective **31 October 2019**.
36. **Merger of UTs (2020):** Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu merged into a single UT effective **26 January 2020**; headquarters at **Daman**.
37. **Present Status of Indian Union:** As of 2026, the Indian Union comprises **28 States and 8 Union Territories**.
38. **Renaming of States and UTs:** United Provinces was renamed **Uttar Pradesh** in 1950 (first State to be renamed); Madras renamed **Tamil Nadu** (1969); Mysore renamed **Karnataka** (1973); Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands renamed **Lakshadweep** (1973); Uttaranchal renamed **Uttarakhand** (2007); Pondicherry renamed **Puducherry** (2006); Orissa renamed **Odisha** (2011).
39. **Citizenship at Commencement (Articles 5–8):** Conferred citizenship on persons domiciled in India (Art. 5), persons migrated from Pakistan (Art. 6), persons migrated to Pakistan and returned (Art. 7), and persons of Indian origin residing outside India (Art. 8).
40. **Parliament's Supreme Power on Citizenship (Article 11):** Article 11 expressly empowers Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law; Parliament enacted the comprehensive **Citizenship Act, 1955**.
41. **Article 1 Terrains Classification:** Territory of India consists of: (1) States; (2) Union Territories; (3) Acquired territories. An acquired territory becomes part of Indian territory from the moment of acquisition, but requires legislation under Art. 2 to be admitted as a State.
42. **Dhar Commission vs JVP vs Fazl Ali Evolution:**
    - **Dhar (1948):** Administrative convenience, NOT language.
    - **JVP (1948–49):** Rejected language as basis; advocated postponement of reorganization.
    - **Fazl Ali (1953–55):** Accepted language as a major factor, but rejected "one language, one state".
43. **Potti Sreeramulu's Sacrifice:** Veteran Gandhian freedom fighter whose 56-day fast unto death forced Prime Minister Nehru to concede the formation of the first linguistic State (Andhra State) in 1953, setting off the nationwide chain reaction for linguistic reorganization.
44. **Shah Commission (1966):** Appointed by the Central Government under Justice J.C. Shah to divide the bilingual State of Punjab; recommended creation of Hindi-speaking Haryana and Punjabi-speaking Punjab.
45. **Enclaves Exchange with Bangladesh (100th Amendment Act, 2015):** India transferred 111 enclaves to Bangladesh and received 51 enclaves from Bangladesh; settled the 4,096 km border; enacted under Article 368 because it involved cession and acquisition of territory.
46. **Five Modes of Acquiring Citizenship:** (1) **Birth** (Jus soli); (2) **Descent** (Jus sanguinis); (3) **Registration** (Persons of Indian origin, spouses of citizens); (4) **Naturalisation** (Residency, good character, language); (5) **Incorporation of Territory** (e.g., Goa, Puducherry, Sikkim).
47. **Three Modes of Losing Citizenship:** (1) **Renunciation** (Voluntary declaration by an Indian citizen of full age); (2) **Termination** (Automatic loss when an Indian citizen voluntarily acquires foreign citizenship under Art. 9); (3) **Deprivation** (Compulsory termination by Central Government for disloyalty, fraud, trading with enemy, or imprisonment within 5 years of naturalisation).
48. **Single Citizenship Constitutional Ideal:** Borrowed from the United Kingdom; promotes national integration; no domicile-based political distinctions; all citizens enjoy equal Fundamental Rights throughout the territory of India.
49. **Overseas Citizens of India (OCI) Cardholders:** Not full citizens; created by Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2005; granted multi-purpose, life-long visa to visit India; do NOT have voting rights, cannot hold constitutional office (President, Judge, MP, MLA), and cannot acquire agricultural land.
50. **Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA), 2019 Specifics:** Extends fast-track Indian citizenship to 6 non-Muslim persecuted minority communities (**Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, Christians**) from **Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Afghanistan** who entered India on or before **31 December 2014**; reduces residency requirement for naturalisation from 11 years to **5 years**; exempts 6th Schedule tribal areas and Inner Line Permit (ILP) areas.
