## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. Under **Article 79**, the Parliament of India consists of the **President**, the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**, and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**; though the President is not a member of either House, he is an integral part of Parliament.
2. **Rajya Sabha Composition (Article 80):** Maximum strength is **250** (238 representatives of States and UTs + 12 nominated by the President from literature, science, art, and social service); present strength is **245**.
3. Representatives of States in Rajya Sabha are elected by the **elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (MLAs)** in accordance with proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote (**PR-STV**); nominated MLAs and MLCs DO NOT vote.
4. Among Union Territories, only **Delhi, Puducherry, and Jammu & Kashmir** have representation in Rajya Sabha because only they have legislative assemblies; all other UTs have no Rajya Sabha seats.
5. **Rajya Sabha Tenure (Article 83(1)):** Rajya Sabha is a **permanent body** and is **NOT subject to dissolution**; one-third of its members retire every second year; the tenure of an individual member is **6 years** (prescribed by the Representation of the People Act, 1951, NOT by the Constitution).
6. **Lok Sabha Composition (Article 81):** Maximum strength originally 552; after the **104th Amendment, 2019** (which ended Anglo-Indian nomination under Art. 331), maximum strength is **550** (up to 530 from States and 20 from UTs); present elective strength is **543**.
7. **Lok Sabha Tenure (Article 83(2)):** Normal term is **5 years** from the date of its first meeting; stands automatically dissolved on expiry; can be dissolved earlier by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.
8. During a **National Emergency**, the term of Lok Sabha can be extended by Parliament by law for **one year at a time** for any length of time (Art. 83(2)); however, extension cannot continue beyond **6 months** after the emergency has ceased to operate (exercised during 5th Lok Sabha in 1976).
9. **Delimitation & Seat Freezes:** The **42nd Amendment, 1976** froze total seats in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies at the **1971 Census** level until 2000; the **84th Amendment, 2001** extended this freeze on total seats until the first census after **2026**; the **87th Amendment, 2003** allowed delimitation/readjustment of electoral constituencies within States based on the **2001 Census**.
10. **Qualifications for Membership (Article 84):** Must be a citizen of India; age not less than **30 years for Rajya Sabha** and not less than **25 years for Lok Sabha**; registered as an elector in any parliamentary constituency.
11. **Disqualifications (Article 102):** Holds office of profit; unsound mind; undischarged insolvent; non-citizen; disqualified under any law made by Parliament (Representation of the People Act, 1951).
12. **Decision on Disqualifications (Article 103):** Question whether an MP has become subject to disqualification under Art. 102(1) is decided by the **President**, whose decision is final; the President **MUST obtain the opinion of the Election Commission of India** and act according to such opinion.
13. **Disqualification on Ground of Defection (10th Schedule):** Decided by the **Chairman of Rajya Sabha** or **Speaker of Lok Sabha**; subject to judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu, 1992*).
14. **Presiding Officers of Parliament (Articles 89 & 93):** Rajya Sabha: **Chairman** (Vice-President of India, ex-officio) and Deputy Chairman (elected by RS members from amongst themselves); Lok Sabha: **Speaker** and **Deputy Speaker** (elected by LS members from amongst themselves).
15. **Speaker of Lok Sabha (Article 93 & 94):** Holds office from date of election until **immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha**; resigns by addressing letter to the **Deputy Speaker**; removed by a resolution passed by a **majority of all the then members (Effective Majority)** with 14 days' notice; when removal resolution is under consideration, Speaker can speak and take part in proceedings and vote in the first instance, but **CANNOT cast a casting vote in a tie** (Art. 96).
16. **Special Powers of Lok Sabha Speaker:** (1) Decides whether a bill is a **Money Bill** and his decision is **final** (Art. 110(3)); (2) Presides over **Joint Sittings** of both Houses (Art. 118(4)); (3) Decides questions of disqualification under the **10th Schedule**; (4) Appoints chairmen of all parliamentary committees of Lok Sabha.
17. **Casting Vote (Article 100(1)):** Speaker or Chairman does not vote in the first instance; exercises a **casting vote** ONLY in the event of an **equality of votes (tie)**.
18. **Speaker Pro Tem:** Appointed by the President (usually the senior-most member of newly elected Lok Sabha) to preside over the first sitting, administer oath to newly elected MPs, and enable election of the regular Speaker.
19. **Sessions of Parliament (Article 85):** Summoned by the President; gap between two sessions **cannot exceed 6 months** (Parliament must meet at least twice a year); by convention, Parliament holds 3 sessions: **Budget Session** (Feb–May), **Monsoon Session** (July–Aug), and **Winter Session** (Nov–Dec).
20. **Prorogation vs Dissolution vs Adjournment:** **Adjournment** terminates a sitting (done by presiding officer); **Adjournment sine die** terminates sitting without naming day for reassembly (presiding officer); **Prorogation** terminates a session (done by **President**); **Dissolution** ends the very life of Lok Sabha (done by **President**; Rajya Sabha is never dissolved).
21. **Quorum (Article 100(3)):** Minimum number of members required to constitute a meeting of either House is **1/10th of the total membership** of the House (55 in Lok Sabha, 25 in Rajya Sabha); if no quorum, presiding officer must adjourn or suspend the meeting.
22. **Voting and Decision (Article 100):** All questions at any sitting of either House or joint sitting are determined by a **majority of votes of members present and voting**, other than the Speaker or Chairman.
23. **Language in Parliament (Article 120):** Business is transacted in **Hindi or English**; presiding officer may permit any member who cannot adequately express himself in Hindi or English to address the House in his **mother tongue**.
24. **Ordinary Bills (Articles 107 & 108):** Can originate in either House; passed by simple majority of both Houses; if deadlock continues for **6 months**, President may summon a **Joint Sitting** (Art. 108).
25. **Joint Sitting of Parliament (Article 108):** Summoned by President; presided over by **Speaker of Lok Sabha** (if absent, Deputy Speaker; if absent, Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha; *Chairman of Rajya Sabha NEVER presides*); governed by Lok Sabha rules; passed by a **majority of total members of both Houses present and voting**; Lok Sabha's numerical superiority (543 vs 245) normally ensures its will prevails.
26. **Joint Sitting Restrictions:** Permissible ONLY for **Ordinary Bills** and **Financial Bills**; **NO Joint Sitting** can be summoned for **Money Bills** (Lok Sabha has supreme authority) or **Constitutional Amendment Bills** (must be passed by each House separately by special majority under Art. 368).
27. **Only 3 Bills passed in Joint Sittings in Indian History:** (1) Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1961; (2) Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1978; (3) Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002.
28. **Money Bill (Article 110):** Contains ONLY provisions dealing with taxes, borrowing, Consolidated Fund custody, appropriation, or incidental matters; certified as Money Bill by the **Speaker** (certificate cannot be questioned in court); introduced **ONLY in Lok Sabha** on prior **recommendation of the President**; Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend, must return within **14 days** with or without recommendations; Lok Sabha may accept or reject recommendations.
29. **Financial Bills (Article 117):** Category I (Art. 117(1) — contains Art. 110 matters + other general legislation; introduced only in LS on President's recommendation; joint sitting applies); Category II (Art. 117(3) — contains provisions involving expenditure from Consolidated Fund; can originate in either House; requires President's recommendation for consideration).
30. **Annual Financial Statement / Budget (Article 112):** Statement of estimated receipts and expenditure of Government of India for that financial year; laid before both Houses by President; expenditure divided into **Charged expenditure** (non-votable) and **Votable expenditure**.
31. **Charged Expenditure on Consolidated Fund (Article 112(3)):** Emoluments of President, Chairman/Deputy Chairman of RS, Speaker/Deputy Speaker of LS, Supreme Court judges' salaries/pensions, High Court judges' **pensions** (*salaries of HC judges are charged on State Consolidated Fund*), CAG salary/pension, debt charges of Government of India; can be discussed in Parliament but **NOT voted upon**.
32. **Stages in Budget Passage:** (1) Presentation of Budget; (2) General Discussion; (3) Scrutiny by Departmentally Related Standing Committees; (4) Voting on Demands for Grants (Lok Sabha exclusively); (5) Passing of **Appropriation Bill** (Art. 114); (6) Passing of **Finance Bill** (gives effect to taxation proposals).
33. **Cut Motions (Lok Sabha only):** (1) **Disapproval of Policy Cut** (demand reduced to ₹1); (2) **Economy Cut** (demand reduced by specified amount); (3) **Token Cut** (demand reduced by ₹100 to voice specific grievance); passage of any Cut Motion amounts to a no-confidence vote and the government must resign.
34. **Guillotine:** On the last day of allotted days for Demands for Grants, the Speaker puts all remaining demands to vote immediately, whether discussed or not.
35. **Vote on Account (Article 116(a)):** Grant in advance made by Lok Sabha to enable the executive to incur expenditure for a part of the financial year pending the regular passage of the Appropriation Bill (usually for 2 months, 1/6th of total budget).
36. **Supplementary, Additional, or Excess Grants (Article 115):** Supplementary grant (when original amount authorized proves insufficient); Additional grant (for unexpected new service); Excess grant (when money spent on service exceeds amount granted; must be approved by PAC before being voted by LS).
37. **Public Accounts Committee (PAC):** Oldest financial committee, set up in **1921** under GOI Act 1919; consists of **22 members (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha)** elected annually by PR-STV; **term 1 year**; **NO Minister** can be a member; Chairman appointed by Speaker from amongst members, by convention from the **Opposition since 1967**; examines appropriation accounts and **CAG audit reports**; termed the 'friend, philosopher, and guide' of CAG.
38. **Estimates Committee:** Set up in **1950** on recommendation of Finance Minister **Dr. John Matthai**; largest parliamentary committee; consists of **30 members, ALL FROM LOK SABHA** (Rajya Sabha has **NO representation**); elected annually by PR-STV; term 1 year; Minister cannot be a member; Chairman appointed by Speaker, always from the **Ruling Party**; termed 'continuous economy committee'.
39. **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU):** Created in **1964** on recommendation of the **Krishna Menon Committee**; consists of **22 members (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha)**; term 1 year; Minister cannot be a member; Chairman appointed by Speaker from amongst **Lok Sabha members only**; examines reports/accounts of PSUs and CAG audit reports on PSUs.
40. **Parliamentary Privileges (Article 105):** Collective privileges (right to publish debates, exclude strangers, punish for breach of privilege and contempt); Individual privileges (freedom of speech in Parliament, immunity from court proceedings for speech/vote, freedom from civil arrest **40 days before and 40 days after** a session — *NO immunity from arrest in criminal cases or preventive detention*).
41. **Question Hour vs Zero Hour:** Question Hour is the first hour of every parliamentary sitting; Starred questions (marked with asterisk, oral answers given, **supplementaries permitted**); Unstarred questions (written answers, **no supplementaries**); Short Notice questions (urgent matter, less than 10 days notice). **Zero Hour** is an informal Indian parliamentary innovation (introduced in 1962), not mentioned in Rules of Procedure; starts at 12:00 noon immediately following Question Hour for raising urgent issues without prior notice.
42. **Adjournment Motion vs Calling Attention Motion:** **Adjournment Motion** introduces an element of censure; requires support of at least **50 members**; interrupts normal business; Lok Sabha only. **Calling Attention Motion** is an Indian procedural innovation (since 1954) to call minister's attention to urgent matter; involves no censure; allowed in both Houses.
43. **No-Confidence Motion vs Censure Motion:** **Censure Motion** can be moved against an individual minister, group of ministers, or council; must state reasons; council need not resign if passed. **No-Confidence Motion** can ONLY be moved against the **entire Council of Ministers**; need not state reasons; requires support of **50 members** to be admitted; Lok Sabha only; if passed, the entire government **MUST resign immediately**.
44. **Guillotine Procedure:** On the final day allotted for the discussion of Demands for Grants, the Speaker applies the **Guillotine**, putting every outstanding grant to the vote of the House whether debated or not, bringing closure to the financial debate.
45. **Votable vs Non-Votable Financial Outlays:** Expenditures charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India (Art. 112(3)) are **non-votable** (can only be discussed); expenditures made from the Consolidated Fund must be sanctioned through an **Appropriation Act** (Art. 114); advances from the Contingency Fund of India (Art. 267) are sanctioned by the President pending parliamentary replenishment.
46. **Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs):** Established in 1993; total **24 committees** (16 work under Lok Sabha Speaker, 8 work under Rajya Sabha Chairman); each DRSC comprises **31 members** (21 from Lok Sabha and 10 from Rajya Sabha); term is **1 year**; Ministers are barred from membership; scrutinize ministry budget demands and annual performance reports.
47. **Lapsing of Bills on Lok Sabha Dissolution (Article 107):** (1) A bill pending in Lok Sabha lapses; (2) A bill passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha lapses; (3) A bill originating and pending in Rajya Sabha, but NOT passed by Lok Sabha, **DOES NOT LAPSE**; (4) A bill passed by both Houses and awaiting Presidential assent **DOES NOT LAPSE**; (5) A bill returned by President for reconsideration **DOES NOT LAPSE**; (6) A bill where joint sitting was notified by President before dissolution **DOES NOT LAPSE**.
48. **Leader of the Opposition (LoP):** Leader of the largest opposition party having at least **1/10th of the total strength (55 seats in Lok Sabha)**; accorded statutory recognition by the **Salaries and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977**; given rank and salary of a Cabinet Minister; serves on statutory selection committees for CVC, CBI Director, CIC, Lokpal, and NHRC.
49. **Whip Architecture:** Derived from British conventions (not mentioned in Constitution, Rules of Procedure, or Parliamentary Statutes); issued by political parties to enforce party discipline and mandatory voting; defying a three-line whip triggers disqualification under the **10th Schedule (Anti-Defection Law)** unless condoned by party within 15 days.
50. **Parliamentary Majorities Classification:**
51. **Composition of Lok Sabha (Article 81):** Maximum permissible strength originally 552 (530 States + 20 UTs + 2 Anglo-Indians); **104th Amendment Act, 2019** discontinued nomination of 2 Anglo-Indians (Art. 331); current maximum strength is **550 (530 States + 20 UTs)**; current actual strength is 543.
52. **Composition of Rajya Sabha (Article 80):** Maximum strength **250** (238 representatives of States and UTs + 12 nominated by President for expertise in Literature, Science, Art, and Social Service); current actual strength is 245 (229 States + 4 UTs [Delhi 3, Puducherry 1] + 12 Nominated; 4 J&K seats currently vacant); Rajya Sabha is a **permanent chamber** not subject to dissolution; one-third of its members retire every second year (member's tenure is **6 years**).
53. **Qualifications for Parliament Membership (Article 84):** Citizen of India; age not less than **30 years for Rajya Sabha** and not less than **25 years for Lok Sabha**; registered elector in any parliamentary constituency in India (representation in RS does NOT require domicile in that state, upheld in *Kuldip Nayar v. UOI, 2006*).
54. **Disqualification on Ground of Defection (Tenth Schedule):** Decided by the **Speaker/Chairman of the House**; in *Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)*, SC held that the Presiding Officer acts as a tribunal and their decision is subject to **Judicial Review**.
55. **Other Disqualifications (Article 102 & 103):** Holding office of profit under Union/State government, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, alien citizenship; questions under Article 102 are decided by the **President in consultation with the Election Commission of India**, whose opinion is binding.
56. **Speaker of Lok Sabha (Article 93):** Elected by Lok Sabha members from amongst themselves by simple majority; resigns by writing to the **Deputy Speaker** (and vice-versa); when a resolution for removal of Speaker is under consideration, Speaker **cannot preside**, can speak and take part in proceedings, has right to vote in first instance, but CANNOT vote in case of equality of votes (Article 96).
57. **Speaker Pro Tem:** Appointed by the President (usually the senior-most elected member of Lok Sabha) to administer oaths to newly elected MPs and preside over the election of the new Speaker.
58. **Casting Vote of Presiding Officers (Article 100):** Chairman of RS and Speaker of LS do not vote in the first instance; they exercise a **casting vote ONLY in the case of an equality of votes (tie)**.
59. **Quorum in Parliament (Article 100(3)):** Minimum presence required to constitute a meeting of either House is **one-tenth (10%) of the total number of members** of that House (55 in Lok Sabha, 25 in Rajya Sabha).
60. **Sessions of Parliament (Article 85):** President summons each House; maximum gap between two sessions of Parliament cannot exceed **6 months**; Parliament must meet at least twice a year; India holds 3 annual sessions by convention: Budget Session (Feb–May), Monsoon Session (July–Sep), Winter Session (Nov–Dec).
61. **Joint Sitting of Parliament (Article 108):** Summoned by President if a bill passed by one House is rejected by the other, or Houses disagree on amendments, or more than 6 months elapse without passing; Presided over by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha** (in his absence, Deputy Speaker; in his absence, Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha; Chairman of Rajya Sabha NEVER presides); Joint Sitting applies ONLY to **Ordinary Bills and Financial Bills Type II**; it does NOT apply to **Money Bills** (Art. 110) or **Constitutional Amendment Bills** (Art. 368); passed by simple majority of total members present and voting.
62. **Three Historical Joint Sittings in India:** (1) Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961), (2) Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill (1978), and (3) Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA, 2002).
63. **Definition of Money Bill (Article 110):** A bill dealing ONLY with imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any tax; borrowing of money; custody of Consolidated/Contingency Fund; appropriation of moneys out of Consolidated Fund; Speaker's certificate that a bill is a Money Bill is **final and conclusive** and cannot be questioned in any court.
64. **Procedure in Respect of Money Bills (Article 109):** Can be introduced ONLY in **Lok Sabha** and ONLY on **prior recommendation of the President**; cannot be introduced in Rajya Sabha; Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a Money Bill; RS must return it with/without recommendations within **14 days**; if not returned within 14 days, deemed passed by both Houses in the form passed by Lok Sabha.
65. **Financial Bills Classification (Article 117):** **Financial Bill (I) (Art. 117(1)):** Contains matters of Art. 110 plus general legislation; introduced ONLY in Lok Sabha on President's recommendation, but otherwise treated as Ordinary Bill (RS can reject/amend; Joint sitting possible); **Financial Bill (II) (Art. 117(3)):** Involves expenditure from Consolidated Fund; can be introduced in either House without prior recommendation, but requires President's recommendation for consideration/passage in either House.
66. **Budget in Parliament (Article 112):** Referred to in Constitution as **'Annual Financial Statement'**; expenditure is divided into: (a) **Expenditure Charged upon Consolidated Fund** (non-votable; discussed by Parliament; e.g. salaries of President, SC judges, CAG, debt charges), and (b) **Expenditure Made from Consolidated Fund** (votable by Lok Sabha in form of Demands for Grants).
67. **Cut Motions in Budget Scrutiny:** (1) **Policy Cut Motion:** Demands amount be reduced to ₹1 (disapproval of policy); (2) **Economy Cut Motion:** Demands amount be reduced by a specified amount; (3) **Token Cut Motion:** Demands amount be reduced by ₹100 (ventilates a specific grievance).
68. **Guillotine Procedure:** On the last day of allotted days for discussing Demands for Grants, the Speaker puts all remaining undiscovered demands to vote without any further discussion.
69. **Appropriation Bill (Article 114) & Vote on Account (Article 116):** No money can be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund of India except under appropriation made by law (Art. 114); **Vote on Account** grants advance money to government to cover expenditure for a part of the financial year (usually 2 months) pending passage of the full Appropriation Bill.
70. **Consolidated Fund vs Contingency Fund vs Public Account:** (1) **Consolidated Fund of India (Art. 266(1)):** All revenues, loans, and recoveries; operated under Parliamentary appropriation; (2) **Public Account of India (Art. 266(2)):** Provident funds, judicial deposits, savings bank deposits; executive operated without parliamentary vote; (3) **Contingency Fund of India (Art. 267):** ₹500 crore corpus placed at disposal of President to meet unforeseen expenditure pending authorization by Parliament.
71. **Public Accounts Committee (PAC):** Oldest parliamentary financial committee (set up 1921 under GOI Act 1919); consists of **22 members (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha)** elected annually by PR-STV; a Minister CANNOT be elected; Chairman appointed by Speaker from Lok Sabha members (by convention since 1967, Chairman belongs to the **Opposition**); examines appropriation accounts and CAG audit reports; CAG acts as 'friend, philosopher, and guide' of PAC.
72. **Estimates Committee:** Largest Parliamentary Committee; set up in 1950 on recommendation of Finance Minister **John Matthai**; consists of **30 members, ALL exclusively from Lok Sabha** (Rajya Sabha has NO representation); ministers barred from membership; Chairman appointed by Speaker from ruling party; examines estimates to suggest economies and administrative reforms ('Continuous Economy Committee').
73. **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU):** Created in 1964 on recommendation of **Krishna Menon Committee**; consists of **22 members (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha)**; examines reports and accounts of PSUs and CAG commercial audit reports; ministers barred from membership.
74. **Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs):** 24 DRSCs (16 under Lok Sabha Speaker, 8 under Rajya Sabha Chairman); each DRSC consists of **31 members (21 Lok Sabha + 10 Rajya Sabha)**; ministers barred from membership; scrutinize Demands for Grants, examine bills, and consider annual reports.
75. **Key Parliamentary Motions:** **No-Confidence Motion:** Admissible ONLY in Lok Sabha; requires support of at least **50 members**; if passed, Ministry must resign; **Censure Motion:** Can be moved against individual minister or entire ministry in Lok Sabha for specific policies; ministry need not resign if passed; **Calling Attention Motion:** Introduced by an MP to call attention of a minister to an urgent public matter (Indian innovation since 1954); **Adjournment Motion:** Introduced in Lok Sabha only to draw attention to definite matter of urgent public importance involving censure of government; requires support of 50 members.
   - **Simple Majority:** Majority of members present and voting (Ordinary bills, Money bills, No-Confidence, President's Rule, Financial Emergency, Art. 2/3 state formation).
   - **Effective Majority:** Majority of all 'then' members, excluding vacancies (Removal of VP in Rajya Sabha under Art. 67(b), Removal of Speaker/Deputy Speaker under Art. 94(c)).
   - **Special Majority (Art. 249 & 312):** 2/3rd of members present and voting.
   - **Special Majority (Art. 368 & 352):** Majority of total membership + 2/3rd of members present and voting.
   - **Special Majority (Art. 61):** 2/3rd of the **TOTAL MEMBERSHIP** of the House (strictest majority in the Constitution).

### 3. Rules of Lapsing of Bills upon Lok Sabha Dissolution

| Scenario | Does the Bill Lapse? | Constitutional Reason / Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Bill originating and pending in Lok Sabha | **LAPSES** | Lok Sabha dissolved; unfinished business terminates |
| Bill passed by Lok Sabha, pending in Rajya Sabha | **LAPSES** | Passed by expired House, pending in other House |
| Bill originating and pending in Rajya Sabha (not passed by LS) | **DOES NOT LAPSE** | Lok Sabha never considered it; RS is permanent |
| Bill passed by both Houses, awaiting President's assent | **DOES NOT LAPSE** | Both Houses completed legislative action |
| Bill returned by President for reconsideration | **DOES NOT LAPSE** | Passed by both Houses already; reconsidered by new LS |
| Joint sitting notified by President before dissolution | **DOES NOT LAPSE** | Presidential notification under Art. 108 supersedes dissolution |

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

1. **Estimates Committee — The All-Lok Sabha Body:**
   - Mnemonic: **"Estimates = Exclusively Lok Sabha (30)"**
   - Rajya Sabha is completely shut out of Estimates because estimates deal with taxation and expenditure grants, which are the exclusive prerogative of the popular house.
2. **Three Financial Committees Member Counts:**
   - **PAC:** 15 + 7 = **22**
   - **Estimates:** 30 + 0 = **30**
   - **COPU:** 15 + 7 = **22**
   - Memory Rule: "22, 30, 22 — No Ministers allowed in any of the three!"
3. **Bill Lapsing Logic on Dissolution:**
   - **"If Lok Sabha has touched it, it DIES. If Lok Sabha has never seen it (pending in RS), it LIVES!"**
   - If both Houses finished passing it, it LIVES (President's table).
4. **Joint Sitting Presiding Officer Succession:**
   - **Speaker &rarr; Deputy Speaker &rarr; Deputy Chairman of RS.**
   - *Chairman of Rajya Sabha is NOT an MP (he is the Vice-President of India), hence cannot preside over MPs assembled together!*
