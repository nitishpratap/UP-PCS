## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. Under **Article 79**, the Parliament of India consists of the **President**, the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**, and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**; although not a member of either House, the President is an integral part of Parliament because no bill can become law without presidential assent.
2. **Rajya Sabha (Article 80):** Maximum constitutionally sanctioned strength is **250** (up to 238 representatives of States and UTs, and **12 nominated by the President** for special knowledge/practical experience in **Literature, Science, Art, and Social Service**).
3. Current Rajya Sabha strength is **245** (225 States, 8 UTs: Delhi 3, Puducherry 1, J&K 4; and 12 Nominated); members are elected by the **elected members of State Legislative Assemblies** through **PR-STV**; voting is by **open ballot** (introduced in 2003 to curb cross-voting).
4. Rajya Sabha is a **permanent body** not subject to dissolution (**Article 83(1)**); one-third of its members retire every second year; each member serves a **term of 6 years** (prescribed by the Representation of the People Act, 1951, NOT by the Constitution).
5. **Lok Sabha (Article 81):** Originally maximum strength was 500; raised to 552; with the discontinuation of 2 nominated Anglo-Indian seats by the **104th Amendment Act, 2019**, maximum strength is **550** (530 from States + 20 from UTs); current active strength is **543** (all directly elected from single-member territorial constituencies).
6. Term of Lok Sabha (**Article 83(2)**): Normal term is **5 years** from date appointed for its first meeting; can be dissolved earlier by the President; during a National Emergency (Art. 352), Parliament can extend the term of Lok Sabha by law for a period not exceeding **1 year at a time**, and not extending beyond 6 months after the emergency proclamation has ceased to operate.
7. Qualifications for MP (**Article 84**): Citizen of India; not less than **30 years of age for Rajya Sabha**, and not less than **25 years of age for Lok Sabha**; subscribed oath before person authorized by Election Commission.
8. Disqualifications of MP (**Article 102**): (a) Holds office of profit; (b) Unsound mind; (c) Undischarged insolvent; (d) Ceases to be Indian citizen; (e) Disqualified under any law made by Parliament (e.g., RPA 1951 for corrupt electoral practices, imprisonment for ≥ 2 years). Decisions under Art. 102 are taken by the **President in accordance with the opinion of the Election Commission** (**Article 103**).
9. Disqualification on Ground of Defection (**Tenth Schedule**): Decided by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha** or **Chairman of Rajya Sabha**; under *Kihoto Hollohan (1992)*, this decision is subject to judicial review.
10. Vacation of Seats (**Article 101**): If an MP is absent from all meetings of a House without its permission for a period of **60 days**, the House may declare his seat vacant (excluding periods of prorogation or adjournment > 4 consecutive days).
11. **Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha (Article 93):** Elected by the Lok Sabha from amongst its members by simple majority; date of election of Speaker is fixed by the **President**, while date of election of Deputy Speaker is fixed by the **Speaker**.
12. Tenure of Speaker (**Article 94**): Remains in office from election until immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha after dissolution; resigns by writing to the **Deputy Speaker** (and Deputy Speaker resigns to the Speaker); removed by a resolution passed by an **Effective Majority** (majority of all the then members of Lok Sabha) after 14 days' notice.
13. Powers of Speaker: Presides over joint sittings of Parliament (Art. 108); certifies whether a bill is a **Money Bill** and his decision is final (**Art. 110(3)**); exercises a **Casting Vote** under Article 100(1) in case of an equality of votes; appoints chairmen of all parliamentary committees.
14. **Pro Tem Speaker (Speaker pro tempore):** Appointed by the President (usually the seniormost member of Lok Sabha) to administer oaths to newly elected MPs and preside over the election of the permanent Speaker; office lapses once the Speaker is elected.
15. **Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Article 89):** The **Vice-President of India** is ex-officio Chairman; does not hold membership of Rajya Sabha; can be removed from office only by removing him as Vice-President; has a casting vote in case of a tie; *cannot preside over a joint sitting*.
16. **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha:** Elected by the Rajya Sabha from amongst its members; resigns by writing to the Chairman; removed by effective majority of Rajya Sabha.
17. **Sessions of Parliament (Article 85):** The President summons each House; maximum gap between two sessions of Parliament **cannot exceed 6 months** (Parliament must meet at least twice a year); typically 3 sessions annually: **Budget Session** (Feb–May), **Monsoon Session** (July–Aug/Sept), **Winter Session** (Nov–Dec).
18. Adjournment vs Prorogation vs Dissolution: **Adjournment** suspends sitting for hours/days/weeks (done by Presiding Officer); **Adjournment sine die** terminates sitting indefinitely (by Presiding Officer); **Prorogation** terminates a session (by President under Art. 85(2)); **Dissolution** ends the very life of Lok Sabha (by President; Rajya Sabha never dissolved).
19. Lapse of Bills on Dissolution (**Article 107**): A Bill pending in Lok Sabha lapses; a Bill passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha lapses; a Bill originating in Rajya Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha (not passed by Lok Sabha) **DOES NOT LAPSE**; a Bill passed by both Houses and awaiting Presidential assent **DOES NOT LAPSE**; a Bill returned by President for reconsideration **DOES NOT LAPSE**; a Bill for which President has notified a Joint Sitting prior to dissolution **DOES NOT LAPSE**.
20. **Quorum (Article 100(3)):** Minimum number of members required to be present to conduct a meeting: **1/10th of the total membership of the House** (**55 members in Lok Sabha**, **25 members in Rajya Sabha**); if no quorum, presiding officer must adjourn or suspend meeting.
21. **Language in Parliament (Article 120):** Business transacted in **Hindi or English**; Presiding Officer may permit any member who cannot adequately express himself in Hindi/English to address the House in his mother tongue.
22. Parliamentary Hours: **Question Hour** is the first hour of a sitting (11:00 AM – 12:00 PM); **Starred Questions** (marked with asterisk; require oral answers; supplementary questions permitted); **Unstarred Questions** (require written answers; no supplementaries); **Short Notice Questions** (urgent matter; < 10 days' notice).
23. **Zero Hour:** Follows Question Hour immediately (starts at 12:00 PM); an **informal Indian parliamentary innovation since 1962**; not mentioned in the Rules of Procedure; members raise urgent matters without prior notice.
24. Types of Motions: **Adjournment Motion** (draws attention to definite matter of urgent public importance; Lok Sabha only; needs support of 50 members; debate lasts ≥ 2.5 hours; involves censure of government); **No-Confidence Motion** (Art. 75; Lok Sabha only; needs support of 50 members; no grounds needed; government falls if passed); **Censure Motion** (Lok Sabha only; specific policy/minister censure; government need not resign if passed); **Calling Attention Motion** (Indian innovation since 1954; calls minister's attention to urgent matter).
25. **Cut Motions (Budgetary Scrutiny):** Moved only in Lok Sabha during Demands for Grants: (1) **Disapproval of Policy Cut** ('that the amount of demand be reduced to ₹1' - represents disapproval of underlying policy); (2) **Economy Cut** ('that amount of demand be reduced by a specified amount'); (3) **Token Cut** ('that amount of demand be reduced by ₹100' - ventilates specific grievance). Passage of a Cut Motion implies lack of confidence and the government must resign.
26. **Legislative Procedure — Ordinary Bills (Articles 107 & 108):** Can be introduced in either House; passes through 3 readings; if deadlocked (rejected by other House, disagreement on amendments, or passed > 6 months without action), President may summon a **Joint Sitting**.
27. **Joint Sitting of Parliament (Article 108):** Summoned by the President; presided over by: (1) **Speaker of Lok Sabha**, (2) Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha, (3) Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha, (4) Person chosen by members present; *Chairman of Rajya Sabha NEVER presides*; bill decided by **Simple Majority of total members present and voting**; only 3 Joint Sittings held in history: Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961), Banking Service Commission Bill (1978), and POTA Bill (2002).
28. **Money Bills (Article 110):** A bill containing *only* provisions dealing with taxation, borrowing, Consolidated/Contingency Funds, audit of accounts; introduced **only in Lok Sabha** with prior Presidential recommendation; Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend, can only make recommendations within **14 days**; if not returned in 14 days, deemed passed; *NO joint sitting for Money Bills*.
29. **Financial Bills (Article 117):** **Financial Bill (I) [Art. 117(1)]** contains matters of Art. 110 plus general legislation; introduced only in Lok Sabha with prior presidential recommendation, but otherwise treated as an Ordinary Bill (can be rejected by RS; joint sitting applicable); **Financial Bill (II) [Art. 117(3)]** involves expenditure from Consolidated Fund; introduced in either House, but cannot be passed by either House without presidential recommendation.
30. **Annual Financial Statement - Budget (Article 112):** The term 'Budget' is not used in the Constitution; the President causes it to be laid before both Houses; contains estimated receipts and expenditure; divided into **Expenditure Charged upon Consolidated Fund** (non-votable, can only be discussed) and **Expenditure Made from Consolidated Fund** (votable Demands for Grants).
31. Expenditure Charged on Consolidated Fund of India (**Article 112(3)**): Emoluments of President; salaries/allowances of Chairman/Deputy Chairman of RS and Speaker/Deputy Speaker of LS; salaries, allowances, and **pensions of Supreme Court Judges**; **PENSIONS ONLY of High Court Judges** (*salaries of HC judges are charged on State Consolidated Fund!*); salary and pension of CAG; debt charges of GOI.
32. **Appropriation Bill (Article 114):** No money can be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund of India except under appropriation made by law passed by Parliament; legalizes expenditure approved under Demands for Grants.
33. **Vote on Account (Article 116(a)):** Grant in advance to the executive to meet expenditure for a part of the financial year pending the passage of the Appropriation Bill (usually 2 months' grant; with Union Budget advanced to 1 February since 2017, Vote on Account is now rarely needed).
34. **Funds of the Union (Articles 266 & 267):** (1) **Consolidated Fund of India (Art. 266(1))**: all revenues, loans, and loan recoveries; withdrawals require parliamentary law; (2) **Public Account of India (Art. 266(2))**: provident funds, judicial deposits, remittances; executive operated; (3) **Contingency Fund of India (Art. 267)**: corpus held by Finance Secretary on behalf of President for unforeseen expenditure pending parliamentary authorization (corpus raised from ₹500 crore to ₹30,000 crore).
35. **Parliamentary Financial Committees:** (1) **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)**: created in 1921 under GOI 1919; **22 members (15 LS + 7 RS)**; elected by PR-STV; term 1 yr; minister cannot be member; examines CAG audit reports; by convention since 1967, Chairman is from **Opposition**; known as 'twin sister' of Estimates Committee.
36. (2) **Estimates Committee:** Created in 1950 on recommendation of John Mathai; **30 members, ALL FROM LOK SABHA ONLY** (Rajya Sabha has zero representation; largest parliamentary committee); term 1 yr; examines estimates to suggest economies in expenditure; nicknamed **'Continuous Economy Committee'**.
37. (3) **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU):** Created in 1964 on recommendation of Krishna Menon Committee; **22 members (15 LS + 7 RS)**; term 1 yr; examines reports and accounts of public sector undertakings (PSUs) and CAG audit reports on PSUs.
38. Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs): Introduced in 1993; currently **24 DRSCs** (16 work under Lok Sabha Speaker, 8 under Rajya Sabha Chairman); each committee has **31 members (21 from Lok Sabha + 10 from Rajya Sabha)**; examine Demands for Grants, bills, and annual reports of ministries.
39. **Exclusive Special Powers of Rajya Sabha:** (1) **Article 249**: Can pass a resolution by 2/3rd majority present and voting empowering Parliament to legislate on a **State List subject** in national interest for 1 year; (2) **Article 312**: Can pass a resolution by 2/3rd majority present and voting to create new **All-India Services**; (3) **Article 67(b)**: Vice-President removal resolution can originate ONLY in Rajya Sabha; (4) Approval of Emergency when Lok Sabha is dissolved (**Arts. 352, 356, 360**).
40. **Parliamentary Privileges (Article 105):** Collective privileges (right to publish debates, exclude strangers, punish for contempt of House) and Individual privileges (freedom of speech in Parliament, **freedom from arrest in civil cases 40 days before and 40 days after session**; *no immunity from arrest in criminal cases or preventive detention*).
