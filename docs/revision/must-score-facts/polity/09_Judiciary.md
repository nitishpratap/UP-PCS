## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. India has a **single integrated judicial system** adopted from the **Government of India Act, 1935**; the Supreme Court stands at the apex of the entire judicial system, followed by High Courts at the State level and Subordinate Courts below them.
2. The single integrated judicial system enforces **both Central and State laws** simultaneously (unlike the USA, where federal courts enforce federal laws and state courts enforce state laws).
3. **Supreme Court Inauguration:** The Supreme Court of India was inaugurated on **28 January 1950**; succeeded the **Federal Court of India** (established under GOI Act 1935) and replaced the Privy Council as the highest court of appeal.
4. **Articles 124 to 147 in Part V** of the Constitution deal with the organization, independence, jurisdiction, powers, and procedures of the Supreme Court of India.
5. **Strength of Supreme Court (Article 124(1)):** Originally fixed at **8 judges** (1 Chief Justice + 7 other judges) in 1950; Parliament is empowered to increase the number of judges by law; increased to 11 (1956), 14 (1960), 18 (1977), 26 (1986), 31 (2008), and currently stands at **34 judges (CJI + 33 judges)** under the 2019 amendment.
6. **Appointment of Judges (Article 124(2)):** Every judge of the Supreme Court is appointed by the **President by warrant under his hand and seal**; in appointing judges other than the CJI, the Chief Justice of India **MUST always be consulted**.
7. **Appointment of Chief Justice of India:** Appointed by the President; by convention since 1973/1993, the **senior-most judge of the Supreme Court** is appointed as the CJI (superseded in 1973 with A.N. Ray and in 1977 with M.U. Beg; convention made binding by Second Judges Case, 1993).
8. **The Three Judges Cases (Collegium Evolution):**
   - ***First Judges Case (S.P. Gupta, 1981):*** SC held that 'consultation' does not mean 'concurrence'; executive has primacy in judicial appointments.
   - ***Second Judges Case (1993):*** SC overruled *S.P. Gupta*; held that 'consultation' means **'concurrence'**; CJI's opinion has primacy; CJI must consult **two senior-most colleagues**.
   - ***Third Judges Case (1998):*** Presidential reference under Art. 143; SC expanded Collegium: CJI must consult a collegium of **FOUR senior-most judges of the Supreme Court**; sole opinion of CJI without consulting collegium does not constitute 'consultation'.
9. **Qualifications of Supreme Court Judge (Article 124(3)):** Must be a citizen of India; AND:
   - (a) Has been a **Judge of a High Court for at least 5 years**; OR
   - (b) Has been an **Advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years**; OR
   - (c) Is, in the opinion of the President, a **distinguished jurist**.
   - *(Note: No minimum age is prescribed in the Constitution for appointment as a judge!)*.
10. **Oath of Supreme Court Judge (Article 124(6)):** Administered by the **President** (or some person appointed by him); contained in the **Third Schedule**.
11. **Tenure of Supreme Court Judge (Article 124(2)):** Holds office until he attains the **age of 65 years**; resigns by addressing letter to the **President**.
12. **Removal of Judges (Article 124(4) & (5)):** Removed by order of the President passed after an address by each House of Parliament supported by a **Special Majority** (majority of total membership of the House + not less than 2/3rd of members present and voting) in the **same session**; sole grounds of removal: **'proved misbehaviour'** or **'incapacity'**.
13. **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968 Procedure:** Removal motion signed by **100 Lok Sabha members or 50 Rajya Sabha members**; admitted by Speaker/Chairman; 3-member inquiry committee (CJI/SC judge + Chief Justice of HC + distinguished jurist); if found guilty, motion debated and voted upon. *No Supreme Court judge has ever been removed from office* (Justice V. Ramaswami in 1993 faced motion but Congress abstained).
14. **Salaries and Allowances (Article 125):** Determined by Parliament; **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** (non-votable); cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment, except during a **Financial Emergency** (Article 360).
15. **Post-Retirement Ban on Practice (Article 124(7)):** A retired Supreme Court judge is **completely prohibited from pleading or acting in any court or before any authority** within the territory of India.
16. **Acting Chief Justice (Article 126):** Appointed by the **President** from amongst SC judges when the office of CJI is vacant or CJI is unable to perform duties.
17. **Ad Hoc Judges (Article 127):** Appointed by the **Chief Justice of India with prior consent of the President** and after consultation with the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court, when there is a lack of **quorum** of permanent judges to hold or continue any session; must be qualified to be an SC judge.
18. **Seat of Supreme Court (Article 130):** The Constitution declares **Delhi** as the seat of the Supreme Court; the CJI is authorized to appoint other place or places as seat of SC, but **ONLY with the approval of the President**.
19. **Original Jurisdiction (Article 131):** Exclusive jurisdiction to decide federal disputes between:
    - (a) Government of India and one or more States;
    - (b) Government of India and any State(s) on one side and one or more other States on the other;
    - (c) Two or more States.
    - Dispute must involve any question on which the existence or extent of a **legal right** depends.
20. **Exclusions from Article 131:** (1) Pre-constitutional treaties, agreements, covenants; (2) Inter-state river water disputes (Art. 262); (3) Matters referred to Finance Commission (Art. 280); (4) Ordinary commercial disputes between Centre and States.
21. **Writ Jurisdiction (Article 32):** Supreme Court is the **guarantor and defender of Fundamental Rights**; can issue writs of Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo-Warranto; limited ONLY to enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
22. **Appellate Jurisdiction (Articles 132–134):**
    - **Constitutional Matters (Art. 132):** Appeal lies if High Court certifies that the case involves a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution.
    - **Civil Matters (Art. 133):** Substantial question of law of general importance needing SC decision.
    - **Criminal Matters (Art. 134):** Appeal lies as of right if High Court reversed acquittal and sentenced accused to death, or withdrew case from lower court and sentenced to death.
23. **Special Leave Petition (SLP, Article 136):** Extraordinary discretionary power; SC can grant special leave to appeal against **ANY judgment, decree, sentence, or order in any cause or matter passed by any court or tribunal in India**; exception: does NOT apply to military tribunals/court-martials.
24. **Advisory Jurisdiction (Article 143):** President can refer any question to the Supreme Court for its opinion:
    - (1) **Question of law or fact of public importance:** SC **may or may not give opinion** (discretionary; e.g., Ayodhya Reference 1993 where SC declined).
    - (2) **Dispute arising out of pre-constitutional treaties/agreements:** SC **MUST give its opinion** (obligatory).
    - In both cases, the opinion of the Supreme Court is **only advisory and NOT binding** on the President.
25. **Court of Record (Article 129):** (1) Judgments, proceedings, and acts of the Supreme Court are recorded for perpetual memory and testimony; have evidentiary value and cannot be questioned in any court; (2) Has the **power to punish for contempt of itself** with simple imprisonment up to 6 months or fine up to ₹2,000 or both.
26. **Power of Judicial Review (Articles 13, 32, 136, 142):** Power to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders of both Central and State governments; basic feature of the Constitution (*Indira Gandhi, 1975*; *Minerva Mills, 1980*).
27. **Complete Justice Power (Article 142):** SC can pass any decree or order necessary for doing **"complete justice"** in any cause or matter pending before it; entirely enforceable throughout India.
28. **Review of Judgments (Article 137):** Supreme Court is **NOT bound by its own previous decisions** and has the power to review any judgment pronounced or order made by it (e.g., *Kesavananda* overruled *Golaknath*; *Maneka Gandhi* overruled *Gopalan*); exercises review through **Curative Petitions** (*Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra, 2002*).
29. **High Courts in the States (Part VI, Articles 214–231):** Article 214 provides for a High Court for each State; **Article 231** empowers Parliament by law to establish a **common High Court for two or more States or UTs**.
30. Total High Courts: India has **25 High Courts**; oldest is **Calcutta High Court (1862)**; newest is **Andhra Pradesh High Court (2019 at Amaravati)**; three common High Courts:
    - **Bombay HC:** Maharashtra, Goa, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu.
    - **Guwahati HC:** Assam, Nagaland, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh.
    - **Punjab & Haryana HC:** Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh.
31. High Court with the **largest number of judges**: **Allahabad High Court** (sanctioned strength 160); established 1866; principal seat at Prayagraj, permanent bench at Lucknow.
32. **Appointment of High Court Judges (Article 217):** Appointed by the **President by warrant under his hand and seal**; appointed after consultation with: (1) Chief Justice of India; (2) Governor of the State; (3) Chief Justice of the High Court (in case of other judges).
33. **Qualifications of High Court Judge (Article 217(2)):** Citizen of India; AND:
    - (a) Has held a **judicial office in India for at least 10 years**; OR
    - (b) Has been an **advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years**.
    - *(Note: Unlike the Supreme Court, there is NO provision for appointing a 'distinguished jurist' as a High Court judge!)*.
34. **Tenure of High Court Judge (Article 217(1)):** Holds office until the **age of 62 years** (raised from 60 to 62 by 15th Amd 1963; SC is 65); resigns by writing to the **President**.
35. **Removal of High Court Judge:** Removed from office by the **President** in the same manner and on the **same grounds as a Supreme Court Judge** (Address by both Houses by special majority for proved misbehaviour or incapacity).
36. **Transfer of High Court Judges (Article 222):** President can transfer a judge from one High Court to another after consultation with the **Chief Justice of India** (CJI consults collegium of 4 senior-most SC judges + Chief Justices of both High Courts).
37. **Oath of High Court Judge (Article 219):** Administered by the **Governor of the State** (or person appointed by him).
38. **Writ Jurisdiction of High Court (Article 226):** Can issue prerogative writs for enforcement of **Fundamental Rights AND for any other legal purpose**; wider subject scope than Article 32; can issue writs against authorities outside territorial jurisdiction if cause of action arises within territory.
39. **Power of Superintendence (Article 227):** High Court has superintendence over **all courts and tribunals throughout the territories** in relation to which it exercises jurisdiction; covers both administrative and judicial superintendence; does NOT apply to military tribunals/court-martials.
40. **Subordinate Courts (Articles 233–237):**
    - **District Judges** appointed, posted, and promoted by the **Governor in consultation with the High Court** (Art. 233);
    - Persons other than district judges appointed to judicial service by Governor in accordance with rules made in consultation with SPSC and High Court (Art. 234);
    - **Control over subordinate courts** (posting, promotion, leave) is vested exclusively in the **High Court** (Article 235).
41. When trying civil cases, the presiding officer is called the **District Judge**; when trying criminal cases, called the **Sessions Judge**; Sessions Judge has power to impose any sentence including **capital punishment (death sentence)**, but death sentence **MUST be confirmed by the High Court** before execution.
42. **Twenty-Five High Courts Geographic Distribution:** Three UTs have their own High Courts or special standing: Delhi (own HC since 1966), Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh (common HC).
43. **Calcutta High Court Historical Primacy:** Established on 1 July 1862 under High Courts Act, 1861; followed by Bombay High Court (August 1862) and Madras High Court (August 1862).
44. **Allahabad High Court Origin:** Established under the Indian High Courts Act 1861 at Agra on 11 June 1866; shifted to Allahabad in 1869; Sir Walter Morgan was its first Chief Justice.
45. **Collegium Composition Difference:** For Supreme Court elevation: **CJI + 4 senior-most SC judges**. For High Court appointments & transfers: **CJI + 2 senior-most SC judges** (along with consulting judges familiar with that HC).
46. **Non-Justiciable Matters under Article 131:** Water disputes under Article 262 are completely excluded from the jurisdiction of all courts, including the Supreme Court; handled by statutory Inter-State Water Dispute Tribunals.
47. **Special Leave Petition Broadest Canvas:** Article 136 is a residual and discretionary power; does not confer a right of appeal, but confers a right to apply for leave to appeal; exercised in exceptional circumstances where grave injustice has occurred.
48. **Article 143 Landmark References:** In *re Berubari Union (1960)*, *re Sea Customs Act (1963)*, *re Special Courts Bill (1978)*, *re Ayodhya (1993)*, *re 2G Spectrum Allocation (2012)*.
49. **Bhopal Gas Leak and Article 142:** Supreme Court bypassed statutory ceilings under the Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster Act to award $470 million in compensation by invoking its extraordinary powers under Article 142.
50. **Subordinate Judicial Hierarchy Hierarchy:** District Court &rarr; Senior Civil Judge / Chief Judicial Magistrate &rarr; Junior Civil Judge / Judicial Magistrate Second Class. At the rural grassroots level, **Gram Nyayalayas** function under the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008.
