## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Part XVII (Articles 343–351)** of the Constitution contains the provisions relating to Official Language; divided into 4 Chapters: Chapter I (Language of the Union), Chapter II (Regional Languages), Chapter III (Language of the Supreme Court and High Courts), Chapter IV (Special Directives).
2. **Official Language of the Union (Article 343(1)):** Declares that the **Official Language of the Union shall be Hindi in Devanagari script**; the form of numerals to be used for the official purposes of the Union shall be the **international form of Indian numerals** (1, 2, 3...).
3. **Transition Period for English (Article 343(2)):** For a period of **15 years** from the commencement of the Constitution (i.e., until 26 January 1965), the **English language** was to continue to be used for all official purposes of the Union for which it was being used before 1950.
4. **Parliament's Power to Extend English (Article 343(3)):** Parliament enacted the **Official Languages Act, 1963** (amended in 1967), which provided that English **MAY continue to be used indefinitely**, in addition to Hindi, for official purposes of the Union and for transaction of business in Parliament.
5. **Commission on Official Language (Article 344(1)):** Mandated the President to appoint an Official Language Commission at the end of 5 years (1955) and 10 years (1960); first Commission appointed in **1955 under B.G. Kher** (Kher Commission submitted report in 1956).
6. **Committee of Parliament on Official Language (Article 344(4)):** Constituted under Section 4 of Official Languages Act, 1963; consists of **30 members (20 from Lok Sabha and 10 from Rajya Sabha)** elected by proportional representation by single transferable vote (**PR-STV**); by convention, chaired by the **Union Home Minister**.
7. **Regional Languages (Articles 345–347):** State Legislature can adopt **any one or more languages in use in the State or Hindi** as the official language of that State (Art. 345); until then, English continues.
8. **Language for Inter-State Communication (Article 346):** Language authorized for use in the Union for official purposes shall be the official language for communication between States and between Union and States (English); two or more States may agree that Hindi shall be the official language of communication between them.
9. **Language of Supreme Court and High Courts (Article 348(1)):** Until Parliament by law otherwise provides:
   - (a) All proceedings in the **Supreme Court and in every High Court**;
   - (b) Authoritative texts of all **Bills, Acts, Ordinances, Orders, Rules, and Regulations** at the Union and State levels, **SHALL BE IN THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE**.
10. **Use of Hindi in High Courts (Article 348(2)):** The **Governor** of a State may, with the **PRIOR CONSENT OF THE PRESIDENT**, authorize the use of Hindi or other official language of the State in proceedings of the High Court (except for judgments, decrees, and orders, which must still be accompanied by an authoritative English translation).
11. **Language of Representations for Redress of Grievances (Article 350):** Every person is entitled to submit a representation for the redress of any grievance to any officer or authority of the Union or a State in **ANY OF THE LANGUAGES used in the Union or in the State**.
12. **Instruction in Mother Tongue at Primary Stage (Article 350A):** Inserted by the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**; places a constitutional duty on every State and local authority to provide adequate facilities for instruction in the **mother tongue at the primary stage** of education to children belonging to linguistic minority groups.
13. **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (Article 350B):** Inserted by the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956** on recommendation of States Reorganisation Commission; appointed by the **President**; investigates all matters relating to safeguards provided for linguistic minorities; submits annual reports to the President, which are laid before each House of Parliament and sent to State Governments; assisted by a Commissioner headquartered at **Prayagraj (Allahabad)**.
14. **Directive for Development of the Hindi Language (Article 351):** Duty of the Union to promote the spread of Hindi, to develop it so that it may serve as a medium of expression for all elements of the composite culture of India, drawing primarily on **Sanskrit** and secondarily on other languages.
15. **Eighth Schedule Languages:** Originally contained **14 languages** in 1950; through subsequent amendments, it now contains **22 languages**:
    - **Original 14 Languages (1950):** Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu.
    - **21st Amendment Act, 1967:** Added **Sindhi** (15th language).
    - **71st Amendment Act, 1992:** Added **Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali** (KMN — 18 languages).
    - **92nd Amendment Act, 2003:** Added **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** (BDMS — 22 languages).
    - *(96th Amendment Act, 2011 renamed 'Oriya' as 'Odia')*.
16. **Languages NOT in the Eighth Schedule:** **English, Bhojpuri, and Rajasthani** are notably ABSENT from the Eighth Schedule.
17. **Classical Languages of India (Total 11):**
    - 2004: **Tamil** (first language to be accorded classical status)
    - 2005: **Sanskrit**
    - 2008: **Telugu** and **Kannada**
    - 2013: **Malayalam**
    - 2014: **Odia**
    - 2024 (October 3): **Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, and Bengali** approved by Union Cabinet.
18. **Criteria for Classical Language Status:** (1) High antiquity of early texts/recorded history over 1,500–2,000 years; (2) Body of ancient literature considered valuable heritage; (3) Original literary tradition not borrowed from another speech community.
19. **Part XXI (Articles 369–392):** Titled 'Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions'; contains asymmetric federalism provisions for specific States to meet regional aspirations, protect tribal traditions, and maintain law and order.
20. **Article 370 (Jammu and Kashmir — Inoperative):** Conferred temporary autonomous status; on **5–6 August 2019**, Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Orders (C.O. 272 and C.O. 273) rendered all clauses of Article 370 inoperative, extending the entire Indian Constitution to J&K; J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 bifurcated State into UT of J&K and UT of Ladakh; upheld unanimously by SC in December 2023.
21. **Article 371 (Maharashtra and Gujarat):** Empowers the President to authorize the Governor to establish **separate development boards** for Vidarbha, Marathwada, and the rest of Maharashtra, and for Saurashtra, Kutch, and the rest of Gujarat; equitable allocation of development funds and technical education facilities.
22. **Article 371A (Nagaland, 13th Amendment 1962):** No Act of Parliament in respect of: (1) Religious or social practices of Nagas; (2) Naga customary law and procedure; (3) Administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to Naga customary law; (4) Ownership and transfer of land and its resources, shall apply to Nagaland **unless the Legislative Assembly of Nagaland so decides by a resolution**. Special responsibility of Governor for law and order.
23. **Article 371B (Assam, 22nd Amendment 1969):** President may provide for the constitution and functions of a committee of the Legislative Assembly consisting of members elected from the tribal areas specified in the Sixth Schedule.
24. **Article 371C (Manipur, 27th Amendment 1971):** President may provide for the constitution and functions of a committee of the Legislative Assembly consisting of members elected from the **Hill Areas** of Manipur; Governor submits annual report to President.
25. **Article 371D & 371E (Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, 32nd Amendment 1973):** Empowers President to provide equitable opportunities and facilities for people of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in public employment and education; creation of Administrative Tribunal for public service disputes; **Article 371E** provides for establishment of a **Central University in Andhra Pradesh** by parliamentary law.
26. **Article 371F (Sikkim, 36th Amendment 1975):** Legislative Assembly of Sikkim to consist of not less than **32 members**; Governor has special responsibility for peace and equitable social/economic advancement; laws in force prior to accession protected.
27. **Article 371G (Mizoram, 53rd Amendment 1986):** Similar to Nagaland; Acts of Parliament regarding religious/social practices, customary law, and land ownership do NOT apply unless Mizoram Assembly resolves; Legislative Assembly to consist of not less than **40 members**.
28. **Article 371H (Arunachal Pradesh, 55th Amendment 1986):** Governor has **special responsibility with respect to law and order** in Arunachal Pradesh (acts in individual judgment on advice of CoM; ceases when President directs); Assembly to consist of not less than **30 members**.
29. **Article 371-I (Goa, 56th Amendment 1987):** Legislative Assembly of Goa shall consist of not less than **30 members**.
30. **Article 371-J (Karnataka, 98th Amendment 2012):** Provides for separate development board for **Hyderabad-Karnataka region** (Kalyana-Karnataka: 6 backward districts — Bidar, Ballari, Kalaburagi, Koppal, Raichur, Yadgir); equitable allocation of funds, reservation in education and state government jobs for local cadre.
31. **Part X (Articles 244 and 244A — Scheduled and Tribal Areas):**
    - **Article 244(1):** Provisions of the **Fifth Schedule** apply to the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in States other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram.
    - **Article 244(2):** Provisions of the **Sixth Schedule** apply to the administration of Tribal Areas in the four North-Eastern States: **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram (AMTM)**.
32. **Fifth Schedule Administration:**
    - Executive power of the Union extends to giving directions to the State regarding administration of Scheduled Areas;
    - Governor submits **annual reports to the President** regarding administration;
    - **Tribes Advisory Council (TAC):** Mandatorily established in each State having Scheduled Areas; consists of not more than **20 members**, of whom three-fourths (3/4th) must be representatives of Scheduled Tribes in the State Legislative Assembly;
    - Governor is empowered to direct that any Act of Parliament or State Legislature **shall not apply to a Scheduled Area** or shall apply with exceptions and modifications; can make regulations prohibiting or restricting transfer of land by or among STs.
33. **Sixth Schedule Administration (AMTM):**
    - Tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram are constituted as **Autonomous District Councils (ADCs)** and **Regional Councils**;
    - Does NOT operate like a separate state, but exercises extensive autonomous administrative and legislative powers;
    - Each District Council consists of not more than **30 members** (4 nominated by Governor, 26 elected on universal adult franchise for a term of 5 years);
    - ADCs can make laws on land, forests, canal water, shifting cultivation (jhum), village administration, inheritance of property, marriage, and social customs; laws require **assent of the Governor**;
    - Can constitute village councils or courts for trial of suits and cases between tribals; can establish primary schools, dispensaries, markets; can collect land revenue and levy taxes.
34. **Sixth Schedule States Exclusion Trap:** The Sixth Schedule applies strictly to **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram (AMTM)**; **Manipur and Nagaland are NOT in the Sixth Schedule** (they are governed by special provisions under Arts. 371C and 371A).
35. **Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities Headquarters:** Headquarters at **Prayagraj (Allahabad)**; regional offices at Belgaum, Chennai, and Kolkata; operates under administrative control of **Ministry of Minority Affairs**.
36. **Sindhi Language Addition Year:** Added by the **21st Amendment Act, 1967** (first language added after Constitution's commencement).
37. **71st Amendment (1992) Languages:** Added **Konkani, Manipuri, and Nepali** (KMN).
38. **92nd Amendment (2003) Languages:** Added **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, and Santhali** (BDMS).
39. **Sanskrit Classical Status:** Declared in **2005**, following Tamil in 2004.
40. **Section 3(3) of Official Languages Act, 1963:** Makes bilingualism statutory; both Hindi and English must be used for resolutions, general orders, rules, notifications, administrative reports, press communiques, contracts, agreements, licenses, and tender notices.
41. **Article 344 Majority Requirement:** Parliamentary Committee on Official Language consists of 30 MPs elected by PR-STV; submits recommendations to President, who after consulting State governments, issues directions.
42. **Authoritative English Text Mandate:** Even if a State Legislature passes a Bill or Act in Hindi or another regional language, Article 348(3) mandates that an **authoritative translation in English published in the Official Gazette** is compulsory.
43. **Article 370 Historic Architecture:** Drafted by N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar; included in Part XXI as a 'temporary provision'; exempted J&K from the general provisions of the Indian Constitution, limiting Parliament's power to Defence, Foreign Affairs, and Communications without state government concurrence.
44. **Autonomous District Councils Count:** Currently 10 ADCs function under the Sixth Schedule: 3 in Assam (Bodoland, Karbi Anglong, Dima Hasao), 3 in Meghalaya (Khasi, Garo, Jaintia), 1 in Tripura (Tripura Tribal Areas), and 3 in Mizoram (Chakma, Mara, Lai).
45. **Tribes Advisory Council in Non-Scheduled States:** A TAC can also be established in any State having Scheduled Tribes but no Scheduled Areas, if the President so directs (e.g., West Bengal and Tamil Nadu).
46. **Governor's Legislative Role in 5th Schedule:** The Governor is uniquely empowered to repeal or amend any Act of Parliament or of the State Legislature in its application to a Scheduled Area, subject to prior approval of the President.
47. **Classic Languages Benefits:** The Union Ministry of Education provides two major annual international awards for scholars of eminence; Centre of Excellence for studies in classical languages established; Central universities provide dedicated chairs for classical languages.
48. **Article 371F Sikkim Special Seat Limit:** Legislative Assembly of Sikkim has 32 seats (unique exception to the 60-seat minimum under Article 170).
49. **Kalyana-Karnataka Development:** Article 371-J inserted in 2012 provides 8% quota in state-level jobs and 70% quota in regional educational institutions for persons from the 6 Hyderabad-Karnataka backward districts.
50. **Language of Union Summary:**
    - Official Language: **Hindi in Devanagari script**
    - Numerals: **International form of Indian numerals**
    - Supreme Court & High Court proceedings: **English** (mandatory under Art. 348 unless Parliament legislates otherwise)
    - Schedule 8 count: **22 languages**
    - Classical Languages count: **11 languages**
