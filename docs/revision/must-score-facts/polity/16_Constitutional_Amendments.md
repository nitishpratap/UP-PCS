## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Part XX (Article 368)** governs the amendment of the Constitution; Parliament exercises **Constituent Power** (distinct from ordinary legislative power) when amending the Constitution.
2. An amendment of the Constitution can be initiated **ONLY by the introduction of a Bill in either House of Parliament**; a State Legislature has **NO POWER** to initiate a constitutional amendment.
3. A Constitutional Amendment Bill can be introduced either by a **Minister or a Private Member**, and does **NOT require prior recommendation of the President**.
4. **No Joint Sitting Provision:** Each House must pass the Constitutional Amendment Bill separately; there is **NO PROVISION FOR A JOINT SITTING** of both Houses under Article 108 to resolve a deadlock on a Constitutional Amendment Bill.
5. **Assent is Mandatory:** Under **Article 368(2)** (amended by the 24th Amendment Act, 1971), the President **MUST GIVE HIS ASSENT** to a Constitutional Amendment Bill; he can neither withhold assent nor return the bill for reconsideration.
6. **Three Classes of Constitutional Amendments:**
   - (1) By **Simple Majority of Parliament** (outside Article 368).
   - (2) By **Special Majority of Parliament** (under Article 368(2)).
   - (3) By **Special Majority of Parliament + Ratification by not less than half the States** by simple majority (under Article 368(2) proviso).
7. **Amendments by Simple Majority (Outside Article 368):** Admission/establishment of new States, formation of new States and alteration of boundaries/names (Articles 2, 3, 4); abolition or creation of Legislative Councils in States (Article 169); Second Schedule salaries; quorum in Parliament; citizenship (Part II); use of English language; Fifth and Sixth Schedule administration.
8. **Special Majority Requirement (Article 368(2)):** Requires: (1) A majority of the **total membership** of the House (more than 50% of total seats regardless of vacancies); AND (2) A majority of not less than **two-thirds of the members of that House present and voting**.
9. Provisions amended by **Special Majority alone**: **Fundamental Rights** (Part III), **Directive Principles of State Policy** (Part IV), and all other provisions not covered under simple majority or federal proviso.
10. **Federal Provisions Requiring State Ratification:** (1) Election of the President and its manner (Articles 54 & 55); (2) Extent of executive power of Union and States (Articles 73 & 162); (3) Supreme Court and High Courts (Articles 124–147, 214–232); (4) Distribution of legislative powers between Union and States (Seventh Schedule); (5) Representation of States in Parliament (Fourth Schedule); (6) Provisions of **Article 368 itself**.
11. **State Ratification Mechanism:** Requires ratification by resolutions passed by the legislatures of **not less than half the States by a SIMPLE MAJORITY** of members present and voting; no time limit is prescribed by the Constitution within which States must ratify.
12. **Basic Structure Doctrine (*Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, 24 April 1973*):** A 13-judge bench ruled (7:6) that while Parliament's constituent power under Article 368 is wide enough to amend any part of the Constitution (including Fundamental Rights), it **CANNOT alter the 'Basic Structure' or foundational framework** of the Constitution.
13. **Elements of Basic Structure:** Supremacy of the Constitution, Republican and Democratic form of government, Secularism, Separation of powers, Federal character, Judicial review, Rule of law, Free and fair elections, Independence of judiciary, Harmony and balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs, Limited amending power of Parliament.
14. **First Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951:** Inserted **Articles 31A and 31B** and the **Ninth Schedule** to protect land reform laws from judicial challenge on grounds of violation of Fundamental Rights; added three new grounds of restriction to freedom of speech under Art. 19(2): 'public order', 'friendly relations with foreign states', and 'incitement to an offence'.
15. **Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956:** Implemented the **States Reorganisation Commission** recommendations; abolished the four-fold classification of States (Part A, B, C, D); created **14 States and 6 Union Territories**; abolished the office of Rajpramukh; provided for appointment of same person as Governor for 2 or more States (Art. 153); allowed common High Court for 2 or more States (Art. 231).
16. **Twenty-Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971:** Enacted to overcome the *Golaknath (1967)* judgment; affirmed that Parliament has power to abridge Fundamental Rights under Article 368; made Presidential assent **mandatory** for amendment bills; declared that Article 13 does not apply to amendments under Article 368.
17. **Twenty-Fifth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971:** Substituted the word 'amount' for 'compensation' in Article 31(2); inserted **Article 31C** giving priority to Directive Principles under **Article 39(b) and 39(c)** over Fundamental Rights under Articles 14 and 19.
18. **Thirty-Ninth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1975:** Placed the election of President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, and Speaker beyond judicial review; in *Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)*, SC struck down the provision shielding PM's election as violative of basic structure (free and fair elections).
19. **Forty-Second Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 ("Mini-Constitution"):** Voluminous amendment enacted during National Emergency based on the **Swaran Singh Committee** recommendations:
    - Added words **'Socialist'**, **'Secular'**, and **'Integrity'** to the Preamble.
    - Added **Part IVA (Article 51A)** containing 10 Fundamental Duties.
    - Added **Part XIVA (Articles 323A–323B)** for Administrative Tribunals.
    - Added three new DPSPs: **Article 39A** (Equal justice & free legal aid), **Article 43A** (Workers' participation in management), **Article 48A** (Environment & wildlife protection).
    - Shifted 5 subjects from State List to **Concurrent List**: Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Protection of wild animals and birds, and Administration of justice (except SC/HC).
    - Froze Lok Sabha and State Assembly seats at the **1971 Census** level until 2000.
    - Extended the term of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies from **5 years to 6 years** (reversed by 44th Amd).
    - Inserted Articles 368(4) and 368(5) to bar judicial review of amendments (later invalidated in *Minerva Mills, 1980*).
20. **Forty-Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978:** Enacted by the Janata Party government to neutralize distortions introduced by the 42nd Amendment:
    - Restored normal **5-year term** of Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.
    - Deleted **Right to Property** from Fundamental Rights (Art. 19(1)(f) and Art. 31) and made it a constitutional right under **Article 300A** in Part XII.
    - Replaced 'Internal Disturbance' with **'Armed Rebellion'** in Article 352.
    - Mandated **written recommendation of Union Cabinet** for proclaiming National Emergency.
    - Reduced parliamentary approval period for National Emergency from 2 months to **1 month**, requiring **Special Majority**.
    - Mandated that enforcement of **Articles 20 and 21 CANNOT BE SUSPENDED** during emergency under Article 359.
    - Restored Supreme Court's power to resolve disputes regarding election of President and Vice-President (Art. 71).
21. **Fifty-Second Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985:** Added the **Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law)**; disqualified MPs and MLAs who voluntarily give up party membership or vote contrary to party whip; exempted splits (1/3rd members) and mergers (2/3rd members).
22. **Sixty-First Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988:** Amended **Article 326** to lower the voting age for elections to Lok Sabha and State Assemblies from **21 years to 18 years** (effective 28 March 1989).
23. **Sixty-Ninth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991:** Inserted Articles **239AA and 239AB**; conferred special status on Union Territory of Delhi, redesignating it as the **National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi** with a 70-member Legislative Assembly and 7-member Council of Ministers (capped at 10% of Assembly strength).
24. **Seventy-Third Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992:** Inserted **Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O)** and the **Eleventh Schedule** (29 functional subjects); granted constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions (Gram Sabha, 3-tier structure, mandatory elections, State Finance Commission, State Election Commission, 1/3rd reservation for women).
25. **Seventy-Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992:** Inserted **Part IXA (Articles 243P to 243ZG)** and the **Twelfth Schedule** (18 functional subjects); granted constitutional status to Urban Local Bodies (Municipalities, Wards Committees, District Planning Committees).
26. **Eighty-Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2001:** Extended the freeze on the total number of seats in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies at the 1971 census level for another 25 years (until the **first census after 2026**).
27. **Eighty-Sixth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002:** The landmark Education Amendment:
    - Inserted **Article 21A** making free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14 years a Fundamental Right.
    - Substituted **Article 45** (DPSP) to provide early childhood care and education for all children until age 6.
    - Added the **11th Fundamental Duty** under **Article 51A(k)** for parent/guardian to provide education to children aged 6–14 years.
28. **Eighty-Ninth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003:** Bifurcated the combined National Commission for SCs and STs into two separate constitutional bodies: the **National Commission for Scheduled Castes (Article 338)** and the **National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A)**.
29. **Ninety-First Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003:** Limited the size of the Council of Ministers:
    - Total number of ministers including PM shall not exceed **15% of the total strength of Lok Sabha** (Art. 75(1A)).
    - In States, total ministers including CM shall not exceed **15% of Legislative Assembly strength**, with a minimum of **12 ministers** (Art. 164(1A)).
    - Completely deleted the **one-third split exemption** under the Tenth Schedule.
30. **Ninety-Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011:** Added **Article 19(1)(c)** right to form co-operative societies, inserted **Article 43B** (DPSP for promotion of co-operatives), and added **Part IXB (Articles 243ZH to 243ZT)**. Note: Part IXB was partially struck down in *Rajendra N. Shah (2021)* for single-State co-ops.
31. **Ninety-Ninth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014:** Established the **National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC)** to replace the SC Collegium; struck down in its entirety as unconstitutional and violative of Basic Structure by a 5-judge bench in the *Fourth Judges Case (2015)*.
32. **One Hundredth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2015:** Operationalized the 1974 **Land Boundary Agreement (LBA)** with Bangladesh; transferred 111 Indian enclaves to Bangladesh and received 51 Bangladeshi enclaves in India; amended First Schedule.
33. **One Hundred and First Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016:** Introduced the **Goods and Services Tax (GST)**; inserted **Article 246A** (special power to make GST laws), **Article 269A** (IGST levy), and **Article 279A** (GST Council); came into effect on **1 July 2017**.
34. **One Hundred and Second Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018:** Conferred constitutional status on the **National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)** by inserting **Article 338B**; inserted **Article 342A** empowering the President to specify Socially and Educationally Backward Classes.
35. **One Hundred and Third Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019:** Provided **10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)** in admissions to educational institutions and public employment by inserting **Articles 15(6) and 16(6)**.
36. **Judicial Ladder of Basic Structure Evolution:**
    - *Shankari Prasad (1951)* &rarr; Parliament has unlimited amending power over FRs under Art. 368.
    - *Sajjan Singh (1965)* &rarr; Reaffirmed *Shankari Prasad*.
    - *I.C. Golaknath (1967)* &rarr; 11-judge bench ruled FRs cannot be abridged by Parliament; Art. 13 applies to Art. 368.
    - **24th Amendment (1971)** &rarr; Declared Art. 13 inapplicable to Art. 368; made presidential assent mandatory.
    - *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)* &rarr; 13-judge bench overruled *Golaknath*, upheld 24th Amendment, and formulated **Basic Structure Doctrine**.
37. **Minerva Mills Affirmation of Limited Amending Power (1980):** SC struck down Section 4 and Section 55 of the 42nd Amendment; held that "a limited amending power is one of the basic features of the Indian Constitution, and therefore, the limitations on that power cannot be destroyed."
38. **Prospective Overruling Doctrine:** First formulated in Indian constitutional jurisprudence in the *Golaknath (1967)* case, holding that the judicial ruling would take effect only prospectively to protect existing land tenure legislation.
39. **First Amendment Reasonable Restrictions:** Added 3 grounds of restriction to Article 19(1)(a) freedom of speech: (1) **Public order**; (2) **Friendly relations with foreign States**; (3) **Incitement to an offence**; also added the word 'reasonable' before restrictions in Art. 19(2).
40. **Seventh Amendment Territorial Reorganization:** Enacted pursuant to the States Reorganisation Act, 1956; replaced classification of States (Part A, B, C, D) with 14 States and 6 UTs; created new High Courts; inserted **Article 350A** (mother tongue primary education) and **Article 350B** (Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
41. **Twenty-Fifth Amendment and Article 31C:** Inserted Article 31C providing that laws giving effect to DPSPs under Article 39(b) and (c) cannot be declared void for violating Arts. 14, 19, or 31; in *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)*, the first part was upheld but the second part ("no law containing a declaration shall be questioned in any court") was struck down.
42. **Thirty-Ninth Amendment Struck Down:** Inserted Article 329A placing elections of PM and Speaker beyond judicial review; struck down in *Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)*—the first time a constitutional amendment was struck down under the Basic Structure Doctrine.
43. **Tenth Schedule 52nd Amendment Ground Rules:** An MP or MLA is disqualified if he voluntarily gives up party membership, or votes/abstains contrary to party direction without prior permission and without condonation within 15 days; nominated members disqualified if they join a party after 6 months; independent members disqualified if they join ANY party after election.
44. **Sixty-First Amendment Adult Franchise:** Amended Article 326; reduced voting age from 21 to 18; passed under the Rajiv Gandhi ministry; expanded the democratic base by over 50 million young voters in 1989.
45. **Sixty-Ninth Amendment Structure for Delhi:** Created Legislative Assembly for Delhi with powers to legislate on State and Concurrent Lists EXCEPT: (1) **Public Order**; (2) **Police**; (3) **Land**. Council of Ministers capped at **10%** of total assembly membership (7 ministers including CM).
46. **Eighty-Sixth Amendment Trifecta:** Operates across Part III (Art. 21A), Part IV (Art. 45), and Part IVA (Art. 51A(k)); enacted in 2002; followed by the Right to Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009 (which came into force on **1 April 2010**).
47. **Ninety-First Amendment Ministerial Discipline:** 15% ceiling applies to Lok Sabha / Legislative Assembly; disqualified defector cannot be appointed minister or hold any remunerative political post during remaining term until re-elected.
48. **Fourth Judges Case (2015) and NJAC Nullification:** SC 5-judge bench (4:1) struck down the 99th Amendment Act and NJAC Act, 2014; held that the primacy of the judiciary in appointment of judges is an essential part of the Basic Structure; revived the Collegium system.
49. **One Hundred and Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019:** Amended **Article 334**; extended reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies for another 10 years (up to **25 January 2030**); **ceased the provision of nominated seats for Anglo-Indians** in Lok Sabha (Art. 331) and State Assemblies (Art. 333).
50. **One Hundred and Sixth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023:** Inserts Article 330A (LS reservation), Article 332A (Assembly reservation), and Article 239AA(2)(b) (Delhi Assembly reservation); grants **33% reservation for women** for a period of 15 years; takes effect after the first census conducted after commencement followed by delimitation.
51. **Article 368 Dual Classification of Amendments:** Article 368 provides two explicit mechanisms: (1) **Special Majority of Parliament** (majority of total membership of each House + not less than 2/3rd members present and voting); (2) **Special Majority of Parliament plus Ratification by Legislatures of not less than half the States by Simple Majority** (required for amending federal provisions).
52. **Federal Provisions Requiring State Ratification under Article 368(2):** (1) Election of the President (Art. 54 & 55); (2) Extent of executive power of Union and States (Art. 73 & 162); (3) Supreme Court and High Courts (Art. 241, Chapter IV of Part V, Chapter V of Part VI); (4) Distribution of legislative powers between Union and States (7th Schedule); (5) Representation of States in Parliament (4th Schedule); (6) The provisions of Article 368 itself.
53. **Amendments by Simple Majority Outside Article 368:** Several constitutional changes are enacted by ordinary law and specifically declared **NOT to be amendments under Article 368**: (a) Admission or creation of new states (Art. 2, 3, 4); (b) Abolition or creation of State Legislative Councils (Art. 169); (c) Second Schedule salaries; (d) Quorum in Parliament; (e) Use of official language; (f) Delimitation of constituencies; (g) Citizenship (Part II); (h) Fifth and Sixth Schedule administration.
54. **Rules Governing Amendment Bills:** Can be introduced in **either House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha); does NOT require prior recommendation of President; can be introduced by a minister or private member; must be passed by **each House separately by special majority**; there is **NO provision for a Joint Sitting** under Article 108 in case of disagreement between the two Houses; President **MUST give assent** (24th CAA 1971 made presidential assent mandatory).
55. **First Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951:** Enacted by Provisional Parliament; inserted **Articles 31A and 31B** and the **Ninth Schedule** to protect land agrarian reform laws from fundamental rights challenge; added three new grounds of restriction to free speech in Art. 19(2): public order, friendly relations with foreign states, incitement to an offence.
56. **Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956:** Implemented State Reorganisation Commission (Fazl Ali Commission) recommendations; abolished old Part A, B, C, D state classifications; created **14 States and 6 Union Territories**; provided for appointment of same person as Governor of two or more states; provided for establishment of Common High Courts; inserted Article 350A (primary education in mother tongue) and Article 350B (Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities).
57. **Twenty-Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971:** Enacted in response to *Golaknath (1967)*; affirmed Parliament’s power to amend any part of the Constitution including Fundamental Rights under Article 368; made presidential assent to constitutional amendment bills mandatory; inserted Article 13(4) and 368(3) declaring Art. 13 inapplicable to Art. 368 amendments.
58. **Forty-Second Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 (Mini-Constitution):** Enacted during National Emergency under Indira Gandhi on Swaran Singh Committee recommendations; added words **'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity'** to Preamble; added **Part IV-A (Fundamental Duties - Art. 51A)**; added **Part XIV-A (Tribunals - Art. 323A/323B)**; transferred 5 subjects from State List to Concurrent List (Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Protection of wild animals, Administration of justice); added DPSPs Articles 39A, 43A, 48A; extended tenure of LS and Assemblies from 5 to 6 years (reversed by 44th); made President bound by cabinet advice (Art. 74).
59. **Forty-Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978 (Emergency Rectification):** Enacted by Janata Party govt under Morarji Desai to undo 42nd Amendment distortions; replaced 'internal disturbance' with **'armed rebellion'** as ground for National Emergency in Article 352; mandated that President can proclaim Emergency ONLY upon receiving **written recommendation of Union Cabinet**; mandated parliamentary approval within 1 month by special majority; restored 5-year tenure of Lok Sabha and Assemblies; deleted Right to Property as Fundamental Right (omitted Art. 19(1)(f) and Art. 31), converting it into a legal right under **Article 300A** in Part XII; provided that Fundamental Rights under **Articles 20 and 21 CANNOT be suspended even during National Emergency**.
60. **Fifty-Second Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985 (Anti-Defection):** Added the **Tenth Schedule** and amended Articles 101, 102, 190, 191; laid down grounds of disqualification for defection of legislators; recognized political parties for first time in constitutional text.
61. **Sixty-First Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988 (Voting Age 18):** Reduced minimum voting age from 21 years to 18 years for elections to Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies by amending Article 326.
62. **Sixty-Ninth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991 (NCT of Delhi):** Conferred special status on Union Territory of Delhi, redesignating it as the **National Capital Territory of Delhi (Article 239AA)** with a 70-member Legislative Assembly and a 7-member Council of Ministers headed by Chief Minister (size of CoM fixed at 10% of total assembly strength).
63. **Eighty-Sixth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 (Right to Education):** Inserted **Article 21A** making free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14 years a Fundamental Right; amended Article 45 DPSP to focus on early childhood care below 6 years; added 11th Fundamental Duty under **Article 51A(k)**.
64. **Ninety-First Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 (CoM Size & Anti-Defection):** Capped size of Union and State Council of Ministers at **15% of total membership of Lok Sabha / State Assembly** (minimum 12 ministers in States); eliminated the 1/3rd split exemption in Tenth Schedule anti-defection law; barred defecting members from holding remunerative political posts.
65. **Ninety-Ninth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014 (NJAC - Struck Down):** Established National Judicial Appointments Commission to replace SC Collegium; struck down in its entirety as unconstitutional and void by Supreme Court in October 2015 for violating judicial independence.
66. **One Hundredth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2015 (Land Boundary Agreement):** Operationalized the 1974 India-Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement; transferred 111 enclaves to Bangladesh and received 51 enclaves from Bangladesh; amended First Schedule regarding territories of Assam, West Bengal, Meghalaya, and Tripura.
67. **One Hundred and First Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 (Goods and Services Tax - GST):** Introduced comprehensive indirect tax regime (GST) from 1 July 2017; inserted **Article 246A** (special power to legislate on GST), **Article 269A** (levy and collection of IGST on inter-state supply), and **Article 279A** (GST Council); omitted Article 268A.
68. **One Hundred and Second Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018 (NCBC):** Granted constitutional status to the **National Commission for Backward Classes (Article 338B)**; inserted Article 342A empowering President to notify Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs).
69. **One Hundred and Third Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019 (10% EWS Reservation):** Provided up to **10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)** in educational institutions and public employment; inserted **Article 15(6) and Article 16(6)**; upheld by Supreme Court in *Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)*.
70. **One Hundred and Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2020:** Extended reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies for another 10 years (up to 25 January 2030) by amending Article 334; **discontinued nomination of Anglo-Indian members** in Lok Sabha (Art. 331) and State Assemblies (Art. 333).
71. **One Hundred and Fifth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021 (State OBC Identification):** Clarified Article 342A to restore the power of State Governments and UTs to directly identify and maintain their own state lists of Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs), undoing the effect of Supreme Court's Maratha reservation judgment (*Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil, 2021*).
72. **One Hundred and Sixth Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam):** Provided **one-third (33%) reservation for women** in the House of the People (Lok Sabha), State Legislative Assemblies, and Legislative Assembly of NCT of Delhi; inserted **Articles 330A, 332A, and 239AA(2)(b)**; sunset clause of **15 years**; reservation to become effective after the first delimitation conducted following the first census published after enactment.

### 1. The Three Amendment Pathways under Indian Constitution

| Category | Parliamentary Majority Required | State Ratification Required? | Constitutional Scope / Subject Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Class 1: Simple Majority (Outside Art. 368)** | Simple majority of members present and voting in each House | **NO** | Formation/alteration of States (Arts. 2–4), Abolition/creation of Vidhan Parishad (Art. 169), Quorum, Official Language, 5th & 6th Schedules |
| **Class 2: Special Majority (Under Art. 368(2))** | Majority of total membership + 2/3rd of members present and voting in each House | **NO** | Fundamental Rights (Part III), Directive Principles (Part IV), and all provisions not covered in Class 1 or Class 3 |
| **Class 3: Special Majority + State Ratification (Art. 368(2) proviso)** | Majority of total membership + 2/3rd present/voting in each House | **YES** (by &ge; 50% of State legislatures by **Simple Majority**) | Presidential Election (54, 55), Extent of executive power (73, 162), SC & HCs (124–147, 214–232), 7th Schedule Lists, 4th Schedule representation, Article 368 itself |

### 3. Basic Structure Case Ladder

| Case | Year | Key Doctrine / Holding | Impact on Article 368 |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Shankari Prasad v. Union of India* | 1951 | Parliament can amend any part including FRs | Article 13 does not apply to Art. 368 |
| *Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan* | 1965 | Reaffirmed Shankari Prasad; Parliament supreme | Questioned if fundamental freedoms could be curtailed |
| *I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab* | 1967 | Fundamental Rights are transcendental; cannot be amended | Article 13(2) includes constitutional amendment acts |
| *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* | 1973 | Formulated **Basic Structure Doctrine**; overruled Golaknath | Parliament can amend FRs, but CANNOT alter Basic Structure |
| *Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain* | 1975 | Struck down 39th Amendment (Art. 329A) | First time an amendment was struck down under Basic Structure |
| *Minerva Mills v. Union of India* | 1980 | Struck down Art. 368(4) & (5); limited amending power is basic | Upheld balance between FRs and DPSPs as basic feature |
| *I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu* | 2007 | Laws added to 9th Schedule after 24 April 1973 subject to review | 9th Schedule is no longer a blanket protective umbrella |
| *Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record (NJAC)* | 2015 | Struck down 99th Amendment and NJAC Act in their entirety | Independence of Judiciary and Collegium reaffirmed as basic feature |

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

1. **Constitutional Amendment Pathway Selector:**
   - Need to change name or boundary of a State? &rarr; **Simple Majority (Outside Art. 368)**
   - Need to change Fundamental Rights or DPSPs? &rarr; **Special Majority (Art. 368(2))**
   - Need to touch the President, High Courts, Supreme Court, or 7th Schedule? &rarr; **Special Majority + Half States' Simple Ratification**
2. **The Post-100 Amendments Rapid Sequence:**
   - **100th:** Boundary (Bangladesh Enclaves)
   - **101st:** Tax (GST — "101 Shagun for One Nation One Tax")
   - **102nd:** Backward (NCBC Constitutional Status — Art. 338B)
   - **103rd:** Economically Weaker (10% EWS Quota)
   - **104th:** Seats Extension & Anglo-Indian Abolition
   - **105th:** State OBC Lists Restored
   - **106th:** Women's 33% Reservation (Nari Shakti)
3. **The 42nd vs 44th Twin Mirror Rule:**
   - If a provision **centralized power, suppressed courts, or extended terms** &rarr; **42nd Amendment (1976)**.
   - If a provision **restored democracy, protected rights, or added safeguards** &rarr; **44th Amendment (1978)**.
4. **Basic Structure Key Milestone Ladder:**
   - **G-K-M Rule:**
   - **G**olaknath (1967: Parliament CANNOT touch FRs)
   - &rarr; **K**esavananda (1973: Parliament can touch FRs, but CANNOT destroy Basic Structure)
   - &rarr; **M**inerva Mills (1980: Amending power itself is limited; limited power is a Basic Feature!)
