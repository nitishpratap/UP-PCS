## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Constitutional Roots of Local Government:** **Article 40** in Part IV (DPSP) directs the State to "take steps to organise village panchayats and endow them with such powers and authority as may be necessary to enable them to function as units of self-government".
2. **Historical Milestone:** **Lord Ripon's Resolution of 1882** is hailed as the **'Magna Carta of Local Self-Government'**; Lord Ripon is celebrated as the **'Father of Local Self-Government in India'**.
3. **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957):** Appointed to evaluate the Community Development Programme (1952) and National Extension Service (1953); recommended **'Democratic Decentralisation'** through a **3-tier Panchayati Raj System**:
   - (1) **Gram Panchayat** at the village level (directly elected);
   - (2) **Panchayat Samiti** at the block level (indirectly elected, executive body);
   - (3) **Zila Parishad** at the district level (advisory and supervisory body, chaired by District Collector).
4. **First Panchayati Raj System:** Inaugurated on **2 October 1959** at **Nagaur, Rajasthan** by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru; **Andhra Pradesh** adopted it second in November 1959.
5. **Ashok Mehta Committee (1977):** Appointed by the Janata Party government; recommended replacing the 3-tier system with a **2-tier system**:
   - (1) **Zila Parishad** at the district level (executive body);
   - (2) **Mandal Panchayat** (covering a group of villages with population of 15,000 to 20,000);
   - Recommended official participation of political parties in elections, compulsory taxation powers, and constitutional recognition.
6. **G.V.K. Rao Committee (1985):** Appointed by Planning Commission; observed that development was bureaucratised, describing Panchayati Raj as **"grass without roots"**; recommended making **Zila Parishad** the principal body for management of all development programmes.
7. **L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986):** Appointed by the Rajiv Gandhi government; **FIRST committee to formally recommend that Panchayati Raj institutions should be constitutionally recognized, protected, and preserved**; recommended Nyaya Panchayats for clusters of villages.
8. **Thungon Committee (1988) & Gadgil Committee (1988):** Both recommended constitutional status for Panchayats, 3-tier structure, fixed 5-year tenure, reservation for SCs/STs/women, and State Finance Commissions; formed the blueprint for the 73rd Amendment Bill.
9. **Seventy-Third Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992:** Inserted **Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O)** titled 'The Panchayats' and the **Eleventh Schedule**; received Presidential assent on 20 April 1993 and came into force on **24 April 1993** (**National Panchayati Raj Day**).
10. **Eleventh Schedule (Article 243G):** Contains **29 functional subjects** devolved to Panchayats (agriculture, land improvement, minor irrigation, animal husbandry, fisheries, social forestry, small-scale industries, rural housing, drinking water, roads, rural electrification, poverty alleviation, education, health, public distribution system).
11. **Article 243A (Gram Sabha):** General assembly of all persons registered in the electoral rolls of a village within the Panchayat area; acts as the foundation of the Panchayati Raj system; exercises powers provided by State law.
12. **Article 243B (Three-Tier System):** Mandates a 3-tier system of Panchayats in every State: (1) Village level; (2) Intermediate level (Block/Panchayat Samiti); (3) District level (Zila Parishad). Exception: A State having a **population not exceeding 20 LAKH** has the option **NOT to constitute Panchayats at the intermediate level**.
13. **Article 243C (Election of Members & Chairpersons):**
    - All seats in Panchayats at all three levels are filled by persons chosen by **DIRECT ELECTION** from territorial constituencies.
    - Chairperson of a Panchayat at the village level is elected in such manner as the State Legislature determines (direct or indirect).
    - Chairperson of a Panchayat at the **intermediate and district levels MUST BE ELECTED INDIRECTLY** by and from amongst the elected members.
14. **Article 243D (Reservation of Seats in Panchayats):**
    - Seats reserved for **SCs and STs** in proportion to their population in the Panchayat area;
    - Not less than **one-third (33.3%)** of the total number of seats reserved for SCs/STs shall be reserved for **women belonging to SCs/STs**;
    - Not less than **ONE-THIRD (including SC/ST women) of total seats** in every Panchayat shall be reserved for **WOMEN** (by rotation);
    - Not less than one-third of total offices of Chairpersons at all levels reserved for women;
    - State Legislature is empowered to provide reservation of seats and chairperson offices for **Backward Classes (OBCs)**.
15. **Article 243E (Duration of Panchayats):** Every Panchayat has a fixed term of **5 years** from the date of its first meeting; cannot be dissolved by executive decree without opportunity of hearing; fresh elections **MUST be completed BEFORE the expiry of its 5-year duration**.
16. **Six-Month Dissolution Rule (Article 243E(3)):** In case of premature dissolution, fresh elections must be completed within **6 MONTHS**; the newly elected Panchayat continues **ONLY for the remainder of the 5-year period** (does not get a fresh 5-year term); exception: if remaining period is less than 6 months, not necessary to hold elections.
17. **Article 243F (Disqualifications for Membership):** Minimum age for contesting Panchayat elections is **21 YEARS** (not 25 years as in Assembly!); no person disqualified on ground that he is less than 25 years if he has attained 21 years. All disqualification disputes referred to an authority determined by State law.
18. **Article 243I (State Finance Commission — SFC):** Governor of a State constitutes a Finance Commission every **5th year** to review financial position of Panchayats and recommend:
    - (a) Distribution between State and Panchayats of net proceeds of taxes, duties, tolls, and fees;
    - (b) Determination of taxes, duties, and tolls assignable to Panchayats;
    - (c) Grants-in-aid to Panchayats from the State Consolidated Fund.
    - Recommendations laid before State Legislature by Governor.
19. **Article 243K (State Election Commission — SEC):** Superintendence, direction, and control of electoral rolls and conduct of all elections to Panchayats (and Municipalities under 243ZA) vested in the State Election Commissioner appointed by the **Governor**.
20. **Security of Tenure of State Election Commissioner (Article 243K(2)):** Can ONLY be removed in like manner and on the like grounds as a **Judge of a High Court**; conditions of service cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment.
21. **Article 243-O (Bar to Interference by Courts in Electoral Matters):** Validity of any law relating to delimitation of constituencies cannot be questioned in court; no election to any Panchayat can be called in question except by an **Election Petition** presented to such authority as provided by State law.
22. **Exemptions from Part IX (Article 243M):** Part IX does NOT apply to: (1) States of **Nagaland, Meghalaya, and Mizoram**; (2) Scheduled areas and tribal areas under Article 244; (3) Hill area of Manipur where District Councils exist; (4) Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council areas.
23. **PESA Act, 1996 (Panchayats Extension to Scheduled Areas Act):** Enacted by Parliament on the recommendation of the **Dileep Singh Bhuria Committee (1995)**; extends Part IX provisions to **Fifth Schedule areas in 10 States** (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan).
24. **Key Features of PESA Act, 1996:**
    - Every village shall have a **Gram Sabha** competent to safeguard and preserve traditions, customs, cultural identity, community resources, and customary mode of dispute resolution;
    - Gram Sabha has **mandatory ownership of Minor Forest Produce (MFP)**;
    - Mandatory consultation with Gram Sabha before land acquisition for development projects and before resettlement/rehabilitation;
    - Mandatory recommendation of Gram Sabha for grant of prospecting license or mining lease for minor minerals;
    - Power to enforce prohibition or regulate/restrict sale and consumption of intoxicants;
    - Power to control money lending to Scheduled Tribes and prevent alienation of tribal lands.
25. **Seventy-Fourth Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992:** Inserted **Part IXA (Articles 243P to 243ZG)** titled 'The Municipalities' and the **Twelfth Schedule**; came into force on **1 June 1993**.
26. **Twelfth Schedule (Article 243W):** Contains **18 functional subjects** devolved to Municipalities (urban planning, regulation of land use, roads and bridges, water supply, public health, fire services, urban forestry, slum improvement, urban poverty alleviation).
27. **Three Types of Municipalities (Article 243Q):**
    - (1) **Nagar Panchayat** for a transitional area (rural to urban);
    - (2) **Municipal Council (Nagar Palika)** for a smaller urban area;
    - (3) **Municipal Corporation (Mahanagar Palika)** for a larger urban area.
    - Exception: Governor may specify that a municipality need not be constituted in an industrial township where municipal services are provided by an industrial establishment.
28. **Article 243S (Wards Committees):** Mandates constitution of Wards Committees consisting of one or more wards within the territorial area of a Municipality having a **population of THREE LAKH or more**.
29. **Article 243T (Reservation in Municipalities):** Seats reserved for SCs and STs in proportion to population; not less than **one-third of total seats reserved for women** (including SC/ST women); State Legislature may provide reservation for backward classes.
30. **Article 243ZD (District Planning Committee — DPC):** Mandates constitution of a DPC at the district level in every State to **consolidate plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities in the district** and prepare a draft development plan for the district as a whole.
31. **DPC Composition (Article 243ZD(2)):** At least **four-fifths (80%) of the members** of the DPC shall be elected by and from amongst the elected members of the **Zila Panchayat** and **Municipalities** in the district, in proportion to the ratio between rural and urban population of the district.
32. **Article 243ZE (Metropolitan Planning Committee — MPC):** In every Metropolitan area (population of **10 lakh or more**), an MPC shall be constituted to prepare a draft development plan; at least **two-thirds of members** elected by and from amongst elected members of Municipalities and chairpersons of Panchayats in the metropolitan area.
33. **First Municipal Corporation in India:** Established at **Madras in 1687–88**; followed by Bombay and Calcutta in 1726.
34. **Eight Types of Urban Local Bodies in India:** Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council, Notified Area Committee, Town Area Committee, Cantonment Board, Township, Port Trust, Special Purpose Agency.
35. **Cantonment Board Administration:** Set up for municipal administration of civilian population in cantonment areas; created and administered under the **Cantonments Act, 2006** (passed by Parliament); works directly under the **Union Ministry of Defence**; consists of partly elected and partly nominated/ex-officio members; military officer commanding the station is the ex-officio President of the Board.
36. **Compulsory Provisions of 73rd Amendment:** Organization of Gram Sabha; 3-tier structure (except states &lt;20 lakh); direct election to all seats; indirect election of chairpersons at intermediate and district levels; minimum age 21 years; reservation for SCs/STs; min 1/3rd reservation for women; fixed 5-year tenure; constitution of State Finance Commission; constitution of State Election Commission.
37. **Voluntary Provisions of 73rd Amendment:** Giving voting rights to MPs/MLAs in local bodies; reservation for backward classes (OBCs); giving financial powers to levy and collect taxes; devolving powers on the 29 subjects in the 11th Schedule; making Panchayats autonomous units of self-government.
38. **Uttar Pradesh Panchayati Raj Details:** Uttar Pradesh has a 3-tier system: Gram Panchayat, Kshetra Panchayat, Zila Panchayat; regulated by UP Panchayati Raj Act, 1947 and UP Kshetra Panchayats and Zila Panchayats Adhiniyam, 1961.
39. **Nyaya Panchayats:** Grassroots judicial tribunals at village level for speedy and inexpensive settlement of small civil and criminal disputes; not made mandatory under 73rd Amendment (left to State discretion).
40. **Sources of Panchayat Revenue:** (1) Internal tax revenues (property tax, cess on land revenue); (2) Non-tax revenues (market fees, user charges); (3) Grants-in-aid from State government (based on SFC recommendations); (4) Central grants under Article 280 (recommended by Central Finance Commission).
41. **Community Development Programme (CDP, 1952):** Launched on 2 October 1952 with US Ford Foundation support; aimed at comprehensive rural development; failed due to excessive bureaucratization and lack of people's participation, necessitating Balwant Rai Mehta committee.
42. **Nagaur Historical Milestone:** Prime Minister Nehru launched the democratic decentralisation experiment at Nagaur (Rajasthan) on 2 October 1959 by switching on an electric power button to unveil the plaque.
43. **Ashok Mehta Mandal Panchayat Concept:** Defined Mandal Panchayat as a cluster of villages with total population of 15,000–20,000; aimed to make local bodies economically viable units of planning.
44. **G.V.K. Rao District Development Commissioner (DDC):** Recommended creation of the post of DDC as the Chief Executive Officer of the Zila Parishad, making the administrative machinery accountable to elected representatives.
45. **Rajiv Gandhi 64th Amendment Bill (1989):** Introduced in Lok Sabha to confer constitutional status on Panchayats; passed by Lok Sabha but defeated in Rajya Sabha in October 1989 due to opposition fears of centralisation.
46. **10 States under PESA (1996):** Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan.
47. **Gram Sabha Absolute Primacy in PESA:** PESA mandates that state legislation shall be in consonance with customary law and traditional management practices of community resources; Gram Sabha is designated as the custodian of tribal traditions.
48. **Notified Area Committee vs Town Area Committee:** Notified Area Committee is created for fast-growing towns or towns not fulfilling conditions for municipality; established by state notification; **entirely nominated body** (no elected members). Town Area Committee is set up for small towns; semi-municipal authority; can be wholly elected, wholly nominated, or mixed.
49. **Port Trust Administration:** Established in major port cities (Kolkata, Mumbai, Chennai) to manage port facilities and civic amenities; established by an Act of Parliament; consists of both elected and nominated members.
50. **Central Council of Local Government:** Set up in 1954 under **Article 263** (Inter-State Council provision) by an order of the President; chaired by the Union Minister for Housing and Urban Affairs; advisory body for formulating urban local government policies.
51. **Historical Committees on Panchayati Raj Chronological Order:** (1) **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957)** - Recommended 3-tier Panchayati Raj system and democratic decentralization; (2) **Ashok Mehta Committee (1977–78)** - Recommended 2-tier system (Zila Parishad + Mandal Panchayat) and political party participation; (3) **G.V.K. Rao Committee (1985)** - Recommended administrative arrangement for rural development ('grass without roots'); (4) **L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986)** - Recommended constitutional recognition for local self-government and Nyaya Panchayats; (5) **P.K. Thungon Committee (1988)** - Strongly urged constitutional entrenchment; (6) **V.N. Gadgil Committee (1988)** - Drafted template of 3-tier system with reservation and State Election/Finance Commissions.
52. **First Panchayati Raj Inauguration in India:** Inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on **2 October 1959 at Nagaur, Rajasthan**; Andhra Pradesh was the second State to adopt it in 1959.
53. **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Key Milestones):** Passed during P.V. Narasimha Rao's premiership; received presidential assent on 20 April 1993; came into force on **24 April 1993** (celebrated annually as **National Panchayati Raj Day**); added **Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O)** and the **11th Schedule (29 functional subjects)**.
54. **74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Urban Local Bodies):** Received assent on 20 April 1993; came into force on **1 June 1993**; added **Part IX-A (Articles 243-P to 243-ZG)** and the **12th Schedule (18 functional items)**.
55. **Article 243-A (Gram Sabha):** Foundation of the Panchayati Raj system; a body consisting of **all persons registered in the electoral rolls** relating to a village comprised within the area of Panchayat; exercises powers and performs functions at village level as State Legislature provides by law.
56. **Article 243-B (Three-Tier Panchayati Structure):** Mandates Panchayats at village, intermediate (Panchayat Samiti/Block), and district (Zila Parishad) levels; Proviso: Intermediate level Panchayat need **NOT be constituted in a State having a population not exceeding 20 lakhs**.
57. **Article 243-C (Composition of Panchayats):** All seats in a Panchayat at all three levels are filled by persons chosen by **direct election from territorial constituencies**; Chairpersons of village panchayats elected as state law provides; Chairpersons of intermediate and district panchayats must be elected **indirectly by and from amongst the elected members**.
58. **Article 243-D (Reservation of Seats in Panchayats):** Mandatory reservation for **SCs and STs in proportion to their population**; not less than **one-third (33%) of total seats reserved for women** (including SC/ST women); reservation for women also applies to offices of Chairpersons at all levels; State Legislature may provide reservation for Backward Classes (OBCs); several states (e.g. Bihar [first in 2006], MP, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand) have increased women reservation to **50%**.
59. **Article 243-E (Duration of Panchayats):** Every Panchayat shall continue for **5 years** from the date appointed for its first meeting and no longer; Election to constitute a Panchayat must be completed: (a) before expiry of its 5-year duration; (b) in case of premature dissolution, within **6 months**; a Panchayat reconstituted after premature dissolution continues ONLY for the **remainder of the 5-year period** (unless remainder is less than 6 months, in which case no mid-term election is held).
60. **Article 243-F (Disqualifications for Membership):** Minimum age for contesting Panchayat elections is **21 years** (unlike 25 years for Lok Sabha/Vidhan Sabha); questions of disqualification referred to authority determined by State Legislature.
61. **Article 243-I & 243-Y (State Finance Commission):** Governor constitutes a Finance Commission every **5 years** to review financial position of Panchayats and Municipalities and recommend distribution of net proceeds of taxes between State and local bodies, assignment of taxes, and grants-in-aid.
62. **Article 243-K & 243-ZA (State Election Commission):** Superintendence, direction and control of preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of all elections to Panchayats and Municipalities is vested in the **State Election Commissioner** appointed by the Governor; conditions of service and tenure determined by Governor; **can be removed from office ONLY in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of a High Court** (cannot be removed by Governor arbitrarily).
63. **Article 243-O & 243-ZG (Bar to Interference by Courts):** Courts have no jurisdiction to question validity of any law relating to delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats; elections to Panchayats/Municipalities can be called in question ONLY by an **election petition** presented to such authority and in such manner as prescribed by state law.
64. **Article 243-Q (Three Types of Municipalities):** (1) **Nagar Panchayat** for a transitional area from rural to urban; (2) **Municipal Council (Nagar Palika Parishad)** for a smaller urban area; (3) **Municipal Corporation (Nagar Nigam)** for a larger urban area; Governor specifies these areas based on population, density, revenue, and economic factors.
65. **Article 243-S (Wards Committees):** Mandates constitution of Wards Committees consisting of one or more wards within territorial area of a Municipality having a **population of 3 lakhs or more**.
66. **Article 243-ZD (District Planning Committee - DPC):** Constituted in every district to consolidate plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities and prepare a draft development plan for the district as a whole; not less than **four-fifths (80%) of members** are elected by and from amongst the elected members of Zila Parishad and Municipalities in proportion to rural/urban population ratio.
67. **Article 243-ZE (Metropolitan Planning Committee - MPC):** Constituted in every Metropolitan area (population 10 lakhs or more); not less than **two-thirds (66.6%) of members** are elected by and from amongst elected members of Municipalities and Chairpersons of Panchayats in the area.
68. **PESA Act, 1996 (Panchayats Extension to Scheduled Areas):** Enacted on recommendation of **Bhuria Committee** to extend Part IX to Fifth Schedule areas across 10 States; recognizes traditional rights, customs, and community resources of tribal communities; Gram Sabha has mandatory powers of consultation in land acquisition, minor forest produce (MFP) ownership, grant of prospecting licenses for minor minerals, and enforcing prohibition.
69. **Compulsory vs Voluntary Provisions of 73rd Amendment:** **Compulsory (Mandatory):** Organization of Gram Sabha, 3-tier system, direct elections to all seats, indirect election of chairpersons at intermediate/district levels, 21 years voting age, 1/3rd women reservation, SC/ST reservation, 5-year tenure, State Finance Commission, State Election Commission; **Voluntary (Discretionary):** Giving voting rights to MPs/MLAs, reservation for OBCs, devolving 29 functional items, granting taxation powers, financial autonomy.
70. **Uttar Pradesh Panchayati Raj Act, 1947 & Municipalities Act, 1916:** Governs local bodies in UP; UP was among first to establish Gaon Sabhas in 1949; amended in 1994 (UP Panchayat Laws Amendment Act 1994) to align with 73rd and 74th Amendments; UP has a 3-tier system: Gram Panchayat (headed by Gram Pradhan), Kshetra Panchayat (Block Pramukh), and Zila Panchayat (Zila Panchayat Adhyaksha); UP currently has **17 Municipal Corporations (Nagar Nigams)** (Shahjahanpur became 17th in 2018).
71. **First Municipal Corporation in India:** Established at **Madras in 1687–88**; followed by Bombay and Calcutta in 1726; Lord Ripon’s Resolution of 1882 is hailed as the **'Magna Carta of Local Self-Government'** in India (Lord Ripon known as 'Father of Local Self-Government in India').
72. **Sources of Municipal Revenue:** (1) Tax revenue (property tax, octroi/entry tax, vehicle tax); (2) Non-tax revenue (user charges, fees, fines); (3) Assigned taxes and shared revenue; (4) Grants-in-aid from State Govt; (5) Borrowings (municipal bonds; Ahmedabad MC issued first municipal bonds in India in 1998; Lucknow MC issued bonds listed on BSE in 2020).
73. **Nyaya Panchayats Status:** Judicial wings of village panchayats recommended by Ashok Mehta and LM Singhvi committees; eliminated or non-functional in most modern state acts due to lack of legal training and procedural issues.
74. **Social Audit in Local Governance:** Mandatory verification of scheme expenditure by Gram Sabha; pioneered under MGNREGA Act 2005 (Section 17); Meghalaya was the first state in India to pass a comprehensive Social Audit Act in 2017.
75. **e-Gram Swaraj & SVAMITVA Schemes:** High-yield national digital local governance initiatives; e-Gram Swaraj portal integrates planning, accounting, and monitoring of Panchayats; SVAMITVA (Survey of Villages and Mapping with Improvised Technology in Village Areas) uses drone technology to map rural inhabited lands and issue property cards ('Gharauni' in UP).
