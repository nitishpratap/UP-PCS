## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Charter Act of 1813 (Educational Clause):** First time the British Parliament allocated state funds for Indian education: set aside **₹1 lakh annually** for the revival of literature and promotion of scientific knowledge; simultaneously permitted Christian missionaries to preach and establish schools in India.
2. **Orientalist–Anglicist Controversy (1823–1835):**
   - General Committee of Public Instruction (appointed 1823) was divided into two equal factions of five members each:
   - **Orientalists (led by H.T. Prinsep & H.H. Wilson):** Wanted public funds spent on Sanskrit, Arabic, and Persian classical literature.
   - **Anglicists (led by Charles Trevelyan):** Wanted public funds spent on modern Western sciences taught through the medium of the English language.
3. **Macaulay’s Minute (2 February 1835):**
   - Authored by **Lord Macaulay** (Law Member and President of General Committee of Public Instruction); approved by Governor-General **Lord William Bentinck on 7 March 1835**.
   - Made English the official medium of instruction for higher education in government institutions.
   - Arrogantly proclaimed: *"A single shelf of a good European library was worth the whole native literature of India and Arabia."*
   - Advanced the **Downward Filtration Theory (अधोमुखी निस्यंदन सिद्धांत)**: Educate a narrow elite class of Indians *"Indian in blood and colour, but English in tastes, in opinions, in morals, and in intellect"*, who would in turn filter modern knowledge downward to the masses.
4. **Wood’s Despatch (19 July 1854) — "Magna Carta of English Education in India":**
   - Sent by **Sir Charles Wood** (President of Board of Control) to Governor-General **Lord Dalhousie**.
   - Completely rejected the Downward Filtration Theory; assumed government responsibility for mass education.
   - Formulated a comprehensive graded ladder of education: Primary Schools (Vernacular) → Middle Schools (Anglo-Vernacular) → High Schools & Colleges (English) → Universities.
   - Recommended the establishment of an **Education Department (DPI)** in every province.
   - Recommended a system of **grants-in-aid** to encourage private enterprise and female education.
   - Recommended establishing affiliating universities on the model of the **University of London**.
5. **Establishment of First Three Universities (1857):**
   - In **1857**, under Lord Canning, universities were established at **Calcutta (Jan 1857)**, **Bombay (July 1857)**, and **Madras (Sept 1857)** based on Wood’s Despatch. Punjab University followed in 1882, and **Allahabad University in 1887** ("Oxford of the East", fourth university in India).
6. **Hunter Education Commission (1882–1883):**
   - Appointed by **Lord Ripon** under **Sir William Wilson Hunter** to review progress since Wood’s Despatch of 1854.
   - Mandate: Concentrated almost exclusively on **primary and secondary education** (university education was outside its purview).
   - Recommended that primary education should be imparted through the **vernacular**, made the direct responsibility of newly created Municipalities and District Boards, and that secondary education should have two streams: literary (university-bound) and vocational/commercial.
7. **Raleigh Commission (1902) & Indian Universities Act, 1904:**
   - Appointed by **Lord Curzon** under **Sir Thomas Raleigh** to investigate conditions of Indian universities; included two Indian members: **Syed Husain Bilgrami** and **Justice Gurudas Banerjee**.
   - Resulted in the **Indian Universities Act, 1904**: Drastically curtailed university autonomy; reduced the number of fellows; increased nominated official members in university senates; imposed strict affiliation conditions on colleges; granted government veto power over university senate regulations (vehemently opposed by G.K. Gokhale).
8. **National Council of Education (NCE, 1906):**
   - Founded on **15 August 1906** in Bengal as part of the Swadeshi Movement's boycott of government schools.
   - Promoted national education: literary, scientific, and technical, on national lines under national control.
   - Set up **Bengal National College** with **Aurobindo Ghosh as its first Principal**.
9. **Sadler University Commission (1917–1919):**
   - Appointed by **Lord Chelmsford** under **Dr. Michael Sadler** (Vice-Chancellor of Leeds University) to investigate Calcutta University; included two Indian members: **Sir Ashutosh Mukherjee** and **Dr. Ziauddin Ahmad**.
   - Key Recommendations:
     - Improvement of secondary education was essential for university reform.
     - Recommended **10+2+3 pattern**: 12-year school course, separating the Intermediate stage (Class 11 & 12) from universities and creating a separate Board of Secondary and Intermediate Education (first adopted in UP in 1921).
     - Recommended establishing residential, teaching, and unitary universities (led to creation of Dacca, Lucknow, Aligarh, and Patna universities).
     - Recommended appointment of female education boards and special provisions for women's higher education.
10. **Hartog Committee (1929):**
    - Appointed as an auxiliary committee of the Simon Commission under **Sir Philip Hartog** to review educational progress.
    - Criticized rapid and indiscriminate expansion of schooling; highlighted the alarming twin problems of **"Wastage"** (dropping out before completing the primary cycle) and **"Stagnation"** (students repeatedly failing and remaining in the same class).
    - Recommended consolidation and quality improvement; advised diverting average students after middle school to technical and vocational courses.
11. **Wardha Scheme of Basic Education / Nai Talim (1937):**
    - Formulated by **Mahatma Gandhi** in a series of articles in *Harijan*; discussed at the All-India National Education Conference held at Wardha in October 1937.
    - Detailed blueprint prepared by the committee chaired by **Dr. Zakir Hussain**.
    - Core principles: Free and compulsory education for all children between ages 7 and 14; education imparted through the **mother tongue**; curriculum centered on a productive basic craft (spinning, weaving, carpentry, agriculture, leatherwork) so that schools could be self-supporting; manual labour recognized as an essential pedagogical tool.
12. **Sargent Plan of Education (1944):**
    - Prepared by **Sir John Sargent** (Educational Adviser to the Government of India) for post-war educational reconstruction.
    - Envisaged universal, free, and compulsory primary education for children aged 6 to 14 within **40 years** (reduced to 16 years by the Kher Committee).
    - Proposed nursery/pre-primary schools for ages 3–6, adult literacy drives, and the establishment of a **University Grants Committee**.
13. **Radhakrishnan Commission (1948–1949):**
    - University Education Commission chaired by **Dr. S. Radhakrishnan**; first commission of free India; recommended creation of the **University Grants Commission (UGC)** (constituted 1953, statutory in 1956).
14. **Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784):**
    - Founded in Calcutta on **15 January 1784** by **Sir William Jones** (judge of the Supreme Court of Calcutta).
    - Warren Hastings encouraged the foundation and was offered the presidency, but **declined** it in favor of Jones.
    - Pioneer of Indological research: Jones translated Kalidasa’s *Abhijnanasakuntalam* (1789) and *Gita Govinda*; **Charles Wilkins** translated the *Bhagavad Gita* into English (1785, first English translation with preface by Hastings).
15. **Calcutta Madrasa (1781) & Sanskrit College Banaras (1791):**
    - **Calcutta Madrasa (Aliyah):** Founded in **1781 by Warren Hastings** for the study of Arabic, Persian, and Muslim law.
    - **Sanskrit College, Banaras:** Founded in **1791 by Jonathan Duncan** (British Resident at Banaras) for the preservation and study of Hindu laws, literature, and philosophy.
16. **Fort William College (1800):**
    - Established at Calcutta on **10 July 1800** by Governor-General **Lord Wellesley** to train young British civil servants in Indian languages, customs, and laws. Closed in 1802 by order of the Court of Directors (civil servants trained at Haileybury in England from 1806).
17. **Serampore Trio & Serampore College (1818):**
    - Baptist missionaries **William Carey, Joshua Marshman, and William Ward** worked in Danish-protected Serampore (Bengal).
    - Introduced the first modern vernacular printing presses; translated the Bible into multiple Indian languages; founded **Serampore College in 1818** (first degree-granting college in Asia).
18. **Hindu College, Calcutta (1817):**
    - Founded in **January 1817** by **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**, **David Hare** (watchmaker and philanthropist), and **Sir Edward Hyde East** (Chief Justice of Supreme Court) to impart secular English and Western scientific education; renamed **Presidency College in 1855** (now Presidency University).
19. **Bethune School (1849):**
    - Founded in Calcutta in **1849** by **John Elliot Drinkwater Bethune** (President of the Council of Education) with the vigorous support of **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**; first institutional school for girls in India.
20. **First Modern Newspaper in India (1780):**
    - ***The Bengal Gazette* (or *Calcutta General Advertiser*)**, popularly known as **Hicky’s Bengal Gazette**, started in Calcutta on **29 January 1780** by **James Augustus Hicky**.
    - Fiercely criticized Governor-General Warren Hastings and Chief Justice Elijah Impey; seized and closed down by the government in March 1782.
21. **Censorship of Press Act, 1799 (Lord Wellesley):**
    - Enacted due to wartime paranoia of French/Napoleonic intrigues in India; imposed strict pre-censorship on all printed material; required printer, editor, and proprietor to clearly print their names on newspapers; abolished by **Lord Hastings in 1818**.
22. **Licensing Regulations of 1823 (John Adam):**
    - Acting Governor-General John Adam made running a printing press without an official government license a penal offense; magistrates empowered to seize unlicensed presses.
    - Forced Raja Ram Mohan Roy to stop publication of his Persian weekly ***Mirat-ul-Akhbar*** in protest.
    - James Silk Buckingham, radical editor of the *Calcutta Journal*, was expelled from India.
23. **Press Act of 1835 (Charles Metcalfe) — "Liberator of the Indian Press":**
    - Governor-General **Sir Charles Metcalfe** (with the advice of Macaulay) repealed Adam’s oppressive 1823 Licensing Regulations; substituted them with a simple procedural declaration by printer/publisher, liberating Indian journalism from 1835 to 1857.
24. **Licensing Act of 1857 (Lord Canning):**
    - Emergency wartime measure passed during the Revolt of 1857 (Act XV of 1857); reintroduced licensing restrictions on all presses for one year; applied equally to English and Indian-language publications.
25. **Press and Registration of Books Act, 1867:**
    - Regulatory (non-punitive) measure; replaced Metcalfe’s Act of 1835; required every book or newspaper to carry the name of the printer, publisher, and place of printing; a copy of every published book had to be submitted to the local government.
26. **Vernacular Press Act, 1878 (Act IX of 1878) — "The Gagging Act":**
    - Enacted by **Lord Lytton** on **14 March 1878**; discriminatory law targeting **only Indian-language (vernacular) newspapers** (English newspapers completely exempted).
    - District Magistrates empowered to demand security bonds from printers and confiscate printing presses without any prior judicial trial; no right of appeal to a court of law ("No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal").
    - **Amrita Bazar Patrika** (Bengali weekly edited by Sisir Kumar Ghosh and Motilal Ghosh) famously converted itself into an **English weekly overnight** to evade the Act!
    - Som Prakash (Vidyasagar), Bharat Mihir, and Dacca Prakash were prosecuted; repealed in **1882 by Lord Ripon**.
27. **Bal Gangadhar Tilak’s Nationalist Press:**
    - Launched two historic papers from Pune in **1881**: ***Kesari* (in Marathi)** and ***The Mahratta* (in English)**.
    - First Indian political leader to go to jail repeatedly for defending freedom of the press and public causes (1882, 1897, and 1908–1914 in Mandalay).
28. **Repressive Press Legislation of 1908 & 1910:**
    - **Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act, 1908 (Lord Minto II):** Empowered magistrates to forfeit printing presses publishing material inciting violence or rebellion; suppressed *Yugantar*, *Sandhya*, and *Bande Mataram*.
    - **Indian Press Act, 1910:** Revived the worst features of Lytton's 1878 Act; required compulsory security deposits of ₹500 to ₹2,000 from all new presses; government empowered to forfeit deposits without judicial review; repealed in 1921 under Lord Reading on the recommendation of Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru Committee.
29. **Indian Press (Emergency Powers) Act, 1931:**
    - Enacted by Lord Irwin during the Civil Disobedience Movement; granted provincial governments sweeping powers to suppress any literature supporting the Congress or civil disobedience.
30. **UP Regional Historical Newspapers (UPPCS Match Master):**
    - ***Swadesh* (स्वदेश):** Published from **Gorakhpur** (edited by Dashrath Prasad Dwivedi).
    - ***Bharat Bandhu* (भारत बंधु):** Published from **Hathras** (edited by Tota Ram).
    - ***Satyawadi* (सत्यवादी):** Published from **Agra**.
    - ***Shakti* (शक्ति):** Published from **Almora** (edited by Badri Datt Pandey).
    - ***The Pioneer*:** Founded at **Allahabad in 1865** by George Allen (conservative Anglo-Indian daily).
    - ***The Leader*:** Founded at **Allahabad in 1909** by **Madan Mohan Malaviya** (mouthpiece of Indian liberals).
    - ***Abhyudaya* (अभ्युदय):** Hindi weekly started by **Madan Mohan Malaviya in 1907** from Allahabad.
    - ***Pratap* (प्रताप):** Celebrated revolutionary Hindi weekly started at **Kanpur in 1913 by Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi**.
    - ***Aaj* (आज):** Renowned Hindi daily started at **Varanasi in 1920 by Shiv Prasad Gupta**.
31. **Master Match of National Newspapers and Editors:**

32. **Vernacular Press Act Escape Legend:** When Lytton's Gagging Act was promulgated on 14 March 1878, the brothers **Sisir Kumar Ghosh** and **Motilal Ghosh** stayed up all night and converted their bilingual Bengali-English paper *Amrita Bazar Patrika* into an exclusively English weekly from the very next morning (21 March 1878), rendering Lytton's magistrates completely powerless!
33. **First Vernacular Newspaper in India:** ***Samachar Darpan* (समाचार दर्पण)**, a Bengali weekly published on **23 May 1818** by the Baptist Missionary Society from Serampore under the editorship of **John Clark Marshman**.
34. **First Hindi Newspaper in India:** ***Udant Martand* (उदन्त मार्तण्ड - "The Rising Sun")**, started on **30 May 1826** in Calcutta by **Pandit Jugal Kishore Shukla** (a resident of Kanpur); celebrated annually as **Hindi Journalism Day (30 May)**.
35. **First Urdu Newspaper in India:** ***Jam-i-Jahan Numa***, started in Calcutta on **27 March 1822** by Harihar Dutta.
36. **First Indian to Publish a Newspaper:** **Gangadhar Bhattacharya**, who published the weekly *Bengal Gazette* in Calcutta in 1816 (in English).
37. **First Printing Press in India:** Brought by **Portuguese Jesuit missionaries to Goa in 1556**; first book printed was on medicinal plants of India (*Colóquios dos simples e drogas da Índia* by Garcia de Orta in 1563).
38. **First Tamil Printing:** The Danish missionaries established a Tamil printing press at **Tranquebar in 1714** under Bartholomew Ziegenbalg, printing the Tamil New Testament.
39. **Macaulay’s Filtration Failure:** Macaulay assumed European ideas would filter down from the English-educated elite to the masses; instead, it created an alienated bureaucratic class detached from the vernacular-speaking illiterate populace.
40. **Deccan Education Society (1884):** Founded in Pune by **Vishnushastri Chiplunkar**, **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**, **Gopal Ganesh Agarkar**, and Mahadev Ballal Namjoshi; established **Fergusson College (1885)** in Pune; Justice M.G. Ranade was its leading patron.
41. **Pachaiyappa’s College (Madras, 1842):** First non-missionary, non-governmental Hindu higher education institution in South India, established from the bequest of philanthropist Pachaiyappa Mudaliar.
42. **Mayo College, Ajmer (1875):** Founded by Lord Mayo exclusively for the education of the sons of Indian princely rulers and aristocracy ("Eton of the East").
43. **Muir Central College, Allahabad (1872):** Founded by Sir William Muir (Lt. Governor of NWP); designed in magnificent Indo-Saracenic style by William Emerson; became the foundational arts college of Allahabad University in 1887.
44. **G.K. Gokhale's Free Primary Education Bill (1911):** Introduced in the Imperial Legislative Council on 16 March 1911 by **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**, demanding statutory free and compulsory primary education across British India; defeated by the official British majority (38 to 13 votes).
45. **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919) Education Shift:** Transferred education entirely to **Indian Provincial Ministers** under Dyarchy; marked by severe financial starvation since finance remained a "Reserved Subject" with the British Governor.
46. **Dr. Zakir Hussain’s Role in Basic Education:** Headed the committee appointed at Wardha in 1937; formulated child-centered, craft-based curriculum emphasizing correlation of subjects with spinning, weaving, and agriculture.
47. **K.T. Telang & Hunter Commission:** Justice Kashinath Trimbak Telang was an influential Indian member of the Hunter Commission (1882); advocated state funding for indigenous schools.
48. **Woodhead Commission (1944–45) vs Sargent Plan (1944):** Woodhead was famine inquiry; Sargent was education blueprint for 40-year universal schooling.

   - **W**ellesley (**Censorship 1799**)

   - **M**etcalfe (**Liberation 1835**)
   - **L**ytton (**Gagging 1878**)
   - **R**ipon (**Repeal 1882**)
   - **M**into (**Forfeiture 1908/1910**).
3. **Sadler Commission Formula:**
   - **10 + 2 + 3** (High School + Intermediate + Degree)
   - First adopted in **UP** (Intermediate Board, 1921).
4. **Hartog vs Wardha Contrast:**
   - **H**artog = **H**alt & consolidate (stop "Wastage")
   - **W**ardha = **W**ork & craft (Gandhi’s *Nai Talim*).
5. **The Serampore Trio:**
   - **C - M - W**
   - **C**arey, **M**arshman, and **W**ard (Baptist Mission).
