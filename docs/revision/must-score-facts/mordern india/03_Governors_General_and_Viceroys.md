## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The **Regulating Act, 1773** created the office of **Governor-General of Bengal**; first holder was **Warren Hastings (1773–1785)**. The **Supreme Court at Calcutta** was established under it in **1774** with **Sir Elijah Impey** as First Chief Justice.
2. The **Charter Act, 1833** elevated the post to **Governor-General of India**; first holder was **Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835)**. The Company was stripped of all commercial trading functions, becoming purely an administrative body.
3. The **Government of India Act, 1858** ended Company rule. The Governor-General was given the additional title of **Viceroy** (Crown's representative); first Viceroy was **Lord Canning (1858–1862)**. The last British Viceroy was **Lord Mountbatten (1947–48)**; the last Governor-General of India (and only Indian) was **C. Rajagopalachari (1948–1950)**.
4. Under **Pitt’s India Act, 1784**, a system of dual control in Britain was established: the **Board of Control** (6 Commissioners) handled political/military affairs, while the **Court of Directors** handled commercial affairs.
5. The **Charter Act, 1813** ended Company's trade monopoly in India (monopoly in tea trade and trade with China retained) and allocated **₹1 lakh annually** for Indian education. The **Charter Act, 1853** introduced **open competition** for the Indian Civil Services (Macaulay Committee of 1854).
6. The **Indian Councils Act, 1861** legalised the **portfolio system** (introduced by Canning in 1859), restored legislative powers to Madras and Bombay, and initiated Indian nomination to legislative councils. The **1892 Act** allowed discussion on the annual budget and indirect elections.
7. The **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)** introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** and appointed the first Indian, **Satyendra Prasanna Sinha**, as Law Member to the Viceroy’s Executive Council.
8. The **Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford)** introduced **Dyarchy in provincial governments** (transferred vs reserved subjects), a bicameral central legislature, established the Public Service Commission, and provided for a statutory review commission after 10 years (Simon Commission).
9. The **Government of India Act, 1935** abolished provincial dyarchy and granted **Provincial Autonomy**; proposed an All-India Federation and Dyarchy at the Centre (which never came into force); established the Federal Court (1937) and Reserve Bank of India (1935).
10. **Warren Hastings (1772–1785)**: Abolished Clive's Dual Government in **1772**; shifted treasury from Murshidabad to Calcutta; initiated the **Ring Fence Policy**; faced the **Raja Chait Singh Banaras Rebellion (1781)** and fled to Chunar; patronized the foundation of the **Asiatic Society of Bengal (15 Jan 1784)** by Sir William Jones (declined chair); impeached in British Parliament on return (later acquitted).
11. **Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793 & 1805)**: Known as the "Father of Civil Services in India"; established the **Permanent Settlement of Bengal (1793)**; codified laws into the **Cornwallis Code (1793)** based on separation of revenue and judicial powers; created the **Daroga** police circle system; won Third Anglo-Mysore War (Treaty of Seringapatam 1792); died at **Ghazipur (UP) on 5 October 1805** where his tomb stands.
12. **Sir John Shore (1793–1798)**: Followed a strict policy of **non-intervention** (Battle of Kharda 1795, Marathas defeated Nizam while British remained neutral); helped Cornwallis draft the 1793 Permanent Settlement regulations.
13. **Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)**: Called himself the "Bengal Tiger"; perfected the **Subsidiary Alliance System** (first signed by Hyderabad 1798); eliminated Tipu Sultan in Fourth Mysore War (1799); signed Treaty of Bassein (1802) with Peshwa Baji Rao II; established **Fort William College (1800)** at Calcutta to train young British civilians.
14. **Sir George Barlow (1805–1807)**: Strict non-interventionist; tenure saw the **Vellore Mutiny of 1806** (sepoys revolted over new turban regulations and banning of caste marks/beards).
15. **Lord Minto I (1807–1813)**: Concluded the **Treaty of Amritsar (1809)** with Maharaja Ranjit Singh through Charles Metcalfe, fixing the River Sutlej as the boundary; sent Malcolm mission to Persia and Elphinstone mission to Kabul.
16. **Lord Hastings (1813–1823)**: Renounced non-intervention; waged **Anglo-Nepal Gurkha War (1814–1816)** ending with Treaty of Sugauli; crushed the **Pindaris (1817–1818)**; waged **Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1819)**, extinguished the Peshwaship and exiled Baji Rao II to Bithoor; introduced Ryotwari system (Munro) in Madras and Mahalwari (Mackenzie) in North-Western Provinces.
17. **Lord Amherst (1823–1828)**: Fought **First Anglo-Burmese War (1824–1826)** ending with Treaty of Yandabo (1826); tenure saw the **Barrackpore Sepoy Mutiny of 1824** (47th Native Infantry refused to sail to Burma).
18. **Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835)**: Most liberal Governor-General; abolished **Sati** via Regulation XVII of **December 1829**; suppressed **Thuggee** under Colonel William Sleeman; prohibited female infanticide; appointed Macaulay as Law Member; resolved Anglicist-Orientalist controversy by adopting English as medium of higher education (**Macaulay's Minute, 1835**); became first Governor-General of India under 1833 Act.
19. **Sir Charles Metcalfe (1835–1836)**: Passed the Press Act of 1835 repealing John Adam's 1823 licensing rules, earning the title **"Liberator of the Indian Press"**.
20. **Lord Auckland (1836–1842)**: Pursued the aggressive Forward Policy toward Afghanistan; plunged Britain into the disastrous **First Anglo-Afghan War (1839–1842)**; renovated the Grand Trunk Road (Calcutta to Delhi).
21. **Lord Ellenborough (1842–1844)**: Terminated the First Afghan War; annexed **Sindh in 1843** through Sir Charles Napier (*"Peccavi"*); **abolished slavery** throughout British India under **Act V of 1843**.
22. **Lord Hardinge I (1844–1848)**: Fought **First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–1846)** ending with Treaty of Lahore and Treaty of Amritsar (selling Kashmir to Gulab Singh); suppressed female infanticide and human sacrifice (Meriah) among the Khonds of Odisha.
23. **Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) — The Moderniser and Expansionist:**
    - Aggressive application of **Doctrine of Lapse** (Satara 1848, Jaitpur & Sambalpur 1849, Baghat 1850, Udaipur 1852, Jhansi 1853, Nagpur 1854).
    - Annexed **Punjab (1849)** after Second Sikh War; took Lower Burma / Pegu (1852); annexed **Awadh (1856)** on grounds of misgovernance (Outram report).
    - First Railway Line: **Bombay to Thane (16 April 1853, 34 km)**.
    - First Electric Telegraph Line: Calcutta to Agra (1853).
    - **Post Office Act (1854)** with uniform half-anna postage stamps across India.
    - Established separate Public Works Department (**PWD**).
    - Educational Magna Carta: **Wood’s Despatch (1854)**.
    - Drafted **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act** (passed 1856).
24. **Lord Canning (1856–1862)**: Passed Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (July 1856); Governor-General during **Revolt of 1857**; first **Viceroy of India** under GOI Act 1858; read Queen Victoria’s Proclamation at **Allahabad Darbar on 1 Nov 1858**; established universities at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras (1857); enacted Indian Penal Code (1860) and High Courts Act (1861).
25. **Lord Elgin I (1862–1863)**: Suppressed the **Wahabi Movement** centered at Patna; died suddenly at Dharamshala (Himachal Pradesh).
26. **Sir John Lawrence (1864–1869)**: Followed foreign policy of **"Masterly Inactivity"** toward Afghanistan; established High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras in **1865**; appointed **Campbell Famine Commission (1866)** after Orissa Famine; created the Indian Forest Department.
27. **Lord Mayo (1869–1872)**: Initiated **financial decentralization (1870)**; conducted the **First (non-synchronous) Census in 1872**; established Dept of Agriculture and Commerce; founded Mayo College at Ajmer; **only Viceroy to be assassinated in office** (by convict Sher Ali Afridi at Port Blair, Andaman Islands, on 8 Feb 1872).
28. **Lord Northbrook (1872–1876)**: Handled Bihar Famine (1873–74); faced the **Kuka Movement** in Punjab (Baba Ram Singh deported to Rangoon); trial of Gaekwad of Baroda; Prince of Wales visited India (1875); resigned over Disraeli’s Afghan policy.
29. **Lord Lytton (1876–1880) — The "Viceroy of Reverse Genius":**
    - Enacted **Vernacular Press Act, 1878** ("The Gagging Act") to muzzle Indian-language newspapers.
    - Passed **Indian Arms Act, 1878** (Indians required license to carry weapons, Europeans exempt).
    - Lowered maximum age limit for ICS examination from 21 to **19 years**.
    - Organised lavish Imperial Delhi Durbar (1 Jan 1877) conferring title **Kaiser-i-Hind** on Queen Victoria while millions starved in the Great Famine of 1876–78.
    - Formed **Richard Strachey Famine Commission (1878)**.
    - Launched Second Anglo-Afghan War (1878–80) under **"Proud Reserve"** forward policy.
30. **Lord Ripon (1880–1884) — "The Good Viceroy" / "Father of Local Self-Government":**
    - Repealed the hated Vernacular Press Act in 1882.
    - Issued famous **Local Self-Government Resolution (1882)** establishing elected local boards and district councils.
    - Conducted **First Regular Synchronous Decennial Census (1881)**.
    - Passed **First Factory Act, 1881** to improve child labour conditions.
    - Appointed **Hunter Commission on Education (1882)**.
    - **Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883)**: Proposed giving Indian magistrates jurisdiction over Europeans; triggered fierce racial backlash by Europeans resulting in compromise.
31. **Lord Dufferin (1884–1888)**: Establishment of the **Indian National Congress (1885)** in Bombay; called INC a "microscopic minority"; fought **Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885)** annexing Upper Burma completely; passed Bengal Tenancy Act (1885).
32. **Lord Lansdowne (1888–1894)**: Passed **Second Factory Act, 1891** (weekly holiday, 11-hour day for women); passed Age of Consent Act 1891 (minimum marriage age 12); appointed **Durand Commission (1893)** to draw the Afghan boundary (Durand Line); Indian Councils Act 1892.
33. **Lord Elgin II (1894–1899)**: Great famine of 1896–97; appointed **Lyall Famine Commission (1897)**; bubonic plague epidemic in Bombay/Pune; assassination of Plague Commissioner Rand by Chapekar brothers (1897); declared: *"India was won by the sword and by the sword it shall be held"*.
34. **Lord Curzon (1899–1905) — Peak of Imperial Control:**
    - **Partition of Bengal (16 October 1905)** creating Bengal and Eastern Bengal & Assam.
    - Appointed **Police Commission (1902)** under Sir Andrew Fraser (created CID).
    - Appointed Universities Commission under Thomas Raleigh resulting in **Indian Universities Act, 1904**.
    - Enacted **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act, 1904** and revived Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) under **John Marshall**.
    - MacDonnell Famine Commission (1900) & Sir Colin Scott-Moncrieff Irrigation Commission (1901).
    - Dispatched **Younghusband Military Mission to Tibet (1903–04)**.
    - Resigned in 1905 after dispute with Commander-in-Chief Lord Kitchener over military member in council.
35. **Lord Minto II (1905–1910)**: Enacted **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)** introducing separate communal electorates for Muslims; Satyendra Prasanna Sinha appointed first Indian Law Member; foundation of Muslim League at Dhaka (1906); Surat Split of Congress (1907).
36. **Lord Hardinge II (1910–1916)**: Held grand **Delhi Durbar of 1911** with King George V and Queen Mary; **annulled Partition of Bengal**; announced **transfer of imperial capital from Calcutta to Delhi**; survived bomb attack by Rash Behari Bose (Dec 1912); Mahatma Gandhi returned to India (9 Jan 1915); foundation of Hindu Mahasabha (1915) and Banaras Hindu University (1916).
37. **Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921)**: Enacted **Government of India Act 1919** (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms) introducing provincial dyarchy; passed repressive **Rowlatt Act (1919)**; **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)** by General Dyer; launch of Non-Cooperation and Khilafat Movements (1920); appointed **Sadler Commission (1917)** on university education.
38. **Lord Reading (1921–1926)**: The only Jewish Viceroy of India; Chauri Chaura incident (4 Feb 1922) and suspension of Non-Cooperation Movement; formation of Swaraj Party (1923) by C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru; **Kakori Train Action (9 Aug 1925)**; suppression of Moplah Rebellion (1921) and Multan riots; foundation of RSS (1925) and CPI (1925).
39. **Lord Irwin (1926–1931)**: Appointment and boycott of **Simon Commission (1927–28)**; Nehru Report (1928); Jinnah’s 14 Points (1929); Lahore Congress resolution for **Purna Swaraj (1929)**; launch of Civil Disobedience Movement with **Dandi Salt March (12 March – 6 April 1930)**; First Round Table Conference (1930); signed **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931)**; execution of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru (23 March 1931).
40. **Lord Willingdon (1931–1936)**: Resumed Civil Disobedience Movement, jailed Gandhi and Congress leaders; Ramsay MacDonald’s **Communal Award (August 1932)** and **Poona Pact (Sept 1932)**; Second (1931) and Third (1932) Round Table Conferences; passed **Government of India Act, 1935**; establishment of Reserve Bank of India (1935).
41. **Lord Linlithgow (1936–1944) — Longest Tenure as Viceroy:**
    - General Elections of 1937 and Congress Ministries formed in 7 provinces.
    - Congress Ministries resigned in Oct–Nov 1939 over unilateral dragging of India into WWII; Muslim League celebrated "Day of Deliverance" (22 Dec 1939).
    - Muslim League passed **Lahore Resolution for Pakistan (March 1940)**.
    - Announced **August Offer (1940)**; Individual Satyagraha launched by Gandhi (Vinoba Bhave first, Nehru second).
    - Handled **Cripps Mission (March 1942)** ("post-dated cheque").
    - **Quit India Movement (August 1942)** launched; widespread arrests; parallel governments in Ballia, Tamluk, Satara.
    - Horrific **Bengal Famine of 1943** (estimated 3 million dead).
42. **Lord Wavell (1944–1947)**: Held **Simla Conference (1945)** to discuss Wavell Plan; end of WWII; INA Trials at Red Fort (Nov 1945); Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny (Feb 1946); **Cabinet Mission (March 1946)** proposing Union of India with grouping; Muslim League's **Direct Action Day (16 Aug 1946)** causing Great Calcutta Killings; **Interim Government (2 Sept 1946)** headed by Jawaharlal Nehru; Constituent Assembly first meeting (9 Dec 1946).
43. **Lord Mountbatten (March 1947 – June 1948)**: Last British Viceroy; announced **3rd June Plan (Mountbatten Plan)** for partition of British India; British Parliament passed **Indian Independence Act, 1947**; oversaw partition and bloody riots; remained first Governor-General of independent Dominion of India till June 1948.
44. **C. Rajagopalachari (June 1948 – 26 Jan 1950)**: First and only Indian Governor-General of India; held office until the Constitution came into effect and Dr. Rajendra Prasad was sworn in as the first President of India.
45. **Chronology of Famine Commissions (Exam Match Trap):**
    - **Campbell Commission (1866)** → Sir John Lawrence
    - **Strachey Commission (1878)** → Lord Lytton
    - **Lyall Commission (1897)** → Lord Elgin II
    - **MacDonnell Commission (1900)** → Lord Curzon
    - **Woodhead Commission (1944–45)** → Lord Wavell (Bengal Famine).
46. **Chronology of Major Education Commissions:**
    - **Macaulay Minute (1835)** → Lord William Bentinck
    - **Wood's Despatch (1854)** → Lord Dalhousie
    - **Hunter Commission (1882)** → Lord Ripon
    - **Raleigh Commission / Universities Act (1902/1904)** → Lord Curzon
    - **Sadler Commission (1917)** → Lord Chelmsford
    - **Hartog Committee (1929)** → Lord Irwin
    - **Sargent Plan (1944)** → Lord Wavell.
47. **Afghan Policy Paradigm (Master Match):**
    - Forward Policy I: **Lord Auckland** (Disaster, 1839–42)
    - Masterly Inactivity: **Sir John Lawrence** (Non-interference, 1864–69)
    - Proud Reserve / Forward Policy II: **Lord Lytton** (Second War, 1878–80).
48. **Viceroys Assassinated / Died in India:**
    - **Lord Cornwallis** died at Ghazipur (UP) in 1805 (Tomb in Ghazipur).
    - **Lord Elgin I** died at Dharamshala in 1863.
    - **Lord Mayo** assassinated at Port Blair (Andamans) by Sher Ali Afridi in 1872 (only assassinated Viceroy).
49. **Factory Acts Progression:**
    - First Factory Act (**1881, Lord Ripon**): Child labour below 7 banned; 7–12 age work limited to 9 hours.
    - Second Factory Act (**1891, Lord Lansdowne**): Weekly holiday; women limited to 11 hours; children below 9 banned.
50. **Summary of British Office Ladder:**
    - Governor of Bengal: Clive (1757)
    - Governor-General of Bengal: Warren Hastings (1773, Regulating Act)
    - Governor-General of India: William Bentinck (1833, Charter Act)
    - Viceroy and Governor-General: Lord Canning (1858, GOI Act)
    - Governor-General of Free India: Mountbatten (1947) & C. Rajagopalachari (1948–50).
