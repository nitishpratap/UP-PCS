## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Three Distinct Popular Protest Streams:**
   - **Peasant Movements:** Fought against excessive land revenue, illegal cesses (*abwabs*), rack-renting, forced commercial crops (indigo *dadan*), eviction (*bedakhli*), and usurious rural moneylenders (*sahukars*).
   - **Tribal Uprisings:** Fought against loss of traditional communal landholding (*Khuntkatti*), forest laws restricting shifting cultivation (*podu/jhum*) and minor forest produce, Christian missionary intrusions, and exploitation by outsider plainsmen (**Dikus**).
   - **Labour Movements:** Fought against 14-hour working days, starvation wages, absence of weekly holidays, physical abuse by European mill managers, and demanded statutory right to form trade unions and strike.
2. **Sanyasi–Fakir Rebellion (~1763–1800, Bengal–Bihar):**
   - Earliest armed resistance against British East India Company rule; triggered by the ruinous Great Bengal Famine of 1770 and British restrictions on holy pilgrimages.
   - Hindu sanyasis (Dasnami order under **Bhawani Pathak** and **Devi Chaudhurani**) joined Muslim fakirs (Madariya order under **Majnu Shah, Chirag Ali, Musa Shah**); raided Company factories and treasuries.
   - Immortalized by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** in his political novels ***Anandamath* (1882)** (source of *Vande Mataram*) and ***Devi Chaudhurani* (1884)**.
3. **Paika Rebellion (1817, Odisha):**
   - Led by **Bakshi Jagabandhu Bidyadhar**, military commander (*Buxi*) of the King of Khurda.
   - Cause: Extinction of the traditional rent-free hereditary land tenures (*paikan*) of the peasant-militia (*Paikas*), currency disruptions (introduction of rupee replacing cowrie), and salt monopoly.
   - Captured Puri and Khurda; suppressed by the British; considered by many historians as the precursor to 1857.
4. **Early North-Eastern Tribal Uprisings:**
   - **Ahom Revolt (~1828, Assam):** Led by **Gomdhar Konwar** against the British failure to evacuate Assam after the First Burmese War (1826); *Examiner Trap: 1828 is correct; 1815 is a standard paper trap.*
   - **Khasi Revolt (1829–1833, Meghalaya):** Led by **Tirut Singh (U Tirot Sing)**, Syiem (chief) of Nongkhlaw; fought against British construction of a military road linking Brahmaputra and Surma valleys through the Khasi hills.
5. **Chotanagpur Tribal Resistance:**
   - **Kol Rebellion (1831–1832):** Inhabitants of Chotanagpur (Ranchi, Singhbhum, Hazaribagh, Palamau); led by **Buddhu Bhagat**, Jhindrai Manki, and Madara Mahato; triggered by the transfer of tribal lands to outsider Sikh and Muslim revenue farmers (*thikadars*); suppressed with heavy artillery.
   - **Santhal Hul (Rebellion, 1855–1856):** Fought in **Damin-i-Koh** (Rajmahal hills, Bihar/Jharkhand); led by four brothers **Sidhu, Kanhu, Chand, and Bhairav**; mobilized over 60,000 Santhals against British zamindars and mahajans; proclaimed the end of Company rule; suppressed by General Lloyd; resulted in Act XXXVII of 1855 creating the separate **Santhal Parganas district** with non-alienable tribal land rights.
6. **Indigo Revolt (1859–1860, Bengal) — The Great Blue Rebellion:**
   - Began in **Govindpur village, Nadia district (Bengal)** in September 1859 under the leadership of **Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Charan Biswas**.
   - Cause: European planters coerced ryots into signing contracts and accepting cash advances (**Dadan / दादनी**) to cultivate indigo on 3/20th or more of their most fertile land at 1/5th market price.
   - Peasant solidarity: Refused cash advances, boycotted planters, physically resisted planter thugs (*lathiyals*), and used legal courts.
   - Supported by the educated intelligentsia: **Harish Chandra Mukherjee** relentlessly exposed planter terror in his journal ***The Hindu Patriot***; **Dinabandhu Mitra** published the historic play ***Nil Darpan* (1860)** (translated into English by Michael Madhusudan Dutt; Rev. James Long was fined and imprisoned for publishing it).
   - Outcome: Government appointed the **Indigo Commission (1860)** under W.S. Seton-Karr; declared indigo cultivation non-profitable and prohibited planters from forcing ryots to sow indigo.
7. **Pabna Agrarian Leagues (1873–1876, Bengal):**
   - Erupted in Yusufshahi pargana of Pabna district; led by **Ishan Chandra Roy (Ishan Raja)**, Shambhu Pal, and Khoodi Mollah.
   - Cause: Zamindars illegally raised rents far above legal limits and attempted to prevent tenants from acquiring occupancy rights under Act X of 1859.
   - Method: Formed peaceful Agrarian Leagues; refused enhanced rents; deposited legal rents in civil courts; raised funds to fight lawsuits ("We want to be the ryots of Her Majesty Queen Victoria and of Her alone").
   - Result: Enactment of the **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885**, protecting occupancy tenants from arbitrary rent-hikes and evictions.
8. **Deccan Riots (1875, Maharashtra):**
   - Erupted in **Supa village, Poona district** in May 1875, spreading to Ahmednagar, Satara, and Sholapur.
   - Cause: Crash in cotton prices following the end of the American Civil War (1865), accompanied by 50% land revenue enhancement by the colonial government; drove ryots into the clutches of usurious Marwari and Gujarati moneylenders (*sahukars*).
   - Method: Peasants formed social boycotts against moneylenders; attacked their houses and shops, seizing and publicly burning mortgage bonds, decrees, and account books without killing.
   - Supported by the **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** (Justice M.G. Ranade).
   - Result: Government appointed the Deccan Riots Commission (1876), leading to the **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act, 1879**, prohibiting the arrest and imprisonment of peasants for civil debt and allowing courts to reduce unfair interest rates.
9. **Birsa Munda & The Ulgulan (1899–1900, Chotanagpur):**
   - Known as the **Munda Ulgulan (The Great Tumult)**; led by **Birsa Munda** (born 15 Nov 1875 at Ulihatu, Ranchi; revered as **"Dharti Aaba"** / Father of the Earth).
   - Cause: Destruction of the traditional communal land system (**Khuntkatti**) by British forest contractors, moneylenders, and landlords (*thikadars*); conversion drives by German/English Christian missions.
   - Movement: Birsa claimed divine revelations; synthesized traditional animist beliefs with Vaishnavism; mobilized Munda and Oraon warriors armed with bows and arrows to overthrow British rule and establish **Birsa Raj** ("Let the Kingdom of the Queen end and our Kingdom be established").
   - Crushed at Sail Rakab hills near Dombari (Jan 1900); Birsa captured and died of cholera in Ranchi jail on **9 June 1900**.
   - Result: Government enacted the **Chotanagpur Tenancy Act, 1908 (CNTA)**, recognizing Khuntkatti rights and legally banning the transfer of tribal land to non-tribals (*Dikus*).
10. **Tana Bhagat Movement (1914, Chotanagpur):**
    - Founded in **1914** among the Oraon tribe of Ranchi by **Jatra Oraon (Jatra Bhagat)**.
    - Religious and messianic reform movement: urged Oraons to give up meat, liquor, and animal sacrifice, and refuse forced labour (*begar*) and payment of rent to landlords and the British government; later integrated fully with Mahatma Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement, adopting khadi and satyagraha.
11. **Kuka Movement (Namdhari Movement, ~1872, Punjab):**
    - Founded as a socio-religious reform movement by **Bhagat Jawahar Mal (Sian Sahib)** and Baba Balak Singh; turned into a powerful political anti-British campaign under **Baba Ram Singh**.
    - Preached boycott of British education, British laws, foreign cloth, and government jobs (early precursor to non-cooperation); advocated cow protection.
    - In 1872, Kuka rebels clashed with British authorities; 65 Kukas were executed by being blown from cannons at Malerkotla; Baba Ram Singh was deported to Rangoon where he died in 1885.
12. **Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar) — Gandhi’s First Civil Disobedience:**
    - Organized on the persistent invitation of local peasant **Raj Kumar Shukla**.
    - Cause: European planters enforced the oppressive **Tinkathia System (तीनकठिया प्रथा)**, forcing ryots to cultivate indigo on **3/20th** of their best land and charging exorbitant illegal cesses (*sharahbeshi*) to release them.
    - Accompanied by **Rajendra Prasad, J.B. Kripalani, Mahadev Desai, Narhari Parikh, and Anugrah Narayan Sinha**.
    - Outcome: Government appointed Champaran Agrarian Committee with Gandhi as member; abolished the Tinkathia system and refunded 25% of the illegally extorted money to peasants; Rabindranath Tagore conferred the title **"Mahatma"** on Gandhi during this campaign.
13. **Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat) — Gandhi’s First Non-Cooperation:**
    - Cause: Severe crop failure in Kheda district due to drought; revenue code exempted peasants from tax if produce was less than 25% of normal, but British officials refused remission and seized cattle and land.
    - Led by **Mahatma Gandhi** and **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (supported by Indulal Yagnik and Mohanlal Pandya); peasants refused to pay revenue; government secretly ordered recovery only from those who could pay.
14. **UP Kisan Sabha (February 1918, Lucknow):**
    - Founded in **February 1918** by **Indra Narayan Dwivedi**, **Gauri Shankar Mishra**, and **Madan Mohan Malaviya**.
    - *Examiner Trap: Neither Baba Ramchandra nor Swami Sahajanand nor Jawaharlal Nehru founded the UP Kisan Sabha in 1918!*
15. **Oudh Kisan Sabha (October 1920, Pratapgarh):**
    - Mobilized by **Baba Ramchandra** (a Maharashtrian Brahman ascetic who recited Tulsidas's *Ramcharitmanas* to organize peasants) alongside **Jawaharlal Nehru**, Gauri Shankar Mishra, and Mata Badal Pande.
    - Formed on **17 October 1920 at Pratapgarh (UP)**; led mass peasant protests against arbitrary evictions (*bedakhli*), high rents, and Nazrana; led to the enactment of the **Oudh Rent (Amendment) Act, 1921**.
16. **Eka Movement (1921–1922, Awadh):**
    - Peasant unity (*Eka*) movement centered in northern Awadh districts: **Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur, and Barabanki**.
    - Led by radical low-caste peasant leader **Madari Pasi**.
    - Religious-symbolic oaths: Peasants took vows before sacred fires promising to pay only recorded rents, demand written receipts, refuse unpaid forced labour (*begar*), refuse to vacate land on eviction orders, and remain united; suppressed by colonial police in 1922.
17. **Moplah Rebellion (Malabar, 1921):**
    - Erupted in **August 1921** among Muslim tenant-farmers (**Moplahs**) of Malabar (Kerala) against high-caste Hindu landlords (**Jenmis**) backed by British administration.
    - Led by **Variyamkunnath Kunjahammed Haji** and **Ali Musliyar**; initially merged with the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements; took a violent communal turn, attacking landlords, police stations, and treasury; brutally suppressed by British troops under Martial Law; infamous **"Wagon Tragedy" (20 Nov 1921)** where 64 Moplah prisoners died of asphyxiation in a closed railway goods wagon.
18. **Bardoli Satyagraha (1928, Gujarat):**
    - Fought against the British government's arbitrary **22% hike in land revenue** in Bardoli taluqa (Surat district).
    - Led with extraordinary organizational discipline by **Vallabhbhai Patel**; women of Bardoli conferred the title **"Sardar"** on him.
    - Peasants refused all revenue payment, courted arrest, and faced land forfeiture; Maxwell-Broomfield Inquiry Commission conceded the injustice and reduced the revenue increase from 22% to **6.03%**.
19. **All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS, 11 April 1936, Lucknow):**
    - Founded during the Lucknow Session of the Indian National Congress in **April 1936**.
    - First President: **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati** (peasant patriarch of Bihar).
    - First General Secretary: **Prof. N.G. Ranga** (peasant leader of Andhra).
    - Kisan Manifesto drafted demanding abolition of zamindari, 50% cut in land revenue, cancellation of agricultural debts, and tenancy rights; Indulal Yagnik published the *Kisan Bulletin*.
20. **Tebhaga Movement (1946–1947, Bengal):**
    - Launched by the Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha (affiliated with CPI) in North Bengal (Dinajpur, Rangpur, Jalpaiguri, Midnapore).
    - Demand: Based on the **Floud Commission Report (1940)**, sharecroppers (**Bargadars**) demanded **two-thirds (Tebhaga)** share of the harvested paddy for themselves, reducing the non-cultivating landlord's (**Jotedar's**) share to one-third.
    - Slogan: *"Nij khamare dhan tolo"* (Thresh paddy in your own courtyard, not jotedar's house); led by Charu Majumdar, Kansari Halder, and Rani Mitra.
21. **Telangana Peasant Armed Struggle (1946–1951, Hyderabad State):**
    - Massive armed peasant uprising in Nalgonda, Warangal, and Khammam districts of the Nizam's princely state of Hyderabad.
    - Led by the Communist Party of India (CPI) and Andhra Mahasabha (Ravi Narayana Reddy, Sundarayya, Chandra Rajeswara Rao).
    - Fought against the brutal forced labour (**Vetti**) and feudal extortions of big landlords (**Deshmukhs / Doras**) and the Nizam's terror militia (**Razakars** under Kasim Razvi).
    - Guerrilla squads (*dalams*) liberated 3,000 villages, redistributed 1 million acres of land; movement continued even after the Indian Army launched **Operation Polo (Sept 1948)**, until officially withdrawn by CPI in October 1951.
22. **Rampa Rebellion (1922–1924, Andhra Pradesh):**
    - Fought in the Godavari agency hill tract; led by ascetic guerrilla hero **Alluri Sitarama Raju**.
    - Protested against the oppressive **Madras Forest Act, 1882**, which banned shifting cultivation (*podu*) and minor forest gathering; carried out lightning guerrilla ambushes against police stations; Raju shot dead on 7 May 1924.
23. **Rani Gaidinliu & Zeliangrong Naga Rebellion (1930s):**
    - Anti-British religious-political tribal revolt in Manipur and Naga hills, launched by **Haipou Jadonang** (executed in 1931).
    - Succeeded by his 16-year-old cousin **Rani Gaidinliu**, who organized guerrilla resistance against British rule; captured in 1932 and sentenced to life imprisonment; Jawaharlal Nehru met her in Shillong jail in 1937, gave her the title **"Rani"**, and campaigned for her release (freed in 1947).
24. **Bhil Uprisings (Western Ghats & Rajasthan):**
    - Bhils of Khandesh (Maharashtra) revolted in **1818** against British intrusion, led by Sewaram (1825).
    - **Bhagat Movement (1883, Rajasthan):** Led by **Govind Guru** among the Bhils of Banswara and Dungarpur; culminated in the horrific **Mangarh Massacre (17 Nov 1913)** where British and princely troops slaughtered over 1,500 peaceful Bhils.
25. **Trade Union Movement in India (Chronological Spine):**
    - **1890:** **Bombay Millhands Association** founded by **N.M. Lokhande** (first labour association in India; published *Dinabandhu*).
    - **1918:** **Madras Labour Union** founded by **B.P. Wadia** (first modern organized trade union in India with regular membership and dues).
    - **1920:** **All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC)** founded in Bombay on **31 October 1920**; **Lala Lajpat Rai** first President, **Chaman Lall** first General Secretary.
    - **1926:** **Trade Unions Act, 1926** passed, granting legal registration and immunity from criminal conspiracy for legitimate union activities.
    - **1947:** **Indian National Trade Union Congress (INTUC)** founded on **3 May 1947** by Sardar Patel, Gulzarilal Nanda, and J.B. Kripalani (Congress wing).
    - **1955:** **Bharatiya Mazdoor Sangh (BMS)** founded on **23 July 1955** by **Dattopant Thengadi** (affiliated with RSS).
    - **1970:** **Centre of Indian Trade Unions (CITU)** formed by CPI(M) stream (UPPCS often maps UTUC → CPI(M) in old papers).
26. **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976:** Enacted pursuant to **Article 23 of the Constitution**; officially freed bonded labourers from generational debt servitude; extinguished all existing bonded debts.
27. **Thakkar Bapa (Amritlal Vithaldas Thakkar, 1869–1951):** Close associate of Mahatma Gandhi; devoted his entire life to the uplift of tribal communities; first to coin and popularize the respectful term **"Adivasi" (आदिवासी - original inhabitant)**; founded the *Bhil Seva Mandal* in 1922.
28. **Ramosi Peasant Force (1879, Maharashtra):** Led by **Vasudev Balwant Phadke** ("Father of Indian Armed Rebellion"); organized Ramosi tribal peasants into an armed guerrilla band to loot British treasuries and establish an independent Indian Republic; captured in 1879 and died on hunger strike in Aden jail (1883).
29. **Gadkari Revolt (1844, Kolhapur):** Hereditary peasant-soldier class (*Gadkaris*) in Kolhapur princely state revolted against British administrative reforms and land revenue exactions; seized Samangarh and Bhudargarh forts.
30. **Waghera Rising (1818–1820, Baroda):** Waghera chiefs of Okha Mandal (Gujarat) revolted against the exactions of the British-backed Gaekwad of Baroda.
31. **Khond Uprising & Meriah Sacrifice (1837–1856):** Led by **Chakra Bisoi** in the hill tracts of Odisha; opposed British suppression of the customary human sacrifice (**Meriah**) by Captain Campbell and the entry of outsider moneylenders.
32. **All India Kisan Sabha Sessions Ladder:**
    - First Session (1936): **Lucknow** (Swami Sahajanand Saraswati).
    - Second Session (1936): **Faizpur** (presided over by N.G. Ranga; held alongside the first rural INC session).
    - Third Session (1938): **Comilla** (Bengal).
    - Fourth Session (1939): **Gaya** (Bihar, Narendra Deva).
33. **Bakhasht Movement in Bihar (1937–1939):** Agitation launched by Swami Sahajanand and Karyanand Sharma against the eviction of tenant-farmers from *Bakhasht* lands (land formerly cultivated by tenants but resumed by zamindars during depression).
34. **Punnapra-Vayalar Revolt (1946, Travancore):** Armed working-class and agricultural labourer uprising organized by the CPI against the autocratic Diwan of Travancore, **Sir C.P. Ramaswami Aiyer**, and his "American Model" constitution; brutally crushed by state armed forces.
35. **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan, 1920):** Founded in **1920** by **Mahatma Gandhi** and **Anasuya Sarabhai**; based on Gandhian principles of arbitration, trusteeship, and peaceful non-strike resolution.
36. **N.M. Joshi’s Pioneer Role in Labour:** Representative of Indian labour at the first International Labour Conference (ILO) in Washington (1919); founded the **Social Service League (1911)**; served as General Secretary of AITUC (1925–29); broke away in 1929 to form the All-India Trade Union Federation (AITUF).
37. **Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929–1933):** British colonial trial of 31 prominent trade unionists and communist leaders (including 3 Englishmen: Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley, Lester Hutchinson, alongside S.A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmad, P.C. Joshi); defended by a National Defence Committee headed by Motilal Nehru and Jawaharlal Nehru.
38. **Public Safety Bill & Trade Disputes Act (1929):** Repressive colonial laws aimed at crushing the communist-led strike wave; Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw bombs into the Central Legislative Assembly on **8 April 1929** specifically to protest against these two anti-labour bills!
39. **Pagal Panthis & Tipu Shah (1825–1835, Bengal):** Semi-religious sect founded by **Karam Shah** in northern Mymensingh; his son **Tipu Shah** organized Hajong and Garo tribal peasants against zamindari rent extortions; stormed British police outposts and established an independent tribal court.
40. **Faraizi Peasant Resistance under Dudu Miyan (1838–1857):** Haji Shariatullah’s son **Dudu Miyan** turned the religious Faraizi movement into an egalitarian peasant resistance; declared that "The earth belongs to God, and no one has the right to demand rent or tax upon it"; organized peasant *lathiyals* against European indigo planters.
41. **Bijolia Movement (1897–1941, Rajasthan):** Longest-running non-violent peasant movement in India; organized by the peasants of Mewar against 84 different feudal cesses (*lag-bag*) and forced labour (*begar*); led by **Sadhu Sitaram Das**, **Vijay Singh Pathik (Bhup Singh)**, and Manikya Lal Verma.
42. **Kisan Sabha in Rajasthan (Shekhawati Movement, 1930s):** Led by **Kharag Singh** and **Harlal Singh** against the oppressive Rajput Jagirdars of the Shekhawati region.
43. **Kisan Sabha in South India:** Organized by **N.G. Ranga** and **E.M.S. Namboodiripad**; established the Indian Peasant Institute at Nidubrolu (Andhra) in 1933 to train rural peasant organizers.
44. **Warli Adivasi Revolt (1945–1947, Maharashtra):** Tribal revolt of the Warli community in Thana district (Maharashtra) against forced bonded labour (*vethbigar*) and sexual exploitation by timber contractors and landlords; organized by **Godavari Parulekar** of the Kisan Sabha and CPI.
45. **Alluri Sitarama Raju's Background:** Born in Mogallu (Andhra); non-tribal astrologer and herbalist who took sannyas at age 18; adopted tribal life among Koyas; championed total prohibition of liquor and wearing khadi, while advocating armed guerrilla warfare to expel the British.
46. **Babu Kunwar Singh’s Regional Agrarian Links:** 80-year-old Rajput chieftain of Jagdishpur; dispossessed of his estate by the Court of Wards; his leadership of 1857 in Bihar united sepoys, taluqdars, and dispossessed peasants into a single anti-Company front.
47. **Chauri Chaura Peasant Character (4 Feb 1922):** The procession at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP) that burnt the police station killing 22 policemen was led by local peasant volunteers of the Kisan Sabha and Congress under **Bhagwan Ahir** (a retired army sepoy).
48. **Master Chronology of Major Peasant Movements:**
    - 1763–1800: Sanyasi-Fakir Rebellion
    - 1817: Paika Rebellion
    - 1859–60: Indigo Revolt (Nadia)
    - 1873–76: Pabna Agrarian Leagues
    - 1875: Deccan Riots (Poona/Ahmednagar)
    - 1917: Champaran Satyagraha
    - 1918: Kheda Satyagraha
    - 1918: UP Kisan Sabha (Dwivedi)
    - 1920: Oudh Kisan Sabha (Baba Ramchandra)
    - 1921–22: Eka Movement (Madari Pasi)
    - 1921: Moplah Rebellion (Malabar)
    - 1928: Bardoli Satyagraha (Sardar Patel)
    - 1936: All India Kisan Sabha (Lucknow, Sahajanand)
    - 1946–47: Tebhaga Movement (Bengal)
    - 1946–51: Telangana Armed Struggle.
49. **Master Chronology of Major Tribal Movements:**
    - 1818–1831: Bhil Uprisings (Khandesh/Rajasthan)
    - 1828: Ahom Revolt (Assam, Gomdhar Konwar)
    - 1829: Khasi Revolt (Meghalaya, Tirut Singh)
    - 1831–32: Kol Rebellion (Buddhu Bhagat)
    - 1855–56: Santhal Hul (Sidhu & Kanhu)
    - 1874: Kharwar Movement (Bhagirath Manjhi)
    - 1899–1900: Munda Ulgulan (Birsa Munda)
    - 1913: Mangarh Massacre (Govind Guru)
    - 1914: Tana Bhagat Movement (Jatra Oraon)
    - 1921: Chenchu Tribal Movement (Andhra)
    - 1922–24: Rampa Rebellion (Alluri Sitarama Raju)
    - 1930s: Zeliangrong Movement (Rani Gaidinliu).
50. **Core Historical Synthesis:** Tribal uprisings were intense, bloody, messianic battles for sovereign autonomy and ancestral land, while peasant movements evolved from legalistic anti-rent protests (Pabna, Deccan) into disciplined Gandhian satyagrahas (Champaran, Bardoli) and finally militant anti-feudal land redistribution struggles (Tebhaga, Telangana).
