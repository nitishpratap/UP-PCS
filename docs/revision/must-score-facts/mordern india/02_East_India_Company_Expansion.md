## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Company rule (कंपनी शासन) phases**: Factories → **Plassey puppet (1757)** → **Diwani direct revenue (1765)** → **Wellesley subsidiary alliance (1798–1805)** → **Dalhousie lapse / annexation (1848–56)**.
2. **Plassey (23 June 1757) — Cause:** Farman misuse, *dastak* abuse, and Siraj-ud-Daulah’s attack on Fort William (**Black Hole, 20 June 1756**; Holwell account).
3. **Plassey — Course & Result:** Robert Clive conspirators were **Mir Jafar (मीर जाफ़र), Rai Durlabh, Jagat Seth, and Omichund**. Clive won with very little fighting (treason). Mir Jafar made puppet Nawab; Company received **24 Parganas zamindari** and ₹1.77 crore indemnity.
4. **Buxar (22 Oct 1764) — Cause:** Mir Qasim’s break with the Company after Plassey: shifted capital from **Murshidabad to Munger (मुंगेर)** (set up arms/firearm factory), abolished internal transit customs duties to place Indian merchants on par with English.
5. **Buxar — Combined Opposition:** **Mir Qasim** (dismissed Nawab of Bengal) + **Shuja-ud-Daula** (Nawab of Awadh) + **Shah Alam II** (fugitive Mughal Emperor) formed the Grand Alliance.
6. **Buxar — Result:** British Commander **Hector Munro** decisively defeated the combined allied forces in a pitched open field battle. Proved British military-tactical superiority; Buxar, not Plassey, made the Company the real master of Northern India.
7. **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765) — First Treaty (Shah Alam II, 12 Aug 1765):** Emperor granted permanent **Diwani (दीवानी) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa** to the EIC; Company gave Shah Alam **₹26 lakh annual tribute** and districts of **Kora and Allahabad** (taken from Awadh).
8. **Treaty of Allahabad — Second Treaty (Shuja-ud-Daula, 16 Aug 1765):** Shuja paid ₹50 lakh war indemnity, surrendered Kora and Allahabad, ceded zamindari of Banaras to Balwant Singh, and accepted British troops in Awadh at his own expense (early buffer state / Ring Fence).
9. **Dual Government in Bengal (1765–1772):** Introduced by **Robert Clive**. **Diwani** (revenue & civil justice) held by Company; **Nizamat** (police & criminal justice) remained with puppet Nawab through Deputy Nazim (**Mohammad Reza Khan** in Bengal, **Shitab Rai** in Bihar). "Authority without responsibility" led to devastating Bengal Famine of 1770 (one-third population starved). Abolished by **Warren Hastings in 1772**.
10. **Ring Fence Policy (1765–1785):** Initiated by **Warren Hastings**; defending neighbours' frontiers to safeguard Company borders (e.g. creating Awadh as a buffer state against Maratha and Afghan invasions).
11. **Subsidiary Alliance System (सहायक संधि प्रणाली):** Perfected by **Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)** (earlier used by Dupleix and Clive). Terms: Indian ruler surrendered foreign affairs, maintained British subsidiary force under British command, paid regular cash or ceded territory, expelled all other Europeans, and accepted a **British Resident** at court.
12. **Subsidiary Alliance Order (Ratta):** **Hyderabad (1798, reaffirmed 1800) → Mysore (1799) → Tanjore (Oct 1799) → Awadh (Nov 1801) → Peshwa (Treaty of Bassein, 1802) → Bhonsle of Berar (1803) → Scindia (1804) → Jodhpur/Jaipur/Macheri/Bundi/Bharatpur (1818)**. Note: Awadh entered under Wellesley in 1801, not Dalhousie.
13. **Doctrine of Lapse (व्यपगत का सिद्धांत / हड़प नीति):** Aggressively applied by **Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856)**. Native states with adopted heirs had rights to personal property but state sovereignty lapsed to British crown unless recognized.
14. **Doctrine of Lapse Order (Ratta):** **Satara (1848) → Jaitpur & Sambalpur (1849) → Baghat (1850) → Udaipur (1852) → Jhansi (1853) → Nagpur (1854)**.
15. **Awadh Annexation Trap (1856):** Dalhousie annexed Awadh in **Feb 1856** on grounds of **maladministration / misgovernance**, based on British Resident **James Outram’s report** (Sleeman wrote earlier report). Awadh was **NOT** annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse.
16. **Battle of Bedara (1759):** Colonel **Francis Forde** defeated Dutch forces near Chinsurah, eliminating Dutch political power in India.
17. **Battle of Wandiwash (1760):** **Sir Eyre Coote** defeated French forces under **Count de Lally** and captured Bussy, effectively breaking French power in India.
18. **First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–69):** Hyder Ali formed alliance with Nizam and Marathas, outmanoeuvred British, appeared at gates of Madras; concluded with defensive **Treaty of Madras (1769)** (mutual restitution of conquests).
19. **Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–84):** Sparked by English capture of French port **Mahe** (in Hyder’s territory); Hyder defeated Baillie; **Sir Eyre Coote** defeated Hyder at **Porto Novo (1781)**; Hyder died of cancer (1782); Tipu signed **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)** on terms of status quo ante bellum.
20. **Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–92):** Tipu attacked Raja of Travancore (British ally); Lord Cornwallis made alliance with Marathas and Nizam, captured Bangalore; concluded with **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**: Tipu ceded **half his territory** (Malabar, Dindigul, Baramahal), paid ₹3.30 crore indemnity, and surrendered **two sons as hostages**.
21. **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799):** Lord Wellesley accused Tipu of conspiring with French (Jacobin club, planted Tree of Liberty at Seringapatam); British attacked Mysore from two sides; **Tipu Sultan died fighting at the gates of Seringapatam on 4 May 1799**. Mysore restored to young Wodeyar prince under Subsidiary Alliance.
22. **First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–82):** Caused by EIC Bombay council signing **Treaty of Surat (1775)** with ambitious Raghunath Rao (Raghoba); Calcutta council rejected it with **Treaty of Purandhar (1776)**; Maratha forces led by Mahadji Scindia and Nana Phadnavis forced British surrender at **Wadgaon (1779)**; ended with **Treaty of Salbai (1782)** (mediated by Mahadji Scindia, guaranteed 20 years of peace).
23. **Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05):** Peshwa Baji Rao II fled Pune after defeat by Jaswant Rao Holkar at Hadapsar; signed the subsidiary **Treaty of Bassein (31 Dec 1802)** with Wellesley; Scindia and Bhonsle fought back; Arthur Wellesley won Assaye and Argaon; Gerard Lake won Laswari; concluded with **Treaty of Deogaon (1803, Bhonsle)** and **Treaty of Surji-Anjangaon (1803, Scindia)**.
24. **Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–19):** **Lord Hastings** attacked Pindari bands (Maratha mercenaries); Peshwa Baji Rao II, Appa Sahib (Bhonsle), and Holkar rose; Marathas defeated at Khadki, Sitabuldi, and Mahidpur; **Peshwaship was abolished** (1818); Baji Rao II was pensioned off with ₹8 lakh/year to **Bithoor (near Kanpur)**; Shivaji's descendant Pratap Singh installed at Satara.
25. **Anglo-Nepal (Gurkha) War (1814–16):** Lord Hastings dispatched British forces under General **David Ochterlony**; concluded with **Treaty of Sugauli (March 1816)**: Nepal ceded Kumaon, Garhwal, and Terai belt, withdrew from Sikkim, and accepted a British Resident at Kathmandu.
26. **First Anglo-Burmese War (1824–26):** Lord Amherst; Burmese general Bandula pushed into Assam; concluded by **Treaty of Yandabo (1826)**: Burma ceded Assam, Manipur, Arakan, and Tenasserim, and paid £1 million indemnity.
27. **First Anglo-Afghan War (1839–42):** Lord Auckland launched the disastrous "Forward Policy" to replace Dost Mohammad with Shah Shuja; British army massacred during retreat from Kabul (Dr. William Brydon lone survivor); ended under Lord Ellenborough who evacuated Kabul.
28. **Annexation of Sindh (1843):** Conquered under Governor-General **Lord Ellenborough** by Major-General **Sir Charles Napier** following victories at **Miani (Feb 1843)** and Dubbo. Napier sent the historic pun *"Peccavi"* ("I have sinned").
29. **First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46):** After Maharaja Ranjit Singh died (1839), Sikh army crossed Sutlej; Governor-General **Lord Hardinge I**; battles of Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal, and **Sobraon (1846)**; ended with **Treaty of Lahore (March 1846)** and **Treaty of Amritsar (1846)** selling Kashmir to Gulab Singh.
30. **Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848–49):** Rebellions of Mulraj (governor of Multan) and Chattar Singh Attariwala; battles of Ramnagar, Chillianwala, and decisive victory at **Gujarat (21 Feb 1849, Battle of the Guns)** by Lord Gough; **Lord Dalhousie annexed Punjab on 29 March 1849**. Boy Maharaja Duleep Singh pensioned to England.
31. **Banaras Rebellion (1781):** Warren Hastings demanded extra troops and ₹5 lakh subsidy from Raja **Chait Singh** of Banaras; Hastings personally visited Banaras, was besieged, and had to flee to **Chunar Fort** ("*Ghode par hauda, hathi par jeen, jaldi bhag gaya Warren Hastings*").
32. **Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784):** Founded by **Sir William Jones** in Calcutta with active encouragement from Warren Hastings; translated *Gita* (Charles Wilkins, 1785), *Abhijnanasakuntalam* (Jones, 1789), and *Manusmriti* (Jones, 1794).
33. **Governor-General Title Transformations:**
    - Governor of Fort William in Bengal (Clive 1757)
    - **Governor-General of Bengal** (Warren Hastings, **Regulating Act 1773**)
    - **Governor-General of India** (Lord William Bentinck, **Charter Act 1833**)
    - **Viceroy and Governor-General of India** (Lord Canning, **Government of India Act 1858**).
34. **Treaty of Amritsar (1809):** Signed between **Maharaja Ranjit Singh** and Charles Metcalfe (on behalf of Lord Minto I), fixing the **Sutlej River** as the permanent boundary between Punjab and British spheres.
35. **Pindaris Suppression:** Lord Hastings launched 1,20,000 troops under himself (Northern army) and Sir Thomas Hislop (Southern army) in **1817–18** to eliminate Pindari leaders **Wasim Mohammad, Chitu, and Karim Khan**.
36. **Alivardi Khan's European Analogy:** Alivardi Khan (Nawab of Bengal 1740–56) famously compared Europeans to **bees (मधुमक्खियाँ)**: *"If left alone, they will give you honey; but if disturbed, they will sting you to death."*
37. **Subsidiary Alliance Mnemonic Sequence:** **H - M - T - A - P - B - S** (Hyderabad 1798, Mysore 1799, Tanjore 1799, Awadh 1801, Peshwa 1802, Bhonsle 1803, Scindia 1804).
38. **Doctrine of Lapse Mnemonic Sequence:** **S - J - S - B - U - J - N** (Satara 1848, Jaitpur 1849, Sambalpur 1849, Baghat 1850, Udaipur 1852, Jhansi 1853, Nagpur 1854).
39. **Bengal Capital Shifts:** Dhaka (Islam Khan) → **Murshidabad (Murshid Quli Khan, 1704)** → **Munger (Mir Qasim, 1762)** to escape British surveillance and interference.
40. **Siraj-ud-Daulah's Fall:** Siraj became Nawab in April 1756 at age 23; besieged Calcutta and captured Fort William (renaming Calcutta to **Alinagar**); signed **Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757)** with Clive before betrayal at Plassey on 23 June 1757.
41. **Clive’s Titles & Assessments:** Clive was called a "heaven-born general" by William Pitt the Elder; twice Governor of Bengal (1757–60, 1765–67); committed suicide in England in 1774.
42. **Tipu Sultan's Innovations:** Tipu introduced a new calendar, a new coinage system, modern rocket artillery (Mysorean rockets), state trading company with overseas factories at Muscat, Ormuz, and Jeddah, and built a modern navy at Wajidabad.
43. **Younghusband Expedition (1903–04):** Led by Francis Younghusband under **Lord Curzon** to Lhasa to counter perceived Russian influence in Tibet, culminating in the **Treaty of Lhasa (1904)**.
44. **Sikh Military System under Ranjit Singh:** Ranjit Singh built the **Fauj-i-Khas** (model brigade trained on European lines by French generals Allard and Ventura, Italian Court, and American Gardner). Second largest modernized army in Asia after the EIC.
45. **Treaty of Deogaon vs Surji-Anjangaon (1803):** Treaty of Deogaon signed by **Raghuji Bhonsle** (ceded Cuttack); Treaty of Surji-Anjangaon signed by **Daulat Rao Scindia** (ceded Ganga-Yamuna doab, Delhi-Agra, and Broach).
46. **Berar Cession (1853):** Dalhousie forced the **Nizam of Hyderabad** to cede the rich cotton-producing province of **Berar** to the British in 1853 to pay for the arrears of the Hyderabad Contingent force.
47. **Treaty of Purandhar (1776) vs Wadgaon (1779):** Purandhar signed by Calcutta council (Colonel Upton) disowning Treaty of Surat; Convention of Wadgaon signed under military humiliation when Marathas trapped British force in the Western Ghats.
48. **Siraj’s Black Hole Incident (1756):** J.Z. Holwell claimed 146 British prisoners were locked in an 18x14 ft cell on 20 June 1756, and 123 died of suffocation. Modern historians consider numbers grossly exaggerated, but it served as British war propaganda for Plassey.
49. **Baji Rao II’s Surrender (1818):** After being hunted down by General Smith and Sir John Malcolm, the last Peshwa Baji Rao II surrendered on 3 June 1818. The Peshwaship was extinguished, and Nana Saheb (Dhondu Pant) was his adopted son whose pension was later stopped by Dalhousie.
50. **Summary of British Expansion Doctrine:** Three distinct tools deployed systematically:
51. **Black Hole Tragedy of Calcutta (20 June 1756):** Account propagated by **J.Z. Holwell**; alleged 146 British prisoners confined in small cell (18x14 ft) resulting in 123 deaths by suffocation; used as pretext for Robert Clive's expedition from Madras.
52. **Treaty of Alinagar (9 February 1757):** Signed between Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah and Robert Clive/Watson; British recovered Calcutta, gained permission to fortify Fort William and mint their own coins in Bengal.
53. **Battle of Plassey Conspiracy (23 June 1757):** Conspirators with Clive included: **Mir Jafar** (Mir Bakshi / Commander-in-Chief), **Rai Durlabh** (treasurer), **Jagat Seth** (Mahatab Chand, imperial banker), **Omichund** (merchant/broker), and **Khadim Khan**.
54. **Mir Qasim's Reforms in Bengal:** Shifted capital from Murshidabad to **Munger (Bihar)** in 1762; established firelock musket and cannon foundry at Munger; reorganized tax collection (*abwabs*); abolished all inland customs duties to equalize Indian and British merchants, precipitating war.
55. **Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764):** Fought at Buxar on the banks of Ganga; British army led by **Major Hector Munro** defeated combined confederacy of **Mir Qasim**, **Shuja-ud-Daulah** (Nawab of Awadh), and Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II**; established British paramountcy in North India.
56. **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765):** Signed by Robert Clive; 1st treaty with Shah Alam II granted the EIC the **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa** in return for an annual tribute of **₹26 lakh** and ceded districts of Kora and Allahabad (carved out of Awadh).
57. **Treaty of Allahabad (2nd Treaty with Awadh):** Shuja-ud-Daulah paid war indemnity of **₹50 lakh**, ceded Kora and Allahabad to Shah Alam II, and agreed to maintain British garrison in Awadh (first step toward subsidiary alliance).
58. **Clive's Dual Government in Bengal (1765–1772):** Division of authority between **Diwani** (revenue collection & civil justice, held directly by EIC) and **Nizamat** (police, criminal justice & administration, left with puppet Nawab under deputy subahdar); abolished by Warren Hastings in 1772.
59. **Deputy Diwans Appointed by Clive (1765):** **Mohammad Reza Khan** for Bengal, **Raja Shitab Rai** for Bihar, and **Rai Durlabh** for Orissa.
60. **First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–69):** Hyder Ali formed alliance with Nizam and Marathas; outmaneuvered British army and reached outskirts of Madras; forced British to sign humiliating **Treaty of Madras (1769)** (mutual restitution of territories and defensive alliance).
61. **Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–84):** Triggered by British capture of French-controlled port of **Mahe** (in Hyder Ali's territory); Hyder Ali defeated Colonel Baillie at Pollilur (1780); Sir Eyre Coote defeated Hyder at **Porto Novo (1781)**; Hyder died of cancer in Dec 1782; war concluded by Tipu Sultan with **Treaty of Mangalore (March 1784)** on basis of mutual restitution.
62. **Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–92):** Lord Cornwallis allied with Marathas and Nizam against Tipu; Cornwallis besieged Seringapatam; concluded by **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**: Tipu ceded half his kingdom (Baramahal, Dindigul, Malabar to British) and paid ₹3.3 crore indemnity, surrendering two minor sons as hostages.
63. **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799):** Lord Wellesley invaded Mysore accusing Tipu of conspiring with Napoleonic France (Tipu planted Liberty Tree at Seringapatam and joined Jacobin Club); Tipu died fighting at the gates of Seringapatam on **4 May 1799**; Mysore restored to Wodeyar child ruler under Subsidiary Alliance.
64. **Treaty of Salbai (17 May 1782):** Ended First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–82); negotiated by Warren Hastings and Mahadji Scindia; British recognized **Madhavrao II (Sawai Madhavrao)** as Peshwa; guaranteed 20 years of mutual peace.
65. **Treaty of Bassein (31 December 1802):** Peshwa **Baji Rao II** fled Pune after defeat by Jaswant Rao Holkar and signed subsidiary treaty with Lord Wellesley; surrendered foreign policy, stationed British force in Pune, and ceded territory worth ₹26 lakh/year.
    1. Direct War / Conquest (Bengal, Mysore, Sikhs, Sindh)
    2. Subsidiary Alliance / Diplomatic Net (Wellesley: Hyderabad, Awadh, Maratha houses)
    3. Doctrine of Lapse / Annexation on Pretexts (Dalhousie: Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, Awadh).

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

1. **Subsidiary Alliance Sequence (Lord Wellesley):**
   - **Mnemonic:** **H - M - T - A - P - B - S**
   - *"**H**is **M**ajesty's **T**roops **A**re **P**rotecting **B**ritish **S**ubjects"*
   - **H**yderabad (1798) → **M**ysore (1799) → **T**anjore (1799) → **A**wadh (1801) → **P**eshwa (1802) → **B**honsle (1803) → **S**cindia (1804).
2. **Doctrine of Lapse Sequence (Lord Dalhousie):**
   - **Mnemonic:** **S - J - S - B - U - J - N**
   - *"**S**unita **J**umps and **S**ings **B**efore **U**ncle **J**ohn's **N**ephew"*
   - **S**atara (1848) → **J**aitpur (1849) → **S**ambalpur (1849) → **B**aghat (1850) → **U**daipur (1852) → **J**hansi (1853) → **N**agpur (1854).
   - *(Awadh is NOT in this list — Awadh was annexed for Misgovernance!)*
3. **Four Anglo-Mysore Wars Treaties:**
   - **Mnemonic:** **M - M - S - D**
   - 1st War (1769): Treaty of **M**adras
   - 2nd War (1784): Treaty of **M**angalore
   - 3rd War (1792): Treaty of **S**eringapatam
   - 4th War (1799): **D**eath of Tipu Sultan at Seringapatam.
4. **Three Anglo-Maratha Wars Treaties:**
   - 1st War: **Salbai** (1782) — 20-year peace
   - 2nd War: **Bassein** (1802) → Deogaon / Surji-Anjangaon (1803)
   - 3rd War: **Peshwaship abolished** (1818); Baji Rao II exiled to Bithoor.
5. **The Buxar Grand Alliance:**
   - **Mnemonic:** **M - S - S** vs **Munro**
   - **M**ir Qasim + **S**huja-ud-Daula + **S**hah Alam II routed by Hector **Munro**.
