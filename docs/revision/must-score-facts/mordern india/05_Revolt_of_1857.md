## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Causes of 1857 Revolt:**
   - **Political:** Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie: Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur); **annexation of Awadh (Feb 1856)** on grounds of misrule; discontinuation of Nana Sahib's pension; humiliating treatment of Mughal Emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar.
   - **Economic:** Destruction of handicrafts and handloom; ruinous land revenue settlements (Summary Settlement 1856 in Awadh dispossessed taluqdars); usurious moneylenders (*sahukars*).
   - **Socio-Religious:** Activities of Christian missionaries; **Religious Disabilities / Lex Loci Act (1850)** allowing converts to inherit ancestral property; **Abolition of Sati (1829)** and **Widow Remarriage Act (1856)** seen as assault on Hindu customs; taxation of temple and mosque lands.
   - **Military:** Low pay; racial arrogance of British officers; **General Service Enlistment Act (1856)** forcing overseas travel (*kala pani* loss of caste); withdrawal of free postal privilege under Post Office Act 1854; stoppage of foreign-service bhatta.
2. **Immediate Cause — Enfield Rifle & Greased Cartridge:**
   - In **December 1856**, the British government decided to replace the old smooth-bore **Brown Bess** musket with the new rifled **Enfield rifle**.
   - The cartridge of the Enfield had a greased paper cover that had to be bitten off with teeth before loading into the rifle.
   - In **January 1857**, a rumor broke out in **Dum Dum** (near Calcutta) that the grease was made of **cow and pig fat** (offensive to both Hindus and Muslims).
   - Rifling training depots were located at **Dum Dum, Ambala, and Sialkot**.
3. **First Sparks of Mutiny:**
   - **19th Bengal Native Infantry at Berhampore (Bengal):** Refused to use the new cartridges on **26 February 1857**; regiment disbanded on 31 March.
   - **Mangal Pandey at Barrackpore (29 March 1857):** Sepoy of the **34th Bengal Native Infantry**; mutinied, shot Sergeant Major James Hewson, and injured Lt. Henry Baugh; Commanding Officer of the Barrackpore division was **Major General John Bennet Hearsey**; Mangal Pandey was hanged on **8 April 1857**; jemadar Ishwari Prasad was hanged on 21 April; 34th BNI was disbanded in May.
4. **Mass Outbreak at Meerut (10 May 1857):**
   - On 24 April 1857, 85 out of 90 troopers of the **3rd Bengal Light Cavalry** at Meerut refused to handle the cartridges; court-martialled and sentenced to 10 years' rigorous imprisonment on 9 May.
   - On Sunday, **10 May 1857**, the sepoys mutinied, broke open the jail, released their comrades, killed European officers, and marched straight to Delhi shouting *"Maro Firangi Ko!"*.
   - European garrison commander at Meerut, **Major General William Hewitt**, commanded 2,200 European soldiers but failed to react or pursue the marching rebels.
5. **Fall of Delhi (11–12 May 1857):**
   - Meerut sepoys crossed the Yamuna via boats, entered the Red Fort on **11 May 1857**, and proclaimed 82-year-old **Bahadur Shah II (Zafar)** as the **Shahenshah-e-Hindustan (Emperor of India)**.
   - Real military authority at Delhi was wielded by **General Bakht Khan**, an artillery subedar from Bareilly who arrived with Bareilly troops and was awarded the title **Saheb-e-Alam Bahadur**.
   - Delhi was captured by the British on **20 September 1857** after intense fighting: **John Nicholson** was mortally wounded; **Lt. William Hodson** captured Bahadur Shah at Humayun’s Tomb and shot his two sons (Mirza Mughal, Mirza Khizr Sultan) and grandson (Mirza Abu Bakr) at point-blank range near **Khooni Darwaza**.
   - Bahadur Shah was tried, found guilty, and exiled to **Rangoon (Burma)**, where he died on **7 November 1862**.
6. **Popular Symbols of the Revolt:**
   - The secret symbols circulating from village to village across North India were **Red Lotus (कमल)** and **Chapatis (रोटी)**.
7. **Kanpur (5 June 1857):**
   - Led by **Nana Sahib** (born Dhondu Pant, adopted son of the last Peshwa Baji Rao II), who was proclaimed **Peshwa on 5 June 1857**.
   - Commander-in-Chief of Nana’s forces was **Tantia Tope** (original name **Ramchandra Pandurang**).
   - Nana's principal diplomatic advisor was **Azimullah Khan** (honored as **Krantidoot** / Ambassador of Revolution, editor of *Payam-e-Azadi*).
   - Major British garrisons under Sir Hugh Wheeler surrendered; infamous incidents at **Satichaura Ghat** and **Bibighar**.
   - British recaptured Kanpur on **6 December 1857** under **Sir Colin Campbell**; Nana Sahib escaped to Nepal; Tantia Tope fled to Kalpi and joined Rani Lakshmibai.
8. **Lucknow / Awadh (30 May 1857):**
   - Led by **Begum Hazrat Mahal** (wife of deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah); proclaimed her 11-year-old minor son **Birjis Qadr** as the Nawab of Awadh.
   - Fierce siege of the **British Residency** in Lucknow: Chief Commissioner **Sir Henry Lawrence** was killed on 4 July 1857; Generals **Havelock** and **Neill** fought through but Neill was killed; **Sir Colin Campbell** with Gurkha forces under Jung Bahadur finally recaptured Lucknow on **21 March 1858**.
   - Begum Hazrat Mahal refused British terms and fled to **Nepal**, where she died in 1879.
9. **Jhansi & Gwalior (June 1857 – June 1858):**
   - Led by **Rani Lakshmibai** (original name **Manikarnika / Manu**, born **19 November 1835** at Golghar, Varanasi; father Moropant Tambe, husband Maharaja Gangadhar Rao).
   - Revolt broke out in Jhansi in **June 1857** (not May).
   - British forces under **Sir Hugh Rose** besieged Jhansi Fort in March 1858; Rani escaped with her adopted son Damodar Rao strapped to her back; joined Tantia Tope at Kalpi and captured **Gwalior Fort** from Scindia.
   - Rani Lakshmibai died heroically in battle against Sir Hugh Rose at **Kotah-ki-Serai near Gwalior on 18 June 1858**.
   - Sir Hugh Rose famously paid tribute: *"Here lay the woman who was the only man among the rebels."* Her samadhi is at **Gwalior**.
10. **Tantia Tope’s Martyrdom:**
    - Fought brilliant guerrilla warfare across Central India, Rajasthan, and Bundelkhand for over a year.
    - Betrayed while asleep in the Paron forest by his trusted friend **Raja Man Singh of Narwar**; captured by Major Meade and hanged at **Shivpuri (MP) on 18 April 1859** (considered the final martyrdom of the 1857 revolt).
11. **Jagdishpur / Bihar (Arrah):**
    - Led by the 80-year-old Rajput zamindar **Babu Kunwar Singh** of Jagdishpur (Bhojpur district).
    - Joined by mutinous Danapur sepoys; liberated Arrah; defeated British forces under Captain Dunbar and Captain Le Grand.
    - Crossing the Ganga, Kunwar Singh was shot in the wrist; he severed his own poisoned arm and offered it to Mother Ganga; died victorious in his castle on **26 April 1858**.
    - Brother **Amar Singh** continued the struggle in Kaimur hills until 1859.
    - Suppressed by **William Taylor** and **Major Vincent Eyre**.
12. **Bareilly & Rohilkhand:**
    - Led by **Khan Bahadur Khan**, grandson of the last Rohilla ruler Hafiz Rahmat Khan.
    - Bahadur Shah Zafar appointed him the **Viceroy / Subedar of Bareilly**; formed an orderly government with both Hindu and Muslim officers; captured and hanged in Bareilly in 1860 by Campbell.
13. **Faizabad:**
    - Led by **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah** (originally from Arcot / Chinapattan, Tamil Nadu; known as **Danka Shah**).
    - Declared holy war (*Jihad*) against British; British government placed an unprecedented **bounty of ₹50,000** on his head; called by British commanders "a man of great abilities and the best soldier among rebels"; betrayed and shot by **Raja Jagannath Singh of Powayan** (Shahjahanpur border) on 5 June 1858.
14. **Allahabad & Banaras:**
    - Led at Allahabad by **Maulvi Liaquat Ali** (a schoolteacher who made **Khusro Bagh** his rebel headquarters and was proclaimed governor by Zafar).
    - Suppressed with extreme brutality by **Colonel James Neill**, who carried out indiscriminate mass hangings along the Grand Trunk Road.
15. **Other Regional Centres:**
    - **Assam:** **Diwan Maniram Dutta Barua** and Piyali Barua proclaimed young prince **Kandarpeshwar Singh** as King; Maniram and Piyali were hanged in 1858.
    - **Auwa (Rajasthan):** **Thakur Kushal Singh of Auwa** defeated joint British and Jodhpur State forces at Chinbawas and Bichhuda; British Political Agent Captain Mock Mason was killed and his head hung on the Auwa fort gate.
    - **Mathura:** Devi Singh; **Kadam Singh** in Meerut countryside; **Shah Mal** in Baraut (Baghpat, organized supply lines for Delhi rebels, died in battle July 1857).
    - **Mandi / Kullu (HP):** Raja Pratap Singh.
    - **Orissa:** Surendra Sai and Ujjwal Sai of Sambalpur.
    - **Gorakhpur:** Gajadhar Singh.
    - **Farrukhabad:** Tufzal Hasan Khan.
    - **Sultanpur:** Shahid Hasan.
16. **Regions that Stayed Aloof / Loyal to British:**
    - **Punjab, Bengal, Madras, Bombay, Rajputana (Jaipur, Chittor, Udaipur), and Hyderabad** stayed largely quiet or actively helped the British.
    - Major Indian rulers who provided military assistance to the British:
      - **Scindia of Gwalior** (Prime Minister Sir Dinkar Rao)
      - **Nizam of Hyderabad** (Prime Minister Sir Salar Jung)
      - **Holkar of Indore**
      - **Ruler of Bhopal** (Sikandar Begum)
      - **Maharaja of Kashmir** (Gulab Singh / Ranbir Singh)
      - **Sikh Princes of Patiala, Nabha, Jind, and Kapurthala**
      - **Rana of Nepal** (Jung Bahadur).
    - Lord Canning remarked: *"If Scindia joins the rebels, I will pack off tomorrow"* and described loyal princes as *"breakwaters to the storm which would have otherwise swept us away."*
17. **Social Classes that Stayed Neutral or Opposed:**
    - Modern educated Indian intelligentsia remained aloof, believing British rule would modernize India.
    - Big merchants, bankers (*sahukars*), and moneylenders actively supported the British (rebels burnt their debt ledgers).
    - Big zamindars of Bengal and taluqdars with secured titles supported the British.
18. **Eyewitnesses and Literary Accounts:**
    - Great Urdu poet **Mirza Asadullah Khan Ghalib** personally witnessed the devastation of Delhi and slaughter of citizens, recorded in his letters and diary (*Dastambu*).
    - **William Howard Russell**, famous war correspondent of *The Times* (London), documented British military atrocities and widespread hatred toward white rule in Awadh and Bihar.
19. **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan’s Treatise (1859):**
    - Wrote ***Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind* (The Causes of the Indian Revolt, 1859)** in Urdu; first Indian critique of colonial misgovernance; identified lack of Indian representation in the Legislative Council as the root cause.
20. **Lord Canning’s Role:**
    - Governor-General during the Revolt of 1857; shifted emergency operational headquarters to **Allahabad**; nicknamed *"Clemency Canning"* by the Anglo-Indian press for attempting to restrain indiscriminate bloodthirsty reprisals.
    - British Prime Minister during 1857 was **Lord Palmerston** (later replaced by Lord Derby).
21. **Peel Commission (1858) & Military Reorganisation:**
    - Appointed under Jonathan Peel to reorganize the Indian Army.
    - Drastic reduction of Indian sepoys: from ~2,38,000 to ~1,40,000; European troops increased from 45,000 to 65,000.
    - Fixed European to Indian ratio: **1:2 in Bengal Army**, **1:3 in Madras and Bombay Armies**.
    - All senior command posts, strategic arsenals, and **artillery** strictly reserved for Europeans.
    - Formulated the **"Martial vs Non-Martial Races"** doctrine: recruitment heavily tilted towards Gurkhas, Sikhs, and Punjabi Muslims who helped suppress the revolt, drastically cutting recruitment of Awadh/Bihar Brahmins and Rajputs.
22. **Government of India Act, 1858 (Act for the Better Government of India):**
    - Passed 2 August 1858; abolished the East India Company and transferred government, territories, and revenues directly to the **British Crown**.
    - Abolished Dual Government in Britain: **Board of Control** and **Court of Directors** abolished.
    - Created the cabinet post of **Secretary of State for India** assisted by a 15-member **Council of India** in London.
    - Governor-General designated as **Viceroy** when representing the Crown in relations with native princely states.
23. **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 November 1858):**
    - Read by **Lord Canning** at a grand royal Darbar in **Allahabad** on **1 November 1858**.
    - Known as the **"Magna Carta of the Indian People"**.
    - Solemn promises: Disclaimed any desire for further territorial annexation; promised non-interference in religious beliefs; guaranteed equal protection of law; offered general amnesty to all rebels except those convicted of murdering British subjects; permitted native princes the right of adoption.
24. **British Officers Suppression Grid (Match Master):**
    - Delhi: **John Nicholson** (killed), **Major Hudson**, **Sir Archdale Wilson**.
    - Kanpur: **Sir Colin Campbell**, **General Havelock**.
    - Lucknow: **Sir Colin Campbell**, **Sir James Outram**, **Henry Lawrence** (killed).
    - Jhansi & Gwalior: **Sir Hugh Rose**.
    - Bihar (Arrah): **William Taylor** and **Major Vincent Eyre**.
    - Allahabad & Banaras: **Colonel James Neill**.
    - Bareilly: **Sir Colin Campbell**.
25. **Historiographical Opinions on 1857 (Master Quote Grid):**
    - **Sir John William Kaye:** *"A duel between Christianity and heathenism."*
    - **Sir John Lawrence & Seeley:** *"Wholly unpatriotic and selfish Sepoy Mutiny with no native leadership and no popular support."*
    - **Sir James Outram & W. Tayler:** *"A Mohammedan conspiracy making capital of Hindu grievances."*
    - **T.R. Holmes:** *"A conflict between civilization and barbarism."*
    - **Benjamin Disraeli:** *"A national rising, not a mere military mutiny."*
    - **V.D. Savarkar (1909):** *"The First War of Indian Independence."*
    - **R.C. Majumdar (1957):** *"Neither first, nor national, nor a war of independence."*
    - **Dr. S.N. Sen (1957, Official Historian):** *"What began as a fight for religion ended as a war of independence."*
    - **Karl Marx (1857 articles in New York Daily Tribune):** *"A national revolt"* reflecting broad peasant-sepoy anti-colonial solidarity.
    - **Jawaharlal Nehru (*Discovery of India*):** *"Essentially a feudal outburst, headed by feudal chiefs and their followers."*
26. **Important Books on 1857:**
    - *The Indian War of Independence 1857* (1909) — **V.D. Savarkar**.
    - *Eighteen Fifty-Seven* (1957) — **Dr. S.N. Sen** (Forward by Maulana Abul Kalam Azad).
    - *The Sepoy Mutiny and the Revolt of 1857* (1957) — **Dr. R.C. Majumdar**.
    - *Civil Rebellion in the Indian Mutinies (1857–1859)* — **S.B. Chaudhuri**.
    - *Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind* (1859) — **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan**.
    - *1857: The Great Rebellion* — **Ashok Mehta**.
27. **Awadh's Unique Depth:** Awadh was the heart of the rebellion. Out of 40,000 British casualties during the revolt, over half were incurred in Awadh. British forces had to conquer Awadh village by village from taluqdars and armed peasants.
28. **Delhi Administrative Committee (Court of Soldiers):** General Bakht Khan set up an elected 10-member administrative council (6 military, 4 civilian) that governed Delhi, settled prices, and issued decrees in the name of Bahadur Shah Zafar.
29. **Kunwar Singh’s Final Triumph:** On 23 April 1858, despite being mortally wounded and missing an arm, Kunwar Singh completely routed the British detachment under Captain Le Grand near Jagdishpur, reoccupying his ancestral palace before passing away.
30. **Azimullah Khan's Journey:** Nana Sahib sent Azimullah Khan to London in 1853 to plead for Baji Rao II’s pension; failing there, Azimullah visited Crimea during the Crimean War, observing Russian-British warfare before returning to plan the uprising.
31. **Maulvi Ahmadullah's Resilience:** Defeated General Campbell at Nawabganj and withstood multiple British columns; his portrait was the only rebel commander's picture published with respect in British military memoirs.
32. **Rani Lakshmibai's Birth and Family Details:** Born at **Golghar in Varanasi** on 19 November 1835; named Manikarnika; father **Moropant Tambe** worked in the court of Peshwa Baji Rao II at Bithoor; married Maharaja Gangadhar Rao of Jhansi in 1842; gave birth to son Damodar Rao who died at 4 months; adopted Anand Rao (renamed Damodar Rao) before Gangadhar Rao died in 1853.
33. **Lord Dalhousie's Annexation of Jhansi:** In **1853**, Dalhousie rejected Anand Rao's adoption under the Doctrine of Lapse, offered Lakshmibai a measly pension of ₹60,000/year, and ordered her to vacate the Jhansi fort, eliciting her famous defiance: *"Main meri Jhansi nahi doongi"* ("I shall not give up my Jhansi").
34. **Rani Lakshmibai's Comrades in Battle:** Fought alongside her trusted female artillery commanders **Jhalkaribai** (who disguised herself as the Queen to allow Lakshmibai to escape) and **Mandar** (who died fighting beside her at Kotah-ki-Serai), along with Afghan bodyguard general **Ghulam Ghaus Khan** and gunner **Khuda Baksh**.
35. **Mangal Pandey’s Native Village:** Mangal Pandey was a resident of village **Nagwa in Ballia district (UP)**.
36. **Meerut Sepoys' Regimental Composition:** The 3rd Bengal Light Cavalry sepoys were largely Muslims from Western UP and Brahmins from Awadh, joined by the 11th and 20th Bengal Native Infantry regiments on 10 May.
37. **Bahadur Shah Zafar’s Poetic Epitaph:** A celebrated Urdu poet, Zafar wrote his famous lament in Rangoon exile: *"Kitna hai badnaseeb Zafar dafn ke liye, Do gaz zameen bhi na mili koo-e-yaar mein"* ("How unfortunate is Zafar, that for his burial, he could not get even two yards of earth in the land of his beloved").
38. **First British Officer Killed in Meerut:** **Colonel John Finnis** of the 11th Native Infantry was shot dead by sepoys on the evening of 10 May 1857 while trying to pacify them.
39. **First British Officer Killed in Delhi:** **Captain Douglas** (commander of the palace guard) and **Simon Fraser** (Commissioner of Delhi) were killed at the Lahore Gate of the Red Fort on 11 May.
40. **British Relief of Kanpur (Bibighar incident aftermath):** General Havelock and Colonel Neill forced captured sepoys to lick bloodstains before executing them and hanged hundreds along the road from Allahabad to Kanpur.
41. **Nana Sahib’s Proclamations:** Nana issued public farmans declaring that the British Raj was finished, proclaiming the brotherhood of Hindus and Muslims, and establishing fixed prices for food items in Kanpur bazars.
42. **Shah Mal of Baraut (Baghpat):** Organized the local Jat peasantry of 84 villages (*Chaurasi Desh*); mobilized intelligence and food grain supply for the Delhi garrison; raided British communications; killed in battle on 18 July 1857 by Dunlap's Khaki Risala.
43. **Raja Devi Singh of Tappa Raya (Mathura):** Peasant-turned-leader who established a parallel government in Mathura district, holding court and dispensing traditional justice until captured and hanged.
44. **Fatehpur (UP):** Led by **Hikmatullah**, a deputy collector who joined the revolt.
45. **Kadam Singh (Parikshitgarh, Meerut):** Gujjar leader who was proclaimed King by thousands of Gujjar and Rajput peasants surrounding Meerut.
46. **Why South India Remained Quiet:** The Madras Army was recruited from diverse social and caste groups, had better officer-soldier relations, and had already undergone the catharsis of the Vellore Mutiny (1806); ryots had individual titles under the Ryotwari system, unlike dispossessed Awadh taluqdars.
47. **Post-1857 Policy of Counterpoise:** The British systematically reorganized regiments by mixing antagonistic castes and communities (e.g. Sikh companies, Pathan companies, Dogra companies in the same battalion) so that "caste prevented camaraderie".
48. **Indian Councils Act 1861 as Direct Consequence:** Recognized that lack of communication with Indian subjects caused 1857; Viceroy Canning nominated three Indians to his Central Legislative Council in 1862: **Raja of Banaras, Maharaja of Patiala, and Sir Dinkar Rao**.
49. **White Mutiny of 1859:** Following the 1858 Crown takeover, European soldiers of the former EIC demanded transfer bounty/discharge; settled peacefully by Canning by offering free passage home to over 10,000 soldiers.
50. **Core Takeaway of 1857:** It was the watershed that ended 100 years of East India Company rule (1757–1857) and began 90 years of direct Crown rule (1858–1947), creating modern Indian political consciousness.
