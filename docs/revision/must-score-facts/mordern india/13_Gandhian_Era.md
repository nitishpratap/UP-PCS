## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. Gandhi was born **2 October 1869** at **Porbandar**; his father **Karamchand (Kaba) Gandhi** was Diwan of Porbandar, Rajkot and Wankaner; he had legal training at the **Inner Temple**, London, and attended **Samaldas College, Bhavnagar**.
2. Gandhi stayed in South Africa for nearly **21 years (1893–1915)**; he arrived to represent merchant **Dada Abdullah** in a legal dispute.
3. Gandhi founded the **Natal Indian Congress (1894)**, established **Phoenix Ashram (1904)** near Durban (his oldest ashram), set up **Tolstoy Farm (1910)** with Hermann Kallenbach, and ran the weekly journal ***Indian Opinion***.
4. Gandhi wrote his foundational philosophical treatise ***Hind Swaraj (हिंद स्वराज)* (1909)** aboard the ship *SS Kildonan Castle* in Gujarati, advocating self-reliance and critiquing modern Western civilization.
5. Gandhi returned to India on **9 January 1915** (commemorated as *Pravasi Bharatiya Divas*). On the advice of his political guru (राजनीतिक गुरु) **Gopal Krishna Gokhale (गोखले)**, he spent his first year touring the country with "ears open and mouth shut".
6. The first Indian National Congress session Gandhi ever attended was the **Calcutta Session of 1901** presided over by Dinshaw Wacha, where he moved a resolution on the condition of Indians in South Africa.
7. Gandhi established the **Satyagraha Ashram** at Kochrab (Ahmedabad) in 1915, which was relocated in 1917 to the banks of the Sabarmati river as **Sabarmati Ashram (साबरमती आश्रम)**.
8. The twin cardinal principles of Gandhi's philosophy and vision of **Ram Rajya (राम राज्य)** were **Truth (सत्य)** and **Non-violence (अहिंसा)**. He described himself to Louis Fischer as a socialist working for **Sarvodaya (सर्वोदय)** (uplift of all).
9. Key philosophical influences on Gandhi included **John Ruskin's** ***Unto This Last*** ("the good of the individual is contained in the good of all"), **Henry David Thoreau's** essay on *Civil Disobedience*, and **Leo Tolstoy's** ***The Kingdom of God is Within You***.
10. In satyagraha methodology, **fasting (उपवास)** is the highest and purest spiritual weapon, whereas a general **strike (हड़ताल)** is considered the last recourse. The central aim of satyagraha is to **convert and transform the opponent's heart**, never to humiliate or coerce him.
11. **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)** in Bihar was Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India**. It challenged the exploitative **Tinkathia system (तीनकठिया प्रथा)**, which legally forced peasants to cultivate indigo on **3/20th** of their land.
12. **Raj Kumar Shukla (राजकुमार शुक्ल)** persistently pursued Gandhi at the Lucknow Congress (1916) and brought him to Champaran. Gandhi was assisted by **Rajendra Prasad, J.B. Kripalani, Mahadev Desai, Narhari Parikh**, and Mazhar-ul-Haq.
13. During the Champaran inquiry, local cook **Batak Mian (बतक मियां)** foiled an assassination attempt by European indigo planters to poison Gandhi's milk. **N.G. Ranga** was a prominent peasant leader who opposed Gandhi's intervention in Champaran.
14. European indigo planters eventually agreed to surrender their illegal exactions and refund **25%** of the extorted money to the ryots, after which the Tinkathia system was abolished within a year.
15. Rabindranath **Tagore conferred the title 'Mahatma' (महात्मा)** upon Gandhi following the success of the Champaran Satyagraha (1917).
16. **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (March 1918)** was Gandhi's **first hunger strike (पहला अनशन)** in India. The dispute arose over the withdrawal of the **Plague Bonus**; mill owner Ambalal Sarabhai opposed the workers, while his sister **Anasuya Sarabhai** supported Gandhi and the workers. Gandhi successfully negotiated a **35% wage increase**.
17. **Kheda Satyagraha (March–June 1918)** in Gujarat was Gandhi's **first real non-cooperation (सविनय असहयोग)** movement in India, demanding revenue remission after total crop failure. **Vallabhbhai Patel** and **Indulal Yagnik** were Gandhi's chief lieutenants here.
18. The **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act of 1919**, popularly known as the **Rowlatt Act (रॉलेट एक्ट)** (drafted by Sir Sidney Rowlatt), authorized the arrest and detention of any suspect without trial for up to 2 years, popularised by the slogan *"No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal"*.
19. Gandhi organized the **Rowlatt Satyagraha Sabha** (Feb 1919) in Bombay and called for a nationwide strike on **6 April 1919**, which marked Gandhi's first all-India mass mobilization.
20. The **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (जलियांवाला बाग नरसंहार)** took place on **13 April 1919 (Baisakhi day)** in Amritsar, when Brigadier-General **Reginald Dyer** ordered troops to fire upon an unarmed crowd protesting the arrest of leaders **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal**.
21. In moral protest against the Jallianwala Bagh firing, Rabindranath **Tagore renounced his Knighthood (नाइटहुड)**, and Sir **Chettur Sankaran Nair (शंकरन नायर)** resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council.
22. The British Government appointed the **Hunter Committee (हंटर कमेटी 1919)**, formally the *Disorders Inquiry Committee* chaired by Lord William Hunter (included Indian members Chimanlal Setalvad, Pandit Jagat Narayan, and Sultan Ahmad Khan), which merely censured Dyer for an "error of judgment".
23. The **Hunter Committee (1919)** on Punjab atrocities must never be confused with the **Hunter Commission (1882)** headed by W.W. Hunter on Indian education.
24. The Indian National Congress appointed its own parallel inquiry committee for Jallianwala Bagh, comprising **Mahatma Gandhi, Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji**, and M.R. Jayakar.
25. The **Khilafat Movement (खिलाफत आंदोलन, 1919–1924)** was launched by the **Ali Brothers (Maulana Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali)**, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, and Hasrat Mohani to protest the dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire (Caliphate) after World War I.
26. Mahatma Gandhi was elected President of the **All-India Khilafat Conference** held at Delhi in **November 1919**, recognizing it as an unparalleled opportunity to forge enduring Hindu-Muslim unity.
27. The **Non-Cooperation Movement (असहयोग आंदोलन)** was approved at the **Special Calcutta Session (September 1920)** presided by Lala Lajpat Rai, and comprehensively endorsed at the landmark **Nagpur Session (December 1920)** presided by C. Vijayaraghavachariar.
28. At the 1920 Nagpur Session, the Congress constitution was re-drafted: membership fee was reduced to 4 annas, a 15-member Congress Working Committee (CWC) was created, and Provincial Congress Committees (PCCs) were reorganised on a **linguistic basis**.
29. During Non-Cooperation, Gandhi surrendered the **Kaiser-i-Hind (कैसर-ए-हिन्द)** gold medal awarded to him for his humanitarian ambulance corps work during the Boer War.
30. The **Tilak Swaraj Fund (तिलक स्वराज फंड)** was launched in 1921 in memory of Bal Gangadhar Tilak (who died on 1 August 1920, the very day NCM was launched); it surpassed its target of **Rs 1 crore** within six months to finance charkhas and constructive activities.
31. The **Chauri Chaura incident (चौरी चौरा कांड)** occurred on **4/5 February 1922** in Gorakhpur district (UP), where an agitated peasant crowd set fire to a police station, killing 22 policemen.
32. Shocked by the violence, Gandhi unilaterally suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement through the **Bardoli Resolution (बारदोली प्रस्ताव)** passed by the CWC on **12 February 1922**, declaring the country was not yet ready for pure non-violent satyagraha.
33. Following NCM's withdrawal, Gandhi was arrested on 10 March 1922 and sentenced to 6 years' imprisonment by Judge **C.N. Broomfield** at the Great Ahmedabad Trial.
34. At the **Gaya Session of INC (December 1922)**, a sharp division arose between the **Pro-changers (Swarajists)** who advocated entering legislative councils to wreck reforms from within, and the **No-changers** (C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad) who insisted solely on constructive village work.
35. The **Swaraj Party (स्वराज पार्टी)** was founded on **1 January 1923** by **Chittaranjan Das (President)** and **Motilal Nehru (Secretary)**. In the 1923 elections, they won 42 of 101 elected seats in the Central Legislative Assembly, later electing **Vitthalbhai Patel** as the first Indian Assembly Speaker (President) in **1925**.
36. The **Belgaum Session of INC (1924)** was the **only** Congress session ever presided over by **Mahatma Gandhi**, where the Swarajists were authorized to work inside councils on behalf of Congress.
37. **Vaikom Satyagraha (वैक्कम सत्याग्रह, 1924–25)** in Travancore, Kerala, was a non-violent struggle led by K.P. Kesava Menon and T.K. Madhavan demanding access for depressed classes (Ezhavas/Pulayas) to roads surrounding the Shiva Temple; Gandhi visited Vaikom in 1925 to broker an agreement.
38. The **Simon Commission (Indian Statutory Commission)** was appointed in **November 1927** under Sir John Simon with 7 British MP members (all-white, no Indian). Its arrival at Bombay on **3 February 1928** was met with black flags and "Simon Go Back" strikes nationwide.
39. The **Nehru Report (August 1928)**, drafted by an all-parties committee chaired by **Motilal Nehru** with Jawaharlal Nehru as secretary, proposed **Dominion Status**, joint electorates with reserved seats for minorities, fundamental rights, and linguistic provinces.
40. Muhammad Ali Jinnah rejected the Nehru Report and formulated his **Fourteen Points (14 सूत्र)** in **March 1929**, demanding 1/3rd Muslim representation in the central legislature, residual powers to provinces, and separate electorates.
41. At the **Lahore Session of INC (December 1929)** presided by **Jawaharlal Nehru**, Congress declared **Purna Swaraj (पूर्ण स्वराज — Complete Independence)** as its ultimate goal, launched the Civil Disobedience Movement, and declared **26 January 1930** as India's first Independence Pledge Day.
42. On 31 January 1930, Gandhi presented his famous **Eleven Points Ultimatum** to Viceroy Lord Irwin covering rupee-sterling ratio reduction, land revenue cut by 50%, total prohibition, and abolition of the salt tax; Irwin's failure to respond triggered the Civil Disobedience Movement.
43. The historic **Dandi March (दांडी यात्रा)** commenced on **12 March 1930** from Sabarmati Ashram with **78 trusted disciples**; after marching **240 miles (385 km)** in 24 days, Gandhi reached Dandi on 5 April and formally broke the Salt Law on the morning of **6 April 1930**.
44. The brutal assault by British police during the non-violent salt raid at **Dharasana Salt Works (धरासना)** led by **Sarojini Naidu, Imam Saheb, and Manilal Gandhi** was witnessed and reported worldwide by American journalist **Webb Miller** of the United Press.
45. In the North-West Frontier Province, **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (Frontier Gandhi)** raised the volunteer corps **Khudai Khidmatgar (خدائی خدمتگار — Servants of God)** or *Red Shirts (लाल कुर्ती)*; during CDM at Peshawar, soldiers of the **Garhwal Rifles** led by **Chandra Singh Garhwali** refused to fire on unarmed Pathan satyagrahis.
46. The **First Round Table Conference (Nov 1930 – Jan 1931)** in London was opened by King George V and chaired by British PM Ramsay MacDonald; the Congress boycotted it completely.
47. The **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (दिल्ली समझौता)** was signed on **5 March 1931** (negotiated through Tej Bahadur Sapru and M.R. Jayakar): Congress agreed to suspend CDM and participate in the Second RTC, while the British released non-violent political prisoners and allowed coastal salt manufacturing.
48. The **Karachi Session of INC (March 1931)**, presided over by **Vallabhbhai Patel**, ratified the Gandhi-Irwin Pact and adopted landmark resolutions on **Fundamental Rights** and the **National Economic Programme** (drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru).
49. At the **Second Round Table Conference (Sep–Dec 1931)** in London, Mahatma Gandhi attended as the **sole representative of the Indian National Congress**, sailing aboard the *SS Rajputana*. The conference broke down over the communal question and separate electorates for Depressed Classes.
50. On 16 August 1932, British PM Ramsay MacDonald announced the **Communal Award (सांप्रदायिक पंचाट)** granting separate electorates to Depressed Classes. In protest, Gandhi began a fast-unto-death at Yerawada Central Jail on 20 September 1932, culminating in the **Poona Pact (पूना पैक्ट, 24 September 1932)** signed by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya, replacing separate electorates with **148 reserved seats** in provincial legislatures (up from 71).

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

### 1. The Early Trio: "CAKE" (के-क)
- **C** = **C**hamparan (1917) — Indigo / Tinkathia / 1st Satyagraha
- **A** = **A**hmedabad (1918) — Mill Strike / 35% hike / 1st Hunger Strike
- **K** = **K**heda (1918) — Peasant Revenue Remission / 1st Non-Cooperation

### 2. Individual Satyagrahis Order (1940): "VNB" (वी-एन-बी)
- **V** = **V**inoba Bhave (1st Satyagrahi, chosen 17 Oct 1940 at Paunar)
- **N** = Jawaharlal **N**ehru (2nd Satyagrahi)
- **B** = **B**rahma Dutt (3rd Satyagrahi)

### 3. Swaraj Party Architects: "CR-MN" (सी.आर. - एम.एन.)
- **CR** = **C**hitta**R**anjan Das (President)
- **MN** = **M**otilal **N**ehru (Secretary)
- *Formed 1 January 1923 after defeat of council-entry resolution at Gaya (1922).*

### 4. 1931 Landmark Sequence: "G-I-K" (गीक)
- **G-I** = **G**andhi-**I**rwin Pact (5 March 1931)
- **K** = **K**arachi Session (29 March 1931 — Fundamental Rights & Bhagat Singh resolution)
- **2nd RTC** = September–December 1931 in London

### 5. 1932 Depressed Classes Milestones: "CAP" (कैप)
- **C-A** = **C**ommunal **A**ward by Ramsay MacDonald (16 August 1932)
- **P** = **P**oona **P**act at Yerawada Jail (24 September 1932)

### 6. Dandi March Metric Formula: "78 — 24 — 240"
- **78** trusted satyagrahi disciples started from Sabarmati
- **24** days of walking (12 March to 5 April 1930)
- **240** miles (approx. 385 km) covered across Gujarat
