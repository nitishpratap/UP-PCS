## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The major arc of state-building followed: Independence (**15 August 1947**) → Constituent Assembly final sitting (**24 January 1950**) → Constitution commencement (**26 January 1950**) → Planning Commission established (**15 March 1950**) → CDP launched (**2 October 1952**) → Andhra State created (**1 October 1953**) → States Reorganisation Act (**1 November 1956**) → NITI Aayog (**1 January 2015**).
2. The **States Department (रियासती विभाग)** was created on **27 June 1947** under the leadership of **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** as Minister and **V.P. Menon** as Secretary, who successfully integrated about **562 princely states** into the Indian Union.
3. The **Instrument of Accession (IoA)** signed by rulers surrendered only three sovereign subjects to the Dominion of India: **Defence, External Affairs, and Communications**.
4. The **Standstill Agreement** was a provisional legal instrument meant to maintain administrative status-quo as on 15 August 1947 regarding trade, customs, and communications until formal accession was executed; it did not constitute sovereign accession.
5. In **Junagadh**, the Muslim ruler (Nawab Muhammad Mahabat Khan III) signed accession to Pakistan despite an 80% Hindu population; citizens revolted and set up the *Arzi Hukumat* (Provisional Government) under **Samaldas Gandhi** in Bombay; following an Indian administrative takeover, a **plebiscite held on 20 February 1948** voted over 99% to join India.
6. In **Hyderabad**, the 7th Nizam (Mir Osman Ali Khan) refused to join India and sponsored the militant fanatic militia **Razakars (रज़ाकार)** led by **Kasim Razvi**; India launched the military police action **Operation Polo (13–18 September 1948)** under Major-General **J.N. Chaudhuri**, forcing the Nizam's surrender.
7. Major-General **J.N. Chaudhuri** was appointed the first Military Governor of Hyderabad following Operation Polo; Hyderabad was integrated as a Part B state with the Nizam continuing as Rajpramukh until 1956.
8. In **Jammu and Kashmir**, Maharaja **Hari Singh** delayed accession until armed Pathan tribesmen (*lashkars*) backed by the Pakistan Army invaded Kashmir; the Maharaja signed the **Instrument of Accession on 26 October 1947**, which was formally accepted by Governor-General Lord Mountbatten on **27 October 1947**.
9. The **Constituent Assembly (संविधान सभा)** held its first meeting on **9 December 1946** in the Constitution Hall (now Central Hall of Parliament) in New Delhi; **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** was elected interim temporary Chairman.
10. On **11 December 1946**, **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent President of the Constituent Assembly, and **H.C. Mukherjee** and **V.T. Krishnamachari** were elected Vice-Presidents.
11. **Jawaharlal Nehru** moved the historic **Objectives Resolution (उद्देश्य प्रस्ताव)** in the Constituent Assembly on **13 December 1946**, which laid down the philosophy and guiding principles of the Constitution; it was unanimously adopted on **22 January 1947** and later became the **Preamble**.
12. **Sir Benegal Narsing Rau (B.N. Rau)** was appointed the **Constitutional Advisor (संवैधानिक सलाहकार)** to the Constituent Assembly; he prepared the initial draft of the Constitution containing 243 articles and 13 schedules.
13. The **National Flag of India (राष्ट्रीय ध्वज)**, designed by **Pingali Venkayya**, was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on **22 July 1947**; the ratio of its width to length is **2:3**.
14. The **Drafting Committee (प्रारूप समिति)** was appointed on **29 August 1947** with **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** as Chairman; its seven members were: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer, Dr. K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter), and T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan).
15. Key Constituent Assembly Committee Chairpersons:
    - **Union Powers Committee & Union Constitution Committee**: **Jawaharlal Nehru**
    - **Provincial Constitution Committee & Advisory Committee on FR/Minorities**: **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**
    - **Rules of Procedure Committee & Steering Committee**: **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**
    - **Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee**: **J.B. Kripalani**
    - **Minorities Sub-Committee**: **H.C. Mukherjee**
16. The framing of the Constitution took exactly **2 years, 11 months, and 18 days**, during which 11 sessions were held over 165 days of deliberations; the final draft was **adopted, enacted, and given to ourselves on 26 November 1949** (celebrated as *Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas*).
17. The Constitution of India came into **full legal commencement on 26 January 1950** (Republic Day), deliberately chosen to commemorate the **Purna Swaraj declaration day of 26 January 1930** adopted at the Lahore Congress.
18. At its final sitting on **24 January 1950**, the Constituent Assembly unanimously adopted **"Jana Gana Mana"** as the National Anthem, **"Vande Mataram"** as the National Song, and elected **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** as the first President of the Republic of India.
19. The original Constitution was handwritten and calligraphed in flowing italic style by **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** in Delhi, while the illumination and miniature paintings on every page were executed by artists of Kala Bhavan, Shantiniketan under **Nandalal Bose** and **Beohar Rammanohar Sinha**.
20. In the **1950 Constitution**, the States of the Indian Union were grouped into four distinct categories:
    - **Part A (9 States)**: Former British Governors' Provinces (Assam, Bihar, Bombay, MP, Madras, Orissa, Punjab, UP, West Bengal).
    - **Part B (8 States)**: Former Princely States / Unions with legislatures (Hyderabad, J&K, Madhya Bharat, Mysore, Patiala & East Punjab States Union / PEPSU, Rajasthan, Saurashtra, Travancore-Cochin).
    - **Part C (10 States)**: Former Chief Commissioners' Provinces & Centrally Administered States (Ajmer, Bhopal, Bilaspur, Coorg, Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Kutch, Manipur, Tripura, Vindhya Pradesh).
    - **Part D (1 Territory)**: Andaman and Nicobar Islands (administered by Lt. Governor).
21. The **French enclave of Chandernagore (चंद्रनगर)** near Calcutta held a plebiscite on **19 June 1949** voting overwhelmingly to join India; de facto transfer occurred in May 1950, and it was legally integrated into **West Bengal on 2 October 1954**.
22. The remaining **French settlements (Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, and Yanam)** were transferred following the historic **Kizhoor Referendum (18 October 1954)** where 170 of 178 municipal councillors and assembly members voted for merger; de facto transfer took place on **1 November 1954**.
23. The French Parliament ratified the Treaty of Cession only in **May 1962**; de jure transfer was finalized on **16 August 1962**, and Pondicherry was constituted a Union Territory by the **14th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1962**.
24. In the **Portuguese enclaves of Dadra and Nagar Haveli**, local liberation volunteers (Azad Gomantak Dal, United Front of Goans, RSS) ousted the Portuguese administration in **July–August 1954**; it was administered independently until integrated as a UT by the **10th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1961**.
25. In Portuguese **Goa, Daman, and Diu**, Portugal under dictator António de Oliveira Salazar adamantly refused negotiated decolonization; the Indian Armed Forces launched the decisive military action **Operation Vijay (18–19 December 1961)**, liberating Goa after 451 years of Portuguese colonial rule.
26. Major-General **K.P. Candeth** was appointed the military administrator of Goa; Goa, Daman, and Diu were formally incorporated into the Indian Union as a Union Territory by the **12th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1962**.
27. Goa attained **full statehood on 30 May 1987** (becoming the **25th State** of the Indian Union) under the Goa, Daman and Diu Reorganisation Act 1987, while Daman and Diu remained a separate Union Territory.
28. The **S.K. Dhar Commission (Linguistic Provinces Commission)** was appointed by Constituent Assembly President Dr. Rajendra Prasad in **June 1948**; its December 1948 report strongly **opposed linguistic reorganisation**, recommending administrative convenience, geographical contiguity, and financial viability instead.
29. The **JVP Committee** (consisting of **Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, and Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya**) was constituted at the Jaipur Congress Session in **December 1948** to review the Dhar report; its April 1949 report also **rejected language** as the basis of reorganising states for the time being.
30. Gandhian activist **Potti Sreeramulu (पोट्टी श्रीरामुलु)** began a fast-unto-death on 19 October 1952 demanding a separate Telugu-speaking state carved out of Madras State; he **died on 15 December 1952 (56th day of fast)**, unleashing widespread violent unrest across the Andhra region.
31. Bowing to popular pressure, Prime Minister Nehru announced the creation of **Andhra State on 1 October 1953** (with **Kurnool** as capital and High Court at Guntur); Andhra became the **first linguistic state (पहला भाषाई राज्य)** created in independent India.
32. Following the creation of Andhra, the Government appointed the **States Reorganisation Commission (SRC / राज्य पुनर्गठन आयोग)** in **December 1953** headed by retired Supreme Court Chief Justice **Fazl Ali (Chairman)**, with members **K.M. Panikkar** and **H.N. Kunzru**.
33. The SRC submitted its report in September 1955: it rejected the theory of *"one language, one state"*, but broadly recognized linguistic and cultural homogeneity along with national security and financial viability as valid criteria for state formation.
34. Parliament enacted the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** and the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**, which abolished the Part A, B, C, D classification and restructured India into **14 States and 6 Union Territories** effective **1 November 1956**.
35. The **Etawah Pilot Project (इटावा पायलट प्रोजेक्ट)** was launched in **September 1948** at Mahewa in Etawah district (UP) by American town planner **Albert Mayer**, with the backing of UP Premier Govind Ballabh Pant, establishing the concept of the multi-purpose village-level worker (VLW).
36. Inspired by the Etawah pilot, the nationwide **Community Development Programme (CDP / सामुदायिक विकास कार्यक्रम)** was inaugurated on **2 October 1952** (Gandhi Jayanti) covering 55 community projects across India, supported by the US Ford Foundation.
37. The **National Extension Service (NES / राष्ट्रीय प्रसार सेवा)** was launched on **2 October 1953** as a permanent, less resource-intensive administrative setup to carry the message of CDP to every village in the country.
38. **Nilokheri (Mazdoor Manzil)** in Karnal (Haryana) was developed by **S.K. Dey** (later India's first Minister for Community Development and Panchayati Raj) as a self-reliant township for 7,000 displaced Partition refugees, serving as an urban-rural development pilot.
39. The **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** was appointed in **January 1957** to evaluate the CDP and NES; its November 1957 report recommended the establishment of a **3-tier system of Democratic Decentralisation (Panchayati Raj)**:
    - Gram Panchayat at Village level (direct election)
    - Panchayat Samiti at Block level (indirect election, executive body)
    - Zilla Parishad at District level (advisory and supervisory body)
40. The Balwant Rai Mehta committee expressly noted that **Nyaya Panchayats** (judicial bodies) were separate and did **not** form one of the three tiers of democratic decentralisation.
41. India's Panchayati Raj system was first inaugurated at **Nagaur district (Rajasthan)** by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on **2 October 1959**; Andhra Pradesh became the second state to adopt Panchayati Raj later that same year (November 1959).
42. The **Planning Commission (योजना आयोग)** was established on **15 March 1950** through an **executive cabinet resolution** (neither constitutional nor statutory) with Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru as its ex-officio Chairman and **Gulzarilal Nanda** as its first Deputy Chairman; constitutional scholar Ashok Chanda referred to it as an *"Economic Cabinet"*.
43. The **National Development Council (NDC / राष्ट्रीय विकास परिषद)** was established on **6 August 1952** by cabinet resolution to review and approve the Five-Year Plans, chaired by the PM and including all Union Cabinet ministers, state Chief Ministers, and Planning Commission members.
44. The **First Five-Year Plan (1951–1956)** was based on the **Harrod-Domar Growth Model**, prioritizing agriculture, irrigation, and power; major multi-purpose dams like **Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud (Mahanadi), and Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC)** were initiated.
45. The **Second Five-Year Plan (1956–1961)** was formulated on the **P.C. Mahalanobis Four-Sector Model**, prioritizing rapid industrialization and heavy capital goods; it established three major public sector steel plants: **Bhilai (Chhattisgarh)** with Soviet USSR assistance, **Rourkela (Odisha)** with West German assistance, and **Durgapur (West Bengal)** with British assistance.
46. The **Third Five-Year Plan (1961–1966)** aimed at self-reliant and self-generating growth (*"Take-off stage"*), but suffered catastrophic failure due to the **Sino-Indian War (1962)**, **Indo-Pak War (1965)**, and severe drought (1965–66), leading to the three-year **"Plan Holidays" (1966–1969)**.
47. The **Panchsheel Agreement (पंचशील सिद्धांत)** — Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence — was signed on **29 April 1954** between Indian Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Chinese Premier Zhou Enlai as part of the Sino-Indian Agreement on Trade and Intercourse between Tibet Region of China and India.
48. The **Bandung Conference (April 1955)** in Indonesia brought together 29 newly independent Asian and African nations, establishing the spirit of Afro-Asian solidarity and laying the diplomatic foundation for the Non-Aligned Movement.
49. The **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM / गुटनिरपेक्ष आंदोलन)** was officially founded at the **First NAM Summit held at Belgrade (Yugoslavia) in September 1961**, spearheaded by five founding leaders: **Jawaharlal Nehru** (India), **Josip Broz Tito** (Yugoslavia), **Gamal Abdel Nasser** (Egypt), **Sukarno** (Indonesia), and **Kwame Nkrumah** (Ghana).
50. Royal privy purses and special privileges guaranteed to former princely state rulers under the Instrument of Accession were officially abolished by the **26th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971** under Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, repealing Articles 291 and 362.

### 1. SRC Members Trio: "FA-KP-HK" (फा-का-ह)

- **KP** = **K**.M. **P**anikkar
- **HK** = **H**.N. **K**unzru
- *Trap Buster:* Neither K.T. Shah nor Pattabhi Sitaramayya was a member of the SRC.

### 2. JVP Committee Trio (1948): "J-V-P"
- **J** = **J**awaharlal Nehru
- **V** = **V**allabhbhai Patel
- **P** = **P**attabhi Sitaramayya

### 3. Enclave Liberation Modes: "P-A-C" (पैक)
- **P** = **P**lebiscite / Vote → Junagadh (1948) & Chandernagore (1949)
- **A** = **A**rmed Police Action → Hyderabad (*Operation Polo*, 1948) & Goa (*Operation Vijay*, 1961)
- **C** = **C**onsensus & Treaty → French Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam (*Kizhoor Referendum 1954 / Treaty 1962*)

### 4. Five-Year Plan Models: "1-Harrod, 2-Maha"
- **1st FYP** = **Harrod**-Domar Model (K.N. Raj) → Agriculture & Multi-purpose Dams (Bhakra, Hirakud, DVC)
- **2nd FYP** = P.C. **Maha**lanobis 4-Sector Model → Heavy Industry & 3 Steel Plants (Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur)

### 5. Foreign Steel Plants in 2nd Plan: "B-R-D" (बी-आर-डी)
- **B** = **B**hilai (Chhattisgarh) — **USSR** (Soviet Union)
- **R** = **R**ourkela (Odisha) — West **Germany** (Krupp-Demag)
- **D** = **D**urgapur (West Bengal) — **UK** (Britain)

### 6. Balwant Rai Mehta 3 Tiers: "V-B-Z"
- **V** = **V**illage level → Gram Panchayat
- **B** = **B**lock level → Panchayat Samiti (key executive unit)
- **Z** = **Z**illa level → Zilla Parishad (supervisory body)
