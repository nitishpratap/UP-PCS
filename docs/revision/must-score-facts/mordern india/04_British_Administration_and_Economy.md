## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. Colonial economic policy moved through three distinct phases (R. Palme Dutt thesis):
   - **Phase 1 (1757–1813): Merchant Capital / Monopoly Trade:** Plunder of Bengal, misuse of *dastaks*, financing British purchases from Bengal revenues without bringing bullion.
   - **Phase 2 (1813–1858): Industrial Capital / Free Trade:** Subordinating India to British industrial capitalism; India became an exporter of raw materials (cotton, silk, indigo) and importer of British machine-made textiles; deindustrialization of Indian handicrafts.
   - **Phase 3 (1858–1947): Financial Capitalism / Foreign Investment:** British capital investment in railways (guaranteed 5%), plantations, coal mines, managing agencies, and public debt ("The Drain of Wealth").
2. **Permanent Settlement (1793) [स्थायी बंदोबस्त / ज़मींदारी प्रथा]:**
   - Introduced by **Lord Cornwallis** on 22 March 1793, planned in collaboration with **Sir John Shore**.
   - Region: **Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Varanasi (UP), and Northern Circars (Madras)**, covering **~19% of British India**.
   - Terms: Zamindars recognized as absolute owners/proprietors of land; revenue demand fixed in perpetuity.
   - State vs Zamindar Share: State received **10/11th** of the rental, Zamindar kept **1/11th**.
   - **Sunset Law (सूर्यास्त कानून):** Failure to pay by sunset of the appointed date resulted in public auction of the zamindari estate.
3. **Ryotwari System (रैयतवाड़ी बंदोबस्त, ~51%):**
   - First experimented by **Captain Alexander Read** in Baramahal district (Madras) in **1792**.
   - Systematically developed and implemented by **Sir Thomas Munro** (Governor of Madras, 1820).
   - Region: **Madras, Bombay (Elphinstone), Assam, and Coorg**, covering **~51% of British India**.
   - Terms: Direct revenue settlement with the individual cultivator (**ryot**); ryots had ownership and transfer rights as long as revenue was paid; revenue revised periodically (every 20–30 years); high tax rate (45% to 55% of gross produce).
4. **Mahalwari System (महालवाड़ी बंदोबस्त, ~30%):**
   - Conceived by **Holt Mackenzie** (Regulation VII of **1822**), modified by **Robert Merttins Bird** ("Father of land settlement in North India") and legalized under **William Bentinck (Regulation IX of 1833)**.
   - Region: **North-Western Provinces (UP), Awadh, Central Provinces, and Punjab**, covering **~30% of British India**.
   - Terms: Settlement made with the entire village community (**Mahal / ग्राम**) represented by the village headman (**Lambardar / मुकद्दम**), with joint and several responsibility for revenue payment; rate fixed at 66% (later reduced to 50% under Saharanpur Rules of 1855).
5. **Impact of Land Settlements:** Created a parasitic class of absentee landlords in Bengal; destroyed ancient village community autonomy; commercialized agriculture; drove peasantry into generational debt traps of local moneylenders (*mahajans / sahukars*).
6. **Cornwallis Code (1793) [कॉर्नवालिस कोड]:**
   - Based on Montesquieu's principle of **Separation of Powers**: separated revenue collection from judicial administration.
   - Deprived District Collectors of judicial and magisterial functions, creating a dedicated **District and Sessions Judge**.
   - Established hierarchical civil courts: **Munsiff’s Court (Indian) → Registrar's Court → District Diwani Court → 4 Provincial Courts of Appeal (Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad, Patna) → Sadar Diwani Adalat** (Calcutta).
   - Criminal justice hierarchy: District Magistrate → 4 Circuit Courts (travelling courts) → **Sadar Nizamat Adalat** (Calcutta).
7. **Thana–Daroga Police System (1793):**
   - Cornwallis relieved zamindars of all police duties; divided districts into police circles (**thanas**) of ~20 square miles, each headed by a paid government officer called **Daroga** (supervised by District Magistrate).
8. **Haileybury College (1806–1858):** Established at Hertford (England) to train nominated British civil servants for India. Replaced Wellesley's Fort William College Calcutta (which was restricted to Indian languages only).
9. **Open Competition for Civil Services:**
   - **Charter Act 1853** abolished patronage system and opened ICS to merit competition.
   - **Macaulay Committee on Indian Civil Service (1854)** formulated competitive exam rules; first exam held in London in 1855.
   - First Indian to qualify: **Satyendranath Tagore (1863)**.
   - Surendranath Banerjee qualified in 1869, later disqualified on trivial grounds (1874).
10. **Simultaneous ICS Examinations:** Began in **1922** held simultaneously in London and **Allahabad**. The **Lee Commission (1923–24)** recommended 50:50 Indian-British ratio in 15 years and led to the establishment of the **Public Service Commission of India (1 Oct 1926)** under Sir Ross Barker.
11. **Drain of Wealth Theory (धन निष्कासन सिद्धांत):**
    - First propounded by **Dadabhai Naoroji** in his paper *"England's Debt to India"* (1867) before the East India Association, London.
    - Systematized in his landmark book ***Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1901)**.
    - Naoroji described Drain as the **"evil of all evils"** and a running hemorrhage sucking India's lifeblood.
    - Official endorsement: INC officially adopted the Drain Theory at its **Calcutta Session in 1896** (presided over by Rahimtulla M. Sayani).
12. **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's Stance:** Sir Syed Ahmad Khan explicitly **rejected** Naoroji's Drain Theory and urged Muslims to remain loyal to the British Crown, considering British rule essential for modernization.
13. **R.C. Dutt (Romesh Chunder Dutt):** Published the two-volume classic ***The Economic History of India* (1902 & 1904)**; proved that nearly half of India's net revenue was drained out annually; examined rural impoverishment under land settlements.
14. **Components of "Home Charges" (गृह प्रभार):**
    - Cost of the Secretary of State's India Office establishment in London.
    - Pensions and furloughs of retired British civil and military personnel.
    - Guaranteed interest (5%) on British capital invested in Indian railways and irrigation works.
    - Interest on public debt raised by British India in London money markets.
    - *Examiner Trap: War expenses fought outside India (e.g. Afghanistan, Burma, China) were NOT part of regular Home Charges, though often unjustly charged to Indian revenues.*
15. **Railway Guarantee System (1849–1869):** British private railway companies were guaranteed a minimum return of **4.5% to 5%** on invested capital directly from Indian tax revenues, with land granted free on a 99-year lease; incentivized wasteful expenditure ("private enterprise at public risk").
16. **First Indian Railways & Telegraph:**
    - First Passenger Railway: **Bombay to Thane (34 km, 16 April 1853)** operated by Great Indian Peninsula Railway (GIPR) with 3 engines (*Sultan, Sindh, Sahib*).
    - Second Passenger Line: Howrah to Hooghly (1854).
    - First Electric Telegraph Line: Calcutta to Agra (1853), directed by **William O'Shaughnessy**.
17. **First Modern Industries in India:**
    - First successful Cotton Textile Mill: **Bombay (1854)** by **Cowasji Nanabhai Davar** (Bombay Spinning and Weaving Co.).
    - First Jute Mill: **Rishra (near Calcutta, 1855)** by George Acland.
    - First modern Iron and Steel Plant: **TISCO at Jamshedpur (1907)** by Jamshedji Tata (commercial production 1911).
    - First Paper Mill: Ballygunge near Calcutta (1870).
18. **Indigo Cultivation Systems:**
    - **Nij System (निज):** Planter cultivated indigo directly on land he owned or rented with hired labourers.
    - **Ryoti System (रैयती):** Planter forced the cultivator (*ryot*) to sign a contract and accept cash advances (**Dadan / दादनी**) to cultivate indigo on 3/20th or more of his best land at fixed non-remunerative prices.
19. **Major Famines in British India:**
    - **Great Bengal Famine (1770):** One-third population starved under Clive's Dual Government.
    - **Orissa Famine (1866):** 1.3 million dead; John Lawrence government failed to distribute grain.
    - **Great Famine of 1876–1878:** Madras, Bombay, Mysore, Hyderabad; 5+ million dead while Lytton held Delhi Durbar.
    - **Famines of 1896–1897 & 1899–1900:** Affected Central India, Bombay, UP under Elgin II and Curzon.
    - **Great Bengal Famine (1943):** Man-made famine under Linlithgow; 3 million starved due to wartime denial policy, hoarding, and rice import cut-off from Burma.
20. **Famine Commissions (Match Ladder):**
    - **Campbell Commission (1866)** under Sir John Lawrence: Stated state has duty to preserve life in famine.
    - **Strachey Commission (1880)** under Lord Lytton: Drafted the **Provisional Famine Code (1883)**; created Famine Relief & Insurance Fund of ₹1.5 crore/year.
    - **Lyall Commission (1897)** under Lord Elgin II: Reaffirmed Strachey code; recommended assistance to weavers and artisans.
    - **MacDonnell Commission (1900)** under Lord Curzon: Emphasized "moral strategy" (early distribution of loans/taccavi) and appointment of a Famine Commissioner.
    - **Woodhead Commission (1945)** under Lord Wavell: Inquired into 1943 Bengal Famine; recommended state control of food grains and rationing.
21. **First Municipal Corporation in India:** Established at **Madras in 1687–88** by Company charter; followed by Bombay and Calcutta in 1726.
22. **Evolution of Local Self-Government:**
    - **Lord Mayo's Resolution of 1870:** First step in financial decentralization; transferred administration of local roads, health, and education to provincial governments.
    - **Lord Ripon's Resolution of 1882:** "Magna Carta of Local Self-Government"; established rural district local boards and urban municipalities with non-official elected majorities and elected chairmen.
    - **Royal Commission on Decentralisation (1907)** under C.E.H. Hobhouse: Recommended village panchayats.
    - **Government of India Act 1919:** Local Self-Government became a **Transferred Subject** under Indian ministers in provinces.
23. **First National Income Estimations:**
    - **Dadabhai Naoroji (1867–68):** First per capita income estimate for India = **₹20 per year**.
    - **William Digby (1899):** Estimated per capita income at ₹18/year.
    - **Lord Curzon (1901):** Official government estimate = ₹30/year.
    - **Dr. V.K.R.V. Rao (1931–32):** First scientific national income estimate using census of production = **₹62 per year**.
24. **Deindustrialization (विऔद्योगीकरण):** Collapse of traditional Indian handicrafts and handloom textiles without the simultaneous growth of modern domestic industries, transforming India from the world's leading exporter of manufactured goods in 1750 (24.5% global share) to less than 2% by 1947. Bentinck observed in 1834: *"The misery hardly finds a parallel in the history of commerce. The bones of the cotton-weavers are bleaching the plains of India."*
25. **De-urbanization and Ruralization:** Decline of historical urban manufacturing centers like Dhaka ("Manchester of India"), Murshidabad, Surat, Patna, and Madurai; displaced weavers and artisans migrated to villages, increasing the agricultural workforce from 61% (1891) to 73% (1921).
26. **Commercialization of Agriculture:** Transition from food-grain cultivation for local village subsistence to cash-crop production (cotton, jute, indigo, tea, sugarcane, oilseeds) for export and domestic British-owned factories; increased peasant vulnerability to international price crashes (e.g., American Civil War cotton boom 1861–65 followed by deep depression triggering 1875 Deccan Riots).
27. **Charter Act 1813 Free Trade Impact:** Abolished Company’s trade monopoly in India; flooded India with cheap machine-woven Lancashire and Manchester cotton textiles; British goods entered India paying nominal duties (2.5%), while Indian textiles in Britain were slapped with tariffs up to 80%.
28. **Managing Agency System (प्रबंध अभिकरण प्रणाली):** European managing agency houses (e.g. Andrew Yule, Bird & Co., Martin Burn) controlled multiple industrial, tea, jute, and coal companies on minimal equity, extracting enormous management commissions and locking out Indian enterprise.
29. **Early Banking Evolution:**
    - First Bank in India: **Bank of Hindostan (1770)** at Calcutta (by Alexander & Co.).
    - Presidency Banks: Bank of Bengal (1806), Bank of Bombay (1840), Bank of Madras (1843).
    - Amalgamated into the **Imperial Bank of India in 1921**, which became the **State Bank of India (SBI) in 1955**.
    - First purely Indian commercial bank: **Oudh Commercial Bank (1881)** in Faizabad.
    - First Indian bank managed entirely by Indians: **Punjab National Bank (1894)** in Lahore (Lala Lajpat Rai among founders).
30. **Currency and Coinage Commissions:**
    - **Mansfield Commission (1866):** Recommended gold currency.
    - **Herschell Committee (1893):** Closed Indian mints to free coinage of silver to stop rupee depreciation.
    - **Fowler Committee (1898):** Recommended Gold Exchange Standard fixing the rupee at **1 shilling 4 pence (1s 4d)**.
    - **Babington Smith Committee (1919):** Recommended fixing rupee at 2s gold.
    - **Hilton Young Commission (1926):** Recommended establishment of Reserve Bank of India and fixed rupee ratio at 1s 6d.
31. **Labour and Agricultural Commissions:**
    - **Royal Commission on Agriculture (1926–1928):** Headed by Lord Linlithgow; led to the establishment of the **Imperial Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) in 1929**.
    - **Whitley Commission on Labour (1929–1931):** (Royal Commission on Labour in India) headed by J.H. Whitley; investigated labour conditions in factories, mines, and plantations.
32. **Factory Legislation Progression:**
    - **First Factory Act (1881, Ripon):** Applied to factories with ≥100 workers using mechanical power; banned employment of children under 7; hours for children aged 7–12 limited to 9 hours/day with 1 hour rest; 4 monthly holidays.
    - **Second Factory Act (1891, Lansdowne):** Applied to factories with ≥50 workers; child employment age raised to 9–14 (hours limited to 7/day); women's work hours limited to 11 hours/day with 1.5-hour rest; mandatory weekly Sunday holiday for all.
    - **Third Factory Act (1911):** Adult male factory work limited to 12 hours/day in textile mills; children limited to 6 hours.
33. **Sunset Law (सूर्यास्त कानून):** A crucial clause of the Permanent Settlement of 1793: If the zamindar failed to deposit the specified revenue by sunset of the specified date, his entire zamindari estate was confiscated and auctioned off publicly.
34. **Dadan System (दादनी प्रथा):** British merchants and European indigo planters forced peasants into accepting cash advances (*dadan*) at below-market rates, trapping cultivators into generational debt and compulsory cash-crop farming.
35. **Early Nationalist Economic Thinkers:**
    - **Dadabhai Naoroji:** Pioneer of Drain theory.
    - **Mahadev Govind Ranade:** Wrote *Essays in Indian Economics* (1898); advocated state-led industrialization.
    - **Romesh Chunder Dutt:** Author of *The Economic History of India* (1902, 1904).
    - **G.K. Gokhale:** Exposed colonial fiscal exploitation in his imperial budget speeches.
    - **G. Subramania Iyer:** Founded *The Hindu* and *Swadesamitran*; fiercely critiqued economic Drain.
    - **Prithwis Chandra Ray:** Authored *The Poverty Problem in India* (1895).
36. **Judiciary Reform by Warren Hastings (1772):** Established in each district a **Mofussil Diwani Adalat** (civil court under Collector) and a **Mofussil Faujdari Adalat** (criminal court under Muslim law officers); apex appeal courts at Calcutta were **Sadar Diwani Adalat** and **Sadar Nizamat Adalat**.
37. **Cornwallis Code Separation of Judiciary:** Deprived Collector of judicial functions; created the post of **District Judge**; abolished court fees; permitted legal suits by Indian subjects against government officials for administrative misconduct.
38. **Bentinck’s Judicial Reforms (1829–1831):** Abolished the 4 Provincial Courts of Appeal and Circuit Courts (which were backlogged); transferred their duties to District and Sessions Judges; allowed **vernacular languages** instead of Persian in lower courts, and English or Persian in higher courts; appointed Indians to higher judicial posts like **Sadar Amins**.
39. **Indian High Courts Act (1861):** Authorized the Crown to establish High Courts; **Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras High Courts** established in **1862**, merging Sadar Adalats and Supreme Courts. Allahabad High Court established in **1866** (initially at Agra, shifted to Allahabad in 1869).
40. **Codification of Laws in India:**
    - First Law Commission established under **Charter Act 1833** headed by **Lord Macaulay**.
    - **Indian Penal Code (IPC):** Drafted 1837, enacted **1860** (took effect 1 Jan 1862).
    - **Code of Civil Procedure (CPC):** Enacted **1859**.
    - **Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC):** Enacted **1861**.
    - **Indian Evidence Act:** Enacted **1872** (drafted by Sir James Fitzjames Stephen).
    - **Indian Contract Act:** Enacted **1872**.
41. **Indian Police Act (1861):** Recommended by the Police Commission of 1860; created the modern Indian Police Service structure with Inspector General (IG) at provincial head, Superintendent of Police (SP) at district head, and Darogas/Inspectors at thana level.
42. **Lee Commission (1923–24) Findings:** Formed on Superior Civil Services under Lord Lee of Fareham; recommended rapid Indianization: 40% British, 40% Indian direct recruits, 20% promoted from provincial services to achieve 50:50 parity in 15 years; established Public Service Commission.
43. **Satyendranath Tagore’s Historic Feat:** In 1863, Satyendranath Tagore (brother of Rabindranath) broke the British monopoly by qualifying for the ICS in London. Followed in 1869 by **Surendranath Banerjee, Romesh Chunder Dutt, and Behari Lal Gupta**.
44. **Subhas Chandra Bose’s ICS Resignation:** Subhas Chandra Bose stood **4th** in the ICS examination in London in 1920; resigned in April 1921 declaring: *"No real service can be rendered to the country as long as one remains in the iron frame of the British bureaucracy."*
45. **Managing Agencies Dominance:** By 1914, just 10 British managing agencies controlled over 75% of industrial capital in eastern India, preventing capital formation among Indian entrepreneurs until the World Wars.
46. **First Indian Joint Stock Bank with Limited Liability:** **Oudh Commercial Bank** founded in **1881** in Faizabad; failed in 1958. Punjab National Bank was founded in **1894** in Lahore.
47. **Imperial Preference (इंपीरियल प्रेफरेंस):** Special low tariff rates granted to British imports entering India, while high import duties were levied on goods from foreign competitors (Japan, Germany, USA); institutionalized at the **Ottawa Conference of 1932**.
48. **Land Revenue Percentages Master Match:**
    - **Permanent Settlement (1793):** ~**19%** of British India (Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Varanasi).
    - **Ryotwari System (1820):** ~**51%** of British India (Madras, Bombay, Assam).
    - **Mahalwari System (1822/1833):** ~**30%** of British India (NWP/UP, Punjab, Central Provinces).
49. **Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901):** Published in London; contained Naoroji’s six essays demonstrating the mathematical Drain from Indian revenue and production shipped to Britain without economic equivalent.
50. **Three Administrative Pillars of the British Raj:**
    1. **Civil Service (Steel Frame):** Policy implementation and revenue administration.
    2. **Army:** External defense, regional conquest, and internal suppression.
    3. **Police:** Maintenance of internal order, crime suppression, and monitoring political movements.

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

1. **Three Land Settlements Percentage Share:**

   - **M**ahalwari = **30%** (Mackenzie, NWP/UP)
   - **Z**amindari / Permanent = **19%** (Cornwallis, Bengal/Bihar).
2. **Land Settlement Architects:**
   - **P - C - S**: **P**ermanent → **C**ornwallis & **S**hore
   - **R - M - R**: **R**yotwari → **M**unro & **R**ead
   - **M - M - B**: **M**ahalwari → **M**ackenzie & **B**ird / Bentinck.
3. **Three Stages of British Colonial Exploitation (R.P. Dutt):**
   - **M - I - F**
   - **M**ercantile (1757–1813): Direct plunder & commercial monopoly
   - **I**ndustrial (1813–1858): Free trade & destruction of Indian handicrafts
   - **F**inancial (1858–1947): Foreign capital, railways, and Drain.
4. **Key Economists Exposing the Drain:**
   - **N - D - R - G**
   - **N**aoroji (Poverty & Un-British Rule)
   - **D**utt (Economic History of India)
   - **R**anade (Essays on Indian Economics)
   - **G**okhale (Budget speeches in Imperial Council).

   - **S**trachey = **S**tate Code (**1880** Strachey Commission created **1883** Famine Code).
