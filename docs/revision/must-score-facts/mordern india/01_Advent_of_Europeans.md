## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. Vasco da Gama reached **Calicut** on **20 May 1498**, guided by the Gujarati navigator **Ahmad ibn Majid**. He opened the all-sea Atlantic route for Portugal. He was received by the Hindu ruler **Zamorin** (सामूतिरी) and did **not** capture **Goa** (गोवा).
2. Black pepper (काली मिर्च) profits from that voyage (about **60x**) pulled more Portuguese merchants to India. Portuguese also introduced the **printing press (Goa, 1556)**, **tobacco, potato, tomato, cashew nut, papaya, and chili** to India.
3. **Francisco de Almeida (अल्मेदा)** was the first **Portuguese Viceroy (वायसराय)** in **1505**. His **Blue Water Policy** (Cartaze/sea-supremacy) meant control of sea lanes, not conquest of the Deccan (दक्कन) interior. He was defeated at Chaul (1508) by joint Egyptian-Gujarati fleet, but won Battle of Diu (1509).
4. **Afonso de Albuquerque** (Governor 1509–15) captured **Goa in 1510** from **Yusuf Adil Shah** (यूसुफ आदिल) of Bijapur (बीजापुर) and made it the capital of **Estado da Índia**. Do not swap him with Almeida as the first Viceroy. He encouraged Portuguese men to marry Indian women (*casados*) and abolished sati in Goa.
5. **Nuno da Cunha** shifted the Portuguese HQ from **Cochin to Goa in 1530** and acquired **Bassein (1534)** and **Diu (1535)** from Bahadur Shah of Gujarat.
6. The first European fort in India was built by Albuquerque at **Cochin in 1503** (**Fort Emmanuel**, before he became Governor). First Portuguese factory (फैक्टरी) was at **Calicut in 1500** (Pedro Álvares Cabral’s voyage).
7. A **cartaze** was a Portuguese sea-pass for Indian Ocean ships (नावाध्यक्ष). A Mughal **farman** was a trade (पण्याध्यक्ष) grant on land — do not swap the two.
8. Arrival order in India: **Portuguese (1498) → Dutch (1605/1596) → English (1608/1600) → Danes (1620) → French (1668)**. Portuguese came first (**1498**) and left last (**1961**, Goa liberation under Operation Vijay). Portuguese Governor-General of Goa at Indian independence in 1947 was **José Ferreira Bossa**; last Governor-General at 1961 liberation was **Manuel António Vassalo e Silva**.
9. The **English East India Company** was chartered on **31 December 1600** by Queen Elizabeth I (Akbar still Mughal emperor). The Dutch **VOC** (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie) was founded in **1602**.
10. The **French East India Company** (Compagnie des Indes Orientales) was founded in **1664** by **Jean-Baptiste Colbert** under **Louis XIV**, with **Pondicherry** (पांडिचेरी) as HQ from **1674**. **Goa** stayed Portuguese; do not call Pondicherry Portuguese.
11. The Danish company dates from **1616**, with the **Tranquebar** (ट्रैंकेबार) factory from **1620**. They sold their settlements to the British in **1845** and never built a land empire.
12. A **factory** (फैक्टरी) was a **trading post** with local permission (warehouse/office/residence of factors), not an industrial production mill and not a territorial colony (उपनिवेश).
13. Early English and Dutch trade at ports like Surat rested on a **Mughal farman**, not on military conquest. Early European presence was coastal and commercial.
14. The three classic EIC **presidencies** were **Madras, Bombay and Bengal**. The early western Presidency sat at **Surat**, then shifted to Bombay in 1687. First **Municipal Corporation** in India was set up at **Madras** in **1687–88**.
15. **William Hawkins (हॉकिन्स)** reached Jahangir (जहाँगीर)’s court in **1608** on the ship *Hector* as an EIC captain who spoke Turkish (given 400 zat mansab and title Khan). **Sir Thomas Roe (रो)** was James I’s official ambassador in **1615–19**. Hawkins did **not** come in 1611 as James I’s envoy.
16. First English temporary factory: **Masulipatnam 1611** (मसूलिपत्तनम). After **Thomas Best** won naval battle at **Swally (1612)** against the Portuguese, the first permanent English factory rose at **Surat in 1613**. Dutch Surat followed in **1616**; French Surat in **1668**.
17. **Francis Day** obtained the Madras lease from the Raja of Chandragiri in **1639**; **Fort St George** followed in **1640**. **Bombay** came as Portuguese dowry to King Charles II marrying Catherine of Braganza in **1661–62** and was leased to the EIC in **1668** for **£10** a year.
18. **Job Charnock** settled at **Sutanuti** (Calcutta) in **1690** (Fort William, fortified 1696). **Shah Jahan** (शाहजहाँ) ordered Qasim Khan to destroy the Portuguese pirate base at **Hooghly (हुगली) in 1632**.
19. Dutch spine: first factory **Masulipatnam 1605**; Coromandel HQ **Pulicat (Fort Geldria (गेल्ड्रिया), 1610)** then shifted to **Nagapattinam (1690)**; Bengal **Chinsurah (Fort Gustavus (गुस्तावस), 1653)**; **Cochin 1663**. Trade = Coromandel textiles, Bengal silk, Bihar saltpetre/opium for Indonesian Spice Islands profits.
20. At **Colachel (10 Aug 1741)**, Travancore under **Marthanda Varma** decisively defeated the Dutch navy. At **Bedara / Chinsurah (25 Nov 1759)**, English under **Colonel Francis Forde** crushed Dutch forces and ended Dutch political challenge in India.
21. **First Carnatic War (1746–48)** — French lead **Dupleix (डुप्ले)/La Bourdonnais**; sparked by English Commodore Curtis Barnet seizing French ships (Austrian Succession context in Europe). Ended by **Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle 1748** (Madras returned to English, Louisbourg in America to French).
22. **Second Carnatic War (1749–54)** — Sparked by dynastic struggles in Hyderabad (Asaf Jah death: Nasir Jang vs Muzaffar Jang) and Carnatic (Anwar-ud-din vs Chanda Sahib). **Ambur 1749**, Clive (क्लाइव)’s siege of **Arcot 1751**; **Dupleix** recalled in 1754; ends with **Treaty of Pondicherry 1754** (negotiated by Godeheu).
23. **Third Carnatic War (1758–63)** — Seven Years War context. **Count de Lally** fails at Madras; **Sir Eyre Coote** wins **Wandiwash (22 Jan 1760)** (वांडिवाश); **Treaty of Paris 1763** leaves French with unfortified factories only.
24. **Wandiwash (1760)** is Anglo–French (Coote defeats Lally). **Plassey (प्लासी) (1757)** is Clive’s Bengal battle against Siraj-ud-Daula. Do not swap commanders or theatres.
25. After **1763** the English dominated European rivals in India. French power was reduced to enclaves (Pondicherry, Chandernagore, Mahe, Karaikal, Yanam); the path to Bengal empire opened next.
26. Portuguese India was a **crown** empire (**Estado da Índia**). Dutch, English, French and Danish presence was mainly **company-led** under royal charters.
27. English beat the French through a stronger navy, Bengal wealth after Plassey (1757), steadier financial home support, and the recall of Dupleix that broke French political continuity.
28. **Hormuz (1515)** and **Malacca (1511)** were Portuguese choke-points for sea lanes captured by Albuquerque. Their main late Indian holdings were **Goa, Daman and Diu**.
29. English Traveller/Envoy order: **Ralph Fitch** (1583–91, before EIC) → **John Mildenhall** (1599, overland) → **William Hawkins (1608)** → **Paul Canning (1612)** → **Nicholas Downton (1614)** → **Sir Thomas Roe (1615–19)**. **Peter Mundy** was an English traveller under Shah Jahan (not Portuguese).
30. Bengal power map: **Bandel (बन्देल्)/Hooghly** Portuguese, **Chinsurah** Dutch, **Chandernagore** (चंद्रनगर) French, **Serampore** (श्रीरामपुर) Danish, **Calcutta** English.
31. Carnatic Wars are **English vs French** in the 1740s–60s in the Carnatic region. Anglo–Mysore (मैसूर) Wars are **English vs Hyder Ali / Tipu Sultan** later (1767–1799).
32. Portuguese **capitanias** were coastal captaincies under fort captains. A **cartaze** was the compulsory naval trade pass for Asian vessels.
33. **Farrukhsiyar (फ़र्रुख़)’s farman of 1717** (termed Magna Carta of Company by Orme) gave EIC duty-free trade privileges in Bengal for an annual payment of ₹3,000 via *dastaks*. It is **not** the **Diwani (दीवानी) of 1765** (granted after Buxar).
34. **Founders of Cities (Match-the-following)**: **Job Charnock** (Calcutta, 1690), **Francis Day** (Madras, 1639), **Gerald Aungier** (Bombay, 1669–77), **François Martin** (Pondicherry, 1674).
35. **Albuquerque’s policies:** control Goa as capital; choke Hormuz/Malacca sea lanes; break Arab spice middlemen; encourage *casados* (Portuguese–Indian marriages); fortify key coastal points — conquest of strategic ports, not Deccan interior like Almeida’s Blue Water line.
36. **Battle of Swally (1612) — Cause/Course/Result:** Portuguese challenged English Surat trade → Captain **Thomas Best** won the naval fight → permanent English **Surat factory (1613)** sanctioned by Jahangir; Swally is not the factory founding date itself.
37. **EIC charter ladder (not Factory Acts):** original **1600** (Elizabeth I) → renewals through 17th–18th centuries → **1813** ends India trade monopoly (tea/China kept) → **1833** ends all Company commercial trade → **1853** opens ICS competition.
38. **Arrival vs charter trap:** Dutch ships reached India before permanent English factories, but EIC charter (**1600**) precedes VOC charter (**1602**). **Levant Company (1592)** was a **land-route** charter — not the sea East India Company.
39. **Dutch Coinage in India:** The Dutch East India Company minted **gold Pagodas** (पैगोडा सिक्के) bearing the image of Lord Venkateshwara at their mint in **Pulicat**.
40. **Battle of St. Thome (Adyar, 1746):** During the First Carnatic War, a small disciplined French force of ~1,000 troops under Captain **Paradis** routed the 10,000-strong army of Nawab **Anwar-ud-din** (led by Mahfuz Khan), exposing the weakness of Indian feudal armies against European drill and artillery.
41. **Child’s War (1688–1690):** Sir **John Child**, Governor of Bombay, blockaded Mughal ports and seized Haj pilgrim ships; Mughal Emperor **Aurangzeb** retaliated by capturing Bombay and Fort St. George, forcing Child to surrender and pay a heavy indemnity of ₹1.5 lakh.
42. **Battle of Ambur (August 1749):** The tripartite alliance of **Dupleix, Muzaffar Jang, and Chanda Sahib** defeated and killed Nawab **Anwar-ud-din** of Carnatic, placing Chanda Sahib on the Carnatic throne.
43. **Siege of Arcot (1751):** **Robert Clive** (then an EIC clerk-turned-soldier) proposed a daring counter-attack on Arcot with just 210 soldiers (80 Europeans, 130 sepoys) to relieve Trichinopoly, successfully holding it for 53 days against 4,000 besiegers.
44. **François Martin’s Role:** François Martin founded **Pondicherry in 1674** after obtaining the village of Valikandapuram from Sher Khan Lodi (Bijapur governor) and built Fort Louis, earning the title "Father of French India".
45. **Dutch Exports and Commodities:** Dutch VOC exported **cotton textiles from the Coromandel coast**, **raw silk and textiles from Bengal**, **saltpetre and opium from Bihar**, and **indigo from Gujarat and Yamuna Valley**, transforming Indian textiles into international currency.
46. **First Factory Locations (Match Master):**
    - Portuguese: **Calicut (1500)** / Cochin (1501)
    - Dutch: **Masulipatnam (1605)**
    - English: **Masulipatnam (1611)** / Surat (1613 permanent)
    - Danish: **Tranquebar (1620)**
    - French: **Surat (1668)** / Pondicherry (1674).
47. **Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748):** Ended Austrian Succession War in Europe and First Carnatic War in India. Result: **Madras was restored to the English** in exchange for the French regaining **Louisbourg** (Cape Breton Island in North America).
48. **Count de Lally’s Blunders:** Lally arrived in 1758, recalled Bussy from Hyderabad (collapsing French influence at the Nizam's court), alienated local sepoys and Indian allies, besieged Madras unsuccessfully, and suffered total defeat at **Wandiwash (1760)**.
49. **Danish Settlement Sale (1845):** The Danish East India Company was never militarily ambitious and sold all its Indian possessions (**Tranquebar, Serampore**) to the British East India Company in **1845** for ₹12.5 lakh.
50. **Operation Vijay (1961):** The Portuguese rule of 451 years in India ended on **19 December 1961** when the Indian Armed Forces liberated **Goa, Daman, and Diu**, making the Portuguese both the **first Europeans to enter India (1498)** and the **last to leave (1961)**.

   - **Order:** **P**ortuguese (1498) → **D**utch (1605) → **E**nglish (1608) → **D**anes (1620) → **F**rench (1668).

   - **Mnemonic:** **A - P - P**
   - 1st War: **A**ix-la-Chapelle (1748)
   - 2nd War: **P**ondicherry (1754)
   - 3rd War: **P**aris (1763)
3. **Portuguese Big Three Governors:**
   - **A - A - C**
   - **Almeida (1505)**: Blue Water Policy (Sea control, not inland)
   - **Albuquerque (1509)**: Real founder, took Goa in 1510, married locals
   - **da Cunha (1530)**: Capital shift from Cochin to Goa; Diu/Bassein

   - **Calcutta** (English) → **Serampore** (Danish) → **Chandernagore** (French) → **Chinsurah** (Dutch) → **Bandel / Hooghly** (Portuguese).
