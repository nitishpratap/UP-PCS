## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Pre-Congress Political Organizations Chronology (Master Sequence):**
   - **Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha (1836):** Formed by associates of Raja Ram Mohan Roy; first organized political forum in India.
   - **Landholders' Society / Zamindari Association (1838):** Founded in Calcutta by **Dwarkanath Tagore**, Radhakant Deb, and Prasanna Kumar Tagore; first political organization in India with regular constitutional methods.
   - **Bengal British India Society (1843):** Founded by George Thompson to collect and disseminate information on the true condition of Indian subjects.
   - **British Indian Association (1851):** Formed in Calcutta by merging Landholders' Society and Bengal British India Society; **Radhakant Deb** (President) and **Debendranath Tagore** (Secretary); petitioned Parliament for Charter Act 1853.
   - **East India Association (1866):** Founded in **London** by **Dadabhai Naoroji** to advocate Indian interests before the British public and Parliament.
   - **National Indian Association (1870):** Founded in London by Mary Carpenter.
   - **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870):** Founded by **M.G. Ranade**, **G.V. Joshi ("Kaka")**, and S.H. Chiplunkar; acted as a mediator between government and ryots; petitioned British Parliament in 1875 for direct representation.
   - **Indian Society (1872):** Founded in London by **Ananda Mohan Bose**.
   - **Indian League (1875):** Founded in Calcutta by **Sisir Kumar Ghosh** (editor of *Amrita Bazar Patrika*).
   - **Indian Association (26 July 1876):** Founded in Calcutta by **Surendranath Banerjee** and **Ananda Mohan Bose** at Albert Hall; first national-scale political organization; led nationwide agitation against lowering the ICS age limit from 21 to 19 (1877–78) and against the Vernacular Press Act.
   - **Madras Mahajan Sabha (1884):** Founded by **M. Veeraraghavachariar**, **G. Subramania Iyer**, and **P. Anandacharlu**.
   - **Bombay Presidency Association (1885):** Founded by the "Bombay Triumvirate" (**Pherozeshah Mehta, K.T. Telang, and Badruddin Tyabji**).
2. **Indian National Conference (1883 & 1885):**
   - Organized by **Surendranath Banerjee** and the Indian Association in Calcutta as an all-India political conference ("precursor / dress rehearsal of the INC"); its second session in Calcutta in Dec 1885 prevented Banerjee from attending the first INC session in Bombay.
3. **Establishment of Indian National Congress (28 December 1885):**
   - Founded by retired British civil servant **Allan Octavian Hume (A.O. Hume)**.
   - Venue: **Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay** (shifted from Pune due to a sudden outbreak of cholera).
   - First President: **Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee)**, prominent Bengali barrister.
   - Total Delegates: Exactly **72 delegates** attended (38 Bombay, 21 Madras, 3 Bengal, 7 NWP/Oudh, 3 Punjab).
   - General Secretary: **A.O. Hume** served as General Secretary for the first 22 years (1885–1906).
   - Viceroy at foundation: **Lord Dufferin (1884–1888)**.
4. **Safety Valve Theory (सेफ्टी वाल्व सिद्धांत):**
   - Advanced by Hume’s biographer **Sir William Wedderburn (1913)**, claiming Hume saw 7 volumes of secret intelligence reports in Simla warning of an imminent bloody sepoy-peasant uprising like 1857.
   - Adopted by Extremist leader **Lala Lajpat Rai** (*Young India*, 1916): Called INC a *"product of Lord Dufferin’s brain"* created to defuse revolutionary discontent.
   - Marxist historian **R. Palme Dutt** (*India Today*): Viewed INC as a bourgeois conspiracy to forestall mass revolution.
   - Counter-thesis ("Lightning Conductor" theory of Gokhale): Early Indian nationalists used Hume as a protective lightning conductor to shield their fledgling organization from immediate British suppression.
5. **British Official Ridicule of Congress:**
   - **Lord Dufferin (1888):** Denounced Congress as representing only a **"microscopic minority"** of the Indian people.
   - **Lord Curzon (1900):** Wrote to Secretary of State George Hamilton: *"The Congress is tottering to its fall, and one of my great ambitions while in India is to assist it to a peaceful demise."*
   - **Lord Elgin II (1898):** Declared: *"India was conquered by the sword and by the sword it shall be held."*
   - **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay:** Criticized: *"Congressmen are beggars, and their meetings are beggars' shows."*
   - **Aurobindo Ghosh:** Stated: *"The Congress is a dying organisation... dying of consumption."*
6. **Key Milestones in Congress Presidentship:**
   - First Session (1885, Bombay): **W.C. Bonnerjee** (72 delegates).
   - Second Session (1886, Calcutta): **Dadabhai Naoroji** (first Parsi President; 436 delegates).
   - Third Session (1887, Madras): **Badruddin Tyabji** (first **Muslim** President; slogan: *"Become Congressmen"*).
   - Fourth Session (1888, Allahabad): **George Yule** (first **British / Foreign** President; venue secured at Darbhanga Castle despite government obstruction).
   - Fifth Session (1889, Bombay): **Sir William Wedderburn** (first British MP to preside; Kadambini Ganguly attended).
   - Sixth Session (1890, Calcutta): **Pherozeshah Mehta** (Kadambini Ganguly, first woman graduate of Calcutta University, addressed the session).
7. **National Song & Anthem in Congress Sessions:**
   - ***Vande Mataram* (Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay):** First sung at the **Calcutta Session of 1896** (presided over by **Rahimtulla M. Sayani**; also the session where Drain Theory was officially endorsed).
   - ***Jana Gana Mana* (Rabindranath Tagore):** First sung on **27 December 1911** at the **Calcutta Session** (presided over by **Pandit Bishan Narayan Dar**).
8. **Swaraj Declared as Goal (Calcutta, 1906):**
   - Presided over by the "Grand Old Man of India", **Dadabhai Naoroji** (his third presidency: 1886, 1893, 1906).
   - Naoroji used the word **"Swaraj" (Self-Government like the UK colonies)** for the first time from the Congress presidential chair.
   - Passed four historic Swadeshi resolutions: **Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, and National Education**.
9. **Surat Split (December 1907):**
   - Presided over by **Dr. Rashbehari Ghosh** (venue shifted from Nagpur to Surat by Moderates).
   - Split erupted over ideological differences and leadership: Extremists led by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, and Lala Lajpat Rai (Lal-Bal-Pal)** wanted an all-India boycott of all British institutions; Moderates under **Pherozeshah Mehta and G.K. Gokhale** wanted to restrict boycott to Bengal and British goods only.
   - Chaotic scene on 27 Dec 1907: Shoes hurled at the dais; session collapsed; Moderates amended the constitution to exclude Extremists from Congress (until 1916).
10. **Lucknow Session (December 1916) — The Historic Reunion & Pact:**
    - Presided over by **Ambica Charan Majumdar (A.C. Majumdar)**.
    - Achieved two historic reconciliations:
      1. **Moderate–Extremist Reunion:** Tilak and Annie Besant re-entered Congress after 9 years.
      2. **Lucknow Pact (Congress–League Pact):** Joint constitutional demand framed with the All India Muslim League (brokered by Tilak and Muhammad Ali Jinnah).
      3. Historic concession: Congress officially conceded **separate communal electorates for Muslims** in provincial legislatures (later criticized as a fatal appeasement leading to partition).
11. **Women Presidents of Congress (Master Trio):**
    - **1917 (Calcutta):** **Annie Besant** — First **Woman** President of INC.
    - **1925 (Kanpur):** **Sarojini Naidu** — First **Indian Woman** President of INC.
    - **1933 (Calcutta):** **Nellie Sengupta** — Third Woman President.
12. **Mahatma Gandhi's Only Presidentship (Belgaum, 1924):**
    - Gandhi presided over the **39th Session at Belgaum (Karnataka) in December 1924** (the only Congress session presided over by Gandhi).
13. **Purna Swaraj Declaration (Lahore, 1929):**
    - Presided over by **Jawaharlal Nehru** (nominated by Gandhi over Patel).
    - Passed the historic **Purna Swaraj Resolution (Complete Independence)**, declaring British rule a crime against humanity.
    - Authorized the launch of the **Civil Disobedience Movement**.
    - On midnight of **31 December 1929**, Nehru unfurled the newly adopted tricolor flag on the banks of the River Ravi.
    - Proclaimed **26 January 1930** as the first **Independence Day** (*Purna Swaraj Divas*).
14. **Karachi Session (March 1931):**
    - Presided over by **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (held 6 days after Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru were hanged on 23 March 1931).
    - Endorsed the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact)**.
    - Adopted two historic resolutions drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru:
      1. **Resolution on Fundamental Rights.**
      2. **Resolution on the National Economic Programme** (first socialist-oriented socio-economic charter of the Congress).
15. **Faizpur Session (1937) — First Rural Session:**
    - Held in **December 1937 at Faizpur (Maharashtra)**, presided over by **Jawaharlal Nehru**; first session of Congress held in a village, adopting an agrarian programme.
16. **Subhas Chandra Bose’s Presidencies (Haripura 1938 & Tripuri 1939):**
    - **Haripura (Gujarat, 1938):** Bose unanimously elected President; set up the **National Planning Committee** with **Jawaharlal Nehru as Chairman**.
    - **Tripuri (Jabalpur, MP, 1939):** Bose contested against Gandhi’s chosen candidate **Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya** and won by 1,580 to 1,377 votes. Gandhi declared: *"Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat"*; Working Committee resigned; Govind Ballabh Pant moved a resolution requesting Bose to form a committee in accordance with Gandhi's wishes; Bose resigned in April 1939; **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** elected President; Bose founded the **Forward Bloc (3 May 1939)**.
17. **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad’s Record Tenures:**
    - Youngest President of INC: Elected at age **35** at the **Delhi Special Session in September 1923**.
    - Longest-serving continuous pre-independence President: Presided at **Ramgarh (1940)** and continued uninterrupted through WWII from **1940 to 1946** (no annual sessions held due to Quit India crackdowns).
18. **Bal Gangadhar Tilak’s Paradox:**
    - Despite being the towering leader of the national movement and Extremist faction, **Bal Gangadhar Tilak never presided over any INC session**! (In 1918, he was chosen President for Delhi, but had to leave for London to pursue a libel suit against Sir Valentine Chirol).
19. **Gopal Krishna Gokhale (1866–1915):**
    - Moderate leader; presided over the **Banaras Session in 1905** (criticized Curzon's Partition of Bengal, endorsed Swadeshi); founded **Servants of India Society (1905)**; political guru of Mahatma Gandhi and M.A. Jinnah.
20. **Valentine Chirol’s Moniker:**
    - British journalist Valentine Chirol, in his book *Indian Unrest* (1910), called **Bal Gangadhar Tilak the "Father of Indian Unrest"** (*Bhartiya Ashanti ke Janak*).
21. **Moderates (1885–1905) vs Extremists (1905–1919):**
    - **Moderates:** Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, D.E. Wacha, W.C. Bonnerjee, S.N. Banerjee, G.K. Gokhale. Belief in British sense of justice and providential connection; technique: **3Ps — Petition, Prayer, Protest**; constitutional agitation within legal boundaries; demanded legislative reform, Indianization of services, tariff protection.
    - **Extremists:** Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai (Lal-Bal-Pal), Aurobindo Ghosh, Ashwini Kumar Dutt. Discarded faith in British benevolence; demanded **Swaraj as a birthright**; technique: **Passive Resistance, Boycott of British goods, Swadeshi, National Education**.
22. **British Committee of the INC (1889, London):**
    - Established in London in **1889** under the chairmanship of **Sir William Wedderburn** to lobby British MPs and public; published the influential weekly journal ***India*** from 1890 to 1921.
23. **Congress Sessions in Uttar Pradesh (9 Landmark Sessions):**
    1. **1888 (Allahabad):** George Yule (First English President).
    2. **1892 (Allahabad):** W.C. Bonnerjee (second term).
    3. **1899 (Lucknow):** Romesh Chunder Dutt (R.C. Dutt).
    4. **1905 (Banaras):** Gopal Krishna Gokhale (Partition condemned).
    5. **1910 (Allahabad):** Sir William Wedderburn.
    6. **1916 (Lucknow):** Ambica Charan Majumdar (Reunion & Lucknow Pact).
    7. **1925 (Kanpur):** Sarojini Naidu (First Indian woman President).
    8. **1936 (Lucknow):** Jawaharlal Nehru (Socialist agenda; AIKS formed).
    9. **1946 (Meerut):** Acharya J.B. Kripalani (President during Independence, Aug 1947).
24. **President at Indian Independence (15 August 1947):**
    - **Acharya J.B. Kripalani** was the President of the Indian National Congress at the time of Indian independence (elected at Meerut, Nov 1946; resigned Nov 1947, succeeded by Dr. Rajendra Prasad).
25. **Dadabhai Naoroji’s Historic Triple Presidencies:**
    - Presided thrice: **1886 (Calcutta)**, **1893 (Lahore)**, and **1906 (Calcutta)**. First Indian elected to the British House of Commons (Liberal MP from Central Finsbury, 1892–1895).
26. **Jawaharlal Nehru’s Presidencies:**
    - Presided 3 times before independence: **1929 (Lahore)**, **1936 (Lucknow)**, and **1937 (Faizpur)** (also 1951, 1952, 1953, 1954 post-independence).
27. **Pattabhi Sitaramayya's Milestone Work:**
    - Official historian of the Congress; authored the authoritative two-volume work ***History of the Indian National Congress*** (1935); became President of the Jaipur Session in 1948.
28. **Special Sessions of Congress:**
    - **1918 (Bombay Special):** Syed Hasan Imam (condemned Montford report).
    - **1920 (Calcutta Special):** Lala Lajpat Rai (Non-Cooperation resolution passed).
    - **1923 (Delhi Special):** Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (permitted Swarajists to contest elections).
29. **Nagpur Session (December 1920) — The Organizational Revolution:**
    - Presided over by **C. Vijayaraghavachariar**.
    - Radically overhauled Congress Constitution (drafted by Gandhi):
      - Goal changed from "self-government by constitutional means" to attainment of **Swaraj by all peaceful and legitimate means**.
      - Created a 15-member **Congress Working Committee (CWC)** to direct daily work.
      - Reorganized Provincial Congress Committees (PCCs) on a **linguistic basis**.
      - Slashed annual membership fee to **4 annas (25 paise)**, transforming Congress into a mass national party.
30. **Amrita Bazar Patrika (1868):**
    - Founded by brothers **Sisir Kumar Ghosh** and **Motilal Ghosh** in Jessore (shifted to Calcutta); played a seminal role in generating early nationalist consciousness; switched to English overnight in March 1878 to evade Lytton’s Vernacular Press Act.
31. **Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883) & White Mutiny:**
    - Introduced by Law Member **Sir Courtenay Ilbert** under Lord Ripon; sought to remove the racial disqualification barring Indian district magistrates from trying European British subjects.
    - Anglo-Indian community organized violent protests, formed the European and Anglo-Indian Defence Association, and planned to kidnap Viceroy Ripon; British government surrendered and amended the bill to allow Europeans to claim a jury with at least half European members; opened Indian eyes to organized racial arrogance, directly inspiring the foundation of the INC in 1885.
32. **A.O. Hume’s Historic Circular (1 March 1883):**
    - Hume addressed Calcutta University graduates: *"Whether in the individual or the nation, all mental and moral development implies the presence of a soul... You, the picked men, the most highly educated of the nation, must also be the most large-hearted and self-sacrificing... If you cannot make a stand for your country, then are our hopes unfounded and India’s future is dark."*
33. **First Session Attendance Breakdown (Bombay, 1885):**
    - Major personalities attending: Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Badruddin Tyabji, K.T. Telang, Dinshaw Wacha, Behramji Malabari, N.G. Chandavarkar, G. Subramania Iyer, P. Anandacharlu, S. Subramania Iyer.
34. **First Indian Woman Graduate to Address Congress:**
    - **Kadambini Ganguly** (first female medical graduate in British India) addressed the **1890 Calcutta Session** of INC; hailed by Tilak and modern historians as the dawn of Indian womanhood's participation in national politics.
35. **Tilak’s Cultural Nationalism (1893 & 1895):**
    - Transformed private religious celebrations into massive public patriotic festivals:
      - **Ganapati Festival (1893)** to foster Hindu solidarity and provide a platform for patriotic speeches and songs.
      - **Shivaji Festival (1895)** at Raigad Fort to inspire self-respect, valor, and anti-imperialist defiance.
36. **Drain of Wealth Official INC Adoption:**
    - INC formally adopted the **Drain of Wealth Theory** at its **Calcutta Session in 1896** (presided over by Rahimtulla Sayani), citing Dadabhai Naoroji's mathematical proof that unrequited wealth was draining from India.
37. **Banaras Session (1905) & Swadeshi:**
    - Presided over by **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**; officially condemned Lord Curzon’s reactionary reactionary administration and Partition of Bengal; supported the Swadeshi and Boycott movement for Bengal.
38. **Bipin Chandra Pal’s Philosophy:**
    - Member of the Lal-Bal-Pal triumvirate; known as the "Father of Revolutionary Thought in India"; preached through the English daily *Bande Mataram* that freedom could not be achieved through petitioning a foreign master.
39. **Aurobindo Ghosh’s "Passive Resistance" Articles (1907):**
    - Published seminal series of articles *"The Doctrine of Passive Resistance"* in *Bande Mataram* (April 1907); formulated comprehensive blueprint for non-violent non-cooperation: boycott of British goods, boycott of government schools, boycott of British law courts, and defiance of unjust laws, anticipating Gandhian non-cooperation by 13 years!
40. **Civil Services Age Agitation (1877–1878):**
    - Lord Lytton reduced the maximum age for appearing in the ICS examination in London from 21 to **19 years** in 1876; Surendranath Banerjee toured India (Agra, Lahore, Amritsar, Meerut, Bombay, Madras) organizing protest meetings, marking the **first all-India political agitation on an issue**.
41. **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha’s Famine Work (1876–77):**
    - Sent peasant survey teams to famine-struck Maharashtra villages; published peasant grievances; launched a peaceful "no-tax" agitation advising ryots not to pay revenue if crops failed, earning British official wrath.
42. **Dadabhai Naoroji’s "Un-British Rule" Concept:**
    - Distinguished between "British" rule (which stood for democratic principles, liberty, and free press in Britain) and "Un-British" rule (despotic, extractive, impoverishing colonial exploitation practiced in India).
43. **A.C. Majumdar’s Famous Lucknow Exclamation (1916):**
    - Presiding over the historic reunion of Moderates and Extremists, Majumdar declared: *"After 10 years of painful separation and wandering through the wilderness, the prodigal sons have returned to their mother's lap!"*
44. **Annie Besant’s Home Rule Push in Congress:**
    - Elected Congress President at **Calcutta (1917)** immediately following her internment by the Madras Government; her presidential address emphasized self-government (*Swaraj*) for India as an integral partner in the British Commonwealth.
45. **Sarojini Naidu at Kanpur (1925):**
    - Celebrated poetess ("Nightingale of India"); in her presidential address at Kanpur (1925), she declared: *"In the battle for liberty, fear is one unforgivable treachery and despair, the one unforgivable sin."*
46. **National Planning Committee (1938):**
    - Constituted at the **Haripura Session (1938)** by President Subhas Chandra Bose; chaired by **Jawaharlal Nehru**; laid down the foundation for state-led planned industrialization in post-independence India.
47. **Forward Bloc Formation (1939):**
    - Formed by **Subhas Chandra Bose** on **3 May 1939** at Makur (Unnao, UP) as a radical leftist bloc within the Indian National Congress after resigning the presidency at Tripuri.
48. **Meerut Session on the Eve of Freedom (1946):**
    - Held in **November 1946 at Meerut (UP)**, presided over by **Acharya J.B. Kripalani**; ratified the Congress participation in the Interim Government and Constituent Assembly.

50. **Historical Arc of Indian Nationalism:** From the defensive petitions of elite landlord associations (1830s–1850s) to middle-class constitutional lobbying (1870s–1880s), transitioning through Extremist boycott and Swadeshi mass mobilization (1905–1908), culminating in the all-encompassing Gandhian mass movements that dismantled the British Empire.
