## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it"** (स्वराज मेरा जन्मसिद्ध अधिकार है और मैं इसे लेकर रहूँगा) was declared by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** at the Lucknow Congress (1916). **"Karo ya Maro" ("Do or Die")** was given by **Mahatma Gandhi** at Gowalia Tank, Bombay on **8 August 1942** (Quit India Movement).
2. **"Inquilab Zindabad" (इंकलाब जिंदाबाद)** was coined in 1921 by Urdu poet and freedom fighter **Maulana Hasrat Mohani** and was later popularized nationwide by **Bhagat Singh** and his comrades of the HSRA.
3. **"Give me blood and I will give you freedom"** (*"तुम मुझे खून दो, मैं तुम्हें आजादी दूंगा"*), **"Dilli Chalo"** (दिल्ली चलो), and **"Jai Hind"** (जय हिंद) were given by **Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose** to the Azad Hind Fauj.
4. ***Vande Mataram*** (वंदे मातरम्), composed in Sanskrit by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** in his 1882 novel ***Anandamath***, was adopted as the **National Song**; it was first sung at the **Calcutta Congress Session of 1896** (presided by Rahimtullah M. Sayani).
5. ***Jana Gana Mana*** (जन गण मन), composed by **Rabindranath Tagore** in Bengali (*Bharoto Bhagyo Bidhata*), was adopted as the **National Anthem**; it was first sung at the **Calcutta Congress Session of 1911** (presided by Bishan Narayan Dar).
6. **"Go Back to the Vedas"** (*"वेदों की ओर लौटो"*) was the clarion call of **Swami Dayanand Saraswati** (founder of Arya Samaj, 1875). **"One Caste, One Religion, One God for Man"** (*"एक जाति, एक धर्म, एक ईश्वर"*) was proclaimed by **Sri Narayana Guru** in Kerala.
7. **"Educate, Agitate, Organise"** (*"शिक्षित बनो, आंदोलन करो, संगठित रहो"*) was the foundational motto given by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** to the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924).
8. The revolutionary patriotic poem **"Sarfaroshi ki tamanna ab hamare dil mein hai"** was written in 1921 by **Bismil Azimabadi** of Patna, and was immortalized as a battle anthem by martyr **Pandit Ram Prasad Bismil** during the Kakori Conspiracy trial.
9. **"Satyameva Jayate"** (सत्यमेव जयते — Truth alone triumphs) is an aphorism from the **Mundaka Upanishad**, adopted as India's national motto on **26 January 1950**; it was popularized during the freedom struggle by **Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya**.
10. **"Aram Haram Hai"** (*"आराम हराम है"*) was popularized by **Jawaharlal Nehru** to inspire post-independence national reconstruction. **"Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan"** (*"जय जवान, जय किसान"*) was coined by **Lal Bahadur Shastri** during the 1965 Indo-Pak War (to which Atal Bihari Vajpayee added *"Jai Vigyan"* in 1998).
11. Bal Gangadhar Tilak published two influential newspapers from Pune: ***Kesari*** (केसरी) in **Marathi** and ***The Mahratta*** in **English**; Tilak was twice imprisoned for sedition based on articles published in *Kesari*.
12. Landmark UP Press Match-List:
    - ***Swadesh*** (स्वदेश) — Published from **Gorakhpur** (edited by Dashrath Prasad Dwivedi).
    - ***Bharat Bandhu*** (भारत बंधु) — Published from **Hathras** (edited by Babu Tota Ram).
    - ***Satyawadi*** (सत्यवादी) — Published from **Agra**.
    - ***Shakti*** (शक्ति) — Published from **Almora** (edited by Badri Datt Pandey).
    - ***Abhyudaya***, ***Leader*** & ***Maryada*** — Published from **Allahabad** (founded by Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya).
    - ***Pratap*** (प्रताप) — Published from **Kanpur** (edited by Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi, which championed the Bijolia peasant movement).
13. Mahatma Gandhi edited four landmark journals: ***Indian Opinion*** (1903–1914, in South Africa in English, Gujarati, Hindi, Tamil), ***Young India*** (1919–1931, in English), ***Navajivan*** (1919–1931, in Gujarati), and ***Harijan*** (1933–1948, in English, Hindi, Gujarati).
14. The **first newspaper published in India** was the ***Bengal Gazette*** (or *Calcutta General Advertiser*), founded on **29 January 1780** at Calcutta by Irishman **James Augustus Hicky** (popularly called *Hicky's Gazette*).
15. The **first Hindi newspaper in India** was ***Udant Martand*** (उदन्त मार्तण्ड — "The Rising Sun"), published as a weekly from Calcutta on **30 May 1826** by **Pandit Jugal Kishore Shukla** (a native of Kanpur, UP); 30 May is observed annually as **Hindi Journalism Day**.
16. Raja Ram Mohan Roy published ***Sambad Kaumudi*** (1821) in Bengali (advocating abolition of Sati) and ***Mirat-ul-Akhbar*** (1822) in Persian (the first Persian newspaper in India).
17. Annie Besant published two celebrated papers from Madras in 1914 to propagate Home Rule: ***New India*** (a daily) and ***The Commonweal*** (a weekly).
18. Sisir Kumar Ghosh and Motilal Ghosh founded the ***Amrita Bazar Patrika*** (1868) in Jessore/Calcutta; originally a Bengali weekly, it **converted overnight into an English daily in 1878** to evade Lord Lytton's repressive Vernacular Press Act.
19. Indian National Congress (INC) Landmark "Firsts":
    - **First Session (1885, Bombay)**: Presided by **W.C. Bonnerjee** (72 delegates attended at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College).
    - **First Muslim President**: **Badruddin Tyabji** at the Madras Session (**1887**).
    - **First British/Foreign President**: **George Yule** at the Allahabad Session (**1888**).
    - **First Woman President**: **Annie Besant** at the Calcutta Session (**1917**).
    - **First Indian Woman President**: **Sarojini Naidu** at the Kanpur Session (**1925**).
    - **First Village/Rural Session**: **Faizpur Session (1937, Maharashtra)** presided by **Jawaharlal Nehru**.
20. **Mahatma Gandhi presided over only ONE Congress session** in his entire life: the **39th Belgaum Session (Karnataka) in December 1924**.
21. **Bal Gangadhar Tilak NEVER presided over any Congress session**, despite being one of the topmost national leaders and the father of Indian unrest.
22. The longest-serving Congress President in pre-independence India was **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**, who was elected at **Ramgarh (1940)** and held office continuously for six critical years (1940–1946) due to World War II and the suspension of elections.
23. Nine landmark INC sessions were held in Uttar Pradesh:
    - **Allahabad (3 sessions)**: 1888 (George Yule), 1892 (W.C. Bonnerjee), 1910 (William Wedderburn).
    - **Lucknow (3 sessions)**: 1899 (Romesh Chunder Dutt), 1916 (Ambica Charan Mazumdar — historic Lucknow Pact), 1936 (Jawaharlal Nehru — socialist goal).
    - **Banaras (1905)**: Gopal Krishna Gokhale (Swadeshi resolution against Bengal Partition).
    - **Kanpur (1925)**: Sarojini Naidu (first Indian woman INC President).
    - **Meerut (1946)**: Acharya J.B. Kripalani (President during transfer of power and Independence).
24. At the **Tripuri Congress (1939)**, Subhas Chandra Bose defeated Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya (Gandhi's candidate) by 203 votes; following post-election disputes over the Working Committee, Bose resigned and **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** took over as President.
25. 1857 Leadership UP Match-List:
    - **Lucknow**: **Begum Hazrat Mahal** (proclaimed her minor son Birjis Qadr as Nawab; suppressed by Colin Campbell).
    - **Kanpur**: **Nana Saheb** (Dhondu Pant) and **Tatya Tope** (Ramchandra Pandurang); suppressed by Colin Campbell.
    - **Jhansi**: **Rani Lakshmibai** (assisted by Dalit warrior **Jhalkari Bai**); suppressed by Sir Hugh Rose.
    - **Allahabad & Banaras**: **Maulvi Liyaqat Ali**; suppressed by Colonel James Neill.
    - **Faizabad**: **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah** (proclaimed jihad against the British; 50,000 reward); suppressed by Campbell.
    - **Bareilly (Rohilkhand)**: **Khan Bahadur Khan**; suppressed by Colin Campbell.
    - **Jagdishpur / Arrah (Bihar)**: **Kunwar Singh** (80-year-old landlord) and his brother Amar Singh; suppressed by William Taylor and Vincent Eyre.
26. Landmark UP Freedom Struggle Sites & Dates:
    - **Chauri Chaura**: 4/5 February 1922 (Gorakhpur) — 22 policemen killed, causing Gandhi to withdraw NCM on 12 Feb 1922.
    - **Kakori Train Action**: **9 August 1925** (near Lucknow) — HRA members looted Government Treasury from 8-Down train; Bismil, Ashfaqulla, Roshan Singh, and Rajendra Lahiri were hanged in December 1927.
    - **Alfred Park (now Chandrashekhar Azad Park), Allahabad**: **27 February 1931** — Chandrashekhar Azad shot himself to avoid capture by police officer J.R.H. Nott-Bower.
27. Chronological Sequence of Landmark Anglo-Indian Treaties:
    1. **Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757)**: Robert Clive & Siraj-ud-Daulah.
    2. **Treaty of Allahabad (Aug 1765)**: Clive with Shah Alam II and Shuja-ud-Daulah (granted Bengal Diwani).
    3. **Treaty of Madras (1769)**: Ended 1st Anglo-Mysore War (defensive alliance with Haidar Ali).
    4. **Treaty of Purandar (1776)**: Warren Hastings with Nana Fadnavis.
    5. **Treaty of Salbai (May 1782)**: Ended 1st Anglo-Maratha War; 20-year mutual peace.
    6. **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)**: Ended 2nd Anglo-Mysore War with Tipu Sultan.
    7. **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**: Ended 3rd Anglo-Mysore War; Tipu ceded half his kingdom.
    8. **Treaty of Bassein (1802)**: Peshwa Baji Rao II accepted Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance.
    9. **Treaty of Sugauli (1816)**: Ended Anglo-Nepalese (Gurkha) War; British gained Kumaon, Garhwal, and Shimla.
    10. **Treaty of Yandabo (1826)**: Ended 1st Anglo-Burmese War; British acquired Assam, Manipur, and Arakan.
28. Landmark British Famine Commissions Chronology:
    - **Campbell Commission (1866)**: Appointed after Orissa Famine under Sir John Lawrence; first commission to recommend government responsibility for famine relief.
    - **Sir Richard Strachey Commission (1880)**: Appointed by Lord Lytton; laid down the foundational **Famine Code (1883)**.
    - **Sir James Lyall Commission (1897)**: Appointed by Lord Elgin II; recommended development of irrigation facilities.
    - **Sir Antony MacDonnell Commission (1900)**: Appointed by Lord Curzon; recommended *"moral strategy"* and appointment of a Famine Commissioner.
29. British Educational Commissions Chronology:
    - **Charles Wood's Despatch (1854)**: Magna Carta of English Education in India; recommended vernacular primary schools, Anglo-vernacular high schools, and universities at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras.
    - **Hunter Commission (1882)**: Headed by W.W. Hunter under Lord Ripon; evaluated progress since 1854; focused strictly on primary and secondary education.
    - **Raleigh Commission / Indian Universities Act (1902/1904)**: Headed by Sir Thomas Raleigh under Lord Curzon; focused on tightening government control over higher university education.
    - **Sadler Commission (1917)**: Headed by Dr. Michael Sadler under Lord Chelmsford; investigated Calcutta University problems; recommended 12-year school course (intermediate stage) and secondary education boards.
    - **Hartog Committee (1929)**: Appointed under Simon Commission framework; focused on rural education and highlighted massive "wastage and stagnation" at primary levels.
    - **Sargent Plan (1944)**: Post-war educational development memorandum; envisioned universal, free, and compulsory elementary education for ages 6–14 within 40 years.
30. Financial and Currency Inquiry Committees:
    - **Mansfield Commission (1886)**: Examined silver currency depreciation.
    - **Herschell Committee (1893)**: Recommended closing mints to free coinage of silver.
    - **Fowler Committee (1898)**: Recommended making the British sovereign legal tender in India at 1s 4d.
    - **Babington Smith Committee (1919)**: Recommended fixing the rupee at 2s gold.
    - **Hilton Young Commission (Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance, 1926)**: Recommended establishing the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**, which was enacted by the RBI Act 1934 and began operations on **1 April 1935**.
31. British Police & Military Commissions:
    - **Peel Commission (1859)**: Formed after the 1857 Revolt to reorganize the Indian Army; recommended maintaining a 1:2 ratio of British to Indian soldiers in Bengal and dividing regiments strictly along caste, community, and regional lines (*"Divide and Rule"*).
    - **Fraser Commission (1902–03)**: Appointed by Lord Curzon under Sir Andrew Fraser; created the **Criminal Investigation Department (CID)** at provincial levels and Central Intelligence Bureau.
    - **Lee Commission (Royal Commission on Superior Civil Services, 1923–24)**: Recommended creating a statutory **Public Service Commission** (established in 1926 under Sir Ross Barker) and accelerating 50:50 Indianization of ICS cadres within 15 years.
32. Landmark British Administrative Acts:
    - **Regulating Act of 1773**: First step of British parliamentary control; Governor of Bengal became Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings); established **Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)** with Sir Elijah Impey as first Chief Justice.
    - **Pitt's India Act of 1784**: Established the system of **Dual Government (दोहरा नियंत्रण)**: Court of Directors managed commercial affairs, while a 6-member **Board of Control** managed political and military affairs.
    - **Charter Act of 1813**: Abolished EIC's trade monopoly in India, opening Indian trade to all British merchants except **trade in tea** and **trade with China**; allocated **Rs 1 lakh annually** for the promotion of Indian literature and sciences; permitted Christian missionaries to preach in India.
    - **Charter Act of 1833**: Completely ended EIC's commercial monopoly (including tea and China); Governor-General of Bengal was redesignated as the **Governor-General of India** (**Lord William Bentinck** became 1st GG of India); added a 4th Legal Member (**Lord Macaulay**) to the GG's Council; initiated codification of Indian laws.
    - **Charter Act of 1853**: Last of the Charter Acts; separated executive and legislative functions of the GG's Council (creating a 12-member Indian Legislative Council); introduced **open competitive examinations** for civil service recruitment (Macaulay Committee of 1854).
    - **Government of India Act 1858 (Act for the Better Government of India)**: Abolished EIC rule and transferred sovereignty directly to the British Crown; abolished Board of Control and Court of Directors; created office of the **Secretary of State for India** (assisted by 15-member Council of India); redesignated GG as the **Viceroy of India** (**Lord Canning** became 1st Viceroy).
    - **Indian Councils Act 1861**: Restored legislative powers to Bombay and Madras (decentralization); empowered Viceroy to issue **Ordinances** valid for 6 months; introduced Lord Canning's **Portfolio System**.
    - **Indian Councils Act 1892**: Introduced indirect elections (recommendations) for non-official members of legislative councils; granted members the right to discuss the annual budget and address questions to the executive.
33. 20th Century Constitutional Reforms:
    - **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909)**: Introduced **separate electorates for Muslims (सांप्रदायिक निर्वाचन प्रणाली)**, legalising communal representation (*"sowing the seeds of bitter conflict"*); Satyendra Prasanna (S.P.) Sinha became the first Indian appointed to the Viceroy's Executive Council as Law Member.
    - **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act 1919)**: Introduced **Dyarchy (द्वैध शासन)** in provincial governments (dividing subjects into *Transferred* and *Reserved*); introduced bicameralism at the Centre (Council of State and Legislative Assembly); established Public Service Commission.
    - **Government of India Act 1935**: Provided for an **All-India Federation** (which never materialized); abolished Dyarchy in provinces and introduced **Provincial Autonomy**; introduced Dyarchy at the Centre; established the **Federal Court of India** (inaugurated 1937 in Delhi with Sir Maurice Gwyer as Chief Justice); created the Reserve Bank of India; separated Burma from British India.
34. Revolutionary Secret Organisations & Formations:
    - **Mitra Mela (1899, Nasik)**: Founded by Vinayak Damodar Savarkar and Ganesh Savarkar; converted into **Abhinav Bharat (1904)**.
    - **Anushilan Samiti (1902, Calcutta)**: Founded by Pramathanath Mitra, Jatindranath Banerjee, and Barindra Kumar Ghosh. Another branch was founded at **Dhaka (1905)** by **Pulin Behari Das**.
    - **Bharat Mata Society (1904, Lahore)**: Founded by Sardar Ajit Singh (Bhagat Singh's uncle) and Sufi Amba Prasad; launched the *Pagri Sambhal Jatta* peasant movement.
    - **India House (1905, London)**: Founded by **Shyamji Krishna Varma**; published ***The Indian Sociologist***; provided scholarships to Savarkar, Lala Har Dayal, and Madan Lal Dhingra.
    - **Ghadar Party (1 November 1913, San Francisco, USA)**: Founded by **Lala Har Dayal**, **Sohan Singh Bhakna (first President)**, and Kartar Singh Sarabha; published weekly journal ***Ghadar*** (carrying slogan: *"Angrezi Raj ka Dushman"*).
    - **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)**: Founded in **October 1924 at Kanpur** by Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal (author of *Bandi Jeevan*), and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee; executed the Kakori Action (1925).
    - **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**: Reorganized on **9–10 September 1928 at Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi** under the leadership of **Chandrashekhar Azad**, Bhagat Singh, and Sukhdev, officially adopting socialism as its ideology.
35. Prominent Indian Women Freedom Fighters:
    - **Madam Bhikaji Cama**: Unfurled the **first Indian National Tricolour Flag abroad** at the International Socialist Conference in **Stuttgart, Germany on 22 August 1907**; revered as the *"Mother of Indian Revolution"*.
    - **Sarojini Naidu**: Known as the *"Nightingale of India"* (*Bharat Kokila*); first Indian woman INC President (Kanpur, 1925); led the Dharasana Salt Raid (1930); became the **first woman Governor of an Indian state (United Provinces, 1947–1949)**.
    - **Aruna Asaf Ali**: Known as the *"Heroine of the 1942 Movement"*; hoisted the Tricolour at Gowalia Tank on 9 August 1942; operated underground with Ram Manohar Lohia; first Mayor of Delhi (1958).
    - **Dr. Usha Mehta**: Operated the clandestine **Secret Congress Radio** during the Quit India Movement (1942) from various secret apartments in Bombay until her arrest.
    - **Captain Lakshmi Sehgal (Swaminathan)**: Appointed by Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose as Commander of the **Rani of Jhansi Regiment** (the all-women combat wing of INA) and Minister of Women's Affairs in the Azad Hind Government (1943).
    - **Matangini Hazra**: 73-year-old freedom fighter in Midnapore, Bengal; fondly called *"Gandhi Buri"* (Old Lady Gandhi); shot dead by British police on 29 September 1942 while leading a procession to capture the Tamluk police station, holding the tricolour aloft until her last breath.
    - **Kanaklata Barua**: 17-year-old girl in Assam (belonging to the *Mrityu Bahini*); shot dead by British police on 20 September 1942 while attempting to hoist the Tricolour at Gohpur police station.
    - **Bina Das**: Bengal revolutionary member of Chhatri Sangha; fired point-blank at Bengal Governor Sir Stanley Jackson during the Calcutta University convocation in **February 1932**.
    - **Santi Ghosh & Suniti Choudhury**: Two teenage schoolgirls of Comilla (now Bangladesh) who shot dead British District Magistrate C.G.B. Stevens in **December 1931**.
    - **Kalpana Datta & Pritilata Waddedar**: Prominent members of Surya Sen's Indian Republican Army (Chittagong Armoury Raid, 1930); Pritilata led the armed raid on the Pahartali European Club (1932) and consumed potassium cyanide to avoid capture.
36. Prominent UP Freedom Fighters & Revolutionary Icons:
    - **Pandit Ram Prasad Bismil**: Born in Shahjahanpur (UP); founder of HRA; executed in Gorakhpur Jail on 19 December 1927 for the Kakori Action; authored the autobiography *Kakori Ke Shaheed*.
    - **Ashfaqulla Khan**: Born in Shahjahanpur (UP); first Muslim revolutionary to be hanged for an Indian conspiracy case (hanged in Faizabad Jail on 19 December 1927).
    - **Thakur Roshan Singh**: Hanged in Naini Central Jail (Allahabad) on 19 December 1927 for the Kakori Action.
    - **Rajendra Nath Lahiri**: Hanged in Gonda Jail on 17 December 1927 (two days before the others) for the Kakori Action.
    - **Chandrashekhar Azad**: Born in Bhavra (MP) but operated in Allahabad and Kanpur; Commander-in-Chief of HSRA; attained martyrdom at Alfred Park, Allahabad on 27 February 1931.
    - **Raja Mahendra Pratap**: Freedom fighter from Hathras (UP); established India's first **Provisional Government of Free India in Kabul (Afghanistan) on 1 December 1915** as President, with Maulvi Barkatullah as Prime Minister.
    - **Chittu Pandey**: Popularly titled *"Sher-e-Ballia"*; formed the first parallel government of 1942 at Ballia (UP) and took administrative control of the district.
    - **Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi**: Crusading journalist of Kanpur; editor of ***Pratap***; sacrificed his life while quelling communal riots in Kanpur in March 1931 (Gandhi hailed his death as an ideal martyrdom).
    - **Hasrat Mohani**: Born in Mohan (Unnao, UP); coined *"Inquilab Zindabad"*; moved the first resolution for complete independence (*Purna Swaraj*) at the Ahmedabad Congress Session (1921); refused to sign the final draft of the Indian Constitution because it retained ties with the Commonwealth.

39. British Colonial Forts & Settlements in India:
    - **Fort St. George (1644)**: Located in **Madras (Chennai)**; built by Francis Day; first fortified English commercial outpost in India.
    - **Fort William (1700)**: Located in **Calcutta (Kolkata)**; named after King William III; built under Charles Eyre and Job Charnock.
    - **Fort St. David (1690)**: Located near **Cuddalore (Tamil Nadu)**; acquired by the British from the Marathas.
    - **Fort St. Angelo (1505)**: Located in **Kannur (Kerala)**; built by the first Portuguese Viceroy Francisco de Almeida.
    - **Fort Manuel (1503)**: Located in **Kochi (Cochin)**; first European fortress built in India by Afonso de Albuquerque.
40. British Viceroys & Signatures Policies Summary:
    - **Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856)**: Doctrine of Lapse (Satara 1848, Sambalpur 1849, Jhansi 1853, Nagpur 1854, Awadh annexed on maladministration 1856); First Railway line (Bombay to Thane, 16 April 1853); First Telegraph line (Calcutta to Agra, 1853); Post Office Act 1854; Wood's Despatch 1854.

50. Top Examiner Traps & Golden Formulae:
51. **Santhal Hool / Rebellion (1855–56):** Led by four Murmu brothers: **Sidhu, Kanhu, Chand, and Bhairav** in the **Daman-i-Koh** region (Rajmahal Hills, Bihar/Jharkhand); erupted against extortionate Bengali moneylenders (*Mahajans*) and British revenue officials; British created separate **Santhal Parganas district** under Regulation 37 of 1855.
52. **Munda Ulgulan / Rebellion (1899–1900):** Led by **Birsa Munda** ('Dharti Aaba' / Father of Earth) in Chotanagpur; fought against erosion of traditional collective land tenure (*Khuntkatti* system) by outsiders (*Dikus*); Birsa proclaimed divine vision and divine kingdom; arrested and died of cholera in Ranchi Jail (June 1900); prompted British to enact **Chotanagpur Tenancy Act (1908)**.
53. **Indigo Revolt (Neel Bidroho, 1859–60):** Began at Govindpur village, Nadia district (Bengal) led by **Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas**; peasants refused to grow indigo and boycotted planters; brilliantly depicted by **Dinabandhu Mitra** in his Bengali play **Neel Darpan (1860)** (translated into English by Michael Madhusudan Dutt, published by Rev. James Long who was jailed for it); British set up **Indigo Commission (1860)** under W.S. Seton-Karr which declared cultivation voluntary.
54. **Pabna Peasant Uprising (1873–76):** Formed Agrarian League in Yusufshahi Pargana, Pabna (Bengal) led by **Ishan Chandra Roy, Shambhu Pal, and Khoodi Mollah**; legalistic resistance opposing arbitrary rent hikes; proclaimed: *'We want to be the ryots of Her Majesty the Queen and of Her only'*; led to the enactment of the **Bengal Tenancy Act (1885)**.
55. **Deccan Riots (1875):** Peasant revolt in Pune and Ahmednagar districts (Maharashtra); directed against immigrant Marwari and Gujarati moneylenders; peasants burnt debt bonds and mortgage decrees; British passed **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act (1879)** restricting land alienation.
56. **Moplah / Malabar Rebellion (1921):** Muslim peasant tenant (*Moplah*) uprising against Hindu landlords (*Janmis*) and British police in Malabar (Kerala); led by **Variyankunnath Kunjahammed Haji** and **Ali Musaliar**; intertwined with Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements; marred by communal violence; **Wagon Tragedy (Nov 1921)**: 67 Moplah prisoners died of suffocation in a closed railway goods wagon.
57. **Bardoli Satyagraha (1928, Gujarat):** Led by **Vallabhbhai Patel** against 22% enhancement of land revenue by Bombay government; peasants organized total no-tax strike and refused to surrender confiscated livestock; British appointed Maxwell-Broomfield Commission which slashed revenue hike to **6.03%**; women of Bardoli conferred upon Vallabhbhai Patel the title of **'Sardar'**.
58. **Tebhaga Movement (1946–47, Bengal):** Led by Kisan Sabha; sharecroppers (*Bargadars*) demanded two-thirds (*tebhaga*) share of harvested paddy instead of traditional half share (*adhiari*) given to *Jotedars*; slogan: *'Nij khamare dhan tolo'* (Take paddy to our own threshing floors).
59. **Telangana Peasant Armed Struggle (1946–51):** Armed peasant rebellion in Hyderabad State against the feudal oppression of Deshmukhs/Jagirdars and armed militia (*Razakars*) of Nizam; led by Communist Party of India; liberated 3,000 villages; precursor to Sardar Patel's **Operation Polo (September 1948)** annexing Hyderabad.
60. **Indian National Congress Presidents - UP Connection:** (1) **George Yule** - Allahabad Session (1888, 1st British President); (2) **William Wedderburn** - Allahabad Session (1889 & 1910); (3) **Madan Mohan Malaviya** - Lahore (1909) and Delhi (1918); (4) **Babu Ambica Charan Mazumdar** - Lucknow (1916 - historic Lucknow Pact); (5) **Sarojini Naidu** - Kanpur Session (1925, 1st Indian Woman President); (6) **Jawaharlal Nehru** - Lucknow (1936); (7) **J.B. Kripalani** - Meerut Session (1946 - President at the time of Indian Independence).
61. **Major All-India Organizations & Founders:** (1) **Landholders' Society / Zamindari Association (1838)**: Dwarkanath Tagore (first political organization in India); (2) **British Indian Association (1851)**: Radhakanta Deb (President) & Debendranath Tagore (Secretary); (3) **East India Association (1866, London)**: Dadabhai Naoroji; (4) **Indian Association of Calcutta (1876)**: Surendranath Banerjee & Anand Mohan Bose (precursor of INC); (5) **Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884)**: M. Veeraraghavachariar, G. Subramania Iyer, and P. Anandacharlu; (6) **Bombay Presidency Association (1885)**: Pherozeshah Mehta, K.T. Telang, and Badruddin Tyabji ('The Triumvirate').
62. **First Session of INC (28–31 December 1885):** Originally scheduled at Pune but shifted to Bombay due to cholera outbreak; held at **Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay**; attended by **72 delegates** (38 from Bombay, 21 from Madras, 3 from Bengal); presided over by **Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee)**; founder-general secretary was retired British civil servant **Allan Octavian Hume (A.O. Hume)**; Viceroy at that time was **Lord Dufferin** (who dismissed Congress as representing a *'microscopic minority'*).
63. **Safety Valve Theory vs Lightning Conductor:** Lala Lajpat Rai formulated the **'Safety Valve Theory'** in *Young India* (1916), arguing Hume created Congress under Lord Dufferin's instructions to prevent violent mass explosion; **G.K. Gokhale** countered with the **'Lightning Conductor'** metaphor, stating early nationalists used Hume as a protective lightning conductor to shield infant national movement from immediate British suppression.
64. **Partition of India Boundary Commission:** Appointed under **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** on 30 June 1947; comprised 5 members each for Bengal and Punjab Boundary Commissions (2 Congress nominees, 2 Muslim League nominees, chaired by Radcliffe); Radcliffe line announced on **17 August 1947** (two days after independence).
65. **Integration of Princely States (1947–48):** States Ministry headed by **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** with **V.P. Menon** as Secretary; 562 out of 565 states signed Instrument of Accession before 15 August 1947; three holdouts integrated subsequently: (1) **Junagadh**: Plebiscite held in Feb 1948 after Nawab fled to Pakistan; (2) **Hyderabad**: Integrated via police action **Operation Polo (13–18 Sep 1948)**; (3) **Jammu & Kashmir**: Maharaja Hari Singh signed Instrument of Accession on **26 October 1947** following Pakistani tribal invasion.
