## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Two Home Rule Leagues (1916) Comparison:**
   - **Tilak’s Indian Home Rule League:** Founded in **April 1916** at the Bombay Provincial Conference in **Belgaum / Poona**; **Joseph Baptista** was President and **N.C. Kelkar** was Secretary; had 6 branches; area of operation: **Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, and Berar**.
   - **Annie Besant’s All India Home Rule League:** Founded in **September 1916** at **Madras / Adyar**; **George Arundale** was Organizing Secretary, **B.P. Wadia** was in charge of publications, and C.P. Ramaswami Aiyar was Secretary; had ~200 branches; area of operation: **Entire rest of India including Bombay city**.
2. **Core Objectives of Home Rule:**
   - Demanded **Self-Government (Home Rule)** within the British Commonwealth on the lines of white dominions (Canada, Australia) using constitutional methods; promoted political education, discussion of national issues, and library networks; avoided violent revolutionary methods.
3. **Internment of Annie Besant (June 1917):**
   - Lord Pentland’s Madras government interned **Annie Besant, George Arundale, and B.P. Wadia** at Ootacamund (Ooty) in June 1917; triggered a nationwide storm of public outrage; **Sir S. Subramania Aiyar** renounced his knighthood in protest; prominent leaders like Motilal Nehru, Jawaharlal Nehru, Tej Bahadur Sapru, and M.A. Jinnah joined the League; British released them in September 1917.
4. **Montagu Declaration (20 August 1917) — "August Declaration":**
   - British Secretary of State for India **Edwin Montagu** made a landmark policy announcement in the British House of Commons:
   - Stated the official British objective in India was the *"increasing association of Indians in every branch of administration, and the gradual development of self-governing institutions, with a view to the progressive realization of responsible government in India as an integral part of the British Empire."*
   - Moderates hailed it as the *"Magna Carta of India"*, while Tilak characterized it as a *"sunless dawn"*; *Examiner Trap: Do not confuse Montagu's 1917 August Declaration with Linlithgow's 1940 August Offer!*
5. **Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms):**
   - Enacted based on the Joint Report on Indian Constitutional Reform (1918) by Montagu and Viceroy Lord Chelmsford.
   - Introduced **Dyarchy (द्वैध शासन)** in provincial executives:
     - **Transferred Subjects** (administered by Governor on advice of elected Indian ministers responsible to legislative council): Education, Health, Agriculture, Local Self-Government, Public Works.
     - **Reserved Subjects** (administered by Governor and his appointed Executive Council without legislative accountability): Law & Order, Police, Justice, Finance, Land Revenue, Irrigation.
   - Introduced **Bicameral Legislature at the Centre**: Council of State (Upper House, 60 members) and Legislative Assembly (Lower House, 140 members).
   - Extended separate communal electorates to **Sikhs, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians, and Europeans**.
   - Provided for the establishment of a statutory **Public Service Commission** (established 1926).
6. **Lucknow Session (December 1916) — Historic Dual Triumph:**
   - Presided over by **Ambica Charan Majumdar (A.C. Majumdar)**.
   - **Reunion:** Moderates and Extremists reunited after 9 years of separation since the 1907 Surat Split (brokered by Tilak and Annie Besant).
   - **Lucknow Pact (Congress–League Pact):** Joint constitutional demand signed with the All India Muslim League (guided by Tilak and Muhammad Ali Jinnah); Congress accepted the principle of **separate communal electorates for Muslims** and fixed representation ratios in provincial councils; Sarojini Naidu hailed Jinnah as the *"Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity"*.
7. **Annie Besant's Landmark Congress Presidency (1917):**
   - Capitalizing on her immense popularity following her internment, Annie Besant was elected **President of the Calcutta Session of the INC in December 1917**, becoming the **first woman President of the Indian National Congress**.
8. **Bal Gangadhar Tilak’s Slogan & Politics:**
   - Slogan: ***"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it!"*** (*स्वराज्य हा माझा जन्मसिद्ध हक्क आहे आणि तो मी मिळवणारच!*).
   - Demanded formation of linguistic states and vernacular education; Tilak never became President of INC; passed away on **1 August 1920** (the very day Mahatma Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement).
9. **Gandhi Renames Home Rule League (1920):**
   - In 1920, **Mahatma Gandhi** was elected President of the All India Home Rule League; renamed it to **Swarajya Sabha (स्वराज्य सभा)**; later merged it into the Indian National Congress during the Non-Cooperation Movement.
10. **Banaras Hindu University (BHU, 1916):**
    - Established under the Banaras Hindu University Act, 1915; foundation stone laid on **4 February 1916** by Viceroy **Lord Hardinge II**.
    - Chief founder and architect: **Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya**; absorbed Annie Besant’s Central Hindu College (founded 1898); first Vice-Chancellor was Sir Sundar Lal.
    - Mahatma Gandhi delivered his first major public address in India on **6 February 1916** at the BHU opening ceremony, castigating Indian princely ostentation and identifying with the impoverished masses.
11. **First Modern Trade Union in India (1918):**
    - **Madras Labour Union** founded in **1918** by **B.P. Wadia** (associate of Annie Besant) among the textile workers of the Buckingham and Carnatic Mills in Madras; first systematic trade union with regular membership, subscription dues, and collective bargaining.
12. **All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC, 31 October 1920):**
    - Founded in **Bombay on 31 October 1920** to represent Indian working-class interests at the newly formed International Labour Organization (ILO) in Geneva.
    - First President: **Lala Lajpat Rai**; First General Secretary: **Dewan Chaman Lall**; Chairman of Reception Committee: **Joseph Baptista**.
    - Lala Lajpat Rai declared in his presidential address: *"Militarism and imperialism are the twin-children of capitalism... The workers of India must organize to protect their rights."*
13. **India's First Representative to the ILO (1919):**
    - **Narayan Malhar Joshi (N.M. Joshi)** was nominated as the representative of Indian labour at the first International Labour Conference (ILO) held in Washington in **1919**.
    - N.M. Joshi also founded the **Social Service League (1911)** and served as General Secretary of AITUC from 1925 to 1929.
14. **First May Day in India (1 May 1923):**
    - Celebrated for the first time on Indian soil at Madras by pioneer communist leader **Malayapuram Singaravelu Chettiar** under the auspices of the *Labour Kisan Party of Hindustan*; first time the red flag was unfurled in India.
15. **Foundation of the Communist Party of India (CPI):**
    - **Formed Abroad (Tashkent, 17 October 1920):** Founded at Tashkent (Soviet Union) by **M.N. Roy**, Evelyn Trent-Roy, Abani Mukherji, Rosa Fitingov, and Mohammad Ali following the Second Congress of the Comintern.
    - **Formed on Indian Soil (Kanpur, December 1925):** First All-India Communist Conference organized at Kanpur by **Satyabhakta**; **Singaravelu Chettiar** elected President, **S.V. Ghate** elected first General Secretary; established CPI headquarters in Bombay.
16. **Early Communist Conspiracy Trials:**
    - **Peshawar Conspiracy Cases (1922–1927):** Prosecution of Muhajirs trained in Tashkent who returned to India via Soviet Central Asia.
    - **Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case (1924):** British colonial government tried **S.A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmad, Shaukat Usmani, and Nalini Gupta** on charges of establishing a branch of the Comintern to deprive the King-Emperor of his sovereignty; sentenced to 4 years' imprisonment; *Examiner Trap: Dr. M.A. Ansari was NOT associated with this case!*
    - **Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929–1933):** Arrest and trial of 31 prominent trade unionists and communist leaders, including three British communists: **Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley, and Lester Hutchinson**, alongside S.A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmad, and P.C. Joshi; defended by Jawaharlal Nehru and Motilal Nehru.
17. **Congress Socialist Party (CSP, 1934):**
    - Formed within the Indian National Congress in **May 1934 at Patna** (preliminary meeting) and formally inaugurated at **Bombay on 21–22 October 1934**.
    - Key Founders: **Acharya Narendra Deva** (first President), **Jayaprakash Narayan (JP)** (first General Secretary), **Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia**, **Ashok Mehta**, **Minoo Masani**, and **Yusuf Meherally**.
    - Objective: Work within Congress to steer the national movement towards a socialist economic orientation; *Examiner Trap: Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose were socialist sympathizers and intellectual mentors, but NEVER formally enrolled as members of the CSP!*
18. **Workers and Peasants Party (WPP, 1927–1928):**
    - Open legal political party formed by communists within the Indian National Congress to mobilize peasants and industrial workers; provincial units united into the **All-India Workers and Peasants Party at Calcutta in December 1928** (presided over by Sohan Singh Josh); spearheaded the Great Bombay Textile Strike of 1928.
19. **Justice Party / South Indian Liberal Federation (1916):**
    - Founded in **Madras in November 1916** by **Dr. T.M. Nair**, **P. Theagaraya Chetty**, and **C. Natesa Mudaliar**; published the English daily *Justice*.
    - Non-Brahmin regional party that opposed Brahmin dominance in education and civil services; contested elections under the 1919 Act and formed the first provincial ministry in Madras (1920); passed the historic **Communal Government Order (1921)** introducing statutory caste reservations in government appointments.
20. **Self-Respect Movement (1925, Tamil Nadu):**
    - Founded in **1925** by **Periyar E.V. Ramasamy Naicker** after breaking away from the Congress; radical rationalist, anti-Brahmin, and atheistic movement; advocated burning of *Manusmriti*, removal of caste titles, non-priestly civil marriages ("Self-Respect Marriages"), and women’s liberation; published ***Kudi Arasu* (1925)**.
21. **National Universities Wave during Non-Cooperation (1920–1921):**
    - **Jamia Millia Islamia (1920):** Founded at Aligarh by Maulana Mohammad Ali, Hakim Ajmal Khan, and Dr. Zakir Hussain (later shifted to Delhi).
    - **Gujarat Vidyapith (1920):** Founded at Ahmedabad by Mahatma Gandhi.
    - **Kashi Vidyapith (1921):** Founded at Varanasi by Babu Shiv Prasad Gupta and Bhagwan Das; Acharya Narendra Deva served as Principal.
    - **Bihar Vidyapith (1921):** Founded at Patna by Mahatma Gandhi and Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
22. **Trade Unions Act, 1926:** Recognized trade unions as legal corporate entities; granted immunity from civil and criminal prosecution to registered union officials for legitimate trade union activities and collective bargaining.
23. **Trade Disputes Act, 1929:** Outlawed strikes in public utility services without one month's prior written notice; declared general sympathetic strikes and political strikes illegal; established Courts of Inquiry and Boards of Conciliation for industrial disputes.
24. **Whitley Commission on Labour (1929–1931):** Royal Commission on Labour in India chaired by **John Henry Whitley**; investigated conditions of labour in industrial undertakings and plantations; recommendations led to the Factories Act of 1934.
25. **Labour Wings of Major Political Streams (Match Master):**
    - **AITUC (1920):** Earliest all-India trade union federation; later affiliated with the Communist Party of India (CPI).
    - **INTUC (3 May 1947):** Indian National Trade Union Congress; founded by **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**; affiliated with the Indian National Congress.
    - **BMS (23 July 1955):** Bharatiya Mazdoor Sangh; founded by **Dattopant Thengadi**; affiliated with the RSS / nationalist stream.
    - **CITU (1970):** Centre of Indian Trade Unions; founded by B.T. Ranadive; affiliated with CPI(M).
    - **UTUC (1949):** United Trade Union Congress (often mapped to left/CPI-M in old UPPCS keys).
26. **Annie Besant’s Periodicals & Publications:**
    - Published two influential newspapers from Madras: ***Commonweal* (weekly, launched Jan 1914)** and ***New India* (daily, launched July 1914)**.
    - Authored *India: A Nation* and *Wake Up, India: A Plea for Social Reform*.
27. **Valentine Chirol Libel Case:**
    - In 1918, Bal Gangadhar Tilak traveled to London to sue British journalist Sir Valentine Chirol for libel (Chirol had written in *Indian Unrest* that Tilak was the "Father of Indian Unrest" and leader of a criminal conspiracy); Tilak lost the lawsuit, but his absence weakened the Home Rule movement in India.
28. **Decline of the Home Rule Movement (1918–1919):**
    - The movement lost momentum due to:
      1. Moderate leaders were pacified by Montagu’s 1917 promise of responsible government.
      2. Annie Besant vacillated after her release, accepting the Montford reforms.
      3. Tilak’s prolonged absence in England (1918–19) and untimely death on 1 August 1920.
      4. Rise of Mahatma Gandhi’s mass satyagraha and Non-Cooperation Movement, which absorbed Home Rule leagues into Congress committees.
29. **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909) Contrast:**
    - The 1909 Act introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** (Dr. Rajendra Prasad characterized Lord Minto as the true "Father of Pakistan"); it did **NOT** introduce Dyarchy (Dyarchy was introduced exclusively by the 1919 Act!).
30. **Acharya Narendra Deva (1889–1956):** Born in Sitapur (UP); prominent socialist theoretician; Principal of Kashi Vidyapith; Vice-Chancellor of Lucknow University and Banaras Hindu University; first President of the Congress Socialist Party (1934).
31. **Jayaprakash Narayan (JP, 1902–1979):** Born in Sitabdiara (Bihar/UP border); studied Marxism in the United States; General Secretary of CSP (1934); played heroic underground role during Quit India Movement (1942) after escaping Hazaribagh Jail; led the Total Revolution (*Sampoorna Kranti*) in 1974; awarded Bharat Ratna posthumously in 1999.
32. **Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia (1910–1967):** Born in Akbarpur (Ambedkar Nagar, UP); leading socialist thinker; edited *Congress Socialist* and *Mankind*; operated the underground Congress Radio with Usha Mehta in 1942; formulated the "Sapta Kranti" (Seven Revolutions) theory.
33. **Radical Democratic Party (1940):** Founded by **M.N. Roy** after breaking away from the Indian National Congress on the issue of supporting the British war effort against European fascism during WWII.
34. The **First Factory Act of 1881** (Lord Ripon) applied to factories with ≥100 workers and prohibited employment of children under 7; the **Second Factory Act of 1891** (Lord Lansdowne) applied to factories with ≥50 workers, raised the minimum child employment age to 9 (limited to 7 hours/day), restricted female labour to 11 hours/day, and mandated weekly Sunday holidays.
35. The **Trade Union Act of 1926** provided legal registration and formal recognition to trade unions, granting union leaders immunity from civil prosecution and criminal conspiracy charges for legitimate trade dispute activities.
36. The **Trade Disputes Act of 1929** established courts of inquiry and conciliation boards for industrial conflicts, while rendering strikes in public utility services (railways, postal, water, power) illegal without a mandatory one-month advance written notice.
37. The **South Indian Liberal Federation**, popularly known as the **Justice Party**, was founded in Madras in **1916** by **Dr. C. Natesa Mudaliar, Sir P. Theagaraya Chetty, and Dr. T.M. Nair** to represent non-Brahmin interests; it opposed the Home Rule League as an upper-caste movement.
38. In the 1920 Madras legislative elections under the GoI Act 1919, the **Justice Party** won a decisive victory and formed the first non-Brahmin ministry in Madras with **A. Subbarayalu Reddiar** as Premier (Chief Minister).
39. The **Self-Respect Movement (आत्म-सम्मान आंदोलन)** was founded in **1925** in Tamil Nadu by **E.V. Ramasamy (Periyar)** after breaking away from the Indian National Congress; he published ***Kudi Arasu*** (Republic) and advocated non-Brahmin pride, rationalism, and self-respect marriages conducted without Brahmin priests.
40. In Punjab, the **Unionist Party** was founded in **1923** by **Sir Fazl-i-Husain and Sir Chhotu Ram** to champion the economic interests of landed agriculturists across Hindu, Muslim, and Sikh communities, successfully holding provincial power for two decades.
41. In Bengal, **A.K. Fazlul Huq (Sher-e-Bangla)** founded the **Krishak Praja Party (1929)** to champion the tenancy rights of Muslim and lower-caste Hindu sharecroppers and tenant cultivators against zamindari exploitation.
42. The **Revolutionary Socialist Party (RSP)** was founded in **1940** by former members of the Anushilan Samiti and CSP led by **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee** and **Tridib Chaudhuri**, rejecting Stalinist popular fronts in favor of permanent socialist revolution.
43. The **Bolshevik Leninist Party of India (BLPI)** was formed in **1942** as a Trotskyist section of the Fourth International, led by Leslie Goonewardene and Colvin R. de Silva.
44. The **All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC)** suffered its **first split in 1929** at the 10th session in Nagpur (presided by Jawaharlal Nehru) over affiliation with the communist Pan-Pacific Trade Union Secretariat; moderate leaders N.M. Joshi, V.V. Giri, and Chaman Lall broke away to form the **Indian Trades Union Federation (ITUF)**.
45. A second split occurred in 1931 when ultra-left communists led by B.T. Ranadive and S.V. Deshpande broke away from AITUC to form the short-lived **Red Trade Union Congress (Red TUC)**.
46. The **Whitley Commission (Royal Commission on Labour in India)** was appointed in **1929** under John Henry Whitley; its 1931 report recommended better working conditions, housing, health facilities, and minimum wage legislation for industrial labour.
47. **Singaravelu Chettiar (1860–1946)** of Madras organized the **first May Day (Labor Day) celebration in India** on **1 May 1923** at Madras Beach, unfurling the first Red Flag on Indian soil.
48. In the **Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case (1924)**, the British charged four pioneer Indian communists — **M.N. Roy, Shaukat Usmani, Muzaffar Ahmad, and S.A. Dange** — with attempting to deprive the King-Emperor of his sovereignty over India; Dange and Muzaffar Ahmad were sentenced to 4 years' rigorous imprisonment.
49. The **Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929–1933)** was the largest state trial against the Indian labour movement, in which **31 prominent trade unionists and communists** (including three British trade unionists: **Philip Spratt, Benjamin Bradley, and Lester Hutchinson**) were arrested and tried for organizing strikes; the trial drew international defense campaigns spearheaded by Albert Einstein and Romain Rolland.
50. The **National Planning Committee (1938)** was established by INC President Subhas Chandra Bose at the Haripura Congress with **Jawaharlal Nehru as Chairman** and K.T. Shah as Secretary, bringing together industrialists, scientists, and socialists to formulate a blueprint for India's independent planned economy.

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

1. **Home Rule Launch Timing Mnemonic:**
   - **T before B:** **T**ilak (**April** 1916) came before **B**esant (**September** 1916).
2. **Dyarchy Subject Division (1919 Act):**
   - **R - R**: **R**eserved = **R**eal power (Police, Justice, Finance, Land Revenue).
   - **T - T**: **T**ransferred = **T**hrown to Indians (Education, Health, Local Bodies, Agriculture).
3. **Labour Trade Union Federations Chronology:**
   - **A - I - B - C**
   - **A**ITUC (1920) → **I**NTUC (1947) → **B**MS (1955) → **C**ITU (1970).
4. **CSP Core Founder Duo:**
   - **N - J**
   - **N**arendra Deva (President) + **J**ayaprakash Narayan (General Secretary).
5. **Tilak's Territory Formula:**
   - **Maharashtra (MINUS Bombay City) + Karnataka + CP + Berar**.
   - *(Bombay City was Besant's!)*
