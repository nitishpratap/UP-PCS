## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. ***Anandamath* (1882):** Masterpiece novel by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** set against the backdrop of the **Sanyasi Rebellion (1763–1800)** and the devastating Bengal Famine of 1770; contains the immortal national song ***Vande Mataram*** (first sung at 1896 Calcutta Congress); Bankim's other famous novels include *Durgeshnandini* (1865), *Kapalkundala* (1866), and *Devi Chaudhurani* (1884).
2. ***Nil Darpan* (The Mirror of Indigo, 1860):** Powerful Bengali drama written by **Dinabandhu Mitra** portraying the horrific exploitation and physical torture of Bengali peasants by European indigo planters; translated into English by **Michael Madhusudan Dutt** and published by **Rev. James Long** (who was prosecuted, fined ₹1,000, and imprisoned by the British court).
3. ***Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1901):** Landmark economic treatise by **Dadabhai Naoroji** ("Grand Old Man of India"); systematically established the **Drain of Wealth Theory**, calculating that Britain drained millions of pounds annually from India without return; argued that colonial rule in India was "un-British" because it violated Britain's own liberal and constitutional principles.
4. ***The Rise and Growth of Economic Nationalism in India* (1966):** Authoritative post-independence historiographical classic written by **Bipan Chandra**; examines the economic thought of early nationalists (Naoroji, Dutt, Ranade, Gokhale); *Examiner Trap: This is modern historiography, not a contemporary 1901 colonial tract!*
5. ***Hind Swaraj* (Indian Home Rule, 1909):** Written by **Mahatma Gandhi** in Gujarati aboard the ship *SS Kildonan Castle* while traveling from London to South Africa; written in a dialogue format between the "Reader" and the "Editor"; fierce critique of modern Western industrial civilization, railways, lawyers, and doctors; advocated soul-force (*Satyagraha*) and rural self-sufficiency; banned by British government in 1910.
6. ***The Story of My Experiments with Truth* (1927):** Mahatma Gandhi’s celebrated autobiography covering his life from childhood up to the 1921 Nagpur Congress session; originally written in Gujarati in weekly installments for his journal *Navajivan*; translated into English by his personal secretary **Mahadev Desai**.
7. ***The Discovery of India* (1946):** Written by **Jawaharlal Nehru** during his five-month imprisonment at **Ahmednagar Fort Jail (1942–1945)** following the Quit India Movement; panoramic philosophical journey through 5,000 years of Indian civilization, history, culture, and ethos; his autobiography is ***Toward Freedom* (1936)**; his letters to his daughter Indira were published as ***Glimpses of World History* (1934)**.
8. ***India Wins Freedom* (1959):** Political autobiography and freedom memoir of **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**; provides an insider account of the negotiations from 1939 to 1947, Partition, and the Cabinet Mission; thirty secret pages withheld at his request were released in 1988.
9. ***Gita Rahasya* (The Secret of the Gita, 1915):** Authored by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** in Marathi while serving a 6-year sentence of rigorous imprisonment in **Mandalay Jail (Burma)**; interpreted the *Bhagavad Gita* as an gospel of selfless, energetic action (*Karma Yoga*) rather than renunciation (*Sannyasa*); his other seminal work on Vedic antiquity is ***The Arctic Home in the Vedas* (1903)** (asserting that Aryans originated in the Arctic regions) and *The Orion* (1893).
10. ***Gulamgiri* (Slavery, 1873):** Written by **Mahatma Jyotirao Phule** in Marathi; powerful polemic against Brahminical supremacy and the caste hierarchy; dedicated to the good people of the United States who fought for the abolition of Black slavery; his other major work is *Shetkaryacha Asud* (The Whipcord of the Peasant, 1881).
11. ***Annihilation of Caste* (1936):** Undelivered presidential address written by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** for the annual conference of the anti-caste reform group *Jat-Pat Todak Mandal* of Lahore (canceled by organizers because Ambedkar refused to tone down his criticism of Hindu scriptures); Ambedkar argued that caste is not a division of labour, but a division of labourers, and called for the destruction of the sanctity of the Shastras.
12. ***The Story of My Deportation* (1908):** Written by **Lala Lajpat Rai** recounting his 6-month deportation without trial to Mandalay Jail (Burma) in May 1907 for leading the Punjab agrarian agitation (*Pagri Sambhal Jatta*); his other books include *Young India* (1916) and *Arya Samaj* (1915).
13. ***Unhappy India* (1928):** Written by **Lala Lajpat Rai** as a stinging, point-by-point patriotic refutation of the racist American journalist **Katherine Mayo's** slanderous book ***Mother India* (1927)** (which Mahatma Gandhi described as a *"Drain Inspector's Report"*).
14. ***A Nation in Making* (1925):** Celebrated political autobiography of **Sir Surendranath Banerjee**; chronicles fifty years of Indian public life, the founding of the Indian Association (1876), the anti-partition agitation of Bengal, and the early Congress movement.
15. ***The Indian War of Independence 1857* (1909):** Written by **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar (Veer Savarkar)** in Marathi while living at India House in London; first historical work to describe the 1857 uprising not as a sepoy mutiny but as a coordinated "First War of Independence"; banned by British authorities before publication; smuggled into India disguised with fake covers like *Pickwick Papers*.
16. ***India Divided* (1946):** Written by **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** (first President of India) while imprisoned in Bankipur jail (Patna); scholarly and statistical examination of the communal problem, proving that Partition was economically, demographically, and militarily disastrous for both communities.
17. ***Pakistan or the Partition of India* (1940):** Written by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**; masterly, dispassionate analysis of the Muslim League's demand for Pakistan, outlining the philosophical, communal, and constitutional issues involved.
18. ***The Indian Struggle* (Two Parts: 1935 & 1942):** Written by **Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose** during his European exile; provides a detailed historical narrative of the Indian national struggle from 1920 to 1942; highly critical of Gandhi’s compromises; banned by the British Government; his unfinished autobiography is ***An Indian Pilgrim* (1948)**.
19. ***Why I Am an Atheist* (1930):** Famous philosophical essay written by **Bhagat Singh** in Lahore Central Jail; published on 27 September 1931 in Lala Lajpat Rai's English weekly *The People*; explained why his atheism was rooted in rational materialism rather than youthful vanity or bravado.
20. ***Bandi Jiwan* (A Life of Captivity, 1922):** Written by revolutionary leader **Sachindra Nath Sanyal**; autobiographical account of underground revolutionary life, the Kakori conspiracy, and Andaman cellular jail; served as the undisputed "Bible of North Indian Revolutionaries", inspiring Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Chandrashekhar Azad.
21. ***The Philosophy of the Bomb* (1930):** Authored by revolutionary thinker **Bhagwati Charan Vohra** with inputs from Chandrashekhar Azad and Yashpal; written as a robust ideological reply to Mahatma Gandhi’s article *"The Cult of the Bomb"*; distributed across India on 26 January 1930.
22. ***Pather Dabi* (Right of Way, 1926):** Celebrated political novel written by **Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay**; serialized in *Bangabani*; glorified the armed revolutionary struggle against British rule through the charismatic character of Sabyasachi; immediately banned by the British colonial government.
23. ***Economic History of India* (Two Volumes: 1902 & 1904):** Masterwork of **Romesh Chunder Dutt (R.C. Dutt)**; meticulously documented the agrarian ruin, deindustrialization, heavy taxation, and financial Drain under British rule from Plassey to the Victorian era.
24. ***Satyarth Prakash* (The Light of Truth, 1875):** The seminal scripture of the Arya Samaj written by **Swami Dayanand Saraswati** in Hindi at Varanasi; expounds Vedic monotheism, social reform, rejection of idol worship, and rational ethics.
25. ***Gitanjali* (1910):** Collection of devotional songs by **Rabindranath Tagore**; translated into English with an introduction by W.B. Yeats; awarded the **Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913** (first non-European Nobel laureate); Tagore's other monumental political novels include ***Gora* (1909)**, ***Ghare Baire* (1916)**, and ***Char Adhyay* (1934)**.
26. ***Firangiya* (1921):** Famous anti-colonial Bhojpuri poem written by **Manoranjan Prasad Sinha**; sung widely during the Non-Cooperation Movement across Bihar and Eastern UP to mobilize rural masses against British rule (*Firangis*).
27. ***Batohiya* (1911):** Iconic Bhojpuri patriotic song written by **Babu Raghuveer Narayan**; celebrated India’s sacred geographical unity from the Himalayas to Kanyakumari; widely sung in the freedom struggle alongside *Vande Mataram*.
28. ***Bidesiya*:** Legendary Bhojpuri folk-drama written by **Bhikhari Thakur** (hailed as the **"Shakespeare of Bhojpuri"**); depicted the social agony, migration, and economic displacement of rural Bihari and Eastern UP labourers under colonial economic ruin.
29. ***Achhut Kee Shikayat* (Complaint of the Untouchable, 1914):** First published Dalit poem in Hindi literature, written by **Heera Dom**; published in the prestigious literary journal *Saraswati* (edited by Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi).
30. ***Godaan* (1936), *Sevasadan* (1918), *Rangbhoomi* (1925), *Karmabhoomi* (1932):** Monumental realist Hindi-Urdu novels by **Munshi Premchand** (Dhanpat Rai Shrivastava); depicted the crushing rural indebtedness, feudal cruelty of zamindars, caste discrimination, and the colonial police apparatus in Awadh and UP.
31. ***Tarana-e-Hind* (1904):** Written by **Sir Muhammad Iqbal**; contains the famous patriotic anthem *"Sare Jahan Se Achha Hindustan Hamara"*; published in the journal *Ittehad*; his philosophical works include *Bang-i-Dra* (1924) and *Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam* (1930).
32. ***Essays on Indian Economics* (1898):** Written by **Justice Mahadev Govind Ranade**; argued against uncritical application of classical British laissez-faire doctrines to India; advocated state-sponsored industrialization and tariff protection.
33. ***Speeches and Writings of Gopal Krishna Gokhale*:** Published compilation of Gokhale's brilliant budget debates in the Imperial Legislative Council exposing colonial military expenditures and salt tax.
34. ***New Lamps for Old* (1893):** Series of brilliant, fiery political articles published by **Aurobindo Ghosh** in the Bombay weekly *Indu Prakash*; launched a devastating critique of the Moderate Congress's timid policy of "praying, petitioning, and protesting".
35. ***The Life Divine* (1939) & *Savitri* (1950):** Philosophical and epic spiritual masterworks composed by **Sri Aurobindo** during his post-political phase at the Pondicherry Ashram; his political philosophy is expounded in *The Ideal of Human Unity* (1919) and *The Foundations of Indian Culture*.
36. ***Prison Diary* (1975) & *Why Socialism?* (1936):** Written by socialist patriarch **Jayaprakash Narayan (JP)**; *Why Socialism?* established the ideological foundations of the Congress Socialist Party (CSP).
37. ***Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind* (Causes of the Indian Revolt, 1859):** First Indian treatise on 1857 written in Urdu by **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan**; presented an honest analysis of British misrule, lack of Indian representation, and cultural insensitivity.
38. ***Bapu: Conversations and Correspondence with Mahatma Gandhi*:** By **Verrier Elwin**; anthropologist who worked extensively on central Indian tribal communities (*The Baiga*, *The Muria and their Ghotul*).
39. ***Problems of the Far East* (1894) & *British Government in India* (1925):** Imperial administrative books written by Viceroy **Lord Curzon**.
40. ***My Indian Years (1910–1916)*:** Viceroy **Lord Hardinge II's** personal memoirs of his turbulent tenure in India (annulment of partition, capital shift, bomb attack).
41. ***War of Without Violence* (1939):** Written by **Krishnalal Shridharani**; one of the earliest English academic treatises explaining Gandhian non-violent satyagraha to the Western world.
42. ***The Soul of India* (1911):** Written by **Bipin Chandra Pal**; explored the spiritual and philosophical underpinnings of Indian nationalism.
43. ***Indian Unrest* (1910):** Authored by British journalist **Sir Valentine Chirol**; branded Tilak as the "Father of Indian Unrest" and accused Arya Samaj of political sedition.
44. ***Freedom at Midnight* (1975):** International narrative history of India's independence and partition by **Larry Collins and Dominique Lapierre**; based on extensive interviews with Lord Mountbatten.
45. ***The Golden Threshold* (1905), *The Bird of Time* (1912), *The Broken Wing* (1917):** Celebrated poetry anthologies by **Sarojini Naidu** ("Nightingale of India" / *Bharat Kokila*).
46. ***Tattvabodhini Patrika* (1843):** Edited by **Akshay Kumar Dutt**; theoretical journal of the Tattvabodhini Sabha promoting scientific outlook, rationalism, and Upanishadic monotheism.
47. ***Dinabandhu* (1877):** Weekly journal founded by **Narayan Meghaji Lokhande** and Jyotiba Phule; first journal in India dedicated exclusively to articulating the grievances of the working class and mill labourers.
48. ***Gora* (1909):** Rabindranath Tagore's longest novel; explores identity, caste, religion, and patriotism through the character of Gora, an orthodox Hindu nationalist who eventually discovers he is an Irish orphan adopted during 1857.

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

1. **The Four Essential "India" Books:**
   - **D**iscovery of India = **N**ehru (**D - N**)
   - **I**ndia Wins Freedom = **A**zad (**I - A**)
   - **I**ndia Divided = **R**ajendra Prasad (**I - R**)
   - **U**nhappy India = **L**ajpat Rai (**U - L**).
2. **Tilak's Dual Masterpieces in Exile:**
   - **G**ita Rahasya (in **Mandalay**, *Karma Yoga*)
