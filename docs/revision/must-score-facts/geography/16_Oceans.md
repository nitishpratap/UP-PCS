## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. By area the oceans rank **Pacific > Atlantic > Indian > Southern > Arctic**. The Indian Ocean was the Greek **Erythraean Sea** and straddles **both sides of the Equator**.
2. Ocean-floor order from the shore is **shelf → slope → rise (or trench) → abyssal plain**. A **guyot** is a flat-topped seamount.
3. The continental shelf is shallow (about **200 m**), rich in fish and oil, and covers roughly **7.5%** of the ocean floor. Abyssal plains are the most extensive floor province (~**76%**).
4. India’s **west shelf is wider than the east**. The Gujarat–Mumbai belt is widest and holds **Bombay High**. The east shelf is narrower but carries large delta fans.
5. The **Telegraphic Plateau** is part of the **North Atlantic Ridge**, not a separate land plateau.
6. Trench–ocean pairs: **Mariana–Pacific**, **Puerto Rico–Atlantic**, **Sunda/Java–Indian**, and **Molloy–Arctic**.
7. **Agulhas** and **Brazil** are **warm** currents. **Humboldt (Peru)** and **California** are **cold**. Do not mark Agulhas or Brazil as cold.
8. The **Benguela** is an **Atlantic** cold current off south-west Africa. It is **not** a Pacific current.
9. Among classic options, the current linked to the **Indian Ocean** is the **Agulhas**. **Kuroshio** is warm (Japan); **Oyashio** is cold (Kurile).
10. **El Niño** sends warm water off Peru, weakens upwelling, and cuts plankton and fish catch. **La Niña** is the cold opposite phase.
11. Open-ocean **salinity** is about **35‰**. The maximum sits near the **Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn**, not on the Equator. The **Red Sea** is high; the **Baltic** is low.
12. Seawater density rises when water is **colder** and **saltier**. Cold salty water sinks and drives the **thermohaline** circulation. **NaCl** is about **77%** of dissolved salts.
13. Surface temperature peaks near the equator. The **thermocline** lies roughly **300–1000 m**. Deep water is cold in every ocean.
14. A **spring tide** forms at **syzygy** (new/full moon) and is **large**. A **neap tide** forms at **quadrature** and is **small**. Tidal period ≈ **12 h 25 min**.
15. A **tidal bore** is famous on the **Hooghly** and the **Amazon**. The **Bay of Fundy** has the world’s greatest tidal range. India’s tidal-energy coast fact is the **Gulf of Khambhat**.
16. **Upwelling** is strongest on **west coasts**: Peru, California, Canary, Benguela, and Somalia.
17. **Constructive** waves build beaches; **destructive** waves erode them. A **tsunami** is not a tide.
18. Ocean currents shape climate (mild or foggy coasts, west-coast deserts), concentrate fish where warm and cold meet or upwelling occurs, and affect navigation routes.
19. Deepest deposit is **red clay**. **Globigerina** and **pteropod** oozes are **calcareous**. **Diatom** and **radiolarian** oozes are **siliceous**. **Manganese nodules** sit on abyssal plains.
20. The **Suez Canal** (Egypt, **1869**) joins the **Mediterranean** and the **Red Sea**. It is a **sea-level** cut — ships do **not** climb stepped chambers. Ends: **Port Said** (N) and **Suez** (S).
21. Suez lakes north→south: **Manzala → Timsah → Great Bitter → Little Bitter**. India–Europe sea route is shorter by about **7000 km**. Suez does **not** itself touch the Atlantic or the open Indian Ocean.
22. The **Panama Canal** (**1914**) joins the **Atlantic/Caribbean** and the **Pacific**. Ships **climb stepped chambers** and use **Gatun Lake**. The **Kiel Canal** joins the **North Sea** and the **Baltic**.
23. UNCLOS belts: **territorial sea 12 nm**, **contiguous zone 24 nm**, **EEZ 200 nm**. The continental shelf starts at **200 nm** and may extend to **350 nm**.
24. Classic fishery centres: **Grand Banks** (Labrador meets Gulf Stream), **Dogger Bank**, Peru upwelling, and the **Kuroshio–Oyashio** mix.
25. India’s **operational** Antarctic stations are **Maitri** and **Bharati**. **Dakshin Gangotri** is not the operational pair. **Himadri** is India’s **Arctic** station.
26. The **Antarctic Treaty** dates to **1959/61**. India joined in **1983**. India is an **observer** in the Arctic Council (Arctic Policy **2022**).
27. Key straits: **Hormuz** (Persian Gulf ↔ Gulf of Oman; **Iran north / Oman south**; ~**25%** seaborne oil), **Malacca**, **Gibraltar**, **Bering**, **Bab-el-Mandeb**, **Palk**, and the **10° Channel**.
28. Persian Gulf coasts = Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar, UAE — **not Oman**. Oman sits on the Gulf of Oman / Musandam side of Hormuz.
29. The **Carlsberg Ridge** lies in the north-west Indian Ocean. The **Ninetyeast Ridge** is not the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
30. The International Seabed Authority manages the seabed **Area** beyond national zones (HQ Jamaica).
31. **Drake Passage** is not the Strait of Magellan. Panama opened in **1914** with stepped chambers; Suez opened in **1869** as a sea-level cut.
32. Hydrosphere ≈ **71%** of Earth. Usable fresh ≈ **<1%** of all water. Of fresh water, ice ≫ groundwater ≫ rivers/lakes.
33. **Datum line** = mean-sea-level height/depth zero. **Halocline** = salinity gradient with depth.
34. **Sargasso** = North Atlantic, **no coast**. **Red Sea** = axial trough. **Diamantina** = Indian Ocean trench/fracture teaching tag.
35. Türkiye clock: **N = Black**, **S = Mediterranean**, **W = Aegean**, **NW = Marmara**. West→east ladder: **Mediterranean → Black → Caspian → Aral**.
36. **Gaza** faces the **Mediterranean**. Baltic coasts include Denmark, Germany, Poland, Baltic States, Russia, Finland, Sweden — **not Norway**. **Jordan** has no Mediterranean coast (only **Aqaba** / Red Sea).
37. Warm currents raise coastal temperature and humidity; cold currents cool and dry coasts and help west-coast deserts. Fog is common where warm and cold currents meet.
38. Coral needs warm clear shallow water; bleaching pairs with about **2°C** sea-surface warming. India’s reef facts: Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kachchh.
39. Hormuz oil/LNG chokepoint: roughly **20 mb/d** oil products (~**25%** seaborne oil) and ~**20%** world LNG in recent baseline years; most flows go to **Asia**.
40. Only **Saudi Arabia** and the **UAE** have meaningful pipeline routes that can skip Hormuz; Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Iran lean almost entirely on the strait.
41. **BBNJ 2023** is the high-seas biodiversity treaty path; UNCLOS **12 / 24 / 200 nm** belts remain the distance spine.
42. Canary and California are **cold** eastern-boundary currents; Gulf Stream and Kuroshio are **warm** western-boundary currents.
43. Somali upwelling is a seasonal Indian Ocean west-coast upwelling fact tied to monsoon winds.
44. North Atlantic Deep Water / thermohaline “conveyor” links dense sinking in high latitudes to global deep circulation.
45. India’s inland waterway **NW-1** is the **Ganga** from Haldia to Prayagraj — a waterway fact often parked beside ocean chapters in mixed papers.
