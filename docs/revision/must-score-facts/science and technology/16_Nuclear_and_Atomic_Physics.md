## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Nuclear fission** is the splitting of a heavy atomic nucleus (e.g. U-235, Pu-239) into two medium-sized fragments of comparable mass upon absorbing a thermal (slow) neutron.
2. In each fission of a Uranium-235 nucleus, approximately **$200\text{ MeV}$** of energy is liberated, along with $2$ to $3$ fast neutrons and energetic gamma rays.
3. Fission of $1\text{ gram}$ of Uranium-235 yields approximately **$5 \times 10^{23}\text{ MeV}$ of energy**, equivalent to detonating **$20\text{ tonnes of TNT}$**.
4. Most of the energy released during nuclear fission appears as the **kinetic energy (thermal heat) of the fission fragments**.
5. When a neutron is captured by a heavy nucleus without causing fission, it forms a heavier isotope via **radiative neutron capture**, not fission.
6. An **Atomic Bomb** utilizes an **uncontrolled chain reaction** of fast neutrons in highly enriched U-235 or Pu-239.
7. **Critical Mass** is the minimum quantity of fissile material required to sustain a self-propagating nuclear chain reaction.
8. The atomic bomb dropped on Hiroshima ("Little Boy", Aug 6, 1945) used **enriched Uranium-235** in a gun-type assembly; the bomb dropped on Nagasaki ("Fat Man", Aug 9, 1945) used **Plutonium-239** in an implosion assembly.
9. A **Nuclear Reactor (Atomic Pile / Molecular Furnace)** sustains a **controlled, self-propagating chain reaction** for peaceful power generation and radioisotope production.
10. The world's first artificial nuclear reactor was built in 1942 at the University of Chicago by **Enrico Fermi** ("Chicago Pile-1").
11. **Nuclear Reactor Fuel:** Fissile materials such as **Uranium-235 ($^{235}_{92}\text{U}$)**, **Plutonium-239 ($^{239}_{94}\text{Pu}$)**, or synthetic **Uranium-233 ($^{233}_{92}\text{U}$)**.
12. **Moderator:** Slows down energetic fast fission neutrons ($2\text{ MeV}$) to thermal speeds ($0.025\text{ eV}$) through elastic collisions so they can induce subsequent fission.
13. Common moderators: **Light water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, most common globally)**, **Heavy water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$)**, **High-purity Graphite**, and **Beryllium oxide (BeO)**.
14. Heavy water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$) is the superior moderator because deuterium has an exceptionally **low neutron absorption cross-section**, permitting the use of un-enriched **natural uranium** as fuel (as in Indian PHWRs).
15. **Control Rods:** Absorb surplus slow-moving thermal neutrons to regulate or halt the fission rate. Inserting control rods dampens the chain reaction; withdrawing them increases reactivity.
16. Control rods are fabricated from neutron-absorbing materials: **Cadmium (Cd) or Boron (B)** (often as boron steel or boron carbide).
17. **Cadmium is NOT a nuclear fuel**; it is strictly a neutron absorber used in control rods and radiation shielding.
18. **Coolant:** Extracts intense heat generated in the reactor core; typical coolants include light water, heavy water, liquid sodium metal (in fast breeder reactors), $\text{CO}_2$ gas, and helium.
19. **Nuclear Fusion:** The thermonuclear coalescence of two or more light nuclei at extreme velocity/temperature to form a heavier, more tightly bound nucleus, with a mass deficit converted to energy ($E = \Delta m c^2$).
20. The energy released per unit mass in **nuclear fusion is vastly greater than in nuclear fission**.
21. Nuclear fusion powers the **Sun and all stars**, primarily through the proton-proton chain and CNO cycle at core temperatures exceeding **$1.5 \times 10^7\text{ K}$**.
22. The theoretical mechanism of stellar thermonuclear energy generation was formulated in 1938 by **Hans Albrecht Bethe** (Nobel Prize 1967).
23. The **Hydrogen Bomb (Thermonuclear Weapon)** operates on the principle of **uncontrolled nuclear fusion** of heavy hydrogen isotopes (deuterium and tritium).
24. Because nuclear fusion requires activation temperatures of millions of degrees, an **atomic fission bomb serves as the trigger (igniter)** inside a hydrogen bomb.
25. The "Father of the Hydrogen Bomb" is Hungarian-American physicist **Edward Teller**; the first full-scale thermonuclear test ("Ivy Mike") was detonated by the USA in 1952 at Eniwetok Atoll.
26. Controlled thermonuclear fusion research utilizes **Tokamaks**—toroidal magnetic confinement chambers that trap superheated plasma using helical magnetic fields (e.g. ITER project in Cadarache, France; SST-1 and ADITYA at IPR Gandhinagar, India).
27. **Mesons** were theoretically predicted in 1935 by Japanese physicist **Hideki Yukawa** as carriers of the strong nuclear force binding nucleons together.
28. The **Positron ($e^+$)**, the antiparticle of the electron, was discovered in cosmic rays in 1932 by **Carl D. Anderson and Victor Hess** (Nobel Prize 1936).
29. **Transuranic elements** (elements with atomic number $Z > 92$, beyond Uranium) were synthesized primarily by **Glenn T. Seaborg** and his team at UC Berkeley (Neptunium, Plutonium, Americium, Curium), NOT by Enrico Fermi.
30. A **Cyclotron** is a particle accelerator invented by **Ernest O. Lawrence** that accelerates **charged particles (protons, deuterons, alpha particles)** to high kinetic energies using alternating electric fields and a static magnetic field.
31. A cyclotron **cannot accelerate neutrons** because neutrons are electrically neutral and experience no Lorentz force.
32. A **Scintillation Counter** is an optical radiation detector that utilizes the luminescent flashes produced when ionizing radiation strikes a scintillator crystal (e.g. NaI); used to detect **gamma-emitting radioisotopes in drinking water**.
33. **Ernest Rutherford** famously expressed skepticism before the Royal Society that atomic energy would ever be harnessed industrially, misjudging the commercial viability of mass-energy conversion.
34. Two German physicists (Gunter Nimtz and Alfons Stahlhofen) claimed that microwave photons exhibited superluminal tunneling velocities, attempting to rebut Einstein's speed-of-light postulate.
35. **Radioactivity** is an intrinsic nuclear property of unstable isotopes that undergo spontaneous decay by emitting alpha particles ($^4_2\text{He}$), beta particles (electrons/positrons), and gamma rays.
36. **Monazite sand** is an anhydrous phosphate of thorium and rare-earth elements, representing India's primary nuclear fuel resource ($13.15\text{ million tonnes}$).
37. **Thorium (Th-232)** is fertile, not fissile; it cannot sustain a chain reaction directly. It must capture a neutron in a breeder reactor to transmute into fissile **Uranium-233 ($^{233}_{92}\text{U}$)**.
38. **Advantages of Thorium over Uranium:**
    - Thorium is $\sim 3$ times more abundant in Earth's crust than uranium.
    - Yields more energy per unit mass of mined ore.
    - Operates in thermal breeder cycles without generating long-lived transuranic actinides (like Plutonium, Americium, Curium).
    - Highly proliferation-resistant (produces no weapon-usable plutonium).
39. Non-nuclear fuel elements commonly tested in PSCs: **Cadmium (absorber), Chromium, Calcium, Lead, Helium (inert gas)**. None of these can be used as nuclear fuels.
40. **Enriched Uranium** refers to natural uranium that has undergone isotopic separation (via gas centrifuges or gaseous diffusion) to artificially elevate the concentration of fissile U-235 above its natural $0.7\%$.
41. Nuclear power reactors operating in India: **Tarapur (Maharashtra), Rawatbhata (Rajasthan), Kalpakkam (Tamil Nadu), Narora (Uttar Pradesh), Kakrapar (Gujarat), Kaiga (Karnataka), Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu)**.
42. **Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant** utilizes Russian-designed **VVER-1000** light-water pressurized water reactors (PWRs), using enriched uranium and light water.
43. **Alpha decay ($\alpha$):** Atomic number decreases by 2 ($Z \to Z - 2$) and mass number decreases by 4 ($A \to A - 4$).
44. **Beta-minus decay ($\beta^-$):** A nuclear neutron converts into a proton, emitting an electron and an antineutrino; atomic number increases by 1 ($Z \to Z + 1$), mass number $A$ remains constant (produces **isobars**).
45. **Gamma emission ($\gamma$):** High-energy electromagnetic photon emitted when an excited nucleus de-excites to ground state; $Z$ and $A$ remain completely unchanged.
46. Penetrating power sequence: **$\gamma$ (Gamma) > $\beta$ (Beta) > $\alpha$ (Alpha)**. Ionizing power sequence: **$\alpha$ > $\beta$ > $\gamma$**.
47. **Radioactive Half-Life ($T_{1/2}$):** Time required for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to disintegrate: $T_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\lambda} = \frac{0.693}{\lambda}$.
48. **Carbon-14 Dating:** Used to date organic archaeological specimens up to $\sim 50,000\text{ years}$; half-life of C-14 is **$5,730\text{ years}$**.
49. **Uranium-Lead Dating:** Used to determine the absolute age of geological rocks, meteorites, and the Earth ($\sim 4.54\text{ billion years}$).
50. Medical radioisotopes: **Cobalt-60** (cancer radiotherapy), **Iodine-131** (thyroid disorders and cancer), **Sodium-24** (circulatory blood clots), **Phosphorus-32** (leukemia and blood disorders), **Technetium-99m** (diagnostic imaging).
