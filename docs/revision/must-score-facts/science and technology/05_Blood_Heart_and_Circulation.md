<summary><strong>🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts</strong> <span class="rev-toggle-hint">(Click to Expand)</span></summary>

1. Blood is a specialized fluid connective tissue consisting of **~55% liquid Plasma** and **~45% cellular Formed Elements**; slightly alkaline with a normal physiological **pH of 7.35–7.45**; total blood volume in a healthy adult is **5 to 6 litres** (~7–8% of body weight).
2. Blood plasma is 90–92% water; plasma proteins include **Albumin** (maintains colloidal osmotic / oncotic pressure, ~4 g/dL), **Globulins** ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ — $\gamma$-globulins act as circulating **antibodies**), and **Fibrinogen** (essential for blood clotting); **$\text{Serum} = \text{Plasma} - \text{Clotting Factors (Fibrinogen)}$**.
3. **Erythrocytes (Red Blood Cells / RBCs)**: Normal count is **5.0–5.5 million / $\mathbf{mm^3}$** in males and 4.5–5.0 million / $\text{mm}^3$ in females; mature mammalian RBCs are **biconcave, circular, and enucleated** (lack nucleus, mitochondria, and Golgi bodies; **Camel and Llama are exceptions: their RBCs are oval and nucleated**).
4. Absence of mitochondria forces RBCs to generate ATP exclusively through **anaerobic glycolysis**, preventing them from consuming any of the oxygen they transport.
5. RBCs are produced in the red bone marrow (**Erythropoiesis**); stimulated by the glycoprotein hormone **Erythropoietin (EPO)** secreted by renal juxtaglomerular cells in response to hypoxia.
6. The average lifespan of a human red blood cell is **$\mathbf{\sim 120\text{ days}}$**; worn-out aged RBCs are destroyed by macrophages in the **Spleen**, which is universally designated as the **"Graveyard of RBCs"** (and acts as a reserve "Blood Bank").
7. **Haemoglobin ($Hb$)**: Conjugated chromoprotein; normal concentration is **$14–17\text{ g/dL}$** in adult men and **$12–15\text{ g/dL}$** in women; consists of the protein globin and 4 iron-porphyrin **Haem** groups containing **ferrous iron ($\mathbf{Fe^{2+}}$)**; imparts the red colour to blood.
8. Each molecule of haemoglobin can bind a maximum of **4 molecules of Oxygen ($O_2$)** to form bright red **Oxyhaemoglobin**; deoxygenated blood is darker bluish-red.
9. **Methemoglobin & Blue Baby Syndrome**: When ferrous iron ($Fe^{2+}$) of haemoglobin is oxidized into ferric iron ($Fe^{3+}$), it forms **Methemoglobin**, which cannot bind or carry oxygen; infants ingesting groundwater contaminated with **Nitrates ($NO_3^-$)** suffer from **Methemoglobinemia (Blue Baby Syndrome)**, presenting with acute cyanosis and chocolate-brown blood.
10. **Leukocytes (White Blood Cells / WBCs)**: Total count is **$6,000–8,000/\mathbf{mm^3}$**; nucleated, lack haemoglobin; divided into **Granulocytes** and **Agranulocytes**; the normal ratio of RBC to WBC is **$600:1$**.
11. **Granulocytes**:
12. **Neutrophils (60–65%)**: Most numerous; multi-lobed nucleus; primary phagocytic first-responders that engulf bacteria and cell debris.
13. **Eosinophils / Acidophils (2–3%)**: Bilobed nucleus; release histaminase; counts surge in **allergic reactions and intestinal helminth parasitic infections**.
14. **Basophils (0.5–1%)**: Least numerous; secrete **Histamine** (vasodilator in inflammation), **Serotonin**, and **Heparin** (natural anticoagulant).
15. **Agranulocytes**:
16. **Monocytes (6–8%)**: Largest WBCs; bean/kidney-shaped nucleus; enter tissues to transform into massive phagocytic **Macrophages**.
17. **Lymphocytes (20–25%)**: Large rounded nucleus; responsible for specific adaptive immunity: **B-lymphocytes** (produce humoral antibodies) and **T-lymphocytes** (mature in the thymus, execute cell-mediated immunity).
18. **Thrombocytes (Blood Platelets)**: Normal count is **$\mathbf{1.5–4.5\text{ lakh}}/\mathbf{mm^3}$**; non-nucleated protoplasmic cell fragments budded from giant marrow **Megakaryocytes**; lifespan 7–10 days; aggregate at wound sites and release **Thromboplastin** to trigger blood clotting; depleted drastically in dengue fever.
19. **Blood Clotting (Coagulation Cascade)**:
20. Step 1: Ruptured tissues and platelets release **Thromboplastin / Thrombokinase**.
21. Step 2: Prothrombin is converted to active **Thrombin** in the presence of **Calcium ions ($\mathbf{Ca^{2+}}$ / Factor IV)** and thrombokinase.
22. Step 3: Thrombin converts soluble **Fibrinogen** into insoluble **Fibrin polymer threads**, forming a network that traps blood cells to produce a clot (**Thrombus**).
23. **Vitamin K** is indispensable for hepatic synthesis of four essential clotting factors: **Factor II (Prothrombin), Factor VII, Factor IX, and Factor X**.
24. **Heparin** is a natural conjugated polysaccharide anticoagulant secreted by basophils and mast cells; prevents intravascular clotting inside healthy blood vessels.
25. In clinical blood banks, blood clotting in collection bags is prevented by adding calcium-chelating chemical anticoagulants: **Sodium Citrate, EDTA, or CPD (Citrate Phosphate Dextrose)**.
26. **ABO Blood Group System**: Discovered by Austrian biologist **Karl Landsteiner (1900–1901)**; blood groups ($A, B, AB, O$) are determined by specific oligosaccharide **antigens on the surface of RBC membranes** and complementary **antibodies in blood plasma**:
27. **Group A**: Has A antigen on RBCs; anti-B antibody in plasma.
28. **Group B**: Has B antigen on RBCs; anti-A antibody in plasma.
29. **Group AB**: Has **both A and B antigens** on RBCs; **NEITHER anti-A nor anti-B antibody** in plasma.
30. **Group O**: Has **NEITHER A nor B antigen** on RBCs; **BOTH anti-A and anti-B antibodies** in plasma.
31. Transfusion rules: **O Negative ($\mathbf{O^-}$)** is the **Universal Donor** for red-cell transfusions (lacks A, B, and Rh antigens, so donor RBCs cannot be attacked by recipient antibodies); **AB Positive ($\mathbf{AB^+}$)** is the **Universal Recipient** (lacks all anti-A, anti-B, and anti-Rh antibodies in plasma, so cannot attack transfused red cells).
32. **Rh Factor (Rhesus Antigen / Antigen D)**: Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner and Alexander S. Wiener (1940)** in the Rhesus macaque monkey; present on RBCs of ~85% of humans (Rh-positive); ~15% are Rh-negative.
33. **Erythroblastosis Fetalis (Haemolytic Disease of the Newborn)**: Occurs when an **Rh-negative mother** conceives an **Rh-positive fetus**; fetal Rh antigens leak into maternal blood during first delivery, sensitizing the mother to produce maternal anti-Rh antibodies (**IgG class**); during a subsequent Rh-positive pregnancy, maternal anti-Rh IgG crosses the placenta and hemolyses fetal erythrocytes, causing severe fatal anaemia, kernicterus, and hydrops fetalis; prevented by administering **Anti-D Rh-immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)** to the mother within 72 hours of delivering an Rh-positive baby.
34. **Bombay Blood Group (hh Phenotype)**: Rare blood phenotype discovered in Mumbai by **Dr. Y. M. Bhende (1952)**; individuals lack the universal precursor **H-gene**, so cannot biosynthesise A or B antigens; tests as Group O in routine ABO grouping, but serum contains anti-A, anti-B, and **anti-H antibodies**; can donate blood to anyone in the ABO system, but can **receive blood ONLY from another Bombay blood group donor**.
35. The human heart is a hollow, muscular, myogenic organ situated in the mediastinum of the thoracic cavity between the lungs, tilted slightly to the left; enclosed within a double-walled fibro-serous sac called the **Pericardium** filled with lubricating pericardial fluid.
36. The human heart contains **$\mathbf{4\text{ chambers}}$**: two thin-walled superior receiving chambers called **Atria (Auricles)** and two thick-walled inferior pumping chambers called **Ventricles**.
37. The **Left Ventricle** possesses the thickest, most muscular myocardial wall (about 3 times thicker than the right ventricle) because it must pump blood at high pressure into the **Aorta** across the entire systemic body circuit.
38. Internal Heart Valves:
39. **Tricuspid Valve**: Possesses 3 muscular cusps; guards the right atrio-ventricular orifice between the right atrium and right ventricle.
40. **Bicuspid Valve (Mitral Valve)**: Possesses 2 muscular cusps; guards the left atrio-ventricular orifice between the left atrium and left ventricle.
41. **Semilunar Valves**: Guard the exit of the right ventricle into the **Pulmonary Artery** (Pulmonary semilunar valve) and the exit of the left ventricle into the **Aorta** (Aortic semilunar valve); prevent backflow into ventricles during diastole.
42. Valve cusps are tethered to papillary muscles in the ventricular walls by tough, inextensible fibrous cords called **Chordae Tendineae ("Heart Strings")**, preventing cusps from inverting into atria during high-pressure ventricular systole.
43. **Nodal Conduction System (Myogenic Autonomic Rhythmicity)**:
44. **SA Node (Sino-Atrial Node / "Heart of Hearts")**: Located in the upper-right corner of the right atrium near the entry of the superior vena cava; generates intrinsic rhythmic action potentials at **70–75 beats/minute**; functions as the natural **Pacemaker of the Heart**.
45. **AV Node (Atrio-Ventricular Node)**: Located in the lower-left corner of the right atrium near the inter-atrial septum; acts as a relay station, introducing a crucial **0.1-second delay** to allow atria to empty completely before ventricular contraction begins; called the **Pacesetter**.
46. **Bundle of His (AV Bundle)**: Specialized muscle fascicle descending down the interventricular septum, bifurcating into Right and Left Bundle Branches.
47. **Purkinje Fibres**: Microscopic conduction fibres spreading rapidly throughout the ventricular myocardium to trigger coordinated contraction from the cardiac apex upward.
48. If the natural SA node pacemaker fails, an artificial battery-powered **Electronic Pacemaker** (invented by Wilson Greatbatch, containing a lithium-iodine battery) is surgically implanted under the skin to stimulate the heart electrically.
49. The **Cardiac Cycle** lasts **$\mathbf{0.8\text{ seconds}}$** at a normal resting rate of 72 beats/minute:
50. **Atrial Systole**: 0.1 second (atria contract, topping up ventricles).
51. **Ventricular Systole**: 0.3 second (ventricles contract at high pressure, pumping blood into aorta and pulmonary artery).
52. **Joint Diastole (Complete Cardiac Diastole)**: 0.4 second (all four chambers relax simultaneously and refill passively).
53. **Stroke Volume**: Volume of blood pumped out by each ventricle during each individual cardiac cycle = **$\mathbf{\sim 70\text{ mL}}$**.
54. **Cardiac Output**: Volume of blood pumped by each ventricle per minute:
55. **Heart Sounds Auscultated via Stethoscope**:
56. **First Heart Sound ("LUBB")**: Low-pitched, booming, longer duration (~0.15s); produced by the sudden, simultaneous **closure of the Atrioventricular Valves (Tricuspid and Bicuspid)** at the onset of ventricular systole.
57. **Second Heart Sound ("DUPP")**: Higher-pitched, sharp, shorter duration (~0.10s); produced by the rapid **closure of the Semilunar Valves** at the onset of ventricular diastole.
58. **Logic:** Heart sounds are produced by the snapping shut of heart valves, **not** by the contraction of muscle tissue itself.
59. **Electrocardiogram (ECG)**: Graphical recording of the electrical vector voltages generated by the heart during cardiac cycles; invented by Dutch physician **Willem Einthoven (Father of ECG, Nobel Prize 1924)**:
60. **P Wave**: Represents **Atrial Depolarization** (electrical excitation spreading from SA node across atria, preceding atrial contraction).
61. **QRS Complex**: Represents **Ventricular Depolarization** (rapid electrical spread triggering ventricular systole).
62. **T Wave**: Represents **Ventricular Repolarization** (return of ventricles from excitation to resting electrical state).
63. **Blood Pressure (BP)**: Lateral hydrostatic pressure exerted by circulating blood against the elastic walls of major systemic arteries; measured in **mm Hg** using a **Sphygmomanometer** placed over the brachial artery at the level of the heart:
64. **Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP)**: Peak arterial pressure during left ventricular contraction = **$\mathbf{120\text{ mm Hg}}$**.
65. **Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP)**: Minimum baseline arterial pressure during ventricular relaxation = **$\mathbf{80\text{ mm Hg}}$**.
66. **Standard Normal Resting BP**: **$\mathbf{120/80\text{ mm Hg}}$**.
67. **Pulse Pressure**: $\text{Systolic} - \text{Diastolic} = 120 - 80 = \mathbf{40\text{ mm Hg}}$.
68. **Hypertension**: Sustained resting arterial pressure $\ge 140/90\text{ mm Hg}$; damages kidneys, retina, and coronary vessels.
69. **Arteries vs Veins Anatomical Distinctions**:
70. **Arteries**: Carry blood **away** from the heart under high pulsatile pressure; walls are thick, muscular, and rich in elastic fibres (thick tunica media); deep-seated; **lack internal valves**; carry bright red oxygenated blood (**Exception: The Pulmonary Artery carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs**).
71. **Veins**: Carry blood **toward** the heart under low continuous pressure; walls are thin with a wide lumen; superficial; **contain crescent-shaped Semilunar Valves** to prevent backflow of blood under gravity; carry dark deoxygenated blood (**Exception: The Pulmonary Veins carry fully oxygenated blood from the lungs into the left atrium**).
72. **Capillaries**: Microscopic microscopic vessels whose walls consist of a single layer of endothelial cells; site of all nutrient, gas, and metabolic exchange between blood and tissue interstitial fluid.
73. **Double Circulation in Humans**: Blood passes through the heart **twice** to complete one full systemic circuit:
74. **Pulmonary Circuit**: $\text{Right Ventricle} \xrightarrow[\text{Deoxygenated}]{\text{Pulmonary Artery}} \text{Lungs} \xrightarrow[\text{Oxygenated}]{\text{Pulmonary Veins}} \text{Left Atrium}$.
75. **Systemic Circuit**: $\text{Left Ventricle} \xrightarrow[\text{Oxygenated}]{\text{Aorta}} \text{Body Tissues} \xrightarrow[\text{Deoxygenated}]{\text{Venae Cavae}} \text{Right Atrium}$.
76. **The Hepatic Portal System**: Specialized venous pathway in which venous blood draining the stomach, pancreas, spleen, and intestines does **not** return directly to the inferior vena cava, but is collected by the **Hepatic Portal Vein** and delivered to capillary sinusoids of the **Liver**; ensures absorbed toxins, glucose, and nutrients are processed and detoxified before reaching the systemic heart.
77. **Coronary Circulation**: The heart myocardium does not absorb nutrients or oxygen directly from blood passing through its chambers; it is supplied exclusively by the **Right and Left Coronary Arteries** (the very first branches arising from the root of the ascending aorta); coronary venous blood returns directly to the right atrium via the **Coronary Sinus**.
78. **Cardiovascular Pathology**:
79. **Atherosclerosis**: Deposition of cholesterol, calcium, and fibrous tissue forming hard atheromatous plaques within arterial walls, narrowing the lumen.
80. **Arteriosclerosis**: Hardening and loss of elasticity of arterial walls due to calcification.
81. **Angina Pectoris**: Severe acute substernal chest pain radiating to the left shoulder and arm caused by transient cardiac ischemia (inadequate oxygen supply to heart muscle).
82. **Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack)**: Sudden acute thrombotic occlusion of a coronary artery, causing irreversible ischemic necrosis (death) of a patch of myocardium.
83. **Lymphatic System & Lymph (Tissue Fluid / Interstitial Fluid)**:
84. As blood flows through arterial capillary beds, hydrostatic pressure forces fluid, water-soluble nutrients, and ions out through capillary pores into intercellular spaces, forming **Interstitial / Tissue Fluid**.
85. Interstitial fluid drained by specialized blind-ended **Lymphatic Capillaries** is termed **Lymph**.
86. **Chemical Composition of Lymph**: Straw-coloured, transparent fluid identical to blood plasma **minus red blood cells, platelets, and large plasma proteins (albumin/fibrinogen)**; contains abundant **Lymphocytes**.
87. **Circulation of Lymph**: Lymphatic capillaries coalesce into lymphatic vessels resembling veins with numerous valves; drain into two large terminal trunks: the **Right Lymphatic Duct** (drains right upper quadrant) and the **Thoracic Duct (Left Lymphatic Duct)** (drains remaining 75% of body); both empty directly into the **Subclavian Veins** at the base of the neck.
88. **Lymph Nodes**: Small oval glandular encapsulated filter organs distributed along lymphatic channels (in neck, armpits, groin, mesentery); filter lymph, trap circulating bacteria, viruses, and metastasizing cancer cells; packed with lymphocytes and plasma cells that mount adaptive immune responses.
89. **The Spleen (Pliha / तिल्ली)**:
90. Largest single mass of lymphatic tissue in the human body; located in the left hypochondriac region of the abdomen beneath the diaphragm.
91. Contains **Red Pulp** (vascular sinusoids that filter blood, remove old worn-out erythrocytes and blood-borne pathogens) and **White Pulp** (lymphoid sheaths housing T and B lymphocytes).
92. Functions: **Graveyard of RBCs**, **Blood Bank** (can contract to release stored red blood cells into systemic circulation during acute haemorrhage), and major antibody-producing lymphoid organ.
93. **Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)**: Emergency life-saving procedure comprising chest compressions (depth 5–6 cm at a rate of 100–120 compressions/minute) combined with artificial rescue breaths to manually preserve blood circulation to the brain and vital organs during cardiac arrest.

</details>

Which blood vessel collects nutrient-rich venous blood from the stomach and intestines and transports it directly to the liver?
A. Renal vein  
B. Hepatic artery  
C. Hepatic portal vein  
D. Inferior vena cava  

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Logic:** The hepatic portal vein carries venous blood containing newly absorbed gastrointestinal nutrients directly into the liver sinusoids.
