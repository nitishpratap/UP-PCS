## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Erythrocytes (Red Blood Cells / RBCs)**: Normal count is **5.0–5.5 million / $\mathbf{mm^3}$** in males and 4.5–5.0 million / $\text{mm}^3$ in females; mature mammalian RBCs are **biconcave, circular, and enucleated** (lack nucleus, mitochondria, and Golgi bodies; **Camel and Llama are exceptions: their RBCs are oval and nucleated**).
2. **Haemoglobin ($Hb$)**: Conjugated chromoprotein; normal concentration is **$14–17\text{ g/dL}$** in adult men and **$12–15\text{ g/dL}$** in women; consists of the protein globin and 4 iron-porphyrin **Haem** groups containing **ferrous iron ($\mathbf{Fe^{2+}}$)**; imparts the red colour to blood.
3. **Methemoglobin & Blue Baby Syndrome**: When ferrous iron ($Fe^{2+}$) of haemoglobin is oxidized into ferric iron ($Fe^{3+}$), it forms **Methemoglobin**, which cannot bind or carry oxygen; infants ingesting groundwater contaminated with **Nitrates ($NO_3^-$)** suffer from **Methemoglobinemia (Blue Baby Syndrome)**, presenting with acute cyanosis and chocolate-brown blood.
4. **Leukocytes (White Blood Cells / WBCs)**: Total count is **$6,000–8,000/\mathbf{mm^3}$**; nucleated, lack haemoglobin; divided into **Granulocytes** and **Agranulocytes**; the normal ratio of RBC to WBC is **$600:1$**.
5. **Neutrophils (60–65%)**: Most numerous; multi-lobed nucleus; primary phagocytic first-responders that engulf bacteria and cell debris.
6. **Eosinophils / Acidophils (2–3%)**: Bilobed nucleus; release histaminase; counts surge in **allergic reactions and intestinal helminth parasitic infections**.
7. **Basophils (0.5–1%)**: Least numerous; secrete **Histamine** (vasodilator in inflammation), **Serotonin**, and **Heparin** (natural anticoagulant).
8. **Monocytes (6–8%)**: Largest WBCs; bean/kidney-shaped nucleus; enter tissues to transform into massive phagocytic **Macrophages**.
9. **Lymphocytes (20–25%)**: Large rounded nucleus; responsible for specific adaptive immunity: **B-lymphocytes** (produce humoral antibodies) and **T-lymphocytes** (mature in the thymus, execute cell-mediated immunity).
10. **Thrombocytes (Blood Platelets)**: Normal count is **$\mathbf{1.5–4.5\text{ lakh}}/\mathbf{mm^3}$**; non-nucleated protoplasmic cell fragments budded from giant marrow **Megakaryocytes**; lifespan 7–10 days; aggregate at wound sites and release **Thromboplastin** to trigger blood clotting; depleted drastically in dengue fever.
11. **Blood Clotting (Coagulation Cascade)**:
12. **Vitamin K** is indispensable for hepatic synthesis of four essential clotting factors: **Factor II (Prothrombin), Factor VII, Factor IX, and Factor X**.
13. **Heparin** is a natural conjugated polysaccharide anticoagulant secreted by basophils and mast cells; prevents intravascular clotting inside healthy blood vessels.
14. **ABO Blood Group System**: Discovered by Austrian biologist **Karl Landsteiner (1900–1901)**; blood groups ($A, B, AB, O$) are determined by specific oligosaccharide **antigens on the surface of RBC membranes** and complementary **antibodies in blood plasma**:
15. **Group A**: Has A antigen on RBCs; anti-B antibody in plasma.
16. **Group B**: Has B antigen on RBCs; anti-A antibody in plasma.
17. **Group AB**: Has **both A and B antigens** on RBCs; **NEITHER anti-A nor anti-B antibody** in plasma.
18. **Group O**: Has **NEITHER A nor B antigen** on RBCs; **BOTH anti-A and anti-B antibodies** in plasma.
19. **Rh Factor (Rhesus Antigen / Antigen D)**: Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner and Alexander S. Wiener (1940)** in the Rhesus macaque monkey; present on RBCs of ~85% of humans (Rh-positive); ~15% are Rh-negative.
20. **Erythroblastosis Fetalis (Haemolytic Disease of the Newborn)**: Occurs when an **Rh-negative mother** conceives an **Rh-positive fetus**; fetal Rh antigens leak into maternal blood during first delivery, sensitizing the mother to produce maternal anti-Rh antibodies (**IgG class**); during a subsequent Rh-positive pregnancy, maternal anti-Rh IgG crosses the placenta and hemolyses fetal erythrocytes, causing severe fatal anaemia, kernicterus, and hydrops fetalis; prevented by administering **Anti-D Rh-immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)** to the mother within 72 hours of delivering an Rh-positive baby.
21. **Bombay Blood Group (hh Phenotype)**: Rare blood phenotype discovered in Mumbai by **Dr. Y. M. Bhende (1952)**; individuals lack the universal precursor **H-gene**, so cannot biosynthesise A or B antigens; tests as Group O in routine ABO grouping, but serum contains anti-A, anti-B, and **anti-H antibodies**; can donate blood to anyone in the ABO system, but can **receive blood ONLY from another Bombay blood group donor**.
22. **Tricuspid Valve**: Possesses 3 muscular cusps; guards the right atrio-ventricular orifice between the right atrium and right ventricle.
23. **Bicuspid Valve (Mitral Valve)**: Possesses 2 muscular cusps; guards the left atrio-ventricular orifice between the left atrium and left ventricle.
24. **Semilunar Valves**: Guard the exit of the right ventricle into the **Pulmonary Artery** (Pulmonary semilunar valve) and the exit of the left ventricle into the **Aorta** (Aortic semilunar valve); prevent backflow into ventricles during diastole.
25. **Nodal Conduction System (Myogenic Autonomic Rhythmicity)**:
26. **SA Node (Sino-Atrial Node / "Heart of Hearts")**: Located in the upper-right corner of the right atrium near the entry of the superior vena cava; generates intrinsic rhythmic action potentials at **70–75 beats/minute**; functions as the natural **Pacemaker of the Heart**.
27. **AV Node (Atrio-Ventricular Node)**: Located in the lower-left corner of the right atrium near the inter-atrial septum; acts as a relay station, introducing a crucial **0.1-second delay** to allow atria to empty completely before ventricular contraction begins; called the **Pacesetter**.
28. **Bundle of His (AV Bundle)**: Specialized muscle fascicle descending down the interventricular septum, bifurcating into Right and Left Bundle Branches.
29. **Purkinje Fibres**: Microscopic conduction fibres spreading rapidly throughout the ventricular myocardium to trigger coordinated contraction from the cardiac apex upward.
30. **Atrial Systole**: 0.1 second (atria contract, topping up ventricles).
31. **Ventricular Systole**: 0.3 second (ventricles contract at high pressure, pumping blood into aorta and pulmonary artery).
32. **Joint Diastole (Complete Cardiac Diastole)**: 0.4 second (all four chambers relax simultaneously and refill passively).
33. **Stroke Volume**: Volume of blood pumped out by each ventricle during each individual cardiac cycle = **$\mathbf{\sim 70\text{ mL}}$**.

## ⚡ Confused Pairs & Common Examiner Traps

& Negative-Marking Eliminators

1. **RBC vs WBC lifespan**: RBC ~**120 days**; many WBCs are short-lived.
2. **Universal donor/recipient**: O negative ≈ universal donor; AB positive ≈ universal recipient — Rh matters.
3. **Pacemaker**: **SA node** is the natural pacemaker — not AV node (AV is relay).
4. **Pulmonary artery**: Carries **deoxygenated** blood — the classic vessel trap.
5. **Pulmonary vein**: Carries **oxygenated** blood to the left atrium.
6. **Systole vs diastole**: Systole = contraction; diastole = relaxation.
7. **Lymph**: Returns tissue fluid; contains lymphocytes — it is **not** the same as blood plasma with RBCs.
8. **Haemophilia**: Clotting-factor disorder — not a low-RBC anaemia by definition.
9. **Blood group antigen**: Group A has A antigen; antibodies are anti-B in plasma.
10. **ECG waves**: P = atrial depolarisation; QRS = ventricular depolarisation; T = ventricular repolarisation.
