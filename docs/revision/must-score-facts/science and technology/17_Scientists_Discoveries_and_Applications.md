## Detailed In-Depth Core Theory

### 9.1 World Physicists & Milestones of Classical & Quantum Mechanics
- **Classical Dynamics:** Sir Isaac Newton's Three Laws of Motion and Universal Gravitation ($F = Gm_1m_2/r^2$) mathematically established terrestrial and celestial mechanics. Galileo Galilei established kinematics, the law of inertia, isochronism of the pendulum, and telescopic discovery of the moons of Jupiter.
- **Electromagnetic Unification:** Michael Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction (1831), establishing that a changing magnetic flux induces an electromotive force. James Clerk Maxwell unified electricity, magnetism, and light into Maxwell's Equations (1865), proving light is an electromagnetic wave. Heinrich Hertz verified radio waves in 1887.
- **Quantum Revolution:** Max Planck solved the ultraviolet catastrophe in 1900 by introducing energy quantization ($E = nh\nu$). Albert Einstein explained the Photoelectric Effect (1905, Nobel 1921), proving light acts as discrete packets of energy (photons). Louis de Broglie established wave-particle duality ($\lambda = h/p$, 1924). Werner Heisenberg formulated the Uncertainty Principle ($\Delta x \Delta p \ge h/4\pi$, 1927). Erwin Schrödinger formulated Wave Mechanics ($i\hbar \partial\psi/\partial t = \hat{H}\psi$, 1926).

### 9.2 Indian Scientific Architects & Institutional Foundation
- **Sir C.V. Raman:** Discovered the Raman Effect on **February 28, 1928** (inelastic photon scattering by molecules causing Stokes and Anti-Stokes frequency shifts). Awarded the **1930 Nobel Prize in Physics** (first Asian science Nobel). Founded Raman Research Institute (RRI, Bengaluru, 1948).
- **Acharya J.C. Bose:** Generated $60\text{ GHz}$ millimeter microwaves in 1895 (predating Marconi's radio patent), invented the Galena crystal detector, founded the Bose Institute (1917), and invented the **Crescograph** measuring plant physiological responses.
- **Satyendra Nath Bose:** Formulated Bose-Einstein Statistics (1924) governing integer-spin particles (**Bosons**), leading to the theoretical prediction of the **Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC)**.
- **Meghnad Saha:** Formulated the **Saha Ionization Equation** (1920) linking stellar spectral lines to stellar temperatures and pressures. Founded the Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics (SINP) and chaired the Calendar Reform Committee (1952) introducing the Saka National Calendar.
- **Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar:** Formulated the **Chandrasekhar Limit** ($M \le 1.44 M_\odot$) in 1930; awarded the 1983 Nobel Prize in Physics. NASA named the Chandra X-ray Observatory in his honor.
- **Dr. Homi J. Bhabha:** Architect of India's nuclear program; founded TIFR (1945), AEET/BARC (1954), and formulated India's 3-stage nuclear power plan.
- **Dr. Vikram Sarabhai:** Architect of India's space program; founded PRL (1947), TERLS (1963), and ISRO (1969).
- **Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam:** Headed SLV-3 project (Rohini-1, 1980); architect of IGMDP (Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul, Nag); 11th President of India.

### 9.3 Inventions, Discoveries & Underlying Scientific Principles
- Every landmark technological breakthrough translates a fundamental physical law into an engineered application:
  - *Electric Motors and Generators:* Direct application of Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction and Lorentz Force ($\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$).
  - *Steam and Internal Combustion Engines:* Conversion of thermal energy into mechanical work governed by the Laws of Thermodynamics.
  - *Optical Fibers and Endoscopes:* Total Internal Reflection when light travels from denser to rarer media at an incidence angle greater than the critical angle ($i > \theta_c$).
  - *Lasers:* Stimulated emission of photons from atoms inverted to a higher energy state (population inversion).
  - *Electron Microscope:* Utilizes the wave properties of high-speed electrons ($\lambda = h/mv$), achieving resolving powers thousand times greater than optical light.

### 9.4 Defence Technology: Missiles, BMD, Aerial & Naval Combat Platforms
- **Ballistic vs Cruise Trajectories:** Ballistic missiles follow high-altitude parabolic arcs dictated by gravity after their rocket fuel burns out. Cruise missiles maintain continuous aerodynamic lift using jet engines, cruising at subsonic or supersonic speeds within the lower atmosphere to evade radar detection.
- **Propulsion Chemistry in Missiles:**
  - *Solid Propellants:* HTPB mixed with ammonium perchlorate oxidizer; allows long-term storage in sealed canisters and instant firing (Agni-I, II, III, IV, V, Agni P, Pralay).
  - *Liquid Propellants:* Hypergolic combinations (UDMH + $N_2O_4$) used in Prithvi; provides throttle control but requires pre-launch fueling.
  - *Ramjets:* Used in Akash and BrahMos; relies on forward vehicle velocity to ram and compress atmospheric air, saving weight by eliminating internal air compressors and oxidizer tanks.
- **Multi-Tiered BMD Architecture:** Designed to neutralize hostile nuclear-tipped ballistic missiles. Exo-atmospheric interception (PAD/AD-1) occurs outside the atmosphere ($>50\text{ km}$), whereas endo-atmospheric interception (AAD) engages targets inside the atmosphere ($15–30\text{ km}$).
- **Naval and Aerial Platforms:** INS Arihant provides secure underwater second-strike nuclear deterrence using K-15/K-4 SLBMs. LCA Tejas provides indigenous air superiority with delta-wing agility and composite materials.

### 9.5 Global Disarmament, Arms Control & Military Alliances
- **Export Control Regimes:** Four multilateral non-treaty cartels (MTCR, HCoC, Australia Group, Wassenaar Arrangement) prevent the clandestine transfer of dual-use technologies, delivery systems, and materials that can be weaponized into WMDs. India is a full member of all four, bolstering its strategic credentials as a responsible nuclear power.
- **Bilateral Treaties:** The expiration of the New START treaty on 5 February 2026 marks the end of formal bilateral nuclear limits between the US and Russia. NATO's expansion to 32 members (Sweden and Finland) marks a historic reorganization of European collective security.

### 9.6 Space Launch Vehicles, Rocket Propulsion & Satellite Systems
- **Rocket Propulsion:** Governed by Newton's Third Law and the Conservation of Linear Momentum. Multi-stage rockets drop dead propellant tank mass in flight to maximize final orbital velocity ($v = v_e \ln(m_0/m_f)$).
- **ISRO Launch Vehicle Hierarchy:**
  - *SLV-3:* 4-stage all-solid experimental rocket.
  - *PSLV:* 4-stage (Solid-Liquid-Solid-Liquid) workhorse for Sun-synchronous Earth observation satellites.
  - *GSLV Mk II:* 3-stage vehicle featuring an indigenous Cryogenic Upper Stage ($LH_2 + LOX$ below $-150^\circ\text{C}$).
  - *LVM3 (Fatboy):* Heavy-lift vehicle capable of launching 4 tonnes to GTO and 8 tonnes to LEO.
  - *SSLV:* Quick-turnaround 3-stage solid launcher for small satellite commercial launches.
- **Orbital Classes:** Geostationary orbits ($35,786\text{ km}$, 24 hr period, zero inclination) maintain a stationary footprint over Earth's equator. Sun-synchronous polar orbits (600–800 km, ~98° inclination) allow daily Earth imaging at consistent solar illumination.

### 9.7 Lunar, Solar, Planetary Exploration & Deep Space Observatories
- **Lunar Missions:** Chandrayaan-1 confirmed lunar water. Chandrayaan-3 achieved the historic first soft landing near the lunar South Pole on 23 August 2023 (*Shiv Shakti Point*). Chandrayaan-4 will perform lunar sample return; Chandrayaan-5 (LUPEX) will explore water ice in permanently shadowed craters.
- **Interplanetary & Solar Observatories:** Mangalyaan reached Mars on its maiden attempt. Aditya-L1 monitors solar flares from Sun-Earth L1. XPoSat measures X-ray polarization. Ground-based astronomical research is spearheaded by MACE at Hanle, Ladakh ($4,270\text{ m}$).

### 9.8 Nanotechnology, Nanomaterials & Quantum Confinement

#### 1. Foundational Milestones & Terminology
- **Conceptual Inception:** On 29 December 1959, American theoretical physicist **Richard Feynman** delivered his legendary lecture at Caltech titled *"There's Plenty of Room at the Bottom"*, proposing the manipulation and control of individual atoms and molecules.
- **Formal Definition:** In 1974, Professor **Norio Taniguchi** of Tokyo Science University first formally coined and defined the term **"Nanotechnology"**:
  $$\text{Nanotechnology consists of the processing, separation, consolidation, and deformation of materials by one atom or one molecule.}$$
- **First Systematic Textbook:** In 1986, **Dr. K. Eric Drexler** published *'Engines of Creation: The Coming Era of Nanotechnology'*, popularizing molecular nanotechnology and mechanosynthesis.
- **Dimensional Threshold:** The nanoscale is defined as the dimensional range from **1 to 100 nanometres** ($1\text{ nm} = 10^{-9}\text{ m}$). A particle having at least one dimension less than $10^{-7}\text{ m}$ ($100\text{ nm}$) exhibits quantum nanoscale properties.
- **Nanoscale Visualization Tools:** Optical microscopes cannot resolve nanoscale objects due to diffraction limits ($\lambda/2 \approx 200\text{ nm}$). Nanoscale surfaces are analyzed using:
  1. **Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM):** Invented by Gerd Binnig and Heinrich Rohrer (Nobel Prize 1986), measuring quantum electron tunneling currents.
  2. **Atomic Force Microscope (AFM):** Measures interatomic forces between a cantilevered tip and the sample surface.
- **Dominant Physical Force:** At the nanoscale, gravitational forces become negligible while **friction, electrostatic forces, and van der Waals interactions** dominate material mechanics.

#### 2. Quantum Confinement Regimes
When the physical dimensions of a semiconductor or conductor approach the de Broglie wavelength of an electron, quantum confinement occurs, transforming continuous energy bands into discrete quantized states:
1. **Quantum Well (2D Structure):** 1 dimension confined to the nano range, 2 dimensions macroscopic. Example: semiconductor ultra-thin epitaxial films, heterostructures.
2. **Quantum Wire (1D Structure):** 2 dimensions confined to the nano range, 1 dimension macroscopic. Example: semiconductor nanowires, carbon nanotubes.
3. **Quantum Dot (0D Structure):** All 3 dimensions confined to the nanoscale ($1.5–10\text{ nm}$). Electrons are localized in all three spatial coordinates, behaving like "artificial atoms" with completely discrete energy levels.
   - Exhibit size-tunable bandgaps and photoluminescence: smaller quantum dots emit higher-energy blue light, while larger dots emit lower-energy red light.

#### 3. Four Generations of Nanotechnology Products
- **Generation I (Passive Nanostructures - 2000 onwards):** Stationary physicochemical properties. Examples: aerosols, metal-oxide nanoparticles (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide in sunscreens and cosmetics), colloids, reinforced polymer nanocomposites.
- **Generation II (Active Nanostructures - 2005 onwards):** Dynamic, stimuli-responsive behavior. Examples: targeted drug delivery vehicles, 3D transistors, smart actuators, sensor amplifiers.
- **Generation III (Systems of Nanosystems - 2010 onwards):** Heterogeneous self-assembling networks. Examples: 3D networking, nanorobotics, bio-hybrid arrays.
- **Generation IV (Molecular Nanosystems - 2020 onwards):** Atomically precise molecular manufacturing, synthetic molecular motors, and mechanochemical factories.

#### 4. Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs) & Industrial Initiatives
- **Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs):** Cylindrical allotropes of carbon with sp$^2$ hybridized graphene lattice.
  - *Medical Delivery:* High specific surface area allows conjugation with antibodies, antigens, and chemotherapeutic drugs, crossing cellular membranes directly.
  - *Biodegradability:* While non-functionalized CNTs can induce platelet activation and clotting, functionalized CNTs are completely biodegradable by natural enzymes (e.g., horseradish peroxidase, myeloperoxidase) and environmental microorganisms.
- **India's First Nano-Cellulose Plant (ICAR-CIRCOT Mumbai):** Utilizes cotton linters and cotton agricultural waste to produce industrial nano-cellulose, demonstrating circular agro-biotechnology.
- **IIT Roorkee Nanofabrication Facility (2025):** Established as Uttarakhand's primary semiconductor nanofabrication hub, synergizing India's Semiconductor Mission, Nano Mission, and National Quantum Mission.

### 9.9 Laser & Maser Technology

#### 1. LASER (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation)
- **Physical Mechanism:** Operates on the quantum process of **stimulated emission** (first predicted theoretically by Albert Einstein in 1917).
  - An incoming photon with energy $h\nu = E_2 - E_1$ stimulates an excited electron to transition to a lower energy state, emitting a second identical photon having the exact same frequency, phase, direction, and polarization.
  - Requires **population inversion** (achieved through optical, electrical, or chemical pumping into a metastable state) within an optical resonant cavity.
- **Core Beam Characteristics:**
  1. *High Monochromaticity:* Extremely narrow spectral bandwidth ($\Delta \lambda \to 0$).
  2. *High Directionality & Collimation:* Negligible divergence over vast propagation distances.
  3. *High Temporal & Spatial Coherence:* All wave packets vibrate strictly in phase.
  4. *High Intensity:* Capability to focus immense optical energy onto diffraction-limited microscopic spots.
- **Theodore Maiman & The Ruby Laser (1960):**
  - On 16 May 1960, **Theodore H. Maiman** at Hughes Research Laboratories (Malibu, California) operated the **world's first functional laser**.
  - Gain medium: Synthetic ruby crystal (corundum $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ doped with chromium ions $\text{Cr}^{3+}$).
  - Emitted optical output: High-intensity coherent pulses of **deep red light at 694.3 nm**.
- **Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics (Garching, Munich, Germany):** World-renowned premier center for fundamental research in quantum optics, laser spectroscopy, and attosecond physics.

#### 2. MASER (Microwave Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation)
- **Pioneering Development:** Built in 1953 by **Charles H. Townes, James P. Gordon, and Herbert J. Zeiger** at Columbia University, utilizing ammonia gas molecules emitting coherent radiation in the microwave spectrum ($24\text{ GHz}$).
- **Applications:**
  - Standard frequency references in ultra-precise **atomic clocks**.
  - Ultra-low-noise microwave amplifiers in deep-space ground communications networks and radio astronomical observatories.
- **Nobel Prize (1964):** Awarded jointly to Charles H. Townes (USA), Nikolai G. Basov (USSR), and Aleksandr M. Prokhorov (USSR) for foundational research in quantum electronics leading to masers and lasers.

### 9.10 Liquid Crystals, Advanced Display Technologies & Rare Earth Phosphors

#### 1. Liquid Crystal Mesophase
- **Thermodynamic Mesophase:** A distinct, stable state of matter intermediate between a three-dimensional crystalline solid and an isotropic liquid.
- **Physical Anisotropy:** Possesses fluid mobility like a liquid while retaining orientational molecular order (optical, electrical, and magnetic anisotropy) like a solid crystal, without a 3D spatial lattice.
- **Molecular Architecture:** Typically consists of rigid, elongated rod-like (*calamitic*) molecules featuring a permanent dipole moment or easily polarizable aromatic moieties.
- **Liquid Crystal Displays (LCDs):** Operates via the electro-optic effect: an externally applied electric field alters the spatial orientation of liquid crystal molecules, modulating the transmission and rotation of polarized light passing through cross-polarizer filters. Requires an active backlighting unit (CCFL or LED).

#### 2. Organic Light Emitting Diodes (OLED) vs LCD
- **Operating Principle:** OLED utilizes electroluminescent organic thin films that emit light directly when an electric current passes through them (self-emissive display).
- **Decisive Advantages of OLED over LCD:**
  1. *Flexible Substrates:* Can be fabricated on flexible plastic (polyimide) polymers rather than rigid glass substrates, enabling curved and foldable displays.
  2. *Wearable Smart Textiles:* Enables roll-up displays embedded directly into garments and flexible fabrics.
  3. *Transparent Displays:* When inactive, the thin organic emitting layers and transparent electrodes permit see-through display panels.
  4. *Superior Contrast & Energy Efficiency:* True black levels (unlit pixels draw zero power), wider viewing angles, and ultra-fast response times.

#### 3. Display Resolutions & Rare Earth Phosphors
- **Full HD Resolution (1080p):** Characterized by **$1920 \times 1080$ pixels** displayed in the standard **16:9** aspect ratio ($1,920$ horizontal pixels by $1,080$ vertical pixels).
- **Rare Earth Elements (REEs) in Displays:**
  - Transition metal and lanthanide elements including **Europium ($Eu$)**, **Terbium ($Tb$)**, and **Yttrium ($Y$)** are essential in television screens and display monitors.
  - Phosphors containing $Eu^{3+}$ emit pure red light, $Tb^{3+}$ emits bright green light, and $Y_2O_3:Eu$ compounds provide exceptional color reproduction and durability due to their long-lived **phosphorescent and luminescent** electronic states.

### 9.11 Indian Polar Research Programmes & Deep-Sea Oceanography

#### 1. Indian Antarctic Programme
- **Institutional Custodian:** Administered by the **National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR)**, Vasco da Gama, Goa under the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES).
- **Inception:** First Indian Antarctic scientific expedition launched in **1981** under the leadership of Dr. S.Z. Qasim. India signed the Antarctic Treaty in 1983.
- **Permanent Research Stations in Antarctica:**
  1. **Dakshin Gangotri (1983–84):** India's 1st permanent research base, situated on an ice shelf. Decommissioned and abandoned in 1990 after being submerged under continuous snow accumulation.
  2. **Maitri (1989):** India's 2nd permanent base, established in the rocky, ice-free Schirmacher Oasis. Features year-round freshwater supply from Lake Priyadarshini.
  3. **Bharati (2012):** India's 3rd and newest base, commissioned on **18 March 2012** at Larsemann Hills overlooking Prydz Bay. Constructed using **134 prefabricated shipping containers**, equipped with state-of-the-art automated environmental laboratories.

#### 2. Indian Arctic Programme & IndARC
- **Himadri Station (2008):** India launched its first scientific expedition to the Arctic in 2007 and inaugurated its dedicated research base **Himadri** at the International Arctic Research Base in **Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard, Norway** in July 2008.
- **IndARC Moored Underwater Observatory (2014):**
  - India's 1st multi-sensor underwater moored observatory deployed on **23 July 2014** in Kongsfjorden fjord, Svalbard (midway between Norway and the North Pole).
  - Primary scientific objective: Long-term monitoring of Arctic oceanographic conditions to understand teleconnections between Arctic ice melting and the dynamics of the **Indian Summer Monsoon**.
- **The Polar Code (2017):** International Maritime Organization (IMO) mandatory code establishing global design, construction, equipment, and environmental safety standards for commercial and research ships operating in harsh polar waters (in force 1 January 2017 under SOLAS and MARPOL conventions).

#### 3. Deep-Sea Exploration & Physical Oceanography
- **Samudrayaan Mission (Deep Ocean Mission):**
  - Spearheaded by MoES and developed by the **National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT), Chennai**.
  - Develops **MATSYA 6000**, an advanced self-propelled 4th-generation manned submersible capable of carrying **3 researchers to an ocean depth of 6,000 metres** for 12 hours (with 96 hours of emergency life support) to survey polymetallic nodules, hydrothermal vents, and benthic biodiversity.
- **CANI Submarine OFC Project:**
  - Chennai-Andaman & Nicobar Islands undersea optical fibre cable project inaugurated on **10 August 2020** by PM Narendra Modi.
  - Spans $2,312\text{ km}$ across the Bay of Bengal, delivering high-speed broadband to Port Blair, Swaraj Dweep, Little Andaman, Car Nicobar, Kamorta, Great Nicobar, Long Island, and Rangat.
- **Oceanographic Vertical Gradient Zones (Clines):**
  - **Pycnocline:** Ocean layer where **density** changes most rapidly with depth. Acts as a dynamic barrier preventing vertical mixing between upper warm surface waters and cold abyssal depths.
  - **Halocline:** Ocean layer where **salinity** changes steeply with depth.
  - **Thermocline:** Transitional layer characterized by a rapid decrease in **temperature** with depth.
  - **Ecocline:** A continuous, gradual zone of transition from one biological ecosystem to another without abrupt physical boundaries.

### 9.13 Interdisciplinary Scientific Disciplines & National Research Institutes

#### 1. Core Interdisciplinary Disciplines
- **Cybernetics:** Coined by **Norbert Wiener** in 1948 (*"Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine"*). Explores feedback control mechanisms across mechanical, biological, and computational architectures.
- **Geodesy:** Applied mathematical science measuring the Earth's geometric figure, spatial orientation, gravitational potential field, and temporal crustal geodynamics.
- **Horology:** The science, craftsmanship, and technology of accurate timekeeping and horological instrumentation.
- **Tribology:** Introduced formally by Peter Jost in 1966, encompassing the multidisciplinary science of interacting surfaces in relative motion—principally **friction, lubrication, and wear**.
- **Plasma State (The 4th State of Matter):** High-temperature state where neutral atoms dissociate into free positive ions and electrons. Most abundant form of ordinary baryonic matter in the universe, constituting stellar cores and the Sun.

#### 2. Key National Research Laboratories & Foundations
- **National Physical Laboratory (CSIR-NPL, New Delhi):** Premier custodian of Indian national measurement standards (SI units, national atomic time standard via Cesium clocks).
- **National Chemical Laboratory (CSIR-NCL, Pune):** Founded in 1950, leading advanced organic synthesis, chemical catalysis, and material science.
- **Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR, Mumbai):** Founded on **1 June 1945** by Dr. Homi J. Bhabha with Sir Dorabji Tata Trust; cradle of India's atomic energy and space science initiatives.
- **Central Electrochemical Research Institute (CSIR-CECRI, Karaikudi, TN):** Founded in 1948 and dedicated on **14 January 1953** by Vice-President Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, Dr. R.M. Alagappa Chettiar, Dr. Shanti Swaroop Bhatnagar, and Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru; South Asia's premier electrochemical research establishment.
- **Indian Institute of Science (IISc, Bengaluru):** Conceived through discussions between Swami Vivekananda and **Jamshedji Tata**, established in **1909**; India's foremost research institution.
- **National Centre for Earth Science Studies (NCESS, Thiruvananthapuram):** Established in 1978 under MoES, pioneering earth system sciences and lithosphere-atmosphere modeling.
- **Central Drug Research Institute (CSIR-CDRI, Lucknow):** Founded in 1951, center for indigenous pharmacological innovations.
- **Doppler Weather Radar (Mukteshwar, Nainital):** Installed in January 2021 under MoES Integrated Himalayan Meteorological Project (IHMP) to provide early warning of cloudbursts, severe thunderstorms, and heavy precipitation.

#### 3. Applied Metrology & Nuclear Technology Milestones
- **Metric System Adoption in India:** The decimalized metric system was officially enforced on **1 April 1957** (introducing the *"Naya Paisa"* system of 100 paise to one rupee).
- **Dry Cell (Leclanché Cell) Operation:**
  - *Anode (Negative Electrode):* Zinc ($Zn$) outer metallic casing ($Zn \to Zn^{2+} + 2e^-$).
  - *Cathode (Positive Electrode):* Central carbon (graphite) rod surrounded by a porous paste of manganese dioxide ($MnO_2$) and carbon black.
  - *Electrolyte:* Moist paste of ammonium chloride ($NH_4Cl$) and zinc chloride ($ZnCl_2$).
- **Timekeeping Mechanisms:**
  - *Quartz Watches:* Regulated by a quartz crystal oscillator exploiting the **inverse piezoelectric effect** (voltage generates mechanical vibration at $32,768\text{ Hz}$).
  - *Automatic Mechanical Watches:* Harness kinetic energy from the wearer's wrist motion via an oscillating eccentric rotor winding the mainspring.
- **Nuclear Weapons Fission History:**
  - Nuclear fission discovered in 1939 by German chemists **Otto Hahn** and **Fritz Strassmann** (bombarding Uranium-235 with thermal neutrons).
  - World War II atomic weapons dropped by the USA:
    1. *"Little Boy"* (Hiroshima, 6 August 1945): Utilized gun-type **Uranium-235** fissile core.
    2. *"Fat Man"* (Nagasaki, 9 August 1945): Utilized implosion-type **Plutonium-239** fissile core.
  - First Heavy Water ($D_2O$) Plant: Commissioned by DAE in **1962 at Nangal, Punjab**.
- **Indian Science Congress Association (ISCA):**
  - Established in 1914 by British chemists **Prof. J.L. Simonsen** and **Prof. P.S. MacMahon**.
  - First session held at the Asiatic Society, Calcutta in January 1914 under the presidency of Sir Asutosh Mukherjee.
  - 108th ISC session held in January 2023 at R.T.M. Nagpur University (*"Science and Technology for Sustainable Development with Women Empowerment"*).
