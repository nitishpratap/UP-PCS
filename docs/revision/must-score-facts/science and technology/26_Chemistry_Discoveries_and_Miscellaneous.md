1. **Antoine Laurent Lavoisier (1743–1794)**: Revered as the **"Father of Modern Chemistry"**. Formulated the fundamental **Law of Conservation of Mass** (1789), proved that combustion and respiration are chemical combinations with oxygen, disproved the ancient phlogiston theory, named the elements Oxygen and Hydrogen, and published the first modern list of chemical elements.
2. **John Dalton (1766–1844)**: Formulated the modern **Atomic Theory (1808)** (matter consists of indivisible atoms); established the **Law of Multiple Proportions** and **Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures**.
3. **Joseph Priestley (1774)** and **Carl Wilhelm Scheele (1772)**: Independently discovered **Oxygen gas ($O_2$)**. Priestley termed it "dephlogisticated air" by heating mercuric oxide; Lavoisier recognized it as an elemental gas and named it oxygen (meaning "acid-former").
4. **Henry Cavendish (1766)**: Discovered **Hydrogen gas ($H_2$)**, calling it "inflammable air" by reacting zinc with dilute sulphuric acid; later proved that burning hydrogen in air produces water, demonstrating that water is a compound, not an element.
5. **Humphry Davy (1778–1829)**: Pioneer of electrochemistry; used electric battery currents to isolate elemental **Sodium ($Na$), Potassium ($K$), Calcium ($Ca$), Magnesium ($Mg$), Strontium ($Sr$), and Barium ($Ba$)**; established that chlorine is an element; invented the **Davy Miner's Safety Lamp** to prevent firedamp explosions in coal mines; discovered the anesthetic properties of nitrous oxide.
6. **Friedrich Wöhler (1828)**: Synthesized **Urea ($NH_2CONH_2$)** by heating inorganic ammonium cyanate ($NH_4CNO$):
7. **Jöns Jacob Berzelius (1779–1848)**: Introduced the modern system of **chemical symbols using Latin letters** ($H, O, C, Fe, Au$); determined accurate atomic weights; discovered the elements Cerium, Selenium, Silicon, and Thorium; coined the terms *catalysis*, *isomerism*, *allotropy*, and *protein*.
8. **August Kekulé (1865)**: Proposed the cyclic hexagonal ring structure of **Benzene ($C_6H_6$)** with alternating double bonds, inspired by a famous daydream of a snake biting its own tail (Ouroboros); established the tetravalency of carbon.
9. **Harold Clayton Urey (1931)**: Discovered **Deuterium ($^2H$ or $D$)**, the heavy isotope of hydrogen, by fractional distillation of liquid hydrogen (Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 1934).
10. **Linus Pauling (1901–1994)**: Formulated the quantum concepts of **orbital hybridization ($sp, sp^2, sp^3$)**, the nature of the chemical bond, the **Pauling Electronegativity Scale** (Fluorine = 4.0), and the $\alpha$-helical secondary structure of proteins (Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 1954; Nobel Peace Prize, 1962).
11. **Acharya Prafulla Chandra Ray (P. C. Ray, 1861–1944)**:
12. Revered as the **"Father of Indian Chemistry"**.
13. Discovered and synthesized **Mercurous Nitrite ($Hg_2(NO_2)_2$)** in 1896, earning global acclaim as the "Master of Nitrites".
14. Founded **Bengal Chemical & Pharmaceutical Works (1901)** in Calcutta, India's first modern indigenous chemical and pharmaceutical enterprise.
15. Authored the comprehensive historical treatise *A History of Hindu Chemistry* (1902).
16. **Sir Shanti Swaroop Bhatnagar (1894–1955)**:
17. Pioneer of magneto-chemistry; invented the high-sensitivity **Bhatnagar-Mathur Magnetic Interference Balance**.
18. First Director-General of the **Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR)**; spearheaded the establishment of national research laboratories across India. The prestigious **SSB Prize** is named in his honor.
19. **Chintamani Nagesa Ramachandra Rao (C. N. R. Rao, b. 1934)**:
20. Internationally recognized solid-state and structural chemist; authority on transition metal oxides, high-temperature superconductivity, and carbon nanomaterials (graphene, nanotubes). Awarded India's highest civilian honor, the **Bharat Ratna (2014)**.
21. **Asima Chatterjee (1917–2006)**:
22. Renowned Indian organic chemist; first woman to receive a Doctor of Science (D.Sc.) degree from an Indian university (Calcutta University, 1944).
23. Pioneered research on vinca alkaloids from *Catharanthus roseus*, developed the anti-epileptic formulation **Ayush-56**, and anti-malarial herbal drugs.
24. **Water Chemistry — Hardness of Water**:
25. **Hard Water**: Water that does not produce lather easily with soap, but forms a sticky, insoluble white precipitate called **Scum** (calcium and magnesium stearates).
26. Caused by the presence of dissolved polyvalent metallic cations, predominantly **Calcium ($Ca^{2+}$)** and **Magnesium ($Mg^{2+}$)**.
27. **Temporary Hardness (Carbonate Hardness)**:
28. Caused by dissolved **Bicarbonates (Hydrogen carbonates) of Calcium and Magnesium**: $Ca(HCO_3)_2$ and $Mg(HCO_3)_2$.
29. Can be removed by simple physical or mild chemical methods:
30. **Boiling**: Converts soluble bicarbonates into insoluble carbonates/hydroxides that precipitate out:
31. **Clark's Method**: Adding a calculated quantity of **Slaked Lime ($Ca(OH)_2$)**:
32. **Permanent Hardness (Non-Carbonate Hardness)**:
33. Caused by dissolved **Chlorides and Sulphates of Calcium and Magnesium**: $CaCl_2, MgCl_2, CaSO_4,$ and $MgSO_4$.
34. Cannot be removed by boiling. Requires chemical or ion-exchange treatment:
35. **Washing Soda Method**: Adding calculated sodium carbonate ($Na_2CO_3$):
36. **Calgon's Method**: Adding **Sodium Hexametaphosphate ($Na_6P_6O_{18}$ or $(NaPO_3)_6$, Calgon = Calcium Gone)**:
37. **Permutit / Zeolite Process**: Passing hard water through a bed of hydrated sodium aluminium silicate (**Zeolite / Permutit**, $Na_2O \cdot Al_2O_3 \cdot xSiO_2 \cdot yH_2O$):
38. **Synthetic Ion-Exchange Resin Method**: Uses cation-exchange resins ($R-SO_3H$) to exchange $Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}$ for $H^+$, followed by anion-exchange resins ($R-NH_3OH$) to exchange $Cl^-, SO_4^{2-}$ for $OH^-$. Yields completely demineralized, deionized pure water.
39. **Heavy Water ($D_2O$, Deuterium Oxide)**:
40. Discovered in 1932 by Harold C. Urey; first isolated in pure form by Gilbert N. Lewis (1933) by prolonged electrolysis of alkaline water.
41. Molecular mass = **$20.03\text{ g/mol}$** (compared to $18.015\text{ g/mol}$ for normal light water $H_2O$).
42. Physical properties: Denser than water ($1.105\text{ g/cm}^3$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$); higher freezing point (**$3.82^\circ\text{C}$**); higher boiling point (**$101.42^\circ\text{C}$**); higher viscosity.
43. **Heavy Water in Nuclear Power Plants**:
44. Used as a **Neutron Moderator** and primary **Coolant** in **Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs)**, which form the bedrock of India's commercial nuclear power fleet (e.g., Rawatbhata, Kakrapar, Narora, Kaiga).
45. *Why Heavy Water is an Ideal Moderator*: Deuterium has a very low neutron capture cross-section ($0.0005\text{ barns}$) compared to normal hydrogen ($0.33\text{ barns}$), allowing PHWRs to operate efficiently using **natural, unenriched uranium ($0.7\% {}^{235}U$)** as fuel.
46. **Heavy Water Production in India**: Managed by the **Heavy Water Board (HWB)** under the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE). Major production facilities operate at **Manuguru (Telangana)** and **Thal (Maharashtra)** utilizing the Girdler-Sulphide ($H_2S-H_2O$) and ammonia-hydrogen exchange processes.
47. **Biological Toxicity of Heavy Water**: Ingesting high concentrations of $D_2O$ is toxic to animals and plants. Deuterium forms stronger hydrogen bonds than protium, altering the 3D conformation of enzymes and significantly slowing metabolic biochemical reaction rates.
48. **Superheavy Water ($T_2O$, Tritium Oxide)**: Formed with radioactive Tritium ($^3H$); molecular mass $\approx 22\text{ g/mol}$. Tritium is a beta emitter with a half-life of $12.32\text{ years}$.
49. **Hydrogen Peroxide ($H_2O_2$)**:
50. Discovered in 1818 by French chemist Louis Jacques Thénard.
51. Non-planar open-book structure with a dihedral angle of $94.8^\circ$ in gas phase.
52. Dilute $3\%$ solution ($10\text{ volume } H_2O_2$) is sold under the trade name **Perhydrol** as a mild antiseptic for cleansing septic cuts and bleaching delicate hair and ivory.
53. Concentrated ($>90\%$) $H_2O_2$ serves as a monopropellant and oxidizer in aerospace rocket engines.
54. **Lunar Caustic**: Chemically **Silver Nitrate ($AgNO_3$)**.
55. Discovered by medieval alchemists; named "lunar" because silver was historically associated with the Moon.
56. When applied to human skin in aqueous solution, it oxidizes epidermal organic matter and reacts with trace light to deposit a dark, indelible stain of **finely divided metallic Silver ($Ag$)**.
57. Essential chemical ingredient in **Indelible Electoral Voter Marking Ink** used by the Election Commission of India.
58. **Red Lead (Sindoor / Minium)**: Chemically **Trilead Tetroxide ($Pb_3O_4$)**.
59. A bright scarlet-red powder; mixed-valence oxide containing $2PbO \cdot PbO_2$.
60. Used traditionally as vermilion/sindoor in Hindu rituals and industrially as an anti-corrosion rust-inhibiting primer paint for structural iron and steel bridges.
61. **Litharge**: Chemically **Lead Monoxide ($PbO$)**, yellowish-red powder used in lead glass and battery plates.
62. **Quicksilver**: Common historic name for elemental **Mercury ($Hg$)**, reflecting its fluid liquid mobility and bright silvery metallic luster.
63. **Vermilion (Cinnabar)**: Chemically **Mercuric Sulphide ($HgS$)**; deep crimson-red mineral pigment.
64. **Calomel**: Chemically **Mercurous Chloride ($Hg_2Cl_2$)**; white, sparingly soluble powder used historically as a medicinal purgative laxative and modernly as the standard **Calomel Reference Electrode** in pH meters and electrochemistry.
65. **Corrosive Sublimate**: Chemically **Mercuric Chloride ($HgCl_2$)**; an extremely toxic white crystalline poison used in taxidermy and wood preservation.
66. **Eosin**: A bright red water-soluble fluorescent dye ($C_{20}H_6Br_4Na_2O_5$, tetrabromofluorescein); standard coloring pigment in commercial **Red Ink** and histological biological tissue stains.
67. **Pearl Ash**: Commercial trade name for **Potassium Carbonate ($K_2CO_3$)**, historically extracted by leaching wood ashes in large iron pots (giving origin to the element name "potassium").
68. **Saltpetre (Nitre)**: Chemically **Potassium Nitrate ($KNO_3$)**; key oxidizer in gunpowder.
69. **Chile Saltpetre**: Chemically **Sodium Nitrate ($NaNO_3$)**; mined extensively in the arid Atacama Desert of Chile; used in nitrogen fertilizers and nitric acid manufacture.
70. **Sal Ammoniac**: Chemically **Ammonium Chloride ($NH_4Cl$)**; white subliming salt used as an electrolyte in Leclanché dry cells and as a soldering flux to clean metal surfaces.
71. **Laughing Gas**: **Nitrous Oxide ($N_2O$)**; discovered by Joseph Priestley (1772); induces euphoric mild hysteria and analgesia; used in dental minor anesthesia.
72. **Dry Ice**: **Solid Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)**; sublimes directly into gaseous $CO_2$ at **$-78.5^\circ\text{C}$ ($-109.3^\circ\text{F}$)** at $1\text{ atm}$ without leaving liquid residue; widely used for commercial cold-chain transport of frozen foods and vaccines.
73. **Baryta Water**: An aqueous solution of **Barium Hydroxide ($Ba(OH)_2$)**; used as an analytical reagent to test for and absorb carbon dioxide, forming insoluble white barium carbonate ($BaCO_3\downarrow$).
74. **Barium Meal (Barytes)**: Chemically **Barium Sulphate ($BaSO_4$)**. Completely insoluble in water and digestive acids; opaque to X-rays (high atomic number of Barium, $Z=56$). Administered orally to patients as a radiopaque contrast suspension for diagnostic **X-ray and CT imaging of the stomach and intestinal tract** without heavy metal toxicity.
75. **Blue Vitriol**: **Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate ($CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$)**; agricultural fungicide (Bordeaux mixture) and copper electroplating bath.
76. **Green Vitriol**: **Ferrous Sulphate Heptahydrate ($FeSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$)**; iron dietary supplement and black ink dye.
77. **White Vitriol**: **Zinc Sulphate Heptahydrate ($ZnSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$)**; lithopone white pigment and ophthalmic astringent.
78. **Epsom Salt**: **Magnesium Sulphate Heptahydrate ($MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$)**; saline osmotic laxative and soothing bath salts.
79. **Potash Alum (Fitkari)**: **$K_2SO_4 \cdot Al_2(SO_4)_3 \cdot 24H_2O$**; water-clarifying coagulant and hemostatic styptic.
80. **Glauber's Salt**: Chemically **Sodium Sulphate Decahydrate ($Na_2SO_4 \cdot 10H_2O$)**; named after German-Dutch alchemist Johann Rudolf Glauber (1625); medicinal purgative.
81. **Philosopher's Wool**: Chemically **Zinc Oxide ($ZnO$)**; fluffy white residue formed when metallic zinc burns vigorously in air; used in calamine ointments, sunscreen lotions (UV blocker), and white paints.
82. **Quicklime vs Slaked Lime vs Milk of Lime**:
83. Quicklime: Anhydrous Calcium Oxide (**$CaO$**).
84. Slaked Lime: Calcium Hydroxide (**$Ca(OH)_2$**).
85. Milk of Lime: A milky white suspension of slaked lime in water.
86. Lime Water: The clear, filtered aqueous supernatant solution of $Ca(OH)_2$ (turns milky with $CO_2$).
87. **Invert Sugar**: An equimolar mixture of **D-glucose and D-fructose** obtained by hydrolyzing cane sugar (sucrose); optical rotation inverts from $+66.5^\circ$ (dextro) to $-19.8^\circ$ (levo); present naturally in bee honey.
88. **Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2021**: Awarded to **Benjamin List** and **David MacMillan** for the development of **Asymmetric Organocatalysis** (using small, non-toxic organic molecules like proline instead of expensive heavy metal catalysts to construct chiral medicinal drugs).
89. **Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2022**: Awarded to **Carolyn R. Bertozzi**, **Morten Meldal**, and **K. Barry Sharpless** for the development of **Click Chemistry and Bioorthogonal Chemistry** (snapping molecular building blocks together cleanly inside living cells without disrupting cellular biology). K. Barry Sharpless joined the elite rank of two-time Nobel laureates (first in 2001).
