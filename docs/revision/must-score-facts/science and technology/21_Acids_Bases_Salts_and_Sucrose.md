## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Arrhenius Theory**: An **acid** is a chemical substance that dissociates in aqueous solution to yield hydrogen ions ($H^+$ or hydronium ions $H_3O^+$); a **base** dissociates to yield hydroxide ions ($OH^-$).
2. **Brønsted-Lowry Theory**: An **acid is a proton ($H^+$) donor**, and a **base is a proton ($H^+$) acceptor**. Every acid has a corresponding conjugate base, and every base has a conjugate acid.
3. **Lewis Theory**: An **acid is an electron-pair acceptor** (electrophile, e.g., $BF_3, AlCl_3, H^+$); a **base is an electron-pair donor** (nucleophile, e.g., $NH_3, H_2O, OH^-$).
4. **Acidic solutions** possess $[H^+] > 10^{-7}\text{ M}$, corresponding to **$\text{pH} < 7$**. The lower the pH value, the stronger the acidity.
5. **Basic (alkaline) solutions** possess $[H^+] < 10^{-7}\text{ M}$ and $[OH^-] > 10^{-7}\text{ M}$, corresponding to **$\text{pH} > 7$**. The higher the pH value, the stronger the alkalinity.
6. **Temperature Effect on pH**: Because auto-ionization of water ($H_2O \rightleftharpoons H^+ + OH^-$) is endothermic, increasing temperature shifts equilibrium to the right, increasing $K_w$. Consequently, at $50^\circ\text{C}$ the pH of pure neutral water drops to $\approx 6.63$, but the water **remains completely neutral** because $[H^+]$ remains exactly equal to $[OH^-]$.
7. **Human Blood pH**: Strictly maintained within the narrow, slightly alkaline window of **$7.35\text{ to }7.45$** (mean $\approx 7.40$). A blood pH drop below $7.0$ (acidosis) or rise above $7.8$ (alkalosis) is fatal.
8. **Human Gastric Juice** contains hydrochloric acid ($HCl$, $\sim 0.5\%$), with a highly acidic pH of **$1.0\text{ to }1.5$**, which activates the protein-digesting enzyme pepsinogen into pepsin and sterilizes ingested pathogens.
9. **Acid Rain** is defined as precipitation with a **$\text{pH} < 5.6$**, caused primarily by atmospheric emissions of sulphur dioxide ($SO_2$) and nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) reacting with rainwater to form sulphuric acid ($H_2SO_4$) and nitric acid ($HNO_3$).
10. **Natural Organic Acids & Food Sources**:
11. **Acetic Acid (Ethanoic Acid, $CH_3COOH$)**: Vinegar ($4–8\%$ aqueous solution).
12. **Formic Acid (Methanoic Acid, $HCOOH$)**: Stings of red ants, bees, wasps, and stinging nettle plant hairs.
13. **Citric Acid ($C_6H_8O_7$)**: Citrus fruits (lemons, oranges, limes, sweet limes).
14. **Tartaric Acid ($C_4H_6O_6$)**: Tamarind (imli), unripe grapes, cream of tartar; essential component of baking powder.
15. **Lactic Acid ($CH_3CH(OH)COOH$)**: Sour milk, curd (dahi), yogurt; muscle accumulation during anaerobic respiration causes muscular fatigue and cramps.
16. **Oxalic Acid ($H_2C_2O_4$)**: Tomatoes, spinach, sorrel; used industrially to remove stubborn rust/ink stains from textiles.
17. **Malic Acid ($C_4H_6O_5$)**: Apples (especially green/unripe apples), cherries.
18. **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C, $C_6H_8O_6$)**: Amla (Indian gooseberry), citrus fruits, guava.
19. **Tannic Acid**: Tea leaves (causes astringent taste).
20. **Butyric Acid**: Rancid, oxidized butter.
21. **Carbonic Acid ($H_2CO_3$)**: Carbonated soft drinks, club soda.
22. **Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi)**: Approximately $80\%$ of all human kidney stones consist of insoluble crystals of **Calcium Oxalate ($CaC_2O_4$)**, formed when high concentrations of dietary oxalic acid bind with urinary calcium.
23. **Acid-Base Indicators**:
24. **Litmus**: Natural purple dye extracted from **Lichens** (Roccella tinctoria). Turns **Red in acidic solutions** and **Blue in basic/alkaline solutions**.
25. **Phenolphthalein**: Synthetic organic indicator. Remains **Colorless in acidic and neutral solutions**, but turns **Intense Magenta/Pink in basic solutions** (pH transition interval $8.2–10.0$).
26. **Methyl Orange**: Synthetic azo indicator. Turns **Red/Pink in acidic solutions** (pH $< 3.1$) and **Yellow in basic/neutral solutions** (pH $> 4.4$).
27. **Turmeric (Haldi)**: Natural yellow indicator. Remains yellow in acids/neutrals, but turns **Reddish-Brown in alkaline solutions** (explaining why yellow curry stains turn deep red when scrubbed with alkaline laundry soap).
28. **Red Cabbage Extract**: Natural anthocyanin indicator. Turns red/pink in acids, purple at neutral pH, and green-to-yellow in strong bases.
29. **Olfactory Indicators** are substances whose characteristic odor changes depending on whether they are in an acidic or basic medium. Examples: **Vanilla extract, Onion juice, and Clove oil** completely lose their smell in alkaline solutions ($NaOH$), but retain their scent in acids.

## ⚡ Confused Pairs & Common Examiner Traps

& High-Yield Pitfalls

| Trap Area | What Students Confuse | Correct Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Baking vs. Washing Soda** | Mixing up $NaHCO_3$ and $Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$. | **Baking Soda is $NaHCO_3$** (contains Hydrogen). **Washing Soda is $Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$** (10 waters). |
| **Plaster of Paris Water Count** | Forgetting whether PoP has $\frac{1}{2}$ or $2$ water molecules. | **Plaster of Paris has $\frac{1}{2}\text{ H}_2\text{O}$** ($CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$). **Gypsum has $2\text{ H}_2\text{O}$** ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$). |
| **pH Direction Rules** | Reading fast and accepting "Acids have pH > 7". | **Acids have pH < 7**. **Bases have pH > 7**. pH 7 is neutral at $25^\circ\text{C}$. |
| **Hot Water Neutrality** | Assuming water at $50^\circ\text{C}$ with pH 6.63 is acidic. | It is **strictly neutral** because $[H^+] = [OH^-]$. Neutrality means equal concentrations, not rigidly pH 7.0. |
| **Sucrose Reducing Ability** | Assuming all sweet sugars are reducing sugars. | **Sucrose is a NON-REDUCING sugar**. Both anomeric carbons are locked in the glycosidic bond. |
| **Sweetest Natural Sugar** | Believing glucose or sucrose is the sweetest sugar. | **Fructose is the sweetest natural sugar** ($\sim 170$ vs sucrose 100). |
| **Ant Sting Acid** | Answering acetic acid for ant bites. | Ant stings inject **Methanoic acid (Formic acid, $HCOOH$)**, not acetic acid. |
| **Kidney Stone Composition** | Assuming kidney stones are made of uric acid or calcium carbonate. | $\approx 80\%$ of kidney stones are composed of insoluble **Calcium Oxalate ($CaC_2O_4$)**. |
| **Aspartame Cooking Trap** | Thinking aspartame can be used in baked goods. | Aspartame **decomposes at cooking temperatures**; only sucralose and saccharin are heat-stable. |
| **Turmeric Color in Base** | Expecting turmeric to turn blue or green in base. | Turmeric turns **reddish-brown in basic solutions** (like soap), and stays yellow in acids. |

## 🎯 Revision Practice MCQs (Mastery Drill)

> **Mastery Rule:** Read the consolidated facts above, attempt these high-yield questions, and score &ge;80% to conquer this chapter.

**Q1.** Arrhenius Theory**: An **acid** is a chemical substance that dissociates in aqueous solution to yield hydrogen ions ($H^+$ or hydronium ions $H_3O^+$); a **base** dissociates to yield hydroxide ions ($OH^-$).
2. **Brønsted-Lowry Theory**: An **acid is a proton ($H^+$) donor**, and a **base is a proton ($H^+$) acceptor**. Every acid has a corresponding conjugate base, and every base has a conjugate acid.
3. **Lewis Theory**: An **acid is an electron-pair acceptor** (electrophile, e.g., $BF_3, AlCl_3, H^+$); a **base is an electron-pair donor** (nucleophile, e.g., $NH_3, H_2O, OH^-$).
4. The **pH scale** was devised in 1909 by Danish biochemist **S.P.L. Sørensen** at the Carlsberg Laboratory:
   $$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[H^+] = \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{[H^+]}\right)$$
5. In pure neutral water at standard room temperature ($25^\circ\text{C}$ or $298\text{ K}$), $[H^+] = [OH^-] = 10^{-7}\text{ M}$, yielding:
   $$\text{pH} = 7.0 \quad (\text{Strictly Neutral})$$
6. **Acidic solutions** possess $[H^+] > 10^{-7}\text{ M}$, corresponding to **$\text{pH} < 7$**. The lower the pH value, the stronger the acidity.
7. **Basic (alkaline) solutions** possess $[H^+] < 10^{-7}\text{ M}$ and $[OH^-] > 10^{-7}\text{ M}$, corresponding to **$\text{pH} > 7$**. The higher the pH value, the stronger the alkalinity.
8. At $25^\circ\text{C}$, the ionic product of water is $K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = 10^{-14}\text{ mol}^2/\text{L}^2$, which dictates:
   $$\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14$$
9. **Temperature Effect on pH**: Because auto-ionization of water ($H_2O \rightleftharpoons H^+ + OH^-$) is endothermic, increasing temperature shifts equilibrium to the right, increasing $K_w$. Consequently, at $50^\circ\text{C}$ the pH of pure neutral water drops to $\approx 6.63$, but the water **remains completely neutral** because $[H^+]$ remains exactly equal to $[OH^-]$.
10. A change of **1 pH unit** represents a **tenfold ($10\times$) change in hydrogen ion concentration**. A solution of pH 2 is $100$ times more acidic than a solution of pH 4 ($10^{4-2} = 10^2$).
11. **Human Blood pH**: Strictly maintained within the narrow, slightly alkaline window of **$7.35\text{ to }7.45$** (mean $\approx 7.40$). A blood pH drop below $7.0$ (acidosis) or rise above $7.8$ (alkalosis) is fatal.
12. The primary biological buffer maintaining human blood pH is the **Carbonic acid–Bicarbonate buffer system**:
    $$H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-$$
    regulated cooperatively by the lungs (eliminating gaseous $CO_2$) and the kidneys (reabsorbing or excreting $HCO_3^-$).
13. **Human Gastric Juice** contains hydrochloric acid ($HCl$, $\sim 0.5\%$), with a highly acidic pH of **$1.0\text{ to }1.5$**, which activates the protein-digesting enzyme pepsinogen into pepsin and sterilizes ingested pathogens.
14. Normal **Human Saliva** has a pH range of **$6.5\text{ to }7.5$**. When oral bacteria ferment dietary sugars, dropping saliva pH below **$5.5$**, tooth enamel (calcium hydroxyapatite, $Ca_{10}(PO_4)_6(OH)_2$) begins to demineralize, initiating dental decay.
15. **Acid Rain** is defined as precipitation with a **$\text{pH} < 5.6$**, caused primarily by atmospheric emissions of sulphur dioxide ($SO_2$) and nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) reacting with rainwater to form sulphuric acid ($H_2SO_4$) and nitric acid ($HNO_3$).
16. **Natural Organic Acids & Food Sources**:
    - **Acetic Acid (Ethanoic Acid, $CH_3COOH$)**: Vinegar ($4–8\%$ aqueous solution).
    - **Formic Acid (Methanoic Acid, $HCOOH$)**: Stings of red ants, bees, wasps, and stinging nettle plant hairs.
    - **Citric Acid ($C_6H_8O_7$)**: Citrus fruits (lemons, oranges, limes, sweet limes).
    - **Tartaric Acid ($C_4H_6O_6$)**: Tamarind (imli), unripe grapes, cream of tartar; essential component of baking powder.
    - **Lactic Acid ($CH_3CH(OH)COOH$)**: Sour milk, curd (dahi), yogurt; muscle accumulation during anaerobic respiration causes muscular fatigue and cramps.
    - **Oxalic Acid ($H_2C_2O_4$)**: Tomatoes, spinach, sorrel; used industrially to remove stubborn rust/ink stains from textiles.
    - **Malic Acid ($C_4H_6O_5$)**: Apples (especially green/unripe apples), cherries.
    - **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C, $C_6H_8O_6$)**: Amla (Indian gooseberry), citrus fruits, guava.
    - **Tannic Acid**: Tea leaves (causes astringent taste).
    - **Butyric Acid**: Rancid, oxidized butter.
    - **Carbonic Acid ($H_2CO_3$)**: Carbonated soft drinks, club soda.
17. **Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi)**: Approximately $80\%$ of all human kidney stones consist of insoluble crystals of **Calcium Oxalate ($CaC_2O_4$)**, formed when high concentrations of dietary oxalic acid bind with urinary calcium.
18. **Acid-Base Indicators**:
    - **Litmus**: Natural purple dye extracted from **Lichens** (Roccella tinctoria). Turns **Red in acidic solutions** and **Blue in basic/alkaline solutions**.
    - **Phenolphthalein**: Synthetic organic indicator. Remains **Colorless in acidic and neutral solutions**, but turns **Intense Magenta/Pink in basic solutions** (pH transition interval $8.2–10.0$).
    - **Methyl Orange**: Synthetic azo indicator. Turns **Red/Pink in acidic solutions** (pH $< 3.1$) and **Yellow in basic/neutral solutions** (pH $> 4.4$).
    - **Turmeric (Haldi)**: Natural yellow indicator. Remains yellow in acids/neutrals, but turns **Reddish-Brown in alkaline solutions** (explaining why yellow curry stains turn deep red when scrubbed with alkaline laundry soap).
    - **Red Cabbage Extract**: Natural anthocyanin indicator. Turns red/pink in acids, purple at neutral pH, and green-to-yellow in strong bases.
19. **Olfactory Indicators** are substances whose characteristic odor changes depending on whether they are in an acidic or basic medium. Examples: **Vanilla extract, Onion juice, and Clove oil** completely lose their smell in alkaline solutions ($NaOH$), but retain their scent in acids.
20. **Neutralization Reaction**: The exothermic chemical reaction between an acid and a base to produce a salt and water:
    $$\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \to \text{Salt} + \text{Water} + \text{Heat}$$
    For any strong acid reacting with a strong base in dilute aqueous solution, the **standard enthalpy of neutralization is constant**:
    $$\Delta H_{\text{neut}}^\circ = -57.1\text{ kJ/mol} \quad (-13.7\text{ kcal/mol})$$
    because the net reaction is always: $H^+\text{(aq)} + OH^-\text{(aq)} \to H_2O\text{(l)}$.
21. **Salt Hydrolysis and Solution pH**:
    - *Salt of Strong Acid + Strong Base* ($NaCl, KNO_3$): Neutral solution ($\text{pH} = 7.0$).
    - *Salt of Strong Acid + Weak Base* ($NH_4Cl, CuSO_4$): Acidic solution ($\text{pH} < 7.0$).
    - *Salt of Weak Acid + Strong Base* ($CH_3COONa, Na_2CO_3$): Basic/alkaline solution ($\text{pH} > 7.0$).
    - *Salt of Weak Acid + Weak Base* ($CH_3COONH_4$): pH depends on relative $K_a$ and $K_b$ values.
22. **Baking Soda**: Chemically **Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate / Sodium Bicarbonate ($NaHCO_3$)**:
    - Prepared industrially by the **Solvay Process** using brine ($NaCl$), ammonia ($NH_3$), and carbon dioxide ($CO_2$).
    - Mild, non-corrosive basic salt.
    - Used in medicine as an **antacid** to neutralize hyperacidity in the stomach ($NaHCO_3 + HCl \to NaCl + H_2O + CO_2 \uparrow$).
    - Used in **soda-acid fire extinguishers**: when inverted, concentrated sulphuric acid mixes with $NaHCO_3$ solution, generating high-pressure $CO_2$ gas that smothers flames.
23. **Baking Powder**: A dry commercial blend of **Baking Soda ($NaHCO_3$)** and a mild edible solid acid like **Tartaric Acid** or cream of tartar (potassium bitartrate).
    - *Why Tartaric Acid is Added*: When heated or mixed with water, $NaHCO_3$ releases $CO_2$ gas which causes dough to rise and become soft/spongy. However, the residual sodium carbonate ($Na_2CO_3$) would impart an unpleasant, bitter alkaline taste. The tartaric acid neutralizes $Na_2CO_3$ into sodium tartrate, completely eliminating the bitter taste.
24. **Washing Soda**: Chemically **Sodium Carbonate Decahydrate ($Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$)**:
    - Contains **10 molecules of water of crystallization**.
    - Undergoes **efflorescence**: when exposed to dry atmospheric air, it loses 9 molecules of water of crystallization to form a white powdery monohydrate:
      $$Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{air}} Na_2CO_3 \cdot H_2O\text{ (Monohydrate)} + 9H_2O \uparrow$$
    - Anhydrous sodium carbonate ($Na_2CO_3$) is known commercially as **Soda Ash**.
    - Widely used in laundry detergents, glass, soap, and paper manufacturing, and for **removing permanent hardness** of water caused by dissolved $CaCl_2$ and $MgSO_4$.
25. **Bleaching Powder**: Chemically **Calcium Oxychloride / Calcium Hypochlorite ($CaOCl_2$)**:
    - Manufactured by passing chlorine gas over dry slaked lime at $40^\circ\text{C}$:
      $$Ca(OH)_2 + Cl_2 \to CaOCl_2 + H_2O$$
    - Gives off a strong odor of chlorine in air because it reacts with atmospheric $CO_2$:
      $$CaOCl_2 + CO_2 \to CaCO_3 + Cl_2 \uparrow$$
    - Functions as a powerful **oxidizing agent, textile bleach, and bactericide/disinfectant** for municipal drinking water sterilization.
26. **Plaster of Paris (PoP)**: Chemically **Calcium Sulphate Hemihydrate ($CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$ or $(CaSO_4)_2 \cdot H_2O$)**:
    - Manufactured by carefully heating mineral Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$) in a rotary kiln to exactly **$373\text{ K}$ ($100^\circ\text{C}$)**:
      $$CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O \xrightarrow{373\text{ K}} CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O + 1\frac{1}{2}H_2O \uparrow$$
    - *Crucial Thermal Trap*: If gypsum is heated above **$473\text{ K}$ ($200^\circ\text{C}$)**, it loses all water of crystallization, yielding anhydrous calcium sulphate ($CaSO_4$) known as **"Dead Burnt Plaster"**, which completely loses the property of setting with water.
    - *Setting Action*: When mixed with water, PoP rehydrates back into a hard crystalline mass of gypsum within 10–15 minutes, undergoing a **slight volume expansion ($\approx 1\%$)**, which makes it ideal for medical orthopedic casts (immobilizing fractured bones), architectural false ceilings, dental moulds, and statue casting.
27. **Gypsum**: Chemically **Calcium Sulphate Dihydrate ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$)**:
    - Naturally occurring mineral containing **2 molecules of water of crystallization**.
    - Added in small amounts ($2–3\%$) to **Portland cement clinker** during final grinding to retard the initial flash setting time of cement, providing masons sufficient working time.
    - Used in agriculture as a soil conditioner to reclaim alkaline/sodic soils and supply calcium and sulphate nutrients.
28. **Blue Vitriol**: Chemically **Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate ($CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$)**:
    - Distinctive deep blue crystalline solid containing 5 water molecules (4 coordinated to $Cu^{2+}$, 1 hydrogen-bonded).
    - Heating above $250^\circ\text{C}$ drives off all water, turning it into white anhydrous $CuSO_4$.
    - Key ingredient in **Bordeaux Mixture** ($CuSO_4 + Ca(OH)_2$ in water), an essential agricultural fungicide sprayed on grapevines and fruit orchards.
29. **Green Vitriol**: Chemically **Ferrous Sulphate Heptahydrate ($FeSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$)**:
    - Pale green crystals used in ink manufacturing, iron supplement medicine (iron deficiency anaemia), and water purification coagulants.
30. **White Vitriol**: Chemically **Zinc Sulphate Heptahydrate ($ZnSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$)**:
    - Used in the manufacture of lithopone pigment, rayon spinning baths, and as an ophthalmic astringent eye-drop.
31. **Oil of Vitriol**: Historical alchemical name for concentrated **Sulphuric Acid ($H_2SO_4$)**, so named because it was originally synthesized by distilling green vitriol crystals.
32. **Epsom Salt**: Chemically **Magnesium Sulphate Heptahydrate ($MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$)**:
    - Used medically as a saline osmotic laxative and in therapeutic soothing bath salts.
33. **Potash Alum (Fitkari)**: Chemically **Potassium Aluminium Sulphate Dodecahydrate ($K_2SO_4 \cdot Al_2(SO_4)_3 \cdot 24H_2O$)**:
    - A classic double salt containing 24 molecules of water of crystallization.
    - Used as a hemostatic agent (stops shaving bleeding by coagulating blood proteins) and for clarifying turbid water.
34. **Caustic Soda**: Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$). Strong base used in soap making, rayon, petroleum refining.
35. **Caustic Potash**: Potassium Hydroxide ($KOH$). Used in luxury soft shaving soaps and alkaline storage batteries.
36. **Slaked Lime**: Calcium Hydroxide ($Ca(OH)_2$). Clear aqueous solution is called **Lime Water**. Reacts with $CO_2$ to turn milky due to insoluble $CaCO_3$ precipitation.
37. **Quicklime**: Calcium Oxide ($CaO$). Prepared by thermal calcination of limestone. Highly exothermic reaction with water yields slaked lime: $CaO + H_2O \to Ca(OH)_2 + \text{heat}$.
38. **Carbohydrates**: Polyhydroxy aldehydes or polyhydroxy ketones (or compounds that yield them upon hydrolysis), having the general empirical formula $C_x(H_2O)_y$ (though exceptions like rhamnose $C_6H_{12}O_5$ and acetic acid $C_2H_4O_2$ exist).
39. **Monosaccharides**: Simplest carbohydrate monomers that cannot be hydrolyzed into smaller carbohydrate units.
    - **Glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$)**: An aldohexose known as **Dextrose, Grape Sugar, or Blood Sugar**. Chief physiological energy fuel of the human body ($4\text{ kcal/g}$).
    - **Fructose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$)**: A ketohexose known as **Levulose or Fruit Sugar**.
    - **Galactose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$)**: Component of milk sugar.
40. **Fructose Sweetness**: Fructose is the **sweetest naturally occurring carbohydrate**, with a relative sweetness rating of **$\sim 170$** (relative to sucrose standardized at $100$). Present abundantly in **honey ($\approx 38\%$)** and sweet ripe fruits.
41. **Disaccharides**: Carbohydrates that yield two monosaccharide molecules upon acid or enzymatic hydrolysis:
    - **Sucrose ($C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$)**: Cane sugar or table sugar. Formed by $\alpha\text{-D-glucose} + \beta\text{-D-fructose}$ linked via an $\alpha-1,\beta-2$ glycosidic bond.
    - **Lactose ($C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$)**: Milk sugar ($\approx 5\%$ in cow milk, $\approx 7\%$ in human milk). Formed by $\beta\text{-D-galactose} + \beta\text{-D-glucose}$ via a $\beta-1,4$ glycosidic bond. Least sweet natural sugar (rating $\approx 16$).
    - **Maltose ($C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$)**: Malt sugar. Formed by two $\alpha\text{-D-glucose}$ units linked via an $\alpha-1,4$ glycosidic bond. Produced during starch digestion by amylase.
42. **Reducing vs. Non-Reducing Sugars**:
    - **Reducing Sugars**: Contain free hemiacetal or hemiketal groups; reduce **Fehling's solution** (blue cupric $\to$ red cuprous oxide $Cu_2O\downarrow$) and **Tollens' reagent** (ammoniacal silver nitrate $\to$ silver mirror).
    - **All Monosaccharides** (Glucose, Fructose, Galactose) and **Maltose & Lactose** are reducing sugars!
    - **Sucrose is a NON-REDUCING sugar** because both of its anomeric carbons (C-1 of glucose and C-2 of fructose) are mutually locked in the glycosidic bond, leaving no free functional group to reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagents.
43. **Invert Sugar**:
    - Sucrose is **dextrorotatory** ($[\alpha]_D = +66.5^\circ$).
    - When sucrose undergoes hydrolysis by dilute acid or the enzyme **invertase**:
      $$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}\text{ (Sucrose)} + H_2O \to C_6H_{12}O_6\text{ (D-Glucose)} + C_6H_{12}O_6\text{ (D-Fructose)}$$
    - D-Glucose is dextrorotatory ($+52.7^\circ$), but D-Fructose is strongly levorotatory ($-92.4^\circ$).
    - The resulting equimolar mixture has a net negative (levorotatory) specific rotation of **$-19.8^\circ$**.
    - Because the sign of optical rotation **inverts from dextro ($+$) to levo ($-$)**, the hydrolyzed product is called **Invert Sugar** (present naturally in honey).
44. **Artificial Sweetening Agents (Non-Nutritive Sweeteners)**:
    - **Saccharin (o-Sulphobenzimide)**: The first popular artificial sweetener (discovered in 1879 by Remsen and Fahlberg); approximately **$550\text{ times}$ sweeter than sucrose**. Excreted unchanged in urine; provides zero calories.
    - **Aspartame**: A methyl ester of phenylalanine/aspartic acid dipeptide; approximately **$100–200\text{ times}$ sweeter than sucrose**. **Thermally unstable at cooking temperatures**, so restricted exclusively to cold foods and soft drinks. Unsafe for individuals suffering from phenylketonuria (PKU).
    - **Sucralose**: Trichloro derivative of sucrose; approximately **$600\text{ times}$ sweeter than sucrose**. Stable at cooking and baking temperatures; non-caloric.
    - **Alitame**: High-potency aspartic acid dipeptide; approximately **$2000\text{ times}$ sweeter than sucrose**. Extremely sweet, making precise sweetness control difficult.
45. **Relative Sweetness Scale Matrix**:
    $$\text{Alitame (2000)} > \text{Sucralose (600)} > \text{Saccharin (550)} > \text{Aspartame (100–200)} > \text{Fructose (170)} > \text{Sucrose (100)} > \text{Glucose (74)} > \text{Maltose (32)} > \text{Lactose (16)}$$
46. **Aqua Fortis**: Alchemical name for **Nitric Acid ($HNO_3$)**.
47. **Muriatic Acid**: Commercial trade name for industrial **Hydrochloric Acid ($HCl$)**.
48. **Battery Acid**: Moderately concentrated **Sulphuric Acid ($H_2SO_4$, $\sim 33–38\%$)** used as the electrolyte in automotive lead-acid storage batteries (specific gravity $1.28$).
49. **Antacids**: Weakly basic compounds that neutralize excess gastric $HCl$ without irritating the stomach lining. Classic examples: **Milk of Magnesia ($Mg(OH)_2$)**, Aluminium Hydroxide gel ($Al(OH)_3$), and Sodium Bicarbonate ($NaHCO_3$).
50. **Aqua Regia**: The royal solvent consisting of **$3\text{ parts concentrated } HCl + 1\text{ part concentrated } HNO_3$** by volume ($3:1$ ratio).

## Detailed Theory Notes

### 4.1 Acid-Base Theories & Mathematical Dynamics of the pH Scale

#### 1. Theoretical Evolution
- **Arrhenius Concept (1884)**: Defined acids as substances producing $H^+$ ions in aqueous solution, and bases as producing $OH^-$ ions. Limitation: Restricted strictly to aqueous systems and failed to explain the basicity of ammonia ($NH_3$).
- **Brønsted-Lowry Concept (1923)**: Acid is a proton ($H^+$) donor; base is a proton ($H^+$) acceptor. Introduces **Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs** differing by exactly one proton ($H^+$):
  $$\underset{\text{Acid}_1}{HCl} + \underset{\text{Base}_2}{H_2O} \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{Acid}_2}{H_3O^+} + \underset{\text{Base}_1}{Cl^-}$$
- **Lewis Concept (1923)**: Broadest electronic definition:
  - *Lewis Acid*: Chemical species capable of accepting an unshared electron pair (electrophile, e.g., $BF_3, AlCl_3, Fe^{3+}, H^+$).
  - *Lewis Base*: Chemical species capable of donating an unshared electron pair (nucleophile, e.g., $:NH_3, H_2\ddot{O}:, OH^-, CN^-$).

```
Arrhenius:      Acid = H⁺ generator in water     | Base = OH⁻ generator in water
Brønsted-Lowry: Acid = Proton (H⁺) donor         | Base = Proton (H⁺) acceptor
Lewis:          Acid = Electron pair acceptor    | Base = Electron pair donor
```

#### 2. Logarithmic pH Scale Dynamics
- Formulated mathematically by S.P.L. Sørensen (1909):
  $$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[H_3O^+]$$
- Because the scale is base-10 logarithmic, a decrease of 1 unit on the pH scale represents a **$10$-fold increase in hydrogen ion concentration**:
  $$\frac{[H^+]_{\text{pH } 2}}{[H^+]_{\text{pH } 5}} = \frac{10^{-2}}{10^{-5}} = 10^3 = 1000\times \text{ higher acidity}$$
- **Neutrality Definition**: Neutrality does NOT universally mean $\text{pH} = 7.0$; neutrality strictly means $[H^+] = [OH^-]$. At $25^\circ\text{C}$, $K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$, so $\text{pH} = 7.0$. At elevated physiological temperatures ($37^\circ\text{C}$), $K_w \approx 2.4 \times 10^{-14}$, so neutral $\text{pH} \approx 6.8$.

### 4.2 Chemical Indicators & Titration Physics

#### 1. Action of Acid-Base Indicators
Indicators are weak organic acids ($HIn$) or weak organic bases whose undissociated molecular form has a visibly distinct color from its ionized conjugate form ($In^-$):
$$HIn\text{ (Color A)} \rightleftharpoons H^+\text{(aq)} + In^-\text{ (Color B)}$$
- In an acidic solution (high $[H^+]$), common-ion effect suppresses indicator dissociation, displaying Color A.
- In an alkaline solution, added $OH^-$ consumes $H^+$, shifting the equilibrium forward to display Color B.

### 4.3 Household Salts & Industrial Chemistry

#### 1. Baking Soda ($NaHCO_3$) vs. Washing Soda ($Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$)
- **Solvay Industrial Process**:
  $$NaCl + H_2O + NH_3 + CO_2 \to NaHCO_3\downarrow + NH_4Cl$$
  Crude sodium hydrogen carbonate precipitates out because of its relatively low solubility in ammonium chloride brine.
- **Thermal Decomposition into Soda Ash**:
  $$2NaHCO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} Na_2CO_3 + H_2O + CO_2 \uparrow$$
- **Hydration into Washing Soda**:
  $$Na_2CO_3 + 10H_2O \to Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O\text{ (Washing Soda Decahydrate)}$$
- **Efflorescence Reaction**: In dry atmospheric conditions, washing soda loses 9 molecules of water spontaneously:
  $$Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{dry air}} Na_2CO_3 \cdot H_2O\text{ (Monohydrate)} + 9H_2O \uparrow$$

#### 2. Plaster of Paris ($CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$) vs. Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$)
- **Thermal Regimes of Gypsum Calcination**:
  1. *At $100^\circ\text{C}$ ($373\text{ K}$)*:
     $$CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O \xrightarrow{373\text{ K}} CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O\text{ (Plaster of Paris)} + 1\frac{1}{2}H_2O$$
  2. *Above $200^\circ\text{C}$ ($473\text{ K}$)*:
     $$CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O \xrightarrow{>473\text{ K}} CaSO_4\text{ (Anhydrous "Dead Burnt Plaster")} + 2H_2O$$
     Dead burnt plaster absorbs water very slowly and has no hydraulic setting strength.
  3. *At $>1000^\circ\text{C}$*:
     $$2CaSO_4 \xrightarrow{>1000^\circ\text{C}} 2CaO + 2SO_2 \uparrow + O_2 \uparrow$$

### 4.4 Carbohydrates, Sugars & Sweetener Dynamics

#### 1. Structural Classification
- **Monosaccharides**:
  - *D-Glucose*: Aldohexose; exists predominantly as a 6-membered pyranose ring ($\alpha\text{-D-glucopyranose}$ and $\beta\text{-D-glucopyranose}$).
  - *D-Fructose*: Ketohexose; exists predominantly as a 5-membered furanose ring ($\alpha\text{-D-fructofuranose}$ and $\beta\text{-D-fructofuranose}$).
- **Disaccharides**:
  - *Sucrose*: Composed of $\alpha\text{-D-glucose} + \beta\text{-D-fructose}$ linked by an $\alpha-1,\beta-2$ glycosidic bond.
  - *Lactose*: Composed of $\beta\text{-D-galactose} + \beta\text{-D-glucose}$ linked by a $\beta-1,4$ glycosidic bond.
  - *Maltose*: Composed of two $\alpha\text{-D-glucose}$ molecules linked by an $\alpha-1,4$ glycosidic bond.

#### 2. Inversion of Cane Sugar (Invert Sugar)
Sucrose is naturally dextrorotatory ($[\alpha]_D = +66.5^\circ$). When cleaved by dilute acid or the enzyme invertase:
$$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11} + H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{invertase}} \underset{\text{Dextrorotatory } (+52.7^\circ)}{\text{D-Glucose}} + \underset{\text{Strongly Levorotatory } (-92.4^\circ)}{\text{D-Fructose}}$$
Because the levorotation of fructose exceeds the dextrorotation of glucose, the optical rotation of the resulting equimolar mixture is levorotatory (**$-19.8^\circ$**). This inversion of the optical rotation gives **Invert Sugar** its name. Invert sugar is sweeter and more resistant to crystallization than sucrose, making it valued in confectionery and commercial honey.
