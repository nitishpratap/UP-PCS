## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The structural and functional unit of the nervous system is the **Neuron**; adult neurons reside permanently in the quiescent $G_0$ phase of the cell cycle and **do not divide** (lack active centrioles).
2. A typical neuron consists of a **Cell Body (Soma/Cyton)** containing Nissl's granules, short branched receptive **Dendrites**, and a single long conducting **Axon** terminating in synaptic knobs; myelin sheath in the peripheral nervous system is secreted by **Schwann cells**.
3. Two neurons communicate across a microscopic gap called a **Synapse** via chemical messengers called **Neurotransmitters** (e.g. **Acetylcholine**, Noradrenaline, Dopamine, GABA); electrical sparks do not jump across ordinary synapses.
4. The central nervous system (CNS) is wrapped in three protective fibro-vascular membranes called **Meninges**: outer tough **Duramater**, middle web-like **Arachnoid mater**, and inner delicate vascular **Piamater** (Mnemonic: **DAP**); space between arachnoid and piamater is filled with shock-absorbing **Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)**.
5. **Cerebrum**: Largest part of the human brain (~80% of brain mass); seat of intelligence, conscious thought, memory, reasoning, voluntary motor control, speech (Broca's area), and sensation; right and left cerebral hemispheres are joined internally by a broad transverse curved band of white nerve fibres called the **Corpus Callosum**.
6. **Hypothalamus**: Situated at the base of the diencephalon; acts as the body's **Thermostat** (regulates core body temperature); controls hunger, thirst, satiety, sleep-wake cycles, emotional responses (anger, fear, pleasure via the limbic system), and regulates the pituitary gland via releasing and inhibiting neurohormones.
7. **Cerebellum**: Second largest part of the brain; displays an internal tree-like branching pattern of white matter called **Arbor Vitae** ("Tree of Life"); coordinates voluntary muscular contractions, maintains posture, and controls **physical equilibrium and body balance**; alcohol intoxication temporarily depresses the cerebellum, causing muscular uncoordination and a staggering gait.
8. **Medulla Oblongata**: Continues inferiorly into the spinal cord; houses vital autonomous reflex centres controlling **respiratory rhythm, cardiac rate, arterial blood pressure (vasomotor centre)**, as well as protective reflexes: swallowing, coughing, sneezing, vomiting, and salivation.
9. Peripheral nerves: Humans possess **$\mathbf{12\text{ pairs}}$ of Cranial Nerves** (arising from brain; Vagus nerve / CN X is the longest and most widely distributed) and **$\mathbf{31\text{ pairs}}$ of Spinal Nerves** (8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal; all spinal nerves are mixed nerves).
10. Autonomic Nervous System (ANS): **Sympathetic System** prepares the body for intense physical exertion ("Fight or Flight": dilates pupils, accelerates heart, dilates bronchi, inhibits gut peristalsis); **Parasympathetic System** restores calm ("Rest and Digest": constricts pupils, slows heart, stimulates digestive peristalsis and salivation).
11. A **Reflex Arc** is an involuntary, rapid, automated, stereotyped response to a sensory stimulus that does not require conscious intervention by the cerebral cortex; sequential pathway: **Receptor → Sensory (Afferent) Neuron → Spinal Cord Interneuron → Motor (Efferent) Neuron → Effector Muscle / Gland**.
12. **The Human Eye Anatomy**: The eyeball wall possesses three layers: outer fibrous tunic (**Sclera** anteriorly continuous with the transparent **Cornea**; cornea lacks blood vessels, absorbs oxygen directly from atmospheric air, making corneal grafts highly successful without immune rejection); middle vascular tunic (**Choroid**, **Ciliary Body**, and pigmented **Iris** with central pupil); inner sensory tunic (**Retina** containing photoreceptor cells).
13. **Photoreceptors: Rods vs Cones**: **Rods** (~120 million) contain the purplish-red pigment **Rhodopsin** (visual purple, requiring Vitamin A/retinol), operate in twilight/dim light (**Scotopic vision**), and cannot perceive colors; **Cones** (~6 million) contain **Iodopsin**, operate in bright light (**Photopic vision**), and mediate color vision (Trichromatic: red, green, blue); the **Fovea Centralis (Yellow Spot / Macula Lutea)** contains exclusively densely packed cones and provides the highest visual acuity.
14. **Common Ophthalmic Defects**: **Myopia (Short-sightedness)**: Eyeball too long or corneal curvature excessive, image forms in front of retina, corrected by **Concave Lens**; **Hypermetropia (Far-sightedness)**: Eyeball too short, image forms behind retina, corrected by **Convex Lens**; **Presbyopia**: Age-related loss of ciliary muscle flexibility, corrected by **Bifocal Lens**; **Astigmatism**: Asymmetrical corneal curvature, corrected by **Cylindrical Lens**; **Cataract**: Progressive opacity/clouding of the crystalline lens, treated by surgical phacoemulsification and intraocular lens (IOL) implant.
15. **The Human Ear Anatomy & Hearing**: Outer ear (Pinna and external auditory meatus); middle ear containing three auditory ossicles that amplify sound vibrations ~20-fold: **Malleus (Hammer)**, **Incus (Anvil)**, and **Stapes (Stirrup)** — the **Stapes is the smallest bone in the human body**; Eustachian tube connects the middle ear cavity to the nasopharynx, equalizing atmospheric air pressure on either side of the tympanic membrane; inner ear contains the fluid-filled coiled **Cochlea** (organ of hearing, Organ of Corti) and **Semicircular Canals** (dynamic and static body balance).
16. **Endocrine Glands (Ductless Glands)**: Secretions are poured directly into circulating blood; target distant organs; e.g. Pituitary, Thyroid, Parathyroid, Adrenal, Pineal, Thymus.
17. **Exocrine Glands**: Possess tubular ducts that deliver secretions locally onto an epithelial surface; e.g. Salivary, Sweat, Sebaceous, Lacrimal (tear), Mammary glands.
18. **Heterocrine / Mixed Glands**: The **Pancreas** acts as both an exocrine gland (acinar cells secrete digestive enzymes via the pancreatic duct) and an endocrine gland (Islets of Langerhans secrete insulin and glucagon into blood).
19. **Pituitary Gland (Hypophysis / Master Gland)**: Located in a depression of the sphenoid bone called the **Sella Turcica**; connected to the hypothalamus via the hypophyseal infundibulum stalk; anterior pituitary (Adenohypophysis) secretes **Growth Hormone (GH)**, **Prolactin (PRL)**, **TSH**, **ACTH**, **FSH**, and **LH**; hypersecretion of GH causes **Gigantism** in children and **Acromegaly** in adults (gorilla-like facial enlargement); hyposecretion causes **Dwarfism** (Pituitary dwarf / Midgets).
20. **Posterior Pituitary Hormones (Neurohypophysis)**: Stores and releases two peptide neurohormones synthesized by hypothalamic neurons: **Oxytocin** (stimulates powerful uterine contractions during parturition/labor and milk ejection / "let-down" from mammary glands upon infant suckling) and **Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin)** (promotes water reabsorption in renal tubules; deficiency causes Diabetes Insipidus).
21. **The Thyroid Gland**: Largest endocrine gland in the human body; butterfly-shaped with two lateral lobes situated on either side of the trachea, joined by the **Isthmus**; follicular cells require **Iodine** to synthesize **Thyroxine ($T_4$)** and **Triiodothyronine ($T_3$)**, which regulate basal metabolic rate (BMR); also secretes **Calcitonin** from parafollicular C-cells, which lowers blood calcium levels.
22. **Thyroid Pathologies**: **Simple / Endemic Goitre**: Enlargement of the thyroid gland due to dietary iodine deficiency (prevalent in sub-Himalayan regions); **Cretinism**: Congenital infant hypothyroidism causing severe mental retardation, dwarfism, and pot belly; **Myxoedema**: Severe adult hypothyroidism causing sluggish metabolism, puffiness, and cold intolerance; **Grave's Disease (Exophthalmic Goitre)**: Hyperthyroid autoimmune disorder characterized by high BMR, tachycardia, and bulging protruding eyeballs (**Exophthalmos**).
23. **The Parathyroid Glands**: Four pea-sized glands embedded on the posterior surface of the thyroid gland lobes; secrete **Parathyroid Hormone (PTH / Collip's Hormone)**, a hypercalcemic peptide hormone that raises blood calcium levels by stimulating bone resorption (osteoclast activity) and renal/intestinal calcium reabsorption; acts as an antagonist to calcitonin; deficiency causes painful tetanic muscle spasms (**Hypocalcemic Tetany**).
24. **Adrenal Cortex (Outer 80%)**: Derived from mesoderm; organized into three zones (Mnemonic: **GFR**): Zona Glomerulosa secretes **Mineralocorticoids** (**Aldosterone**, regulates sodium/potassium balance and blood pressure); Zona Fasciculata secretes **Glucocorticoids** (**Cortisol**, "stress hormone", stimulates gluconeogenesis, anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive); Zona Reticularis secretes small amounts of sex corticoids/androgens.
25. **Adrenal Medulla (Inner 20%)**: Derived from ectoderm (sympathetic neural crest cells); secretes catecholamines **Adrenaline (Epinephrine)** and **Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine)**; known as the **"Fight-or-Flight" / Emergency / 3F (Fright, Fight, Flight) Hormones**; rapidly increases heart rate, cardiac output, respiratory rate, pupillary dilation, and glycogen breakdown into glucose during acute emotional or physical stress.
26. **The Pineal Gland (Epiphysis Cerebri)**: Located on the dorsal roof of the diencephalon; secretes **Melatonin**, which regulates the 24-hour diurnal **Circadian Rhythm (Sleep-Wake Cycle)**, core body temperature, and delays premature puberty.
27. **The Thymus Gland**: Located behind the sternum in the anterior mediastinum; secretes **Thymosins**, which stimulate the maturation and differentiation of **T-lymphocytes**; acts as the primary "training school" of cellular immunity; prominent in infants, undergoes progressive age-related atrophy after puberty, leading to weakened immunity in the elderly.
28. **Endocrine Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans)**: Microscopic clusters of endocrine cells scattered throughout the pancreas: **Alpha ($lpha$) cells** (~20%) secrete **Glucagon** (hyperglycemic hormone, stimulates hepatic glycogenolysis); **Beta ($eta$) cells** (~70%) secrete **Insulin** (hypoglycemic 51-amino-acid peptide, facilitates cellular glucose uptake and glycogenesis; discovered by **Banting and Best, 1921**); **Delta ($\delta$) cells** (~5%) secrete **Somatostatin** (inhibits both insulin and glucagon secretion).
29. **Male Reproductive System Anatomy**: Primary sex organs are the **Testes**; suspended outside the abdominal cavity in the **Scrotum**, which maintains an optimal testicular temperature **$2–2.5^\circ\text{C}$ lower** than normal core body temperature, essential for viable **Spermatogenesis**; failure of testes to descend into the scrotum is called **Cryptorchidism**; microscopic **Seminiferous Tubules** produce spermatozoa, while **Interstitial Cells of Leydig** secrete **Testosterone** (male androgen).
30. **Male Accessory Ducts & Glands**: Sperm travel from seminiferous tubules through Rete Testis $\rightarrow$ Vasa Efferentia $\rightarrow$ **Epididymis** (site of physiological sperm maturation and motility acquisition) $\rightarrow$ **Vas Deferens** $\rightarrow$ Ejaculatory Duct $\rightarrow$ Urethra; accessory glands include paired **Seminal Vesicles** (secrete alkaline fluid rich in **Fructose**, providing energy for sperm), single **Prostate Gland** (secretes milky slightly alkaline fluid), and paired **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) Glands** (secrete lubricating pre-ejaculatory fluid).
31. **Female Reproductive System Anatomy**: Primary sex organs are the paired **Ovaries**; produce female gametes (ova) and secrete steroid hormones (**Estrogen** from ovarian Graafian follicles and **Progesterone** from the Corpus Luteum); paired **Fallopian Tubes (Oviducts)** consist of Fimbriae, Infundibulum, **Ampulla** (the normal site of **Fertilisation** where sperm meets ovum), and Isthmus; lead into the pear-shaped **Uterus (Womb)** composed of perimetrium, thick smooth muscle myometrium, and glandular inner endometrium.
32. **The Menstrual Cycle Phases**: Normal human cycle lasts ~28 days; **Menstrual Phase** (days 1–5, shedding of functional endometrial lining due to sharp decline in progesterone and estrogen); **Follicular / Proliferative Phase** (days 6–13, FSH stimulates Graafian follicle development and estrogen production, rebuilding the endometrium); **Ovulatory Phase** (day 14, rapid surge in **Luteinizing Hormone / LH Surge** triggers rupture of the mature Graafian follicle and release of the secondary oocyte); **Luteal / Secretory Phase** (days 15–28, ruptured follicle transforms into the **Corpus Luteum**, which secretes large amounts of **Progesterone** to maintain endometrial thickness for pregnancy).
33. **Fertilisation, Blastocyst Implantation & Placenta**: Fertilisation occurs in the **Ampulla of the fallopian tube**; the single-cell zygote undergoes rapid mitotic cleavage divisions into a morula and then a hollow sphere of cells called the **Blastocyst**; blastocyst embeds into the uterine endometrium (**Implantation**, occurs 6–8 days post-fertilisation); outer trophoblast cells develop chorionic villi, interdigitating with uterine tissue to form the **Placenta** (acts as a structural and physiological exchange organ and temporary endocrine gland).
34. **Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**: Secreted by placental syncytiotrophoblasts immediately after blastocyst implantation; rescues and maintains the corpus luteum, preventing menstrual breakdown and sustaining progesterone secretion; excreted in maternal urine and detected by home monoclonal antibody pregnancy test kits (**Prega News / Gravindex test**).
35. **Contraception & Reproductive Health**: **Barrier Methods**: Condoms (prevent STI/HIV transmission and pregnancy), diaphragms, cervical caps; **Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)**: Non-medicated (Lippes Loop), Copper-releasing (**Copper-T / CuT**, Cu ions suppress sperm motility and fertilising capacity), and Hormone-releasing (LNG-20, Progestasert); **Oral Contraceptive Pills**: Combined estrogen-progestin pills inhibit ovulation; **Saheli (Centchroman)** is a once-a-week non-steroidal oral contraceptive pill developed by the **Central Drug Research Institute (CDRI), Lucknow**; **Surgical Sterilization**: Permanent contraception via **Vasectomy** in males (ligation of vas deferens) and **Tubectomy** in females (ligation of fallopian tubes).
36. **Human Adult Skeleton Breakdown (206 Bones)**: Composed of **Axial Skeleton (80 bones)**: Skull (22 bones: 8 cranial + 14 facial), Hyoid bone (1), Ear ossicles (6: 3 in each ear), Vertebral Column (26 bones: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 1 fused sacrum, 1 fused coccyx), Sternum (1), and Ribs (24 bones / 12 pairs); and **Appendicular Skeleton (126 bones)**: Pectoral girdle (4: 2 clavicles + 2 scapulae), Upper limbs (60: 30 in each arm), Pelvic girdle (2 hip / coxal bones), and Lower limbs (60: 30 in each leg).
37. **Special Cranial and Thoracic Bone Facts**: The **Hyoid bone** is a U-shaped solitary bone located at the base of the buccal cavity above the larynx; it does not articulate with any other bone in the human skeleton; the human neck has **7 Cervical Vertebrae** (true for almost all mammals, including the giraffe); first cervical vertebra ($C_1$) is the **Atlas** (articulates with occipital condyles for "Yes" nodding movement); second vertebra ($C_2$) is the **Axis** (has odontoid peg for "No" rotational head movement).
38. **Human Rib Cage Anatomy**: 12 pairs of ribs categorized into three classes: **True Ribs (Pairs 1–7)** articulate posteriorly with thoracic vertebrae and anteriorly attach directly to the sternum via costal hyaline cartilages; **False Ribs (Pairs 8–10)** do not attach directly to the sternum, but join the costal cartilage of the 7th rib; **Floating Ribs (Pairs 11 and 12)** do not attach anteriorly at all, having free anterior cartilaginous tips that shield kidneys.
39. **Upper and Lower Limb Skeletal Anatomy**: Upper limb (30 bones): **Humerus** (arm) $\rightarrow$ **Radius & Ulna** (forearm) $\rightarrow$ **8 Carpals** (wrist) $\rightarrow$ **5 Metacarpals** (palm) $\rightarrow$ **14 Phalanges** (fingers: 2 in thumb, 3 in each finger); Lower limb (30 bones): **Femur (Thigh bone, longest, heaviest, and strongest bone in the human body)** $\rightarrow$ **Patella (Kneecap, a sesamoid bone formed within the quadriceps tendon)** $\rightarrow$ **Tibia & Fibula** (shin and calf; tibia is the main weight-bearing shin bone) $\rightarrow$ **7 Tarsals** (ankle; heel bone is calcaneus) $\rightarrow$ **5 Metatarsals** (sole) $\rightarrow$ **14 Phalanges** (toes).
40. **Bone Histology & Composition**: Bone is a specialized rigid vascular connective tissue; matrix consists of organic collagen fibers (~33%) and inorganic mineral salts (~67%), predominantly crystalline **Calcium Phosphate / Hydroxyapatite**; concentric cylindrical functional units of compact bone are **Haversian Systems (Osteons)** traversed by central Haversian canals transmitting blood vessels and nerves; bone cells include **Osteocytes** (mature bone cells inside lacunae), **Osteoblasts** (bone-forming cells), and **Osteoclasts** (multinucleated bone-resorbing cells).
41. **Joints of the Human Skeleton**: Three functional and structural classes: **Fibrous / Synarthrodial Joints (Immovable)**: Bones joined by dense collagen fibers with no articular cavity (e.g., **Sutures of the skull**, teeth in sockets/gomphosis); **Cartilaginous / Amphiarthrodial Joints (Slightly Movable)**: Bones connected by hyaline or fibrocartilage allowing limited movement (e.g., **Intervertebral discs** between adjacent vertebrae, **Pubic symphysis**); **Synovial / Diarthrodial Joints (Freely Movable)**: Separated by a fluid-filled synovial cavity, lined by synovial membrane secreting lubricating **Synovial Fluid**, and cushioned by articular hyaline cartilage.
42. **Synovial Joint Subtypes**: **Ball and Socket Joint**: Multiaxial, maximum range of movement in all planes (e.g., Shoulder joint between head of humerus and glenoid cavity of scapula; Hip joint between head of femur and acetabulum); **Hinge Joint**: Uniaxial movement like a door hinge (e.g., **Elbow joint, Knee joint, Interphalangeal finger joints**); **Pivot Joint**: Rotational movement around a central axis (e.g., **Atlanto-axial joint** between atlas and axis, proximal radio-ulnar joint); **Gliding Joint**: Flat surfaces sliding over one another (e.g., **Intercarpal joints** between wrist bones, intertarsal joints); **Saddle Joint**: Biaxial joint like rider on saddle (e.g., **Carpometacarpal joint of the human thumb**, enabling opposable thumb grip).
43. **Connective Support Tissues: Tendon vs Ligament**: **Tendon**: Dense regular white fibrous connective tissue composed of tough, inelastic, parallel collagen bundles; **attaches muscle to bone**; strain or rupture causes muscle tendonitis; **Ligament**: Dense regular yellow elastic connective tissue composed of elastic fibers and collagen; **attaches bone to bone** across a joint; provides joint stability; excessive stretching or tearing of a ligament is clinically defined as a **Sprain** (sprain = ligament tear; strain = tendon/muscle tear).
44. **Cartilage Characteristics**: Flexible, non-vascular supporting connective tissue; extracellular matrix consists of chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans synthesized by **Chondrocytes** situated inside lacunae; lacks direct blood vessels (nutrients diffuse slowly through the fibrous perichondrium, explaining extremely slow healing of cartilaginous injuries); **Hyaline Cartilage** is the most abundant type (covers articular ends of long bones, costal cartilages, rings of trachea, embryonic skeleton).
45. **Three Types of Human Muscle Tissues**: **Skeletal Muscle (Striated / Voluntary)**: Cylindrical, unbranched, multinucleated fibers with peripheral nuclei; displays alternating light and dark transverse striations; attached to bones, under conscious somatic motor control; prone to fatigue; **Smooth Muscle (Non-striated / Involuntary / Visceral)**: Spindle-shaped (fusiform), unbranched, single central nucleus; lacks striations; lines walls of internal hollow organs (stomach, intestines, blood vessels, bronchi); autonomous, slow sustained contractions, fatigue-resistant; **Cardiac Muscle (Striated / Involuntary)**: Found exclusively in the myocardial wall of the heart; cylindrical, branched, uninucleated fibers; displays cross-striations and unique **Intercalated Discs** (gap junctions allowing synchronous wave-like contraction of the syncytium); myogenic, highly resistant to fatigue.
46. **Microscopic Structure of Skeletal Muscle Fibers**: Functional contractile unit of a muscle myofibril is the **Sarcomere** (segment between two successive transverse **Z-lines**); composed of overlapping thin and thick myofilaments: **Thin Filaments** are composed of **Actin**, surrounded by regulatory protein filaments **Tropomyosin** and calcium-binding **Troponin** complexes; **Thick Filaments** are composed of the motor protein **Myosin** with protruding cross-bridge heads containing ATPase enzymes.
47. **Sliding Filament Theory of Muscle Contraction**: Proposed by **H.E. Huxley and A.F. Huxley (1954)**; states that muscle contraction occurs not by shortening of myofilaments, but by the **sliding of thin actin filaments past thick myosin filaments toward the center of the sarcomere (H-zone)**; when a motor nerve action potential depolarizes the muscle membrane (sarcolemma), the sarcoplasmic reticulum floods the cytoplasm with **Calcium ions ($\mathbf{Ca^{2+}}$)**; $Ca^{2+}$ binds to troponin, displacing tropomyosin and exposing active binding sites on actin; myosin heads bind actin, hydrolyze ATP, and execute a power stroke; during contraction, the **Sarcomere shortens, the I-band narrows, the H-zone disappears, while the A-band length remains completely unchanged**.
48. **Rigor Mortis**: State of muscular rigidity and stiffening of the body that sets in 3 to 4 hours after biological death; caused by cessation of cellular respiration, leading to complete depletion of **ATP**; without ATP, myosin cross-bridge heads cannot detach from actin filaments, locking muscles in a permanent contracted state until autolytic lysosomal enzymes decompose muscle proteins (~15–24 hours later).
49. **Muscle Fatigue & Lactic Acidosis**: During prolonged intense anaerobic muscular exertion, oxygen supply to skeletal muscle tissues becomes insufficient for aerobic oxidative phosphorylation; muscle cells switch to anaerobic glycolysis, converting pyruvate into **Lactic Acid** via lactate dehydrogenase; accumulation of lactic acid decreases intracellular pH, impairs calcium binding to troponin, and precipitates **Muscle Fatigue / Cramps**; lactic acid is transported via blood to the liver and converted back into glycogen via the **Cori Cycle**.
50. **Key Skeletal and Muscular Pathologies Drill**: **Osteoporosis**: Age-related metabolic bone disorder characterized by decreased bone mineral density and deterioration of bone microarchitecture, leading to fragility fractures (common in post-menopausal women due to deficiency of **Estrogen**); **Rickets & Osteomalacia**: Defective bone mineralization due to deficiency of **Vitamin D or Calcium**; **Arthritis**: Inflammatory inflammation of joints; **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Autoimmune disorder where immune antibodies attack the synovial membrane, forming invasive inflammatory pannus tissue; **Gout**: Deposition of needle-shaped monosodium urate crystals in joints due to elevated uric acid; **Myasthenia Gravis**: Autoimmune neuromuscular disorder where autoantibodies block acetylcholine receptors at motor endplates, causing progressive skeletal muscle weakness and fatigue.

### 2. Functional Architecture of the Human Brain

| Brain Subdivision | Major Anatomical Structures | Core Physiological Center & Function | Clinical Deficit / Result of Damage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forebrain (Prosencephalon)**| **Cerebrum (Cortex)** | Seat of **intelligence, memory, reasoning, conscious sensation, voluntary movement** | Dementia, paralysis, aphasia (loss of speech) |
| **Forebrain** | **Hypothalamus** | Master homeostatic center: **thermoregulation (body thermostat), hunger, thirst, sleep-wake cycle, osmolarity**, controls pituitary | Poikilothermia, hyperphagia, diabetes insipidus |
| **Forebrain** | **Thalamus** | Major **relay station** for all sensory inputs (except smell) to cerebral cortex | Sensory processing disruption, pain syndromes |
| **Midbrain (Mesencephalon)** | **Corpora Quadrigemina** | 4 colliculi: Superior (visual reflexes) & Inferior (auditory reflexes) | Visual and auditory reflex coordination deficits |
| **Hindbrain (Rhombencephalon)**| **Cerebellum** | Coordinates **voluntary motor movements, posture, muscle tone, and body equilibrium (balance)** | **Ataxia**, loss of muscular coordination, drunken gait |
| **Hindbrain** | **Medulla Oblongata** | Autonomic involuntary reflex centers: **cardiovascular, respiration, swallowing, vomiting, coughing, sneezing** | **Fatal respiratory / cardiac arrest** if damaged |
