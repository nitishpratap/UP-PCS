## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. In the central administration of the **Delhi Sultanate**, the four primary imperial ministries were:
   - **Diwan-i-Wizarat** (Finance, taxation, and general revenue administration, headed by the *Wazir* / Prime Minister).
   - **Diwan-i-Arz** (Military affairs, recruitment, inspection, and salaries, created by Sultan **Ghiyasuddin Balban**, headed by the *Ariz-i-Mamalik*).
   - **Diwan-i-Insha** (Royal state correspondence, imperial edicts, and farmans, headed by the *Dabir-i-Khas*).
   - **Diwan-i-Rasalat** (Foreign diplomatic affairs, religious matters, and humanitarian appeals).
2. Specialized departments created by individual Delhi Sultans:
   - **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj** (Department to investigate and recover arrears from corrupt revenue officials) — created by **Alauddin Khalji**.
   - **Diwan-i-Riyasat** (Department of market control, commerce, and trader registration) — created by **Alauddin Khalji**.
   - **Diwan-i-Kohi** (Department of agriculture to bring barren lands under state cultivation through *Sondhar* agricultural loans) — created by **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**.
   - **Diwan-i-Khairat** (Charity department to support poor Muslims, orphans, and arrange marriages for destitute girls) — created by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**.
   - **Diwan-i-Bandagan** (Department of royal slaves, managing over 180,000 imperial slaves trained in crafts) — created by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**.
   - **Diwan-i-Istihqaq** (Department of pensions for elderly and disabled citizens) — created by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**.
3. **Sultan Ghiyasuddin Balban** (1266–1287) instituted the extreme Persian court etiquette of **Sijda** (prostration on the ground before the Sultan) and **Paibos** (kissing the Sultan's feet) to elevate royal majesty, celebrating the Persian spring festival of **Nowruz** (नवरोज़).
4. Balban propounded the divine theory of kingship: the Sultan is the **Niyabat-i-Khudai** (Representative / Deputy of God on Earth) and the **Zill-i-Ilahi** (Shadow of God on Earth).
5. The **Iqta System** (इक्ता प्रणाली) was introduced into India by Sultan **Shamsuddin Iltutmish**; it was an assignment of revenue from a designated territory given to civil or military officers (*Iqtadars / Muqtis / Walis*) in lieu of cash salary, with surplus revenue (**Fawazil**) deposited into the imperial treasury; Firoz Shah Tughlaq made the Iqta system strictly hereditary.
6. **Alauddin Khalji** (1296–1316) revolutionized Sultanate administration by:
   - Introducing the **Dagh** (branding of royal cavalry horses) and **Chehra / Huliya** (descriptive roll register of individual soldiers) to eliminate military fraud.
   - Strictly enforcing comprehensive **Market Control Regulations**, fixing prices for grain, cloth, cattle, and slaves across three distinct markets in Delhi, overseen by the **Shahna-i-Mandi** (market superintendent), with spies (**Munhiyans**) reporting directly to the Sultan.
   - Imposing the land revenue at **50% of the gross agricultural produce** (*Kharaj*), based on actual land measurement (**Masahat**), along with two new direct taxes: **Ghari** (house tax) and **Chari** (grazing tax on milch cattle).
7. **Amir Khusrau** (1253–1325), hailed as the **'Parrot of India' (Tuti-e-Hind)**, served as the court poet to seven Delhi Sultans (from Balban to Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq); he pioneered the **Sabaq-i-Hindi** (Indian style of Persian poetry), formulated the **Khayal** and **Tarana** genres, created the **Qawwali**, and is credited with inventing the **Sitar** and **Tabla**.
8. Master literary works of Amir Khusrau:
   - **Qiran-us-Sadain** (Meeting of two stars: Sultan Kaiqubad and his father Bughra Khan)
   - **Miftah-ul-Futuh** (Victories of Jalaluddin Khalji)
   - **Khazain-ul-Futuh / Tarikh-i-Ilahi** (Victories and southern Deccan military campaigns of Alauddin Khalji led by Malik Kafur)
   - **Ashika** (Tragic romance of Gujarat princess Deval Devi and Prince Khizr Khan)
   - **Nuh Sipihr** (Nine Skies; impassioned praise of India's flora, fauna, climate, philosophy, and languages)
   - **Tughlaqnama** (Rise of Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq; Khusrau's final work).
9. **Ziauddin Barani** authored the monumental Persian historical chronicle **Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi** (covering Delhi Sultans from Balban's accession in 1266 to the sixth year of Firoz Shah Tughlaq's reign, 1357) and the political treatise on governance, the **Fatawa-i-Jahandari**.
10. **Minhaj-i-Siraj Juzjani** authored **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** (1260 CE), an indispensable primary chronicle of the early Delhi Sultanate and the Turkic conquests of Northern India down to Sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud.
11. **Ibn Battuta**, the famed Moroccan traveler from Tangier, visited India during the reign of **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (arrived 1333 CE); he was appointed the **Qazi (Chief Judge) of Delhi** for eight years, later sent as Tughlaq's ambassador to China, and recorded his travels in the famous travelogue **Rihla** (सफरनामा).
12. **Sher Shah Suri** (1540–1545), founder of the Sur Empire, introduced enduring administrative reforms:
    - Standardized silver currency called the **Rupiya** (178 grains pure silver, predecessor of modern Rupee) and copper coin called the **Dam**.
    - Reconstructed and paved the **Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-i-Azam)** from Sonargaon (Bengal) to Attock (Peshawar, Pakistan), building shaded tree avenues, distance markers (*Kos Minars*), and **1,700 roadside caravanserais (Sarais)**.
    - Issued title deeds (**Patta**) to cultivators fixing revenue, securing agreements (**Qabuliyat**) accepting responsibility.
13. The **Mansabdari System** (मनसबदारी व्यवस्था), the administrative and military steel-frame of the Mughal Empire, was introduced by Emperor **Akbar** in **1571 CE** (11th regnal year), adapted from Mongolian decimal military traditions.
14. Every Mughal civil and military officer was assigned a single composite **Mansab (Rank)** consisting of two numerical grades:
    - **Zat** (ज़ात / personal rank): Determined the officer's personal status, precedence in court, and baseline salary.
    - **Sawar** (सवार / cavalry rank): Determined the exact number of mounted cavalrymen (*sawars*) and horses the officer was required to maintain for state service.
15. In the Mansabdari hierarchy, the **Sawar rank could never exceed the Zat rank**; a mansabdar holding equal Zat and Sawar was First Class, one holding Sawar between half and equal was Second Class, and one holding Sawar less than half was Third Class.
16. Modifications to the Mansabdari system:
    - Emperor **Jahangir** introduced the **Du-aspa Sih-aspa** (दो-अस्पा सिह-अस्पा) rule, allowing select favored commanders to maintain double or triple the contingent of horses without promoting their Zat rank.
    - Emperor **Shah Jahan** introduced the **Monthly Scale (Mahina System / Month-ratios)** (e.g., 6-month, 8-month scales) due to the deficit between estimated revenue (*Jama*) and actual realization (*Hasil*).
17. The **Dahsala System** (दहसाला प्रणाली / Ain-i-Dahsala), also known as the **Zabti System**, was introduced by Akbar in **1580 CE** (24th regnal year), formulated by his finance minister **Raja Todar Mal**.
18. Under the Dahsala system, the average yield and average market prices of each crop were calculated over the **preceding 10 years (1570–1580 CE)**; the state demanded **one-third (1/3rd)** of the average produce, converted into fixed cash rates per bigha based on regional price schedules (*Dastur-ul-Amal*).
19. Under Akbar's land classification, agricultural land was divided into four categories:
    - **Polaj** (पोलज): Land cultivated continuously every year without interruption; highest revenue.
    - **Parauti** (परौती): Land left fallow for 1 or 2 years to recover natural fertility.
    - **Chachar** (चाचर): Land left fallow for 3 or 4 years before being recultivated.
    - **Banjar** (बंजर): Uncultivated wasteland left unploughed for 5 or more years; lowest tax rate.
20. Religious and philosophical evolution of Emperor **Akbar**:
    - **1562:** Prohibited the enslavement of prisoners of war and their families.
    - **1563:** Abolished the **Pilgrimage Tax** (*Tirtha Yatra Kar*) on Hindus visiting holy places.
    - **1564:** Abolished the discriminatory **Jizya Tax** (जज़िया) on non-Muslim subjects (*Zimmis*).
    - **1575:** Constructed the **Ibadat Khana** (इबादत खाना / Hall of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri for Thursday evening theological debates among Sunni theologians, later expanded in 1578 to include Shias, Hindus (Purushottam, Devi), Jains (Honavijaya Suri), Parsis (Dastur Meherji Rana), and Christian Jesuits (Father Monserrate, Aquaviva).
    - **1579:** Promulgated the **Mahzarnama (Infallibility Decree)**, drafted by Shaikh Mubarak, declaring Akbar the supreme arbiter (*Imam-i-Adil*) entitled to choose between conflicting interpretations of Islamic law in the interest of public welfare.
    - **1582:** Initiated **Tauhid-i-Ilahi / Din-i-Ilahi** (Divine Monotheism), an ethical code of conduct emphasizing rationalism, sun worship, non-violence, and universal tolerance; only 18 prominent courtiers embraced it, with **Raja Birbal (Mahesh Das)** being the solitary Hindu member (Raja Man Singh openly refused).
    - Established the state philosophical principle of **Sulh-i-Kul** (सुल्ह-ए-कुल / Universal Peace and Harmony among all religions).
21. The **Navaratnas (Nine Jewels)** of Akbar’s imperial court:
    1. **Abul Fazl** (Court historian, prime minister, author of *Akbarnama* & *Ain-i-Akbari*)
    2. **Faizi** (Poet laureate / *Malik-ush-Shu'ara*, translated *Lilavati* into Persian)
    3. **Mian Tansen** (Supreme musician, disciple of Swami Haridas)
    4. **Raja Birbal** (Mahesh Das; supreme wit, advisor, commander)
    5. **Raja Todar Mal** (Finance minister, architect of Dahsala land revenue system)
    6. **Raja Man Singh** (Commander-in-chief, governor of Bengal and Kabul)
    7. **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** (Son of Bairam Khan, Hindi poet of *Dohas*, translated *Baburnama* into Persian)
    8. **Fakir Aziao-Din** (Sufi mystic and religious advisor)
    9. **Mullah Do-Piyaza** (Witty advisor and strategist).
22. The **Translation Department (Maktab Khana)** established by Akbar at Fatehpur Sikri:
    - **Mahabharata** was translated into Persian by a team led by **Abdul Qadir Badauni**, Naqib Khan, and Faizi, illustrated under the title **Razmnama** (Book of War).
    - **Ramayana** was translated into Persian by **Abdul Qadir Badauni**.
    - **Rajatarangini** (History of Kashmir) was translated into Persian by **Mulla Shah Muhammad** and Badauni.
    - **Atharvaveda** was translated by Haji Ibrahim Sarhindi.
    - **Lilavati** (Bhaskaracharya's mathematical treatise) was translated into Persian by **Faizi**.
    - **Panchatantra** was translated into Persian by Abul Fazl under the title **Anwar-i-Suhaili** (Lights of Canopus).
    - **Tuzuk-i-Baburi** (Babur’s memoirs in Chagatai Turkish) was translated into Persian by **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** as the *Baburnama*.
23. **Prince Dara Shikoh** (eldest son of Shah Jahan), a scholar-mystic of the Qadiri Sufi order, translated **50 Upanishads** from Sanskrit into Persian in 1657 with the help of Varanasi pandits under the title **Sirr-i-Akbar** (The Great Secret); he also authored the philosophical treatise **Majma-ul-Bahrain** (The Mingling of the Two Oceans), demonstrating the fundamental spiritual unity between Vedanta and Sufism.
24. **Gulbadan Begum** (daughter of Emperor Babur and sister of Humayun) authored the **Humayun-nama** at the request of her nephew Akbar, providing an invaluable intimate domestic account of the Mughal harem and court.
25. Foreign travelers visiting Mughal India:
    - **Captain William Hawkins** (1608–1611) visited the court of **Jahangir** representing the English East India Company; received a mansab of 400 and the title *English Khan*.
    - **Sir Thomas Roe** (1615–1619) arrived as the official ambassador of King James I of England to **Jahangir’s** court at Ajmer, successfully securing imperial farmans for English trade factories.
    - **Father Anthony Monserrate** visited the court of **Akbar** (First Jesuit Mission 1580–1582) and authored *Commentarius*.
    - **Francois Bernier** (French physician, 1656–1668) visited during the reigns of Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb, authoring *Travels in the Mogul Empire*; described the War of Succession, crown ownership of all land, and karkhanas.
    - **Jean-Baptiste Tavernier** (French merchant and jeweler) visited India six times between 1630 and 1668, providing detailed accounts of the diamond mines of Golconda and the Peacock Throne of Shah Jahan.
    - **Niccolao Manucci** (Italian traveler and artilleryman) spent five decades in India, authoring the monumental memoirs **Storia do Mogor**.

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

### 1. Four Sultanate Central Ministries — Mnemonic: `W-A-I-R` (वज़ीर आया इंशा रसूल)

- **P**arauti (Fallow 1–2 years)
- **C**hachar (Fallow 3–4 years)
- **B**anjar (Barren 5+ years)

- **1575:** **I**badat Khana built
- **1579:** **M**ahzar issued
- **1580:** **D**ahsala system introduced
- **1582:** **D**in-i-Ilahi declared
- **1601:** **B**uland Darwaza built for Gujarat victory
