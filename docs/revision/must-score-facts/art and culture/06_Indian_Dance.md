## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The **Sangeet Natak Akademi (संगीत नाटक अकादेमी)** officially recognises **eight** classical dances: Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, and Sattriya.
2. **Sattriya** (सत्रीय) of Assam was recognized as a classical dance in **November 2000**. If an exam option lists seven, Sattriya has been omitted.
3. **Chhau** (छऊ) is a UNESCO-inscribed martial dance (2010); it is recognized as classical by the Ministry of Culture, but is **not** on the SNA official eight list.
4. **Bharatanatyam** (भरतनाट्यम्) originated in **Tamil Nadu** as *Sadir Natyam* or *Dasi Attam*, traditionally performed by Devadasis in Shaivite temples.
5. The modern revival of Bharatanatyam was spearheaded by **E. Krishna Iyer** and **Rukmini Devi Arundale**, who founded **Kalakshetra** at Adyar, Chennai in 1936.
6. The foundational physical stance of Bharatanatyam is **Araimandi** (अर्धमण्डली / half-sitting demi-plié position with knees turned outward).
7. The standard Bharatanatyam solo performance order (*Margam*) is: **Alarippu** (invocation) → **Jatiswaram** (pure nritta) → **Shabdam** (first abhinaya) → **Varnam** (central climax combining nritta + nritya) → **Padam** (slow emotional abhinaya) → **Javali** (lighter tempo) → **Tillana** (fast rhythmic finale) → **Mangalam**.
8. **Kathak** (कथक) is the only classical dance originating in **North India / Uttar Pradesh** (उत्तर प्रदेश), derived from ancient temple storytellers called *Kathakars*.
9. Kathak evolved from temple devotion to court entertainment under the Mughal emperors and the **Nawabs of Awadh** (notably Nawab Wajid Ali Shah).
10. The hallmarks of Kathak are intricate footwork (**Tatkar**), rhythmic syllables (**Bols**), and dazzling pirouettes (**Chakkars**).
11. Kathak has three major traditional Gharanas: **Lucknow Gharana** (founded by Ishwar Prasad, perfected under Thakur Prasad & Kalka-Bindadin; emphasizes graceful abhinaya, bhava, and Thumri), **Jaipur Gharana** (emphasizes speed, technical virtuosity, and vigorous footwork), and **Banaras Gharana** (founded by Janaki Prasad; distinct bol syllables).
12. Kathak is accompanied by **Hindustani classical music** with the **Tabla** and **Pakhawaj**, unlike South Indian forms which rely on Carnatic music and Mridangam.
13. **Kathakali** (कथकली) is a stylized all-male classical dance-drama of **Kerala**, developed from earlier temple arts *Ramanattam* and *Krishnanattam*.
14. Kathakali is characterized by elaborate facial makeup (**Chutti**), magnificent headgears, and vibrant facial colour-coding: **Pacha** (green = noble heroes/gods like Rama), **Kathi** (knife = villainous noble kings like Ravana), **Thadi** (bearded: Red beard = vicious demons like Dussasana; White beard = divine beings like Hanuman; Black beard = hunter primitives), and **Minukku** (shining orange-yellow = women, sages, and Brahmins).
15. Kathakali movements are intensely vigorous (**Tandava** dominant) and use no spoken dialogue; actors communicate entirely through 24 mudras and striking facial-eye expressions (*Navarasas*), accompanied by Sopana sangeetam, Chenda, and Maddalam drums.
16. The modern revival of Kathakali was led by poet **Vallathol Narayana Menon**, who founded the **Kerala Kalamandalam** in 1930 at Cheruthuruthy.
17. **Kuchipudi** (कुचिपुड़ी) originated in **Kuchelapuram village**, Krishna district of **Andhra Pradesh**, codified in the 17th century by the Vaishnavite saint **Siddhendra Yogi**.
18. Originally an all-male Brahmin troupe tradition (*Bhagavathalu*), Kuchipudi combines spoken dialogue, song, and dance based on themes from the *Bhagavata Purana*.
19. A signature highlight of Kuchipudi is **Tarangam** (तरंगम), where the dancer balances gracefully with feet poised on the raised rim of a brass plate, often while carrying a pitcher filled with water on the head.
20. Another unique Kuchipudi item is **Manduka Shabdam** (the story of the frog maiden) and **Bhamakalapam** (depicting Satyabhama's pride and love for Krishna).
21. Leading legends of Kuchipudi include **Vempati Chinna Satyam**, **Yamini Krishnamurthy**, and **Raja & Radha Reddy**.
22. **Mohiniyattam** (मोहिनीअट्टम्) is a graceful solo classical dance of **Kerala**, performed exclusively by women.
23. The name literally means 'Dance of the Enchantress' (*Mohini*, the female avatar of Lord Vishnu), embodying the seductive, lyrical **Lasya** style.
24. Mohiniyattam is characterized by soft, swaying, circular torso movements (*Atavukal*) mimicking ocean waves and palm fronds; it uses the distinctive white or off-white gold-bordered **Kasavu sari** and a side-bun hair wreath of jasmine flowers.
25. Mohiniyattam flourished under Maharaja **Swathi Thirunal** of Travancore and was revived in the 20th century by Vallathol Narayana Menon and **Kalyanikutty Amma** ('Mother of Mohiniyattam').
26. **Odissi** (ओडिसी) of **Odisha** is chronicled in the rock reliefs of **Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves** (e.g., Rani Gumpha, 2nd century BCE) and the sculptural friezes of Konark Sun Temple.
27. Odissi evolved through two historical lineages: **Maharis** (temple female dancers) and **Gotipuas** (young boys dressed in female attire performing acrobatic sequences outside temples).
28. The definitive posture of Odissi is **Tribhanga** (त्रिभंग / three-bend deflection of head, torso, and hips) and **Chowk** (चौक / square, masculine stance mimicking Lord Jagannath).
29. Modern Odissi was codified and revived in the 1950s by legendary gurus: **Kelucharan Mohapatra**, **Pankaj Charan Das**, and **Deba Prasad Das**.
30. The typical repertoire of Odissi: **Mangalacharan** → **Battu Nrutya** (nritta honoring Shiva) → **Pallavi** (lyrical elaboration of raga) → **Abhinaya** (expressive rendition, especially Jayadeva’s *Gita Govinda*) → **Moksha** (spiritual liberation).
31. **Manipuri** (मणिपुरी) of **Manipur** is deeply intertwined with Meitei culture and Vaishnavism, celebrated for its sublime devotional **Ras Lila**.
32. Manipuri Ras Lila was patronised and codified by 18th-century king **Bhagya Chandra** (Kartik / Ningthou Ching-Thang Khomba), who designed the signature cylindrical stiff skirt called **Kumil** (or *Potloi*).
33. Manipuri dance is uniquely gentle, fluid, and continuous; feet never strike the ground sharply, and the knees remain close together.
34. The masculine, acrobatic aspect of Manipuri dance is **Pung Cholom** (drum dance) and **Kartal Cholom** (cymbal dance), where dancers perform vigorous leaps while playing their instruments.
35. Manipuri was brought to national and global prominence by **Rabindranath Tagore**, who introduced it at Visva-Bharati, Santiniketan in 1919 after witnessing it at Sylhet.
36. **Sattriya** (सत्रीय) was created in the 15th century by the Vaishnavite saint-reformer **Srimanta Sankaradeva** in the **Sattras** (monasteries) of Assam as part of the *Ekasarana Dharma*.
37. Sattriya was traditionally performed by male monks called **Bhokots** in monastic prayer halls (*Namghar*); its music is based on **Borgeet** (hymns composed by Sankaradeva and Madhavadeva) and accompanied by the **Khol** drum and **Tala** (cymbals).
38. Sattriya incorporates two distinct styles: **Paurashik Bhangi** (masculine/vigorous) and **Stri Bhangi** (feminine/graceful).
39. The ancient bedrock text of all Indian classical dances is the **Natyashastra** (नाट्यशास्त्र) authored by Sage **Bharata Muni** (~200 BCE – 200 CE), known as the 'Fifth Veda' (*Panchama Veda*).
40. The Natyashastra enumerates **Nine Emotional Sentiments (Navarasas)**: Shringara (love), Hasya (comic/mirth), Karuna (pathos/compassion), Raudra (fury/anger), Veera (valour/heroism), Bhayanaka (fear/terror), Bibhatsa (disgust/odious), Adbhuta (wonder/marvel), and **Shanta** (peace/tranquillity, added later by Abhinavagupta).
41. Natyashastra categorizes dramatic expression into **Four Abhinayas**: **Angika** (body gestures), **Vachika** (speech/song), **Aharya** (costume/makeup/props), and **Sattvika** (involuntary physical emotional states like tears, perspiration, trembling).
42. Dance in India is structurally divided into two complementary energies: **Tandava** (तांडव / vigorous, masculine, dynamic, associated with Lord Shiva) and **Lasya** (लास्य / lyrical, graceful, tender, associated with Goddess Parvati).
43. Dance is also triply classified by function: **Nritta** (नृत्त / pure, abstract rhythmic movement without storytelling), **Nritya** (नृत्य / expressive interpretive dance combining rhythm with abhinaya and sentiment), and **Natya** (नाट्य / dramatic theatrical storytelling with narrative plot).
44. The definitive manual for hand gestures (*Hasta Mudras*) is the **Abhinaya Darpana** (अभिनय दर्पण) composed by **Nandikesvara**.
45. Do not confuse **Kathak** (the North Indian classical dance) with **Kathaka**, an ancient reciter or sage mentioned in Vedic and epic texts.
46. Manipuri classical **Ras Lila** must never be confused with **Braj Raslila**; the latter is a folk theatrical dance of Mathura–Vrindavan in Uttar Pradesh.
47. **Charkula** (चरकुला) is an iconic folk dance of the **Braj region** of Uttar Pradesh, wherein female dancers balance a multi-tiered wooden pyramid holding **108 lit oil lamps** on their heads while dancing to Rasiya songs.
48. Other prominent folk dances of Uttar Pradesh include **Karma** (Sonbhadra & Mirzapur tribal belt), **Dhobiya** (Purvanchal washermen community), **Pai Danda** (Bundelkhand Ahir community martial stick dance, equivalent to Dandiya), **Rai** (Bundelkhand women’s celebratory dance), and **Nautanki** (folk opera).
49. **Chhau** features three geographically and stylistically distinct branches: **Seraikella Chhau** (Jharkhand; uses stylized masks), **Purulia Chhau** (West Bengal; uses vibrant oversized theatrical masks), and **Mayurbhanj Chhau** (Odisha; performed **without** masks).
50. **Cheraw** (बम्बू डांस / Bamboo dance) belongs exclusively to **Mizoram** (not Manipur), where dancers step rhythmically between horizontally clapped bamboo staves.
51. **Bihu** is the celebratory agricultural folk dance of **Assam**; **Sattriya** is the classical monastic dance of Assam.
52. **Lavani** is the sensuous, rhythmic folk dance of **Maharashtra**, traditionally performed to the Dholki drum and linked with **Tamasha** folk theatre.
53. **Theyyam** (तेय्यम) and **Koodiyattam** (कूडियाट्टम) belong to **Kerala**; Koodiyattam is Sanskrit temple theatre recognized in UNESCO ICH (2008).

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

### 1. The 8 Classical Dances — Formula: `B-K-K-K-M-O-S-M`
- **B**haratanatyam (Tamil Nadu)
- **K**athak (Uttar Pradesh / North)
- **K**athakali (Kerala)
- **K**uchipudi (Andhra Pradesh)
- **M**ohiniyattam (Kerala)
- **O**dissi (Odisha)
- **S**attriya (Assam — added 2000)
- **M**anipuri (Manipur)
*Memory Hook:* **2 from Kerala** (Kathakali + Mohiniyattam), **1 from UP** (Kathak), **5 others** each from coastal/eastern states (TN, AP, OD, MN, AS).

### 2. Four Types of Abhinaya — Mnemonic: `A-V-A-S` (आवास)
- **A**ngika (Body movements, limbs, mudras)
- **V**achika (Speech, vocal recitation, songs)
- **A**harya (Costumes, makeup, jewelry, stage props)
- **S**attvika (Emotional, mental, inward spiritual feelings — trembling, tears)

### 3. The 3 Chhau Traditions — Mnemonic: `S-P-M`
- **S**eraikella → **J**harkhand (Masks)
- **P**urulia → **W**est Bengal (Grand theatrical masks)
- **M**ayurbhanj → **O**disha (**No masks** → *M-O-N*: Mayurbhanj Odisha No-mask!)

### 4. Folk Dances of Uttar Pradesh — Mnemonic: `C-R-N-D-P` (चरण दीप)
- **C**harkula (Braj — 108 lamps)
- **R**aslila (Braj — Krishna Lila)
- **N**autanki (Kanpur/Hathras — folk opera)
- **D**hobiya (Purvanchal — washermen community)
- **P**ai Danda (Bundelkhand — Ahir stick martial dance)
