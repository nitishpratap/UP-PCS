## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The sacred **Kumbh Mela** (कुंभ मेला) is celebrated cyclically every **12 years** across **four sacred riverine sites** in India:
   - **Prayagraj (Uttar Pradesh)** — at the **Triveni Sangam** (confluence of Ganga, Yamuna, and mythical subterranean Saraswati).
   - **Haridwar (Uttarakhand)** — on the banks of the sacred river **Ganga**.
   - **Ujjain (Madhya Pradesh)** — on the banks of the river **Shipra** (क्षिप्रा).
   - **Nashik-Trimbakeshwar (Maharashtra)** — on the banks of the river **Godavari**.
2. An **Ardh Kumbh** (अर्ध कुंभ / Half Kumbh) is celebrated every **6 years**, but takes place exclusively at **two sites**: **Prayagraj** and **Haridwar**.
3. **Magh Mela** (माघ मेला) is an **annual** holy bathing festival held every year during the Hindu month of Magh (January–February) at the Triveni Sangam in Prayagraj; it is not the 12-year Kumbh.
4. **Pushkar Fair** (पुष्कर मेला) of **Rajasthan** is the world’s largest **camel and livestock fair**, held annually on **Kartik Purnima** (October–November) around the sacred Pushkar Lake and the world's rare consecrated **Brahma Temple**.
5. Do not confuse the Rajasthan Pushkar Fair with the **Pushkar Kumbh**, which took place in **May 2025 at Mana / Keshav Prayag in Uttarakhand** (the last Indian village near Badrinath).
6. **Nauchandi Mela** (नौचंदी मेला) of **Meerut, Uttar Pradesh**, is a historic fair celebrating communal and religious harmony, taking place shortly after Holi for approximately a month; pilgrims pay homage simultaneously at the Hindu **Nauchandi Devi Temple** and the dargah of Sufi saint **Syed Salar Masood (Bale Mian)**.
7. **Bateshwar Mela** (बटेश्वर मेला) in **Agra district, Uttar Pradesh**, is a renowned annual cattle, horse, and camel fair held on the banks of the Yamuna on Kartik Purnima, famous for its crescent cluster of **101 Shiva temples**.
8. **Deva Sharif Mela** (देवा शरीफ मेला) in **Barabanki district, Uttar Pradesh**, is celebrated during the month of Kartik to commemorate the annual Urs of the venerated Sufi saint **Haji Waris Ali Shah** (who preached 'Love is Universal Faith').
9. **Shakumbhari Devi Mela** (शाकंभरी देवी मेला) is organized twice a year during Navratri at the foothills of the Shivalik range in **Saharanpur district, Uttar Pradesh**.
10. **Dadri Mela** (ददरी मेला) in **Ballia district, Uttar Pradesh**, is the second largest cattle fair in India after Sonepur, founded in honor of sage Bhrigu's disciple Dardar Muni, held on Kartik Purnima.
11. **Khichdi Mela** (खिचड़ी मेला) is celebrated annually on **Makar Sankranti** at the **Gorakhnath Temple** in **Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh**, the headquarters of the Nath Panth monastic order.
12. **Lathmar Holi** (लट्ठमार होली) is an iconic pre-Holi celebration held exclusively in **Barsana** (birthplace of Radha) and **Nandgaon** (village of Krishna) in the Braj region of Uttar Pradesh, where women playfully strike men with wooden bamboo sticks (*lathis*) while men defend themselves with leather shields.
13. Other high-frequency fairs of Uttar Pradesh include **Mudia Poono Mela** (Govardhan, Mathura), **Soron Mela** (Kasganj; birthplace of Tulsidas controversy/Varaha shrine), **Kampil Mela** (Farrukhabad; sacred Jain pilgrimage site of 13th Tirthankara Vimalanatha), and **Syed Salar Ghazi Fair** (Bahraich).
14. Uttar Pradesh hosts over **2,250 fairs annually**, with the highest concentration occurring in **Mathura district** (over 85 fairs per year).
15. **Nanda Devi Raj Jat** (नंदा देवी राज जात) is the 'Himalayan Kumbh' of **Uttarakhand**, organized once every **12 years**; it is an arduous 280-km barefoot pilgrimage starting from **Nauti village (Karnaprayag, Chamoli)** and terminating at the high-altitude glacial lake **Roopkund / Homkund** (at the base of Mount Trishul), led by a sacred four-horned ram called **Chausingha Khadu**.
16. **Jauljibi Mela** (जौलजीबी मेला) is an ancient commercial and cultural border fair held in **Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand**, at the scenic confluence of the **Kali and Gori rivers**, historically linking traders from Kumaon, Garhwal, Tibet, and Nepal.
17. **Bagwal Mela** (बगवाल मेला) is celebrated on Raksha Bandhan at the **Devidhura Varahi Devi Temple** in **Champawat district, Uttarakhand**, where four local clans (*Khams*) traditionally pelt stones at each other using wooden shields (*Chhatoli*) until an auspicious amount of blood is shed to appease the Goddess.
18. **Purnagiri Mela** takes place during Chaitra Navratri on the sacred hill of Purnagiri in **Champawat, Uttarakhand**, honoring the spot where Goddess Sati's belly (*Nabhi*) fell.
19. **Hornbill Festival** (हॉर्नबिल महोत्सव), hailed as the **'Festival of Festivals'**, is celebrated annually from **1 to 10 December** at **Kisama Heritage Village** (near Kohima), **Nagaland**.
20. The Hornbill Festival is organized by the State Tourism and Arts & Culture departments to foster inter-tribal harmony among all **17 major Naga tribes**, named after the Indian Hornbill bird revered in Naga folklore.
21. **Ambubachi Mela** (अंबुबाची मेला) is celebrated annually during mid-June (Assamese month of *Ahar*) at the **Kamakhya Temple on Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, Assam**.
22. Ambubachi marks the annual menstruation cycle of Goddess Kamakhya (representing Mother Earth's creative fertility); during the three days, the sanctum remains closed and the Brahmaputra River is believed to turn red, after which pilgrims receive pieces of sanctified red cloth called **Raktavastra**.
23. **Bihu** (बिहू) is the national festival of **Assam**, celebrated three times a year across agricultural cycles:
    - **Rongali / Bohag Bihu** (mid-April) — celebrates the Assamese New Year, sowing of crops, and joy (*Rong*).
    - **Kongali / Kati Bihu** (mid-October) — celebrated with austere earthen lamps (*Akashbanti*) under Tulsi plants to protect standing paddy crops from pests during lean food periods.
    - **Bhogali / Magh Bihu** (mid-January) — post-harvest feast festival, marked by building and burning conical bamboo-straw bonfires called **Meji** and banquet pavilions called **Bhelaghar**.
24. **Chapchar Kut** (चापचार कुट) is the grandest spring festival of the **Mizo people** in **Mizoram**, celebrated in March after the arduous slash-and-burn clearing of jhum bamboo forests, featured by joyous community singing and the **Cheraw bamboo dance**.
25. **Wangala Festival** (वांगला), also known as the **'100 Drums Festival'**, is the post-harvest Thanksgiving festival of the matrilineal **Garo tribe** in **Meghalaya** (and Assam), honoring the Sun God of fertility, **Misi Saljong**.
26. **Nongkrem Dance Festival** (नोंगक्रेम) is a five-day annual religious thanksgiving festival of the indigenous **Khasi tribe** held at **Smit** (the cultural capital of the Khyrim Syiemship in Meghalaya), where unmarried women dance in ceremonial silk robes.
27. **Sekrenyi** (सेक्रेनी) is a 10-day purification festival celebrated in February by the **Angami Naga tribe** of Nagaland, involving ritual male cleansing in sacred village wells.
28. **Moatsu Mong** is a lively post-sowing festival celebrated in the first week of May by the **Ao Naga tribe** of Nagaland.
29. **Torgya Festival** is a three-day monastic ritual festival celebrated by the Buddhist **Monpa tribe** at the **Tawang Monastery in Arunachal Pradesh**, featuring sacred costumed and masked dances (*Chham*) to ward off evil spirits.
30. **Losar** (लोसार) is the Tibetan Buddhist **New Year festival**, celebrated with prayers, butter lamps, and mask dances in **Ladakh**, **Arunachal Pradesh** (Tawang), **Sikkim**, and **Himachal Pradesh** (Spiti/Kinnaur).
31. **Losoong / Namsoong** is the traditional Sikkimese New Year celebrated in December, marking the end of harvest by the **Bhutia** (Losoong) and **Lepcha** (Namsoong) communities in **Sikkim**, featuring the famous *Black Hat* Cham dance at Phodong Monastery.
32. **Saga Dawa** (सागा दावा) is the holiest Buddhist festival in **Sikkim**, commemorating the triple-blessed day of Lord Buddha's **Birth, Enlightenment, and Parinirvana** on the full moon day of the fourth Tibetan month.
33. **Kharchi Puja** (खारची पूजा) is the premier royal tribal festival of **Tripura**, held in July at Puran Agartala, where **Fourteen Gods (Choddodevatha)** are cleansed and propitiated at the Saidra river; it is followed a week later by **Ker Puja** (a strict lockdown ritual to protect the capital).
34. **Sangai Festival** is the grand state cultural festival of **Manipur** (November), named after the rare, endangered state animal—the brow-antlered **Sangai deer** found exclusively in **Keibul Lamjao National Park** on Loktak Lake.
35. **Yaoshang** (याओशांग) is Manipur's most vibrant spring festival, celebrated for five days coinciding with Holi by the Meitei community, famous for the traditional nocturnal circular folk dance **Thabal Chongba**.
36. **Pongal** (पोंगल) is a four-day thanksgiving harvest festival celebrated in **Tamil Nadu** during mid-January, coinciding with the sun's northward journey (*Uttarayana*):
    - *Day 1 (Bhogi Pongal):* Burning of discarded household rubbish in a bonfire to Lord Indra.
    - *Day 2 (Surya Pongal):* Boiling freshly harvested rice and milk in a new clay pot until it overflows (*'Pongalo Pongal!'*), dedicated to the Sun God.
    - *Day 3 (Mattu Pongal):* Decorating, feeding, and venerating cattle, celebrated with the traditional bull-taming sport **Jallikattu** (held prominently in Alanganallur, Madurai).
    - *Day 4 (Kaanum Pongal):* Family visits and social reunions.
37. **Onam** (ओणम) is the ten-day national harvest festival of **Kerala**, celebrated in the Malayalam month of *Chingam* (August–September) to welcome the annual homecoming of the benevolent mythical demon king **Mahabali**.
38. Key highlights of Onam:
    - **Pookkalam** (intricate floral floor rangoli carpets laid outside households).
    - **Onasadya** (grand nine-course vegetarian feast served on banana leaves).
    - **Vallam Kali** (legendary snake boat races, foremost being the **Nehru Trophy Boat Race** held on Punnamada Lake in Alappuzha).
    - **Pulikali** (vibrant painted tiger folk dance in Thrissur).
39. **Thrissur Pooram** (त्रिशूर पूरम), hailed as the 'Mother of all Poorams' in Kerala, is an electrifying temple festival at Vadakkunnathan Temple in Thrissur, renowned for the *Kudamattam* (competitive exchange of glittering silk parasols atop 30 caparisoned elephants) and *Ilanjithara Melam* percussion symphony.
40. **Bastar Dussehra** (बस्तर दशहरा) in **Chhattisgarh** is the **longest festival in the world**, celebrated continuously for **75 days** (from Shravan Amavasya to Ashvin Shukla Trayodashi); unlike North Indian Dussehra, it has **nothing to do with Rama killing Ravana**, but is dedicated exclusively to the local tribal patron goddess **Ma Danteshwari**, featured by the pulling of a colossal eight-wheeled wooden chariot.
41. **Medaram Jathara** (मेदराम जतारा), also known as **Sammakka Saralamma Jathara**, is a colossal biennial tribal pilgrimage celebrated by the **Koya tribe** in Mulugu district, **Telangana**; it is the **second largest human congregation in India after the Kumbh Mela** and the largest tribal gathering in Asia, commemorating the heroic mother-daughter rebellion against an unjust Kakatiya tax collector.
42. **Sonepur Cattle Fair** (सोनपुर मेला / Harihar Kshetra Mela) in **Bihar**, held at the sacred confluence of the **Ganga and Gandak rivers** on Kartik Purnima, is the **largest cattle fair in Asia**, historically renowned for trading elephants, horses, and livestock.
43. **Surajkund International Crafts Mela** (सूरजकुंड शिल्प मेला) in **Faridabad, Haryana**, held every year in February around the 10th-century reservoir built by Tomar king Surajpal, is the **world's largest crafts fair**, showcasing artisans from dozens of partner nations and a designated Indian theme state.
44. **Madhavpur Ghed Mela** (माधवपुर घेड मेला) is celebrated annually in coastal **Porbandar district, Gujarat**, on Ram Navami to commemorate the mythological wedding of Lord Krishna with **Princess Rukmini of Vidarbha** (symbolically connecting Gujarat with Arunachal Pradesh and North-East India).
45. **Tarnetar Fair** (तरणेतर मेला) is a colorful folk and matchmaking fair held at the Trinetreshwar Temple in Surendranagar, **Gujarat**, celebrated for traditional Garba, folk dances, and richly hand-embroidered umbrellas held by courting Koli and Rabari youths.
46. State-wise Traditional New Year Days in India:
    - **Ugadi / Yugadi:** Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Karnataka (Chaitra Shukla Pratipada).
    - **Gudi Padwa:** Maharashtra and Goa (marked by hoisting silk flags on bamboo poles / *Gudi*).
    - **Vishu:** Kerala (famous for *Vishukkani* / viewing auspicious items like gold, rice, mirrors, and yellow *Kani Konna* flowers first thing in the morning).
    - **Puthandu:** Tamil Nadu (mid-April).
    - **Pohela Boishakh:** West Bengal and Tripura.
    - **Pana Sankranti / Maha Vishuba Sankranti:** Odisha (marked by drinking sweet *Pana* water).
    - **Navreh:** Kashmiri Pandits (Chaitra Shukla Pratipada; auspicious *Thali* viewing).
47. **Chhath Puja** (छठ पूजा) is an ancient Vedic sun-worship festival celebrated primarily in **Bihar, Jharkhand, and Eastern Uttar Pradesh** on the 6th day of Kartik (*Kartik Shukla Shashthi*); it is dedicated to **Surya** and his sister **Chhathi Maiya**, notable for being the only Vedic festival where both the **setting sun (*Sandhya Arghya*)** and the **rising sun (*Usha Arghya*)** are worshipped standing in knee-deep river water.
48. **Gangaur Festival** (गणगौर) is celebrated in **Rajasthan** (and parts of MP/UP) for 18 days culminating on Chaitra Shukla Tritiya, where women worship clay idols of **Gauri (Parvati)** and **Isar (Shiva)** for marital bliss and longevity.
49. **Kullu Dussehra** (कुल्लू दशहरा) in **Himachal Pradesh** begins on the tenth day of Navratri (*Vijayadashami*) and continues for seven days at Dhalpur Maidan, where over 200 local deities converge to pay homage to Lord Raghunathji.
50. **Dev Deepawali** (देव दीपावली / 'Diwali of the Gods') is celebrated in **Varanasi (Kashi), Uttar Pradesh**, on **Kartik Purnima** (15 days after Diwali), where millions of earthen lamps (*diyas*) illuminate all 84 ghats along the crescent Ganga to celebrate Lord Shiva's victory over the demon Tripurasura.

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

### 1. The 4 Kumbh Mela Sites & Sacred Rivers — Mnemonic: `P-H-U-N` (पहुंचे कुंभ)
- **P**rayagraj &rarr; **T**riveni Sangam (Ganga + Yamuna + Saraswati)
- **H**aridwar &rarr; **G**anga
- **U**jjain &rarr; **S**hipra
- **N**ashik &rarr; **G**odavari
*Astrological Hook:* Sun/Jupiter in **Leo (Simha)** gives the name **Simhastha Kumbh** to both Ujjain and Nashik!

### 2. Major North-East Festivals — Mnemonic: `H-A-C-W` (हैक / HACW)
- **H**ornbill &rarr; **N**agaland (Kisama, Dec 1–10)
- **A**mbubachi &rarr; **A**ssam (Kamakhya, June)
- **C**hapchar Kut &rarr; **M**izoram (Bamboo jhum, March)
- **W**angala &rarr; **M**eghalaya (100 Drums, Garo)

### 3. Top UP Fairs by Location — Mnemonic: `N-B-D-S-K` (नया भारत देश हमारा)
- **N**auchandi &rarr; **M**eerut (Hindu-Muslim harmony)
- **B**ateshwar &rarr; **A**gra (101 Shiva temples & livestock)
- **D**eva Sharif &rarr; **B**arabanki (Waris Ali Shah Urs)
- **S**hakumbhari &rarr; **S**aharanpur (Shivalik hills Navratri)
- **K**hichdi &rarr; **G**orakhpur (Makar Sankranti at Gorakhnath)

### 4. Tamil Nadu vs Kerala Harvest Formula: `P-T` vs `O-K`
- **P-T:** **P**ongal belongs to **T**amil Nadu (January)
- **O-K:** **O**nam belongs to **K**erala (August–September)
