## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **National Handloom Day** is observed annually on **7 August**, instituted by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in 2015 to commemorate the historic formal proclamation of the **Swadeshi Movement** at the Calcutta Town Hall on **7 August 1905**, which boycotted foreign goods in favor of indigenous handloom fabrics.
2. **Kajari** (कजरी) is the celebrated folk song tradition of Eastern Uttar Pradesh (centered around **Mirzapur** and Varanasi), sung by women during the rainy monsoon season (*Shravana* and *Bhadrapada*), evoking the romance, longing (*Viraha*), and joy of lovers, closely linked with Goddess Vindhyavasini and Radha-Krishna.
3. **Birha** (बीरहा) is the prominent narrative folk ballad tradition of the Bhojpuri belt in **Purvanchal (Eastern Uttar Pradesh)** and Bihar, traditionally sung with robust vocal vigor by the Ahir/Yadav community to the accompaniment of the *Kartal* and dholak, recounting social legends, heroic feats, and modern events.
4. **Alha** (आल्हा) is the world-renowned martial heroic ballad of **Bundelkhand** (centered in **Mahoba**, Uttar Pradesh) and neighbouring regions, composed originally in Bundeli/Braj by the 12th-century Chandela court bard **Jagnik** in the epic poem **Alha-Khand** (Parmal Raso), narrating the **52 legendary battles fought by the Rajput warrior brothers Alha and Udal** against Prithviraj Chauhan to defend the Chandela kingdom.
5. Alha is traditionally sung in the rainy season by itinerant bards in a stirring, high-pitched heroic tempo (*Veera Rasa*), accompanied by the dholak and metallic cymbals.
6. **Rasiya** (रसिया) is the romantic and devotional folk music of the **Braj region** (Mathura, Vrindavan, Agra, Aligarh in Uttar Pradesh), celebrating the divine romantic play of Radha and Krishna, sung with ecstatic fervour during the festival of Holi accompanied by the *Nagara* and *Bamb* (giant kettledrum).
7. **Chaiti** (चैती) is a semi-classical seasonal folk genre of Eastern Uttar Pradesh (Purvanchal / Banaras), sung during the spring month of **Chaitra** (March–April), characterized by the melancholic musical refrain *"Ho Rama"* addressing Rama and romantic separation.
8. **Sohar** (सोहर) is the auspicious celebratory folk song of Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, sung by women during domestic ceremonies celebrating the **birth of a child** (particularly a son), commemorating the birth of Lord Rama or Lord Krishna.
9. **Folk songs of Uttarakhand**:
   - **Jhora** (झोड़ा): Joyous collective circle dance-song of the Kumaon hills, sung during spring and autumn fairs accompanied by the *Hurka* drum.
   - **Chhapeli** (छपेली): Seductive romantic duet dance-song of Kumaon, performed by courting couples holding a mirror (*Aina*) in the left hand and a handkerchief (*Rumal*) in the right hand.
   - **Nyoli** (न्योली): Lyrical, melancholic love poetry sung solo by shepherds and hill travelers echoingly across deep Himalayan pine valleys.
   - **Pandavani / Pandav Nritya**: Folk epic ballad tradition of Garhwal dramatizing episodes of the *Mahabharata*.
10. **Phulkari** (फुलकारी / literally 'Flower Work') is the celebrated folk embroidery craft of **Punjab** (holding a joint GI tag with Haryana and Rajasthan), executed entirely by women on coarse hand-spun, dyed cotton khadi cloth (*Khaddar*) using vibrant untwisted silk floss thread (*Pat*); embroidered exclusively from the reverse side using the darn stitch.
11. When a Phulkari is completely covered with dense embroidery so that the base cloth is entirely invisible, it is called a **Bagh** (बाग / Garden); iconic varieties include *Chope* (red wedding shawl gifted by maternal grandmother), *Vari da Bagh*, and *Thirma*.
12. **Chikankari** (चिकनकारी) of **Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh** (GI tagged), is a delicate 400-year-old white shadow-work needlecraft traditionally executed on fine white muslin (*mulmul*), patronized by the Nawabs of Awadh; it incorporates over **36 distinct hand-embroidery stitches**, categorized into flat stitches (**Tepchi**, **Bakhiya** / shadow work), raised embossed knot stitches (**Phanda**, **Murri** / rice-grain shaped knots), and open lattice eyelet work (**Jali**).
13. **Zardozi** (ज़रदोज़ी) of **Lucknow** (GI tagged) is an opulent metallic embroidery tradition derived from Persian (*Zar* = gold, *Dozi* = sewing), using real gold and silver metallic wire (*Kalabattu*), sequins (*Sitara*), and beads hammered onto heavy velvet, satin, and silk stretched tightly on a wooden frame called the **Adda**.
14. **Kantha** (कांथा) of **West Bengal** and Odisha is an eco-friendly folk embroidery craft where women layer old, discarded cotton sarees and dhotis, quilting them together using simple running stitches (*Kantha stitch*) with colored threads drawn from the saree borders to depict folk motifs, animals, and mythological tales.
15. **Sujani** (सुजनी) of **Bihar** (GI tagged) is a traditional quilted folk embroidery practiced by rural women in Bhusra village (Muzaffarpur), sewing together layers of old cloths with fine chain and running stitches, historically used as baby wrapping quilts and now depicting social narratives of women's empowerment.
16. **Kasuti** (कसूती) is an intricate traditional folk embroidery of **Karnataka** (Dharwad, Hubballi; GI tagged), embroidered without tracing or knots on Ilkal sarees, counting warp and weft threads geometrically to depict temple Gopurams, palanquins, peacocks, and Tulsi Vrindavan across four traditional stitches: *Gavanti*, *Murgi*, *Negi*, and *Menthi*.
17. **Kashmiri Kashida** is the world-renowned needlework of Jammu & Kashmir, employing fine satin and chain stitches to depict local Himalayan nature (chinar leaves, saffron blossoms, birds, almonds) on warm woolen shawls and ferans.
18. **Chamba Rumal** (चंबा रूमाल) of **Himachal Pradesh** (GI tagged) is an exquisite double-faced silk needlework (*Do-rukha*) executed on unbleached muslin, replicating the delicate lines, colors, and themes of Pahari miniature paintings (Raslila, Mahabharata) identically on both sides of the square kerchief.
19. Traditional Indian Saree and Handloom Weaving Hubs:
    - **Banaras Brocades (UP):** Heavy pure silk interwoven with gold and silver zari using *Jala* or Jacquard techniques (*Kinkiab*).
    - **Chanderi (MP):** Lightweight translucent fabric blending fine cotton and silk with shimmering gold zari borders, patronized by the Scindias of Gwalior.
    - **Maheshwari (MP):** Reversible border sarees initiated by Queen **Ahilyabai Holkar** of Indore in the 18th century, featuring geometric motifs derived from Maheshwar fort carvings.
    - **Paithani (Maharashtra):** Pure silk saree from Paithan (Aurangabad) on the Godavari River, featuring an ornate tapestry-woven gold pallu with signature **Mor (Peacock)**, parrot, and lotus motifs.
    - **Patan Patola (Gujarat):** Supreme masterwork of **Double Ikat** silk weaving from Patan, where both warp and weft yarns are resist-dyed before weaving so that the geometric design aligns perfectly on the loom; identical on both sides; patronized by Solanki king Kumarapala.
    - **Bandhani / Bandhej:** Ancient resist tie-and-dye fabric of **Gujarat** (Jamnagar) and **Rajasthan** (Jaipur, Jodhpur), where fingernails pinch fabric into tiny dots (*bindis*) bound with thread before dyeing.
    - **Leheriya:** Signature tie-and-dye fabric of **Rajasthan** creating diagonal wave-like rainbow ripple patterns (*Lehar* = wave), worn during the monsoon festival of Teej.
    - **Pochampally Ikat (Telangana):** Single and double ikat geometric tie-and-dye on cotton and silk; won India's first textile GI tag in 2004.
    - **Telia Rumal (Telangana):** Oiled yarn resist double ikat handkerchief, historically treated with castor oil and sheep dung, woven in geometric red, white, and black colors (GI tagged).
    - **Kanchipuram Silk (Tamil Nadu):** Heaviest mulberry silk sarees woven with three shuttles using the **Korvai** interlocking technique (where the body and borders are woven separately and interlinked seamlessly).
    - **Baluchari Saree (West Bengal):** Traditional silk saree originating in Murshidabad (now woven in Bishnupur, Bankura), renowned for its elaborate pallu depicting scenes from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and colonial nawabs travelling in steam trains and buggies.
    - **Jamdani:** Fine figured muslin handwoven with supplementary weft technique in Bengal, producing floral motifs that appear to float on the sheer cotton fabric (inscribed on UNESCO ICH).
    - **Muga Silk of Assam:** Endemic wild golden-yellow silk produced by the caterpillar *Antheraea assamensis*, possessing high tensile strength, natural golden gloss that increases with every wash, and acid-resistant durability; holds exclusive GI status for Assam.
    - **Eri Silk (Endi):** The thermal wild silk of Assam, also known as **Ahimsa Silk (Peace Silk)** because the moth emerges naturally from the cocoon without killing the pupa.
    - **Tussar Silk (Kosa):** Coarse, textured copper-gold wild silk cultivated by tribal communities in Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh.
20. **Dhokra / Dokra** (ढोकरा) is an ancient tribal metal casting craft using the **Lost-Wax Casting Technique (Cire Perdue)** to create bell-metal and brass figurines of tribal gods, folk musicians, horses, and lamps, practiced by traditional nomadic *Dhokra Damar* metalsmiths in **Bastar (Chhattisgarh)**, Bankura (West Bengal), and Mayurbhanj (Odisha).
21. **Bidriware** (बिदरी क्राफ्ट) of **Bidar, Karnataka** (GI tagged), is an 500-year-old metal inlay craft introduced under the Bahmani Sultans; an alloy of **Zinc and Copper (approx 16:1)** is cast, engraved with intricate floral patterns, inlaid with pure **Silver wire or sheet**, and then rubbed with a special soil found only in the inner ruins of Bidar Fort (containing potassium nitrate) that chemically turns the base metal jet black while keeping the silver gleaming bright white.
22. **Meenakari** (मीनाकारी) is the art of enameling metal surfaces with vibrant mineral colors (copper oxide, cobalt); introduced into Rajasthan from Persia by Raja Man Singh I of Amber in the 16th century:
    - **Jaipur Gold Meenakari:** Red, green, and blue enamel on 24-karat gold jewelry.
    - **Banaras Gulabi Meenakari (UP):** Rare translucent **pink enamel** on silver ornaments and figurines (GI tagged).
23. **Usta Art** (उस्ता कला) of **Bikaner, Rajasthan** (GI tagged), is the unique miniature painting and embossed gold leaf work (*Munawwat*) executed on cured camel hide (water containers / *Kuppis*, lamps, and mirrors), brought from Iran by Maharaja Anup Singh.
24. **Bankura Terracotta Horses** of Panchmura village, **West Bengal** (GI tagged), are iconic terracotta folk votive figures characterized by erect pointed ears, hollow cylindrical bodies, and symmetrical geometry, used as the official logo of All India Handicrafts.
