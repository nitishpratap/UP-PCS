## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The bronze statuette of the **'Dancing Girl'** (नर्तकी की कांस्य मूर्ति), excavated at **Mohenjo-daro** in 1926 by Ernest Mackay, is a 10.5-cm masterpiece of the Indus Valley Civilization (~2500 BCE) cast using the ancient **Lost-Wax Technique (Cire Perdue / मधुच्छिष्ट विधान)**; she stands in a relaxed **Tribhanga** posture with her right hand on her hip and her left arm completely covered with bangles.
2. The **Steatite Bearded Priest-King** of Mohenjo-daro is carved from soft soapstone, draped in an elegant shawl adorned with carved **trefoil floral motifs** running over his left shoulder and leaving the right shoulder bare, wearing a circular medallion fillet across his forehead.
3. The colossal bronze sculpture of a **Chariot drawn by a pair of bullocks and driven by a standing nude man**, discovered at **Daimabad** (Ahmedabad/Ahmednagar, Maharashtra), is the largest and heaviest bronze sculpture of the Harappan era.
4. **Mauryan court sculpture** (3rd c. BCE) is world-renowned for its colossal monolithic sandstone pillars quarried from **Chunar** (near Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh), polished to a dazzling, mirror-like glassy finish (**Mauryan Polish**).
5. The **Lion Capital at Sarnath** (सारनाथ का सिंह शीर्ष), commissioned by Emperor Ashoka to commemorate Buddha's first sermon, consists of four parts:
   - A bell-shaped inverted lotus base.
   - An abacus circular drum carved with **Four Animals** marching in high relief: an **Elephant** (East / Maya's conception), a galloping **Horse** (South / Great Departure / Kanthaka), a **Bull** (West / Zodiac of Buddha's birth), and a **Lion** (North / Shakya clan / enlightenment), separated by **Four 24-Spoked Dharma Chakras**.
   - **Four Asiatic Lions** standing back-to-back facing the four cardinal directions, symbolizing the universal proclamation of Dhamma.
   - Originally crowned by a massive 32-spoked crowning wheel (**Maha-Dharmachakra**), fragments of which are preserved in the Sarnath Museum.
6. The Government of India adopted the Sarnath Lion Capital as the **State Emblem of India on 26 January 1950**, displaying three lions visible on the crest, the Dharma Chakra in the center flanked by the **Bull on the right** and the **Horse on the left**, with the national motto **"Satyameva Jayate"** (from Mundaka Upanishad) inscribed below in Devanagari.
7. Popular Mauryan folk sculpture is exemplified by monumental freestanding polished statues of semi-divine fertility spirits called **Yakshas** and **Yakshis**:
   - **Didarganj Yakshi** (दीदारगंज यक्षी / Patna, Bihar) — a life-sized polished Chunar sandstone female figure holding a flywhisk (*Chauri*) in her right hand, celebrated for voluptuous feminine proportions, intricate jewelry, and mirror polish.
   - **Parkham Yaksha** (Mathura, UP) and **Besnagar Yakshi** (Vidisha, MP).
8. The post-Mauryan period (1st c. BCE – 3rd c. CE) saw the flourishing of **Three Distinct Schools of Sculpture**:
   - **Gandhara School** (North-West / modern Pakistan & Afghanistan)
   - **Mathura School** (Uttar Pradesh / Yamuna basin)
   - **Amaravati School** (Andhra Pradesh / Krishna-Godavari basin).
9. **Gandhara School of Sculpture** was a synthesis of indigenous Buddhist themes with **Greco-Roman (Hellenistic)** artistic aesthetics (hence called *Greco-Buddhist Art*), heavily patronized by the **Kushan rulers** (notably King Kanishka).
10. Key characteristics of Gandhara sculptures:
    - Primary medium: **Bluish-grey schist stone** in early phases, and **stucco (lime plaster)** and terracotta in later phases.
    - Buddha is depicted with sharp European/Greek facial features resembling the Greek god **Apollo**, wavy curly hair gathered into an **Ushnisha** (topknot cranial protuberance), heavy realistic Roman toga drapery falling in deep sharp folds covering both shoulders, calm half-closed eyes, and subtle mustaches.
    - Prominent centers: **Taxila** (Pakistan), **Peshawar**, **Hadda**, and **Bamiyan** (Afghanistan; colossal rock-cut standing Buddhas destroyed in 2001).
11. **Mathura School of Sculpture** was a **purely indigenous Indian development** rooted in the traditional Yaksha-Yakshi aesthetic, flourishing in and around Mathura under the patronage of the **Kushans** (1st–3rd c. CE) and Guptas.
12. Key characteristics of Mathura sculptures:
    - Primary medium: Distinctive **Spotted Red Sandstone** (*Lal Balua Patthar*) quarried from Sikri/Rohtas.
    - Encompassed all three contemporary religions: **Buddhism**, **Hinduism** (earliest known sculpted representations of Vishnu, Shiva as Linga/Mukhalinga, Surya, Durga Mahishasuramardini, and Balarama), and **Jainism** (votive tablets called **Ayagapatas** and seated Tirthankaras excavated from Kankali Tila, Mathura).
    - Early Mathura Buddha: Joyful, smiling, energetic face, robust muscular torso, shaven head or snail-shell curls, transparent light drapery clinging tightly to the body leaving the right shoulder bare, right hand raised in **Abhaya Mudra** (fearlessness), seated on a lion throne flanked by Padmapani and Vajrapani.
    - Imperial portrait statues of rulers: The famous **Headless Statue of Emperor Kanishka** clad in heavy northern felt coat and riding boots, holding a sword and mace.
13. **Amaravati School of Sculpture** flourished on the banks of the Krishna River in Andhra Pradesh (Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda, Jaggayyapeta) under the patronage of the **Satavahana** and **Ikshvaku** dynasties.
14. Key characteristics of Amaravati sculptures:
    - Primary medium: Pure white **Palnad limestone** (commonly called white marble).
    - Sculptures are predominantly **narrative relief panels** adorning the stupa drum, railings, and gateways rather than individual freestanding idols, depicting lively Jataka tales with dynamic movement, slender elongated limbs, and spontaneous human emotions.
    - Signature motif: **The Taming of the Mad Elephant Nalagiri** by Buddha, where narrative movement unfolds within a single circular medallion.
15. **Gupta Sculpture (4th–6th c. CE)** represents the classical golden zenith of Indian plastic art, achieving supreme balance between external physical beauty and inward spiritual serenity:
    - Replaced the physical muscularity of Kushan art with graceful, refined contours.
    - Elimination of heavy drapery folds in favor of **clinging transparent diaphanous wet-look drapery**.
    - Introduction of richly carved, elaborate floral halos (**Prabhavali**) adorned with geometric bands, lotus rosettes, and flying celestial beings (*Gandharvas*).
16. The **Seated Buddha in Dharmachakrapravartana Mudra** from **Sarnath** (Chunar sandstone, Gupta period) is universally regarded as the absolute masterpiece of Asian Buddhist sculpture; the Buddha sits in padmasana, his fingers delicately turning the Wheel of Law, his eyes downcast in deep meditative absorption, backed by an ornate floral halo with two flying apsaras, while the pedestal below depicts five disciples and the wheel flanked by deer.
17. The **Sultanganj Buddha** (Bhagalpur district, Bihar) is a monumental **7.5-foot tall copper/bronze statue** of the standing Buddha dating to the Gupta-Pala transition (~500–700 CE), weighing over one tonne, cast via lost-wax technique; his right hand is in Abhaya mudra and left hand holds the robe hem (discovered during railway construction in 1861 by E.B. Harris, now housed in the Birmingham Museum, UK).
18. The colossal rock-cut relief of **Varaha Avatar** (the Boar Incarnation of Vishnu rescuing Goddess Bhudevi/Earth from the cosmic ocean of cosmic chaos) at **Cave 5 of Udayagiri Caves** in Vidisha, Madhya Pradesh, is the grandest sculptural embodiment of Gupta imperial resurgence under Chandragupta II.
19. The **Deogarh Dashavatara Temple** (Lalitpur, UP) contains three monumental sculpted wall panels (*rathikas*) depicting Vishnu: **Gajendramoksha** (Vishnu rescuing the elephant from crocodile), **Nara-Narayana** penance, and **Sheshashayi Vishnu** (Anantasayana / Vishnu reclining on the serpent Shesha floating on the cosmic waters, while Lakshmi massages his feet and Brahma emerges on a lotus from his navel).
20. **Chola Bronze Sculptures (10th–13th c. CE)** from Tamil Nadu represent the pinnacle of South Indian metal casting, executed with the lost-wax method (*Madhuchehishtavidhana*) as processional idols (*Utsava Murtis*), with world acclaim centered on the cosmic **Nataraja**.
21. The symbolic iconography of the **Chola Bronze Nataraja (Lord Shiva as Cosmic Dancer of Tandava)**:
    - **Upper Right Hand:** Holds the **Damaru** (hourglass drum), symbolizing the cosmic sound (*Nada*) and the origin of **Creation**.
    - **Upper Left Hand:** Holds the **Agni** (flame of fire) on the palm, symbolizing the cosmic power of **Destruction / Dissolution**.
    - **Lower Right Hand:** Raised in **Abhaya Mudra**, symbolizing divine protection, preservation, and solace to the devotee.
    - **Lower Left Hand:** Points gracefully downward across the chest towards the raised left foot in **Gajahasta Mudra** (elephant trunk gesture), symbolizing divine grace and the path to spiritual **Liberation (Moksha)**.
    - **Right Foot:** Firmly stamps down upon the prostrate demonic dwarf **Apasmara Purusha** (Muyalaka), symbolizing the crushing of ignorance, ego, and worldly delusion.
    - **Raised Left Foot:** Lifted in mid-air in the *Bhujangatrasita* stance, representing the refuge and salvation offered to the seeker.
    - **Hair Locks:** Flowing wild dreadlocks (*Jatas*) flying laterally during cosmic dance, holding the miniature figure of **Ganga** (River Ganges descending) and a crescent moon.
    - **Prabhamandala (Tiruvasi):** A complete circular aureole of radiant cosmic flames framing the deity, symbolizing the eternal, unceasing cycle of cosmic time and existence.
22. The colossal monolithic statue of **Lord Gommateshwara (Bahubali)** at Indragiri Hill in **Shravanabelagola** (Hassan district, Karnataka), standing **57 feet (17.5 m) tall**, is the **world's largest freestanding monolithic stone statue**; carved from a single block of grey granite in **981 CE** by **Chavundaraya**, the military commander and prime minister of Western Ganga King Rachamalla IV.
23. Gommateshwara is depicted standing naked in the rigid ascetic posture called **Kayotsarga** (dismissing the body), with climbing vines (*Madhavi creepers*) entwining his legs and arms, ant-hills rising around his feet, symbolizing complete motionless meditation until achieving omniscience (*Kevala Jnana*).
24. The grand **Mahamastakabhisheka** (महामस्तकाभिषेक) festival is held once every **12 years** at Shravanabelagola, wherein the colossal 57-foot statue is anointed from head to foot with hundreds of pots of milk, sugarcane juice, saffron paste, sandalwood, and gold coins.
25. **Bronze casting centers** of living classical sculpture: **Swamimalai** in Thanjavur district, Tamil Nadu, holds a prestigious **GI tag** for its hereditary sthapathis who continue casting Chola-style bronze statues using the ancient lost-wax technique based on the measurements of the *Shilpa Shastra*.

| Feature / Dimension | Gandhara School | Mathura School | Amaravati School |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary Region** | Gandhara, Peshawar, Taxila (North-West) | Mathura, Sonkh, Kankali Tila (UP) | Krishna-Godavari valley, Amaravati (AP) |
| **Royal Patronage** | **Kushans** (notably Kanishka) | **Kushans** and later Guptas | **Satavahanas** and Ikshvakus |
| **Sculptural Material** | **Bluish-grey schist stone** and stucco | **Spotted red sandstone** (*Lal Balua*) | **White Palnad limestone** (marble) |
| **Artistic Influence** | Strong **Greco-Roman (Hellenistic)** influence | **Purely indigenous Indian** Yaksha tradition | **Purely indigenous Indian** aesthetic |
| **Religious Scope** | **Exclusively Buddhist** (Mahayana focus) | **All Three:** Buddhist, Hindu & Jain | **Exclusively Buddhist** (Hinayana/Mahayana) |
| **Buddha's Features** | Apollo-like face, wavy hair, sharp nose, Roman toga drapery in heavy folds covering both shoulders | Smiling energetic face, shaven head or snail curls, transparent drapery on left shoulder | Slim, elongated, graceful figures in crowded narrative relief panels |
| **Artistic Format** | Independent devotional statues in monasteries | Devotional idols, royal portraits, votive tablets | **Narrative stupa railing reliefs** (Jatakas) |

- **B**ull (Zodiac of Buddha's birth)
- **E**lephant (Queen Maya's divine dream)

- **M**athura &rarr; **M**ottled red sandstone (Smiling indigenous / 3 religions)
- **A**maravati &rarr; **A**ndhra white limestone (Dynamic Jataka reliefs)
