11. **Bengali** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).
12. **High Antiquity:** Recorded history and early literary texts spanning a historical antiquity of **1500 to 2000 years**.
13. **Valuable Heritage:** A treasured corpus of ancient literature and texts cherished as invaluable heritage by generations of speakers.
14. **Original Literary Tradition:** A literary heritage that is demonstrably authentic and original, not borrowed from another speech community.
15. **Linguistic Distinctiveness:** A distinct divergence between the classical language and its modern forms or vernacular offshoots, acknowledging potential historical discontinuity across later evolutionary stages.
16. **Garbhagriha (Sanctum Sanctorum):** The innermost, unadorned sacred chamber housing the principal deity, designed to focus divine energy.
17. **Mandapa:** A pillared hall situated in front of the sanctum, serving as a gathering space for devotees during rituals.
18. **Antarala (Vestibule):** An intermediate foyer or transit vestibule connecting the Garbhagriha with the main Mandapa.
19. **Shikhara (North) / Vimana (South):** The monumental mountain-like spire rising skyward directly over the Garbhagriha.
20. **Amalaka:** A large, fluted circular stone disc resembling a myrobalan fruit, installed at the apex of a Nagara Shikhara.
21. **Kalasha:** A pot- or pitcher-shaped decorative finial placed directly atop the Amalaka as the highest point of the temple.
22. **Gopuram:** A towering, monumental gateway providing entrance through the perimeter walls of South Indian Dravidian temple complexes.
23. **Panchayatana Style:** An architectural layout where the main central shrine is symmetrically flanked by **four smaller subsidiary shrines** at the corners (prominent examples include the Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh, and the Kandariya Mahadeva and Lakshmana temples at Khajuraho).
24. **Hornbill Festival (Nagaland):** Held annually from 1 to 10 December at the Naga Heritage Village in Kisama; acclaimed as the "Festival of Festivals", it showcases the collective traditions of all 16 recognized Naga tribes.
25. **Bihu (Assam):** Celebrated across three distinct agricultural cycles: *Rongali (Bohag) Bihu* in spring (April) marking the Assamese New Year, *Kongali (Kati) Bihu* in autumn marking crop protection, and *Bhogali (Magh) Bihu* in winter marking the post-harvest feast.
26. **Chapchar Kut (Mizoram):** Celebrated with great joy in spring after the completion of jhum (slash-and-burn) clearing operations; famously features the traditional *Cheraw* bamboo dance.
27. **Losar (Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh):** The traditional Buddhist and Tibetan New Year festival, observed with monastic mask dances (*Cham*) and elaborate symbolic offerings.
28. **Pushkar Fair (Ajmer, Rajasthan):** One of the world's largest livestock and camel trading fairs, organized during Kartik Purnima along the holy banks of Lake Pushkar.
29. **Thrissur Pooram (Kerala):** Staged at the Vadakkunnathan Temple in Thrissur; renowned for competitive temple displays featuring caparisoned elephants, dynamic parasol exchanges (*Kudamattam*), and majestic traditional percussion ensembles (*Panchavadyam*).
30. **Wangala Festival (Garo Hills, Meghalaya):** Known as the "100 Drum Festival", this post-harvest celebration is observed by the Garo tribe in honor of Saljong, the Sun God of fertility.
31. **Kumbh Mela & Magh Mela (Prayagraj):** Organized at the sacred Triveni Sangam (confluence of the Ganga, Yamuna, and mythical Saraswati); the Maha Kumbh takes place every 12 years, with the annual Magh Mela held during the Hindu month of Magha.
32. **Bateshwar Fair (Bateshwar, Agra):** A renowned historic cattle and livestock fair hosted along the Yamuna riverbank, famous for its picturesque riverside cluster of 101 Shiva temples.
33. **Nauchandi Mela (Meerut):** A celebrated symbol of communal fraternity held after Holi, where pilgrims pay homage concurrently to the shrine of Nav Chandi Devi and the dargah of Sufi saint Hazrat Bale Miyan.
34. **Shakumbhari Devi Mela (Saharanpur):** Organized twice every year during the auspicious Chaitra and Ashwin Navratris at the historic Shakumbhari Devi Peeth in the Shivalik foothills.
35. **Devi Patan Mela (Tulsipur, Balrampur):** A major annual fair held at the sacred Devi Patan Shaktipeeth, deeply revered across the Indo-Nepal Terai region.
