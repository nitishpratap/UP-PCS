## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The **Sangeet Natak Akademi (संगीत नाटक अकादेमी)** was established in **1952** by the Ministry of Education (inaugurated by President Dr. Rajendra Prasad at New Delhi) as the first national academy for performing arts (music, dance, drama).
2. The **Sahitya Akademi (साहित्य अकादेमी)** was established in **1954** by the Government of India (inaugurated on 12 March 1954), dedicated to the promotion of Indian literature across **24 recognized languages**.
3. The **24 languages recognized by Sahitya Akademi** comprise: the **22 languages of the Eighth Schedule** of the Constitution + **English** + **Rajasthani**.
4. **Bhojpuri** is **NOT** recognized as an independent language by the Sahitya Akademi or the Eighth Schedule, despite widespread regional usage.
5. The **Lalit Kala Akademi (ललित कला अकादेमी)** (National Academy of Art) was inaugurated in **1954** at New Delhi by Education Minister Maulana Abul Kalam Azad to promote visual arts (painting, sculpture, graphics).
6. The **National School of Drama (NSD)** was established in **1959** by the Sangeet Natak Akademi; it became an autonomous institution under the Ministry of Culture in 1975 (legendary director: **Ebrahim Alkazi**).
7. The **Jnanpith Award (ज्ञानपीठ पुरस्कार)**, instituted in **1961** by Bharatiya Jnanpith (founded by industrialist Sahu Shanti Prasad Jain and Rama Jain), is India’s **highest and most prestigious literary award**.
8. The first Jnanpith Award was conferred in **1965** on Malayalam poet **G. Sankara Kurup** for his poetry anthology *Odakkuzhal* (The Bamboo Flute).
9. The Jnanpith citation includes a bronze replica of **Goddess Saraswati (Vagdevi)**, modelled on the 11th-century statue from the Bhojshala temple at Dhar (Madhya Pradesh).
10. The first woman writer to receive the Jnanpith Award was **Ashapoorna Devi** (1976, Bengali) for her novel *Pratham Pratishruti*.
11. The first Hindi author to receive the Jnanpith Award was **Sumitranandan Pant** (1968) for *Chidambara*.
12. Other illustrious Hindi Jnanpith laureates: **Ramdhari Singh Dinkar** (1972, *Urvashi*), **Sachchidananda Hirananda Vatsyayan 'Agyeya'** (1978, *Kitni Navon Mein Kitni Baar*), **Mahadevi Varma** (1982, *Yama*), **Naresh Mehta** (1992), **Nirmal Verma** (1999), **Kunwar Narayan** (2005), **Amarkant & Shrilal Shukla** (2009), and **Kedarnath Singh** (2013).
13. Prior to 1982, Jnanpith was awarded for a specific literary work; since 1982, it is awarded for a writer's **lifetime contribution** to Indian literature.
14. The **Saraswati Samman (सरस्वती सम्मान)** was instituted in **1991** by the **K.K. Birla Foundation**, awarded annually for outstanding prose or poetry in any of the **22 Eighth Schedule languages** published within the preceding 10 years.
15. The first recipient of the Saraswati Samman in 1991 was **Dr. Harivansh Rai Bachchan** for his four-volume autobiography (*Kya Bhoolun Kya Yaad Karun*, *Needa Ka Nirman Phir*, etc.).
16. The **Vyas Samman (व्यास सम्मान)**, also instituted in **1991** by the **K.K. Birla Foundation**, is awarded annually exclusively for outstanding **Hindi literature** published in the past 10 years.
17. The first recipient of the Vyas Samman was eminent Hindi critic **Dr. Ram Vilas Sharma** (1991) for *Bharat ke Pracheen Bhasha Parivar aur Hindi*.
18. **Ustad Bismillah Khan Yuva Puraskar** is conferred by the **Sangeet Natak Akademi** (not Sahitya Akademi) to artists below the age of **35 years** in music, dance, and theatre.
19. **Sahitya Akademi Yuva Puraskar** is conferred by the **Sahitya Akademi** to young authors aged **35 and below** across all 24 recognized languages.
20. The **Bharat Ratna** (instituted in 1954) has been conferred upon **six legendary music personalities**:
    - **M.S. Subbulakshmi** (1998 — First musician ever honored)
    - **Pandit Ravi Shankar** (1999 — Sitar maestro)
    - **Ustad Bismillah Khan** (2001 — Shehnai maestro of Varanasi)
    - **Lata Mangeshkar** (2001 — Playback singing legend)
    - **Pandit Bhimsen Joshi** (2009 — Kirana gharana classical vocalist)
    - **Dr. Bhupen Hazarika** (2019 — Bard of Brahmaputra; singer, composer, filmmaker)
21. Do not confuse the former President of India **Dr. Zakir Husain** (awarded Bharat Ratna in **1963** for public affairs) with the world-famous tabla maestro **Ustad Zakir Hussain** (son of Alla Rakha, awarded Padma Vibhushan in 2023).
22. The **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999** came into force in India on **15 September 2003**.
23. The apex statutory body administering GI registrations is the **Geographical Indications Registry** located at **Chennai**, Tamil Nadu, under the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry).
24. A Geographical Indication (GI) registration is valid for an initial period of **10 years**, renewable indefinitely for successive periods of 10 years.
25. The **first product in India to receive a GI tag** was **Darjeeling Tea (darjeeling tea logo & leaf)** in **2004–2005**.
26. **Tamil Nadu** holds the highest number of GI tags in India (60+), closely followed by **Uttar Pradesh** (58+ official registrations, expanding beyond 75).
27. **Varanasi (Kashi)** is hailed as India’s foremost **GI Capital city**, with over **23 distinct GI products** registered from the holy city and its surrounding Purvanchal districts alone.
28. Iconic GI tags of **Varanasi (Uttar Pradesh)**:
    - *Banaras Brocades and Sarees* (world-famous silk brocades with zari work)
    - *Varanasi Wooden Lacquerware and Toys*
    - *Banaras Gulabi Meenakari* (pink enamel on silver, brought from Persia)
    - *Varanasi Metal Repousse Craft*
    - *Banaras Glass Beads* (world's largest handmade glass bead hub)
    - *Banaras Zardozi*
    - *Banarasi Paan* (Betel leaf)
    - *Banaras Langra Mango* (sweet green mango)
    - *Ramnagar Bhanta* (white round brinjal)
    - *Chandauli Adamchini Rice* (aromatic non-basmati rice).
29. Other iconic craft and textile GI tags of **Uttar Pradesh**:
    - **Lucknow Chikankari** (delicate white shadow-work needle embroidery on fine muslin)
    - **Lucknow Zardozi** (gold/silver metallic embroidery on velvet and satin)
    - **Bhadohi Carpet / Handmade Carpet of Mirzapur** (India's largest hand-knotted woolen carpet cluster)
    - **Kannauj Perfume (Attar)** ('Perfume Capital of India'; ancient Deg-Bhapka hydro-distillation method using copper stills)
    - **Moradabad Metal Craft** ('Peetal Nagari' / Brass City of India)
    - **Aligarh Locks & Hardware** ('Taala Nagari')
    - **Firozabad Glassware** ('Suhag Nagari' / Bangle and glass capital)
    - **Saharanpur Wood Carving** (intricate carved sheesham wood screens and furniture)
    - **Gorakhpur Terracotta** (ornate baked clay elephants, horses, and temple bells)
    - **Khurja Pottery** (glazed ceramic earthenware and bone china)
    - **Nizamabad Black Clay Pottery** (Azamgarh; lustrous black pottery engraved with silver mercury foil)
    - **Mirzapur Brassware** (cast brass utensils and water vessels)
    - **Sambhal Horn Craft** (handcrafted decorative combs and cutlery from buffalo horn)
    - **Mainpuri Tarkashi** (fine brass wire inlay hammered into sheesham wood)
    - **Kalpi Handmade Paper** (Jalaun; ancient paper craft revived by Mahatma Gandhi)
    - **Agra Durrie / Leather Footwear**
    - **Amroha Dholak** (percussion instruments from mango wood).
30. Agricultural GI tags of **Uttar Pradesh**:
    - **Kalanamak Rice** ('Buddha's Gift' / Black husk aromatic rice of Siddharthnagar, Basti, Gorakhpur)
    - **Malihabadi Dussehri Mango** (Lucknow)
    - **Allahabad Surkha Guava** (Prayagraj; apple-red interior sweet guava)
    - **Rataul Mango** (Baghpat)
    - **Muzaffarnagar Gur** (Jaggery)
    - **Pratapgarh Aonla** (Indian gooseberry).
31. Iconic GI tags of **Uttarakhand**:
    - **Kumaon Aipan Art** (traditional ritual floor/wall red-and-white folk painting using Geru and Biswar rice paste)
    - **Almora Bal Mithai** (fudge confection made from roasted khoya coated with white sugar balls)
    - **Uttarakhand Tejpat** (first product from Uttarakhand to receive GI tag in 2016; bay leaf from Chamoli/Bageshwar)
    - **Munsyari White Rajma** (Pithoragarh; high-altitude soft kidney beans)
    - **Ringal Craft** (bamboo basketry and weaving by rural artisans of Chamoli/Rudraprayag)
    - **Thulma & Chutka** (warm handwoven woollen fleece blankets made by Bhotia weavers)
    - **Bhotia Dan** (traditional hand-knotted pile carpets of Bhotia tribe)
    - **Uttarakhand Tamta Craft** (traditional coppersmith craft of Almora)
    - **Ghat / Manduwa (Finger Millet)** and **Jhangora (Barnyard Millet)**
    - **Uttarakhand Bichhu Buti (Nettle Fiber) Fabrics** (sustainable natural textile).
32. Celebrated GI textile and handloom products across Indian States:
    - *Pashmina Shawl & Kani Shawl:* Jammu & Kashmir (fine undercoat wool of Changthangi mountain goat)
    - *Kullu Shawl & Chamba Rumal:* Himachal Pradesh (geometric border shawls & double-faced silk embroidery)
    - *Kota Doria:* Rajasthan (fine chequered weave / *Khat* pattern on cotton-silk)
    - *Chanderi Fabric & Maheshwari Sarees:* Madhya Pradesh (silk-cotton lightweight translucent sarees)
    - *Paithani Saree:* Maharashtra (pure silk saree with peacock/parrot pallu border)
    - *Patola Saree of Patan:* Gujarat (double ikat weave using natural dyes; both warp and weft dyed before weaving)
    - *Pochampally Ikat:* Telangana ('Silk City of India'; single and double ikat geometric patterns; GI 2004)
    - *Kanchipuram Silk Saree:* Tamil Nadu (pure mulberry silk with gold zari borders woven with interlocking *Korvai* technique)
    - *Muga Silk of Assam:* Assam (natural golden yellow silk produced by endemic *Antheraea assamensis* silkworm; washable and extremely durable)
    - *Mysore Silk:* Karnataka (soft crepe silk sarees woven with pure 24-karat gold zari).
33. Celebrated GI craft, sculpture, and painting products across Indian States:
    - *Bastariya Dhokra / Bell Metal Craft:* Chhattisgarh & Madhya Pradesh (ancient lost-wax metal casting technique)
    - *Tanjore Paintings:* Tamil Nadu (embossed gold foil relief with semi-precious stone insets on wooden board)
    - *Mysore Rosewood Inlay:* Karnataka
    - *Bidriware:* Karnataka (zinc-copper alloy blackened with soil from Bidar Fort, inlaid with pure silver wire)
    - *Kondapalli Toys:* Andhra Pradesh (lightweight soft wood *Tella Poniki* painted with vegetable dyes)
    - *Channapatna Toys:* Karnataka ('Gombegala Ooru' / Toy Town; non-toxic vegetable-lacquered ivory wood toys).
34. Celebrated GI agricultural and sweet products across India:
    - *Kashmiri Saffron (Zafran):* Jammu & Kashmir (Pampore karewas; dark red, high crocin content)
    - *Alphonso Mango (Hapus):* Maharashtra (Ratnagiri, Sindhudurg)
    - *Shahi Litchi:* Bihar (Muzaffarpur)
    - *Katarni Rice & Zardalu Mango:* Bihar (Bhagalpur)
    - *Magahi Paan:* Bihar (Nawada, Nalanda, Gaya)
    - *Banganapalle Mango:* Andhra Pradesh
    - *Banglar Rasogolla (WB)* vs *Odisha Rasagola (OD):* Both awarded distinct GI tags (WB 2017, Odisha 2019)
    - *Bikaneri Bhujia:* Rajasthan (moth bean flour and spices, manufactured since 1877 under Maharaja Dungar Singh)
    - *Tirupati Laddu:* Andhra Pradesh (temple Prasadam at Venkateswara Temple).

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

### 1. Three National Cultural Academies of India — Chronology: `S-S-L`
- **S**angeet Natak Akademi &rarr; **1952** (Performing Arts)
- **S**ahitya Akademi &rarr; **1954** (Letters / 24 Languages)
- **L**alit Kala Akademi &rarr; **1954** (Visual Arts / Painting / Sculpture)

### 2. Landmark Firsts of the Jnanpith Award — Mnemonic: `S-S-A`
- **S**ankara Kurup, G. &rarr; First ever winner (1965, Malayalam)
- **S**umitranandan Pant &rarr; First Hindi winner (1968, *Chidambara*)
- **A**shapoorna Devi &rarr; First female winner (1976, Bengali)

### 3. Top GI Craft Clusters of Uttar Pradesh
- **Locks:** Aligarh
- **Glass / Bangles:** Firozabad
- **Brass / Peetal:** Moradabad
- **Perfume / Attar:** Kannauj
- **Chikankari / Zardozi:** Lucknow
- **Carpet:** Bhadohi & Mirzapur
- **Black Pottery:** Nizamabad (Azamgarh)
- **Tarkashi (Brass Wire Inlay):** Mainpuri

### 4. Language Scope Formula: `22 vs 24`
- **Eighth Schedule:** Strictly **22** languages.
- **Sahitya Akademi:** **24** languages (**22 + English + Rajasthani**).
- *Remember:* Neither includes Bhojpuri or Maithili-Bhojpuri mix!
