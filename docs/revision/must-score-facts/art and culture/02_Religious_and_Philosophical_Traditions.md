## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. Indian philosophical systems are broadly classified into **Astika** (आस्तिक / Orthodox — those that accept the authority of the Vedas) and **Nastika** (नास्तिक / Heterodox — those that reject the authority of the Vedas, namely Buddhism, Jainism, and Charvaka/Lokayata).
2. The **Six Orthodox Schools of Indian Philosophy (Shad Darshana / षड्दर्शन)** are:
   - **Samkhya** (सांख्य) founded by Sage **Kapila**
   - **Yoga** (योग) founded by Sage **Patanjali**
   - **Nyaya** (न्याय) founded by Sage **Aksapada Gautama**
   - **Vaisheshika** (वैशेषिक) founded by Sage **Uluka Kanada**
   - **Purva Mimamsa** (पूर्व मीमांसा) founded by Sage **Jaimini**
   - **Uttara Mimamsa / Vedanta** (उत्तर मीमांसा / वेदांत) founded by Sage **Badarayana**.
3. **Samkhya** is the oldest philosophical system, postulating an absolute metaphysical dualism between two eternal realities: **Purusha** (पुरुष / pure, unchanging consciousness, passive witness) and **Prakriti** (प्रकृति / primordial unmanifest matter/nature).
4. In Samkhya, creation begins when Purusha comes into proximity with Prakriti, causing an imbalance among Prakriti's three fundamental qualities (**Gunas**): **Sattva** (purity, harmony, lightness), **Rajas** (passion, activity, motion), and **Tamas** (inertia, darkness, heaviness). Original classical Samkhya was non-theistic (**Nirishvara Samkhya**), asserting that the world evolutes without the necessity of a creator God.
5. **Yoga** philosophy provides the practical psychological and spiritual methodology for realizing the discriminative knowledge of Samkhya, codified in Patanjali’s **Yoga Sutra**.
6. Patanjali formulated the **Eightfold Path of Yoga (Ashtanga Yoga / अष्टांग योग)**:
   - **Yama** (moral ethical restraints: Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha)
   - **Niyama** (personal observances: Saucha, Santosha, Tapas, Svadhyaya, Ishvara-pranidhana)
   - **Asana** (steady, comfortable physical posture)
   - **Pranayama** (rhythmic breath regulation)
   - **Pratyahara** (sensory withdrawal from external objects)
   - **Dharana** (focused mental concentration on a single point)
   - **Dhyana** (unbroken meditative contemplation)
   - **Samadhi** (transcendental superconscious absorption).
7. **Nyaya** philosophy is a system of logical realism and epistemological rigor founded by Aksapada Gautama in the *Nyaya Sutra*, asserting that suffering is caused by ignorance and liberation (*Apavarga*) is attained through valid knowledge (*Prama*).
8. Nyaya recognizes **Four Valid Means of Knowledge (Pramanas / प्रमाण)**:
   - **Pratyaksha** (प्रत्यक्ष / direct sensory perception)
   - **Anumana** (अनुमान / logical inference, following a 5-step syllogism: Pratijna, Hetu, Udaharana, Upanaya, Nigamana)
   - **Upamana** (उपमान / comparison and analogy)
   - **Shabda** (शब्द / authoritative verbal testimony of a reliable person / *Apta*).
9. **Vaisheshika** philosophy, founded by Uluka Kanada in the *Vaisheshika Sutra*, is an analytical metaphysics based on **Atomic Pluralism (Paramanu-vada / परमाणुवाद)**, asserting that all physical objects in the material universe are formed by the aggregation of indivisible, indestructible, eternal atoms of four physical elements: Earth (*Prithvi*), Water (*Jala*), Fire (*Tejas*), and Air (*Vayu*), interacting in Ether (*Akasha*), Time (*Kala*), Space (*Dik*), Self (*Atman*), and Mind (*Manas*).
10. Vaisheshika categorizes the entire universe into **Seven Padarthas (Categories of Reality)**: **Dravya** (substance), **Guna** (quality), **Karma** (action/motion), **Samanya** (generality/universal), **Vishesha** (particularity/individuality), **Samavaya** (inherent inseparable relation), and **Abhava** (non-existence).
11. **Purva Mimamsa** (or simply *Mimamsa*), founded by Jaimini in the *Mimamsa Sutra*, is deeply rooted in Vedic ritualism and hermeneutics, asserting that the Vedas are eternal, authorless (**Apaurusheya**), and infallible; dharma consists in the strict performance of Vedic sacrificial injunctions (**Vidhi**).
12. Mimamsa was further developed by two influential commentator schools: the school of **Kumarila Bhatta** (which accepts 6 Pramanas, adding *Arthapatti* / postulation and *Anupalabdhi* / non-apprehension) and the school of **Prabhakara** (which accepts 5 Pramanas).
13. **Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta)**, founded by Badarayana in the **Brahma Sutras** (or *Vedanta Sutras*), is the philosophical inquiry into the nature of **Brahman** (Ultimate Reality) based on the philosophical reflections of the Upanishads.
14. The canonical bedrock of all Vedanta schools is the **Prasthanatrayi (प्रस्थानत्रयी / Three Foundational Source Texts)**:
    - **Shruti Prasthana** — The Principal **Upanishads**
    - **Smriti Prasthana** — The **Bhagavad Gita**
    - **Nyaya Prasthana** — The **Brahma Sutras** of Badarayana.
15. **Advaita Vedanta (अद्वैत / Absolute Non-Dualism)** was systematized in the 8th century CE by **Adi Shankaracharya** (disciple of Govinda Bhagavatpada), encapsulated in his famous dictum:
    - *"Brahma Satyam Jagan Mithya, Jivo Brahmaiva Naparah"* (Brahman alone is real; the empirical world is an illusion / *Mithya*; the individual soul is non-different from Brahman).
16. Adi Shankara asserted that the empirical world is perceived through **Maya** (cosmic illusion) and individual ignorance (**Avidya**); Ultimate Reality is **Nirguna Brahman** (attributeless, formless, pure consciousness), and liberation (*Moksha*) is attained through transcendental spiritual knowledge (**Jnana Marga**).
17. Adi Shankaracharya established **Four Cardinal Monastic Centers (Amnaya Peethas / चार धाम)** across India to safeguard and revitalize Sanatana Dharma:
    - **Dakshinamnaya Sri Sharada Peetham at Sringeri** (Karnataka) — associated with the **Yajurveda**; Mahavakya: *Aham Brahmasmi* (I am Brahman).
    - **Purvamnaya Govardhan Math at Puri** (Odisha) — associated with the **Rigveda**; Mahavakya: *Prajnanam Brahma* (Consciousness is Brahman).
    - **Pashchimamnaya Kalika / Dwaraka Peeth at Dwarka** (Gujarat) — associated with the **Samaveda**; Mahavakya: *Tat Tvam Asi* (That Thou Art).
    - **Uttaramnaya Jyotirmath at Badrinath / Joshimath** (Uttarakhand) — associated with the **Atharvaveda**; Mahavakya: *Ayam Atma Brahma* (This Self is Brahman).
18. **Vishishtadvaita (विशिष्टाद्वैत / Qualified Non-Dualism)** was expounded by **Sri Ramanujacharya** (1017–1137 CE), asserting that Brahman is **Saguna** (possessing infinite auspicious attributes), personified as Lord **Narayana / Vishnu**.
19. Ramanuja posited that individual souls (*Chit*) and insentient matter (*Achit*) are real, eternal entities that constitute the 'body' (*Sharira*) of God (*Sharirin*); liberation is attained through total loving surrender (**Bhakti** and **Prapatti / Sharanagati**) to Lord Vishnu.
20. **Dvaita Vedanta (द्वैत / Absolute Dualism)** was established by **Sri Madhvacharya** (1238–1317 CE), who rejected non-dualism and posited five eternal, absolute, irreducible distinctions (**Pancha Bheda**): between God and Soul, between God and Matter, between Soul and Soul, between Soul and Matter, and between Matter and Matter.
21. **Dvaitadvaita (द्वैताद्वैत / Dualistic Non-Dualism or Bhedabheda)** was founded by **Nimbarkacharya**, asserting that the individual soul and world are both distinct from Brahman and yet completely dependent upon Him, focusing on the devotion of Radha-Krishna.
22. **Shuddhadvaita (शुद्धाद्वैत / Pure Non-Dualism)** was founded by **Vallabhacharya** (1479–1531 CE), who propounded the **Pushtimarg** (Path of Grace), asserting that the entire universe is the real, unpolluted manifestation of Sri Krishna without the intermediary of Maya.
23. **Achintya Bhedabheda (अचिन्त्यभेदाभेद / Inconceivable Oneness and Difference)** was formulated by **Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** (1486–1534 CE) and codified by Baladeva Vidyabhushana, asserting that the soul's relation with Krishna is incomprehensible to human logic, celebrated through congregational chanting (**Harinama Sankirtana**).
24. **Charvaka (Lokayata / चार्वाक दर्शन)**, traditionally attributed to Sage **Brihaspati**, is the extreme materialistic school of ancient India; it accepted only direct sensory perception (**Pratyaksha**) as valid knowledge, firmly rejecting the soul, karma, rebirth, heaven/hell, and God:
    - *"Yavajjivet sukham jivet, rinam kritva ghritam pibet"* (As long as you live, live happily; take a loan and drink ghee, for once the body turns to ashes, there is no return).
25. **Buddhism (बौद्ध धर्म)** was founded in the 6th century BCE by **Siddhartha Gautama (The Buddha)** of the Shakya clan:
    - *Birth:* **Lumbini** (Kapilavastu, modern Nepal)
    - *Great Renunciation (Mahabhinishkramana):* Left home at age 29
    - *Enlightenment (Nirvana/Bodhi):* At age 35 under the Bodhi tree at **Bodh Gaya** (Bihar) on the banks of Niranjana / Phalgu River
    - *First Sermon (Dharmachakrapravartana):* At the Deer Park in **Rishipatana / Sarnath** (Varanasi, UP) to five ascetics (*Panchavaggiya*)
    - *Mahaparinirvana (Death):* At age 80 in **Kushinagar** (UP), capital of the Mallas.
26. The core doctrine of Buddhism rests on the **Four Noble Truths (Chatvari Arya Satyani)**:
    1. *Dukkha:* Life is full of suffering.
    2. *Dukkha Samudaya:* Suffering has a cause, which is craving/desire (*Tanha / Trishna*).
    3. *Dukkha Nirodha:* Suffering can be ceased by extinguishing desire (*Nirvana*).
    4. *Dukkha Nirodha Gamini Pratipada:* The path leading to the cessation of suffering is the **Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga)**.
27. The **Eightfold Path (अष्टांगिक मार्ग)** is divided into three ethical divisions: **Prajna** (Wisdom: Right View, Right Resolve), **Shila** (Conduct: Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood), and **Samadhi** (Meditation: Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration); it advocates the **Madhyama Pratipada (Middle Path)** between extreme asceticism and sensory indulgence.
28. Foundational Buddhist philosophical tenets: **Pratityasamutpada** (प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद / Dependent Origination — "When this exists, that comes to be"), **Anatta / Anatman** (अनात्मन / Non-self — no permanent unchanging soul), and **Anicca / Anitya** (अनित्य / Impermanence — all conditioned phenomena are transient).
29. The **Four Buddhist Councils (संगीतियां)**:
    - **1st Council (483 BCE):** Held at **Saptaparni Cave, Rajagriha** under King **Ajatashatru** (Haryanka); presided by **Mahakassapa**; compiled the **Vinaya Pitaka** (monastic discipline recited by Upali) and **Sutta Pitaka** (Buddha's discourses recited by Ananda).
    - **2nd Council (383 BCE):** Held at **Vaishali** under King **Kalasoka** (Shishunaga); presided by **Sabbakami**; first schism occurred between orthodox **Sthaviravadins** (Theravada) and progressive **Mahasanghikas**.
    - **3rd Council (250 BCE):** Held at **Pataliputra** under Emperor **Ashoka** (Maurya); presided by **Moggaliputta Tissa**; compiled the third pitaka, the **Abhidhamma Pitaka** (philosophical analysis), completing the **Tripitaka**; decided to send Buddhist missions abroad.
    - **4th Council (72 CE):** Held at **Kundalvana, Kashmir** under King **Kanishka** (Kushan); presided by **Vasumitra** with **Ashvaghosha** as vice-chairman; permanent schism into **Hinayana** (Lesser Vehicle / Theravada) and **Mahayana** (Greater Vehicle).
30. **Hinayana** (Theravada) revere Buddha as an enlightened human teacher, practice Pali texts, do not worship idols (use symbols like Footprints, Stupa, Wheel, Bodhi Tree), and strive for personal liberation as an **Arhat**.
31. **Mahayana** deify Lord Buddha, believe in idol worship and temples, adopt Sanskrit texts, and aspire for the ideal of the **Bodhisattva** (compassionate beings who delay their own Nirvana to liberate all suffering humanity, such as Avalokiteshvara/Padmapani, Manjushri, and Maitreya — the Future Buddha).
32. **Vajrayana (Tantric Buddhism)** developed in eastern India (Bengal and Bihar under the Palas) during the 8th century, emphasizing mantras, mudras, mandalas, and mystical union to attain rapid enlightenment, headquartered at Vikramashila University.
33. **Jainism (जैन धर्म)** is an ancient eternal religion revealed through **24 Tirthankaras** (Ford-makers across the stream of existence):
    - **1st Tirthankara:** **Rishabhanatha / Adinatha** (born in **Ayodhya**, Uttar Pradesh; emblem: Bull)
    - **23rd Tirthankara:** **Parshvanatha** (born in **Varanasi**, Uttar Pradesh; prince of King Ashvasena; emblem: Serpent; formulated the Four Vows)
    - **24th Tirthankara:** **Vardhamana Mahavira** (born 599/540 BCE at **Kundagrama** near Vaishali, Bihar; Jnatrika kshatriya clan; achieved *Kevala Jnana* under a Sal tree on the Rijupalika River; attained Nirvana at **Pavapuri**, Bihar at age 72; emblem: Lion).
34. The core ethical pillars of Jainism are the **Triratna (Three Jewels / रत्नत्रय)**:
    1. **Samyak Darshana** (Right Faith)
    2. **Samyak Jnana** (Right Knowledge)
    3. **Samyak Charitra** (Right Conduct).
35. Right Conduct in Jainism is practiced through the **Five Great Vows (Pancha Mahavratas)**:
    - **Ahimsa** (Non-violence in thought, word, and deed)
    - **Satya** (Truthfulness)
    - **Asteya** (Non-stealing)
    - **Aparigraha** (Non-possession / detachment from wealth)
    - **Brahmacharya** (Chastity / celibacy) — **added specifically by Mahavira** (the first four were established by Parshvanatha).
36. Epistemological and metaphysical tenets of Jainism:
    - **Anekantavada** (अनेकांतवाद / Doctrine of Many-Sidedness of Reality — truth and reality are complex and have multiple facets).
    - **Syadvada** (स्याद्वाद / Doctrine of Conditional Truth — all judgments are relative, conditional, and hold good only from a specific point of view, expressed through sevenfold predication / *Saptabhangi Naya*).
37. Major schism in Jainism occurred around the 3rd century BCE due to a 12-year famine in Magadha:
    - **Digambara** (दिसंबर / Sky-clad): Followed **Bhadrabahu** to Shravanabelagola (Karnataka); monks remain strictly naked, reject women attaining Moksha directly, and hold that Mahavira never married.
    - **Svetambara** (श्वेतांबर / White-clad): Remained in Magadha under **Sthulabhadra**; wear simple unstitched white robes, accept women can attain Moksha (e.g., 19th Tirthankara Mallinatha as female), and maintain canonical texts (*Agamas*).
38. The **Two Jain Councils**:
    - **1st Council (~300 BCE):** Held at **Pataliputra** under the leadership of **Sthulabhadra**; compiled the 12 Angas (Digambaras boycotted and rejected these texts).
    - **2nd Council (512 CE):** Held at **Valabhi (Gujarat)** under the chairmanship of **Devardhi Kshamasramana**; finalized and wrote down the Svetambara canonical texts (*Agamas*).
39. **Sallekhana (Santhara / संथारा)** is the ancient Jain ritual practice of voluntarily relinquishing life by gradual fasting when approaching death or incurable affliction; Mauryan Emperor **Chandragupta Maurya** famously performed Sallekhana at **Shravanabelagola** alongside Acharya Bhadrabahu (~297 BCE).
40. **Ajivika sect** (आजीवक संप्रदाय) was founded in the 6th century BCE by **Makkhali Gosala** (a contemporary of Buddha and Mahavira), propounding absolute, unalterable determinism or fatalism (**Niyati / नियतिवाद**), asserting that human effort (*Purushartha*) has no power over destiny; Ashoka and Dasharatha excavated the Barabar and Nagarjuni caves for Ajivika ascetics.
41. The **Bhakti Movement** originated in South India (Tamil country) between the 6th and 9th centuries CE through two groups of devotional poet-saints:
    - **Alvars** (12 Vaishnavite saints worshipping Vishnu; compiled the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*; included **Andal**, the only female Alvar saint / 'Meera of the South', and Nammalvar).
    - **Nayanars** (63 Shaivite saints worshipping Shiva; compiled hymns in the *Tevaram* and *Tirumurai*; foremost being Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar, Manikkavacakar, and female saint Karaikkal Ammaiyar).
42. The Bhakti movement was brought from South India to North India in the 14th century by Saint **Ramananda** (born at Prayagraj), celebrated in the adage: *"Bhakti dravidi upaji, laye Ramananda"* (Bhakti was born in the Dravida south, Ramananda brought it to the north).
43. Ramananda founded the **Ramanandi Sampradaya**, preached in the vernacular Hindi language in Varanasi, and admitted disciples from all castes, including:
    - **Kabir** (weaver)
    - **Ravidas / Raidas** (cobbler)
    - **Sena** (barber)
    - **Dhanna** (Jat peasant)
    - **Pipa** (Rajput prince)
    - **Sadhana** (butcher).
44. North Indian Bhakti developed two distinct theological streams:
    - **Nirguna Bhakti** (devotion to a formless, attributeless, transcendent God, rejecting idol worship, ritualism, and caste barriers): **Sant Kabir** (Varanasi; *Bijak*, *Sakhis*, *Dohas*), **Guru Nanak Dev** (founder of Sikhism; Kartarpur, Langar community kitchen).
    - **Saguna Bhakti** (devotion to God with human attributes, divine incarnations/avatars, using idols and emotional poetry): **Tulsidas** (composed *Ramcharitmanas* in Awadhi, *Vinaya Patrika*), **Surdas** (blind poet of Braj Bhasha; *Sursagar*, *Sur Saravali*), **Mirabai** (Rajasthan princess singing impassioned bhajans to Giridhar Gopal / Krishna), and **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** (Bengal; Gaudiya Vaishnavism).
45. The **Varkari Panth** of Maharashtra centered around the worship of Lord **Vithoba / Vitthala** (a manifestation of Krishna) at the sacred shrine of **Pandharpur**; its prominent saints: **Sant Dnyaneshwar** (wrote *Bhavartha Dipika / Dnyaneshwari*), **Sant Namdev**, **Sant Eknath**, and **Sant Tukaram** (composed devotional *Abhangas*; contemporary of Shivaji).
46. The **Sufi Movement** (सूफी आंदोलन) represented the mystical, esoteric dimension of Islam, emphasizing divine love (*Ishq*), spiritual meditation (*Zikr*), musical assemblies (*Sama*), and devotion to a spiritual guide (*Pir / Murshid*); it was organized into various orders called **Silsilas**.
47. The **Chishti Silsila** was founded in India by **Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti** (Gharib Nawaz), who arrived during Muhammad Ghori's invasion (~1192 CE) and established his sacred Dargah at **Ajmer**, Rajasthan; Chishti saints embraced poverty, simple community service, and musical gatherings (*Sama*).
48. The lineage of great Chishti saints: Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti (Ajmer) &rarr; **Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki** (Delhi; Qutub Minar dedicated to him) &rarr; **Baba Fariduddin Ganjshakar** (Faridkot/Pakpattan; his verses are included in the Guru Granth Sahib) &rarr; **Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya** (*Mahbub-e-Ilahi*, Delhi) &rarr; **Shaikh Nasiruddin Mahmud** (*Chirag-i-Dehli*) and **Shaikh Salim Chishti** (Fatehpur Sikri, blessed Akbar with son Jahangir).
49. **Amir Khusrau** (1253–1325), disciple of Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya, was buried at his master's feet in Delhi; he called himself *Tuti-e-Hind*, created the Hindavi language, and pioneered Qawwali.
50. Other major Sufi Silsilas:
    - **Suhrawardi Silsila:** Founded by Shaikh Shihabuddin Suhrawardi, established in India by **Shaikh Bahauddin Zakariya** of Multan; unlike Chishtis, they accepted state patronage, accumulated wealth, and held government posts.
    - **Qadiri Silsila:** Founded by Shaikh Abdul Qadir Gilani; favored by Mughal prince **Dara Shikoh** (who translated 50 Upanishads into Persian under the title *Sirr-i-Akbar*).
    - **Naqshbandi Silsila:** Established in India by Khwaja Baqi Billah and popularized by **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi** (Mujaddid Alif Sani); most orthodox and conservative order, strictly opposing Akbar's Din-i-Ilahi and music (*Sama*).

## 📊 Quick Reference Grid

### 1. Six Orthodox Schools of Indian Philosophy (Shad Darshana)

| Darshana School | Founding Sage | Foundational Text | Core Epistemology / Theory of Reality | Concept of Liberation (*Moksha*) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Samkhya** | **Kapila** | *Samkhya Sutra* | Dualism of **Purusha** (Consciousness) and **Prakriti** (Matter/3 Gunas); Satkaryavada | Realization that Purusha is completely aloof from Prakriti |
| **Yoga** | **Patanjali** | *Yoga Sutra* | Systematic practical spiritual path; **Ashtanga Yoga**; accepts Ishvara as guide | Attainment of *Kaivalya* through cessation of mental fluctuations (*Chitta-vritti-nirodha*) |
| **Nyaya** | **Aksapada Gautama** | *Nyaya Sutra* | Logical realism; **4 Pramanas** (Perception, Inference, Comparison, Testimony) | Freedom from pain and ignorance through valid knowledge (*Apavarga*) |

| **Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta)**| **Badarayana** | *Brahma Sutras* | Philosophy of Upanishads; inquiry into nature of **Brahman**; Prasthanatrayi | Realization of the true identity of Atman with Brahman |

### 2. The Six Vedantic Philosophical Sub-Schools

| Vedantic School | Founder / Acarya | Era & Base | Nature of Brahman & World | Way to Liberation (*Marga*) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Advaita** | **Adi Shankaracharya** | 8th C. CE; Kaladi | **Nirguna Brahman** alone is real; world is **Maya** / illusion; *Atman = Brahman* | **Jnana Marga** (Transcendental Knowledge) |
| **Vishishtadvaita** | **Sri Ramanujacharya** | 11th–12th C.; Srirangam| **Saguna Brahman (Narayana)**; world & souls are real attributes/body of God | **Bhakti & Prapatti** (Loving surrender) |
| **Dvaita** | **Sri Madhvacharya** | 13th C.; Udupi | Absolute dualism; **Pancha Bheda**; God, souls, and matter are eternally distinct | Devotion and grace of Lord Vishnu |
| **Dvaitadvaita** | **Nimbarkacharya** | 13th C.; Mathura | Soul and world are different yet non-separate/dependent on Brahman (Krishna) | Bhakti directed towards Radha-Krishna |
| **Shuddhadvaita** | **Vallabhacharya** | 15th–16th C.; Varanasi | Pure non-dualism; world is real manifestation of Krishna without Maya; **Pushtimarg** | Divine grace of God (*Pushti*) |
| **Achintya Bhedabheda**| **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** | 16th C.; Navadvipa | Inconceivable simultaneous difference and non-difference | **Sankirtana** (Chanting holy names) |

### 3. The Four Buddhist Councils Matrix

| Council | Year | Venue | Royal Patron | Presiding Monk | Major Doctrinal Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st Council** | 483 BCE | **Saptaparni Cave, Rajagriha** | **Ajatashatru** (Haryanka) | **Mahakassapa** | Sutta Pitaka (Ananda) & Vinaya Pitaka (Upali) recited and compiled |
| **2nd Council** | 383 BCE | **Vaishali** | **Kalasoka** (Shishunaga) | **Sabbakami** | First schism: Orthodox **Sthaviravadins** vs liberal **Mahasanghikas** |
| **3rd Council** | 250 BCE | **Pataliputra** | **Ashoka** (Maurya) | **Moggaliputta Tissa** | **Abhidhamma Pitaka** compiled; completed Tripitaka; dispatched missions |
| **4th Council** | 72 CE | **Kundalvana, Kashmir** | **Kanishka** (Kushan) | **Vasumitra** (VP: Ashvaghosha) | Permanent schism into **Hinayana** and **Mahayana**; Sanskrit commentaries |

## 🧠 Memory Anchors & Mnemonics

### 1. Six Schools of Philosophy & Founders — Formula: `K-P-G-K-J-B`

- **P**atanjali &rarr; **Y**oga
- **G**autama &rarr; **N**yaya
- **K**anada &rarr; **V**aisheshika
- **J**aimini &rarr; **P**urva Mimamsa
- **B**adarayana &rarr; **V**edanta

### 2. Four Buddhist Councils Venues & Kings:
- **Venues Formula: `R-V-P-K`** (Rajagriha &rarr; Vaishali &rarr; Pataliputra &rarr; Kashmir)
- **Kings Formula: `A-K-A-K`** (Ajatashatru &rarr; Kalasoka &rarr; Ashoka &rarr; Kanishka)

### 3. Four Shankaracharya Mathas — Clockwise from South: `S-P-D-B`
- **S**ringeri (South &rarr; Karnataka &rarr; Yajurveda)
- **P**uri (East &rarr; Odisha &rarr; Rigveda)
- **D**warka (West &rarr; Gujarat &rarr; Samaveda)
- **B**adrinath (North &rarr; Uttarakhand &rarr; Atharvaveda)

### 4. 23rd vs 24th Tirthankara Birthplaces in UP / Bihar:
- **Parshvanatha (23rd):** Born in **Varanasi (UP)**; 4 vows; Serpent symbol.
- **Mahavira (24th):** Born in **Kundagrama, Vaishali (Bihar)**; added 5th vow (Brahmacharya); Lion symbol.
