## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. India possesses **45 UNESCO World Heritage Properties (विश्व धरोहर स्थल)** as of 2026, placing India 6th globally in terms of total World Heritage sites:
   - **37 Cultural Properties** (including monuments, archaeological ruins, historic cities)
   - **7 Natural Properties** (Kaziranga, Keoladeo, Manas, Sundarbans, Nanda Devi & Valley of Flowers, Western Ghats, Great Himalayan National Park)
   - **1 Mixed Property (Cultural + Natural)**: **Khangchendzonga National Park** in Sikkim (inscribed in 2016).
2. The **First Four World Heritage Sites of India** were inscribed simultaneously in **1983**:
   - **Ajanta Caves** (Maharashtra)
   - **Ellora Caves** (Maharashtra)
   - **Agra Fort** (Uttar Pradesh)
   - **Taj Mahal** (Uttar Pradesh).
3. **Uttar Pradesh** hosts **4 UNESCO World Heritage Cultural Properties**:
   - **Agra Fort** (inscribed 1983)
   - **Taj Mahal** (inscribed 1983)
   - **Fatehpur Sikri** (inscribed 1986)
   - **Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath** (inscribed 2026).
4. **Uttarakhand** hosts **1 UNESCO World Heritage Natural Property**:
   - **Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks** (inscribed 1988, expanded to include Valley of Flowers in 2005).
5. The **UNESCO World Heritage Convention** was adopted by the General Conference of UNESCO at Paris in **1972** (Convention Concerning the Protection of the World Cultural and Natural Heritage), which India ratified in **1977**.
6. The **UNESCO Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage** was adopted in **2003**; India ratified it in **2005**, recognizing oral traditions, performing arts, social practices, rituals, festive events, and traditional craftsmanship.
7. India has **15 living elements inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH)**:
   1. **Koodiyattam** (Sanskrit temple theatre of Kerala; 2008)
   2. **Tradition of Vedic Chanting** (sacred recitation of Vedas; 2008)
   3. **Ramlila** (traditional performance of the Ramayana in North India; 2008)
   4. **Ramman** (religious festival and ritual theatre of the Garhwal Himalayas, Saloor-Dungra village, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand; 2009)
   5. **Chhau Dance** (martial dance with masked traditions of Purulia, Seraikella, and Mayurbhanj; 2010)
   6. **Kalbelia folk songs and dances of Rajasthan** (sensuous serpent community dance; 2010)
   7. **Mudiyettu** (ritual dance-drama depicting the battle of Kali and Darika in Kerala; 2010)
   8. **Buddhist Chanting of Ladakh** (sacred recitation of Buddhist texts in monasteries of Ladakh; 2012)
   9. **Sankirtana** (ritual singing, drumming on Pung, and dancing of Manipur; 2013)
   10. **Traditional brass and copper craft of utensil making among the Thatheras of Jandiala Guru**, Punjab (2014)
   11. **Yoga** (ancient philosophical and physical discipline; 2016)
   12. **Nawrouz / Nowruz** (Persian New Year; multinational inscription; 2016)
   13. **Kumbh Mela** (world's largest peaceful pilgrimage at Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain, Nashik; 2017)
   14. **Durga Puja in Kolkata** (grand public festival celebrating female energy and artistic craftsmanship; 2021)
   15. **Garba of Gujarat** (ritual community circle dance during Navratri; 2023).
8. **Ramman** (रम्माण) is the unique **UNESCO ICH element from Uttarakhand** (inscribed in 2009), celebrated annually in late April in the twin villages of **Saloor-Dungra in Joshimath block, Chamoli district**; villagers wear 18 hand-carved wooden masks (*Pattar*) depicting deities (Narasimha, Rama, Bhumal Devta) to reenact local epics without spoken dialogue, sung to the beats of the *Dhol-Damau*.
9. The **Traditional Brass and Copper Craft of the Thatheras of Jandiala Guru** (Amritsar, Punjab) is the **only Indian craft technique** inscribed on the UNESCO ICH list (2014); brass and copper sheets are heated, hammered into shapes with wooden mallets, and hand-chiseled with tiny indentations (*poth*) to preserve nutritional properties of food and water.
10. **India's First World Heritage City** is the **Historic City of Ahmadabad** (Gujarat), inscribed in **2017** for its preserved medieval architectural fabric (*Pols*, traditional wooden community residential gates and bird feeders).
11. **Jaipur City, Rajasthan**, was inscribed as India’s **second World Heritage City** in **2019**, celebrated for its 1727 urban town planning on a grid plan guided by Vedic Vastu Shastra under Sawai Jai Singh II and architect Vidyadhar Bhattacharya.
12. **Moidams of the Ahom Dynasty** at Charaideo, Assam (inscribed 2024), represents the 600-year-old royal mound-burial funerary architecture of the Tai-Ahom kings; a moidam consists of an underground vaulted brick/stone chamber housing the royal remains, covered by a massive hemispherical earthen mound surrounded by an octagonal boundary wall and open pavilion.
13. **Maratha Military Landscapes of India** (inscribed 2025) comprises **12 strategic forts** developed between the 17th and 19th centuries under Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and the Peshwas:
    - 11 forts in Maharashtra: Salher, Shivneri, Lohagad, Khanderi, Raigad, Rajgad, Pratapgad, Suvarnadurg, Panhala, Vijaydurg, Sindhudurg.
    - 1 fort in Tamil Nadu: **Gingee Fort** (the 'Troy of the East').
14. The **Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas** (Karnataka; inscribed 2023) encompasses three world-renowned 12th–13th century star-shaped temples: the **Chennakeshava Temple at Belur**, the **Hoysaleswara Temple at Halebidu**, and the **Keshava Temple at Somanathapura**.
15. **Santiniketan** (Birbhum district, West Bengal; inscribed 2023) was established in 1901 by **Rabindranath Tagore** as an open-air residential educational community based on ancient Indian *Ashram* ideals, evolving into Visva-Bharati University in 1921.
16. **Kakatiya Rudreshwara (Ramappa) Temple** at Palampet (Mulugu district, Telangana; inscribed 2021), built in 1213 CE by Recharla Rudra under Kakatiya king Ganapatideva, is celebrated for its **floating porous bricks** (sponge-like bricks that float on water), sandbox foundation technique, and carved black basalt bracket figures (*Madanikas*).
17. The **UNESCO Creative Cities Network (UCCN)**, launched in 2004, recognizes cities that place creativity and cultural industries at the heart of their development; Indian member cities include:
    - **Jaipur** (Crafts and Folk Art, 2015)
    - **Varanasi** (Music, 2015 — Banaras Gharana, Shehnai, Thumri)
    - **Chennai** (Music, 2017 — Carnatic music festival Season)
    - **Mumbai** (Film, 2019 — Bollywood and cinema industries)
    - **Hyderabad** (Gastronomy, 2019 — Hyderabadi Biryani and culinary heritage)
    - **Srinagar** (Crafts and Folk Art, 2021 — Pashmina, papier-mache, carpet weaving)
    - **Gwalior** (Music, 2023 — Cradle of Gwalior Gharana and Tansen)
    - **Kozhikode** (Literature, 2023 — India's first City of Literature).
18. The **UNESCO Memory of the World Register** preserves documentary heritage of universal value; Indian collections inscribed include:
    - **Rigveda Manuscripts** (30 ancient birch-bark and paper manuscripts preserved at BORI, Pune; inscribed 2007)
    - **Gilgit Manuscripts** (5th–6th century birch-bark Buddhist texts discovered at Gilgit; inscribed 2017)
    - **Shanti Parva of the Mahabharata** (BORI, Pune)
    - **Maitrayani Samhita**
    - **Abhinavagupta’s Tantraloka manuscripts**
    - **Ramcharitmanas** of Tulsidas, **Panchatantra** of Vishnusharma, and **Sahridaya-aloka-lochana** (added recently).
19. Under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act (AMASR Act, 1958), a monument of national importance has a statutory **Prohibited Area of 100 meters** around the monument (where no construction of any kind is permitted) and a further **Regulated Area of 200 meters** (where construction requires permission from the National Monuments Authority / NMA).
20. The **National Culture Fund (NCF)** was established by the Government of India in **1996** as a charitable trust under the Charitable Endowments Act, 1890, enabling public-private partnerships (PPP) and corporate social responsibility (CSR) contributions to restore monuments.
