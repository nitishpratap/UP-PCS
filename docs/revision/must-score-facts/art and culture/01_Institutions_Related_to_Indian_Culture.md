## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The **Asiatic Society of Bengal** was founded on **15 January 1784** at Fort William in Calcutta by the English philologist and jurist **Sir William Jones**.
2. Governor-General **Warren Hastings** was invited to be the first President of the Asiatic Society, but he politely **declined in favour of Sir William Jones**, who remained President until his death in 1794.
3. The Asiatic Society launched the research journal **Asiatic Researches** in 1788 and pioneered the English translations of ancient Sanskrit masterpieces: Charles Wilkins translated the *Bhagavad Gita* (1785), while William Jones translated Kalidasa’s *Abhijnanasakuntalam* (1789) and *Manusmriti* (1794).
4. The **Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute (BORI)** at Pune, Maharashtra, was founded in **1917** in honour of pioneer Indologist **Ramkrishna Gopal Bhandarkar**; it is world-renowned for publishing the 19-volume Critical Edition of the *Mahabharata* (completed in 1966).
5. In the ancient Indian **Gurukul system** (गुरुकुल), students lived as residential family members of the guru's household; the teacher never demanded fixed commercial tuition fees beforehand, receiving voluntary **Guru Dakshina** (गुरु दक्षिणा) only upon the completion of graduation (*Samavartana* ceremony).
6. Classical Indian pedagogy recognized **18 Vidyas** (comprising 4 Vedas, 6 Vedangas, 4 Upavedas, Mimamsa, Nyaya, Puranas, and Dharmashastras) and **64 Kalas** (practical arts and vocational crafts).
7. The Ashrama lifecycle divided human life into four 25-year spans: **Brahmacharya** (celibate student learning), **Grihastha** (householder family life), **Vanaprastha** (gradual retirement/contemplation), and **Sannyasa** (complete renunciation).
8. The **Calcutta Madrasa (Madrasa-i-Aliya)** was founded in **October 1780 / 1781** by Governor-General **Warren Hastings** at his own expense to educate Muslims in Arabic, Persian, and Islamic law.
9. The **Sanskrit College at Banaras** was established in **1791** by British Resident **Jonathan Duncan** to cultivate the laws, literature, and religion of the Hindus.
10. **Fort William College** was founded on **10 July 1800** at Calcutta by Governor-General **Lord Wellesley** to train British civil servants in Indian vernacular languages, law, and customs; it established formal departments for Hindustani (headed by **John Gilchrist**), Bengali, Arabic, Persian, and Sanskrit.
11. **Takshashila (Taxila / तक्षशिला)**, situated in the Rawalpindi district of modern **Pakistan**, was the capital of the Gandhara kingdom and flourished from ~6th century BCE to 5th century CE as the world's earliest renowned center of higher education.
12. Takshashila was **not a single centralized university campus** with formal colleges or hostels, but rather a vibrant decentralized hub of hundreds of independent master teachers (*Acharyas*) where students aged 16+ enrolled for advanced specialized training.
13. Eminent historical luminaries educated or teaching at Takshashila:
    - **Chanakya (Kautilya / Vishnugupta)** — composed the foundational treatise on statecraft, the *Arthashastra*.
    - **Panini** — ancient grammarian who composed the foundational Sanskrit linguistic treatise, the *Astadhyayi*.
    - **Jivaka** — personal physician to King Bimbisara and Lord Buddha, who studied Ayurvedic medicine and surgery at Takshashila for seven years.
    - **Charaka** — author of the foundational medical compendium *Charaka Samhita*.
    - **King Prasenajit** of Kosala.
14. **Nalanda Mahavihara (नालंदा महाविहार)**, located in Nalanda district, **Bihar**, was founded in the **early 5th century CE** by Gupta Emperor **Kumaragupta I** (who held the title *Shakra-aditya* or *Mahendraditya*).
15. Nalanda flourished under the royal patronage of the Imperial Guptas, Emperor **Harshavardhana** of Kannauj (who donated the revenues of 100 to 200 villages to the university), and the **Pala Emperors of Bengal and Bihar** (such as Devapala, who granted 5 villages at the request of King Balaputradeva of the Sailendra dynasty of Sumatra/Java).
16. Nalanda was primarily a premier international Mahayana Buddhist university, but admitted students of all sects to study logic (*Hetuvidya*), grammar (*Sabdavidya*), medicine (*Chikitsavidya*), astronomy, and the Vedas.
17. Admission to Nalanda was intensely competitive; candidates had to pass rigorous oral scholastic examinations conducted at the entrance gate by the gatekeeper scholar called the **Dwarapandita** (द्वारपंडित), with only 20–30% of aspirants gaining entry.
18. The legendary library of Nalanda was named **Dharmaganja** (धर्मगंज / Mart of Religion), housing hundreds of thousands of handwritten palm-leaf manuscripts across three multi-storeyed palatial buildings:
    - **Ratnasagara** (रत्नसागर / Ocean of Jewels)
    - **Ratnodadhi** (रत्नोदधि / Sea of Jewels; a nine-storey colossal tower)
    - **Ratnaranjaka** (रत्नरंजक / Jewel-Adorned).
19. Chinese pilgrim-monk **Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)** spent five years at Nalanda (637–642 CE), studying Buddhist philosophy (Yogacara) under the venerable octogenarian Chancellor **Shilabhadra** (शीलभद्र); fellow pilgrim **I-Tsing (Yijing)** arrived in 673 CE and studied for ten years.
20. Nalanda was destroyed and burned to ashes in **~1193 CE** by the Turkic military commander **Ikhtiyar-ud-din Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khalji** during his eastern campaign, massacring thousands of Buddhist monks and burning the Dharmaganja library for several months.
21. The archaeological ruins of Nalanda Mahavihara were inscribed on the **UNESCO World Heritage List in July 2016**.
22. The new **Nalanda University** was revived as an international institution under the Nalanda University Act, 2010, located at **Rajgir**, Bihar, operating under the administrative oversight of the **Ministry of External Affairs (MEA)**.
23. **Vikramashila University (विक्रमशिला)**, located at Antichak village in Bhagalpur district, **Bihar**, was founded in the late 8th century CE by Pala Emperor **Dharmapala** to counter the perceived decline in Buddhist monastic discipline.
24. Vikramashila became the foremost world center for **Vajrayana (Tantric) Buddhism**, metaphysics, logic, and philosophy; it was laid out as a massive square monastery with 108 temples and a central stupa.
25. The most illustrious scholar of Vikramashila was **Atisa Dipankara Srijnana** (982–1054 CE), who served as the chief chancellor before embarking on a historic mission to Tibet to reform and revive Tibetan Buddhism.
26. Vikramashila was destroyed alongside Nalanda in **1200 CE** by the raiding armies of **Bakhtiyar Khalji**.
27. **Valabhi (वल्लभी)**, situated in the Bhavnagar district of Saurashtra, **Gujarat**, was the royal capital of the **Maitraka dynasty** (5th–8th c. CE) and flourished as the premier western center of **Hinayana Buddhism** (Theravada), statecraft, public administration, law, and commerce, documented by both Hiuen Tsang and I-Tsing.
28. **Odantapuri (ओदंतपुरी)**, located at modern Bihar Sharif in Bihar, was established by Pala dynasty founder **Gopala I** (8th c. CE); its monastic architecture served as the physical model for the iconic Samye Monastery in Tibet.
29. **Jagaddala Mahavihara (जगद्दल)** in Varendra (North Bengal), founded by Pala king **Ramapala** (late 11th c. CE), was the last great seat of Vajrayana Buddhist learning in eastern India before the Turkic invasions.
30. **Somapura Mahavihara (सोमपुर)** at Paharpur (now in Bangladesh), founded by Pala Emperor **Dharmapala** (late 8th c. CE), was the largest Buddhist monastery south of the Himalayas, designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.
31. **Kanchi (Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu)** was the premier ancient **Ghatika** (scholastic assembly/college) of South India, patronized by the Pallava kings; Mayurasharman, the founder of the Kadamba dynasty, traveled to Kanchi with his guru to complete his Vedic studies.
32. The **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** was founded in **1861** under the British Raj, with British military engineer and Indologist **Sir Alexander Cunningham** appointed as its first Archaeological Surveyor (and later Director-General), universally revered as the **'Father of Indian Archaeology'**.
33. The modern institutional foundation of the ASI was reorganized by Viceroy **Lord Curzon**, who passed the landmark **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act, 1904** and appointed **Sir John Marshall** as Director-General in 1902 (who oversaw the groundbreaking excavations of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro in the 1920s).
34. The **Indian Museum** at Kolkata, West Bengal, was founded in **1814** by Danish botanist **Dr. Nathaniel Wallich** at the cradle of the Asiatic Society of Bengal; it is the **oldest and largest multipurpose museum in India and the Asia-Pacific region**.
35. The **National Museum, New Delhi**, was established in **1949** (initially housed in Rashtrapati Bhavan, inaugurated at its present Janpath building in 1960 by Vice President Dr. S. Radhakrishnan); it functions directly under the Ministry of Culture.
36. The **National Archives of India (NAI)** was originally founded as the Imperial Record Department in **March 1891 at Calcutta**, headed by G.W. Forrest, before being relocated to New Delhi in 1911 upon the transfer of the imperial capital.
37. The **National Library of India** at Belvedere Estate, Alipore, Kolkata, was formed under Lord Curzon via the **Imperial Library Act, 1902**, uniting the Calcutta Public Library (1836) and the Imperial Library; after independence, it was renamed the National Library under the National Library Act, 1948, opened to the public in 1953 by Education Minister Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
38. The **Salar Jung Museum** in **Hyderabad, Telangana**, inaugurated in December 1951, is one of the three National Museums of India; it houses the colossal single-man private art collection of Nawab Mir Yousuf Ali Khan (**Salar Jung III**), former Prime Minister of Hyderabad.
39. The **Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA)**, established in **1987** at New Delhi as an autonomous trust under the Ministry of Culture (founded with scholar **Dr. Kapila Vatsyayan** as academic mentor), is dedicated to integrated research across oral traditions, performing arts, and visual arts through its five divisions (*Kalanidhi*, *Kalakosa*, *Janapada Sampada*, *Kaladarsana*, *Sutradhara*).
40. The **Centre for Cultural Resources and Training (CCRT)** was established in **May 1979** at New Delhi under the Ministry of Culture, dedicated to training school teachers and students in linking education with regional culture and folklore.
41. The **National Mission for Manuscripts (NMM)** was established in **February 2003** by the Ministry of Tourism and Culture to identify, catalog, and preserve India's estimated 10 million handwritten manuscripts; its national catalog portal is called **Kriti Sampada** (rebranded in 2025 as the **Gyan Bharatam Mission**).
42. The **National Gallery of Modern Art (NGMA)** was inaugurated on **29 March 1954** by Vice President Dr. S. Radhakrishnan at **Jaipur House**, near India Gate, New Delhi, housing the premier national collection of modern and contemporary Indian art from 1857 onward (featuring works of Raja Ravi Varma, Abanindranath Tagore, Nandalal Bose, and Amrita Sher-Gil).
43. The **Victoria Memorial Hall** at Kolkata, commissioned by Lord Curzon after the death of Queen Victoria in 1901 and designed by Sir William Emerson in Indo-Saracenic and Italian Renaissance styles, opened in 1921 as a national museum.
44. The **Allahabad Museum** in Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh, founded in 1931 within Chandrashekhar Azad Park, is one of the four prestigious National Museums under the Ministry of Culture, renowned for its stone sculptures, miniature paintings, and the pistol of martyr Chandrashekhar Azad.
45. The **Nehru Memorial Museum and Library (NMML)**, housed at the historic **Teen Murti Bhavan** in New Delhi (former official residence of the Commander-in-Chief and first PM Jawaharlal Nehru), was established in 1964 and renamed the **Prime Ministers' Museum and Library Society (Pradhanmantri Sangrahalaya)** in 2023.
