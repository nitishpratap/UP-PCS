## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Shramana** (श्रमण) renouncer traditions (often **nastika** / नास्तिक, rejecting the absolute authority of the Vedas) arose in the **Ganga valley in the 6th century BCE** against Brahmanical sacrificial orthodoxy (*yajna*). Core heterodox schools: **Buddhism, Jainism, Ajivikas, and Charvaka / Lokayata**.
2. **Gautama Buddha (Siddhartha)** was born in **563 BCE** in the **Shakya kshatriya** clan at **Lumbini** (near Kapilavastu, modern southern Nepal), marked by Ashoka’s **Rummindei** pillar. Father was **Suddhodana**; mother was **Mahamaya** (Koliyan clan); raised by stepmother **Mahapajapati Gotami** (first Buddhist nun).
3. The **Great Renunciation (*Mahabhinishkramana*)** occurred at age **29** when Siddhartha left his palace on his horse **Kanthaka** and charioteer **Channa** after witnessing the **Four Sights**: an old man, a sick man, a dead body, and an ascetic.
4. Early teachers: **Alara Kalama** at Vaishali (taught meditation and philosophy of Upanishads/Samkhya) and **Uddaka Ramaputta** at Rajagriha.
5. **Enlightenment (*Sambodhi / Nirvana*)** was attained at age **35** on **Vaishakha Purnima** under a Pipal tree (**Bodhi Tree**) on the banks of river **Niranjana (Phalgu)** at **Bodh Gaya** (Uruvela, Bihar).
6. **First Sermon (*Dharmachakrapravartana* — "Turning of the Wheel of Law")** was delivered to five ascetic companions (*Panchavaggiya*) at the Deer Park in **Rishipattana / Sarnath** near Varanasi (UP).
7. **Mahaparinirvana (Death)** occurred in **483 BCE** at the age of **80** in a Sala grove at **Kushinagar** (capital of the Malla republic, eastern UP). His last words to Ananda: *"Appo Deepo Bhava"* ("Be a light unto yourself"). His last convert was ascetic **Subhadda**.
8. **Four Noble Truths (*Chatvari Arya Satyani*)**:
   - 1. **Dukkha**: Life is full of suffering.
   - 2. **Dukkha Samudaya**: Craving / desire (*Tanha / Trishna*) is the cause of suffering.
   - 3. **Dukkha Nirodha**: Suffering can be ceased by extinguishing desire (*Nirvana*).
   - 4. **Dukkha Nirodha Gamini Pratipada (Magga)**: The path to cease suffering is the **Noble Eightfold Path** (*Ashtangika Marga*).
9. **Noble Eightfold Path (*Ashtangika Marga*)**: Categorized into **Prajna** (Right View, Right Resolve), **Shila** (Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood), and **Samadhi** (Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration). It represents the **Middle Path (*Madhyama Pratipada*)** avoiding extremes of asceticism and luxury.
10. **Pratityasamutpada (Dependent Origination)**: The foundational Buddhist philosophical law of universal causation: *"When this exists, that comes to be; from the arising of this, that arises"*. It operates through the **Twelve Nidanas** (links of causation, *Dvadasa Nidana*).
11. **Anatta (Anatman)**: The core Buddhist doctrine of **"No-Soul"** — there is no permanent, unchanging, eternal soul or self (*Atman*). A human being is merely a temporary bundle of **Five Aggregates (*Skandhas*)**: form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness.
12. **Anicca (Anitya)**: The doctrine of **universal impermanence** — all conditioned things are transient and in constant flux (*Kshanikavada* — doctrine of momentariness).
13. **First Buddhist Council (483 BCE)**: Convened at **Sattapanni Cave, Rajagriha** under king **Ajatashatru** (Haryanka dynasty), presided by elder **Mahakassapa**. **Upali** recited the **Vinaya Pitaka** (monastic discipline); **Ananda** recited the **Sutta Pitaka** (Buddha’s discourses).
14. **Second Buddhist Council (383 BCE)**: Convened at **Balukarama Monastery, Vaishali** under king **Kalashoka (Kakavarna)** of the Shishunaga dynasty, presided by **Sabakami**. Resulted in the **First Great Schism**: orthodox elders separated as **Sthaviravadins (Theravadins)** while the reformist majority separated as **Mahasanghikas**.
15. **Third Buddhist Council (c. 250 BCE)**: Convened at **Asokarama, Pataliputra** under emperor **Ashoka Maurya**, presided by **Moggaliputta Tissa**. Compiled the **Abhidhamma Pitaka** (philosophical analysis; Tissa composed *Kathavatthu* refuting heresies) and dispatched Buddhist missionaries across India, Sri Lanka (Mahinda & Sanghamitta), and Hellenistic kingdoms.
16. **Fourth Buddhist Council (72 CE)**: Convened at **Kundalvana in Kashmir** under Kushana emperor **Kanishka I**, presided by **Vasumitra** with poet **Ashvaghosha** as vice-president. Resulted in the final split into **Hinayana** ("Lesser Vehicle" / Theravada) and **Mahayana** ("Great Vehicle"); compiled Sanskrit encyclopedic commentaries named **Mahavibhasha Shastra**.
17. **Hinayana vs Mahayana**:
    - **Hinayana (Theravada)**: Orthodox; considers Buddha a supreme human teacher; ideal is the **Arhat** (achieving personal liberation); scriptures written in **Pali**; strictly prohibits image worship (uses symbols: foot-prints, stupa, umbrella).
    - **Mahayana**: Considers Buddha an eternal cosmic deity; ideal is the **Bodhisattva** (compassionate being who delays own Nirvana to liberate all beings); scriptures written in **Sanskrit**; practices **image/idol worship** with elaborate rituals (birth of Gandhara & Mathura Buddhist art).
18. **Vajrayana (Tantric Buddhism)**: Developed in eastern India (Bengal and Bihar under Palas) around the 8th century CE; centers on secret mantras, mudras, mandalas, tantric rituals, and female consorts (*Taras*); centers at **Vikramashila** and **Odantapuri**.
19. **Major Bodhisattvas of Mahayana**:
    - **Avalokiteshvara (Padmapani)**: Personification of universal **compassion** (*Karuna*); depicted holding a lotus; celebrated in Lotus Sutra.
    - **Manjushri**: Personification of transcendent **wisdom** (*Prajna*); holds a flaming sword of wisdom and a book (*Prajnaparamita*).
    - **Vajrapani**: Wielder of the thunderbolt (*Vajra*); represents the power of all Buddhas.
    - **Maitreya**: The **Future Buddha**, currently residing in the Tushita heaven, who will incarnate on earth when the Dharma declines.
    - **Ksitigarbha**: Guardian of the earth and savior of souls trapped in hell realms.
    - **Amitabha (Amitayus)**: Buddha of Infinite Light; presides over the Western Pure Land (*Sukhavati*).
20. **Madhyamaka (Sunyavada)**: Founded by **Nagarjuna** (2nd c. CE, court of Satavahanas); foundational text: *Mulamadhyamakakarika*; propounds that all phenomena are **Sunya** (empty of intrinsic, independent existence) because they arise dependently (*Pratityasamutpada*). Nagarjuna is called the *"Einstein of India"*.
21. **Yogachara (Vijnanavada)**: Founded by brothers **Asanga and Vasubandhu** (4th–5th c. CE); asserts **idealistic subjective monism**: the external world is an illusion; only consciousness (*Vijnana / Alayavijnana*) truly exists.
22. **Tripitaka (The Three Baskets of Pali Canon)**:
    - **Vinaya Pitaka**: Monastic discipline, codes of conduct for monks (*Bhikkhus*) and nuns (*Bhikkhunis*); contains *Patimokkha*, *Suttavibhanga*, *Khandhaka*, and *Parivara*.
    - **Sutta Pitaka**: Buddha’s discourses and sermons; divided into **5 Nikayas**: *Digha Nikaya* (Long discourses; contains *Mahaparinibbana Sutta*), *Majjhima Nikaya* (Middle-length), *Samyutta Nikaya*, *Anguttara Nikaya* (contains list of **16 Mahajanapadas**), and *Khuddaka Nikaya* (contains *Dhammapada*, *Jatakas*, *Theragatha*, *Therigatha*).
    - **Abhidhamma Pitaka**: Philosophical, psychological, and metaphysical systematization of doctrine; 7 books including *Dhammasangani* and *Kathavatthu*.
23. **Milinda Panha ("Questions of King Menander")**: Celebrated Buddhist text in Pali recording the theological debates between Indo-Greek king **Menander I (Milinda)** and Buddhist sage **Nagasena** (~150 BCE), leading to Menander's conversion to Buddhism.
24. **Buddhist Monastic Architecture**:
    - **Chaitya**: A sacred Buddhist prayer hall or shrine with a stupa at the apsidal rear end (e.g., **Karle Chaitya**, Bhaja, Ajanta Cave 19 & 26).
    - **Vihara**: A residential monastery with living cells for monks arranged around a central pillared courtyard (e.g., Kanheri, Nalanda).
    - **Stupa**: A hemispherical earthen/brick relic mound (*Anda*) crowned by a square railing (*Harmika*) and an umbrella rod (*Chhatra*), surrounded by a processional circumambulatory path (*Pradakshina-patha*) and ceremonial gateways (*Toranas*).
25. **Jainism Antiquity**: Jain tradition reveres **24 Tirthankaras** (Ford-makers across the ocean of worldly existence); not founded by Mahavira, who was the 24th and final Tirthankara.
26. **1st Tirthankara**: **Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)**; emblem is the **Bull**; born in **Ayodhya**; credited with teaching agriculture, crafts, and societal organization; mentioned in the **Rigveda** along with **Arishtanemi (22nd Tirthankara)**; also described in the *Vishnu Purana* and *Bhagavata Purana* as an incarnation of Vishnu.
27. **23rd Tirthankara**: **Parshvanatha**; historical prince born in **Varanasi** (son of king Ashvasena and queen Vama) ~250 years before Mahavira; emblem is the **Hooded Serpent**; attained Kaivalya and Nirvana on **Sammed Shikhar (Mount Parasnath, Giridih, Jharkhand)**; propounded the **Four Vows (*Chaturyama*)**: Ahimsa (non-violence), Satya (truth), Asteya (non-stealing), and Aparigraha (non-possession).
28. **24th Tirthankara**: **Vardhamana Mahavira**; born **599 BCE** (traditional date; shorter chronology uses 540 BCE) at **Kundagrama near Vaishali** (Bihar) in the **Jnatrika kshatriya** clan of the Vajji confederacy; father was **Siddhartha**; mother was **Trishala** (sister of Lichchhavi chief **Chetaka**; sister-in-law of Bimbisara); wife was **Yashoda**; daughter was **Anojja / Priyadarsana**.
29. Mahavira renounced worldly life at age **30**; practiced rigorous asceticism for 12 years; attained supreme omniscience (**Kevala Jnana**) at age **42** outside the town of **Jrimbhikagrama** on the banks of river **Rijupalika** under a Sala tree. Henceforth called **Jina** (conqueror), **Kevalin**, **Mahavira**, and **Nirgrantha** ("free from all bonds").
30. Mahavira added the **Fifth Vow: Brahmacharya (Celibacy)** to Parshvanatha’s four vows, completing the **Panchamahavratas**.
31. Mahavira passed away (**Nirvana**) in **527 BCE** (or 468 BCE) at age **72** at **Pavapuri (Pawa)** near Rajgriha in modern Nalanda district (Bihar), in the palace of king Hastipala of the Malla republic.
32. **Jain Philosophy Core Concepts**:
    - **Jiva and Ajiva**: Reality is divided into conscious living souls (**Jiva**) and non-conscious insentient matter (**Ajiva**). Every entity—including stones, water droplets, fire, wind, and trees—possesses a living Jiva that feels pain.
    - **Extreme Ahimsa**: Non-violence is the supreme ethical principle (*Ahimsa Paramo Dharmah*). Jain monks wear mouth cloths (*Muhapatti*) and sweep the path with brushes to avoid killing microscopic organisms.
    - **Karma**: Karma is conceived as physical, subtle atomic matter (*Karma-pudgala*) that flows into the soul (*Asrava*), binds it (*Bandha*), can be stopped (*Samvara*), and burned off through ascetic mortification (*Nirjara*), leading to final liberation (**Moksha / Kaivalya**).
33. **Anekantavada (Metaphysical Multi-sidedness)**: The core Jain philosophical doctrine that reality is infinitely complex and multi-faceted; no single human statement can express the absolute, complete truth of an object.
34. **Syadvada (Epistemological Relativism)**: The logical method of expressing Anekantavada through seven-fold conditional predication (**Saptabhangi Naya**), prefixing every judgment with *"Syat"* ("in some respect / conditionally"):
    - 1. *Syad-asti* (In some respect, it is).
    - 2. *Syad-nasti* (In some respect, it is not).
    - 3. *Syad-asti-nasti* (In some respect, it is and it is not).
    - 4. *Syad-avaktavyam* (In some respect, it is inexpressible).
    - 5. *Syad-asti-avaktavyam* (In some respect, it is and is inexpressible).
    - 6. *Syad-nasti-avaktavyam* (In some respect, it is not and is inexpressible).
    - 7. *Syad-asti-nasti-avaktavyam* (In some respect, it is, is not, and is inexpressible).
35. **Triratna (The Three Jewels of Jainism)**:
    - **Samyag Darshana** (Right Faith / Perception)
    - **Samyag Jnana** (Right Knowledge)
    - **Samyag Charitra** (Right Conduct)
36. **First Jain Council (c. 300 BCE)**: Convened at **Pataliputra** under the leadership of **Sthulabhadra** during the reign of Chandragupta Maurya; compiled the **12 Angas**; confirmed the initial division into:
    - **Svetambara** ("White-clad"): Led by Sthulabhadra; stayed in Magadha during the 12-year famine; wore simple white robes; believed women could achieve liberation (*Moksha*) as women; accepted 19th Tirthankara Mallinatha as female.
    - **Digambara** ("Sky-clad"): Led by **Bhadrabahu**; migrated south to **Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)** with Chandragupta Maurya during the famine; strictly practiced complete nudity; maintained women must be reborn as men to achieve Moksha; rejected the Pataliputra compilation of Angas.
37. **Second Jain Council (512 CE)**: Convened at **Vallabhi in Gujarat** under the presidency of **Devardhi Kshamasramana**; finally committed the canonical **Jain Agamas** (12 Angas, 12 Upangas, 10 Prakirnakas, 6 Chhedasutras, 4 Mulasutras) into written form in the **Ardhamagadhi Prakrit** language.
38. **Sallekhana / Santhara**: The voluntary religious fast unto death undertaken by Jain ascetics and devout householders when life is nearing its natural end, celebrated as the highest test of detachment from the physical body. **Chandragupta Maurya** died by Sallekhana at Shravanabelagola (~298 BCE).
39. **Gommateshwara (Bahubali) Colossus**: The world's largest monolithic free-standing stone statue (57 ft high), carved out of grey granite atop Vindhyagiri hill at **Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)** in **981 CE** by **Chavundaraya**, commander of the Western Ganga king Rachamalla. Revered for the decennial **Mahamastakabhisheka** festival.
40. **Ajivika School**: Founded by **Makkhali Gosala** (former companion of Mahavira who broke away); propounded radical, absolute **Determinism / Fatalism (*Niyati-vada*)**: human effort, free will, and karma are completely powerless; all events, joy, pain, and rebirth are strictly predetermined by cosmic fate (*Niyati*).
41. **Barabar and Nagarjuni Caves (Jehanabad, Bihar)**: Four rock-cut granite cave chambers (Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Karan Chaupar, Vishwamitra) excavated and **gifted by Emperor Ashoka and his grandson Dasharatha to the AJIVIKA monks** (NOT to Buddhists!).
42. **Charvaka / Lokayata (Materialist Philosophy)**: Traditional founder sage **Brihaspati** (popularized by Charvaka); radical **Materialism**: rejects God, soul, rebirth, karma, heaven, and hell; accepts **Perception (*Pratyaksha*) as the ONLY valid source of knowledge (*Pramana*)**; matter consists of 4 physical elements (earth, water, fire, air; rejects ether/akasha); famous maxim: *"While life remains, let a man live happily, let him feed on ghee even though he runs into debt, for once the body is reduced to ash, how can it ever return?"*
43. **Buddhism vs Jainism Core Contrasts**:
    - **Soul**: Buddhism rejects an eternal soul (**Anatta**); Jainism affirms eternal, conscious, individual souls (**Jiva**) in all living and non-living matter.
    - **Path**: Buddhism prescribes the **Middle Path (*Madhyama Pratipada*)**; Jainism prescribes **rigorous, extreme asceticism** and bodily mortification.
    - **Ahimsa**: Jainism practices **absolute, uncompromising Ahimsa**; Buddhism prohibits killing but monks may consume meat offered in alms if not slaughtered specifically for them.
    - **Language of Early Scriptures**: Early Buddhism used **Pali**; Early Jainism used **Ardhamagadhi Prakrit**.
44. **Royal Patrons of Buddhism**: Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Prasenjit, Ashoka, Kanishka I, Harshavardhana, and the Pala dynasty of Bengal and Bihar (Dharmapala founded Vikramashila).
45. **Royal Patrons of Jainism**: Bimbisara & Ajatashatru (early phase), Chandragupta Maurya, **Kharavela of Kalinga** (Hathigumpha inscription begins with Namokar Mantra), Western Gangas, Rashtrakuta kings (Amoghavarsha I wrote *Kavirajamarga*), Chalukyas of Gujarat (Kumarapala converted by Acharya Hemachandra).
46. **Ashoka’s Dhamma**: Ashoka’s personal religion was Buddhism (declared in Bhabru edict), but the **Dhamma** inscribed in his rock edicts was **not sectarian Buddhism**; it was an inclusive civic and moral ethical code based on filial piety, respect for Brahmanas and Shramanas, non-slaughter of animals, and universal religious tolerance (Major Rock Edict XII).
47. **Dilwara Jain Temples (Mount Abu, Rajasthan)**: Spectacular white marble temple complex renowned for intricate ceiling carvings; built by ministers of the Solanki / Chaulukya rulers of Gujarat:
    - **Vimal Vasahi** (dedicated to Rishabhanatha): Built by minister **Vimal Shah** in **1031 CE**.
    - **Luna Vasahi** (dedicated to Neminatha): Built by brothers **Tejpala and Vastupala** in **1230 CE**.
48. **First Council Compilation Details**: Sutta Pitaka divided into 5 Nikayas; Vinaya Pitaka into Suttavibhanga, Khandhaka, and Parivara. First Council took place in the year of Buddha’s death (483 BCE).
49. **Jamali**: The first disciple and later the first schismatic rebel (*Bahurata-vada*) of Vardhamana Mahavira was his own son-in-law **Jamali** (husband of Anojja/Priyadarsana).
50. **Samannaphala Sutta**: The celebrated Buddhist text in the *Digha Nikaya* where king **Ajatashatru** visits Gautama Buddha in the mango grove of physician Jivaka at Rajagriha to inquire about the immediate fruits of the contemplative life, presenting a critique of the six contemporary heretical teachers (Purana Kassapa, Makkhali Gosala, Ajita Kesakambali, Pakudha Kaccayana, Sanjaya Belatthiputta, and Nigantha Nataputta/Mahavira).
51. **Gautama Buddha Life Milestones (Sequence & Places):** Born in **563 BCE at Lumbini** (near Kapilavastu, Nepal) to King Shuddhodana (Shakya clan) and Queen Mahamaya (died 7 days after birth; raised by stepmother Gautami); original name **Siddhartha**; married Yashodhara, had son **Rahula**; Four Great Sights: Old man, Sick man, Dead body, and Ascetic; **Mahabhinishkramana** (Great Renunciation) at age 29 on horse Kanthaka accompanied by charioteer Channa.
52. **Enlightenment & Preaching:** Attained Enlightenment (**Nirvana / Sambodhi**) at age 35 under a Peepal tree (Bodhi tree) on the banks of **River Niranjana (Phalgu)** at **Bodh Gaya**; first sermon preached at Deer Park in **Sarnath (Rishipattana)** to 5 former ascetic companions, an event called **Dharmachakra Pravartana** ('Turning the Wheel of Law'); preached primarily in **Pali**; attained **Mahaparinirvana** at age 80 (483 BCE) at **Kushinagar** (capital of Mallas) under a Sal tree; last words: *'Decay is inherent in all component things; work out your salvation with diligence'*; last disciple converted was **Subhadda**.
53. **Four Buddhist Councils (Chronology & Patrons):** (1) **1st Council (483 BCE):** At **Sattapanni Cave, Rajgriha**; Patron: **Ajatashatru** (Haryanka); President: **Mahakassapa**; compiled Buddhist canon into **Vinaya Pitaka** (rules of monastic discipline, recited by Upali) and **Sutta Pitaka** (sermons and philosophical doctrines, recited by Ananda); (2) **2nd Council (383 BCE):** At **Vaishali**; Patron: **Kalasoka** (Shishunaga); President: **Sabbakami**; first schism between orthodox **Sthaviravadins** (Theravada) and liberal **Mahasanghikas**; (3) **3rd Council (250 BCE):** At **Pataliputra**; Patron: **Emperor Ashoka** (Maurya); President: **Moggaliputta Tissa**; compiled **Abhidhamma Pitaka** (philosophical analysis) and book *Kathavatthu*; decided to send missionary monks abroad (Mahendra & Sanghamitra to Sri Lanka); (4) **4th Council (72 CE):** At **Kundalvana, Kashmir**; Patron: **Kanishka** (Kushan); President: **Vasumitra**, Vice-President: **Ashvaghosha**; final division of Buddhism into **Hinayana** ('Lesser Vehicle' - orthodox, Pali, monastic) and **Mahayana** ('Greater Vehicle' - liberal, Sanskrit, Bodhisattva worship, Buddha as divine deity).
54. **Three Jewels (Triratna) & Four Noble Truths (Arya Satya):** Triratna: **Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha**; Four Noble Truths: (1) *Dukkha* (World is full of suffering); (2) *Samudaya* (Desire/*Tanha* is the cause of suffering); (3) *Nirodha* (Suffering can be ended by extinguishing desire); (4) *Magga* (The path to end suffering is the **Eightfold Path / Ashtangika Marga**).
55. **Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga - Middle Path):** Right View (*Samyak Drishti*), Right Resolve (*Samyak Sankalpa*), Right Speech (*Samyak Vak*), Right Action (*Samyak Karmanta*), Right Livelihood (*Samyak Ajiva*), Right Effort (*Samyak Vyayama*), Right Mindfulness (*Samyak Smriti*), and Right Samadhi (*Samyak Samadhi*); avoids extremes of severe self-mortification and sensual indulgence.
56. **Vardhamana Mahavira Life & Lineage:** 24th and last Tirthankara of Jainism; born in **540 BCE at Kundagrama** (near Vaishali) into the Jnatrika Kshatriya clan; father Siddhartha, mother **Trishala** (Lichchhavi princess, sister of King Chetaka); married Yashoda, had daughter Anojja (Priyadarsana); renounced household life at age 30 after parents' death; wandered 12 years as an ascetic.
57. **Mahavira Enlightenment & Kaivalya:** Attained supreme omniscience (**Kaivalya**) at age 42 under a Sal tree on the banks of **River Rijupalika** near **Jrimbhikagrama**; became known as **Jina** ('Conqueror' of senses), **Kevalin**, **Mahavira**, and **Nirgrantha** ('Free from all bonds'); preached first sermon at Vipulachala hill in **Rajgriha**; passed away / attained Nirvana at age 72 (468 BCE) at **Pavapuri** (near Rajgir, Bihar) in the palace of King Hastipal of Mallas.
58. **24 Tirthankaras & Symbols (Exam Key):** (1) **Rishabhanatha / Adinatha:** First Tirthankara (Symbol: **Bull**); born at Ayodhya; mentioned in Rigveda and Bhagavata Purana; (2) Ajitanatha (Elephant); (22) **Neminatha / Arishtanemi:** (Conch shell); associated with Krishna; (23) **Parshvanatha:** (Serpent hood); Prince of Varanasi; lived 250 years before Mahavira; established four vows: Ahimsa (Non-violence), Satya (Truth), Asteya (Non-stealing), and Aparigraha (Non-possession); (24) **Mahavira:** (Symbol: **Lion**); added the fifth vow: **Brahmacharya** (Celibacy).
59. **Two Jain Councils (Sangeeti):** (1) **1st Jain Council (300 BCE):** Held at **Pataliputra**; Presided by **Sthulabhadra** under patronage of Chandragupta Maurya; compiled 12 Angas; formal split into **Digambaras** ('Sky-clad' / naked, led by Bhadrabahu who migrated to Shravanabelagola, Karnataka during famine) and **Svetambaras** ('White-clad', led by Sthulabhadra who stayed in Magadha); (2) **2nd Jain Council (512 CE):** Held at **Vallabhi (Gujarat)**; Presided by **Devardhi Kshamasramana**; finalized and committed all Jain Agamas and canonical texts to writing in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit.
60. **Jain Epistemology & Doctrines:** (1) **Anekantavada:** Doctrine of the manifold nature of reality / pluralism; truth has infinite aspects; (2) **Syadvada:** Doctrine of conditional predication (Seven-fold judgment - *Sapta-bhangi naya*): 'May be it is', 'May be it is not', etc.; (3) Strict **Ahimsa** carried to extreme levels (filtering drinking water, sweeping ground before walking, wearing mouth cloth); rejection of Vedic authority and animal sacrifices; belief that even inanimate objects possess souls (*Jiva*).
