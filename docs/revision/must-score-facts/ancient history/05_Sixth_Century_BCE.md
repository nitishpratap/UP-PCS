## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The sixth–fourth centuries BCE mark the **Second Urbanisation** of India, characterized by widespread **iron tools**, **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)**, and **silver punch-marked coins (*Aahat / Karshapana*)**.
2. The Buddhist text **Anguttara Nikaya** (part of *Sutta Pitaka*) and the *Mahavastu* provide the canonical list of the **Sixteen Mahajanapadas (सोलह महाजनपद)** existing in the age of Gautama Buddha.
3. The Jain text **Bhagavati Sutra (Vyakhyaprajnapti)** provides an alternative list of 16 states, but the Buddhist *Anguttara Nikaya* list is the standard historical authority for competitive exams.
4. Among the 16 Mahajanapadas, **fourteen were monarchical kingdoms (*Rajyas*)** and **two were republican oligarchies (*Gana-sanghas*)**: the **Vajji** confederacy and the **Malla** republic.
5. **Asmaka (Assaka)** was the **ONLY Mahajanapada situated south of the Vindhya mountains** in Dakshinapatha, located on the banks of river **Godavari** with its capital at **Potana (or Potali)**.
6. **Magadha** was the most powerful Mahajanapada. Its initial capital was **Girivraja / Rajagriha** (enclosed by five protective hills: Vaibhara, Vipula, Ratna, Giddhakuta, and Udaya), later shifted to **Pataliputra** by king **Udayin**.
7. Four geopolitical factors enabled Magadha's supremacy:
   - Rich deposits of **high-grade iron ore** in Rajgir, Gaya, and adjacent Chhotanagpur hills (for weapons and agricultural axes).
   - Abundance of **wild elephants** in eastern forests (first Indian power to deploy war elephants on a massive scale).
   - Unsurpassed **fluvial highway network** (confluence of Ganga, Son, Gandak, and Ghaghra) providing commercial transport and military mobility.
   - Exceptionally fertile Gangetic alluvium yielding heavy agricultural surplus.
8. **Anga** (capital **Champa**, ancient *Malini*, modern Bhagalpur and Munger in Bihar) was a major riverine and oceanic trading entrepôt; annexed to Magadha by king **Bimbisara**.
9. **Kasi** (capital **Varanasi**, situated between rivers Varuna and Asi) was celebrated for fine cotton textiles, silk (*Kasika*), and perfumes; annexed to Kosala and later incorporated into Magadha.
10. **Vatsa** (capital **Kausambi**, situated on the Yamuna in modern Prayagraj/Kaushambi, UP) was ruled by the famous romantic hero king **Udayana** (subject of Bhasa’s play *Swapnavasavadattam*); Kausambi features massive burnt-brick fortifications.
11. **Kosala** (capital **Sravasti** in the north, **Saketa / Ayodhya** in the south) was ruled by king **Prasenjit** (contemporary and friend of Buddha); Buddha spent the maximum number of rainy-season retreats (**25 Vassavasas**) in the Jetavana monastery at Sravasti.
12. **Avanti** (central Malwa) was divided into northern Avanti with capital **Ujjayini** and southern Avanti with capital **Mahishmati** (separated by the Vetravati river); ruled by the fierce warrior king **Pradyota (Mahasena)**.
13. King Bimbisara of Magadha maintained cordial diplomatic relations with Avanti and sent his celebrated royal physician **Jivaka** to Ujjayini to cure king Pradyota of acute jaundice.
14. **Saurasena** (capital **Mathura** on the Yamuna) was ruled by king Avantiputra; Megasthenes recorded that the Sourasenoi people worshipped *Herakles* (identified with Vasudeva Krishna).
15. **Panchala** was divided by the Ganga into Northern Panchala (capital **Ahichchhatra** in Bareilly district, UP) and Southern Panchala (capital **Kampilya** in Farrukhabad district, UP).
16. **Kuru** (capital **Indraprastha** near modern Delhi and **Hastinapur** in Meerut); according to Puranic tradition, after Hastinapur was destroyed by massive Ganga floods, Kuru king **Nichakshu** shifted his capital to **Kausambi**.
17. **Matsya** (capital **Viratnagar / Bairat** in Jaipur/Alwar, Rajasthan) was the traditional hiding place of the Pandavas during their 13th year of exile (*Agyatavasa*).
18. **Chedi** (capital **Suktimati / Sotthivati** in Bundelkhand, UP/MP) was ruled in epic tradition by Shishupala; later a branch of Chedis founded the Mahameghavahana dynasty in Kalinga (King Kharavela).
19. **Gandhara** (capital **Taxila / Takshashila** and Pushkalavati in northwestern Pakistan) was renowned for its world-famous ancient university and trade route; ruled by king **Pushkarasarin** who sent an embassy to Bimbisara.
20. **Kamboja** (capital **Rajpur / Hataka** in Hazara/Rajouri) was celebrated across the ancient world for its superior breed of war horses; described by Kautilya as *Varta-Shastropajivi* (guild-state living by agriculture, trade, and arms).
21. **Vajji** was a powerful republican confederacy (*Gana-sangha*) of **eight clans (*Ashtakula*)**, the most prominent being the **Lichchhavis** of **Vaishali**, the **Videhas** of Mithila, and the **Jnatrikas** of Kundagrama.
22. **Vaishali** is celebrated as the **world's first republic**; governed by an assembly of 7,707 *Rajas* who met in the Santhagara (parliament hall).
23. **Malla** was a republican confederacy divided into two autonomous branches: one with capital at **Kushinagar** (where Gautama Buddha attained Mahaparinirvana) and the other at **Pawa / Pavapuri** (where Vardhamana Mahavira attained Nirvana).
24. **Other Famous Gana-Sanghas (Republics)** of 6th c. BCE:
    - **Shakyas** of Kapilavastu (clan of Buddha).
    - **Koliyas** of Ramagrama.
    - **Moriyas** of Pipphalivana (ancestral house of Chandragupta Maurya).
    - **Bhaggas** of Sumsumara Hill.
    - **Bulies** of Allakappa.
    - **Kalamas** of Kesaputta (clan of Alara Kalama).
25. **Haryanka Dynasty (c. 544–412 BCE)**: Founded by **Bimbisara** (Shrenika); consolidated his empire through matrimonial alliances:
    - Married **Kosaladevi** (sister of Prasenjit of Kosala), receiving Kasi as dowry (annual revenue of 100,000 coins).
    - Married **Chellana** (daughter of Lichchhavi chief Chetaka of Vaishali), mother of Ajatashatru.
    - Married **Khema** (daughter of king of Madra clan in central Punjab).
26. **Ajatashatru (Kunika, c. 492–460 BCE)**: Imprisoned and starved his father Bimbisara to death; pursued an aggressive policy of military expansion; waged a 16-year war against the Vajji confederacy, successfully annexing Vaishali.
27. In the war against Vaishali, Ajatashatru introduced two revolutionary military siege weapons:
    - **Rathamusala**: An armored chariot equipped with rotating scythes/blades that wreaked havoc on enemy infantry.
    - **Mahashilakantaka**: A giant war catapult designed to hurl massive boulders into enemy fortifications.
28. Ajatashatru built the military watch-fort of **Pataligrama** at the confluence of Ganga and Son, which later evolved into imperial Pataliputra. He convened the **First Buddhist Council** at Rajagriha in 483 BCE.
29. **Udayin (c. 460–444 BCE)**: Son of Ajatashatru; **officially transferred the capital of Magadha from Rajagriha to Pataliputra** due to its central strategic and commercial location.
30. **Shishunaga Dynasty (c. 412–344 BCE)**: Founded by minister **Shishunaga** who overthrew the patricidal Haryanka line; achieved the historic triumph of **annexing Avanti (Malwa)**, terminating the 100-year rivalry between Magadha and Ujjayini.
31. **Kalashoka (Kakavarna)**: Son of Shishunaga; permanently fixed Pataliputra as the sole capital and convened the **Second Buddhist Council** at Vaishali in 383 BCE.
32. **Nanda Dynasty (c. 344–321 BCE)**: Founded by **Mahapadma Nanda** through the overthrow of the Shishunagas; hailed by Puranas as **"Sarvakshatrantaka"** (destroyer of all Kshatriyas) and **"Ekarat"** (sole sovereign emperor).
33. Mahapadma Nanda conquered Kalinga; the **Hathigumpha inscription** of Kharavela records that a Nanda king dug an irrigation canal from the Tansuli river to Kalinga and took away a sacred Jina image (*Jinasana*) as a war trophy to Magadha.
34. Last Nanda ruler was **Dhana Nanda** (called *Agrammes* or *Xandrames* by Greek historians); commanded an immense standing army of 200,000 infantry, 20,000 cavalry, 2,000 four-horse chariots, and 3,000 war elephants; his massive military deterred the war-weary soldiers of **Alexander the Great** from crossing the Beas (Hyphasis) river in 326 BCE.
35. Dhana Nanda was overthrown around **321 BCE** by **Chandragupta Maurya**, guided by the political genius of **Chanakya (Kautilya / Vishnugupta)**.
36. **Urban Centers & Terminology**:
    - **Pura / Nagara**: Fortified urban market town.
    - **Mahanagara**: Great metropolitan capitals (6 great cities in Buddha's time: Champa, Rajagriha, Sravasti, Saketa, Kausambi, Varanasi).
    - **Nigama**: Commercial market town inhabited by merchants.
    - **Gramaka**: Rural agricultural settlement.
37. **Santhagara**: The general assembly hall in republican Gana-sanghas where voting was conducted using colored wooden voting tickets called **Salakas**, distributed by an officer named **Salaka-gahapaka**.
38. **Social Mobility & Shreshthins**: The 6th century BCE witnessed the rise of wealthy merchant bankers (**Shreshthins**) and merchant guilds (**Shrenis**), who heavily patronized the new heterodox movements (Buddhism and Jainism) which opposed Vedic ritual animal sacrifice and usury condemnation.
39. **Gahapati**: Wealthy landowner, peasant proprietor, and head of a rural household who paid agricultural taxes to the state.
40. **Coinage Revolution**: The 6th century BCE marked the introduction of metallic currency in India — **Punch-Marked Coins (*Aahat Mudra / Karshapana / Pana*)**, made primarily of silver and copper, stamped with distinct symbols (sun, six-arm wheel, hill, tree, bull, elephant) without any royal names or inscriptions.
41. **Eight of the 16 Mahajanapadas** were located in the territory of modern **Uttar Pradesh**: **Kasi, Kosala, Vatsa, Malla, Chedi, Panchala, Saurasena, and Kuru**.
42. **Three Mahajanapadas** were located in modern **Bihar**: **Magadha, Anga, and Vajji**.
43. **One Mahajanapada** was located in **Rajasthan**: **Matsya**.
44. **One Mahajanapada** was located in **Madhya Pradesh**: **Avanti**.
45. **Two Mahajanapadas** were located in the **North-Western Frontier (Pakistan/Afghanistan)**: **Gandhara and Kamboja**.
46. **One Mahajanapada** was located in **South India (Maharashtra/Telangana)**: **Asmaka**.
47. **Sutta Pitaka 5 Nikayas**:
    - **Digha Nikaya**: 34 long discourses (includes *Brahmajala Sutta* on 62 philosophical views; *Samannaphala Sutta* on benefits of monkhood; *Mahaparinibbana Sutta* on Buddha's final days).
    - **Majjhima Nikaya**: 152 middle-length discourses.
    - **Samyutta Nikaya**: Themed connected discourses (includes *Dharmachakrapravartana Sutta*).
    - **Anguttara Nikaya**: Numerical grouped discourses (grouped by units of 1 to 11; contains list of 16 Mahajanapadas).
    - **Khuddaka Nikaya**: 15 miscellaneous short texts (includes *Dhammapada*, *Udana*, *Itivuttaka*, *Sutta Nipata*, *Theragatha*, *Therigatha*, and 547 *Jataka* birth stories).
48. **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)**: The diagnostic ceramic hallmark of the Second Urbanization; glossy, mirror-like black surface; manufactured from fine alluvial clay fired at high temperature under reducing conditions; marks the transition from regional chiefdoms to pan-Indian empires.
49. **Bimbisara vs Bindusara Trap**: **Bimbisara** is the founder of the **Haryanka dynasty** in 6th c. BCE (contemporary of Buddha); **Bindusara** is the son of Chandragupta Maurya and father of Emperor Ashoka in 3rd c. BCE.
50. **Ajatashatru’s Alliances and Wars**: Fought his maternal uncle Prasenjit of Kosala over Kasi revenue; peace was established when Prasenjit married his daughter **Vajira** to Ajatashatru, formalizing Magadha's permanent possession of Kasi.

### 3. Monarchies vs Republics (Gana-Sanghas) in 6th c. BCE

| Category | Characteristic Features | Prime Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Monarchical States (*Rajyas*)** | Single hereditary king (*Rajan*); standing army paid from treasury; centralized taxation; Brahmanical court purohita | Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti, Anga, Gandhara |

   - **Order:** **H**aryanka $

ightarrow$ **M**aurya.
2. **The King Names Confusion Anchor:**
   - **Bim-bi-sara (Haryanka)** = **B**efore (6th c. BCE, contemporary of **B**uddha).
   - **Bin-du-sara (Maurya)** = **D**own in time (3rd c. BCE, son of Chandragupta, father of Ashoka).

   - **Uday-in (Magadha)** = Built **in** Pataliputra (Pataliputra founder).
   - **Uday-an (Vatsa)** = **A**ctor / Romantic King of Kausambi (*Swapnavasavadattam*).

   - **V**atsa (Udayana)
