## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. **Hathnora** on the Narmada in Madhya Pradesh yielded a ***Homo erectus*** skull, found by **Arun Sonakia** in **1982**. It is not a modern *Homo sapiens* fossil.
2. **Attirampakkam** in the Kortallayar valley of Tamil Nadu has Acheulian tools about **1.5 million years** old. It is a **tool** site, not a hominin fossil site.
3. **Robert Bruce Foote** recognised the first Indian Paleolithic tools at **Pallavaram** (Tamil Nadu) in **1863**. He is called the Father of Indian Prehistory.
4. **Bagor** on the Kothari in Rajasthan is the Mesolithic **animal-domestication** key. **V.N. Misra** excavated it. It is not a Paleolithic shrine.
5. **Sarai Nahar Rai** (सराय नाहर राय), **Mahadaha**, and **Damdama** in Pratapgarh, Uttar Pradesh, are Mesolithic **burial** sites of the Ganga plain. Domestication is Bagor; burials are Pratapgarh.
6. **Non-geometric** microliths (backed blades) belong to the earlier Mesolithic. **Geometric** microliths (lunates, triangles, trapezes) belong to the later Mesolithic.
7. **Mehrgarh** (मेहरगढ़) in Balochistan is the earliest farming village of the subcontinent, around **7000 BCE**. First crops are **wheat and barley**. Period I is **aceramic** (farming without pottery).
8. **Koldihwa** and **Mahagara** in the Belan valley of Mirzapur–Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh, are the early **rice** pair. **G.R. Sharma** excavated them.
9. **Mahagara** alone has the **cattle pen with hoof marks**. **Koldihwa** is known for **rice-husk impressions** in pottery. Keep the two sister facts separate.
10. **Lahuradewa** in **Sant Kabir Nagar**, Uttar Pradesh, has early Ganga-plain **rice** at a lake edge. It is **not** a Belan valley site.
11. **Burzahom** (बुरज़होम) near Srinagar has Neolithic **pit dwellings** about 2–4 m deep and a famous **dog burial**. **Gufkral** in Pulwama means “cave of the potter.”
12. **Chirand** in Saran, Bihar, is the Gangetic Neolithic site famous for **bone and antler tools**. It has no Burzahom-style pit dwellings and no South Indian ash mounds.
13. **Bhimbetka** (भीमबेटका) in Raisen, Madhya Pradesh, was first recognised by **V.S. Wakankar** in **1957–58**. UNESCO listed it in **2003**. There are about **750+** shelters and **500+** painted ones.
14. **Baghor** in the Son valley of Madhya Pradesh is an Upper Paleolithic stone **shrine**. **Bagor** in Rajasthan is Mesolithic **domestication**. Never swap the spellings.
15. **Inamgaon** in Maharashtra is the **Jorwe** type site. **Navdatoli** on the Narmada in Madhya Pradesh is **Malwa** culture, excavated by **H.D. Sankalia**.
16. **OCP** (Ochre Coloured Pottery) belongs to the western UP–Haryana–Rajasthan **doab**. **Hastinapur** (हस्तिनापुर) is a classic OCP site. It is not mature Harappan urban ware.
17. **Megaliths** are mainly **Iron Age** burial monuments of the Deccan and south (dolmens, cists, stone circles). They are not Stone Age Chalcolithic villages. Classic names include **Brahmagiri**, **Adichanallur**, **Maski** (मास्की), and **Hallur**.
18. **V. Gordon Childe** coined the phrase **Neolithic Revolution** for the shift from food collection to food production. The change in South Asia was staggered by region, not one national year.
19. **H.D. Sankalia** excavated **Langhnaj** (Gujarat Mesolithic) and **Navdatoli**. He did **not** discover Bhimbetka. **Madho Swaroop Vats** is a Harappan archaeologist and a Bhimbetka distractor.
20. **Chopani Mando** in the Belan valley of Uttar Pradesh shows Paleolithic–Mesolithic–Neolithic **continuity** and wild-rice gathering before full Belan farming.
21. **Prehistoric** = no writing. **Proto-historic** = undeciphered script (Indus / Copper Age). **Historic** North India is often dated from about the **6th century BCE**.
22. **Damdama** has a **triple** burial; **Sarai Nahar Rai** has a **four-skeleton** grave. Bone implements cluster at **Mahadaha** and **Sarai Nahar Rai**.
23. **Lahuradewa** (Sant Kabir Nagar) is the booklet key for earliest subcontinental **agriculture / rice** when listed; otherwise **Mehrgarh** is the wheat–barley settled-life key.
24. **OCP** was christened by **B.B. Lal** at **Hastinapur**. **Jorwe** adults lie **north–south** under house floors; children often rest in **double urns**.
25. **Ganeshwar–Jodhpura** in Rajasthan is a copper-working culture that supplied metal toward the Harappan zone. **Balathal** shows an early fortification-like wall in the Ahar belt.
26. **Patne** in Maharashtra is Upper Paleolithic and is known for **ostrich eggshell** beads. **Kurnool caves** (Andhra Pradesh) yield Upper Paleolithic **bone** tools.
27. **Hunsgi** and **Isampur** in Karnataka are Acheulian factory / quarry sites. **Gudiyam** in Tamil Nadu is a Paleolithic rock-shelter / cave site.
28. **Ahar–Banas** (Mewar, Rajasthan, about **2100–1500 BCE**) is **contemporary with the Harappan** civilisation, knew **rice**, and used white-painted **black-and-red ware**. The stem “rice was unknown to them” is false.
29. **Adamgarh** near Hoshangabad, Madhya Pradesh, shows a Paleolithic-to-Mesolithic sequence on the Narmada and early domestication claims. **Sivapithecus** Siwalik fossils are **not** *Homo* evidence against Hathnora.
30. **Hathnora** (Narmada Man) lies in the **Narmada valley of Madhya Pradesh**.
31. **Arun Sonakia** of the GSI discovered it in **1982**.
32. **Attirampakkam** lies in the **Kortallayar valley of Tamil Nadu**, near Chennai.
33. **Shanti Pappu’s** team dated the tools.
34. **Pallavaram** is in the Chennai belt of Tamil Nadu.
35. **Robert Bruce Foote** recognised Paleolithic tools there in **1863**.

**Q1.**

Which of the following Indian Archaeologists first visited 'Bhimbetka Caves' and discovered prehistoric significance of its rock paintings?

A. Madho Swaroop Vatsa
B. H.D. Sankalia
C. V.S. Wakankar
D. V.N. Mishra

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Correct Answer:** **C** (V.S. Wakankar)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Archaeologist Profile:** Dr. **Vishnu Shridhar Wakankar** (1919–1988), an eminent Indian archaeologist and art historian from Vikram University, discovered the Bhimbetka rock shelters in **1957–1958**. He was honored with the Padma Shri in 1975 for his groundbreaking prehistoric discoveries.
- **Site Geography:** Bhimbetka is located in the Vindhyan sandstone ranges of Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh (specifically near Abdullahganj, ~45 km south of Bhopal). It exhibits unbroken cultural stratigraphy from the Lower Paleolithic Acheulian horizon to the medieval period.
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - **Madho Swaroop Vatsa:** Associated with Harappan archaeology (excavated Harappa 1926–1934; author of *Excavations at Harappa*).
  - **H.D. Sankalia:** Led Deccan College excavations across Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Madhya Pradesh (Navdatoli, Nevasa, Langhnaj).
  - **V.N. Mishra:** Collaborated in later comprehensive surveys and excavations of Bhimbetka's rock-shelter III F-23 in the 1970s.

**Key Exam Takeaway / Trap:**
- Remember the exact discovery anchor: **Dr. V.S. Wakankar, 1957–58, Raisen district, MP**. UNESCO inscribed Bhimbetka on the World Heritage List in **2003**.
</details>

**Q2.**

Which among the following sites provides evidence of domestication of animals in the Mesolithic period?

A. Odai
B. Bori
C. Bagor
D. Lakhnia

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Correct Answer:** **C** (Bagor)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Evidence of Pastoralism:** In the Indian subcontinent, unequivocal excavated evidence of animal domestication begins in the **Mesolithic period** (Late Stone Age). 
- **Site Specifics:** **Bagor**, located on the Kothari river in Bhilwara district (Rajasthan), was excavated by **V.N. Misra**. Phase I yielded geometric microliths alongside faunal remains showing sheep, goat, and cattle domestication dated to c. 5000–4500 BCE.
- **Central Indian Counterpart:** **Adamgarh** near Hoshangabad (Narmadapuram, MP), excavated by **R.V. Joshi**, yielded a parallel Mesolithic assemblage containing domestic dog, cattle, buffalo, sheep, and goat remains.
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - **Odai:** Minor lithic scatter in Tamil Nadu.
  - **Bori:** Lower Paleolithic Acheulian handaxe site in Maharashtra (~1.4 Ma).
  - **Lakhnia:** Vindhyan rock shelter in Mirzapur, UP (famed for rock paintings and burials).

**Key Exam Takeaway / Trap:**
- UPPCS pairing formula: Mesolithic animal domestication = **Bagor (Rajasthan) + Adamgarh (MP)**. Always eliminate Paleolithic (Bori, Attirampakkam) and pure burial sites (Sarai Nahar Rai, Damdama).
</details>

**Q3.** With reference to early humans in India, which of the following statements is/are correct?

1. Hathnora on the Narmada yielded a Homo sapiens cranium found by Robert Bruce Foote in 1863.
2. Attirampakkam in Tamil Nadu is famous as a Homo sapiens fossil site of the same horizon.
3. Robert Bruce Foote identified Paleolithic tools at Pallavaram in 1863.

A. 1 and 2

B. Only 3

C. 2 and 3

D. 1 and 3

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Correct Answer:** **B** (Only 3)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Statement 1 is INCORRECT:** While the fossil cranium at **Hathnora** on the Narmada River was indeed discovered in India, it was discovered by **Dr. Arun Sonakia of the Geological Survey of India on December 5, 1982**, NOT by Robert Bruce Foote in 1863. Furthermore, physical anthropologists classify the fossil cranium as an archaic hominin (**Homo erectus narmadensis** / archaic *Homo sapiens*), not anatomically modern *Homo sapiens*.
- **Statement 2 is INCORRECT:** **Attirampakkam** in the Kortallayar basin (Tamil Nadu) is an internationally celebrated Lower Paleolithic **Acheulian stone tool site** (excavated by Robert Bruce Foote in 1863, and recently by Shanti Pappu revealing Acheulian tools dating to ~1.5 million years ago). It has yielded rich stone tools, animal footprints, and faunal fossils, but **no Homo sapiens fossil cranium**.
- **Statement 3 is CORRECT:** **Robert Bruce Foote** discovered India's first recognized Paleolithic stone implement—an Acheulian quartzite bifacial handaxe—at **Pallavaram** near Chennai (Madras) on **May 30, 1863**. This historic discovery laid the foundation of prehistoric studies in India, earning Foote the title of "Father of Indian Prehistory."

**Key Exam Takeaway / Trap:**
- Disentangle the discoverers:
  - **Hathnora Hominin Fossil (1982)** = **Arun Sonakia** (Homo erectus, Narmada valley).
  - **Pallavaram Handaxe (1863)** = **Robert Bruce Foote** (First Paleolithic tool in India).
  - **Attirampakkam** = Acheulian tool site (NOT a Homo sapiens fossil site).
</details>

**Q4.** Consider the following statements about Paleolithic tool traditions:

1. Lower Paleolithic tools are typically handaxes and cleavers of the Acheulian tradition.
2. Middle Paleolithic tools are mainly handaxes and cleavers of the Acheulian tradition.
3. The Soanian industry of the Soan–Siwalik belt is mainly handaxe-cleaver based.

A. 1 and 2

B. 2 and 3

C. 1 and 3

D. Only 1

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Correct Answer:** **D** (Only 1)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Statement 1 is CORRECT:** The **Lower Paleolithic** period in peninsular and central India is predominantly characterized by the **Acheulian tool tradition**, named after Saint-Acheul in France. Diagnostic tools are bifacial **handaxes** (pear-shaped or oval core tools flaked on both sides) and **cleavers** (tools with a wide, transverse cutting edge), accompanied by choppers and polyhedrons.
- **Statement 2 is INCORRECT:** The **Middle Paleolithic** witnessed a major technological shift from core tools to **flake tools** (known in India as the **Nevasan tradition**, after Nevasa in Maharashtra). The characteristic tool types were **scrapers (side, convex, round, end), points, borers, and awls** made on flakes struck from prepared cores of fine-grained chert, jasper, and agate—NOT handaxes and cleavers.
- **Statement 3 is INCORRECT:** The **Soanian industry** (discovered in the Soan/Sohan river valley of the Potwar plateau, Pakistan, by De Terra and Paterson) is celebrated for its **pebble chopper and chopping tools**, NOT microliths! Microliths are minute composite blades characteristic of the much later **Mesolithic Age**.

**Key Exam Takeaway / Trap:**
- Tool traditions by phase:
  - **Lower Paleolithic:** Acheulian handaxes, cleavers & Soanian pebble choppers.
  - **Middle Paleolithic:** Nevasan flake tools (scrapers, points, borers).
  - **Upper Paleolithic:** Blades and burins.
  - **Mesolithic:** Microliths (1–8 cm composite tools).
</details>

**Q5.** Arrange the following cultural stages in chronological order (earliest first):

1. Neolithic
2. Paleolithic
3. Mesolithic
4. Chalcolithic

Select the correct answer from the code given below:

A. 2, 1, 3, 4

B. 3, 2, 1, 4

C. 2, 3, 1, 4

D. 2, 3, 4, 1

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Correct Answer:** **C** (2, 3, 1, 4)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Standard Prehistoric Chronological Progression:**
  - **2. Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age, ~2.5 Million BCE to c. 10,000 BCE):** Longest phase of human history, marked by hunting, food gathering, and chipped core/flake stone tools. Subdivided into Lower, Middle, and Upper Paleolithic.
  - **3. Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age, c. 10,000 BCE to c. 7000/6000 BCE):** Transitional Holocene phase characterized by hunting-foraging, beginnings of animal domestication (Bagor, Adamgarh), and microlithic composite tools.
  - **1. Neolithic Age (New Stone Age, c. 7000 BCE to c. 2500 BCE in NW / later elsewhere):** Marked by the "Neolithic Revolution"—food production, agriculture (wheat, barley, rice), animal husbandry, polished stone celts, pottery, and permanent villages.
  - **4. Chalcolithic Age (Stone-Copper Age, c. 2500/2100 BCE to c. 700 BCE):** Metal-using non-urban village cultures utilizing copper tools alongside stone tools (e.g., Ahar, Malwa, Jorwe cultures).
- **Correct Sequence:** 2 (Paleolithic) → 3 (Mesolithic) → 1 (Neolithic) → 4 (Chalcolithic).

**Key Exam Takeaway / Trap:**
- Universal prehistoric ladder: **Paleolithic → Mesolithic → Neolithic → Chalcolithic → Bronze Age (Harappan) → Iron Age**.
</details>

**Q7.** Which of the following pairs is/are correctly matched?

1. Copper plus stone villages — hallmark of mature Harappan citadel cities
2. Microlith — hallmark of the Mesolithic
3. Polished/ground stone — hallmark of the Neolithic

A. Only 3

B. Only 2 and 3

C. Only 1

D. Only 1 and 2

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Correct Answer:** **B** (Only 2 and 3)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Pair 1 is INCORRECT:** "Copper plus stone villages" describes the **Chalcolithic cultures** (e.g., Ahar, Kayatha, Malwa, Jorwe), which were fundamentally **rural, non-urban farming villages**. The Mature Harappan civilization was an **urban Bronze Age civilization** based on bronze metallurgy (copper-tin alloy), brick-built planned grid cities, sophisticated drainage systems, citadels, and overseas maritime trade.
- **Pair 2 is CORRECT:** **Microliths** (tiny flaked stone blades measuring 1 to 8 cm, retouched into geometric forms like lunates, triangles, and trapezes and hafted onto wooden or bone handles as sickles, arrows, and harpoons) are the definitive **hallmark of the Mesolithic Age**.
- **Pair 3 is CORRECT:** **Polished and ground stone tools** (particularly polished celts, adzes, chisels, and ring-stones with smooth grinding surfaces) represent the technological diagnostic **hallmark of the Neolithic Age**.

**Key Exam Takeaway / Trap:**
- Technological signatures to memorize:
  - Paleolithic = Chipped core and flake stone tools.
  - Mesolithic = Microliths.
  - Neolithic = Polished / ground stone tools.
  - Chalcolithic = Stone-copper rural farming villages.
  - Harappan = Bronze Age urban planned cities.
</details>

Select the correct answer from the code given below:

**Correct Answer:** **A** (Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A) / standard PCS key)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Assertion (A) is true:** **Bagor** in Bhilwara district, Rajasthan, is the benchmark and most extensively excavated Mesolithic site in India providing clear stratigraphical and faunal evidence of animal domestication (sheep, goat, cattle) dating to the 5th millennium BCE.
- **Reason (R) is true:** The landmark excavations at Bagor were directed by **Prof. V.N. Misra** of Deccan College, Pune, between 1968 and 1970 on the left bank of the Kothari River.

Thus, (R) is true, but it is not the logical explanation of (A).

1. Lahuradewa in Sant Kabir Nagar is a Belan valley sister site of Koldihwa.
2. Koldihwa is known for rice-husk impressions in pottery.

A. 2 and 3

C. 1 and 3

D. Only 2

**Correct Answer:** **A** (2 and 3)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Statement 1 is INCORRECT:** **Lahuradewa** is located in **Sant Kabir Nagar district** in the **Sarayupar plain (trans-Ghaghra plain)** of Eastern Uttar Pradesh, NOT in the Belan Valley. The Belan Valley sites (Koldihwa, Mahagara, Chopani Mando) are located in the southern part of Prayagraj (Allahabad) and Mirzapur districts, in the Vindhyan foothill drainage system of the Belan River.
- **Statement 2 is CORRECT:** **Koldihwa** (on the Belan River, Prayagraj) is celebrated in prehistoric literature for yielding early **rice-husk impressions** (*Oryza sativa*) embedded in the clay paste of coarse handmade pottery and carbonized rice grains.

**Key Exam Takeaway / Trap:**

**Q10.** Consider the following statements:

1. Mehrgarh Period I is aceramic Neolithic with wheat and barley farming.
2. South Indian Neolithic ash mounds (Piklihal, Utnur) are also typical of Burzahom.
3. V. Gordon Childe coined the phrase Neolithic Revolution.

A. 1 and 3

B. Only 1

C. Only 1 and 2

D. Only 3

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Correct Answer:** **A** (1 and 3)

**Detailed Explanation:**
- **Statement 1 is CORRECT:** **Mehrgarh Period I** (c. 7000–6000 BCE) represents an **aceramic (pre-pottery) Neolithic** culture. The inhabitants built multi-roomed mud-brick houses, harvested domesticated six-row barley and einkorn/emmer wheat using flint sickles, and domesticated cattle, sheep, and goats, but had not yet developed pottery (baskets coated with bitumen were used instead).
- **Statement 2 is INCORRECT:** **Ash mounds** (accumulated and burned cattle dung hills) are exclusively characteristic of the **Neolithic pastoral cultures of South India / Deccan** (e.g., Sanganakallu, Piklihal, Kupgal, Utnur, Budihal in Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh).
