## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. Following the collapse of the imperial Guptas around **550 CE**, northern India fragmented into several regional powers:
   - **Maukharis** of **Kannauj (Kanyakubja, UP)**.
   - **Pushyabhutis (Vardhanas)** of **Thanesar (Kurukshetra, Haryana)**.
   - **Maitrakas** of **Vallabhi (Saurashtra, Gujarat)**.
   - **Gaudas** of **Bengal** under king **Shashanka**.
   - **Later Guptas** of **Malwa and Magadha** under Deva Gupta.
2. The **Maukharis of Kannauj** were the first to make **Kannauj** (rather than Pataliputra) the supreme political and imperial capital of Northern India. Key rulers included **Harivarman**, **Adityavarman**, **Ishwaravarman**, **Ishanavarman** (first to assume the title *Maharajadhiraja* around 554 CE after repelling the Hunas and Gaudas), and **Sarvavarman**.
3. The **Asirgarh Copper Seal** (MP) and **Haraha Stone Inscription** (Barabanki district, UP, dated 554 CE) provide the complete genealogy and military triumphs of the Maukhari dynasty.
4. The last Maukhari king was **Grahavarman**; in an effort to counter the hostile alliance of Malwa and Gauda, he married **Rajyashri**, daughter of king Prabhakaravardhana of Thanesar and sister of **Harshavardhana**.
5. Around **606 CE**, **Deva Gupta of Malwa** and **Shashanka of Gauda** launched a joint attack on Kannauj, killed Grahavarman, imprisoned queen Rajyashri, and occupied Kannauj.
6. King **Rajyavardhana** of Thanesar (elder brother of Harsha) marched swiftly, defeated the Malwa army, but was treacherously assassinated by **Shashanka of Gauda** during a truce negotiation.
7. **Harshavardhana (606–647 CE)** ascended the throne of Thanesar in **October 606 CE** at the young age of **16**, inaugurating the **Harsha Era (606 CE)**.
8. Guided by his loyal cousin **Bhandi** and general **Simhanada**, Harsha rescued his widowed sister **Rajyashri** just as she was about to commit Sati in the Vindhya forests, guided by the Buddhist monk **Divakaramitra**.
9. Harsha merged the kingdoms of Thanesar and Kannauj, **transferred his imperial capital permanently to KANNAUJ (Kanyakubja)**, and assumed the titles **Rajaputra**, **Siladitya**, and later **Maharajadhiraja**.
10. Harsha conquered Punjab, Rajasthan, Gujarat (defeated Dhruvasena II of Vallabhi, who became his son-in-law), Bihar, and Bengal (after Shashanka's death around 637 CE), bringing all of northern India into an imperial confederacy.
11. **Battle of the Narmada (c. 618–619 CE)**: When Harshavardhana attempted to expand his empire south of the Vindhyas into the Deccan, he was decisively defeated and halted on the banks of the **Narmada River** by the illustrious **Badami Chalukya king Pulakeshin II**.
12. This southern defeat is famously celebrated in the **Aihole Inscription (634 CE)** composed by the Jain poet **Ravikirti** on the Meguti temple in Karnataka: *"Harsha (joy) whose lotus-feet were covered with the rays of jewels of chiefs, had his Harsha (joy) melted away by fear when his elephants were destroyed by Pulakeshin!"*
13. **Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang, 602–664 CE)**: Renowned Chinese Buddhist pilgrim, styled the **"Prince of Pilgrims"**, traveled overland to India during Harsha's reign, residing in India for **15 years (630–645 CE)**; author of the monumental travelogue **Si-Yu-Ki** ("Buddhist Records of the Western World").
14. Xuanzang spent **5 years at Nalanda Mahavihara**, studying Buddhist logic, grammar, and Yogachara philosophy under the venerable chancellor and supreme patriarch **Shilabhadra**.
15. **Kannauj Grand Assembly (643 CE)**: Convened by Harsha in honor of Xuanzang to propagate the superiority of Mahayana Buddhism; attended by 20 tributary kings (including Bhaskaravarman of Kamarupa/Assam), 3,000 Buddhist monks, and 2,000 Brahmanas and Jains; lasted 21 days; when disgruntled orthodox Brahmanas attempted to assassinate Harsha and set fire to the assembly pavilion, Harsha arrested 500 ringleaders and banished them.
16. **Prayag Mahamoksha Parishad (Quinquennial Assembly, 643 CE)**: Every five years, Harsha held a grand 75-day charitable assembly at the **confluence of Ganga and Yamuna at Prayagraj (Prayag)**; here he distributed the accumulated royal treasury surplus of 5 years to Buddhist monks, Brahmanas, Jains, ascetics, the poor, and orphans, even giving away his own royal robes and crown, begging a worn cloth from his sister Rajyashri. The assembly witnessed by Xuanzang was Harsha's **sixth** such gathering.
17. In his personal religious life, Harsha began as a devout worshipper of **Surya** and **Shiva** (styled *Parama-maheshwara* in his Banskhera and Madhuban copper plates), but in his later life under the influence of his sister Rajyashri and Xuanzang, he became a dedicated patron of **Mahayana Buddhism**.
18. **Harsha as an Author**: Harsha was an accomplished Sanskrit dramatist who authored three celebrated Sanskrit plays:
    - **Priyadarsika**: Romantic four-act comedy on the union of King Udayana and princess Priyadarsika.
    - **Ratnavali**: Masterpiece romantic Sanskrit comedy of palace intrigue between King Udayana and princess Ratnavali.
    - **Nagananda**: Five-act Buddhist drama depicting prince **Jimutavahana** sacrificing his own body to divine eagle Garuda to save the life of serpent Sankhachuda.
19. **Banabhatta (Bana)**: Harsha's celebrated court poet; authored:
    - **Harshacharita**: The **first formal historical biography (*Akhyayika*) in Sanskrit literature**, narrating Harsha's ancestry, early life, and rise to power.
    - **Kadambari**: One of the world's earliest and greatest romantic novels in prose, displaying unmatched ornamental Sanskrit compound sentences.
    - **Chandishataka**: 100 verses praising Goddess Durga.
20. Other literary luminaries at Harsha's court included **Mayura** (author of *Mayurashataka* / *Suryashataka*), **Matanga Divakara**, and philosopher **Bhartrihari** (author of the *Vakyapadiya* and the *Shatakatraya*: Niti, Vairagya, Shringara).
21. **Harsha's Official Inscriptions**:
    - **Banskhera Copper Plate (Shahjahanpur district, UP, dated 628 CE)**: Features the ornate personal signature of Harsha (*Sva-hasto mama Maharajadhiraja Sri Harshasya*), proving he was an accomplished calligrapher.
    - **Madhuban Copper Plate (Mau district, UP, dated 631 CE)**: Records tax-exempt land grants to learned Brahmanas.
22. **Administration under Harsha**:
    - Heavily feudalized compared to the Guptas; high state officials were not paid in cash, but received land assignments and villages (*agrahara* / *bhoga*).
    - Law and order was weaker than in Gupta times; Xuanzang records that he was **robbed by brigands multiple times** during his travels in the Gangetic plain.
    - Punishment included mutilation of limbs and banishment into wild forests; trial by ordeal (fire, water, poison) was common.
    - Army was massive, comprising 100,000 cavalry, 50,000 infantry, and 60,000 war elephants.
23. **Harsha's Administrative Officers**:
    - **Mahasandhivigrahika**: Minister for War and Peace (Foreign Affairs).
    - **Mahabaladhikrita**: Supreme Commander of Military Forces.
    - **Baladhikrita**: Commander of infantry and cavalry divisions.
    - **Brihadashvavaru**: Head of the royal cavalry forces.
    - **Katuka**: Commander of the elephant corps.
    - **Bhandagaradhikarana**: Chief Superintendent of the Royal Storehouse and Treasury.
    - **Mimamsaka**: Chief Justice / Legal Arbiter.
    - **Uparika**: Provincial Governor.
24. **Harsha's Chinese Diplomatic Missions**:
    - Harsha dispatched a Brahmin envoy to the Tang imperial court in China in **641 CE**.
    - Tang Emperor Taizong reciprocated by sending Chinese diplomatic embassies led by **Li Yibiao** (643 CE) and **Wang Xuance** (647 CE).
25. **Death of Harsha (647 CE)**: Harsha died in 647 CE without leaving a male heir; his minister **Arunashva (Arjuna)** usurped the throne of Kannauj and attacked the Chinese mission of **Wang Xuance**; Wang Xuance fled to Nepal and Tibet, returned with Tibetan and Nepalese allied cavalry, captured Arunashva, and took him as a prisoner to Chang'an, China.
26. **Badami Chalukyas (Western Chalukyas, 543–753 CE)**:
    - Founded by **Pulakeshin I (543 CE)**, who fortified **Vatapi (modern Badami, Bagalkot district, Karnataka)** as his capital and performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice.
    - Greatest emperor was **Pulakeshin II (610–642 CE)**: Defeated Kadambas of Banavasi, Gangas of Mysore, Latas, Gurjaras, and Emperor Harshavardhana on the Narmada; dispatched a diplomatic embassy to Persian Sassanid king **Khosrow II (Parvez)** in 625 CE (depicted in Cave 1 of Ajanta).
27. **Pallava-Chalukya Long Wars**:
    - Pulakeshin II defeated Pallava king **Mahendravarman I** near Kanchi around 630 CE.
    - Mahendravarman's son **Narasimhavarman I (Mahamalla)** launched a devastating counter-invasion in **642 CE**, defeated Pulakeshin II in three battles, captured and burnt the Chalukya capital **Vatapi**, killed Pulakeshin II, and assumed the triumphant title **"Vatapikonda"** ("Conqueror of Vatapi").
28. **Chalukya Architecture (Vesara Style)**:
    - Developed the hybrid **Vesara style** (blend of northern Nagara and southern Dravida styles) centered in northern Karnataka:
    - **Aihole**: Celebrated as the **"Cradle of Indian Temple Architecture"**; features over 70 stone temples, including the **Lad Khan Temple**, **Durga Temple** (apsidal plan resembling a Buddhist chaitya), and **Huchimalligudi Temple**.
    - **Badami**: Famous for four rock-cut cave temples (Cave 1 dedicated to Shiva with an 18-armed Nataraja, Cave 2 & 3 dedicated to Vishnu, Cave 4 dedicated to Jain Tirthankaras).
    - **Pattadakal (UNESCO 1987)**: Site for royal coronations; features 10 magnificent structural temples: **Virupaksha Temple** (built by Queen Lokamahadevi to commemorate Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas, modeled on the Kailasanatha temple of Kanchi), **Mallikarjuna Temple**, and **Papanatha Temple**.
29. **Pallavas of Kanchi (c. 575–897 CE)**:
    - Established imperial sovereignty under **Simhavishnu (575–600 CE)** with capital at **Kanchipuram (Tamil Nadu)**.
    - **Mahendravarman I (600–630 CE)**: Great patron of arts, music, and literature; assumed titles *Gunabhara*, *Mattavilasa*, *Chitrakarapuli* ("Tiger among painters"), *Vichitrachitta* ("Curious-minded"); authored the hilarious Sanskrit farce **Mattavilasa Prahasana** (ridiculing drunken Kapalika Shaivites and corrupt Buddhist monks); initiated the construction of **rock-cut cave temples (*Mandapas*)** without using brick, timber, or mortar (e.g., at Mandagapattu, Trichy, Mamandur).
    - **Narasimhavarman I Mahamalla (630–668 CE)**: Sacked Vatapi (642 CE); founded the coastal port city of **Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram)**; carved the monolithic **Pancha Rathas** (Dharmaraja, Bhima, Arjuna, Draupadi, Nakula-Sahadeva rathas) from single granite boulders; carved the world's largest open-air stone bas-relief: **"Arjuna's Penance" (Descent of the Ganga)**.
    - **Narasimhavarman II Rajasimha (700–728 CE)**: Shifted from rock-cut caves to structural stone temples; built the magnificent **Shore Temple at Mamallapuram** and the **Kailasanatha Temple at Kanchipuram**.
    - **Dandin**: Renowned Sanskrit writer and rhetorician; lived in the court of Narasimhavarman II Rajasimha at Kanchipuram; authored *Kavyadarsha* and *Dashakumaracharita*.
30. **Maitrakas of Vallabhi (475–776 CE)**:
    - Founded by Senapati **Bhatarka** at **Vallabhi (Gujarat)**; became a major commercial port and celebrated center of Buddhist and secular learning, rivaling Nalanda; visited by Xuanzang and I-tsing.
    - Patronized the **Second Jain Council (512 CE)** under Devardhi Kshamasramana.
31. **Gaudas of Bengal**:
    - Under king **Shashanka (c. 600–637 CE)** with capital at **Karnasuvarna** (Murshidabad); staunch Shaivite; accused by Buddhist sources (Banabhatta and Xuanzang) of cutting down the sacred **Bodhi Tree at Bodh Gaya** and throwing its roots into the fire.
32. **Tripartite Struggle (त्रिपक्षीय संघर्ष)**:
    - In the 8th century CE, the political vacuum left by Harsha's death sparked a 200-year struggle among **three great imperial powers** to control the imperial city of **Kannauj (Kanyakubja)**:
      - 1. **Gurjara-Pratiharas** of Western India (Nagabhata I, Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja).
      - 2. **Palas** of Bengal and Bihar (Gopala, Dharmapala, Devapala).
      - 3. **Rashtrakutas** of the Deccan (Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha I, Krishna III).
    - The struggle was ultimately won by the **Gurjara-Pratiharas**, who established Kannauj as their imperial capital in the 9th century.
33. **Xuanzang's Description of Indian Society**:
    - Recorded that the caste system had rigidified into 4 primary varnas alongside numerous subcastes; mixed marriages were prohibited; widows were forbidden to remarry.
    - Described **Prayag** as a sacred tirtha where pilgrims fasted to death at the sacred banyan tree (*Akshayavat*) to attain heaven.
    - Praised the Indian character: *"The ordinary people, though of quick temper, are upright and honorable; in money matters they are without craft, and in administering justice they are considerate"*.
34. **Nalanda Mahavihara Under Harsha**:
    - Supported by the revenues of **100 (or 200) villages** remitted directly to the university by royal charter; enrolled over 10,000 resident monk-students from China, Korea, Japan, Tibet, and Sumatra; admission was extremely rigorous, decided by the gatekeeper scholar (*Dwarapala*).
35. **I-tsing (Yijing, 671–695 CE)**: Chinese Buddhist pilgrim who arrived in India via the maritime oceanic route shortly after Harsha; spent 10 years at Nalanda translating Buddhist Sanskrit texts into Chinese; author of *Record of Buddhist Practices Sent Home from the Southern Sea*.
36. **Harsha's Religious Transition**:
    - Started his reign worshipping the Sun and Shiva, as reflected in the titles of his ancestral line (*Parama-aditya-bhakta* for father Prabhakaravardhana; *Parama-maheshwara* for elder brother Rajyavardhana).
    - Fully patronized Mahayana Buddhism in later years, forbidding animal slaughter and constructing thousands of stupas and viharas along the Ganga.
37. **Village Self-Governance (Gramakutas)**: Rural administration in the Deccan under the Chalukyas was headed by the *Gramakuta* / *Patel*, assisted by village assemblies, continuing local self-governing traditions.
38. **Rathas of Mamallapuram**: Five monolithic granite temples named after the Pandavas:
    - **Dharmaraja Ratha**: Largest, three-storied pyramid shrine with an octagonal dome.
    - **Bhima Ratha**: Barrel-vaulted roof (*Salashikhara*).
    - **Draupadi Ratha**: Smallest, single-story hut-shaped shrine dedicated to Durga.
39. **Pattini Worship**: Instituted across Tamilakam by Chera king Senguttuvan, commemorating Kannagi's purity and marital loyalty.
40. **Vikramaditya II (Chalukya)**: Great Chalukya king who invaded Kanchi three times, defeated the Pallavas, but spared the city from destruction and donated golden ornaments to the Kailasanatha temple, inscribed on a pillar.
41. **Ravikirti**: The composer of the Aihole prashasti; a devout Jain who built the Meguti temple; in the final verse he boasts: *"By his composition for the king, he has attained the fame of Kalidasa and Bharavi"*, providing a definitive chronological terminus ante quem for Kalidasa before 634 CE.
42. **Bharavi**: Classical Sanskrit poet who lived in the court of Pallava king Simhavishnu; author of the immortal epic **Kiratarjuniya** (depicting the combat between Arjuna and Shiva disguised as a mountain hunter / Kirata).
43. **Bhatti (Bhattikavya / Ravanavadha)**: 7th-century Sanskrit poem written in Gujarat to illustrate the complex grammatical rules of Panini alongside the Ramayana narrative.
44. **Bhartrihari**: Sanskrit philosopher-poet of 7th c. CE who lived in Malwa; authored the philosophical treatise **Vakyapadiya** (foundations of Sanskrit linguistic philosophy) and the **Shatakatraya**: *Niti-shataka* (100 verses on ethics), *Shringara-shataka* (on romantic love), and *Vairagya-shataka* (on worldly renunciation).
45. **Buddhism Decline Beginnings**: During Harsha's era, Buddhism was already declining across northwestern and central India; Xuanzang lamented the ruined and abandoned monasteries at Taxila, Sravasti, and Kapilavastu, while Brahmanism, Shaivism, and Vaishnavism were vigorously reviving.
46. **Banskhera Signature**: The royal signature at the foot of the Banskhera copper plate is hailed as a masterpiece of ancient Indian royal calligraphy.
47. **Karnasuvarna**: The capital city of Shashanka of Gauda, located near modern Berhampore in Murshidabad district, West Bengal; excavated by ASI confirming the Buddhist monastery of Raktamrittika described by Xuanzang.
48. **Simhavishnu's Conquests**: Pallava king who assumed the title *Avanisimha* ("Lion of the Earth"); wiped out the Kalabhras in the Tamil country, conquered the Chola territory up to the Kaveri, and inaugurated the glorious Imperial Pallava era.
49. **Naval Expeditions of Narasimhavarman I**: Dispatched two major naval fleets from Mamallapuram to **Sri Lanka** to assist his ally, the exiled Sinhalese prince **Manavarma**, in regaining the throne of Anuradhapura.
50. **Threshold of Early Medieval India**: The passing of Harsha in 647 CE, followed shortly by the eclipse of the Badami Chalukyas (753 CE) and Pallavas (897 CE), marks the definitive transition from Classical Ancient India into the fragmented feudal landscape of Early Medieval India.
