## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. North-west entry gates into ancient India were the **Khyber** and **Bolan** passes through the Hindu Kush and Sulaiman ranges.
2. The **first foreign invasion of India** was launched by the **Achaemenian (Persian) Empire** under king **Cyrus the Great (558–530 BCE)**, who conquered the borderland regions of Gedrosia and Gandhara.
3. Persian emperor **Darius I (522–486 BCE)** conquered the Indus valley (*Hindush*) around **516 BCE**, making it the **20th Satrapy (province)** of the Persian Empire; this satrapy was the richest and most populous, paying an annual tribute of **360 Euboic talents of gold dust** (one-third of Persia's total revenue).
4. Three famous Persian inscriptions record this Indian conquest: the **Behistun Inscription**, the **Persepolis Inscription**, and the **Naqsh-e-Rustam Inscription**.
5. Indian soldiers equipped with cane bows and iron-tipped cane arrows fought in Greece under Persian King **Xerxes (486–465 BCE)** against the Greek Spartans at the famous **Battle of Thermopylae (480 BCE)**.
6. The Persian invasion introduced into northwestern India the **Kharosthi script** (written from **right to left**, derived from Aramaic) and the **Aramaic script**, which Ashoka later used in his northwestern rock edicts at Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra.
7. Persian influence shaped Mauryan imperial art: **bell-shaped capital lotuses**, highly polished mirror finish on stone surfaces, and the concept of issuing royal edicts on rock cliffs.
8. **Alexander the Great (King of Macedonia)** invaded India in **326 BCE** after conquering the Persian Empire of Darius III (Battle of Gaugamela / Arbela, 331 BCE).
9. Alexander crossed the Hindu Kush through the Khyber Pass; the ruler of Taxila, **Ambhi (Omphis)**, surrendered without fighting and allied with Alexander against his rival King Porus.
10. The heroic **Battle of the Hydaspes (Jhelum)** was fought in **326 BCE** between **Alexander and King Porus (Puru)**, ruler of the territory between the Jhelum and Chenab rivers. Porus fought valiantly with his 200 war elephants before being captured; impressed by his royal dignity (*"Treat me as a king ought to treat a king"*), Alexander reinstated Porus and enlarged his kingdom.
11. Alexander founded two cities on the Jhelum: **Nicaea** ("City of Victory", at the battle site) and **Bucephala** (named in memory of his beloved war horse Bucephalus who died there).
12. Alexander's victorious army advanced as far as the **Hyphasis (Beas) river**, where his battle-weary, homesick soldiers—terrified by reports of the massive 200,000-strong army and 3,000 war elephants of the **Nanda king of Magadha**—staged a mutiny and refused to march further east.
13. Alexander was forced to retreat down the Indus river; his campaign in India lasted a total of **19 months (326–325 BCE)**. He divided his conquered Indian territories among three satraps (including Porus and Ambhi) and sailed through the Indian Ocean under admiral **Nearchus**.
14. Alexander died of fever at **Babylon** in **323 BCE** at the young age of **32**, triggering the partition of his vast empire among his Macedonian generals (*Diadochi*).
15. In **305 BCE**, **Seleucus I Nicator** (Alexander's general who established the Seleucid Empire in Syria and Persia) crossed the Indus to reclaim Alexander's Indian satrapies, but was decisively defeated by **Chandragupta Maurya**.
16. The **Treaty of 305 BCE** resulted in:
    - Seleucus ceding four strategic provinces to the Mauryan Empire: **Aria (Herat), Arachosia (Kandahar), Gedrosia (Balochistan), and Paropamisadae (Kabul)**.
    - A matrimonial alliance (*Epigamia*), traditionally believed to involve a Greek princess marrying into the Mauryan court.
    - Chandragupta Maurya presenting **500 war elephants** to Seleucus, which proved decisive in the Battle of Ipsus (301 BCE) in Greece.
    - Seleucus dispatching his ambassador **Megasthenes** to reside permanently at the Mauryan court in Pataliputra, who authored the landmark book **Indica**.
17. The chronological order of post-Mauryan foreign invader waves into northwestern India: **Indo-Greeks (Bactrian Greeks) $ightarrow$ Shakas (Scythians) $ightarrow$ Indo-Parthians (Pahlavas) $ightarrow$ Kushanas (Yueh-chi)**.
18. **Indo-Greeks (Bactrian Greeks)** were the **FIRST foreign rulers in India to issue regular GOLD COINS**, and the **first to issue coins bearing the portraits and names of kings** along with bilingual/bidirectional legends (Greek on obverse, Kharosthi on reverse).
19. Famous Indo-Greek kings:
    - **Demetrius I**: Invaded Punjab around 180 BCE, establishing Indo-Greek rule with capital at **Sakala (Sialkot)**.
    - **Menander I (Milinda, c. 165–145 BCE)**: Most celebrated Indo-Greek ruler; converted to Buddhism by Buddhist sage **Nagasena** after the philosophical debates recorded in the Pali classic **Milinda Panha** ("Questions of King Menander").
20. The **Shakas (Scythians)** were nomadic tribes pushed out of the Oxus basin by the Yueh-chi; they entered India through the Bolan Pass and established five regional branches:
    - 1. Afghanistan
    - 2. Taxila (Northern Satraps)
    - 3. Mathura (Northern Satraps)
    - 4. Western India (Maharashtra / Kshaharata branch)
    - 5. Upper Deccan / Ujjain (Western Satraps / Kardamaka branch)
21. The **Kshaharata branch** in western India was ruled by **Bhumaka** and **Nahapana**; Nahapana's vast empire was completely destroyed and his coins restruck by Satavahana emperor **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (recorded in the Jogalthambi hoard of Nasik).
22. The **Kardamaka branch** in Ujjain was founded by **Chashtana**; its most celebrated ruler was **Rudradaman I (130–150 CE)**.
23. **Rudradaman I** issued the famous **Junagarh Rock Inscription (dated 150 CE)** in Gujarat:
    - **First long, elaborate inscription written in chaste, classical Kavya Sanskrit** (earlier inscriptions were in Prakrit).
    - Records the complete history of the **Sudarshana Lake** in Saurashtra: built under Chandragupta Maurya (Pushyagupta), canals added under Ashoka (Tushaspha), breached by terrible storms and rebuilt on a massive scale by Rudradaman's governor **Suvisakha entirely from the royal treasury without levying any extra tax or forced labor (*Vishti*)**!
24. The **Indo-Parthians (Pahlavas)** originated in Iran and succeeded the Shakas in northwestern India; their most famous king was **Gondophares (c. 20–46 CE)**, during whose reign **Saint Thomas the Apostle** arrived in northwestern India to propagate Christianity.
25. The **Kushanas** belonged to the **Yueh-chi (Yuezhi)** nomadic confederacy originating in the steppes of Central Asia / Chinese borderlands.
26. **Kujula Kadphises (Kadphises I)**: United the five Yueh-chi principalities; crossed the Hindu Kush and established the Kushana dynasty in India; issued **copper coins** imitating Roman emperor Augustus.
27. **Vima Kadphises (Kadphises II)**: Son of Kujula; conquered northern India up to Varanasi; **FIRST KUSHANA RULER TO ISSUE GOLD COINS** (the *Dinar*); was a devout Shaivite who assumed the title **"Sarvaloka Ishwara Mahishwara"** and depicted Lord Shiva with Nandi on his coins.
28. **Kanishka I (c. 78–101 CE)**: Greatest Kushana emperor; established the **Saka Era in 78 CE** (used today as the basis of the official Indian National Calendar); ruled from twin imperial capitals: **Purushapura (Peshawar)** and **Mathura**.
29. Kanishka convened the **Fourth Buddhist Council** at **Kundalvana in Kashmir (72 CE)**, presided by **Vasumitra** with **Ashvaghosha** as vice-president; patronized the final division of Buddhism into **Hinayana and Mahayana**.
30. Great luminaries at Kanishka's court:
    - **Ashvaghosha**: Renowned Buddhist philosopher, dramatist, and poet; author of *Buddhacharita* (epic biography of Buddha in Sanskrit), *Saundarananda*, and *Sariputraprakarana* (earliest surviving Sanskrit drama).
    - **Nagarjuna**: Great philosopher of the *Madhyamaka* (Sunyavada) school; author of *Mulamadhyamakakarika*; compared to Einstein for formulating the relativity of cosmic phenomena.
    - **Charaka**: Court royal physician; author of **Charaka Samhita** (foundational compendium of Ayurveda internal medicine).
    - **Vasumitra**: Buddhist scholar who authored the encyclopedic *Mahavibhasha*.
    - **Mathara**: Trusted prime minister.
    - **Agesilaos**: Greek engineer/architect who built Kanishka's giant wooden relic stupa and monastery at Peshawar (described by Xuanzang).
31. Kanishka adopted the high imperial title **Devaputra** ("Son of Heaven", borrowed from the Chinese emperors).
32. The Kushana Empire controlled the lucrative **Great Silk Route** linking China, Central Asia, India, and the Roman Empire, collecting heavy transit tolls that fueled unprecedented economic prosperity.
33. Kushanas issued the **purest gold coins** in ancient India, maintaining a remarkably stable gold standard based on the Roman *Aureus* / *Denarius*.
34. The Kushana period witnessed the birth of the two great schools of Indian sculptural art: **Gandhara Art** and **Mathura Art**.
35. **Gandhara School of Art (Greco-Buddhist Art)**:
    - Center: Peshawar, Taxila, Swat, and Jalalabad (NW frontier).
    - Material: Soft **grey / blue schist stone**, stucco, terracotta.
    - Characteristics: **Hellenistic Greek style applied to Buddhist themes**; Buddha depicted with realistic Greek facial features, wavy curly hair, sharp nose, deep drapery folds reminiscent of a Roman toga (*Apollo-like Buddha*).
    - Patronage: Actively patronized by the **Kushanas** and earlier Indo-Greeks.
36. **Mathura School of Art**:
    - Center: Mathura (Uttar Pradesh) and adjacent Ganga-Yamuna doab.
    - Material: **Spotted red sandstone** (from Sikri/Kankali Tila).
    - Characteristics: Completely **indigenous Indian tradition**; Buddha depicted with an open, smiling, serene Indian face, shaved head, right hand raised in *Abhaymudra*, diaphanous clinging drapery, and a large decorated halo (*Prabhamandala*).
    - Syncretism: Produced sculptures for **all three religions**: **Buddhism** (Buddhas and Bodhisattvas), **Brahmanism** (Shiva, Vishnu, Surya, Durga, Kartikeya), and **Jainism** (Ayagapatas and Tirthankaras at Kankali Tila).
    - Dynastic Sanctuary: Royal portrait statues of Kushana emperors in Central Asian military dress (e.g., the famous **headless statue of Kanishka** wearing a tunic and heavy boots) found at **Mat near Mathura**.
37. **Amaravati School of Art**:
    - Center: Lower Krishna-Godavari valley (Andhra Pradesh: Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda).
    - Material: **White marble-like limestone**.
    - Characteristics: Narrative relief panels depicting scenes from Buddha's life and Jatakas; dynamic movement, sensuous human forms; strictly patronized by the **Satavahanas** and later **Ikshvakus** (NOT the Kushanas).
38. **Heliodorus Garuda Pillar at Besnagar (Vidisha, MP)**:
    - Erected in **113 BCE** during the reign of Shunga king **Bhagabhadra**.
    - Inscribed by **Heliodorus**, the Greek ambassador sent by Indo-Greek king **Antialcidas of Taxila**.
    - Heliodorus explicitly declares himself a **"Bhagavata"** and dedicates the pillar to **"Devadeva Vasudeva"** (Lord Krishna) — **first epigraphic record of a foreigner converting to Vaishnavism**.
39. **Hunas**: Ferocious nomadic pastoral warriors from Central Asia who breached northwestern passes in the 5th century CE:
    - **White Hunas (Hephthalites)** invaded India during the reign of Gupta emperor **Kumaragupta I** and were initially crushed by **Skandagupta** (recorded in Bhitari pillar, c. 455 CE).
    - Led later by ruthless kings **Toramana** and his son **Mihirakula** (who established his capital at Sakala/Sialkot and was a fanatic Shaivite).
    - Mihirakula was decisively defeated around **528–532 CE** by the confederacy of **Yashodharman of Malwa** (celebrated in the Mandasor pillar inscription) and **Narasimhagupta Baladitya** of Magadha.
40. **Curtain in Indian Theatre (Yavanika)**: The Indian dramatic curtain is called **Yavanika**, a term derived from **Yavana (Greeks)**, showing Greek theatrical influence.
41. **Indian Astrology & Astronomy**: Ancient Indian astronomical texts (such as the *Gargi Samhita* and Varahamihira’s *Brihat Samhita*) acknowledge indebtedness to Greek astronomy: *"The Greeks are barbarians, but the science of astronomy originated among them and for this they must be revered like gods"*. The solar calendar, the names of days of the week, and zodiac signs (*Rashis*) were adapted from Greco-Roman models.
42. **Hydaspes vs Hyphasis**: The Battle against Porus was fought on the **Hydaspes (Jhelum)**; the mutiny where Alexander's army stopped was on the **Hyphasis (Beas)**. Never swap the two rivers!
43. **Bucephalus**: Alexander's favorite war horse; died following the Battle of Hydaspes; commemorated by founding the city of Bucephala on the Jhelum.
44. **Nicanor, Philip, and Eudemus**: Macedonian officers left by Alexander to govern his northern and western satrapies, whose assassinations and expulsions cleared the way for Chandragupta Maurya's swift northwest consolidation.
45. **St. Thomas the Apostle**: Landed in India around 52 CE during Gondophares' reign; traveled to the Malabar coast (Kerala) and later to Mylapore (Chennai), establishing the Syrian Christian community of India (*Saint Thomas Christians*).
46. **Yueh-chi Tribe**: The Chinese historical chronicles (*Shiji* and *Hanshu*) record that the Kushanas were one of the five branches of the **Ta-Yueh-chi** nomadic confederacy driven westward by the Xiongnu (Huns).
47. **Mat Inscription (Mathura)**: The dynastic shrine (*Devakula*) at Mat near Mathura yielded life-size portrait sculptures of Vima Kadphises, Kanishka, and Chashtana, illustrating divine kingship.
48. **Rabatak Inscription (Baghlan, Afghanistan)**: Discovered in 1993; written in the **Bactrian language and Greek script**; conclusively establishes the royal genealogy of Kanishka: Kujula Kadphises (great-grandfather) $ightarrow$ Vima Taktu (grandfather) $ightarrow$ Vima Kadphises (father) $ightarrow$ **Kanishka I**.
49. **Kanishka's Casket**: A gilded copper reliquary casket found inside the Shah-ji-ki-Dheri stupa near Peshawar, inscribed with Kanishka's name and containing authentic bone relics of the Buddha.
50. **Coins Metrology**: The Kushana gold coins (*Dinars*) were identical in weight (~8.04 grams / 124 grains) and purity to the Roman Imperial *Aureus*, proving high-value bullion monetization of international trade along the Silk Road.
