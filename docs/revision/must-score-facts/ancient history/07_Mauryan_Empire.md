## 🎯 Consolidated Must-Score Facts

1. The Mauryan Empire flourished from **c. 321 to 185 BCE**, representing the **first pan-Indian subcontinental empire**.
2. **Chandragupta Maurya (321–297 BCE)** founded the empire by overthrowing Dhana Nanda with the strategic guidance of his mentor **Chanakya (Kautilya / Vishnugupta)**.
3. Greek classical authors (Strabo, Arrian, Plutarch) refer to Chandragupta Maurya as **Sandrokottos** (सैंड्रोकोट्स) or **Androkottos**; in 1793, **Sir William Jones** first conclusively identified Sandrokottos with Chandragupta Maurya.
4. **Treaty of 305 BCE**: Chandragupta Maurya defeated **Seleucus I Nicator** (Macedonian ruler of Western Asia), who ceded four eastern satrapies: **Aria (Herat), Arachosia (Kandahar), Gedrosia (Balochistan), and Paropamisadae (Kabul)**, received **500 war elephants**, and sent **Megasthenes** as ambassador to Pataliputra.
5. In Jain tradition (*Parishishtaparvan* by Hemachandra), Chandragupta Maurya abdicated the throne, embraced Jainism under **Acharya Bhadrabahu**, migrated south to **Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)** during a 12-year Magadhan famine, and ended his life by religious fasting unto death (**Sallekhana / Santhara**) around **298 BCE** on Chandragiri hill.
6. **Kautilya's Arthashastra**: The foundational Sanskrit treatise on statecraft, political economy, and military strategy; divided into **15 Adhikaranas (books)** and 180 Prakaranas; rediscovered in 1905 and published in 1909 by **R. Shamasastry**.
7. **Saptanga Theory of State (सप्तांग सिद्धांत)**: Kautilya conceptualizes the state as an organic body with **seven essential limbs**:
   - 1. **Swami** (The King / Head)
   - 2. **Amatya** (Ministers, Bureaucrats / Eyes)
   - 3. **Janapada / Rashtra** (Territory and Population / Legs)
   - 4. **Durga** (Fortified Capital / Arms)
   - 5. **Kosha** (Treasury / Mouth)
   - 6. **Danda / Bala** (Armed Forces, Justice / Brain)
   - 7. **Mitra** (Allies, Friends / Ears)
8. **Megasthenes' Indica**: An eyewitness account of Mauryan society and administration; original work is lost but fragments survive in quotations by Strabo, Diodorus, Arrian, and Pliny.
9. Megasthenes famously divided Indian society into **Seven Castes / Classes (*Heptageneia*)**:
   - 1. **Philosophers** (Sophists — Brahmanas and Shramanas; exempt from taxes)
   - 2. **Farmers / Peasantry** (Cultivators — largest class, devoted solely to agriculture)
   - 3. **Herdsmen and Hunters** (Pastoralists)
   - 4. **Artisans and Traders** (Craftsmen, armourers)
   - 5. **Soldiers / Warriors** (Second largest class; maintained by state treasury)
   - 6. **Inspectors / Overseers** (*Episkopoi* — Spies reporting to king/magistrates)
   - 7. **Councillors and Assessors** (High administrative officials and magistrates)
10. Megasthenes noted two anomalous observations: that **slavery did not exist in India** (because Indian domestic servitude was mild compared to harsh Greek chattel slavery) and that **Indians did not know writing** or written loan contracts (since transactions were oral based on mutual trust).
11. **Bindusara (297–273 BCE)**: Son of Chandragupta Maurya; bore the title **Amitraghata** ("Slayer of Foes"; Greek *Amitrochates*); patronized the **Ajivika** sect; requested Syrian king **Antiochus I Soter** to send him sweet wine, dried figs, and a sophist/philosopher (Antiochus sent the figs and wine, but replied that Greek law forbade selling philosophers).
12. **Deimachus** of Syria (envoy of Antiochus I) and **Dionysius** of Egypt (envoy of Ptolemy II Philadelphus) served as foreign ambassadors during Bindusara's reign.
13. **Ashoka the Great (268–232 BCE)**: Served as viceroy at **Taxila** (where he quelled a rebellion) and **Ujjain** during Bindusara's reign; ascended the throne in 272 BCE, but his formal coronation took place in **268 BCE** after a 4-year succession struggle.
14. In his inscriptions, Ashoka is predominantly styled **Devanampiya** ("Beloved of the Gods") and **Piyadassi** ("Of Gracious Countenance"). His personal name **"Ashoka"** appears in only **four inscriptions**: **Maski, Gurjara, Nittur, and Udegolam**.
15. **Kalinga War (261 BCE)**: Fought in Ashoka's **8th regnal year**; vividly described in **Major Rock Edict XIII**: 100,000 slain, 150,000 captured, and many times that number perished from disease/famine; Ashoka felt deep sorrow and repentance, leading to his renunciation of military conquest (**Bherighosha**) in favour of moral and cultural conquest through righteousness (**Dhammaghosha**).
16. **Ashoka's Conversion**: Converted to Buddhism under the influence of monk **Upagupta** (or *Nigrodha* in Pali tradition); in his **10th regnal year** he visited Bodh Gaya, replacing pleasure tours (*Viharayatras*) with religious pilgrimages (**Dhammayatras**); in his **20th regnal year** he visited Lumbini (Buddha's birthplace), erecting the **Rummindei pillar**.
17. **Rummindei Pillar Inscription (Lumbini, Nepal)**: Commemorates the birthplace of the Buddha; Ashoka granted major tax exemptions to the village of Lumbini:
    - Reduced the land revenue tax (**Bhaga**) from the standard 1/6th to **1/8th** (*Athabhagiya*).
    - Completely abolished the religious tribute / cess (**Bali**).
18. **Bhabru (Bairat, Rajasthan) Minor Rock Edict**: Ashoka explicitly declares his personal faith in the **Triratna: Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha**, addressing the Buddhist clergy as *Piyadassi Raja Magadha*.
19. **Decipherment of Ashokan Inscriptions**: First deciphered in **1837** by **James Prinsep**, founding secretary of the Asiatic Society of Bengal, who identified the script as ancient **Brahmi**. (The first Ashokan inscription was discovered in 1750 by Father Tieffenthaler in Delhi).
20. **Scripts Used in Ashokan Inscriptions**:
    - **Brahmi**: Written left-to-right; used across the vast majority of central, eastern, and southern India.
    - **Kharosthi**: Written right-to-left; used in northwestern Pakistan edicts (**Shahbazgarhi** and **Mansehra**).
    - **Aramaic and Greek**: Used in Afghanistan edicts (**Kandahar bilingual inscription**, Taxila, Lampaka/Laghman).
21. **Ashoka's Major Rock Edicts (14 MREs)**:
    - **MRE I**: Prohibition of animal sacrifice and festive gatherings (*Samaja*).
    - **MRE II**: Medical care for humans and animals across the empire and neighboring states (**Cholas, Pandyas, Satiyaputras, Keralaputras**, and Antiochus II of Syria).
    - **MRE III**: Orders **Yuktas, Rajukas, and Pradeshikas** on five-year administrative tours (*Anusamyana*).
    - **MRE IV**: Proclamation of *Dhammaghosha* replacing *Bherighosha*.
    - **MRE V**: Appointment of **Dhamma Mahamattas** (in 14th regnal year) to promote welfare and morality.
    - **MRE VI**: King's constant accessibility: reporters (**Pativedakas**) ordered to report public matters at all times.
    - **MRE VII**: Plea for universal religious tolerance among all sects.
    - **MRE VIII**: Institution of **Dhammayatras** (pilgrimages of piety); Ashoka's visit to Bodh Gaya (10th regnal year).
    - **MRE IX**: Condemnation of meaningless popular ceremonies; praise for *Dhamma-mangala*.
    - **MRE X**: Denunciation of individual glory and fame.
    - **MRE XI**: Incomparable virtue of the gift of Dhamma (*Dhamma-dana*).
    - **MRE XII**: Exhortation for mutual tolerance; honor for all sects; appointment of female affairs officers (*Ithijhakha Mahamattas*).
    - **MRE XIII**: Devastation of **Kalinga War (261 BCE)**; triumph of Dhamma-Vijaya; names **five contemporary Hellenistic Greek rulers**:
      - 1. **Antiochus II Theos** of Syria
      - 2. **Ptolemy II Philadelphus** of Egypt
      - 3. **Antigonus Gonatas** of Macedonia
      - 4. **Magas** of Cyrene
      - 5. **Alexander** of Epirus
    - **MRE XIV**: Summary explaining the purpose and geographical distribution of the edicts.
22. **The 7 Major Pillar Edicts (MPE)**: Inscribed on monolithic polished sandstone pillars; found at Topra (Haryana, shifted to Delhi by Firoz Shah Tughlaq), Meerut (shifted to Delhi), Allahabad-Kosam, Lauriya-Araraj, Lauriya-Nandangarh, and Rampurva (Bihar).
23. **Ashoka's Sarnath Lion Capital**: Monolithic sandstone column featuring **four Asiatic lions** seated back-to-back atop a circular abacus carved with four animals: **Elephant** (East - conception), **Horse** (South - renunciation), **Bull** (West - birth), and **Lion** (North - attainment); separated by four 24-spoked Dharma wheels; adopted as the **State Emblem of the Republic of India on 26 January 1950** with the motto *"Satyam Eva Jayate"* inscribed below.
24. **Barabar Cave Inscriptions**: Emperor Ashoka gifted the **Sudama Cave** and **Nyagrodha Cave** in the Barabar hills (Jehanabad, Bihar) in his 12th and 19th regnal years to the **Ajivika monks** (not to Buddhists).
25. **Third Buddhist Council**: Convened around **250 BCE** at **Pataliputra** under Ashoka's royal patronage, chaired by **Moggaliputta Tissa**; compiled the *Abhidhamma Pitaka* (*Kathavatthu*) and dispatched Buddhist missionary embassies to Sri Lanka (Ashoka's son **Mahinda** and daughter **Sanghamitta**, carrying a branch of the sacred Bodhi tree), Suvarnabhumi (Sona and Uttara), Kashmir-Gandhara (Majjhantika), and Yona/Greece.
26. **Mauryan Provincial Administration (Five Provinces)**:
    - 1. **Uttarapratha** (Northern Province) — Capital: **Taxila (Takshashila)**.
    - 2. **Avantiratha** (Western Province) — Capital: **Ujjayini (Ujjain)**.
    - 3. **Dakshinapatha** (Southern Province) — Capital: **Suvarnagiri** (near Bellary, Karnataka).
    - 4. **Prachya / Magadha** (Eastern/Central Province) — Capital: **Pataliputra**.
    - 5. **Kalinga** (Annexed Province) — Capital: **Tosali** (or Dhauli).
    - Provinces were administered by royal princes termed **Kumara** or **Aryaputra**.
27. **Southern Border Kingdoms**: Major Rock Edicts II and XIII list the independent border powers to the south: **Cholas, Pandyas, Satiyaputras, Keralaputras, and Tamraparni (Sri Lanka)**. These were **border allies**, NOT annexed provinces of the Mauryan Empire!
28. **Mauryan High Officials (18 Tirthas)**:
    - **Mantri**: Chief Minister / Prime Minister.
    - **Purohita**: Chief Royal Priest.
    - **Senapati**: Supreme Commander-in-Chief of Armed Forces.
    - **Yuvaraja**: Crown Prince.
    - **Samaharta**: **Collector General of Revenue** (assesses taxes, prepares annual state budget).
    - **Sannidhata**: **Chief Treasury Officer & Custodian of Royal Storehouses** (*Koshadhyaksha*).
    - **Pradeshta**: Chief Magistrate of criminal courts (*Kantakashodhana*).
    - **Vyavaharika**: Chief Judge of civil courts (*Dharmasthiya*).
    - **Dandapala**: Chief of Police administration.
    - **Durgapala**: Governor of imperial forts.
    - **Antapala**: Governor of frontier outposts and border security.
29. **Key Department Superintendents (Adhyakshas)**:
    - **Sitadhyaksha**: Superintendent of Crown Agriculture lands (*Sita*).
    - **Pautavadhyaksha**: Superintendent of Weights and Measures.
    - **Panyadhyaksha**: Superintendent of Trade and Commerce (price regulation).
    - **Sulkadhyaksha**: Superintendent of Tolls, Customs, and Octroi.
    - **Akaradhyaksha**: Superintendent of Mines.
    - **Lohadhyaksha**: Superintendent of Iron and Metallurgy.
    - **Navadhyaksha**: Superintendent of Shipping, Ports, and Waterways.
    - **Mudradhyaksha**: Superintendent of Passports and Royal Seals.
    - **Rupadarshaka**: Inspector of Coins and Currency (tested silver *Pana* purity).
    - **Samsthadhyaksha**: Superintendent of Merchant Guilds and Markets.
30. **Megasthenes' City Administration of Pataliputra**: Administered by a supreme municipal commission of **30 Astynomoi** divided into **6 boards of 5 members each**:
    - *Board 1*: Industrial arts and craftsmanship regulation.
    - *Board 2*: Care, lodging, and surveillance of foreign visitors.
    - *Board 3*: Registration of births and deaths (vital statistics).
    - *Board 4*: Trade, retail commerce, and weights inspection.
    - *Board 5*: Inspection of manufactured articles and distinguishing new from second-hand goods.
    - *Board 6*: Collection of the **10% sales tax** on goods sold (evasion punishable by death).
31. **Megasthenes' Military Administration**: The Mauryan army (commanded by Senapati) was superintended by a governing board of **30 members divided into 6 committees of 5 members each**:
    - *Committee 1*: Navy (under *Navadhyaksha*).
    - *Committee 2*: Military transport, commissariat, and war equipment.
    - *Committee 3*: Infantry (*Padati*).
    - *Committee 4*: Cavalry (*Ashva*).
    - *Committee 5*: War Chariots (*Ratha*).
    - *Committee 6*: War Elephants (*Hasti*).
32. **Ashoka's District Administration Officers**:
    - **Rajuka (रज्जुक)**: Derived from *Rajju* (measuring rope); district head responsible for land measurement, revenue assessment, and judicial administration (Ashoka granted them autonomous judicial power to award rewards and punishments in Pillar Edict IV).
    - **Yukta (युक्त)**: Subordinate revenue and accounting clerk assisting Rajukas.
    - **Pradeshika (प्रादेशिक)**: Divisional commissioner conducting regular five-year inspection tours (*Anusamyana*).
    - **Dhamma Mahamatta (धम्म महामात्र)**: Specialized moral cadres created in Ashoka's 14th regnal year (MRE V) to propagate Dhamma, ensure justice for prisoners, and promote religious communal harmony.
33. **Espionage Network (Gudhapurushas)**: Kautilya describes a pervasive intelligence service directed by the *Mahamatyapasarpa*; divided into:
    - **Sanstha** (Stationary spies): Operating in disguise within institutions: *Kapatika* (fraudulent student), *Udasthita* (renegade monk), *Grihapatika* (peasant), *Vaidehaka* (merchant), *Tapasa* (hermit).
    - **Sanchara** (Wandering / roving spies): Secret agents constantly traveling: *Satri* (secret agent), *Tikshna* (brave assassin), *Rasada* (poisoner), *Bhikshuki* (mendicant nun).
34. **Courts of Law (Judicial System)**:
    - **Dharmasthiya**: Civil courts dealing with marriage, inheritance, boundary disputes, contracts, and debt.
    - **Kantakashodhana**: Criminal courts ("removal of thorns") dealing with criminal offenses, treason, thieves, murder, and corrupt officials.
35. **Mauryan Taxes**:
    - **Bhaga**: King's share of agricultural produce (standard **1/6th** / *Shadbhaga*).
    - **Bali**: Religious cess / tribute (abolished by Ashoka at Lumbini).
    - **Sita**: Income/produce from crown agricultural lands.
    - **Vishti**: Forced unpaid labor demanded for public irrigation works and fortifications.
    - **Pranaya**: Emergency war levy (demanding 1/3 to 1/4 of grain) during fiscal distress.
    - **Vartani**: Road cess / transit duty collected by frontier officers (*Antapala*).
    - **Udakabhaga**: Water tax / irrigation cess varying from 1/5th to 1/3rd of produce.
    - **Hiranya**: Tax paid in cash/gold.
36. **Sohgaura and Mahasthangarh Plates**:
    - **Sohgaura Copper Plate** (Gorakhpur, UP): Earliest known copper-plate inscription in Brahmi; mentions maintenance of state granaries and **famine relief measures** in ancient India.
    - **Mahasthangarh Limestone Plaque** (Bogura, Bangladesh): Records distribution of paddy and oil seeds to relieve people during famine in Pundranagara.
37. **Sudarshana Lake Construction**:
    - Artificial reservoir constructed in Girnar (Junagadh, Gujarat) by **Pushyagupta Vaishya**, provincial governor under Chandragupta Maurya.
    - Canals added by **Tushaspha** (Yavana governor under Ashoka).
38. **Royal Highway (Uttarapath)**: Grand highway connecting Taxila in the northwest to Tamralipti in Bengal via Pataliputra; maintained by rural officers (*Agronomoi*) who installed milestones every 10 stadia; later rebuilt by Sher Shah Suri as the *Sadak-e-Azam* (Grand Trunk Road).
39. **Pataliputra City Structure**: Described by Megasthenes as a parallelogram (9 miles long, 1.5 miles wide) situated at the confluence of the Ganga and Son rivers, surrounded by a massive wooden palisade with **570 towers and 64 gates**, protected by a deep moat 600 feet wide and 30 cubits deep.
40. **Decline of the Mauryan Empire**: Ashoka died in **232 BCE**; empire fragmented into western and eastern halves; ruled by successors: Kunala, Dasharatha (who gifted Nagarjuni caves to Ajivikas), Samprati (patron of Jainism), Salisuka, Devavarman, Satadhanvan, and Brihadratha.
41. **Fall of the Dynasty (185 BCE)**: The last Mauryan emperor **Brihadratha** was assassinated during a military parade review by his own commander-in-chief (**Senapati**) **Pushyamitra Shunga**, who founded the **Shunga Dynasty in 185 BCE**.
42. **Spies Classification**: Kautilya's *Arthashastra* strictly distinguishes between fixed municipal agents (**Sanstha**) and wandering field intelligence operatives (**Sanchara**).
43. **Ashokan Edict Typology**: Inscriptions are categorized into: 14 Major Rock Edicts (MRE), 2 Separate Kalinga Rock Edicts (Dhauli and Jaugada), Minor Rock Edicts (MRE), 7 Major Pillar Edicts (MPE), Minor Pillar Edicts, and Cave Inscriptions (Barabar).
44. **All Men Are My Children**: In the Separate Kalinga Edicts (Dhauli & Jaugada), Ashoka proclaims his paternal concept of monarchy: *"Save Munise Paja Mama"* (**"All men are my children"**).
45. **Ashokan Pillar Metallurgy & Monoliths**: Carved from single blocks of buff-colored fine-grained sandstone quarried at **Chunar (near Varanasi, UP)** and transported hundreds of miles along rivers; polished with a glass-like sheen unique to Mauryan sculpture.
46. **Dhauli Rock-Cut Elephant (Odisha)**: Earliest rock-cut sculpture in India; front half of an elephant carved directly out of living rock above Major Rock Edicts, symbolizing the Buddha entering his mother's womb as a white elephant.
47. **Rampurva Bull Capital (Bihar)**: Magnificent Mauryan bull capital now installed in the foyer of Rashtrapati Bhavan, New Delhi.
48. **Lauriya-Nandangarh Pillar (Bihar)**: Carries a single lion capital and a row of flying geese (*Hamsas*) etched on the abacus, preserved intact in its original location.
49. **Didarganj Yakshi (Patna)**: Life-size polished sandstone sculpture of a female fly-whisk bearer (*Chauri-bearer*) with high mirror finish; masterpiece of Mauryan court art.
50. **Mauryan Mint & Coinage**: State mint was managed by the **Lakshanadhyaksha**; standard currency was the silver **Pana** (3/4 tola punch-marked coin bearing sun, six-arm wheel, crescent-on-hill, and peacock motifs), and the copper **Mashaka** and **Kakanika**.
51. **Foundation of Mauryan Empire (321 BCE):** **Chandragupta Maurya** overthrown the tyrannical last Nanda King, **Dhana Nanda**, with the strategic genius of his mentor **Chanakya (Kautilya / Vishnugupta)**; established capital at Pataliputra; unified Northern and Central India under single imperial rule.
52. **War with Seleucus Nicator (305 BCE):** Chandragupta defeated Seleucus I Nicator (Alexander's general in Syria/Babylon); treaty signed: Seleucus ceded four satrapies—**Aria (Herat), Arachosia (Kandahar), Gedrosia (Baluchistan), and Paropamisadae (Kabul)**; Chandragupta gifted Seleucus **500 war elephants**; Seleucus sent Greek ambassador **Megasthenes** to Pataliputra court; matrimonial alliance concluded.
53. **Megasthenes' Indica (Key Observations):** Described Pataliputra as a magnificent wooden palisade city with 64 gates and 570 towers surrounded by deep moat; recorded that Indian society was divided into **7 classes / castes**: (1) Philosophers, (2) Farmers/Cultivators (largest class), (3) Herdsmen/Shepherds, (4) Artisans/Traders, (5) Soldiers/Military, (6) Overseers/Superintendents, (7) Councillors/Assessors; noted **absence of slavery** and usury in India (misperception due to humane treatment); described administration of Pataliputra managed by **6 boards of 5 members each** (30 commissioners).
54. **Kautilya's Arthashastra:** Monumental Sanskrit treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and military strategy; divided into **15 Books (Adhikaranas)** and 180 chapters; discovered in 1905 and published in 1909 by **R. Shamasastry**; expounds the **Saptanga Theory of the State (7 Limbs)**: (1) *Swami* (The King/Ruler), (2) *Amatya* (Ministers/Bureaucracy), (3) *Janapada* (Territory and Population), (4) *Durga* (Fortified Capital), (5) *Kosa* (Treasury), (6) *Danda / Bala* (Army/Military), (7) *Mitra* (Allies).
55. **Mauryan Administration & Key Officials (Adhyakshas):** King assisted by *Mantriparishad*; (1) *Samaharta*: Collector General of Revenue / Chief Tax Officer; (2) *Sannidhata*: Chief Treasury Officer / Royal Storekeeper; (3) *Sitadhyaksha*: Superintendent of Crown Agricultural Lands (*Sita*); (4) *Pautavadhyaksha*: Superintendent of Weights and Measures; (5) *Panyadhyaksha*: Superintendent of Commerce; (6) *Akaradhyaksha*: Superintendent of Mines; (7) *Lavanadhyaksha*: Superintendent of Salt; (8) *Navadhyaksha*: Superintendent of Shipping; (9) *Sulkaadhyaksha*: Collector of Tolls/Customs; (10) *Dhammamahamatras*: Special moral/religious welfare officers created by Ashoka in his 14th regnal year.
56. **Chandragupta's Later Life & Sallekhana:** Converted to Jainism under influence of Acharya **Bhadrabahu**; abdicated throne in favor of son Bindusara; accompanied Bhadrabahu to South India during 12-year famine in Magadha; ended his life by voluntary ritual starvation (**Sallekhana / Santhara**) at **Chandragiri Hill, Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)** in ~298 BCE.
57. **Bindusara (Amitraghata / Amitrochates):** Reigned 298–273 BCE; title **Amitraghata** means 'Slayer of Foes'; supported the **Ajivika sect** (propounded by Makkhali Gosala, believing in *Niyati* / absolute determinism); Greek king Antiochus I of Syria sent ambassador **Deimachus**; Egyptian king Ptolemy II Philadelphus sent ambassador **Dionysius**; Bindusara requested Antiochus I to send sweet wine, dried figs, and a sophist/philosopher (Antiochus sent wine and figs but replied that Greek law forbade selling philosophers).
58. **Ashoka's Kalinga War (261 BCE):** Fought in Ashoka's **8th regnal year (9th year after coronation)**; bloodiest campaign in ancient India; described graphically in **Major Rock Edict XIII (MRE XIII)**: 100,000 slain, 150,000 captured, and many died of famine and disease; sight of carnage caused profound remorse (*Anushochana*); transformed Ashoka from **Bherighosha** (war drums) to **Dhammaghosha** (sound of righteousness); converted to Buddhism by monk **Upagupta**.
59. **Decipherment of Ashokan Inscriptions:** First deciphered in **1837** by **James Prinsep** (Founding Secretary of Asiatic Society of Bengal), who identified the mysterious ruler referred to as **'Devanampiya Piyadasi'** ('Beloved of the Gods, of Gracious Mien') as Emperor Ashoka.
60. **Languages & Scripts of Ashokan Edicts:** Written in 4 scripts and 3 languages: (1) **Brahmi script in Prakrit language:** Used throughout India; written left to right; (2) **Kharosthi script:** Used in North-West India (Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra in Pakistan); derived from Aramaic; written right to left; (3) **Aramaic and Greek scripts:** Used in bilingual inscriptions at **Kandahar (Shar-i-Kuna, Afghanistan)** and Taxila.

| **Major Rock Edict VII** | Universal religious harmony; all sects may reside everywhere. |
| **Major Rock Edict VIII** | Replaced royal hunting trips (*Viharayatras*) with moral pilgrimages (**Dhammayatras**); visited Bodh Gaya. |
| **Major Rock Edict IX** | Condemned meaningless superstitions and rituals; commended *Dhamma-mangala*. |
| **Major Rock Edict X** | Denounced desire for personal fame and military glory. |
| **Major Rock Edict XI** | Declared the gift of Dhamma (*Dhamma-dana*) to be supreme above all gifts. |
| **Major Rock Edict XII** | Directive on mutual tolerance and reverence for other sects; appointment of female affairs officers (*Ithijhakha Mahamattas*). |
| **Major Rock Edict XIII** | **MOST IMPORTANT EDICT**: Records the **Kalinga War (261 BCE)**, deep remorse for 100,000 killed; victory by Dhamma declared supreme; **mentions 5 Hellenistic Greek Kings**: <br>1. **Antiochus II Theos** (Syria) <br>2. **Ptolemy II Philadelphus** (Egypt) <br>3. **Antigonus Gonatas** (Macedonia) <br>4. **Magas** (Cyrene) <br>5. **Alexander** (Epirus). |
