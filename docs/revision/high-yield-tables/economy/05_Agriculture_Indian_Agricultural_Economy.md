### Cropping Seasons in India (शस्य ऋतुएं)

| Season | Sowing Period | Harvesting Period | Major Crops Grown | Climatic Requirements & Regional Spread |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kharif (खरीफ)** | **June – July** (Onset of South-West Monsoon) | **September – October** (Autumn) | **Rice (धान), Maize (मक्का), Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut, Soybean, Arhar/Tur, Urad, Moong** | High temperature, high humidity, abundant rainfall (100–200 cm). Pan-India coverage. |
| **Rabi (रबी)** | **October – November** (Onset of Winter) | **March – April** (Spring / Early Summer) | **Wheat (गेहूं), Barley (जौ), Gram (चना), Mustard (सरसों), Linseed (अलसी), Peas (मटर), Potato (आलू)** | Cool growing season, bright warm sunshine at ripening. ### Source-wise Share in Net Irrigated Area of India

| Irrigation Source | National Share (%) | Geographical Distribution & Leading States | Economic Advantages & Environmental Hazards |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Wells & Tube-wells (नलकूप एवं कुएं)** | **~62% – 64%** (Dominant Source) | Heavily concentrated in the Indo-Gangetic Plains: **Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Bihar**. | **Advantages:** Independent farmer control, on-demand irrigation, high application efficiency.<br>**Hazards:** Over-exploitation of aquifers, creating "dark zones" and ground subsidence; power subsidy distortion. |
| **Canals (नहरें)** | **~23% – 25%** (Second Largest) | Extensive in alluvial river plains: **Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan (Indira Gandhi Canal)**. | **Advantages:** Harnesses perennial river runoff; low recurring operational cost for farmers.<br>**Hazards:** Waterlogging (*Seep*), soil salinization and alkalinization (*Reh/Kallar*), siltation of canals. |
| **Tanks (तालाब / जलाशय)** | **~3% – 4%** | Ancient indigenous system prevalent in the hard-rock Deccan plateau: **Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka**. | **Advantages:** Eco-friendly, rainwater harvesting, recharges groundwater.<br>**Hazards:** Silt accumulation, highly dependent on local seasonal rainfall, seasonal drying. |
| **Other Sources (Lifts, Springs, Streams)** | **~7% – 9%** | Mountainous valleys, coastal deltas, wetlands: **Uttarakhand, Himachal, North-Eastern states**. | Localized small-scale mountain systems (*Kuhl* in Himachal). |

## 🔍 Key Table Observations & Memory Anchors

- **Exam Anchor:** Small and Marginal Farmers (SMFs, holding < 2.0 ha) account for ~86.2% of all operational land holdings in India, but operate only ~47.3% of the total operated area.
- **Exam Anchor:** Marginal holdings (< 1.0 ha) alone constitute 68.5% of all agricultural land holdings in India, with an average operational size of just 0.38 hectare.
- **Exam Anchor:** Kharif crops (खरीफ) are sown with the onset of the South-West Monsoon in June–July and harvested in September–October; major crops include Rice, Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut, Soybean, Tur/Arhar, Urad, and Moong.
- **Exam Anchor:** Rabi crops (रबी) are sown in October–November (winter onset) and harvested in March–April (spring); major crops include Wheat, Barley, Gram (चना), Mustard (सरसों), Linseed (अलसी), Peas (मटर), and Potato.
- **Exam Anchor:** Zaid crops (जायद) are short-duration summer crops grown from March to June between Rabi harvest and Kharif sowing; predominantly include cucurbitaceous crops: Watermelon (तरबूज), Muskmelon (खरबूजा), Cucumber (खीरा), Bitter gourd (करेला), Moong, and leafy vegetables/fodder.
- **Exam Anchor:** Rice (चावल) is the most cultivated crop in India, occupying the largest gross cropped area (~47 million hectares); West Bengal is the largest producer of Rice, while Punjab has the highest per-hectare productivity.
- **Exam Anchor:** Wheat (गेहूं) is the second most important cereal crop in India, occupying ~31 million hectares; Uttar Pradesh is the largest producer of Wheat in terms of total absolute output, while Punjab records the highest yield/productivity per hectare.
- **Exam Anchor:** Shree Anna (श्री अन्न / Millets) was championed by India, leading the UN to declare 2023 as the International Year of Millets; Rajasthan is the largest producer of Bajra (Pearl Millet), while Karnataka/Maharashtra lead in Jowar (Sorghum).
