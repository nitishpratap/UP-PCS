## 📖 Thematic Revision Summary (Reformatted from PDF)

### Mauryan Empire (321–185 BCE)

**Rulers:**
| Ruler | Key Event |
|-------|-----------|
| Chandragupta Maurya (321–297 BCE) | Founded empire; defeated Nanda dynasty; treaty with Seleucus |
| Bindusara (297–272 BCE) | Called "Amitrochates" (destroyer of foes) by Greeks |
| Ashoka (268–232 BCE) | Kalinga War (261 BCE) → adopted Buddhism |

**Arthashastra:** Written by Kautilya/Chanakya — treatise on statecraft, economy, military strategy

**Ashoka's Dhamma:**
- Non-violence, religious tolerance, truth, compassion
- Spread via Rock Edicts (14 Major), Pillar Edicts (7), Minor Rock Edicts

**Kalinga War (261 BCE):**
- Fought in modern Odisha
- 1 lakh killed → Ashoka renounced war
- Sent missionaries: son Mahendra to Sri Lanka, daughter Sanghamitra with Bodhi tree sapling

**Administration:**
- Capital: Pataliputra (modern Patna)
- Provinces: 5 (Taxila, Ujjain, Tosali, Suvarnagiri, Pataliputra)
- Spy system (Guptas) and Mahamattas (welfare officers)
- Megasthenes (Greek ambassador) wrote Indica

**Examiner Traps:**
- Chandragupta became Jain (not Buddhist) later in life; died at Shravanabelagola
- Bindusara ≠ Bimbisara (Bimbisara was Haryanka dynasty, pre-Maurya)
- Ashoka's symbol = Lion Capital of Sarnath = India's national emblem

**Q1. UPPCS Prelims 2024, Q20**

Consider the following statements with respect to Ashoka:

1. The definition of Dhamma given by Ashoka is taken from "Rahulovada-sutta".
2. In the 14th year of his coronation, Ashoka appointed a new type of staff, called Dhamma-mahamatras.

A. Both 1 and 2 B. Neither 1 nor 2 C. Only 1 D. Only 2

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: D — Only 2.** Dhamma-mahamatras in the 14th year. *Rahulovada* definition is false.
