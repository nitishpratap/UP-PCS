## Content

### Context — how to write current affairs answers

Current affairs in GS-II are not newspaper summaries. UPPCS expects the candidate to identify the event, connect it with India's interests, explain the wider regional or global significance and end with a balanced way forward. A good current affairs answer has four layers: **event**, **background**, **India's interest** and **future direction**.

For example, G20 is not only a summit; it is about global governance, Global South, climate finance, digital public infrastructure and India's status. Russia-Ukraine is not only a war; it shows security dilemmas, NATO-Russia rivalry, energy insecurity and India's strategic autonomy. WHO during COVID is not only a health body; it raises questions of global health governance, funding, transparency and vaccine equity.

| Current-affairs event | GS-II analytical angle |
|-----------------------|------------------------|
| G20 Presidency | Global governance, India's leadership, Global South, climate and development. |
| Russia-Ukraine War | Security dilemma, great-power rivalry, sanctions, food-energy crisis. |
| COVID-19 and WHO | Global health governance, institutional limits, vaccine equity. |
| USA-Iran tension | Energy security, Gulf diaspora, Chabahar, strategic autonomy. |
| Afghanistan withdrawal | Terrorism, regional balance, humanitarian diplomacy. |
| OBOR/BRI | Sovereignty, connectivity, debt, China challenge and alternatives. |

### India's G20 Presidency, 2023

India's G20 Presidency was significant because it placed India at the centre of global economic and diplomatic coordination. The theme **One Earth, One Family, One Future** reflected the civilisational idea of **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** and linked development, environment and human welfare.

The New Delhi Leaders' Declaration was a major diplomatic success because it achieved consensus despite Russia-Ukraine divisions. It also brought the **African Union** into G20 as a permanent member, strengthening Global South representation. India promoted **Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)**, financial inclusion, green development, climate finance, women-led development, resilient supply chains and sustainable lifestyles through **LiFE**. The **Global Biofuels Alliance** and India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor idea reflected India's attempt to shape future energy and connectivity.

G20 also helped India project itself as a bridge between the developed world and developing countries. India held Voice of Global South consultations and raised debt stress, food security, climate finance and technology access. For UPPCS, the answer should not only celebrate the Presidency; it should also mention challenges such as implementation, geopolitical rivalry, climate finance gaps and reform of multilateral institutions.

### G20 Rome Summit, 2021

The Rome G20 Summit of 2021 was shaped by the theme of **People, Planet and Prosperity**. It came after the COVID shock and focused on global recovery, vaccine access, climate action, sustainable development and economic resilience.

For **People**, the summit emphasised health systems, vaccine distribution and inclusive recovery. For **Planet**, it discussed climate action, biodiversity and sustainable energy transition. For **Prosperity**, it focused on economic recovery, resilient supply chains, taxation, jobs and development finance. The summit also supported global tax cooperation, including the idea of minimum corporate tax to reduce base erosion.

India's position combined climate responsibility with development needs. India supported sustainable lifestyles, vaccine equity and inclusive recovery while insisting that climate transition should not reduce development space for poorer countries.

### India and world leadership

The statement that India is ready for world leadership has a strong basis but must be analysed critically. India's strengths include democracy, population, economic growth, digital public infrastructure, space capability, pharmaceuticals, diaspora, military capacity, G20 leadership and Global South credibility. India has shown leadership through **Vaccine Maitri**, **International Solar Alliance**, **Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure**, **Mission LiFE**, disaster relief and digital public goods.

| Strength | Leadership value |
|----------|------------------|
| Democracy and demography | Legitimacy and human capital. |
| Economy | Market, manufacturing potential and growth engine. |
| Digital Public Infrastructure | Replicable model for financial inclusion and governance. |
| Space and science | Chandrayaan and low-cost technology capability. |
| Diaspora | Soft power, investment and political bridges. |
| G20 and Global South | Agenda-setting capacity. |
| Climate initiatives | Responsible leadership without abandoning development. |

However, world leadership is not automatic. India faces challenges: China border tension, Pakistan-sponsored terrorism, neighbourhood instability, manufacturing gap, poverty, unemployment, social cohesion concerns, dependence on imported energy and defence technology, and limited permanent role in UNSC. Therefore, India is ready to play a larger leadership role, but sustained domestic capacity and diplomatic consistency are necessary.

### Russia-Ukraine conflict

The Russia-Ukraine conflict emerged from a mix of historical, security and geopolitical causes. Russia sees NATO's eastward expansion as a security threat and wants strategic depth in its neighbourhood. Ukraine's desire for closer ties with the EU and NATO increased tension. The 2014 Crimea annexation, Donbas conflict, failure of the Minsk agreements, Russian identity narratives and Black Sea security concerns deepened the crisis.

The war affected the world through energy price rise, food insecurity, fertiliser shortage, sanctions, refugee flows, military escalation and global polarisation. India followed a policy of strategic autonomy: it called for dialogue and respect for sovereignty, avoided camp politics, evacuated students through Operation Ganga, managed energy imports and maintained ties with Russia, Ukraine, Europe and the US.

The key lesson is that security architecture without trust can produce conflict. Diplomacy, inclusive security arrangements and respect for territorial integrity are essential.

### WHO during COVID-19

WHO played a central role during COVID-19 by declaring a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, issuing technical guidance, coordinating surveillance, sharing information, supporting research through the Solidarity Trial, advising on masks/testing/vaccines and supporting vaccine equity efforts through COVAX with partners such as Gavi and CEPI.

Its role was useful because no country could fight a pandemic alone. WHO provided a common platform for information and technical standards. It supported weaker health systems and kept global health on the diplomatic agenda.

However, WHO faced criticism. It was accused of delayed warnings, excessive dependence on member-state data, weak enforcement power, funding dependence and insufficient transparency around early outbreak information. These limits show that WHO needs stronger funding, faster alert mechanisms, independent investigation powers and better pandemic treaty architecture.

### USA-Iran tensions and India

US-Iran tension has roots in the JCPOA nuclear deal dispute, US sanctions, Iran's regional role, Gulf security, maritime incidents and rivalry involving Israel and Gulf states. For India, the tension is not distant. It affects crude oil prices, supply diversity, Chabahar port, connectivity to Afghanistan and Central Asia, shipping security, banking channels and the safety of Indian diaspora in the Gulf.

India's response should be pragmatic. It must diversify energy sources, maintain strategic petroleum reserves, support de-escalation, preserve Chabahar, protect diaspora, coordinate with Gulf partners and retain strategic autonomy. India should avoid becoming a party to bloc politics while safeguarding its economic and security interests.

### Afghanistan after US withdrawal

The withdrawal of US troops from Afghanistan changed India's regional security environment. Taliban control raised concerns about terrorism, Pakistan-backed influence, radicalisation, narcotics, minority rights and the future of Indian development projects. It also complicated India's access to Central Asia.

India's approach should combine security vigilance with humanitarian engagement. India has continued assistance in forms such as wheat, medicines and development goodwill. It must coordinate with Iran, Central Asia, Russia and other partners while ensuring Afghan territory is not used for terrorism against India. The Afghanistan question is a test of patience, realism and humanitarian diplomacy.

### OBOR/BRI and India-China relations

China's **One Belt One Road**, later called the **Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)**, seeks infrastructure and connectivity across Asia, Europe, Africa and the Indian Ocean. India did not join it because the **China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC)** passes through Pakistan-occupied Kashmir, violating India's sovereignty. India also worries about debt stress, lack of transparency, environmental risks and strategic use of ports.

OBOR reflects the competitive nature of India-China relations. Cooperation exists in trade, BRICS, SCO, climate negotiations and global institutions, but competition dominates in border disputes, Indian Ocean presence, CPEC, technology, trade imbalance and regional influence. After Doklam and Galwan, trust declined sharply. India's response includes border preparedness, Atmanirbhar defence, resilient supply chains, QUAD, Act East, SAGAR, neighbourhood connectivity, Chabahar, IMEC-type corridors and transparent infrastructure partnerships.

The correct conclusion is not that India-China relations are only hostile. They are a mix of cooperation, competition and conflict, but OBOR has increased India's strategic distrust.

### Contemporary relevance

- **G20 India 2023** remains important because India projected itself as the voice of the Global South.
- **New Delhi Declaration** showed India's ability to build consensus amid great-power divisions.
- **Russia-Ukraine war** continues to affect energy, food, defence supplies and global alignments.
- **WHO reform** remains relevant after COVID because pandemics require faster alerts and fairer funding.
- **US-Iran tensions and West Asian conflict** affect oil, shipping, Chabahar and diaspora safety.
- **Afghanistan** remains linked to terrorism, humanitarian crisis and Pakistan's regional role.
- **OBOR/BRI** remains a core issue in India-China strategic competition.
- **India's world leadership claim** depends on domestic capacity, inclusive growth and credible diplomacy.

### Limits — avoid overstatement in current affairs

Current affairs answers often become one-sided. India did a successful G20 Presidency, but implementation and geopolitics remain hard. WHO played a role in COVID, but it had institutional limits. India has leadership potential, but poverty, unemployment, border threats and institutional reform gaps remain. Russia-Ukraine has historical causes, but invasion and sovereignty concerns cannot be ignored. OBOR offers connectivity for some countries, but India's sovereignty and strategic concerns are serious. Balanced judgement is the key to marks.

### Q5: Discuss the role of WHO during COVID-19. (2020, 12M, 200W)

**Introduction:**
> WHO was the main global health institution during COVID-19, providing guidance, coordination and technical support, though its limitations were also exposed.

**Positive Role:**
- **Emergency Declaration** — WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern.
- **Technical Guidance** — It issued advisories on testing, masks, treatment and surveillance.
- **Information Platform** — It maintained global data and risk communication.
- **Research Support** — Solidarity Trial supported treatment research.
- **Vaccine Equity** — WHO supported COVAX with partners for vaccine access.
- **Capacity Support** — It helped weaker health systems with technical assistance.
- **Norm Setting** — It coordinated global health standards and terminology.

**Criticism:**
- **Delayed Response Charge** — Early warning and transparency were questioned.
- **Member-State Dependence** — WHO depends on data supplied by governments.
- **Weak Enforcement** — It cannot compel sovereign states.
- **Funding Dependence** — Earmarked voluntary funds limit flexibility.

**Keywords (underline in exam):** WHO · COVID-19 · PHEIC · Solidarity Trial · COVAX · Vaccine Equity · Global Health Governance

**Exam diagram (30 sec):**
```
WHO role = guidance + data + research + vaccine equity
Limit = enforcement + funding + transparency
```

**Conclusion:**
> WHO was indispensable during COVID, but pandemic governance needs stronger funding, transparency and enforcement mechanisms.

### Q6: USA-Iran tensions: causes, impact on India and response. (2019, 12M, 200W)

- **Strategic Balance** — India must manage US, Iran, Israel and Gulf ties.

**India's Response:**
- **Strategic Autonomy** — Avoid bloc alignment.
- **Diversification** — Use multiple oil suppliers and strategic reserves.
- **Dialogue** — Support de-escalation.
- **Protect Diaspora** — Prepare consular and evacuation mechanisms.

**Keywords (underline in exam):** USA-Iran · JCPOA · Sanctions · Chabahar · Energy Security · Gulf Diaspora · Strategic Autonomy

**Exam diagram (30 sec):**
```
US-Iran tension -> oil + Chabahar + diaspora + shipping
India -> balance + diversify + de-escalate
```

**Conclusion:**
> India should protect its interests through balanced diplomacy and energy resilience.

### Q7: How will withdrawal of U.S. troops from Afghanistan affect India? (2018, 12M, 200W)

- **Counter-Terror Vigilance** — Strengthen intelligence and border security.
