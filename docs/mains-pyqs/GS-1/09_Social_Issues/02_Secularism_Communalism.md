## Content

### Context — Secularism and communalism in India

India's plural religious landscape — Hindu majority (~80%), Muslims (~14%), Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, tribal faiths — was scarred by Partition (1947), born from communal politics, making secular constitutional order existential priority. **Secularism:** state has no official religion, treats all faiths equally, protects religious freedom (Articles 25–28), prohibits discrimination (Article 15). Preamble "Secular" inserted by 42nd Amendment (1976). **Communalism:** political mobilization using religious identity against "the other" — instrumental religion (Bipan Chandra). **Secularisation:** sociological decline of religion's social authority — distinct from secularism as legal principle.

### Indian vs Western secularism

| Feature | Western | Indian |
|---------|---------|--------|
| State-religion | Strict separation | Principled distance plus equal respect |
| Public religion | Often excluded | Festivals, personal laws coexist |
| Philosophy | Anti-clericalism | Sarva dharma sambhava |
| Minority | Individual focus | Group plus individual — Art 30 |

Rajeev Bhargava: contextual secularism — balances liberty, equality, fraternity in engaged state.

### Communalism — Bipan Chandra stages

Literature/ideology → movements (cow riots 1890s) → organizations (RSS 1925, Muslim League 1906) → mass politics and violence (Partition, post-Independence riots). Modern drivers: vote-bank politics, economic competition, social media hate, weak riot deterrence.

### Nation, citizenship, and communalism

Nation is territorial-civic — Art 1 Union of States, Preamble sovereignty — not ethno-religious European nation-state. Citizenship (Part II, Art 5–11): jus soli in Citizenship Act 1955, single citizenship, Art 15 equality. Communal nationalism defines nation by religion — excludes minorities psychologically. CAA 2019 debates in Assam/Northeast refract citizenship through ethnic lens. Ambedkar's constitutional morality requires citizenship detached from majoritarianism.

### Communal violence — Paul Brass chain

Examples: 1984 Delhi; 1992–93 Babri/Mumbai; 2002 Gujarat; 2013 Muzaffarnagar fake video; 2020 Delhi CAA clashes. NHRC, SITs, Sachar Committee on Muslim marginalization.

### Secularism vs secularisation politics — 2019 statement

Partial truth: violence peaks at secular boundary disputes (UCC, CAA, temple-state); orthodox leadership may react to secularisation (Engineer). Against: communal violence opposes secularism — Brass shows elite competition primary; secularisation incomplete — high religiosity; NE ethnic conflicts non-religious. Verdict: secularism/secularisation are contested terrains; violence serves power using sacred symbols.

### Secularism indispensable for liberal democracy

Art 14–15 equal citizenship; Art 29–30 minority protection; rule of law; free speech; courts adjudicate triple talaq, hate speech neutrally. Practices: no state religion; multi-faith holidays; personal law pluralism with UCC debate. Without secular orientation: majoritarian tyranny — illiberal ethnic democracy. Communal conflict deters investment and social trust.

### Communalism vs composite culture

Bhakti-Sufi, Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb (UP), Guru Nanak, Gandhi's prayer meetings — anti-communal resources. Freedom Movement united communities against divide-and-rule. Festivals in mixed localities — everyday secular practice.

### Institutional safeguards

Art 15, 25–28; NCM (1992); NHRC; Election Model Code; SC hate speech rulings; fast-track riot courts.

### Communalism vs faith vs secularism — distinctions

Communalism = political weapon (Bipan Chandra). Faith = personal devotion. Secularism = state equal treatment. Faith ≠ communalism; secularism ≠ anti-God.

### Indian secularism in practice — orientation and practices

Secularism in India operates as both constitutional orientation and daily practice. Orientation: state derives authority from Constitution, not scripture; public officials swear allegiance to Constitution; courts adjudicate religious disputes with reference to fundamental rights, not fatwa or shastra alone. Practices: no official state religion; government grants for pilgrim infrastructure extend across faiths (debated as equal treatment or pseudo-secularism); personal law pluralism allows Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Parsi codes — negotiated diversity with ongoing Uniform Civil Code debate (Article 44 DPSP). Festival holidays for Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Gurpurab in public calendars reflect multi-faith public culture. Supreme Court interventions — Shayara Bano (triple talaq), Sabarimala entry, Section 377 decriminalization — demonstrate secular adjudication mediating tradition versus rights.

Gandhi's sarva dharma sambhava (equal respect for all faiths) and Nehru's scientific temper represent complementary secular ideals — not hostile to religion but opposed to state favouring one faith. Ambedkar's constitutional morality insists fraternity (Preamble) requires equal citizenship psychologically, not merely on paper — communalism violates this by making minority feel suspect in their own country.

### Communal violence — production model and prevention

Paul Brass's "production of riot" thesis is essential: riots require preparation (hate speech, rumour), activation (trigger incident), organization (political protection), and benefit (electoral polarisation, property grab). Asghar Ali Engineer documented economic competition beneath religious rhetoric — shopkeepers, land mafia, political contractors profit from violence. V.N. Rao and subsequent commissions recommended fast-track courts, victim compensation, police neutrality, and hate speech curbs — prevention requires deterrence, not only post-riot relief.

1984 Delhi (Sikh pogrom after Indira Gandhi assassination), 1992–93 (Babri demolition and Mumbai riots), 2002 Gujarat (Godhra train fire aftermath), 2013 Muzaffarnagar (fake video in election season), 2020 Delhi (CAA protest clashes) — each shows chain of instigation, anti-social trigger, political support, vested finance — not spontaneous religious eruption. Comment on 2018 quote: substantially agree with nuance that mass fear and historical grievance also present — not only conspiracy.

### Religion, ethnic violence, and secularism politics (2019 statement unpacked)

Ethnic violence in NE (tribe, land, language) falls outside narrow religious frame. Balanced verdict: secularism and secularisation provide contested language and timing; root drivers remain power, property, and patronage.

### Nation and citizenship — expanded critical examination

The Indian nation in constitutional theory is territorial and civic — citizens of the Union of States bound by common Constitution, not by religious ancestry. Citizenship Act 1955 operationalizes jus soli and descent rules; Article 15 prohibits religion-based discrimination by state; single citizenship prevents sub-national citizenship that could fragment loyalty. Communalism distorts this by implying Hindus alone are true nationals, or that Muslims are inherently suspect — psychological second-class citizenship even when law guarantees equality. Partition memory intensifies mutual fear encoded in ghettoization and electoral polarisation. CAA 2019 debate illustrates friction between persecuted-minority protection narrative and Assamese demographic anxiety and Muslim citizen exclusion fears. Ambedkar's constitutional morality requires fraternity in Preamble — without it formal citizenship becomes hollow. Critical verdict: nation-citizenship framework is anti-communal by design; communal politics subverts it existentially.

### Secularism indispensable — expanded discussion

Liberal democracy requires no citizen fear state because of faith, minorities participate fully in public life, and law adjudicates without religious bias. Without secular orientation, majoritarian democracy becomes tyranny of majority. Secular practices: constitutional oath; multi-faith calendar; personal law pluralism with UCC debate; courts on triple talaq and Sabarimala. Economic and soft power arguments: communal violence deters investment and tourism; pluralist credentials support diaspora and G20 leadership. Election Commission Model Code restricts communal appeals during polls — secular practice in electoral process. Fast-track riot courts and NHRC investigations attempt deterrence against engineered violence — institutional enforcement separates constitutional promise from social reality.

### Limits and balanced view

Indian secularism messy — pilgrim subsidies debated as pseudo-secularism. Secularism ≠ anti-religion. Communalism affects all religions — Sikh 1984, NE ethnic. Preamble Secular via 1976 not original. Personal laws debated feature of Indian model. UCC not magic bullet. Minority rights (Art 29–30) are liberal requirement. Comment on riot quote: substantially agree with nuance on mass psychology. Faith communities contribute to charity, education, and moral life — secular state protects all without establishing any — distinguishing Indian model from both theocracy and militant atheism. Communal harmony requires proactive governance: riot deterrence, hate speech curbs, minority welfare per Sachar recommendations, and civic education affirming constitutional fraternity — secularism is maintained through daily practice, not merely through constitutional text.
