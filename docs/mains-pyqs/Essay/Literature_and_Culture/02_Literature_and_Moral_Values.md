> Essay · UPPCS 2019 · Section A · 50 marks · 700 words  
> हिंदी शीर्षक: **साहित्य और नैतिक मूल्य**

## Model essay (~700 words)

Moral values are the habits by which a person decides what is due to another: truth, compassion, courage, restraint, justice, fidelity. They are not born fully formed. A child first meets them in a story — a promise kept, a cruelty named, a king who fails. Literature is that first public school of the inner life. Where religion gives commandment and law gives penalty, literature gives *experience*: we inhabit another’s choice and feel its cost.

Indian civilisation has long joined *sahitya* to *niti*. The *Mahabharata* is a war book that will not let victory remain innocent; Yudhishthira’s dice and Krishna’s counsel keep asking what dharma is when duties collide. The *Gita* sits inside a poem, not a statute. **Tulsidas**’s *Ramcharitmanas*, still recited in the towns and villages of Uttar Pradesh, made *maryada* — self-limit in power — a household language. **Kabir** cut through ritual to a severe ethic: do not kill, do not lie, do not fatten on another’s labour. Bhakti did not abolish morals; it democratised them. Awadhi, Braj and the folk *Ramlila* carried values further than any court treatise.

Literature teaches morals without a blackboard. **Aristotle** saw in tragedy a *catharsis*: pity and fear clean the passions. Indian poetics speaks of *rasa* and *karuna*. We do not become just because a novelist scolds us; we become less able to enjoy injustice after we have lived inside Hori’s hut or Draupadi’s insult. **Premchand** understood this. *Kafan* is morally ferocious because it refuses a pious ending; hunger has eaten even funeral dignity. *Nirmala* makes the cruelty of mismatched marriage felt, not footnoted. **Tagore**’s *Gora* and *Home and the World* test patriotism against humanity. **Tolstoy** asked whether art that does not kindle fellow-feeling is art at all. The moral work is in the *seeing*.

This is why crude moralism fails. A pamphlet that announces “be good” is soon unread. Great writing may show vice in full colour — Dostoevsky’s underground man, Manto’s Partition sketches, Chughtai’s stubborn women — not to advertise sin but to refuse a lie. The opposite error is also real. Words that glamourise hatred, caste humiliation or the hunting of a community are not “bold”; they are a failure of value. Freedom of form is not freedom to poison the well. The writer’s *tapas* is accuracy: do not fake the poor, do not prettify the tyrant, do not sell contempt as courage.

Our moment makes the link urgent. Markets reward speed and sensation. Screens compress the person into a target. Hate travels in slogans; advertising treats desire as the only good. If literature becomes only entertainment, moral language thins into exam-phrases: *satya*, *ahimsa*, *seva* recited and not practised. School syllabi that treat the poem as grammar, not as a trial of character, waste the one instrument that can still make a young reader pause. Climate theft, gendered violence, the migrant’s walk of 2020, the lonely elderly — these are moral facts before they are policy files. A literature that cannot feel them has already chosen a value: indifference.

The reader completes the circuit. Books do not inject virtue; they offer a mirror and a neighbour. To read slowly, to allow discomfort, to refuse the cheap thrill of another’s humiliation — that is the citizen’s share. The state may fund libraries and translation; it must not appoint a policeman as the critic. Moral value grows in argument, as the epics themselves argue.

Literature and moral values, then, are not two subjects glued by a conjunction. Literature is how a people rehearse being human. It cannot replace the law or the temple, but without it both become hollow. The test of a book is not how loudly it praises goodness, but whether, after we close it, cruelty is a little harder to defend.

## Opening variants (pick one)

1. **Definitional:** Moral values are how we treat the person who cannot pay us back. Literature is the memory of such treatments.
2. **Image:** Before a child knows the word *dharma*, she knows the story in which the weak were not left alone.
3. **Contrast:** Law punishes after the act; literature works on the wish before it.

## Closing variants (pick one)

1. A nation of laws still needs a literature of conscience. The second keeps the first from becoming cruel.
2. Preach less, portray more: that is how literature serves morals without becoming a tract.
3. Close the book. If the face of the stranger is still a blur, the values did not arrive.
