## Model essay (~700 words)

The 2021 Hindi title is exacting: *kala aur samajik mukti*. It is not a request to praise galleries. *Mukti* in the Indian tongue is release — from fear, caste insult, gendered silence, colonial shame, the inner policeman. Art is one of the few public crafts that can rehearse that release before the law arrives. A society may print “liberty” on its coins and still live unfree if it cannot sing, paint, stage or narrate the wound.

Social unfreedom first attacks the voice. The unheard are told their speech is crude, their gods unofficial, their grief unliterary. Bhakti answered by stealing the high metres for the weaver and the wanderer. **Kabir**’s *ulta bhasha* is artistic form as social insolence: the priest is no longer the only licensed speaker. **Meera** leaving the palace for the street is a woman’s body claiming public song. Folk *Nautanki*, *Ramlila* and *Alha* in the Hindi belt were not “culture shows”; they were how a village practised being a public. Art here is not luxury after bread. It is how bread-eaters remember they have a face.

The modern period sharpened the same edge. **Premchand** did not free the peasant by a manifesto; he made Hori unforgettable, which is a political fact. The **IPTA** theatre, **Safdar Hashmi**’s street play, **Faiz**’s *Bol, ki lab azad hain tere* — these treat the stage and the ghazal as instruments of *mukti*. **Tagore** feared a nationalism that would cage the mind; that is liberty as inner air, not only a flag. Dalit painting, autobiography and poetry — from **Omprakash Valmiki** to contemporary galleries of protest — insist that beauty was long a gated park. **Streevadi** writing and performance do the same for the female body as subject, not ornament. Ambedkar’s insistence on the right to appear in the public square has an aesthetic twin: the right to be *represented* without pity or joke.

None of this survives if art itself is unfree. A censored theatre cannot emancipate a censored people. Article **19(1)(a)** is therefore not a hobby-clause for novelists; it is a social-liberty clause. Emergency silences, colonial sedition nets, mob vetoes on films and paintings, and the new veto of the coordinated troll — all shrink the space in which a society can imagine itself otherwise. **M.F. Husain** driven into exile, books withdrawn under threat, campus plays cancelled: these are not quarrels about taste. They are tests of whether *mukti* is only for those who already sit inside the consensus.

Liberty of art, however, is not the liberty to hunt. Hate masquerading as “bold expression”, caste humiliation sold as comedy, and the sexualised display of the powerless are not emancipation. Article **19(2)** exists because one person’s shout can become another’s unfreedom. The adult distinction is this: the state and the mob must not appoint themselves chief critic; the critic, the reader and the open court of argument must. Propaganda in the name of the people is also unfreedom: it uses art as a whip. *Mukti* needs a stubborn middle — form free, target not the weak.

The market adds a quieter cage. If only what sells is made, the poor appear as villains or mascots. If English prestige decides what is “global art”, Awadhi and Bhojpuri remain folklore. Social liberty in art includes the liberty of Indian languages and of the amateur choir, the school play, the village *akhara*. During the 2020–21 isolation, songs and small videos carried grief across locked doors: proof that people reach for art when other liberties shrink.

So art and social liberty are a double lock. Without free art, social *mukti* has no language. Without a hunger for others’ *mukti*, art becomes a fireworks show for the already free. A republic is not finished when it counts votes; it is unfinished until the last person may tell her story without asking permission of fear.

## Opening variants (pick one)

1. **Split the title:** *Mukti* is social before it is aesthetic. Art is one of its languages.
2. **Image:** A locked throat can still vote. That is why art is not optional to liberty.
3. **Contrast:** Licence shocks. Liberty frees. The essay is about the second.

## Closing variants (pick one)

1. Count the silences in a city: that is the real index of its art and of its freedom.
2. Let the weak speak in colour, metre and joke. That is *samajik mukti* with a tune.
3. Two keys, one lock: the artist’s courage and the citizen’s right to hear.
