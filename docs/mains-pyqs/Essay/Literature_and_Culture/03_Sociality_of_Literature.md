## Model essay (~700 words)

“Sociality of literature” is easy to mishear as another lecture on the writer’s duty. The 2020 wording is sharper. It asks: in what sense is literature *itself* a social thing? A poem is not a lonely lightning in a private skull. It uses a tongue that others already speak; it borrows metres, myths and insults that a people have stored; it waits for a listener. No audience, no literature — only a notebook.

Language is the first proof. Hindi, Awadhi, Braj, Urdu and English are not neutral tools. They carry hierarchy and intimacy: who may say *tu*, who must say *aap*; which pain has a respectable vocabulary and which is called slang. When **Tulsidas** chose Awadhi, he did not merely “simplify” Rama; he relocated Rama into a social world of the Hindi belt. When **Kabir** mixed the bazaar’s speech with the saint’s, he made a public of weavers and wanderers. Sociality begins in the word.

The writer, too, writes from a body in history. Caste, gender, region, class and faith are not extra-literary gossip; they are the angle of vision. **Premchand** could see Hori because he stood near the indebted village, not above it as a tourist of poverty. **Phanishwar Nath Renu**’s *Maila Anchal* is sociality as *anchal*: a district’s festivals, rumours and elections become form. **Omprakash Valmiki**’s *Joothan* shows that “universal humanism” often hid a caste address. **Ismat Chughtai** and **Mahasweta Devi** made the woman’s and the tribal’s speech literary, which is a social act before it is a moral one. The lonely genius is a late myth. Even the hermit-poet eats grain grown by someone else and quotes a tradition he did not invent.

Reception is the third social fact. Indian *rasa* theory already assumes a *sahridaya* — a heart capable of answering. The *Ramlila* of Ramnagar is literature as a city-event: text, crowd, season and power meet. Print created a new public in the nineteenth century; the serial novel taught strangers to feel together. Translation is sociality across borders: a Malayalam novel entering Hindi does not lose society; it joins two. Today the screen multiplies readers and also fragments them into bubbles. A viral couplet may reach crores and still fail to become a conversation. Sociality is not mere numbers; it is *shared meaning*.

Does this mean literature is only a mirror? No. A mirror that cannot imagine is a dead glass. Literature is also society dreaming — of a less cruel order, of a forbidden love, of a river that is not a drain. **Tagore** warned that the nation can become a cage; that warning is social imagination, not minutes of a meeting. Science fiction, fantasy and *bhakti* vision are social too: they show what a community can bear to hope. Crude “reflection theory” — that the novel is a photocopy of the economy — insults both art and society. Relative autonomy is real: a great line can outlive the class that paid for the ink.

The honest limit must be stated. Much of India still lives outside the literary public. Literacy, price of books, English prestige and caste comfort decide who is heard. During the migrant walk of 2020, poetry and testimony circulated on phones even as many walkers had no time to read. Sociality that congratulates itself in seminars, while the field remains unread, is a small club calling itself the world. The remedy is not guilt; it is libraries, school reading as pleasure, Indian-language publishing, and the humility to listen to oral forms — *Alha*, *birha*, *qawwali* — as literature, not as “raw material”.

So the sociality of literature is fourfold: it is social in language, in the located writer, in the receiving public, and in the imagined “we” it tries to create. To deny this is to treat art as perfume. To flatten it into propaganda is to treat readers as troops. Between those errors lies the living work: a person speaking in a borrowed tongue to strangers, hoping they will answer.

## Opening variants (pick one)

1. **Distinction:** Duty asks what literature *should do*. Sociality asks what literature *is*. It is a relation, not a luxury.
2. **Image:** A poem unused is a letter without an address. The address is society.
3. **Language:** Before the writer chooses a theme, the mother tongue has already chosen a world.

## Closing variants (pick one)

1. Literature is society learning to speak to itself without a whip.
2. Where there is no *sahridaya*, there is only noise. Sociality is the answering heart.
3. Write as if someone hungry might overhear you. That “someone” is the social.
