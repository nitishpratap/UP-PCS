## Model essay (~700 words)

Literature is language given a durable form: poem, story, play, novel, essay. It entertains, but that is not its whole work. A society that treats literature as decoration weakens its own memory and its own conscience. The social responsibility of literature is to keep human experience visible, especially where power would prefer silence.

India has never treated the word as a private luxury. The *Ramayana* and the *Mahabharata* are not only sacred narratives; they are public debates on dharma, kingship, war and pity. Bhakti poets such as **Kabir**, **Tulsidas**, **Surdas** and **Meera** used the vernacular so that ethics could leave the court and enter the street. Folk forms — *Alha*, *Nautanki*, *Ramlila*, Awadhi *birha* — carried the same duty in performance. Literature first preserves a people’s inner history: how they loved, feared, worshipped and resisted.

Its second duty is to hold a mirror without flattery. **Munshi Premchand**, writing from the Hindi-Urdu heartland, made the peasant, the woman and the indebted farmer the centre of the novel. *Godan* is not a pastoral postcard of rural Uttar Pradesh; it is a record of *begar*, caste insult, moneylender and the last, tragic wish for a cow. **Charles Dickens** did similar work for industrial England. When literature refuses this seeing, the poor become statistics and the powerful become legends.

The third duty is to enlarge sympathy until it becomes a force for reform. **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee**’s *Anandamath* and *Vande Mataram* gave anti-colonial feeling a song. **Rabindranath Tagore** warned that nationalism without humanism becomes a new prison. Progressive writers — **Nirala**, **Muktibodh**, **Faiz Ahmed Faiz** — treated the poem as a public square. Later, **Dalit** writing such as Omprakash Valmiki’s *Joothan* and **Streevadi** writing such as Mahasweta Devi’s stories did what polite literature had postponed: they named humiliation in the first person. Harriet Beecher Stowe’s *Uncle Tom’s Cabin* and Tolstoy’s moral fiction show that this is not only an Indian claim. Sartre called such work *literature engagée*: writing that accepts it already acts in the world.

Responsibility, however, is not the same as a party pamphlet. Literature that only shouts a programme soon dies, because it has no inner life. **Art for art’s sake** is a useful protest against crude propaganda, but it cannot be the last word in a country of hunger and hurt. The writer’s ethics lie in a hard middle: freedom of form, honesty of fact, refusal to spread hatred, stereotype or cheap communal fire. Plato feared poets because they move the city; that fear is the proof of literature’s power. Power unused for dignity is waste; power used for poison is crime.

The present adds new tests. Screens multiply words and thin out attention. Hate speech travels faster than a novel. Advertisement language sells desire as meaning. In such a climate, literature’s social duty is to restore slow seeing: the farmer’s debt after a failed monsoon, the migrant worker of 2020, the girl kept from school, the river turned into a drain. Climate, caste atrocity, mental loneliness and the dignity of labour are not “extra themes”; they are the matter of any serious Hindi, Urdu or English writing today. Translation is also a duty: it lets a Bhojpuri pain speak to a Malayalam ear, and an African novel speak to an Indian classroom.

The reader is not innocent. To buy only sensation and ignore difficult books is to vote against literature’s public role. Schools that teach poetry as grammar, not as life, also fail. A republic of equal citizens needs citizens who can imagine another person’s interior.

Thus the social responsibilities of literature are fourfold: to remember, to reveal, to reform without becoming a slogan, and to keep language clean of lies. A civilisation is known by the stories it refuses to forget and the silences it refuses to protect. Literature is that refusal, written so that it can be shared.

## Opening variants (pick one)

1. **Definitional:** Literature is society thinking aloud. If that thinking is cowardly, the society soon becomes so.
2. **Anecdote:** Valmiki’s first *shloka* is said to have been born from *karuna* at a bird’s killing. Indian poetry begins as a protest against needless hurt.
3. **Contrast:** A slogan commands; a story persuades. Responsibility lives in the second.

## Closing variants (pick one)

1. Literature cannot pass a law, but it can make an unjust law feel unbearable. That is its republic.
2. The writer owes beauty to the page and justice to the street. Drop either, and the work is incomplete.
3. In an age of noise, the social duty of literature is to restore the human face to the statistic.

## Traps

| Trap | Better move |
|------|-------------|
| Only Western names (Shakespeare, Dickens). | Lead with Premchand, Kabir, Tagore, Dalit-Stree writing. |
| Turning the essay into a speech against cinema/WhatsApp. | One line on digital noise is enough. |
| Listing 20 books. | 5–6 named works, each doing a job. |
| “Literature has no duty, only art.” | Acknowledge freedom, then show why silence helps the strong. |
| “Literature must preach.” | Distinguish art from pamphlet. |
| Ignoring UP. | Premchand (Lamhi), Ramcharitmanas, folk theatre. |
| No conclusion on the **reader**. | Duty is shared: writer, teacher, reader, state (no censorship as virtue-signalling). |
| Crossing 700 words with history dump. | Four duties + one limit + today + close. |
