## 1. Oceans — Basic Facts

| Fact | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Largest ocean | **Pacific** |
| Smallest ocean | **Arctic** |
| Deepest ocean | **Pacific** |
| Deepest point | **Challenger Deep** |
| Challenger Deep location | **Mariana Trench, Pacific** |

**Order by area:** Pacific → Atlantic → Indian → Southern → Arctic

## 2. Pacific Ocean

Largest and deepest ocean.

Important features:

> **Mariana Trench**

> **Ring of Fire**

> **East Pacific Rise**

> **Hawaii**

> **Pacific → Largest + deepest**

> **Mariana → Deepest oceanic trench**

> **Ring of Fire → Pacific margins**

## 3. Atlantic Ocean

The **Atlantic Ocean** is defined by the **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**, **North Atlantic Drift**, **Gulf Stream**, **Sargasso Sea**, and the **Bermuda region**.

> **Atlantic → Mid-Atlantic Ridge**

## 4. Indian Ocean

The **Indian Ocean** is monsoon-dominated, with the **Arabian Sea**, **Bay of Bengal**, reversing **currents**, **IOD**, and major **island groups**.

Marginal seas include the **Arabian Sea**, **Bay of Bengal**, **Andaman Sea**, and **Red Sea**.

## 5. Southern Ocean

The **Southern Ocean** surrounds **Antarctica**.

The **Antarctic Circumpolar Current** (West Wind Drift) flows **west to east** around the continent.

> **Southern Ocean → Antarctica**

## 6. Arctic Ocean

The **Arctic Ocean** is the **smallest ocean**, centred on the **North Pole**.

Marginal seas include **Beaufort**, **Barents**, **Kara**, **Laptev**, **East Siberian**, and **Chukchi**.

> **Arctic → smallest**

## 7. Ocean Floor

Major ocean-floor divisions from land to deep ocean:

> **Continental shelf → continental slope → continental rise → abyssal plain → mid-ocean ridge → ocean trench**

## 8. Continental Shelf

The **continental shelf** is the gently sloping submerged extension of a continent.

Depth is generally **up to ~200 m**. It is shallow, biologically productive, and rich in **fisheries**, **petroleum**, and **natural gas**.

> **Shelf → shallow + resource-rich**

## 9. Continental Slope

The **continental slope** begins near the **edge of the continental shelf**.

It is steeper than the shelf and connects the **shelf to the deep ocean floor**.

## 10. Continental Rise

The **continental rise** lies at the base of the continental slope and is built by **sediment accumulation**.

> **Shelf → Slope → Rise → Abyssal plain**

## 11. Abyssal Plain

The **abyssal plain** is a broad, extremely flat deep-ocean floor at depths commonly **~3,000–6,000 m**.

> **Abyssal plain = very flat deep ocean floor**

## 12. Mid-Ocean Ridge

A **mid-ocean ridge** is a long underwater mountain system at a **divergent plate boundary** where magma rises and forms new oceanic crust.

Examples: **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**, **Central Indian Ridge**, **East Pacific Rise**.

> **Mid-ocean ridge → divergence → new crust**

## 13. Ocean Trench

An **ocean trench** is a long, narrow, very deep depression formed at a **subduction / convergent boundary**.

Examples: **Mariana**, **Java/Sunda**, **Peru–Chile**, **Tonga**.

## 14. Mariana Trench

The **Mariana Trench** lies in the **western Pacific** and contains **Challenger Deep** — the deepest known point on Earth.

> **Challenger Deep → Mariana Trench, Pacific → deepest point on Earth**

## 15. Seamount

A **seamount** is an underwater mountain rising from the ocean floor that does **not reach the surface**.

> **Seamount = submarine mountain**

## 16. Guyot

A **guyot** is a flat-topped submerged seamount formed when a volcanic island is eroded near sea level and then subsides below the surface.

> **Guyot → flat-topped seamount**

## 17. Oceanic Island

**Oceanic islands** form mainly by **volcanism / hotspot** activity.

Examples include **Hawaii** and **Iceland**. Iceland also lies across the **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**.

## 18. Coral Island

**Coral islands** often develop around volcanic islands.

The classic reef sequence is:

> **Fringing reef → Barrier reef → Atoll**

## 19. Coral Reefs

**Coral reefs** are built by **coral polyps**.

They need warm water (~**20–30°C**), shallow depth, sunlight, clear water, low sediment, and normal salinity.

> **Coral → warm + shallow + clear + sunlight**

## 20. Fringing Reef

**Immediately adjacent to coast/island**
> **Fringing → coast**

## 21. Barrier Reef

**Separated from coast by lagoon**
> **Great Barrier Reef → Australia**
> **Barrier reef → lagoon between reef and coast**

## 22. Atoll

Ring-shaped reef enclosing lagoon.
subsiding volcanic island.
> **Atoll → ring reef + central lagoon**

## 23. Coral Reef Sequence

> Darwin subsidence model: volcanic island → fringing reef → barrier reef → atoll
> **Fringing → Barrier → Atoll**

## 24. Ocean Temperature

Varies with latitude, depth, season, currents, winds, upwelling, enclosed seas.
> **Surface temperature decreases Equator → Poles**

## 25. Vertical Temperature Structure

Surface mixed layer.
thermocline.
deep ocean.

## 26. Thermocline

Zone of **rapid temperature decrease with depth**.
strongest in tropical/subtropical.
weak/absent in high-latitude cold waters.

## 27. Deep Ocean

Below thermocline temperature changes slowly.
deep water **generally cold + dense**.

## 28. Ocean Salinity

Average open-ocean salinity **~35‰ (35 PSU)**.
controls: evaporation.
precipitation.
river discharge.
sea-ice freezing/melting.
ocean circulation.

## 29. High Salinity

Where **evaporation > precipitation**.
subtropical regions.
enclosed seas especially high.
> **High evaporation → high salinity**

## 30. Low Salinity

Where **precipitation + river inflow > evaporation**.
high latitudes.
near large river mouths.
> **River discharge → lower salinity**

## 31. Sea-Ice Formation & Salinity

> Freezing: most salt stays in surrounding water → **salinity ↑**
> melting: freshwater enters → **salinity ↓**

## 32. Ocean Density

Density ↑ with salinity.
density ↑ as temperature ↓.
> **Cold + salty → dense → sinks** → thermohaline circulation

## 33. Thermohaline Circulation

> Driven by **temperature + salinity → density**
Thermo = temperature.
Haline = salinity.
**Density-driven ocean circulation**

## 39. North Atlantic Drift

## 51. Upwelling

divergence.
winds.

> **Nutrients ↑ → phytoplankton ↑ → fisheries ↑**

## 55. Waves

Oscillatory water movement.
wind-generated at surface.
deep water: particles move in **orbits**, not forward with wave.

## 56. Wave Parts

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Crest | Highest point |
| Trough | Lowest point |
| Wavelength | Crest-to-crest |
| Wave height | Crest-to-trough vertical |
| Wave period | Time between crests |

## 57. Tsunami

Usually **undersea earthquake + seafloor displacement**.
also landslide/volcanic eruption.
deep ocean: fast + low height.
near coast: slows + height increases.
> **Tsunami → seafloor displacement**

## 58. Tides

Periodic sea-level rise/fall.
primarily **Moon's gravity + Sun's gravity**.
Moon dominant (closer).

## 59. Spring Tide

Sun + Earth + Moon **aligned**.
**New Moon + Full Moon**
**Highest tidal range**
> **Spring tide → New + Full Moon**

## 60. Neap Tide

Sun + Earth + Moon at **right angles**.
**First + Third Quarter**
**Lowest tidal range**
> **Neap → Quarter Moon**

## 61. Spring vs Neap

| Spring | Neap |
| --- | --- |
| New + Full Moon | Quarter Moon |
| Sun & Moon aligned | Sun & Moon at ~90° |
| Maximum range | Minimum range |
| Higher high / lower low | Lower tidal range |

## 62. Tidal Types

| Type | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Diurnal | 1 high + 1 low per lunar day |
| Semidiurnal | 2 high + 2 low per lunar day |
| Mixed | 2 high + 2 low, unequal heights |

## 63. Tidal Bore

## 67. Western vs Eastern Boundary Currents

| Boundary | Character | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |

## 68. El Niño

**Abnormal warming** of central/eastern tropical Pacific.
trade winds weaken.
warm water shifts east.
Walker circulation weakens/changes.

## 69. La Niña

**Abnormally cool** central/eastern tropical Pacific.
trade winds strengthen.
eastern upwelling strengthens.
> **El Niño → warm east
> La Niña → cool east**

## 70. Normal Pacific vs El Niño

| Normal | El Niño |
| --- | --- |
| Strong trades | Weaker trades |
| Warm water piled west | Warm water shifts east |
| Strong eastern upwelling | Reduced eastern upwelling |
| Cool eastern Pacific | Warmer eastern Pacific |
| Stronger Walker circulation | Weaker/altered Walker |

## 71. Indian Ocean Dipole

IOD = SST difference western vs eastern tropical Indian Ocean.
**Positive:** west warm, east cool.
**Negative:** west cool, east warm.
**Positive = West Warm.
Negative = East Warm**.
