## 2. 🔄 Rotation of Earth

### Rotation = Earth spins on its own axis

**Direction:** West → East

Therefore:

> **Sun appears to rise in East and set in West.**

### Effects of rotation

| Effect | Ratta |
| --- | --- |
| Day & night | Rotation |
| Apparent daily movement of Sun | Rotation |
| Difference in local time | Rotation + longitude |
| Coriolis force | Rotation |
| Flattening at poles | Rotation |
| Bulging at equator | Rotation |

### 🔴 Coriolis Force

| Hemisphere | Deflection |
| --- | --- |
| Northern Hemisphere | **Right** |
| Southern Hemisphere | **Left** |
| Equator | Zero |

**Mnemonic:**

> **N → Right | S → Left**

### Coriolis force is maximum

> **At the poles**

### Coriolis force is zero

> **At the Equator**

### Important trap

Coriolis force:

* does **not** create wind
* does **not** determine wind speed
* changes the **direction** of moving air/water.

## 3. ☀️ Revolution of Earth

Earth revolves around the Sun in an **elliptical orbit**.

### Important dates

| Date | Event | Sun overhead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **21 March** | March Equinox | Equator |
| **21 June** | Summer Solstice | Tropic of Cancer |
| **23 September** | September Equinox | Equator |
| **22 December** | Winter Solstice | Tropic of Capricorn |

Dates can vary by a day depending on the year.

### 🔴 Equinox

**March + September**

> Day ≈ Night throughout the Earth.

### 🔴 Solstice

**June + December**

> Maximum seasonal contrast between hemispheres.

## 4. 🌞 Perihelion & Aphelion

| Term | Meaning | Approx. date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Perihelion** | Earth closest to Sun | **3 January** |
| **Aphelion** | Earth farthest from Sun | **4 July** |

### ⚠️ Common trap

**Seasons are NOT caused by Earth's changing distance from the Sun.**

Main cause:

> **23½° axial tilt + revolution around Sun**

## 5. 🌐 Latitude

Latitude = angular distance **north or south of Equator**.

Measured:

> **0°–90° N/S**

### Important parallels

| Latitude | Name |
| ---: | --- |
| 0° | Equator |
| 23½° N | **Tropic of Cancer** |
| 23½° S | **Tropic of Capricorn** |
| 66½° N | **Arctic Circle** |
| 66½° S | **Antarctic Circle** |
| 90° N | North Pole |
| 90° S | South Pole |

### 🔴 Indian relevance

**Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 Indian states:**

> Gujarat → Rajasthan → Madhya Pradesh → Chhattisgarh → Jharkhand → West Bengal → Tripura → Mizoram

### 🧠 Ratta

> **Guj-Raj-MP-Chha-Jha-WB-Tri-Mizo**

## 6. 🌍 Longitude

Longitude = angular distance **east or west of Prime Meridian**.

Range:

> **0°–180° E/W**

### Prime Meridian

> **0° longitude**

Passes through:

> **Greenwich, London**

### International Date Line

Approximately:

> **180° longitude**

But it is **not a perfectly straight line**; it deviates to avoid dividing countries/islands.

---

## 7. ⏰ Time & Longitude

Earth rotates:

> **360° in 24 hours**

Therefore:

> **15° = 1 hour**

> **1° = 4 minutes**

> **1′ longitude = 4 seconds**

### 🔴 Golden Formula

> **Time difference = Longitude difference × 4 minutes**

### Example

Delhi and a place differ by 10° longitude.

> 10 × 4 = **40 minutes**

## 8. 🇮🇳 Indian Standard Time

Indian Standard Meridian:

> **82°30′ E**

Passes near:

> **Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh**

IST:

> **UTC/GMT + 5 hours 30 minutes**

### 🔴 Must ratta

> **82°30′ E → IST → Mirzapur, UP**

### ⚠️ Trap

India's standard meridian is **82°30′ E**, NOT 82° E.

## 9. 🌐 Important Latitude Facts

### Equator

* 0°
* divides Earth into Northern and Southern Hemispheres
* longest latitude
* great circle

### All parallels of latitude

* run east-west
* are parallel to one another
* never meet
* decrease in length toward poles.

### 🔴 Important

> **Equator is the only latitude that is also a great circle among the parallels.**

## 10. 🌍 Great Circle

A great circle:

> Divides Earth into two equal halves.

Examples:

* Equator
* Any meridian + its opposite meridian

### ⚠️ Trap

**All meridians individually are not complete great circles.**

A meridian combined with its opposite meridian forms a great circle.

## 15. 🌍 Earth's Interior

Earth is broadly divided into:

> **Crust → Mantle → Core**

Evidence about Earth's interior comes especially from **earthquake waves, gravity, heat flow, magnetic field and laboratory studies**.

---

## 16. 🥚 Crust

Outermost layer.

### Two major types

| Type | Oceanic crust | Continental crust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thickness | Thinner | Thicker |
| Composition | Basaltic | More granitic |
| Density | Higher | Lower |
| Age | Generally younger | Generally older |
| Main rocks | Basalt | Granite |

### 🔴 Ratta

> **Oceanic → thin + dense + basaltic**

> **Continental → thick + less dense + granitic**

---

## 17. 🌋 Mantle

Extends from:

> Moho → ~2,900 km depth

Composition dominated by:

> **Silicate minerals rich in Mg and Fe**

Mantle is predominantly solid but can flow extremely slowly over geological time.

---

## 18. 🔥 Core

Mainly:

> **Iron + Nickel**

Two parts:

| Layer | State |
| --- | --- |
| Outer core | **Liquid** |
| Inner core | **Solid** |

### 🔴 Why outer core is liquid?

S-waves do not pass through it.

### 🔴 Why inner core is solid?

Extremely high pressure maintains solid state despite high temperature.

### 🧠 Golden association

> **S-wave → cannot pass through liquid**

> **Outer core → liquid**

---

## 19. ⚡ Discontinuities

| Discontinuity | Separates |
| --- | --- |
| **Conrad** | Upper & lower continental crust |
| **Moho / Mohorovičić** | Crust & mantle |
| **Gutenberg** | Mantle & outer core |
| **Lehmann** | Outer core & inner core |

### 🔴 Most important

> **Moho → Crust/Mantle**

> **Gutenberg → Mantle/Core**

> **Lehmann → Outer Core/Inner Core**

---

## 20. 🪨 Lithosphere & Asthenosphere

### Lithosphere

> **Crust + uppermost rigid mantle**

Characteristics:

* rigid
* brittle
* broken into tectonic plates

### Asthenosphere

> Weak/ductile zone below lithosphere

Characteristics:

* hotter
* plastic/ductile
* allows plate movement

### 🔴 Trap

> **Lithosphere ≠ crust**

Lithosphere includes:

> **Crust + uppermost mantle**

## 21. 🪨 Rocks

Three major rock types:

> **Igneous → Sedimentary → Metamorphic**

---

## 22. 🔥 Igneous Rocks

Formed by:

> **Cooling and solidification of magma/lava**

### Intrusive

Magma cools:

> **Below Earth's surface**

Cooling:

> Slow

Crystals:

> Large

Examples:

> **Granite, Gabbro**

### Extrusive

Lava cools:

> **At/on Earth's surface**

Cooling:

> Rapid

Crystals:

> Small

Examples:

> **Basalt, Pumice, Obsidian**

### 🔴 Ratta

> **Granite → Intrusive**

> **Basalt → Extrusive**

---

## 23. 🪨 Sedimentary Rocks

Form through:

> Deposition → compaction → cementation

Characteristics:

* generally layered
* fossils commonly found
* often porous

Examples:

> **Sandstone, Limestone, Shale, Conglomerate**

### 🔴 Fossils

> **Most commonly associated with sedimentary rocks**

### ⚠️ Trap

Metamorphism can destroy fossils.

---

## 24. 🔥 Metamorphic Rocks

Pre-existing rocks altered by:

> **Heat + Pressure**

without complete melting.

| Original | Metamorphic |
| --- | --- |
| Limestone | **Marble** |
| Shale | **Slate** |
| Granite | **Gneiss** |
| Sandstone | **Quartzite** |
| Coal | **Graphite** |

### 🔴 Must ratta

> **Limestone → Marble**

> **Shale → Slate**

> **Sandstone → Quartzite**

> **Granite → Gneiss**

## 25. 🔄 Rock Cycle

> Magma
> ↓ cooling
> **Igneous rock**
> ↓ weathering/erosion/deposition
> **Sedimentary rock**
> ↓ heat + pressure
> **Metamorphic rock**
> ↓ melting
> **Magma**

### ⚠️ Important

Any rock can potentially transform into another rock type under appropriate geological conditions.

## 28. 🌍 Continental Drift

Proposed by:

> **Alfred Wegener**

Year:

> **1912**

Theory:

> Continents were once joined in a supercontinent.

Supercontinent:

> **Pangaea**

Surrounding ocean:

> **Panthalassa**

### Pangaea divided into:

> **Laurasia + Gondwanaland**

---

## 29. 🧩 Wegner's Evidences

### 🔴 Major evidence

**1. Jigsaw fit**

> South America ↔ Africa

**2. Fossils**

> Mesosaurus fossils in South America + Africa

**3. Geological similarity**

Similar rock formations across continents.

**4. Glacial evidence**

Ancient glacial deposits found in present tropical regions.

**5. Coal deposits**

Coal deposits in Antarctica indicate past different climatic conditions.

### ⚠️ Main weakness

Wegener could not satisfactorily explain:

> **What force moved the continents?**

## 30. 🌊 Sea-Floor Spreading

Associated strongly with:

> **Harry Hess**

Process:

> Magma rises at mid-ocean ridges → cools → creates new oceanic crust → older crust moves away.

### 🔴 Key association

> **Mid-Ocean Ridge → Divergent boundary → New crust**

## 31. 🧲 Paleomagnetism

Magnetic minerals preserve information about Earth's magnetic field when rocks form.

### Important evidence

Symmetrical magnetic stripes on either side of mid-ocean ridges:

> **Evidence for sea-floor spreading**

---

## 32. 🌎 Plate Tectonics

Modern theory combines:

> Continental drift + Sea-floor spreading + movement of lithospheric plates

Earth's lithosphere is divided into moving plates. Earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain belts are strongly concentrated around plate boundaries.

---

## 33. 🔴 Three Types of Plate Boundaries

| Boundary | Movement | Main features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Divergent** | Apart | Mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys |
| **Convergent** | Towards | Trenches, volcanoes, mountains |
| **Transform** | Past each other | Faults, earthquakes |

---

## 34. ↔️ Divergent Boundary

Movement:

> **→ ←**

Actually plates move:

> **← →**

Creates:

* new crust
* mid-ocean ridges
* rift valleys

### Examples

> **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**

> **East African Rift**

### 🔴

> **Divergent → Constructional boundary**

Because new crust is produced.

---

## 35. 💥 Convergent Boundary

Movement:

> **→ ←**

Three important combinations:

### Oceanic + Continental

> Oceanic plate subducts

Produces:

> **Volcanic mountains**

Example:

> Andes

### Oceanic + Oceanic

Produces:

> **Island arc + trench**

Example:

> Japan / Aleutians

### Continental + Continental

Neither easily subducts.

Produces:

> **Fold mountains**

Example:

> **Himalayas**

### 🔴

> **India + Eurasia → Himalayas**

---

## 36. 🔄 Transform Boundary

Movement:

> **⇆**

Plates slide horizontally past one another.

Produces:

> **Earthquakes**

Classic example:

> **San Andreas Fault**

### ⚠️ Trap

Transform boundary:

> **Does NOT normally create or destroy crust.**

---

## 48. 🌐 Earthquakes

Earthquake:

> Sudden release of energy within Earth's crust/lithosphere producing seismic waves.

Main cause:

> **Movement along faults / tectonic activity**

### 🔴

> **Focus/Hypocentre → Point inside Earth where earthquake originates**

> **Epicentre → Point on surface directly above focus**

---

## 49. 🌊 Earthquake Waves

### P-waves

> **Primary waves**

Characteristics:

* fastest
* compressional
* travel through solids + liquids + gases

### S-waves

> **Secondary waves**

Characteristics:

* slower than P
* transverse/shear
* travel through solids
* **cannot travel through liquids**

### Surface waves

> Travel along Earth's surface.

Generally:

> **Most destructive**

---

## 50. 🧠 Earthquake Wave Ratta

> **P → Primary → fastest → solid + liquid + gas**

> **S → Secondary → shear → solid only**

> **Surface → surface → highly destructive**

---

## 51. 📏 Earthquake Magnitude vs Intensity

### Magnitude

Measures:

> **Energy released at source**

Common modern measure:

> **Moment Magnitude Scale (Mw)**

### Intensity

Measures:

> **Observed effects/damage at a particular location**

Traditionally:

> **Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI)**

### 🔴

> **Magnitude → earthquake itself**

> **Intensity → effect at a place**

---

## 52. 🌊 Tsunami

Usually caused by:

> **Large undersea earthquake involving sudden vertical displacement of seafloor**

Other possible causes:

* volcanic eruption
* submarine landslide
* rarely meteorite impact.

### ⚠️ Trap

> **Every undersea earthquake does NOT produce a tsunami.**

A major tsunami generally requires significant displacement of the seafloor/water column.

### Endogenic forces

> **Inside Earth**

Examples:

> **At/near Earth's surface**

* weathering

## 57. ⚠️ Most Confusing Facts

### 1. 23½° vs 66½°

> **23½° → Tropics**

> **66½° → Polar Circles**

---

### 2. Rotation vs Revolution

> **Rotation → Day/night**

> **Revolution + tilt → Seasons**

---

### 3. Perihelion vs Aphelion

> **Perihelion → nearest → January**

> **Aphelion → farthest → July**

---

### 4. Focus vs Epicentre

> **Focus → Underground**

> **Epicentre → Surface**

---

### 5. Magnitude vs Intensity

> **Magnitude → Energy released**

> **Intensity → Damage/effects at location**

---

### 6. Magma vs Lava

> **Magma → below**

> **Lava → surface**

---

### 7. Lithosphere vs Crust

> **Crust → one layer**

> **Lithosphere → crust + uppermost mantle**

---

### 8. Weathering vs Erosion

> **Weathering → breakdown in place**

> **Erosion → removal/transport**

---

### 9. Delta vs Estuary

> **Delta → deposition**

> **Estuary → tidal/funnel-shaped river mouth**

---

### 10. Fold vs Fault

> **Fold → bending**

> **Fault → fracture + displacement**
