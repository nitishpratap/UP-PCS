## 1. INDIA'S CLIMATE — BASIC

India has:

> **Tropical Monsoon Climate**

Main characteristic:

> **Seasonal reversal of winds**

Climate is influenced by:

* Latitude
* Himalayas
* Indian Ocean
* Arabian Sea
* Bay of Bengal
* Monsoon winds
* Relief
* Altitude
* Jet streams
* El Niño/La Niña
* Indian Ocean Dipole

### MASTER RATTA

> **India → Tropical Monsoon Climate**

> **Monsoon → Seasonal reversal of winds**

## 2. LATITUDE & INDIAN CLIMATE

India extends approximately:

> **8°4′N → 37°6′N**

Tropic of Cancer:

> **23°30′N**

passes through central India.

Therefore India has:

> **Tropical + subtropical climatic characteristics**

## 3. HIMALAYAS & INDIAN CLIMATE

Himalayas act as:

> **Climatic barrier**

They:

* prevent extremely cold continental air from Central Asia from freely entering northern India
* obstruct moisture-bearing monsoon winds
* promote rainfall through uplift
* help retain monsoonal circulation over the subcontinent.


> **Himalayas → Barrier + monsoon uplift + winter protection**

## 4. INDIAN OCEAN & CLIMATE

India is surrounded by:

> Arabian Sea + Bay of Bengal + Indian Ocean

Ocean influence:

> Moderates temperatures in coastal regions.


> **Coast → Maritime influence**

> **Interior → Continental influence**

## 5. CONTINENTALITY

Interior areas:

> Greater annual temperature range.

Coastal areas:

> Smaller annual temperature range.

Example:

> **Delhi → Greater temperature range**

> **Mumbai → Smaller temperature range**


> **Distance from sea ↑ → Continentality ↑**

## 6. FOUR SEASONS OF INDIA

IMD broadly recognises:

### 1. Cold Weather Season

> **December–February**

### 2. Hot Weather Season

> **March–May**

### 3. Southwest Monsoon Season

> **June–September**

### 4. Retreating/Northeast Monsoon Season

> **October–November**

### MASTER RATTA

> **D-J-F → Cold**

> **M-A-M → Hot**

> **J-J-A-S → SW Monsoon**

> **O-N → Retreating**

## 7. COLD WEATHER SEASON

Period:

> **December–February**

Main features:

* cool temperatures
* generally dry conditions over most of India
* northeast trade winds dominate
* western disturbances affect northwestern India.

## 8. WINTER PRESSURE PATTERN

During winter:

> Land cools rapidly.

Northern India develops:

> **High pressure**

Winds:

> **Northeasterly**

They generally move:

> Land → Ocean

Therefore:

> Most of India is relatively dry.


> **Winter → Land high pressure → NE winds → Dry India**

## 9. WINTER RAINFALL — NORTHWEST INDIA

Main cause:

> **Western Disturbances**

Origin region:

> Mediterranean/West Asian region

Movement:

> West → East

Associated with:

> Subtropical Westerly Jet.


> **Western Disturbance → Winter rain → NW India**

## 10. WESTERN DISTURBANCES & RABI

Winter rainfall is important for:

> **Rabi crops**

Especially:

> **Wheat**

Regions:

> Punjab + Haryana + Rajasthan + western UP + Himalayan region.


> **Western Disturbances → Winter rain → Wheat**

## 11. HOT WEATHER SEASON

Period:

> **March–May**

Temperature:

> Rises progressively.

Highest temperatures:

> Northwestern and central India.

By May:

> Northwestern India can experience very high temperatures.

## 12. LOO

Strong:

> **Hot + dry + gusty wind**

Season:

> **Summer**

Region:

> **Northwestern and northern plains**

Especially:

> Rajasthan + Haryana + Punjab + Delhi + Uttar Pradesh + Bihar.


> **Loo → May/June → Hot + dry → North India**

## 13. PRE-MONSOON THUNDERSTORMS

Common in:

> Eastern and northeastern India

Especially:

> West Bengal + Assam.

Characteristics:

> Short-duration violent thunderstorms.

## 14. KAL BAISAKHI

Also:

> **Nor'westers**

Region:

> **West Bengal + Assam and adjoining areas**

Season:

> Pre-monsoon

Characteristics:

> Violent thunderstorms.

Importance:

> Rainfall useful for some crops.


> **Kal Baisakhi → Bengal → Thunderstorm**

## 15. MANGO SHOWERS

Region:

> **Kerala + Karnataka**

Season:

> Pre-monsoon

Importance:

> Helps early mango ripening.


> **Mango showers → Kerala/Karnataka → Mango**

## 16. BLOSSOM SHOWERS

Region:

> **Kerala + Karnataka**

Importance:

> Beneficial for coffee flowering.


> **Blossom showers → Coffee**

## 17. PRE-MONSOON HEAT LOW

By May:

> Intense heating develops low pressure over northwestern India.

This becomes important for:

> **Southwest monsoon circulation**


> **May heating → NW Indian low pressure → Monsoon circulation**

## 18. SOUTHWEST MONSOON

Main rainy season:

> **June–September**

Origin:

> Indian Ocean region.

Major branches:

> **Arabian Sea branch**

> **Bay of Bengal branch**


> **SW Monsoon → June–September**

## 19. SOUTHWEST MONSOON MECHANISM

Master chain:

> **Summer heating**

↓

> **Low pressure over Indian subcontinent**

↓

> **ITCZ shifts north**

↓

> **Cross-equatorial flow**

↓

> **Coriolis deflection**

↓

> **Southwesterly winds**

↓

> **Arabian Sea + Bay of Bengal branches**

↓

> **Rainfall over India**

### RATTA THIS AS ONE CHAIN.

## 20. ITCZ & INDIA

ITCZ:

> **Inter Tropical Convergence Zone**

During Northern Hemisphere summer:

> Shifts northward.

It moves toward:

> Indian subcontinent.

Important for:

> **Southwest monsoon circulation**


> **Summer → ITCZ northward → Indian monsoon**

## 21. CROSS-EQUATORIAL FLOW

During summer:

> Winds from Southern Hemisphere cross Equator.

After crossing:

> Coriolis force deflects them rightward in Northern Hemisphere.

Result:

> **Southwesterly winds**


> **Cross-equatorial flow → SW monsoon winds**

## 22. ARABIAN SEA BRANCH

Approaches:

> **Western coast of India**

Major rainfall region:

> **Western Ghats**

Heavy rainfall:

> Konkan + Goa + coastal Karnataka + Kerala.


> **Arabian Sea branch → Western Ghats**

## 23. BAY OF BENGAL BRANCH

Moves toward:

> Northeast India + Ganga plains.

Important rainfall regions:

> Assam + Meghalaya + West Bengal + Bihar + Uttar Pradesh.


> **Bay branch → Northeast + Ganga Plain**

## 24. WESTERN GHATS & RAINFALL

Moist Arabian Sea winds:

> Forced to rise over Western Ghats.

Result:

> **Orographic rainfall**

Windward side:

> Wet

Leeward:

> Rain shadow.


> **Western Ghats → Orographic rainfall**

## 25. RAIN SHADOW OF WESTERN GHATS

Leeward side:

> **Deccan Plateau**

Receives:

> Much less rainfall than west coast.

Important cities:

> Pune region

> Bengaluru region

> Interior Maharashtra/Karnataka.


> **West coast → Wet**

> **Deccan interior → Rain shadow**

## 26. MEGHALAYA RAINFALL

Extremely heavy rainfall because:

> Moist Bay of Bengal winds + topographic uplift.

Important:

> **Khasi Hills**

Locations:

> Mawsynram

> Cherrapunji


> **Meghalaya → Orographic rainfall**

## 27. MAWSYNRAM

Location:

> **Meghalaya**

Known for:

> **Extremely high annual rainfall**


> **Mawsynram → Very high rainfall**

## 28. WESTERN RAJASTHAN — LOW RAINFALL

Characteristics:

> Very low rainfall.

Reasons include:

* subtropical high-pressure influence
* continentality
* distance from major moisture sources
* Aravalli orientation.


> **Western Rajasthan → India's major arid region**

## 29. ARAVALLI & MONSOON

Aravalli orientation:

> **Southwest → Northeast**

It runs broadly parallel to the direction of the southwest monsoon over Rajasthan.

Therefore:

> Limited orographic uplift.


> **Aravalli → Parallel to SW monsoon → Limited rainfall enhancement**

## 30. MONSOON ONSET

Normal onset over Kerala:

> **Around 1 June**

From Kerala:

> Monsoon advances northward.


> **Kerala → First major onset region**

## 31. MONSOON BURST

Monsoon burst:

> Sudden increase in rainfall at onset.

Characteristics:

> Rapid transition from pre-monsoon heat to widespread monsoon rain.


> **Burst → Sudden widespread rainfall**

## 32. MONSOON ADVANCE

General progression:

> **Kerala → Karnataka/Maharashtra → central India → northern India**

By July:

> Most of India is under monsoon influence.

## 33. MONSOON WITHDRAWAL

Withdrawal generally begins:

> **Northwestern India**

around:

> **September**

Then:

> Progresses southward.


> **Onset → Southwest/northward advance**

> **Withdrawal → Northwest/southward retreat**

## 34. RETREATING MONSOON

Period:

> **October–November**

Also:

> Northeast monsoon season.

Important region:

> **Tamil Nadu coast**


> **Retreating monsoon → Tamil Nadu rainfall**

## 35. NORTHEAST MONSOON

Winds:

> **Northeasterly**

Direction:

> Land → Bay of Bengal

After crossing Bay:

> Pick up moisture.

Important rainfall:

> **Tamil Nadu + southeastern coast**


> **NE Monsoon → Tamil Nadu**

## 36. COROMANDEL COAST

Important because:

> Receives substantial rainfall during **October–December**.

Main source:

> **Northeast/retreating monsoon**


> **Coromandel → NE Monsoon**

## 37. BAY OF BENGAL CYCLONES

Major season:

> **October–November**

Can affect:

> Odisha

> Andhra Pradesh

> Tamil Nadu

> West Bengal

> Bangladesh.


> **Post-monsoon → Bay of Bengal cyclones**

## 38. MONSOON TROUGH

Elongated low-pressure zone:

> **Northwestern India → Gangetic plains → Bay of Bengal**

Important for:

> Distribution of monsoon rainfall.

Its position:

> Shifts north/south during season.


> **Monsoon trough → Controls rainfall distribution**

## 39. ACTIVE MONSOON

Characteristics:

> Monsoon trough positioned favourably for widespread rainfall.

Rainfall:

> High over central/northern India.

## 40. BREAK IN MONSOON

Period:

> Temporary reduction in rainfall over much of India.

Often:

> Rain increases along Himalayan foothills.


> **Break → Central India rainfall decreases**

> **Himalayan foothills may receive more rain**

## 41. MONSOON VARIABILITY

Major controls:

> ENSO

> IOD

> MJO

> Jet streams

> Snow conditions

> Land-sea thermal contrast

> Monsoon trough

> Tropical disturbances.

## 42. EL NIÑO

El Niño:

> **Warming of central/eastern tropical Pacific Ocean**

Effects:

> Weakens normal Walker circulation.

India:

> Often associated with **weaker southwest monsoon**.


> **El Niño → Pacific warm → Indian monsoon often weak**

## 43. LA NIÑA

La Niña:

> **Cooling of central/eastern tropical Pacific Ocean**

Effects:

> Strengthens normal Walker circulation.

India:

> Often associated with **stronger monsoon**.


> **La Niña → Pacific cool → Indian monsoon often strong**

## 44. IOD

**Indian Ocean Dipole**

Measures:

> Temperature contrast between western and eastern tropical Indian Ocean.

### Positive IOD

> **Western Indian Ocean → warmer**

> **Eastern Indian Ocean → cooler**

Often:

> Favourable for Indian monsoon.

### Negative IOD

> **Western → cooler**

> **Eastern → warmer**

Often:

> Less favourable.

## 45. IOD MASTER

> **Positive IOD = West Warm + East Cool**

> **Negative IOD = West Cool + East Warm**

## 46. ENSO + IOD

Important:

> IOD can modify the influence of ENSO on Indian monsoon.

Therefore:

> El Niño does not automatically guarantee drought.


> **ENSO + IOD → Monsoon variability**

## 47. MJO

**Madden–Julian Oscillation**

Tropical atmospheric disturbance involving:

> **Convection + circulation**

Typical period:

> **~30–60 days**

Influences:

> Monsoon active/break cycles.


> **MJO → Intra-seasonal monsoon variability**

## 48. SUBTROPICAL WESTERLY JET

Winter:

> Located south of Himalayas/over northern India region.

Associated with:

> **Western Disturbances**


> **Winter + STWJ → Western Disturbances**

## 49. TROPICAL EASTERLY JET

Develops strongly during:

> **Summer**

Associated with:

> **Indian southwest monsoon**

Direction:

> East → West


> **Summer → Tropical Easterly Jet → Monsoon**

## 50. TIBETAN PLATEAU

Summer heating:

> Strong heating of elevated plateau.

Effect:

> Helps establish thermal circulation favourable to South Asian monsoon.


> **Tibetan Plateau heating → Monsoon support**

## 51. INDIA — RAINFALL DISTRIBUTION

General rainfall pattern:

### Very high

> Northeast

> Western Ghats

### Moderate

> Ganga plains

### Low

> Deccan rain-shadow regions

### Very low

> Western Rajasthan

## 52. VERY HIGH RAINFALL REGIONS

Major:

> **Meghalaya**

> **Western Ghats**

> **Andaman & Nicobar**


> **Orographic uplift → Heavy rainfall**

## 53. LOW RAINFALL REGIONS

Major:

> Western Rajasthan

> Ladakh

> Deccan rain-shadow areas.


> **Ladakh → Cold desert**

> **Rajasthan → Hot desert**

## 54. LADAKH CLIMATE

Type:

> **Cold desert**

Reason:

> Located in rain shadow of Himalayas.

Characteristics:

> Very low precipitation + large temperature variation.


> **Ladakh → Cold desert → Rain shadow**

## 55. CHERRAPUNJI

Location:

> **Meghalaya**

Characteristics:

> Extremely high rainfall.

Rainfall mechanism:

> Orographic uplift.


> **Cherrapunji → Khasi Hills → Orographic rain**

## 56. MUMBAI

Climate:

> Tropical maritime.

Rainfall:

> Mainly Southwest monsoon.

Reason:

> Arabian Sea branch + Western Ghats.


> **Mumbai → SW monsoon + Arabian Sea branch**

## 57. PUNE

Compared with Mumbai:

> Much drier.

Reason:

> **Rain-shadow location east of Western Ghats**


> **Pune → Rain shadow**

## 58. CHENNAI

Rainfall maximum:

> **October–December**

Main reason:

> Northeast/retreating monsoon.


> **Chennai → NE Monsoon**

## 59. KOLKATA

Main rainfall:

> **Southwest monsoon**

Also affected by:

> Bay of Bengal systems.

Climate:

> Humid tropical monsoon.

## 60. DELHI

Rainfall:

> Mainly Southwest monsoon.

Winter precipitation:

> Western Disturbances.

Climate:

> Strong continental influence.


> **Delhi → Summer monsoon rain + winter Western Disturbance**

## 61. UTTAR PRADESH CLIMATE

UP has:

> **Tropical monsoon climate**

Rainfall mainly:

> **Southwest monsoon**

General rainfall gradient:

> **East → More rainfall**

> **West → Less rainfall**


> **UP rainfall → East > West**

## 62. EASTERN UP

Generally:

> Higher rainfall than western UP.

Reason:

> Greater influence of Bay of Bengal monsoon branch.

## 63. WESTERN UP

Compared with eastern UP:

> Lower rainfall.

Greater:

> Continental influence.

## 64. UP — SEASONAL RAINFALL

Most rainfall:

> **June–September**

Therefore:

> Southwest monsoon is the principal rainy season.


> **UP → SW Monsoon → June–September**

## 65. UP — TEMPERATURE

Summer:

> Very hot.

Winter:

> Cold, especially northern/interior areas.

Annual temperature range:

> Greater than coastal India.

Reason:

> **Continental location**

## 66. UP — WINTER WEATHER

Important phenomena:

> Fog

> Cold waves

> Western Disturbances

> Winter rainfall.


> **Winter UP → Fog + cold waves + occasional Western Disturbance rain**

## 67. UP — WESTERN DISTURBANCES

Affect:

> Mainly western/northern UP.

Produce:

> Winter rainfall.

Importance:

> Wheat/rabi agriculture.

## 68. UP — PRE-MONSOON WEATHER

Features:

> Heat waves

> Dust storms

> Thunderstorms

> Local convectional rain.

## 69. UP — MONSOON VARIABILITY

Rainfall varies spatially because of:

* distance from Bay of Bengal
* monsoon branch strength
* relief
* local circulation
* monsoon trough position.


> **Eastern UP → relatively wetter**

> **Western UP → relatively drier**

## 70. CLIMATE CLASSIFICATION — KÖPPEN

Broad Köppen groups:

> **A → Tropical**

> **B → Dry**

> **C → Warm temperate**

> **D → Snow/continental**

> **E → Polar**

## 71. TROPICAL WET CLIMATE

India:

> Western coast

> Northeast

> Andaman & Nicobar.

Characteristics:

> High temperature + high rainfall.

## 72. TROPICAL MONSOON CLIMATE

Large part of:

> India

Characteristics:

> Seasonal rainfall + distinct wet/dry periods.

## 73. ARID CLIMATE

Region:

> **Western Rajasthan**

Characteristics:

> Very low rainfall + high evaporation.

## 74. SEMI-ARID CLIMATE

Transition zone:

> Rajasthan → Punjab/Haryana/central-western India.

Rainfall:

> More than desert but still relatively low.

## 75. MOUNTAIN CLIMATE

Region:

> **Himalayas**

Characteristics vary with:

> Altitude + slope + aspect.

Temperature:

> Generally decreases with elevation.

## 76. MARITIME CLIMATE

Coastal India:

> Moderate temperatures.

Characteristics:

> Lower annual temperature range.

## 77. DIURNAL TEMPERATURE RANGE

Difference between:

> Daily maximum + minimum temperature.

Greater in:

> Dry desert areas.

Lower in:

> Humid/coastal regions.


> **Desert → Large diurnal range**

> **Coast → Small diurnal range**

## 78. ANNUAL TEMPERATURE RANGE

Generally:

> Interior > Coastal

Reason:

> Continentality vs maritime influence.

## 79. RELIEF & RAINFALL

Mountains can:

> Force moist air to rise.

Result:

> **Orographic rainfall**

Examples:

> Western Ghats

> Khasi Hills

> Himalayas.

## 80. WINDWARD & LEEWARD

### Windward

> Air rises → cools → condensation → rainfall.

### Leeward

> Air descends → warms → dries.


> **Windward = Wet**

> **Leeward = Dry**

## 81. INDIAN TROPICAL CYCLONES

Major source regions:

> **Bay of Bengal**

> **Arabian Sea**

Most important seasons:

> **April–May**

> **October–November**

## 82. BAY OF BENGAL CYCLONES

Why important:

> Warm tropical waters + moisture + favourable atmospheric conditions.

Major affected regions:

> Odisha

> Andhra Pradesh

> West Bengal

> Tamil Nadu

> Bangladesh.

## 83. ARABIAN SEA CYCLONES

Can affect:

> Gujarat

> Maharashtra

> Goa

> Kerala

> Arabian Peninsula.

Important:

> Cyclone activity can occur pre- and post-monsoon.

## 84. STORM SURGE

Cyclones can cause:

> **Abnormal rise of sea level**

because of:

> Strong winds + low pressure.

Major danger:

> Coastal flooding.


> **Cyclone → Storm surge → Coastal flooding**

## 85. CONVECTIONAL RAINFALL IN INDIA

Common during:

> Pre-monsoon/summer

Process:

> Strong surface heating → air rises → cooling → condensation → rainfall.

Associated with:

> Thunderstorms.

## 86. OROGRAPHIC RAINFALL IN INDIA

Major locations:

> Western Ghats

> Meghalaya

> Himalayan foothills.


> **Mountain uplift → Orographic rainfall**

## 87. CYCLONIC RAINFALL

Produced by:

> Tropical cyclones/depressions + frontal systems.

India:

> Tropical systems especially important along eastern coast.

## 88. MONSOON DEPRESSIONS

Often originate over:

> **Bay of Bengal**

Move:

> West/northwest across central/northern India.

Important:

> Contribute significantly to monsoon rainfall.


> **Bay of Bengal → Monsoon depressions → Rainfall**

## 89. MONSOON DEPRESSIONS & UP

UP can receive:

> Significant monsoon rainfall from systems moving west/northwest from Bay of Bengal.


> **Bay systems → Ganga Plain rainfall**

## 90. INDIAN MONSOON — TWO BRANCHES

> **Arabian Sea branch**

> **Bay of Bengal branch**

### Arabian branch

> Western coast → Western Ghats → Deccan.

### Bay branch

> Northeast → Ganga plain → Northwest India.

## 91. MONSOON RAINFALL — WEST TO EAST

Across much of the northern plains:

> Rainfall generally increases toward east.

Reason:

> Bay of Bengal branch loses moisture as it moves westward.


> **Northern Plains → Rainfall generally East > West**

## 92. HIMALAYAN RAINFALL

Himalayan foothills:

> Receive substantial monsoon rainfall.

Reason:

> Moisture-bearing winds forced upward by relief.

## 93. RAJASTHAN RAINFALL

Extremely low in:

> Western Rajasthan

Gradually increases:

> Toward eastern/southeastern Rajasthan.

## 94. PENINSULAR RAINFALL

Western coast:

> Heavy rainfall.

Interior Deccan:

> Rain shadow.

Tamil Nadu:

> Important rainfall during retreating/NE monsoon.

> Generally decreases with altitude.

> Mountain regions cooler than nearby plains.

> Shimla vs Delhi.

## 101. ASPECT OF SLOPE

> Sun-facing slopes can receive greater solar heating.

> Local temperature/vegetation differs by slope aspect.
