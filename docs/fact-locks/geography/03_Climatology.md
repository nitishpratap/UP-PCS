## 2. Atmospheric Composition

**Permanent gases:** Nitrogen + Oxygen + Argon + other trace gases

**Variable gases:** Water vapour + Carbon dioxide + Ozone + aerosols

!!! prelims "Water vapour"
 **Highly variable** spatially and temporally — clouds, precipitation, greenhouse effect.

## 3. ️ Layers of Atmosphere

From Earth's surface upward:

> **Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere → Exosphere**

!!! mnemonic "Layer order"
 **T S M T E**

## 4. ️ Troposphere

Lowest layer. Thickness **~8 km at poles → ~18 km near Equator** (average ~13 km). Contains most atmospheric mass + water vapour. Almost all ordinary weather.

> **Troposphere → Weather**

> **Temperature generally decreases with altitude** — average lapse **~6.5°C/km**

## 5. ️ Tropopause

Boundary: **Troposphere + Stratosphere**. Height variable — **higher at Equator**, **lower at Poles**.

## 6. Stratosphere

Above troposphere. **Ozone layer**. Temperature **increases upward** (ozone absorbs UV).

> **Stratosphere → Ozone → UV absorption**

## 7. Mesosphere

Above stratosphere. Temperature **decreases upward**. **Most meteors burn up here.**

## 8. Thermosphere

Above mesosphere. Temperature **increases strongly**. **Ionosphere** region. **Auroras.**

## 9. Exosphere

Outermost layer — extremely thin gas; gradually merges with **outer space**.

## 10. ️ Atmospheric Temperature

Sun is main source, but atmosphere heated **indirectly from Earth's surface**:

> Sun → shortwave → surface → longwave terrestrial → atmosphere

> **Atmosphere is heated largely from below.**

## 11. Insolation

**Insolation = incoming solar radiation.** Varies by latitude, season, daylight duration, angle of incidence, atmospheric conditions, cloud cover.

> **Vertical rays → concentrated energy**

> **Oblique rays → spread → weaker heating**

## 12. Angle of Sun's Rays

High Sun → concentrated → stronger heating. Low Sun → spread → weaker heating.

## 13. Heat Budget

Incoming solar ≈ outgoing terrestrial (long-term **global heat balance**). Tropics → energy surplus; poles → deficit; circulation redistributes heat.

## 14. ️ Albedo

**Albedo = fraction of solar radiation reflected.**

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| High albedo | Snow, ice, clouds |
| Low albedo | Forest, ocean, dark surfaces |

## 15. ️ Normal Lapse Rate

Troposphere average: **6.5°C/km** (~1°C per 165 m). **Height ↑ → Temperature ↓**

## 16. ️ Inversion of Temperature

Normally temperature falls with height; in **inversion** it **increases with altitude** in a layer → cold air trapped → fog, pollution, stable air.

!!! trap "Inversion"
 **Temperature inversion → pollution trapping**

## 17. ️ Atmospheric Pressure

Force of air weight. **Barometer**.
**mb / hPa**
Standard sea level **1013.25 hPa**.

## 18. Altitude & Pressure

**Height ↑ → Pressure ↓** (less air above)

## 19. Global Pressure Belts

| Latitude | Belt |
| --- | --- |
| **0°** | Equatorial Low |
| **30°** | Subtropical High (N & S) |
| **60°** | Subpolar Low (N & S) |
| **90°** | Polar High (N & S) |

!!! mnemonic "Pressure belt master"
**0 – Low.
30 – High.
60 – Low.
90 – High**.

## 20. Equatorial Low Pressure Belt

Around Equator: heating → rising air → weak winds → humidity → convectional rain. **Doldrums / ITCZ region**

## 21. Subtropical High Pressure Belt

**~25°–35°** — descending air → dry. Major deserts **~20°–30°**.

> **30° → Subtropical High → Descending air → Deserts**

## 22. Subpolar Low Pressure Belt

**~60°** — convergence of Westerlies + Polar Easterlies → rising air, frontal activity.

## 23. Polar High Pressure Belt

**~90°** — cold dense air sinks.

## 25. Trade Winds

| Hemisphere | Flow |
| --- | --- |
| NH | **NE → SW** |
| SH | **SE → NW** |

## 26. Westerlies

> **30°–60°**, **West → East**. NH: SW→NE
> SH: NW→SE

## 27. Polar Easterlies

**East → West** — Polar High → Subpolar Low

## 28. Coriolis Effect on Winds

> **N → Right
> S → Left
> Equator → zero**

## 29. ITCZ

**Inter Tropical Convergence Zone** — trades converge → rising air → clouds → heavy rain → migrates seasonally.

## 30. ITCZ & Indian Monsoon

## 44. Air Mass

| --- | --- |

## 45. ️ Fronts

Boundary between contrasting air masses.

| Front | Movement |
| --- | --- |
| Warm | Warm air over cold |
| Cold | Cold under warm |
| Stationary | Neither advances much |
| Occluded | Cold overtakes warm |

## 46. Humidity

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Absolute | Mass of vapour per volume |
| Specific | Mass of vapour per mass of moist air |
| Relative | % of saturation at that temperature |

> **Relative humidity depends strongly on temperature**

## 47. Relative Humidity

When air temperature **increases**, relative humidity generally **decreases** if moisture content remains constant.

When temperature **decreases**, relative humidity **increases**.

At saturation: **RH = 100%**

## 48. Dew Point

Temperature at which air becomes **saturated** when cooled at constant pressure/moisture.

## 55. ️ Types of Rainfall

> **Convectional**

> **Orographic**

> **Cyclonic/frontal**

## 56. ️ Convectional Rainfall

> Heating → rise → cool → condense → rain. Equatorial
heavy, short, often afternoon.

## 57. ️ Orographic Rainfall

Moist air forced over mountains — **windward wet**, **leeward rain shadow**

## 58. ️ Rain Shadow

> Dry **leeward** side — descending air warms/dries. **Western Ghats → wet west coast
dry Deccan interior**.

## 59. Cyclone

**Low pressure** centre.
NH **counter-clockwise**.
SH **clockwise**.

## 60. Anticyclone

**High pressure**
NH **clockwise**.
SH **counter-clockwise**.

## 61. Tropical Cyclone

Warm-core.
warm tropical oceans.
Coriolis.
weakens over land.
strongest winds in **eyewall**. Needs warm ocean + moisture + Coriolis + low vertical wind shear.

## 62. Tropical Cyclone — Coriolis

Rare **exactly at Equator** — Coriolis ≈ zero

## 63. Eye of Cyclone

Calm, low-pressure centre.
surrounded by **eyewall** (strongest winds).

## 65. ️ Tornado

Narrow violent vortex from thunderstorm to surface.
smaller, shorter-lived than tropical cyclone.

## 66. Tropical vs Temperate Cyclone

| Feature | Tropical | Temperate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Warm | Generally cold |
| Main energy | Latent heat | Temperature gradients |
| Fronts | Generally absent | **Present** |
| Location | Tropics/subtropics | Mid/high latitudes |
| Size | Generally smaller | Generally larger |
| Eye | Often present | Usually absent |
| Structure | Warm-core | Frontal |

## 67. El Niño

**Abnormal warming** of central/eastern tropical Pacific.
**ENSO**
weakens Walker circulation.
India: **often weaker monsoon**.

## 68. ️ La Niña

Cool central/eastern Pacific.
stronger Walker.
India: **often stronger monsoon**.

## 69. ENSO

**El Niño–Southern Oscillation** = ocean (El Niño/La Niña) + atmosphere (Southern Oscillation)

## 70. Walker Circulation

Normal Pacific: **warm west + rising**.
**cool east + sinking**
surface **easterly trades**.

## 71. El Niño & Walker Circulation

El Niño: trades weaken → warm water east → eastern Pacific warmer/rising → **Walker weakens**

> **El Niño → Trade winds weaken → Warm water east → Walker weakens**

## 72. Indian Ocean Dipole — IOD

SST difference **western vs eastern** tropical Indian Ocean.

| Phase | Western IO | Eastern IO | Monsoon hint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Positive** | Warmer | Cooler | Often more favourable |
| **Negative** | Cooler | Warmer | Often less favourable |

## 73. IOD Master Ratta

> **Positive IOD → West warm + East cool**

> **Negative IOD → West cool + East warm**

IOD can **moderate El Niño's effect** on Indian monsoon.

## 84. ️ Monsoon Burst

Sudden widespread heavy rain after arrival

## 85. ️ Monsoon Break

Temporary rainfall decrease.
monsoon trough shift.

## 86. Monsoon Trough

> Low-pressure belt NW India → Bay of Bengal
controls rainfall distribution.
shifts north/south.

## 87. ️ Orographic Effect — India

**Western Ghats** — wet coast, dry interior.
**Himalayas** — block/uplift.
**NE hills** — orographic enhancement.

## 88. ️ Tibetan Plateau & Monsoon

Summer heating helps **South Asian monsoon circulation**

## 89. ️ Cross-Equatorial Flow

Summer NH: flow toward Asian low.
SH winds cross Equator.
> Coriolis → **south-westerly monsoon**

## 90. ️ Monsoon — Master Mechanism

> **Summer heating → Land low pressure → ITCZ north → Cross-equatorial flow → Coriolis → SW winds → Arabian Sea + Bay branches → Indian monsoon**
