# Prelims Analysis

This is the **revision control room** for UPPCS Prelims GS Paper-I. It converts 2018–2025 PYQ tags into a practical plan: which subject to open first, what to memorise, what traps repeat, and how to practise.

Use it in three situations:

1. **Before starting revision:** choose the high-weight subjects first.
2. **Before mocks:** know which formats to practise.
3. **Last week:** revise only heat-map topics and trap lists.

## Paper Trend That Matters Most

UPPCS has moved away from pure one-line factual MCQs. In 2024–2025, the paper used many more:

- **Multi-statement** questions.
- **Assertion–Reason** questions.
- **Match List-I / List-II** questions.
- **NOT correctly matched** questions.

So the right preparation is not “read more facts”. The right preparation is **compare more facts**: article vs body, pass vs state, site vs feature, treaty vs purpose, book vs author.

## How to Use a Subject Page

1. Read **Exam Decision** first. It tells you how much time the subject deserves.
2. Follow **Do First**. These are the chapters to finish before touching low-yield areas.
3. Memorise **Must-Know Locks**. These are the facts UPPCS turns into option traps.
4. Use **Mock Audit** after every test. If your mistakes repeat there, revise that cluster.
5. Open the linked **Subject Notes** only after choosing the target chapter.

!!! note "Tagging caveat"
    Counts are decision aids, not a syllabus boundary. Some 2018–2020 questions sit under broad **General Studies** tags. Art and Culture is under-tagged because culture often appears inside History. Environment ROI is higher than the tagged count because reports, schemes, and protected areas leak into Economy and Geography. **Current Affairs** is the reverse problem in 2024–25: many functional CA stems are tagged IR / Defence / Science / Economy — see [CA Analysis](../current-affairs/00_Analysis_2018_2025.md#repo-pyq-reconciliation-important).
