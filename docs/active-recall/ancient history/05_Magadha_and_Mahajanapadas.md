## Mahajanapadas and Magadha

**Q1.** What were the 16 mahajanapadas in the Buddhist list, and which text names them?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Anguttara Nikaya.** Jain list is the **Bhagavati**. The 16: **Kashi, Kosala, Anga, Magadha, Vajji, Malla, Chedi, Vatsa, Kuru, Panchala, Matsya, Surasena, Asmaka, Avanti, Gandhara, Kamboja.** There are **16, not 18**. **Shakya is not** one of them.

## Haryanka

**Q6.** Who is generally regarded as the founder of the Haryanka dynasty, and what is his date-band?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bimbisara (Shrenika), about 544–492 BCE.** Capital **Girivraja**. About 52 years. Contemporary of the **Buddha and Mahavira**. Epic **Jarasandha / Brihadratha** is **not** this exam house.

</details>

**Q7.** Which kingdom did Bimbisara conquer first, and which city came as Kosala dowry?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Anga**, with port **Champa**. **Kashi** came through the **Kosala** marriage. First Magadha bite is **Anga**, not Avanti.

</details>

**Q8.** Name Bimbisara’s three marriage alliances.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kosaladevi** — Kosala / Kashi dowry. **Chellana** — Licchavi / Vaishali. **Khema** — Madra. **Jivaka** treated **Pradyota** of Avanti.

</details>

**Q9.** Who was Amrapali, and which religions did Bimbisara patronise?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Amrapali** is the famous courtesan of **Vaishali** in Buddhist memory. Bimbisara met **Gautama Buddha** and also sits in Jain memory. **Jetavana** is **Anathapindika’s** gift at **Shravasti**, not a Bimbisara building.

</details>

**Q10.** How did Bimbisara die?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Imprisoned and killed by his son Ajatashatru** (parricide; Jain name **Kunika**).

</details>

## Ajatashatru

**Q11.** Who succeeded Bimbisara, and which two wars define him?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ajatashatru (~492–460 BCE).** War with **Kosala** over Kashi, then marriage to **Vajira**. Long war (~16 years) on **Vajji / Licchavis**.

</details>

**Q12.** Who were the Vajji, which base did Ajatashatru fortify, and which two war-engines are named?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Gana of eight clans, capital Vaishali, Licchavis leading.** Base = **Pataligrama**. Engines = **rathamusala** (scythed chariot) and **mahashilakantaka** (stone-thrower). Ministers **Vassakara** and **Sunidha** split the Licchavi assembly.

</details>

**Q13.** Which Buddhist council belongs to Ajatashatru?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: First Council at Rajagriha**, presided by **Mahakassapa**. He also claimed Buddha relics and a Rajagriha stupa.

</details>

**Q14.** Who shifted the capital to Pataliputra?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Udayin (~460–444 BCE).** Ajatashatru **fortified Pataligrama**. **Chandragupta Maurya did not found** the city. Weak tail: Anuruddha, Munda, Nagadasaka. Then minister **Shishunaga**.

</details>

## Why Magadha rose

**Q15.** Why did Magadha become the most powerful mahajanapada? Use the FIRST chain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**F** fertile alluvium. **I** iron (Rajgir–Chhotanagpur). **E** elephants and timber from eastern forests. **R** Ganga–Son rivers (navy, tolls, irrigation). **S** strong unbroken houses. **T** trade/strategic hinge — **not** Arabian-Sea ports. Rajgir is the **five-hill** fort. Pataliputra is the **river junction**.

</details>

**Q16.** Why was Pataliputra a better imperial capital than Rajgir, and why did Magadha beat the north-west?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Ganga + **Son** = river navy, east–west roads, easier grain and tolls. The north-west faced **Achaemenids then Alexander**. Magadha sat on **eastern iron, elephants, and rain-fed surplus**. Rivals (Prasenajit, Pradyota, Udayana) had one strong king each, then gaps.

</details>

## Shishunaga

**Q17.** Who founded the Shishunaga dynasty, which kingdom did he conquer, and which city was a temporary capital?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Shishunaga** (ex-viceroy of Kashi). He crushed **Avanti** (Pradyota line). **Ujjain** enters Magadha. Tradition: sat first at **Vaishali**, then **Pataliputra**. He did **not** found Pataliputra. Avanti is **not** Bimbisara’s prize.

</details>

**Q18.** Who was Kalashoka, and which council is his?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kalashoka / Kakavarna**, Shishunaga’s successor. **Second Council at Vaishali (~383 BCE)** — Vinaya dispute; **Sthavira / Mahasanghika** split. Last Shishunaga often **Nandivardhana**, overthrown by Mahapadma.

</details>

## Nanda

**Q19.** Who founded the Nanda dynasty, and which titles are attached to him?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mahapadma Nanda (Ugrasena).** Puranic titles **ekarat** and **sarvakshatrantaka**. Hostile texts give a **shudra / barber** origin. He rolls leftover Kuru, Panchala, Kosala, Vatsa. **Hathigumpha** later remembers a Nanda and a Jina image from **Kalinga** — that inscription is **Kharavela’s**.

</details>

**Q20.** Who was the last Nanda, what army numbers do Greeks give, and did Alexander fight him?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dhana Nanda = Greek Agrammes / Xandrames.** **Curtius:** 2 lakh foot, 20,000 horse, 2,000 chariots, 3,000 elephants. Alexander **stopped at the Beas**. He **never** marched on Pataliputra. Nine Nandas = Mahapadma + eight sons.

</details>

**Q21.** Who overthrew the Nandas, with whose help, and about when?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chandragupta Maurya and Chanakya / Kautilya, about 322 BCE.** The Mauryas inherit Nanda revenue and a four-fold army. That is **not** yet Ashokan Dhamma.

</details>

## Foreign accounts and Pataliputra

**Q22.** Who was Megasthenes, which court, which book, and what does he say of Pataliputra?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Seleucid envoy at Chandragupta Maurya’s court.** Work = ***Indica*** (survives in later writers). Pataliputra is a huge **wooden-palisade** city with a **moat**. Use Greeks critically. **Diodorus and Curtius** describe the **Nanda** army.

</details>

**Q23.** Name the rivers of Pataliputra and which dynasty made it the imperial capital.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ganga and Son** (and nearby streams). **Mauryas** made it the imperial seat. It was already Magadha’s capital from **Udayin**.

</details>

## Magadha and religion

**Q24.** Which Magadhan rulers link to which Buddhist councils, and which Jain / Buddhist sites sit in Magadha–Bihar?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Bimbisara** supported the Buddha. **Ajatashatru** — **First Council, Rajgir**. **Kalashoka** — **Second, Vaishali** (Vajji land). **Ashoka** — **Third, Pataliputra** (also Magadha, Mauryan). Jain **Pavapuri**. Enlightenment **Bodh Gaya**. Third Council is Magadha; Second is **Vaishali**, not Magadha’s capital.

</details>

## Conceptual

**Q26.** Why is Magadha called the start of India’s first major imperial tradition, and how does it link to the Second Urbanisation?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** From Bimbisara to the Nandas, one Gangetic state eats the rest, then the Mauryas inherit that machine. Towns, **NBPW**, punch-marked silver, and **setthi** trade sit in this same **second urbanisation** (after Harappa). **PGW** is Later Vedic, not this urban ware.

</details>

**Q27.** How did Bimbisara and Ajatashatru together build Magadha’s lead?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bimbisara** — Anga port + marriage net (Kashi, Licchavi, Madra). **Ajatashatru** — Kosala settlement + Vajji war + Pataligrama. Geography (FIRST) then meets these two hungry kings.

</details>
