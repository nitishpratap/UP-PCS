## 1. Basic and chronology

**Q1.** What is the other name commonly used for the Indus Valley Civilization?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Harappan Civilization**, after the first excavated site **Harappa**. Some books also say **Sindhu–Saraswati**. It is the same culture.

</details>

**Q2.** What is the approximate period of the Mature Harappan Civilization?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: About 2600–1900 BCE.** This is the urban peak that exams mean by “IVC.”

</details>

**Q3.** Name the three broad phases of the Harappan Civilization.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Early Harappan (~3300–2600 BCE) → Mature Harappan (~2600–1900 BCE) → Late Harappan (~1900–1300 BCE).** Early is regional and pre-urban (Kot Diji, Amri, Hakra, Sothi). Late is de-urbanisation (Cemetery-H, Jhukar, Rangpur Lustrous Red Ware).

</details>

**Q4.** Which discovery is generally considered to have brought the Harappan Civilization to scholarly attention?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The excavation of Harappa**, followed by **John Marshall’s 1924** announcement in the *Illustrated London News*. **Charles Masson** noticed the ruins in **1826**. **Cunningham** published a Harappa seal in **1875**.

</details>

**Q5.** Who excavated Harappa, and in which year?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Daya Ram Sahni in 1921.** **M.S. Vats** later worked on the Harappa granary. **Wheeler** dug there in **1946**. Wheeler did **not** discover the civilization.

</details>

**Q6.** Who excavated Mohenjo-daro, and in which year?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: R.D. Banerji in 1922.** The name means “mound of the dead” (Sindh).

</details>

**Q7.** Which civilization was contemporary with the Harappans in Mesopotamia?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mesopotamia** (Sumer / Akkad / later Babylon). Egypt is also contemporary. Origin is **indigenous** (Mehrgarh → Early Harappan), not a Mesopotamian colony.

</details>

## 2. Geographical extent

**Q8.** What are the northernmost, southernmost, easternmost, and westernmost Harappan sites?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: North — Manda** (Jammu, Chenab). **South — Daimabad** (Maharashtra, Pravara). **East — Alamgirpur** (Meerut, UP). **West — Sutkagen-dor** (Makran). Do not swap **Mandi** (Bijnor, UP) with **Manda**.

</details>

**Q9.** Which large Harappan site is in Haryana?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rakhigarhi** (Hisar) — the **largest** Harappan site in India, **seven mounds**. It is **not** in Uttar Pradesh. **Bhirrana** is often among the **oldest**. **Balu** was the 2020 Haryana match.

</details>

**Q10.** Which major Harappan site is in Rajasthan?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kalibangan** (Hanumangarh) on the **Ghaggar**, excavated by **B.B. Lal**.

</details>

**Q11.** Name two major Harappan sites in Gujarat.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Lothal** (dockyard, Bhogavo) and **Dholavira** (Kutch, reservoirs). **Padri** was the 2020 Gujarat match. **Surkotada** is also in Kutch.

</details>

**Q12.** Which Harappan site sits on the Ghaggar–Hakra system?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kalibangan.** The Ghaggar–Hakra belt is the Saraswati-zone cluster.

</details>

**Q13.** What was the approximate geographical extent of the Harappan Civilization?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: About 12.5–13 lakh km²** — from Makran / Afghanistan to western UP, and from Jammu to Gujarat / Maharashtra. **Shortughai** in Afghanistan is a **lapis lazuli** colony.

</details>

## 4. Town planning

**Q24.** What were the two major divisions of most Harappan cities?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Citadel (usually west, raised) and Lower Town (east, larger, residential).** Dholavira adds a **middle town**.

</details>

**Q25.** What was the purpose of the Citadel?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Public, administrative, and ceremonial structures** — granaries, Great Bath, assembly buildings. Elite use is inferred, not proved by a palace name.

</details>

**Q26.** What was generally located in the Lower Town?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Houses, workshops, and everyday occupation.**

</details>

**Q27.** What was the characteristic pattern of Harappan streets?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Grid pattern** — streets meet at right angles. **Banawali** is an exception with a **radial / oval** plan.

</details>

**Q28.** What makes Harappan drainage particularly significant?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An extensive planned network, often covered**, with house bathrooms and wells. Best at **Mohenjo-daro**. **Weak at Kalibangan**.

</details>

**Q29.** What material was predominantly used for construction?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Burnt (baked) bricks.** Dholavira uses more **stone**.

</details>

**Q30.** What was the characteristic ratio of Harappan bricks?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1 : 2 : 4** (thickness : width : length).

</details>

**Q31.** Were Harappan cities generally planned before construction, or did they grow randomly?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Generally planned** before building. This is why exams call IVC **urban**, not pastoral.

</details>

**Q32.** What was the significance of covered drains?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Wastewater and sanitation** — civic control of dirty water, not just house-level soak pits.

</details>

## 5. Agriculture and economy

**Q33.** What were the major crops cultivated by the Harappans?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Wheat, barley, peas, sesame, mustard, and cotton.** Rice husk is reported at **Lothal**. **Banawali** has barley.

</details>

**Q34.** The earliest archaeological evidence of cotton cultivation is associated with which civilization?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Harappan Civilization** — among the earliest cotton in the world. Greek *sindon* for cotton cloth is a later memory of the Indus name.

</details>

**Q35.** Which animal was probably the most important domesticated animal for Harappan agriculture?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Cattle** (humped bull is common on seals). Sheep, goat, and buffalo were also kept. **Horse** at **Surkotada** is **disputed**.

</details>

**Q36.** Did the Harappans know the plough?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Kalibangan has a **ploughed field**. Banawali has a terracotta **plough** model.

</details>

**Q37.** What evidence of ploughing has been found at Kalibangan?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A pre-Harappan / Early Harappan ploughed field** with two furrow directions.

</details>

**Q38.** What were the major occupations of Harappan people?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Agriculture, craft (beads, pottery, shell, metal), trade, and building.** There were **no coins**.

</details>

**Q39.** What evidence proves extensive trade with Mesopotamia?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mesopotamian texts name Meluhha.** Indus **seals** and goods appear in the Gulf and Mesopotamia. Imports include **lapis** (Shortughai / Badakhshan), copper, gold, and shell.

</details>

**Q40.** What was the Mesopotamian name possibly associated with the Indus region?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Meluhha.** Magan and Dilmun are other Gulf names, not the Indus itself.

</details>

</details>

<details markdown="1">

**Ans: Generally right to left.** Some lines may be boustrophedon.

</details>

**Q49.** On which objects is Harappan writing commonly found?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Steatite seals, pottery, tablets, copper/bronze objects, and the Dholavira signboard.**

</details>

**Q50.** Approximately how many signs are associated with the Harappan script?

## 8. Religion and society

**Q51.** What evidence suggests worship of a mother goddess?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Numerous female terracotta figurines.** This is inference, not a named temple cult.

</details>

**Q52.** Which deity-like figure is commonly called the Pashupati seal figure?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A horned, possibly yogic seated figure** on a Mohenjo-daro seal. Marshall read it as proto-Shiva. That reading is **debated**.

</details>

**Q53.** What animals are associated with the Pashupati seal?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Elephant, rhinoceros, buffalo, and tiger.** The **horse is not** on this seal.

</details>

**Q54.** Was there evidence of temple structures comparable to Mesopotamian ziggurats?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** No identified palace or stone temple. The **Great Bath** is the main public ritual structure.

</details>

**Q55.** What evidence suggests the worship of trees?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Tree motifs on seals**, sometimes with a figure in a peepal.

</details>

**Q56.** Which tree appears frequently in Harappan religious symbolism?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Peepal (Ficus religiosa).**

</details>

**Q57.** What evidence exists for burial?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Graves with bodies**, sometimes with pots and ornaments. Harappa Cemetery **R-37**. **Farmana** (Haryana) is a Mature cemetery.

</details>

**Q58.** Did Harappans practice cremation?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Burial is the main evidence.** Some Late / Cemetery-H contexts suggest other treatments, including possible cremation. Do not treat cremation as the Mature Harappan default.

</details>

**Q59.** What does the absence of monumental royal palaces suggest about Harappan political organization?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Power was not advertised as a named god-king** in the Egyptian or Mesopotamian palace-pyramid style. Civic planning still implies some central authority.

</details>

## 10. Burial and society

**Q68.** What were the common burial practices of Harappans?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mainly inhumation in graves**, with regional variation. Harappa has coffin burial in R-37. **Sanauli** (Baghpat, 2018) has wooden coffins, copper swords, and **chariot burials** — Late Harappan / OCP, **not** a Mature grid-city.

</details>

**Q69.** What is meant by extended burial?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The body is buried whole, stretched out**, usually with the head in a set direction.

</details>

**Q70.** What is meant by fractional burial?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Only selected bones** are buried after the body has decayed or been treated elsewhere (secondary burial).

</details>

**Q71.** What do grave goods tell historians about Harappan society?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Differences in pots, ornaments, and grave type** point to status and belief. They do not give us named classes.

</details>

**Q72.** Was Harappan society completely egalitarian?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** House sizes, citadel vs lower town, and unequal grave goods show ranking. The exact hierarchy is still debated.

</details>

**Q73.** Around which period did the Mature Harappan urban system decline?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Around 1900 BCE.**

</details>

**Q74.** Was the decline caused by one single factor?

<details markdown="1">

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q75.** Name at least four proposed causes of Harappan decline.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Climate shift / aridity, Ghaggar drying, floods, trade decline, land overuse, and resource stress.** Wheeler’s **Aryan-massacre** reading of Mohenjo-daro is **rejected**.

</details>

**Q76.** What environmental changes may have contributed to the decline?

<details markdown="1">

</details>

**Q77.** What role might changes in river courses have played?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Migration or drying of rivers** (Indus floods; Ghaggar–Hakra desiccation) could cut agriculture, river trade, and city water.

</details>

**Q78.** What happened to large urban centres after the decline?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Cities shrank.** People shifted to smaller rural / Late Harappan settlements. Second urbanisation comes much later with **NBPW** towns, not as a Harappan revival.

</details>

**Q79.** Is a large-scale Aryan invasion now accepted as the sole explanation for Harappan decline?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Invasion is **not** the accepted sole cause.

## High-level conceptual

**Q85.** Why is the Harappan Civilization called urban? Give five features.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Planned **grid** streets. **Citadel and lower town**. **Burnt bricks 1:2:4**. **Covered drains** and wells. Specialised **crafts and long-distance trade**. Public works (Great Bath, granaries, dock, reservoirs). Economy type in match-lists = **Urban** (2020 Q21). Rigvedic = **pastoral**.

</details>

**Q86.** What are five strong pieces of evidence for Harappan trade?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Lothal dockyard** and boat models (also at Mohenjo-daro). **Meluhha** in Mesopotamian texts. Indus **seals** abroad. **Shortughai** lapis colony. Standard **weights**. Shell, carnelian, and copper/bronze goods.

</details>

**Q87.** Compare Harappan town planning with contemporary Mesopotamian urban centres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Harappa** stresses grid, brick ratio, and covered drains, with **no** palace-ziggurat skyline. **Mesopotamia** has named kings, temples, and ziggurats, with less uniform drainage. Both are Bronze Age and traded.

</details>

**Q88.** Why is the Harappan Civilization considered technologically advanced despite the absence of iron?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bronze lost-wax casting, standardised weights, baked bricks, planned water and drains, bead drills, and cotton.** Iron is a later Gangetic lock, not a Harappan failure.

</details>

**Q89.** What does the Harappan drainage system tell us about their administration?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Civic rules over private waste** — house outlets into street drains imply some city authority, not only household custom.

</details>

**Q90.** Why is the Great Bath historically significant?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is the clearest public ritual tank**, unique to **Mohenjo-daro**, with bitumen-style watertight brickwork and surrounding rooms.

</details>

**Q91.** What is the significance of Dholavira’s water-management system?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rock-cut reservoirs and storm-water channels** in an arid island setting. Planning here is **stone + water**, not only Indus brick drains.

</details>

**Q92.** Why is Lothal important for understanding Harappan maritime trade?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dock, warehouse, bead factory, boat models, and a Gulf-facing Gujarat location** — the standard maritime lock.

</details>

**Q93.** Why is Kalibangan important for agriculture and religion?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ploughed field plus fire altars.** Drains are poorer than at Mohenjo-daro. Site is **Rajasthan / Ghaggar**.

</details>

**Q94.** Why is Rakhigarhi important for the geographical scale of the Harappan Civilization?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is among the largest sites**, in **Hisar, Haryana**, showing a huge Indian cluster beyond the Indus in Pakistan. Trap: calling it UP or the easternmost site.

</details>

**Q95.** What are the major limitations in reconstructing Harappan political and religious life?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The script is unread.** There are **no** named kings or temple labels. Seals and figurines are **mute**. Palaces are unidentified.

</details>
