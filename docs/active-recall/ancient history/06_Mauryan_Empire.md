## Foundation and chronology

**Q1.** Around which year was the Mauryan Empire founded, by whom, and whom did he overthrow?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: About 322/321 BCE. Chandragupta Maurya** overthrew **Dhana Nanda** with **Chanakya / Kautilya / Vishnugupta**. Greeks call him **Sandrokottos**. **William Jones** fixed the synchronism. Range of the house: **c. 322–185 BCE**.

</details>

**Q2.** Name the three great Mauryas in order, with rough dates.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chandragupta (322–297) → Bindusara Amitraghata (297–273) → Ashoka** (accession ~272, consecration **268–232**). Last king **Brihadratha**, killed by **Pushyamitra Shunga** in **185 BCE**.

</details>

## Chandragupta

**Q3.** Which Greek ruler fought Chandragupta, what treaty, what swap?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Seleucus I Nicator, about 305–303 BCE.** Chandragupta got north-west tracts (Arachosia / Gedrosia / Paropamisadae). Seleucus got **500 war elephants**. A marriage alliance is also remembered.

</details>

**Q4.** Who was Megasthenes, which book, which court?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Seleucid envoy at Chandragupta’s court.** ***Indica*** is lost; we read him through **Strabo and Arrian**. He describes Pataliputra, a huge army, and **seven** occupational classes. **Pliny** later cites 6 lakh foot, 30,000 horse, 9,000 elephants.

</details>

**Q5.** Jain end of Chandragupta?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bhadrabahu**; **sallekhana** at **Shravanabelagola**.

</details>

## Ashoka — war and Dhamma

**Q7.** Ashoka’s father, grandfather, pre-imperial posts, accession year?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bindusara; Chandragupta.** Governor of **Ujjain** and, in tradition, **Taxila**. Emperor from **c. 268 BCE**.

</details>

**Q8.** Kalinga War — year, edict, numbers, and was Ashoka a pacifist after it?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: About 261 BCE. Major Rock Edict XIII** — about **1 lakh killed, 1.5 lakh deported**, many more hurt. He drops **bherighosha** for **dhammaghosha**. He is **not** a total pacifist: the army and provinces remain. Policy is **restrained conquest + Dhamma-vijaya**.

</details>

**Q9.** What is Ashoka’s Dhamma, and is it identical with Buddhism?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A state ethical code** — non-violence, respect for parents, compassion, **tolerance of sects**, welfare of humans and animals. It is **not** the Four Noble Truths as a catechism. He patronised Buddhism but Dhamma is **broader imperial policy**. **Dhamma-vijaya** = moral conquest, not more provinces.

</details>

**Q10.** Who were Dhamma-mahamatras, and from which year?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Officers for Dhamma and welfare**, including among sects and border peoples. Created in the **14th regnal year** (RE V). UPPCS **2024**: they are real; **“Dhamma is taken from Rahulovada” is false.** Bhabru lists *Laghulovada* as a **monk’s text**, not the definition of state Dhamma.

</details>

## Inscriptions and edicts

**Q11.** Materials, scripts, language of Ashokan edicts?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rocks and pillars.** **Brahmi** in most of India. **Kharoshthi** at **Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra**. **Greek and Aramaic** at **Kandahar**. Language mostly **Prakrit**. Title **Devanampiya Piyadassi**.

</details>

<details>

<details>

**Ans: Major Rock Edict XIII** — Antiochus, Ptolemy, Antigonus, Magas, Alexander (of Epirus / Corinth).

</details>

## Administration, army, economy

**Q15.** Was the empire centralised, and who sat at the top?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Highly centralised** around the **emperor** at **Pataliputra**, with a **mantriparishad**. Provinces existed, often under **kumaras**.

</details>

**Q16.** Match officials. Row order is **not** the answer.

| Officer | Function |
| ------- | -------- |
| Mahamatra | ? |
| Rajuka | ? |
| Yukta | ? |
| Pradeshika | ? |
| Samaharta | ? |
| Sannidhata | ? |
| Dhamma-mahamatra | ? |
| Agronomai (Megasthenes) | ? |

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Mahamatra** — high officer. **Rajuka** — land and justice in the countryside. **Yukta** — subordinate accounts. **Pradeshika** — provincial circuit. **Samaharta** — **collects** revenue. **Sannidhata** — **stores** the treasury. **Dhamma-mahamatra** — moral/welfare cadre. **Agronomai** = rural **revenue / land** (2020 D), not weights (*pautavadhyaksha*).

</details>

**Q17.** Name the four provincial capitals under kumaras.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Taxila, Ujjain, Tosali, Suvarnagiri.** Far south **Chola, Pandya, Satiyaputra, Keralaputra** are **neighbours**, not provinces.

</details>

**Q18.** Mauryan army components and Megasthenes’ boards?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chaturanga** — infantry, cavalry, chariots, elephants. Head **senapati**. Megasthenes: **six army boards** and **six city boards** (crafts, foreigners, census, and so on).

</details>

**Q19.** Main revenue terms, mines, guilds, Sudarshana Lake?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Bhaga** — produce share, often **one-sixth**. **Bali** — extra / tribute (Rummindei drops it). **Kara** — a tax. Mines under the **state**. **Shrenis** organise crafts and trade. Roads move army and grain. **Sudarshana Lake** (Junagadh, Gujarat) is begun in Chandragupta’s time (Pushyagupta) and later repaired by **Rudradaman**.

</details>

## Arthashastra

**Q20.** Author, Saptanga, Mandala, Matsyanyaya, and the dating caution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Author: Kautilya.** It is **statecraft**, not only economics. Spies: **sanstha** (stationary) and **sanchara** (mobile). **Saptanga:** Swami, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosha, Danda, Mitra. **Mandala** = circle of states. **Matsyanyaya** = fish-law without a king. **Do not** treat the finished book as a photocopy of Mauryan files. Date and layers are debated.

</details>

## Society

**Q21.** Megasthenes on society, women, and dasa?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** He lists **seven** groups and claimed Indians did not use slaves — **Arthashastra contradicts** that with **dasas**. Women appear in work, palace, and law, inside a **patriarchal** frame. Ancient dasa is **not** Atlantic plantation slavery.

</details>

## Art, architecture, extent

**Q22.** What is Mauryan polish, and name key pillars?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mirror-like finish on monolithic sandstone.** Shaft is **one stone**, animal capital on top. **Sarnath Lion Capital** = State Emblem. Abacus: **horse, bull, elephant, lion.** Also Lauriya Nandangarh, Rampurva. **Didarganj Yakshi** shows the same polish.

</details>

**Q23.** Sanchi, Barabar, and who got the caves?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Sanchi** brick core = **Ashoka**. **Barabar** (Bihar) = gift to **Ajivikas**, not Buddhists. **Dasharatha** continues that gift. **Lomas Rishi** has the chaitya-arch facade.

</details>

**Q24.** Extent under Ashoka, and what stayed outside?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kandahar to Bengal, Himalaya to Karnataka.** Extreme **Tamil** south stayed outside as **friendly neighbours**. Afghanistan: **Kandahar** Greek–Aramaic edicts. **Kanaganahalli** labels a king **Raya Asoka**.

</details>

## Decline

**Q25.** Who followed Ashoka, who was last, who killed him, which house next?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dasharatha** (lists vary). Last = **Brihadratha**. Killer = **Pushyamitra Shunga** (185 BCE). Causes: weak heirs, size, provincial **kumaras**, fiscal strain. **Ashoka alone did not** destroy the empire.

</details>

## High-level

**Q29.** Why is this the first subcontinental empire, and how did Ashoka change policy?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Magadha’s resource base + Chandragupta’s war + Chanakya’s statecraft cover most of the north and much of the peninsula. After Kalinga, Ashoka **keeps the state** but sells **Dhamma-vijaya** through **edicts** spoken to subjects in Prakrit.

</details>

**Q30.** Name the major sources for Mauryan history.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ashokan edicts, Arthashastra (with caution), Megasthenes/Strabo, Buddhist and Jain texts, punch-marked coins, NBPW / archaeology, Puranas** (Vishnu Purana lists Mauryas — 2023).

</details>
