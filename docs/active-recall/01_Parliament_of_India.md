## Level 1 — One-line factual recall

**Q1.** Which Article establishes the Parliament of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 79.** It says there shall be a Parliament for the Union.

</details>

**Q2.** Parliament consists of which three components?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha.** The President is a part of Parliament even though he sits in neither House.

</details>

**Q3.** What is the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha under the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 550** under Art. **81** — **530** from the States and **20** from the Union Territories. Present elected strength is **543**. Anglo-Indian nomination of **2** (old textbook **552**) ended with the **104th Amendment**.

</details>

**Q4.** What is the present constitutional maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 250.** Art. **80** allows **238** representatives of States and UTs plus **12** nominated by the President.

</details>

**Q5.** What is the minimum age for Lok Sabha membership?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 25 years** (Art. **84**).

</details>

**Q6.** What is the minimum age for Rajya Sabha membership?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 30 years** (Art. **84**).

</details>

**Q7.** What is the normal term of the Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 5 years** from the date of its first meeting (Art. **83**). During a National Emergency it may be extended by one year at a time, not beyond six months after the Emergency ends.

</details>

**Q8.** Is the Rajya Sabha subject to dissolution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is a continuing House. One-third of members retire every two years.

</details>

**Q9.** What fraction of Rajya Sabha members retire every two years?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: One-third.**

</details>

**Q10.** Who summons and prorogues Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President** (Art. **85**). The President may also dissolve the Lok Sabha.

</details>

**Q11.** Who addresses the first session after each general election?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President.** He also addresses the first session of each year (Art. **87**).

</details>

**Q12.** What is the maximum gap allowed between two sessions of Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Six months.** There must not be more than six months between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next.

</details>

## Level 4 — Money Bill rapid fire

**Q15.** Who decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha.** Art. **110(3)** says the Speaker’s decision is final.

</details>

**Q16.** Can a Money Bill be introduced in the Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It can be introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, and only on the **President’s recommendation**.

</details>

**Q17.** Within how many days must the Rajya Sabha return a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 14 days.**

</details>

**Q18.** Can the Rajya Sabha amend a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It cannot amend on its own.** It may only **recommend** amendments. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject those recommendations.

</details>

**Q19.** What happens if the Rajya Sabha does not return a Money Bill within the prescribed period?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is deemed passed by both Houses** in the form passed by the Lok Sabha.

</details>

**Q20.** Can a joint sitting be held for a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Art. **108** joint sitting does not apply to a Money Bill. Lok Sabha prevails under Art. **109**.

</details>

## Level 5 — Ordinary Bill process

**Q21.** In which House may an ordinary Bill be introduced?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Either House** of Parliament (Art. **107**).

</details>

**Q22.** Name any three stages of a Bill in one House.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: First Reading, Second Reading, Third Reading.** Second Reading includes general discussion, committee stage if referred, and clause-by-clause consideration.

</details>

**Q23.** When can a joint sitting be summoned?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Deadlock on an ordinary Bill** — the other House **rejects** it, the Houses **disagree on amendments**, or more than **six months** pass without the other House passing it. The **President** summons the joint sitting (Art. **108**).

</details>

**Q24.** Who presides over a joint sitting?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha.** In the Speaker’s absence, the Deputy Speaker; then the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. The Vice-President as Chairman of RS does **not** preside.

</details>

**Q25.** Name one Bill for which a joint sitting is not permitted.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Money Bill.** A **Constitution Amendment Bill** (Art. **368**) also cannot go to a joint sitting.

</details>

## Level 6 — Financial control

**Q26.** What is the constitutional name of the Annual Budget?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Annual Financial Statement** (Art. **112**).

</details>

**Q27.** What are Demands for Grants?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Detailed expenditure proposals** of ministries and departments, laid in the Lok Sabha after the Budget. Voting is on these demands.

</details>

**Q28.** Which House votes on Demands for Grants?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Lok Sabha only.** Rajya Sabha may discuss the Budget but has **no** power to vote the demands.

</details>

**Q29.** What is a Guillotine in parliamentary procedure?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Putting the remaining Demands for Grants to vote** without further detailed discussion, once the allotted time is over.

</details>

**Q30.** What is the difference between a Vote on Account and an Interim Budget?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Vote on Account** is a grant for a **short period** (usually two months) so government can spend until the full Budget is passed. An **Interim Budget** is a **full Budget for a short term**, usually presented by a government about to face elections; it covers both receipts and expenditure for that period.

</details>

## Level 7 — Parliamentary devices

**Q31.** Give a one-line definition of **Question Hour**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The first hour** of a sitting, when members ask questions to ministers.

</details>

**Q32.** Give a one-line definition of **Zero Hour**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The time immediately after Question Hour** when members raise matters of urgent public importance. It is an **Indian innovation** and is **not** in the Rules of Procedure.

</details>

**Q33.** Give a one-line definition of a **Starred Question**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Oral answer** on the floor. **Supplementary** questions are allowed. Marked with an asterisk.

</details>

**Q34.** Give a one-line definition of an **Unstarred Question**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Written answer.** **No** supplementary questions.

</details>

**Q35.** Give a one-line definition of a **Short Notice Question**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A question on an urgent matter** asked with **shorter notice** than the usual ten days. Answered orally.

</details>

**Q36.** Give a one-line definition of an **Adjournment Motion**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It interrupts normal business** to discuss a definite matter of **urgent public importance**. It needs the Speaker’s consent and is a censure-type device in the Lok Sabha.

</details>

**Q37.** Give a one-line definition of a **Calling Attention Motion**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A member calls a minister’s attention** to a matter of urgent public importance. The minister makes a brief statement. Indian innovation.

</details>

**Q38.** Give a one-line definition of a **No-Confidence Motion**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It tests whether the Council of Ministers still has the confidence of the Lok Sabha** (Art. **75**). It can be moved **only in the Lok Sabha**. If passed, the Council must resign.

</details>

**Q39.** Give a one-line definition of a **Censure Motion**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It expresses disapproval of a specific policy or action** of the government. It can be moved only in the Lok Sabha. Passage does **not** automatically force resignation unless it is treated as loss of confidence.

</details>

## Level 8 — Officers of Parliament

**Q40.** Who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Vice-President of India** (Art. **64** / Art. **89**).

</details>

**Q41.** Who presides in the absence of the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Deputy Chairman**, elected by the Rajya Sabha from among its members.

</details>

**Q42.** Who elects the Speaker of the Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The members of the Lok Sabha** from among themselves (Art. **93**).

</details>

**Q43.** Who decides questions of disqualification under the Tenth Schedule?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker** (Lok Sabha) or the **Chairman** (Rajya Sabha). The Supreme Court in *Kihoto Hollohan* (1992) held this decision is subject to **judicial review**.

</details>

**Q44.** Can the Speaker vote in the first instance?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The Speaker does not vote in the first instance. The Speaker has only a **casting vote** in case of a tie (Art. **100**).

</details>

**Q45.** What is a casting vote?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The vote of the presiding officer used only to break a tie.**

</details>

## Level 9 — Special powers of Rajya Sabha

**Q46.** Which Article allows the Rajya Sabha to authorise Parliament to legislate on a State List subject?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 249.**

</details>

**Q47.** What majority is required for a resolution under Article 249?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Two-thirds of members present and voting.**

</details>

**Q48.** For how long does such authorisation remain in force initially?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: One year.** It can be renewed by a similar resolution, each time for one year. Laws so made remain in force for **six months** after the resolution ceases.

</details>

**Q49.** Which Article empowers the Rajya Sabha to create All-India Services?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 312.** A resolution of **two-thirds of members present and voting** in the Rajya Sabha is required.

</details>

## Level 11 — Privileges

**Q51.** What are parliamentary privileges?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Special rights, immunities and exemptions** enjoyed by each House, its committees and members, so that they can function without obstruction (Art. **105**).

</details>

**Q52.** Name two privileges enjoyed by members individually.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Freedom of speech in Parliament** (Art. **105(1)**). **Immunity from court proceedings** for anything said, or any vote given, in Parliament or a committee (Art. **105(2)**).

</details>

**Q53.** Name one collective privilege of each House.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The right to regulate its own procedure** and to **punish for breach of privilege** or contempt. Courts do not inquire into the validity of parliamentary proceedings (Art. **122**).

</details>

## Level 12 — Anti-defection

**Q54.** Which Schedule contains anti-defection provisions?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Tenth Schedule.**

</details>

**Q55.** Which constitutional amendment inserted the Tenth Schedule?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 52nd Amendment, 1985.**

</details>

**Q56.** Disqualification occurs on voluntarily giving up membership of which entity?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The political party** on whose ticket the member was elected. Voting or abstaining against the party whip on a specified vote is also a ground, unless prior or later permission is given.

</details>

**Q57.** Who decides the disqualification petition under the Tenth Schedule?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Speaker (LS) or Chairman (RS).** The decision is subject to judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan*, 1992).

</details>

**Q58.** Which amendment deleted the exemption for splits?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 91st Amendment, 2003.** The one-third split exemption was removed. Merger still needs **two-thirds** of the party’s legislators.

</details>

## Level 13 — Conceptual

**Q59.** Why is the Rajya Sabha called a permanent House? Give two implications.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** It is **not dissolved**. **One-third** of members retire every **two years**, so the House continues. Implication 1: there is always a Council of States even after a Lok Sabha election. Implication 2: experienced members remain while new ones join.

</details>

**Q60.** Why is the Council of Ministers collectively responsible only to the Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Art. 75(3)** makes the Council collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**. It remains in office only while it enjoys that House’s confidence. A no-confidence motion cannot be moved in the Rajya Sabha.

</details>

**Q61.** Distinguish between prorogation and adjournment.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Adjournment** ends a **sitting** for a specified time and is done by the **presiding officer**. **Prorogation** ends a **session** and is ordered by the **President**. Pending notices lapse on prorogation; a Bill pending in a House does not lapse.

</details>

**Q62.** Distinguish between dissolution and prorogation.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dissolution** ends the **Lok Sabha itself**. **Prorogation** ends only a **session**. After dissolution, Bills pending in the Lok Sabha lapse; a Bill pending only in the Rajya Sabha (not passed by LS) does not lapse.

</details>

**Q63.** Why can the Rajya Sabha discuss the Budget even though it does not vote on Demands for Grants?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** The Budget is a **national financial statement**. Rajya Sabha has a **deliberative** role. **Voting** on Demands for Grants and the Appropriation Bill’s grant part is a **Lok Sabha** power because the Council of Ministers is responsible to that House.

</details>

**Q64.** How does the Speaker’s certification affect judicial review of a Money Bill? Write the basic constitutional position.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Art. 110(3)** says the Speaker’s decision is **final**. Courts generally show **restraint**. Limited judicial review has been discussed by the Supreme Court (including in the **Aadhaar** and later **Rojer Mathew** line) where certification is alleged to be a fraud on the Constitution.

</details>
