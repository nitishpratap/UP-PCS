## Level 2 — Regulating Act, 1773

**Q11.** What were the major provisions of the Regulating Act of 1773?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** The Governor of Bengal became Governor-General of Bengal with a **four-member** Executive Council. Bombay and Madras were made subordinate to Bengal in war, revenue and diplomacy. A Supreme Court was set up at Calcutta in **1774**. Company servants were restricted from private trade and from accepting presents. The Court of Directors had to report civil, military and revenue affairs to the British government.

</details>

**Q12.** Which presidencies were brought under the control of the Governor-General of Bengal under this Act?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bombay and Madras.** They were made subordinate in war, revenue and diplomacy.

</details>

**Q13.** How many members were there in the Governor-General’s Council under the Regulating Act, 1773?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Four members.** Pitt’s India Act, 1784 later reduced the Council from four to three.

</details>

**Q14.** Which important judicial institution was established at Calcutta under the Regulating Act?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Supreme Court at Calcutta**, established in **1774**.

</details>

**Q15.** Who was the first Chief Justice of this court?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Sir Elijah Impey.**

</details>

**Q16.** What was the major weakness of the Regulating Act of 1773?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ambiguity between the Supreme Court’s jurisdiction and the Governor-General-in-Council.** The Governor-General could also be overruled by his Council. The **Act of Settlement, 1781** later clarified the conflict.

</details>

## Level 4 — Comparison recall

**Q106.** What was the difference between the Regulating Act, 1773 and Pitt’s India Act, 1784?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1773 first brought Company territorial rule under Parliament and created the Governor-General of Bengal plus the Calcutta Supreme Court.** 1784 created Dual Control through the Board of Control and reduced the Governor-General’s Council from four to three.

</details>

**Q107.** How did the Company’s trade position change from 1813 to 1833?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1813 ended the India-trade monopoly except tea and China.** 1833 ended commercial functions completely, including tea and China.

</details>

**Q108.** What changed from 1833 to 1853 regarding legislative functions and civil services?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1833 centralised legislation and only attempted open competition in spirit.** 1853 separated the Legislative Council from the Executive Council and **implemented** open ICS competition.

</details>

**Q109.** What changed from 1858 to 1861 after transfer of power from Company to Crown?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1858 transferred government to the Crown and created the Secretary of State and the Viceroy.** 1861 began Indian nomination to the legislature, restored Bombay and Madras legislative powers, and legally recognised the portfolio system.

</details>

**Q110.** How did Indian representation change from 1892 to 1909?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1892 added indirect election by nomination-on-recommendation, plus budget discussion and questions.** 1909 enlarged councils, allowed an Indian on the Viceroy’s Executive Council, and introduced **Muslim separate electorates**.

</details>

**Q111.** What was the major difference in the electoral system between 1909 and 1919?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1909 used limited and largely indirect communal representation, with separate electorates for Muslims.** 1919 introduced **direct elections** on a wider though still limited franchise, and extended separate electorates to more communities.

</details>

**Q112.** What happened to dyarchy from 1919 to 1935?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1919 introduced dyarchy in the provinces.** 1935 abolished provincial dyarchy and replaced it with provincial autonomy, while proposing dyarchy at the Centre which never ran.

</details>

**Q113.** What was the fundamental difference between the Government of India Act, 1935 and the Indian Independence Act, 1947?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1935 was a British scheme of limited self-government inside the Empire.** 1947 ended British rule, created two independent dominions, and made each Constituent Assembly sovereign for constitution-making.

</details>

## Level 5 — Ten-minute Act chain

For each Act recall only four things: **why → major provision → first or unique feature → constitutional legacy**.

**Q119.** Reconstruct **1773**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: first Parliamentary regulation of Company rule. Major provision: Governor-General of Bengal with a four-member Council. Unique: Supreme Court at Calcutta, 1774, Impey. Legacy: centralised Bengal supremacy over Bombay and Madras.

</details>

**Q120.** Reconstruct **1784**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: cure 1773 defects. Major provision: Dual Control. Unique: Board of Control. Legacy: Crown political supremacy while Company trade continued.

</details>

**Q121.** Reconstruct **1813**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: charter renewal and free-trade pressure. Major provision: India-trade monopoly ended except tea and China. Unique: ₹1 lakh for education; missionaries allowed. Legacy: state role in education.

</details>

**Q122.** Reconstruct **1833**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: centralise British Indian government. Major provision: Governor-General of India; Company purely administrative. Unique: Bentinck; Law Member Macaulay. Legacy: all-India executive and central legislation.

</details>

**Q123.** Reconstruct **1853**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: last charter, open-ended Company future. Major provision: Legislative Council separated from Executive. Unique: open ICS competition implemented. Legacy: competitive civil service.

</details>

**Q124.** Reconstruct **1858**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: Revolt of 1857. Major provision: Crown rule; Secretary of State with 15-member Council of India. Unique: first Viceroy Canning. Legacy: Parliamentary responsibility for Indian administration.

</details>

**Q125.** Reconstruct **1861**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: associate Indians after 1857 and restore provincial legislation. Major provision: nominated Indians; Bombay and Madras legislatures restored. Unique: portfolio system legally recognised (Canning). Legacy: departmental cabinet working and legislative decentralisation.

</details>

**Q126.** Reconstruct **1892**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: nationalist demand for more voice. Major provision: more non-officials; budget discussion; questions. Unique: indirect election by nomination-on-recommendation. Legacy: first limited financial discussion in councils.

</details>

**Q127.** Reconstruct **1909**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: Morley–Minto association-without-responsibility. Major provision: enlarged councils; Indian on Viceroy’s Executive Council. Unique: Muslim separate electorates; S.P. Sinha. Legacy: communal electorate (later rejected by the Constitution).

</details>

**Q128.** Reconstruct **1919**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: Montagu 1917 promise of responsible government. Major provision: provincial dyarchy; bicameral Centre; direct elections. Unique: PSC path (Central PSC 1926); Simon after 10 years. Legacy: parliamentary Centre and PSC idea.

</details>

**Q129.** Reconstruct **1935**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: longest pre-independence statute after RTCs. Major provision: provincial autonomy; three lists 59/54/36. Unique: Federation and Centre dyarchy never ran; Federal Court 1937. Legacy: largest structural borrow into the Constitution.

</details>

**Q130.** Reconstruct **1947**.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Why: Mountbatten Plan and transfer of power. Major provision: two dominions from 15 August 1947; SoS abolished. Unique: Mountbatten first GG of independent India; Rajagopalachari first Indian GG. Legacy: sovereign Constituent Assemblies and end of British Parliamentary authority.

</details>
