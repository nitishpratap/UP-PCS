</details>

**Q2.** Which Articles deal with elections in that Part?

<details>

**Ans: Articles 324 to 329.** Seat reservation sits nearby at **330–334**. Local polls are **243K / 243ZA**.

</details>

**Q3.** Which Article establishes the Election Commission of India?

<details>

**Ans: Article 324.**

</details>

**Q4.** What is the constitutional status of the Election Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is a constitutional body.** Independent of the executive in superintendence of the named elections.

</details>

**Q5.** What does Article 324 broadly provide?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Superintendence, direction and control** of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, the President and the Vice-President, vested in the Election Commission.

</details>

**Q6.** What elections are conducted or supervised by the Election Commission of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, State Assemblies, State Legislative Councils, President, Vice-President.** Not Speaker. Not Panchayat or municipal polls. President/VP **disputes** go to the Supreme Court under **Article 71**.

</details>

**Q7.** Does the ECI conduct elections to the Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, State Assemblies, State Councils, President, Vice-President, Panchayats and Municipalities?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes to the first six. No to Panchayats and Municipalities** (State Election Commission).

</details>

**Q8.** Which elections are not conducted by the Election Commission of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Panchayat and municipal elections, and the election of Speaker / Deputy Speaker / Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman** (the House elects those).

</details>

**Q9.** Which body conducts Panchayat and Municipality elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The State Election Commission.**

</details>

**Q10.** What is the constitutional basis for the State Election Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243K** (Panchayats) and **Article 243ZA** (Municipalities) — the **same** Commission.

</details>

## Round 2 — Election Commission of India

**Q11.** Who appoints the Chief Election Commissioner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President.** Under the **CEC Act, 2023**, a Selection Committee of the **PM, LoP in the Lok Sabha, and a Union Cabinet Minister** recommends. The **CJI is not** on that 2023 panel.

</details>

**Q12.** Who appoints the other Election Commissioners?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, by the same 2023 process.

</details>

**Q13.** What is the constitutional position of the Election Commission regarding the executive?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is independent in poll superintendence.** The President and Governors must provide staff when asked (**324(6)**).

</details>

**Q14.** Is the Election Commission a single-member or multi-member body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Multi-member since 1 October 1993** (CEC + two ECs). It was single from 1950, briefly three in 1989, then one again in 1990. Decisions are by **majority**. Votes are equal. **Removal is not equal.**

</details>

**Q15.** Who determines the number of Election Commissioners?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, from time to time (**324(2)**).

</details>

**Q16.** Who acts as the Chairman of the Election Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Chief Election Commissioner.**

</details>

**Q17.** What is the tenure or age limit of the CEC and Election Commissioners under current law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 6 years or age 65**, whichever is earlier (CEC Act **2023**). Salary is aligned with the **Cabinet Secretary**, not an SC judge.

</details>

**Q18.** What is the removal procedure for the Chief Election Commissioner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The same as a Supreme Court judge** — Parliament address, proved misbehaviour or incapacity (**324(5)**).

</details>

**Q19.** Can the Chief Election Commissioner be removed by the President at pleasure?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q20.** On what grounds can the Chief Election Commissioner be removed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Proved misbehaviour or incapacity**, by the impeachment-style process used for an SC judge. Service conditions cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment.

</details>

**Q21.** What is the removal procedure for other Election Commissioners?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the CEC.**

</details>

**Q22.** Can other Election Commissioners be removed independently by the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** CEC recommendation is required.

</details>

**Q23.** Who can recommend removal of an Election Commissioner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Chief Election Commissioner.**

</details>

**Q24.** What is the constitutional significance of security of tenure for the CEC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It keeps the referee independent of the executive.** Other ECs have a weaker shield.

</details>

## Round 3 — Electoral roll and voters

**Q25.** What is universal adult suffrage?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Every adult citizen may vote**, without a property, tax or literacy test. India used it from the **first** Lok Sabha.

</details>

**Q26.** Which Article provides for elections based on adult suffrage?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 326** (Lok Sabha and State Assemblies). Not 324.

</details>

**Q27.** What is the current minimum voting age in India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 18 years.** Contesting LS/Assembly is still **25**; Rajya Sabha **30**.

</details>

**Q28.** Which Constitutional Amendment reduced the voting age from 21 to 18?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 61st Amendment, 1988**, in force **28 March 1989**. Not the 62nd, 63rd or 86th.

</details>

**Q29.** Can a person vote in more than one constituency?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** One person, one vote.

</details>

**Q30.** Can a person be registered as a voter in more than one electoral roll?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q31.** What are the basic qualifications for voter registration?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Citizen of India, 18 or more on the qualifying date, ordinarily resident in the constituency, not disqualified.** A person in prison or police custody cannot vote (**s.62(5)**), even if on the roll. Preventive detention is the usual exception.

</details>

**Q32.** Can a non-citizen vote in Indian elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q33.** Can an NRI register as a voter?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes, as an overseas elector** in the home constituency, as the rules allow. Default is still to vote there unless a special postal scheme is notified.

</details>

**Q34.** What is the difference between an elector and a candidate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An elector is on the roll and may vote at 18.** A candidate must also meet **Arts. 84 / 173** (age, oath, citizenship) and RPA nomination rules.

</details>

**Q35.** What is meant by a general electoral roll?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: One territorial roll for all electors in that constituency** (Article **325**). EPIC is identity, not a substitute for being on the roll.

</details>

**Q36.** Does the Constitution permit separate electoral rolls based on religion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Article **325** bars a special roll on religion, race, caste or sex. SC/ST **reservation of a seat** is not a communal roll — all electors still vote.

</details>

## Round 4 — Electoral systems

**Q37.** What electoral system is generally used for Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: First-past-the-post** in single-member territorial constituencies.

</details>

**Q38.** What is the First-Past-the-Post system?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The candidate with the most votes wins.** Two-member seats ended in **1961**. Elected LS strength is **543**.

</details>

**Q39.** Does a candidate need more than 50% of votes to win under FPTP?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** A plurality is enough.

</details>

**Q40.** Which voting system is used for Lok Sabha, State Assembly, Rajya Sabha, President and Vice-President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: LS and Assembly = FPTP. Rajya Sabha, President and Vice-President = proportional representation by STV.** Legislative Councils also use PR-STV in the listed slices.

</details>

**Q41.** What is proportional representation by means of the Single Transferable Vote?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Electors rank candidates.** Surplus and eliminated votes transfer until seats fill (Droop quota idea). India does **not** use a national party-list for the Lok Sabha.

</details>

**Q42.** Why is STV used for the election of President and Vice-President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To reflect a ranked, more proportional choice in a small electoral college**, with a secret ballot.

</details>

**Q43.** Why is STV used for Rajya Sabha elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: MLAs elect that State’s Rajya Sabha seats by STV.** Party voting is by **open ballot (2003)** to check cross-voting — not the same secrecy as President/VP.

</details>

**Q44.** Is voting in Presidential and Vice-Presidential elections conducted by secret ballot?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Rajya Sabha party vote is **open**.

</details>

**Q45.** What is the difference between FPTP and proportional representation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: FPTP rewards the plurality in one constituency.** PR-STV transfers ranked preferences so minorities in a college can still win a seat.

</details>

**Q46.** Who forms the electoral college for the election of the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Elected MPs of both Houses plus elected MLAs of the States and of Delhi and Puducherry** (Article **54**).

</details>

**Q47.** Do nominated MPs participate in the Presidential election?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q48.** Do elected members of State Legislative Assemblies participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q49.** Do members of State Legislative Councils participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q50.** Do elected members of the Delhi and Puducherry Legislative Assemblies participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Other UTs without such Assemblies do not.

</details>

**Q51.** Why is the value of votes different for different MLAs?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To weight States by population** so a small Assembly cannot swamp a large State.

</details>

**Q52.** How is the value of an MLA’s vote calculated?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: State’s 1971 population ÷ (elected MLAs × 1000).**

</details>

**Q53.** How is the value of an MP’s vote calculated?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Total value of all MLA votes ÷ number of elected MPs.**

</details>

**Q54.** What is the purpose of weighted voting in the Presidential election?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parity between the Union and the States as groups, and among States by population** (Article **55**).

</details>

**Q55.** What voting system is used for the Presidential election?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: PR by STV, secret ballot.** Doubts and disputes = **Article 71**, Supreme Court.

</details>

**Q56.** Who elects the Vice-President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Members of both Houses of Parliament**, as one electoral college (Article **66**).

</details>

**Q57.** Do nominated members of Parliament participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, unlike the Presidential election.

</details>

**Q58.** Do State Legislative Assembly members participate in the Vice-Presidential election?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q59.** What voting system is used for the Vice-President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: PR by STV, secret ballot.**

</details>

**Q60.** Why is the Vice-Presidential electoral college different from the Presidential one?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Vice-President is Chair of the Rajya Sabha**, so only Parliament elects him. State MLAs stay out. Nominated MPs stay in.

</details>

## Round 6 — Political parties and the RPA

**Q61.** What is a political party in Indian election law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An association registered with the ECI under s.29A of the RPA, 1951** (RUPP until recognised).

</details>

**Q62.** Who registers political parties in India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Election Commission of India.**

</details>

**Q63.** Under which law is political-party registration carried out?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.**

</details>

**Q64.** What is the significance of Section 29A of the RPA, 1951?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is the registration statute.** Registration is **not** the same as recognition as a national or State party.

</details>

**Q65.** What is the difference between a national party, a State party and a registered unrecognised party?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: RUPP = registered only. State / national = recognised under the Symbols Order, 1968**, after vote-and-seat tests. ECI reviews after general elections. Do not freeze “always six national parties.”

</details>

**Q66.** Who grants recognition to political parties?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The ECI**, under the Election Symbols Order, **1968**.

</details>

**Q67.** What is the significance of recognition as a National or State party?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A reserved symbol, more star campaigners (40 vs 20), broadcast time, and lighter proposer/deposit rules.**

</details>

**Q68.** What electoral benefits can recognised parties receive?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Reserved symbol, star-campaigner list of 40, airtime, and office-land concessions as rules allow.**

</details>

**Q69.** What is an election symbol?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The mark allotted so voters can identify a candidate or party on the ballot or EVM.**

</details>

**Q70.** Who allots election symbols?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The ECI.** In a split, it decides name and symbol (**Para 15** of the Order).

</details>

**Q71.** What is the difference between reserved and free election symbols?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Reserved = recognised national/State parties. Free = RUPPs and independents**, from ECI’s free list.

</details>

**Q72.** What is the Representation of the People Act, 1950 primarily concerned with?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Electoral rolls, allocation of seats, and delimitation machinery.**

</details>

**Q73.** What is the Representation of the People Act, 1951 primarily concerned with?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Conduct of elections, nominations, qualifications and disqualifications, corrupt practices, offences, and election petitions.**

</details>

**Q74.** Which Act deals mainly with allocation of seats, delimitation, electoral rolls and voter qualifications for the roll?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: RPA 1950** (plus Registration of Electors Rules). Adult suffrage itself is **Article 326**.

</details>

**Q75.** Which Act deals mainly with conduct, membership qualifications/disqualifications, corrupt practices, offences and disputes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: RPA 1951.**

</details>

**Q76.** What is an election petition?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The only statutory suit to set aside an LS or Assembly election**, under **Article 329(b)** and the 1951 Act.

</details>

**Q77.** Who decides election disputes relating to Parliamentary and Assembly elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The High Court.** Appeal lies to the Supreme Court. Not the ECI as the trial court.

</details>

**Q78.** Can a person directly approach the Supreme Court to challenge an ordinary election result?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No**, as a first-instance election petition. President/VP disputes go to the **SC under Article 71**.

</details>

**Q79.** Which court generally has jurisdiction over election petitions?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The High Court**, filed within **45 days**.

</details>

## Round 7 — Delimitation and reservation

**Q80.** What is delimitation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Drawing (and redrawing) the boundaries and number of territorial constituencies.**

</details>

**Q81.** Why is delimitation necessary?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: So each seat is as equal in population as practicable**, after a census, subject to the freeze.

</details>

**Q82.** Who conducts delimitation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Delimitation Commission** under a Delimitation Act. The ECI conducts polls; it does **not** itself draw the map, even if the CEC sits on the Commission.

</details>

**Q83.** What is the Delimitation Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A statutory panel** (Acts **1952, 1962, 1972, 2002**). Typical chair is a retired Supreme Court judge. ECI members sit *ex officio*. Associate MPs have **no vote**.

</details>

**Q84.** Is the Delimitation Commission a constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q85.** Is it a statutory body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q86.** Who appoints the members of the Delimitation Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President / Central government under the Act.**

</details>

**Q87.** Are the orders of the Delimitation Commission subject to ordinary judicial review?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Gazette publication = law. **Article 329** bars a court challenge to delimitation or seat-allotment.

</details>

**Q88.** What is the constitutional basis for periodic readjustment of constituencies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 82** (Lok Sabha) and **Article 170(3)** (Assemblies), subject to the freeze in **81(3) / 170**.

</details>

**Q89.** Why was delimitation frozen for several decades?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: So States that cut fertility were not punished with fewer seats.** The **42nd Amendment** froze allocation on the **1971** census till 2000.

</details>

**Q90.** Which Constitutional Amendment extended the freeze on readjustment based on population?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 84th Amendment (2001)** froze State-wise LS/Assembly **totals** till the first census after **2026**. The **87th (2003)** allowed **internal redrawing** on the **2001** census without changing those State totals.

</details>

**Q91.** What is the significance of the 2026 constitutional timeline regarding delimitation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: After the first census following 2026, State-wise seat numbers may change with population.** That is the North–South seat fight. Women’s reservation (**106th**) also waits on that delimitation.

</details>

**Q92.** What constitutional provisions provide reservation of seats for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 330** (LS) and **Article 332** (Assemblies). All electors vote; only SC/ST candidates may contest a reserved seat.

</details>

**Q93.** Is that reservation based on population?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, in proportion to SC/ST population. Which constituency is reserved is the Commission’s call.

</details>

**Q94.** What is the constitutional basis for reservation of seats for SC/ST in the Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 330.**

</details>

**Q95.** What is the constitutional basis for reservation in State Assemblies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 332.**

</details>

**Q96.** What was the original constitutional period for SC/ST political reservation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ten years from 1950** (Article **334**).

</details>

**Q97.** Which Constitutional Amendments extended this reservation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Successive extensions, including the 79th and 95th, then the 104th to 25 January 2030.**

</details>

**Q98.** What is the significance of the 104th Constitutional Amendment?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It extended SC/ST seat reservation to 25 January 2030 and ended Anglo-Indian nomination** under **331 / 333** (effect 25 January 2020).

</details>

**Q99.** What happened to the Anglo-Indian nominated representation after the 104th Amendment?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It ended.** Do not write that two LS seats are still nominated.

</details>

**Q100.** What is the significance of the 106th Constitutional Amendment, 2023, for women’s political representation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: One-third of LS and State Assembly seats for women**, including within the SC/ST quota (Nari Shakti Vandan). PRI/ULB already have **≥1/3** under **243D / 243T**.

</details>

**Q101.** Is women’s reservation in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies immediately applicable?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It did not apply to the 2024 Lok Sabha.

</details>

**Q102.** What constitutional conditions are linked to its implementation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A delimitation after the next census.**

</details>

## Round 8 — Anti-defection and MCC

**Q103.** Which Constitutional Amendment introduced the Anti-Defection Law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 52nd Amendment, 1985.**

</details>

**Q104.** Which Schedule contains the Anti-Defection Law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Tenth Schedule.** The Ninth Schedule is validation of laws, not defection.

</details>

**Q105.** What constitutes defection?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Voluntarily giving up party membership (including by conduct), or voting/abstaining against the whip** unless condoned in 15 days. An independent joining a party after election is also caught. A nominated member may join a party only within **six months**.

</details>

**Q106.** What is the significance of the one-third split provision?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It once saved a split of one-third of the legislature party.** It was **deleted** in 2003.

</details>

**Q107.** Is the one-third split provision still valid?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The **91st Amendment, 2003** removed it.

</details>

**Q108.** What is the current exception relating to merger?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Only a merger**, if **two-thirds** of the legislature party agree.

</details>

**Q109.** What percentage of legislators is required for a valid merger?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not less than two-thirds** of the members of the legislature party.

</details>

**Q110.** Who decides questions of disqualification under the Anti-Defection Law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker or Chairman.** He must decide in a reasonable time (*Keisham Meghachandra*, 2020).

</details>

**Q111.** Can the Speaker’s or Chairman’s decision be challenged in court?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, by judicial review.

</details>

**Q112.** Which landmark case established judicial review over the Speaker’s decision?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kihoto Hollohan (1992).**

</details>

**Q113.** What is the Model Code of Conduct?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: ECI guidelines for parties and candidates**, from announcement of the schedule till results. T.N. Seshan made it bite.

</details>

**Q114.** Is the MCC a constitutional law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q115.** Is the MCC a statutory law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is a **convention**. Teeth come from **324** plus the RPA where a breach is also an offence. The 48-hour silence is also **s.126 RPA**, which **is** statute.

</details>

**Q116.** When does the MCC generally come into force?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: From the date the ECI announces the election programme.**

</details>

**Q117.** Who enforces the MCC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Election Commission.**

</details>

**Q118.** Can violation of every MCC provision automatically lead to criminal punishment?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Only if the act is also an offence under the RPA or IPC.

</details>

**Q119.** What is the purpose of the MCC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A level field** — no mixing official machinery with campaign, no last-minute grants that buy votes.

</details>

**Q120.** What is the difference between the MCC and election law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: MCC is a convention. RPA / IPC are law.** s.126’s 48-hour ban is law, not merely MCC.

</details>

## Round 9 — Expenditure, NOTA, EVM, powers, petitions

**Q121.** What is an election expenditure limit?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A notified ceiling on a candidate’s campaign spend.** Do not freeze a rupee figure.

</details>

**Q122.** Who determines the expenditure limits for candidates?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The ECI, by notification under the RPA.**

</details>

**Q123.** Is there a separate expenditure limit for political parties in the same candidate-ceiling sense?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parties must keep accounts.** Star-campaigner travel of recognised parties (**up to 40 names**) is kept **out** of the candidate’s ceiling except when the campaign is essentially for that candidate. Do not treat party spend as the same numbered cap as a candidate’s.

</details>

**Q124.** What is the significance of maintaining election expenditure accounts?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Q140.** What does Article 324 mean by the ECI’s powers of superintendence, direction and control?

</details>

**Q142.** Can the ECI countermand an election?

<details>

**Q143.** Can the ECI order a re-poll?

</details>

**Q144.** What is a re-poll?

<details>

**Ans: Voting again at that polling station**, not a fresh all-India election.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Returning Officer decides nomination.** Sitting MP/MLA disqualification on 102/191 grounds is **President/Governor on the ECI’s binding opinion (103 / 192)**. Failure to lodge accounts: **ECI under s.10A**. Do not say the ECI is a criminal court.

<details>

**Ans: Yes.**

**Q149.** What is the role of the ECI in enforcing the Model Code of Conduct?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

</details>

**Q150.** What is an election petition? (repeat lock)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The exclusive High Court suit to void an LS/Assembly result** — **329(b)**, 45 days, grounds in **s.100**.

</details>

**Q151.** Who can file an election petition?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A candidate or an elector of that constituency**, as the 1951 Act provides.

</details>

**Q152.** What are grounds on which an election can be challenged?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Corrupt practice; improper acceptance or rejection of nomination; improper reception or rejection of votes; non-compliance that materially affects the result.**

</details>

**Q153.** What are corrupt practices under election law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Section 123 favourites — bribery, undue influence, religion/caste appeal as listed, hiring vehicles, exceeding authorised expenditure, booth capturing.**

</details>

**Q154.** What is booth capturing?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Seizing a polling station to stuff or prevent votes.** It can justify a **re-poll**. It is also a corrupt practice / offence.

</details>

**Q155.** Can an election be challenged before the election process is completed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ordinary courts are barred mid-process by Article 329.** Wait for the petition after the result, except the narrow 324 emergency the ECI itself handles.

</details>

**Q156.** What does Article 329 say about judicial interference in elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No court shall question delimitation or allotment of seats.** An election to Parliament or a State Legislature shall not be called in question except by an **election petition**.

</details>

**Q157.** What is the constitutional principle that election disputes should generally be challenged through the prescribed election-petition mechanism?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 329(b)** — complete the poll, then petition. President/VP = **71**, not 329.

</details>
