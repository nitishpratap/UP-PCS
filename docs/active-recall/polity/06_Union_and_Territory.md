## Round 1 — Article 1

**Q1.** What does Article 1 say about India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.** Names and extent sit in the First Schedule.

</details>

**Q2.** What is the exact constitutional expression used: Federation of States or Union of States?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Union of States.** The word federation is not used in Article 1.

</details>

**Q3.** Why did the Constitution-makers prefer the term Union of States?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ambedkar’s lock: the Union is indestructible and States have no right to secede.** States are destructible because Parliament may reorganise them.

</details>

**Q4.** What are the three categories mentioned in Article 1 regarding India’s territory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Territories of the States. Union Territories. Such other territories as may be acquired (Art. 1(3)).

</details>

**Q5.** What is meant by territories of the States, Union Territories, and territories that may be acquired?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: States** are the units listed in the First Schedule with their own Part VI machinery. **Union Territories** are administered by the President under Part VIII. **Acquired territories** are foreign or other lands that later become part of India under Art. 1(3)(c).

</details>

**Q6.** Is the territory of India the same as the territory of the Union of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The territory of India is wider.

</details>

**Q7.** What is the difference between India, Union of India, and territory of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: India / Bharat is the name of the country.** **Union of India** in the federal sense means the States. **Territory of India** means States plus Union Territories plus acquired territories.

</details>

**Q8.** Can India acquire foreign territory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Article 1(3)(c) expressly contemplates territories that may be acquired.

</details>

**Q9.** By what methods can India acquire territory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: By the ordinary modes of international law** — cession, occupation, accretion, prescription, and conquest (as historically used). Parliament may then provide by **ordinary law**. Cession of Indian land **outward** is a different rule.

</details>

## Round 2 — Articles 2 and 3

**Q10.** What does Article 2 deal with?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Admission into the Union, or establishment, of new States** on such terms as Parliament thinks fit. Classic path: territory that was **not already a State** (Sikkim).

</details>

**Q11.** What is the difference between Article 2 and Article 3?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 2 admits or establishes a new State that was not already a State of the Union.** Article 3 forms or alters **existing** States (and can also form or merge UTs).

</details>

**Q12.** Under Article 2, who has the power to admit or establish new States?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parliament**, by law.

</details>

**Q13.** Under Article 3, who has the power to form or alter States?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parliament**, by law, subject to the President’s recommendation and reference for the State’s views.

</details>

**Q14.** Can Parliament create a new State, increase or diminish a State’s area, and alter its boundaries or name?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes. Article 3 lists all five powers:** form a new State; increase area; diminish area; alter boundaries; alter name.

</details>

**Q15.** Can Parliament alter the boundaries of a State without that State’s consent?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Consent is not required. Only **views** are sought.

</details>

**Q16.** Is the consent of the concerned State binding on Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The State Legislature’s view is **not binding**.

</details>

**Q17.** Who can introduce a Bill under Article 3?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A member in either House of Parliament**, but **only on the President’s recommendation**. The State Legislature cannot itself change the State’s name.

</details>

**Q18.** Can an Article 3 Bill be introduced in the Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** It may be introduced in **either House**.

</details>

**Q19.** What is the role of the President in an Article 3 Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President must recommend introduction.** Before recommending, the President refers the Bill to the concerned State Legislature for its views, and may fix a time-limit.

</details>

**Q20.** When must the President refer the Bill to the concerned State Legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Before recommending introduction**, if the Bill affects the area, boundaries or name of a State.

</details>

**Q21.** Is the President required to accept the opinion of the State Legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Views are sought, not consent.

</details>

**Q22.** What happens if the State Legislature rejects the proposal?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parliament may still pass the Bill.** Silence or a missed time-limit also does not block Parliament.

</details>

**Q23.** Is a constitutional amendment under Article 368 required for changing the boundaries of a State under Article 3?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Article 4 says such a law is **not** an amendment under Article 368. A **simple majority** is enough.

</details>

**Q24.** Why is Article 3 considered evidence of the strong unitary character of the Indian federation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parliament can reorganise States without their consent.** The Union is indestructible; States are destructible.

</details>

## Round 3 — Article 4

**Q25.** What does Article 4 deal with?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Supplemental, incidental and consequential provisions** in laws under Articles 2 and 3, including amendment of the First and Fourth Schedules.

</details>

**Q26.** What is the relationship between Article 3 and Article 4?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 3 is the power. Article 4 is the legal effect.** An Article 3 law may rewrite the Schedules and still is not an Article 368 amendment.

</details>

**Q27.** Are laws made under Articles 2 and 3 considered constitutional amendments under Article 368?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Article 4 says they shall not be deemed amendments under Article 368.

</details>

**Q28.** Why can Parliament pass an Article 3 law through the ordinary legislative process?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Because Article 4 takes it outside Article 368.** Special majority and State ratification are not required.

</details>

**Q29.** Can an Article 3 law amend the First Schedule?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** The First Schedule lists names and territories of States and UTs.

</details>

**Q30.** Can it amend the Fourth Schedule?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** The Fourth Schedule allocates Rajya Sabha seats.

</details>

**Q31.** Why are such changes not treated as amendments under Article 368?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 4 expressly says so.** Reorganisation is treated as ordinary law-making, not as rewriting the Constitution’s amending procedure.

</details>

## Round 4 — State reorganisation

**Q32.** What was the first State created on a linguistic basis in independent India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Andhra State**, on **1 October 1953**. First capital was **Kurnool**, not Hyderabad.

</details>

**Q33.** Which movement led to the creation of Andhra State?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Telugu linguistic-State movement**, after Dhar (1948) and JVP (1948–49) had rejected language as the main basis.

</details>

**Q34.** Who was Potti Sriramulu?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Telugu leader who fasted unto death** and died on **15 December 1952**, forcing the Union to create Andhra State.

</details>

**Q35.** In which year was Andhra State created?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1953** (1 October).

</details>

**Q36.** Why did the Government appoint the States Reorganisation Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Andhra’s creation broke the Dhar/JVP “no language” line** and forced a Union-wide review of State boundaries.

</details>

**Q37.** Who was the chairman of the States Reorganisation Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Justice Fazl Ali.**

</details>

**Q38.** Name the other two members of the SRC.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: K.M. Panikkar and H.N. Kunzru.** Pattabhi Sitaramayya was **JVP**, not SRC. K.T. Shah was not an SRC member.

</details>

**Q39.** When was the SRC appointed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 29 December 1953.** The report was submitted on **30 September 1955**.

</details>

**Q40.** When did the States Reorganisation Act, 1956 come into effect?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1 November 1956.** Result: **14 States and 6 UTs**.

</details>

**Q41.** What was the major basis adopted for reorganising States in 1956?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Language**, as a major factor, not as the sole test.

</details>

**Q42.** Was language the only factor considered by the SRC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It also weighed unity and security, financial-administrative convenience, and welfare. It rejected “one language = one State” as the only test.

</details>

**Q43.** What happened to the distinction between Part A, Part B, Part C and Part D States after the 7th Constitutional Amendment?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It was abolished.** The present scheme of States plus Union Territories replaced A/B/C/D and Rajpramukhs.

</details>

**Q44.** Which Constitutional Amendment is closely associated with the States Reorganisation Act, 1956?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 7th Amendment, 1956.**

</details>

## Round 7 — Union Territories

**Q66.** What is a Union Territory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A territorial unit administered by the Union**, not a full State under Part VI.

</details>

**Q67.** Who administers Union Territories?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, through an Administrator (Art. 239).

</details>

**Q68.** Who is the constitutional head or administrator of a Union Territory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An Administrator appointed by the President.** The Administrator may be designated **Lieutenant Governor**. That designation is not a separate office like a State Governor.

</details>

**Q69.** Are all Union Territories administered in exactly the same manner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Some have no legislature. Delhi, Puducherry and J&K have an Assembly and Council of Ministers.

</details>

**Q70.** Which Union Territories have legislative assemblies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir.** Ladakh does **not**.

</details>

**Q71.** Which Union Territory has a Lieutenant Governor and an elected legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Delhi, Puducherry and J&K** all have an LG plus an elected legislature. Delhi’s special code is Article **239AA**.

</details>

**Q72.** Which Union Territory has a special constitutional provision under Article 239AA?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The National Capital Territory of Delhi.**

</details>

**Q73.** What is the significance of Article 239AA?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It creates Delhi’s Legislative Assembly and Council of Ministers**, with a reserved triplet of **public order, police and land** for the Centre.

</details>

**Q74.** Which Constitutional Amendment inserted Article 239AA?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 69th Amendment, 1991.** The 70th Amendment (1992) put Delhi and Puducherry MLAs in the Presidential electoral college.

</details>

**Q75.** What is the special status of Delhi?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is a Union Territory styled National Capital Territory**, with an Assembly, but not a full State. NCR is a separate **planning** region under a 1985 statute, not the 69th Amendment.

</details>

## Round 10 — Acquisition and cession

**Q96.** What is the difference between acquisition of territory and cession of territory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Acquisition adds foreign territory to India.** Cession transfers Indian territory to a foreign State.

</details>

**Q97.** Can the Union acquire foreign territory without a constitutional amendment?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Parliament may provide by **ordinary law**. Cession outward needs an amendment.

</details>

**Q98.** Which case is associated with the Berubari Union issue?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: In re Berubari Union (1960)**, a Supreme Court advisory opinion.

</details>

**Q99.** Why was a constitutional amendment considered necessary for transferring Indian territory to another country?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Court held that cession alters Article 1 and the First Schedule** and cannot be done by ordinary law. Preamble plus Article 1 do not authorise giving land away by a mere statute.

</details>

**Q100.** Which Constitutional Amendment dealt with the Berubari agreement?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 9th Amendment, 1960** (Nehru–Noon, transfer to Pakistan). The **100th Amendment, 2015** later implemented the India–Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement.

</details>

**Q101.** What is the difference between Article 3 reorganisation and cession of Indian territory to a foreign country?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 3 redistributes territory inside India** by ordinary law. **Cession to a foreign State** needs a **constitutional amendment**.

</details>

## Round 12 — Memory map

**Q107.** Complete the Articles 1–4 map.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:**

| Article | Lock |
|---------|------|

| 2 | Admission / establishment of **new States** |
| 3 | Form a State; increase / decrease **area**; alter **boundaries**; alter **name** |

**Q108.** Recite the Article 3 procedure and the UPPCS trap.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bill → President’s recommendation → State Legislature’s views → Parliament (simple majority, either House) → President.** Trap: consent is **not** required. Views are **sought**. Views are **not** binding.

</details>

**Q109.** Recite the State reorganisation chain from 1953 to 2014.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** 1953 Andhra State → 1953 SRC (Fazl Ali) → 1956 SRA + 7th Amendment → 1960 Maharashtra and Gujarat → 1963 Nagaland → 1966 Punjab/Haryana → 1971 Himachal Pradesh State → 1972 Manipur, Tripura, Meghalaya → 1975 Sikkim → 2000 Chhattisgarh, Uttaranchal, Jharkhand → 2014 Telangana. Present map: **28 States + 8 UTs**.

</details>
