**Q10.** What is the constitutional objective of Panchayati Raj?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Units of self-government at the village level**, giving flesh to DPSP **Article 40**. Local government remains a **State List** subject. The 73rd supplies only the skeleton.

</details>

**Q11.** What is a Gram Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The body of persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village** within a Panchayat area.

</details>

**Q12.** Who constitutes the Gram Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: All registered electors of the village**, not only the elected Panches.

</details>

**Q13.** Is Gram Sabha a constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes** (Art. 243A). Its actual powers are fixed by **State law**.

</details>

**Q14.** Which Article deals with Gram Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243A.**

</details>

**Q15.** What is the difference between Gram Sabha and Gram Panchayat?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Gram Sabha is the village electorate (direct democracy).** Gram Panchayat is the elected executive body at the village level.

</details>

**Q16.** What are the three levels of Panchayati Raj generally provided under the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Village, intermediate and district.**

</details>

**Q17.** Which Article provides for the three-tier structure?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243B.**

</details>

**Q18.** Is the three-tier system mandatory for every State?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Village and district are mandatory.** The intermediate tier may be skipped if the State’s population is **not more than 20 lakh**.

</details>

**Q19.** Which category of States can have a two-tier Panchayati Raj system?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: States with population not exceeding 20 lakh** (for example Goa and Sikkim).

</details>

**Q20.** Why was the two-tier exception provided?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A small State does not need a block-level tier** between village and district.

</details>

**Q21.** What are the three levels?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Village, intermediate and district.** In UP the names are Gram Panchayat, **Kshetra Panchayat** and Zila Panchayat.

</details>

**Q22.** Which is the village-level institution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Gram Panchayat.**

</details>

**Q23.** Which is the intermediate-level institution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Panchayat Samiti / Block / Mandal.** In UP it is **Kshetra Panchayat**.

</details>

**Q24.** Which is the district-level institution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Zila Parishad / Zila Panchayat.**

</details>

**Q25.** Which Article deals with elections to Panchayats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243K.**

</details>

**Q26.** Who conducts Panchayat elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The State Election Commission.**

</details>

**Q27.** Who appoints the State Election Commissioner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Governor.**

</details>

**Q28.** Is the State Election Commissioner independent of the State Government?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, in tenure and conditions of service. Conditions cannot be varied to disadvantage after appointment.

</details>

**Q29.** Who can remove the State Election Commissioner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Only by the process used for a High Court judge**, not at the Governor’s pleasure.

</details>

**Q30.** Is the removal procedure similar to that of a High Court judge?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Do not mix this with the CEC, who is removed like a **Supreme Court** judge.

</details>

**Q31.** Can Parliament conduct Panchayat elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Superintendence is with the SEC.

</details>

**Q32.** Can the Election Commission of India conduct Panchayat elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The ECI conducts Parliament and State legislature elections only.

</details>

**Q33.** Which Article deals with reservation in Panchayats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243D.**

</details>

**Q34.** What proportion of seats is constitutionally reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: In proportion to their population** in the Panchayat area, rotated among constituencies.

</details>

**Q35.** Is reservation for SC/ST based on their population in the Panchayat area?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q36.** What does the Constitution say about reservation of offices of Chairpersons for SC/ST?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chairperson offices are also reserved in proportion**, and rotated among Panchayats.

</details>

**Q37.** Is reservation for women constitutionally mandated?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** The floor is **not less than one-third**, including of SC/ST seats and of chairperson offices.

</details>

**Q38.** What minimum proportion of seats must be reserved for women?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not less than one-third** of seats filled by direct election. The constitutional floor is **1/3, not 50%**. UP’s 50% is by **State law**.

</details>

**Q39.** Does women’s reservation apply to general seats, SC seats and ST seats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Not less than one-third of SC seats and of ST seats must also be for women. That is included in the overall one-third, not an extra stack.

</details>

**Q40.** Does the Constitution provide reservation for OBCs in Panchayats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It allows the State to provide it.** It is not a 73rd mandate.

</details>

**Q41.** If yes, is it constitutionally mandatory or can States provide it through legislation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Optional, by State law.**

</details>

**Q42.** Can States provide more than one-third reservation for women?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** Several States, including **UP**, provide **50%** by State law.

</details>

**Q43.** What is the normal tenure of a Panchayat?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Five years** from the date of its first meeting (Art. 243E).

</details>

**Q44.** Can a Panchayat be dissolved before five years?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, under State law.

</details>

**Q45.** What happens if a Panchayat is dissolved before completing its term?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An election must generally be held within six months.** The new body sits only for the **remainder** of the original five-year term.

</details>

**Q46.** Within what period must elections generally be held after dissolution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Before the expiry of six months.**

</details>

**Q47.** What happens if the remaining period of the dissolved Panchayat is less than six months?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An election for that remainder need not be held.**

</details>

**Q48.** Can the term of a Panchayat be extended during a National Emergency?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Unlike the Lok Sabha, Part IX has no Emergency-extension clause.

</details>

**Q49.** Can a Panchayat continue indefinitely without elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Elections must be completed before the five-year term expires (*Kishan Singh Tomar*).

</details>

**Q50.** Which Article deals with the powers and responsibilities of Panchayats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243G**, read with the Eleventh Schedule.

</details>

**Q51.** Does the Constitution itself directly transfer all 29 subjects to Panchayats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The State **may** endow them. The Schedule is a list, not an automatic transfer.

</details>

**Q52.** Who decides the extent of powers and responsibilities of Panchayats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The State legislature**, by law.

</details>

**Q53.** Which Article deals with Panchayat taxation and funds?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243H.**

</details>

**Q54.** Can Panchayats levy taxes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes, if the State authorises it.**

</details>

**Q55.** Who authorises Panchayats to levy taxes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The State legislature.**

</details>

**Q56.** What is the role of the State Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It recommends tax sharing, assignment of taxes, grants-in-aid, and measures to improve PRI finances.**

</details>

**Q57.** Which Article provides for the State Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243I.** The Governor constitutes it. Municipal review uses **243Y**, which applies 243I.

</details>

**Q58.** How frequently is the State Finance Commission constituted?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Every five years**, or earlier.

</details>

**Q59.** Who constitutes the State Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Governor.** The report is laid before the State legislature with an action-taken memorandum.

</details>

**Q60.** What is the relationship between the State Finance Commission and Panchayat finances?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The SFC is the State-level analogue of Article 280** for Panchayats (and Municipalities). Non-implementation is political, not a constitutional void.

</details>

**Q61.** Which Article deals with the District Planning Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243ZD** (in Part IXA, but it consolidates **both** Panchayat and municipal plans).

</details>

**Q62.** What is the purpose of the District Planning Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To consolidate plans of Panchayats and Municipalities in the district** and prepare a draft development plan for the district.

</details>

**Q63.** Who constitutes the District Planning Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The State**, by law.

</details>

**Q64.** What proportion of its members are elected?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not less than four-fifths.**

</details>

**Q65.** From whom are members of the District Planning Committee elected?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: From the elected members of the district Panchayat and of the Municipalities**, in proportion to rural and urban population.

</details>

**Q66.** What does the District Planning Committee consolidate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities in the district.**

</details>

**Q67.** What is the difference between a District Planning Committee and a Metropolitan Planning Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: DPC is for every district (rural + urban) with ≥ 4/5 elected.** MPC is only for a metropolitan area (≥ 10 lakh) with ≥ 2/3 elected (Art. 243ZE).

</details>

</details>

</details>

**Q87.** What is a Municipal Corporation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

</details>

**Q88.** Which Article deals with the composition of Municipalities?

**Ans: Article 243R.**

</details>

**Q89.** Are all members of a Municipality directly elected?

## Part C — Co-operative Societies

**Q125.** Which Constitutional Amendment gave constitutional status to co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

</details>

**Q126.** Which Part was inserted into the Constitution for co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Part IXB** (Arts. 243ZH–243ZT).

</details>

**Q127.** Which Articles deal with co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Articles 243ZH to 243ZT**, plus DPSP **43B** and FR **19(1)(c)** (right to form co-operative societies).

</details>

**Q128.** Which Article deals with incorporation and regulation of co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243ZI.**

</details>

**Q129.** Which Amendment added Article 43B?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 97th Amendment.**

</details>

**Q130.** Where is Article 43B located?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Part IV — Directive Principles.**

</details>

**Q131.** Is Article 43B a Fundamental Right or a Directive Principle?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Directive Principle.** The Fundamental Right is forming co-operatives under **19(1)(c)**.

</details>

**Q132.** What does Article 43B seek to promote?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management** of co-operative societies.

</details>

**Q133.** Which Schedule was added specifically for co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: None.** The 97th did **not** add a new Schedule. Co-operatives are **not** in the Eleventh Schedule.

</details>

**Q134.** Was a new Schedule actually created for co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q135.** What are the major constitutional principles governing co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Voluntary and open membership, democratic member control, member economic participation, autonomy, and professional management** (Art. 43B plus Part IXB).

</details>

**Q136.** What is meant by voluntary and open membership?

<details>

**Ans: Anyone who can use the services and accept the responsibilities may join**, without arbitrary bar.

</details>

**Q137.** What is meant by democratic member control?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Members control the society through elected boards** and general-body decisions.

</details>

**Q138.** What is meant by member economic participation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Members contribute capital and share surplus** according to co-operative principles.

</details>

**Q139.** What is meant by autonomous functioning?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The society is self-governing**, not a department of the State.

</details>

**Q140.** Why is professional management emphasised?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: So boards are not captured by politics or incompetence**, and accounts and audits stay professional.

</details>

**Q141.** Why is regular election of boards important?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A board shall not be superseded merely to avoid elections.** Tenure is five years from the date of election.

</details>

**Q142.** What is the maximum term of an elected board?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Five years** (Art. 243ZJ). Maximum **21** directors, including reservation for SC/ST and women.

</details>

**Q143.** Can the board of a co-operative society be superseded?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, in specified cases (Art. 243ZL).

</details>

**Q144.** What is the maximum period for which a board can generally be superseded?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Six months.** For a co-operative **bank**, up to **one year**.

</details>

**Q145.** Are there exceptions to this limit?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes — co-operative banks (one year)** to align with banking regulation. A board is not to be superseded only to skip elections.

</details>

**Q146.** Which type of co-operative society falls under the Union’s regulatory domain?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Multi-State co-operative societies** (Union List Entry 44). State co-operatives are State List Entry 32.

</details>

**Q147.** Which authority regulates multi-State co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Central Registrar** under Union law.

</details>

**Q148.** Which law governs multi-State co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002.**

</details>

**Q149.** Does the 97th Amendment regulate only multi-State co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It tried to cover all co-operatives.** After *Rajendra N. Shah* (2021), **Part IXB survives for multi-State societies only.**

</details>

**Q150.** What is the constitutional distinction between State co-operative societies and multi-State co-operative societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: State co-ops = State List, State law.** Multi-State = Union List, MSCS Act. Part IXB is now inoperative for State co-ops.

</details>

**Q151.** Was the 97th Constitutional Amendment completely upheld by the Supreme Court?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q152.** Which part of the 97th Amendment was struck down?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Part IXB, insofar as it applied to State co-operative societies.** Arts. **19(1)(c)** and **43B** were left intact.

</details>

**Q153.** Why was that portion struck down?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It needed ratification by half the States under the proviso to Article 368(2)** because it touched the State List, and that ratification was not obtained.

</details>

**Q154.** Which constitutional requirement was involved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: State ratification of a federal amendment.**

</details>

**Q155.** Does the invalidation affect State-level co-operative societies in the same way as multi-State societies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Part IXB is void for **State** co-ops and **survives for multi-State** co-ops (*Union of India vs Rajendra N. Shah*, 2021).

</details>
