## Round 1 — Three buckets

**Q1.** What is a constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An institution whose existence, composition, powers or functions are provided in the Constitution itself.**

</details>

**Q2.** What is a statutory body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A body created by an Act of Parliament or a State Legislature.**

</details>

**Q3.** What is an executive or non-statutory body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A body created by government resolution or order**, without a constitutional article and without a parent creating Act of the CVC/Lokpal type.

</details>

**Q4.** What is the main difference between a constitutional body and a statutory body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Constitutional = written into the Constitution. Statutory = written into an ordinary law.** Repealing an Act cannot kill a constitutional body; a 368 amendment would be needed to delete the article.

</details>

**Q5.** What is the main difference between a statutory body and an executive body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Statutory needs an Act. Executive needs only a resolution or order.**

</details>

**Q6.** Can Parliament create a statutory body without amending the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** An ordinary Act is enough.

</details>

**Q7.** Can an executive body be created without passing a law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** NITI Aayog is the lock (Cabinet Resolution, 2015).

</details>

**Q8.** Which type of body can generally be abolished by repealing its parent Act?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A statutory body.**

</details>

**Q9.** Which type of body can generally be abolished by executive order?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An executive body.**

</details>

**Q10.** Is every government body a constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Most are statutory or executive.

</details>

## Round 2 — NHRC

**Q11.** Is the National Human Rights Commission a constitutional or statutory body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Statutory.** It is **not** Article 338.

</details>

**Q12.** Under which Act was the NHRC established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.**

</details>

**Q13.** In which year was the Protection of Human Rights Act enacted?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1993.**

</details>

**Q14.** In which year was the NHRC established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1993** — founded **12 October 1993**. First Chair was Justice Ranganath Misra.

</details>

**Q15.** What is the principal function of the NHRC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To inquire into human-rights violations by public servants**, visit jails, review laws, and recommend prosecution or compensation.

</details>

**Q16.** What are “human rights” under the Protection of Human Rights Act?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rights relating to life, liberty, equality and dignity** guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in international covenants and enforceable by Indian courts.

</details>

**Q17.** Who appoints the Chairperson and members of the NHRC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, on the recommendation of a selection committee. After 2019, the Chair may be a retired **CJI or a retired Supreme Court judge**.

</details>

**Q18.** What is the role of the selection committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It recommends names to the President.** It is chaired by the **Prime Minister** and includes the Lok Sabha Speaker, the Union Home Minister, Leaders of Opposition in both Houses, and the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

</details>

**Q19.** What is the tenure of NHRC members?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: After the 2019 amendment, 3 years or age 70**, whichever is earlier. Reappointment is allowed. It was originally 5 years or 70.

</details>

**Q20.** What is the maximum age limit for the Chairperson?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 70 years.**

</details>

**Q21.** Can the NHRC directly punish a person for violating human rights?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It can only recommend.

</details>

**Q22.** Does the NHRC have the powers of a civil court while investigating complaints?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, for the inquiry. It still cannot punish.

</details>

**Q23.** Can the NHRC investigate human-rights violations committed by private individuals?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Its core mandate is violations by public servants.** A purely private dispute is not its ordinary beat.

</details>

**Q24.** What is the limitation regarding the armed forces?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Inquiry is restricted.** It works through reports routed via the Centre, not as a court martial. The 2019 amendment did not change that.

</details>

**Q25.** What kind of recommendations can the NHRC make?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Prosecution, compensation, and other relief**, plus law-reform advice. The report goes to the Central government and is laid in Parliament.

</details>

**Q26.** Are NHRC recommendations binding?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** They are recommendatory.

</details>

## Round 4 — CIC

**Q42.** Is the Central Information Commission constitutional or statutory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Statutory**, under the RTI Act.

</details>

**Q43.** Under which Act was the CIC established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Right to Information Act, 2005.**

</details>

**Q44.** In which year was the RTI Act enacted?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 2005** (enacted 15 June; in force **12 October 2005**). MKSS is the movement lock.

</details>

**Q45.** What is the primary function of the CIC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Second appeals and complaints under the RTI Act** against Central public authorities. It may order disclosure and penalise a PIO.

</details>

**Q46.** Who appoints the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President.**

</details>

**Q47.** What is the composition of the selection committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Prime Minister, Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and a Union Cabinet Minister.** The State Information Commission uses Governor + CM + LoP + a Cabinet Minister.

</details>

**Q48.** What is the tenure or age limit for Information Commissioners under the current law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: After the 2019 amendment, term, salary and conditions are as the Central government prescribes.** The old lock was 5 years or age 65, aligned toward the CEC formula.

</details>

**Q49.** Can the CIC impose a penalty on a Public Information Officer?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, up to **₹25,000**.

</details>

**Q50.** Can the CIC order disclosure of information?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, subject to the Act’s exemptions (including the public-interest override).

</details>

**Q51.** Is the CIC an independent body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is a statutory independent commission**, but the **2019 amendment** cut the old tenure-and-salary shield by leaving those to the government.

</details>

**Q52.** What is the difference between the CIC and a State Information Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: CIC = Central public authorities, appointed by the President.** SIC = State public authorities, appointed by the **Governor**. Both rest on the **same 2005 Act**.

</details>

## Round 5 — CVC

**Q53.** Is the Central Vigilance Commission constitutional or statutory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Statutory since 2003.** It has **no Article**. UPPCS 2020 lock.

</details>

**Q54.** Under which Act was the CVC given statutory status?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The CVC Act, 2003**, after *Vineet Narain*.

</details>

**Q55.** In which year was the CVC originally established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1964**, as an **executive** body, after the Santhanam Committee (1962). First CVC was Nittoor Srinivasa Rau.

</details>

**Q56.** Why was the CVC established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To be the Union’s vigilance watchdog** against corruption in Central organisations. It is **not** a police force.

</details>

**Q57.** Who appoints the Central Vigilance Commissioner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, on the recommendation of the **Prime Minister, Union Home Minister, and Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha**.

</details>

**Q58.** What is the composition of the CVC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Central Vigilance Commissioner plus two Vigilance Commissioners.**

</details>

**Q59.** Who heads the CVC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Central Vigilance Commissioner.**

</details>

**Q60.** What is the tenure of the Central Vigilance Commissioner and Vigilance Commissioners?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 4 years or age 65**, whichever is earlier. No further Central employment afterwards.

</details>

**Q61.** What is the relationship between the CVC and the CBI?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The CVC superintends the CBI in Prevention of Corruption Act cases.** It does **not** appoint the CBI Director. That panel is **PM + LoP LS + CJI**.

</details>

**Q62.** Does the CVC investigate every corruption case itself?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It does not register FIRs or try offences. The CBI or the department investigates.

</details>

**Q63.** What is the CVC’s role regarding Central Government organisations?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Advise ministries, run the Chief Vigilance Officer network, and take whistle-blower complaints (PIDPI).** It reports to the President for laying in Parliament.

</details>

## Round 6 — Lokpal and Lokayukta

**Q64.** Is the Lokpal a constitutional or statutory body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Statutory.**

</details>

**Q65.** Under which Act was the Lokpal established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013.**

</details>

**Q66.** In which year was the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act passed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 2013.**

</details>

**Q67.** When did the Lokpal become operational?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Act came into force on 16 January 2014.** The first Chair, Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose, took office in **March 2019**.

</details>

**Q68.** What is the main objective of the Lokpal?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To inquire into allegations of corruption against specified public functionaries of the Union.**

</details>

**Q69.** Who appoints the Lokpal Chairperson and members?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, on the Selection Committee’s recommendation. A separate **Search Committee** only shortlists names.

</details>

**Q70.** Who constitutes the selection committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Prime Minister (chair), Speaker of the Lok Sabha, Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, CJI or an SC judge nominated by the CJI, and an eminent jurist.** The **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not on the panel** (2024).

</details>

**Q71.** What categories of persons can become Lokpal members?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chairperson plus a maximum of eight members.** At least half must be **judicial**. At least half must be from **SC, ST, OBC, minorities or women**. Tenure is **5 years or age 70**.

</details>

**Q72.** Can the Prime Minister come under the Lokpal’s jurisdiction?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, with filters. It is false to say the PM is wholly outside.

</details>

**Q73.** Are there exceptions or limitations regarding investigation of the Prime Minister?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Full-bench inquiry, and at least two-thirds of members must approve.** No inquiry on international relations, external or internal security, public order, atomic energy, or space. The PM does not sit on that bench.

</details>

**Q74.** Can Members of Parliament come under the Lokpal?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, except speech or vote inside the House. The judiciary as such is also out.

</details>

**Q75.** Does the Lokpal have jurisdiction over Central Government employees?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes** — Groups A to D, plus specified boards, trusts and NGOs receiving notified foreign funds.

</details>

**Q76.** What is the difference between Lokpal and Lokayukta?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Lokpal is the Union ombudsman under the 2013 Act. Lokayukta is a State body under a State Act** (UP Act **1975**; Maharashtra **1971** was first). Design and CM-coverage vary by State.

</details>

**Q77.** Is the Lokayukta established uniformly across all States by the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** There is no constitutional Lokayukta article. The 2013 Union Act asked States to set one up. Many already had one.

</details>

## Round 7 — NGT

**Q78.** Is the National Green Tribunal constitutional or statutory?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Statutory** (NGT Act, **2010**). It is **not** Article 323A (that is CAT).

</details>

**Q79.** Under which Act was the NGT established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.**

</details>

**Q80.** In which year was the NGT established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 2010** — Tribunal constituted **18 October 2010**. Principal Bench is New Delhi.

</details>

**Q81.** What is the principal purpose of the NGT?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Speedy disposal of environmental disputes** under scheduled Acts, aiming at about **six months**.

</details>

**Q82.** What type of cases does the NGT deal with?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Civil environmental disputes** under Acts such as Water 1974, Air 1981, EPA 1986, Forest Conservation 1980, Biological Diversity 2002. The **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** and the **Indian Forest Act, 1927** are **not** in the Schedule.

</details>

**Q83.** Does the NGT have jurisdiction over criminal cases?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is not a criminal court. Appeal lies to the **Supreme Court**. High Court writs still survive (*L. Chandra Kumar*).

</details>

**Q84.** What is the composition of the NGT?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Chair (sitting or retired SC judge or High Court Chief Justice), judicial members, and expert members.**

</details>

**Q85.** What is the role of expert members?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Scientists and engineers sit with judges**, so it is not a purely lawyers’ court.

</details>

**Q86.** Can the NGT award compensation for environmental damage?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, including under the polluter-pays principle.

</details>

**Q87.** What major environmental principles can the NGT apply?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Sustainable development, polluter pays, and the precautionary principle.** It follows natural justice and is not strictly bound by the CPC or the Evidence Act.

</details>

**Q88.** What is the difference between the NGT and ordinary courts?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is a specialised environmental tribunal with experts.** Once it takes up a scheduled dispute, a **civil court shall not** hear that dispute.

</details>

<details>

**Ans: Statutory.** Competition Act **2002**, operational **2009**. It replaced the MRTP Commission.

</details>

**Ans: The Competition Act, 2002.**

</details>

**Q91.** What is the main purpose of the Competition Act?

<details>

</details>

**Q92.** What is the role of the CCI?

<summary>Show answer</summary>

</details>

**Q93.** What is meant by an anti-competitive agreement?

<details>

</details>

**Q94.** What is abuse of dominant position?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Using market power to exclude rivals or exploit consumers**, under section 4 — dominance itself is not illegal.

</details>

**Q95.** What are combinations under competition law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mergers, amalgamations and acquisitions above notified thresholds** (sections 5–6).

</details>

**Q96.** Can the CCI impose penalties?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, often as a percentage of turnover.

</details>

**Q97.** Who appoints members of the CCI?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Central government.** Chairperson plus up to **six** members. Ministry of Corporate Affairs.

</details>

**Q98.** Is SEBI constitutional or statutory?

**Ans: Statutory since 1992.** It began as an **executive** body in **1988**.

</details>

**Q99.** Under which Act did SEBI receive statutory status?

</details>

**Q100.** In which year did SEBI become a statutory body?

<details>

</details>

**Q101.** What is the primary function of SEBI?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Protect investors, regulate the securities market, and register intermediaries.** Quasi-legislative and quasi-judicial.

</details>

**Q102.** What is meant by protecting investor interests?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Fair issue and trading of securities, disclosure, and action against fraud and insider abuse.**

</details>

**Q103.** What is SEBI’s role in regulating the securities market?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It regulates stock exchanges, brokers, mutual funds and listed companies** in the capital market. It is not the RBI, IRDAI or PFRDA.

</details>

**Q104.** Who appoints the SEBI Chairperson?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Central government.**

</details>

**Q105.** Can SEBI impose penalties?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q106.** What does TRAI stand for?

<details>

</details>

**Q107.** Is TRAI constitutional or statutory?

</details>

**Q108.** Under which Act was TRAI established?

</details>

**Q109.** In which year was TRAI established?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1997.**

</details>

**Q110.** What is the primary function of TRAI?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To regulate telecommunication services** — tariffs, interconnection, quality of service — and to protect consumers in that market.

</details>

**Q111.** Does TRAI regulate telecommunications providers?

<details>

**Ans: Yes**, as the sector regulator. It is not SEBI and not CCI.

</details>

**Q112.** What is the difference between TRAI’s regulatory role and the government’s policy-making role?

<details>

</details>

**Q122.** Which organisation did NITI Aayog replace?

</details>

**Q123.** Who is the Chairperson of NITI Aayog?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Prime Minister.**

</details>

**Q124.** Who is the Vice-Chairperson?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Nominated by the PM.** First Vice-Chairperson was **Arvind Panagariya** (2018 PYQ).

</details>

**Q125.** What is the role of the Governing Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is the federal table — PM plus Chief Ministers and Lieutenant Governors.** CEO is Secretary rank.

</details>

**Q126.** Which principle is associated with NITI Aayog’s approach to Centre–State relations?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Cooperative federalism, together with competitive federalism** (indices, rankings, Aspirational Districts).

</details>

**Q127.** What is meant by cooperative federalism?

<details>

**Ans: Centre and States work as a team** on a shared national agenda, rather than the old Plan-fund command model.

</details>

**Q128.** What is meant by competitive federalism?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: States compete on outcomes** through rankings and performance, while the Centre hand-holds laggards.

</details>

**Q129.** Does NITI Aayog allocate funds to States like the former Planning Commission?

</details>

**Q147.** What are NIA Special Courts?
