## Round 1 — What counts

**Q1.** What is a Constitutional Body in the Indian Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An institution whose existence, composition, powers or functions are provided in the Constitution itself**, not merely in an ordinary Act.

</details>

**Q2.** Name the core constitutional bodies that UPPCS expects you to map to Articles.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: ECI, UPSC/SPSC, Finance Commission, CAG, Attorney General, Advocate General, Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, NCSC, NCST, NCBC, GST Council, Inter-State Council, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission.**

</details>

## Round 2 — Instant article map

**Q3.** Which Article provides for the Election Commission of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 324** (cluster **324–329**).

</details>

**Q4.** Which elections does the Election Commission of India conduct?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parliament, State Legislatures (Assembly and Council), President, and Vice-President.** Not Speaker. Not Panchayat or municipal polls.

</details>

**Q5.** Which Articles provide for the Union and State Public Service Commissions?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Articles 315–323.** Article **315** is the parent clause.

</details>

**Q6.** Which Article provides for the Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 280.**

</details>

**Q7.** What is the key function of the Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To recommend distribution of tax proceeds and grants between the Union and the States.** The President constitutes it. The report goes to the **President**, not to NITI Aayog.

</details>

**Q8.** Which Articles provide for the Comptroller and Auditor General?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Articles 148–151.**

</details>

**Q9.** Which Article provides for the Attorney General of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 76.** Highest law officer of the Union.

</details>

**Q10.** Which Article provides for the Advocate General of a State?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 165.** Highest law officer of the State.

</details>

**Q11.** Which Article provides for the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 350B** (7th Amendment, 1956 — **not** the 9th).

</details>

**Q12.** Which Article provides for the National Commission for Scheduled Castes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 338.** The SC list is Article **341**.

</details>

**Q13.** Which Article provides for the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 338A** (89th Amendment, 2003). The ST list is Article **342**.

</details>

**Q14.** Which Article provides for the National Commission for Backward Classes as a standing constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 338B** (102nd Amendment, 2018). The Central SEBC list is Article **342A**. Do not confuse with the ad hoc Article **340** commission.

</details>

**Q15.** Which Article provides for the GST Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 279A** (101st Amendment). The President constitutes it. The Union Finance Minister chairs it.

</details>

**Q16.** Which Article provides for the Inter-State Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 263.** It is constitutional and **recommendatory**. It is not NITI Aayog and not a Zonal Council.

</details>

**Q17.** Which Article provides for the State Election Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243K** for Panchayats. The **same** Commission runs municipal elections under Article **243ZA**.

</details>

**Q18.** Which Articles provide for the State Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 243I** (Panchayats). Article **243Y** uses the **same** Commission for municipalities.

</details>

## Round 4 — Constitutional vs statutory vs executive

**Q21.** How is a constitutional body created?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Directly by the Constitution.**

</details>

**Q22.** Give examples of constitutional bodies.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: ECI, CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission, NCSC, NCST, NCBC, GST Council, Inter-State Council, Attorney General, Advocate General.**

</details>

**Q23.** How is a statutory body created?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: By an Act of Parliament or a State Legislature.**

</details>

**Q24.** Give examples of statutory bodies.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: NHRC, CIC, Lokpal, CVC, National Commission for Women, National Green Tribunal.** Zonal Councils are also statutory (States Reorganisation Act, 1956).

</details>

**Q25.** How is an executive or non-constitutional body created?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: By executive resolution or order**, not by a constitutional article and not (in the classic case) by a creating Act.

</details>

**Q26.** What is the classic executive-body example for UPPCS?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: NITI Aayog** (Cabinet Resolution, **1 January 2015**). It replaced the Planning Commission. It did **not** replace the Finance Commission.

</details>

**Q27.** Is the National Human Rights Commission a constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is **statutory** under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. Do not pair it with Article **338**.

</details>

**Q28.** Is the Central Vigilance Commission a constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It has **no Article**. It became statutory under the CVC Act, **2003**.

</details>

**Q29.** Is the Solicitor General of India a constitutional office?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Only the **Attorney General** (Art. **76**) is constitutional. Solicitor General and Additional Solicitor General are statutory/executive law officers.

</details>

**Q30.** Is the National Development Council a constitutional body?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is extra-constitutional (1952). Do not mix it with Article **263**.

</details>

## Round 5 — ECI ≠ SEC

**Q31.** Article 324 Election Commission versus Article 243K State Election Commission — what is the split?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: ECI = Parliament, State Houses, President, Vice-President.** SEC = **Panchayat** (243K) and **municipal** (243ZA) elections.

</details>

**Q32.** Does the Election Commission of India conduct elections to the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q33.** Does the Election Commission of India conduct elections to State Legislative Assemblies and Councils?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q34.** Does the Election Commission of India conduct the elections of the President and the Vice-President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes, it conducts them.** Doubts and disputes of the President/VP election go to the **Supreme Court under Article 71**, not to the ECI.

</details>

**Q35.** Who conducts Panchayat elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The State Election Commission under Article 243K.** The Governor appoints the Commissioner. Removal is like a **High Court judge**.

</details>

**Q36.** Who conducts municipal elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The same State Election Commission, under Article 243ZA.**

</details>

**Q37.** Who elects the Speaker of the Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The House itself.** The ECI does **not** conduct the Speaker’s election.

</details>

## Round 6 — Finance Commission ≠ State Finance Commission

**Q38.** Article 280 versus Article 243I — what is the split?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 280 = Union Finance Commission (Centre ↔ States).** 243I = State Finance Commission (State ↔ local bodies). 243Y applies that same SFC to municipalities.

</details>

**Q39.** Who constitutes the Union Finance Commission, and what is its composition?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President.** Chairperson plus **four** other members. Constituted every **five years** (or earlier).

</details>

**Q40.** To whom does the Finance Commission submit its report?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, who lays it in Parliament with an explanatory memorandum. **Not** NITI Aayog.

</details>

**Q41.** Who constitutes the State Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Governor**, every five years, under Article **243I**.

</details>

**Q42.** Does NITI Aayog replace the Finance Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** NITI replaced the **Planning Commission**. Article **280** still lives.

</details>

## Round 7 — CAG 148–151

**Q43.** What is the one-line identity of the CAG?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The CAG is the auditor of government money** — accounts of the Union and the States after expenditure. India is **Auditor**, not UK-style Comptroller of issue of money.

</details>

**Q44.** What does Article 148 provide?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Appointment of the CAG by the President**, tenure and independent conditions. Removal is like a **Supreme Court judge**. No further office under the Union or a State.

</details>

**Q45.** What does Article 149 provide?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Duties and powers of the CAG**, as prescribed by Parliament (today the DPC Act, 1971).

</details>

**Q46.** What does Article 150 provide?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The form of accounts of the Union and the States** is prescribed by the President **on the advice of the CAG**.

</details>

**Q47.** What does Article 151 provide?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Audit reports.** Union reports go to the **President** (laid in Parliament). State reports go to the **Governor** (laid in the State Legislature). The **PAC** examines the reports. The CAG does not sit in the PAC.

</details>

**Q48.** Is the CAG the same as the Advocate General?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** CAG = **148**. Advocate General = **165**. Attorney General = **76**. “AG” in accounts talk is not the Advocate General.

</details>

## Round 8 — Law officers, PSCs, commissions

**Q49.** How is the Attorney General appointed, and how is he removed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Appointed by the President. Holds office during the pleasure of the President.** Qualification is that of a **Supreme Court judge**. He may speak in the Houses but has **no vote**. Salary is **not** charged. Private practice is allowed except against the Government of India.

</details>

**Q50.** How is the Advocate General appointed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: By the Governor.** Qualification is that of a **High Court judge**. Office is during the **pleasure of the Governor**.

</details>

**Q51.** Who appoints UPSC members, and is UPSC advice binding?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President appoints.** Tenure is **6 years or age 65**. Advice is **not binding**. Expenses are **charged**.

</details>

**Q52.** Who appoints a State PSC, and who removes its members?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Governor appoints. The President removes** (not the Governor). Extra functions for an SPSC are conferred by the **State Legislature** (Art. 321).

</details>

**Q53.** Who chairs the GST Council, and what is the voting rule?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Union Finance Minister.** Centre has **one-third** weight; all States together have **two-thirds**. A decision needs **three-fourths** of the weighted votes. Quorum is **one-half**.

</details>

**Q54.** Who chairs the Inter-State Council, and is it a court?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Prime Minister** (Sarkaria recommendation; set up **1990**). It is recommendatory. Zonal Councils are statutory and are chaired by the **Union Home Minister**.

</details>

**Q55.** Article 338 versus 338A versus 338B versus 340 — remember the four.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 338 = standing NCSC. 338A = standing NCST. 338B = standing NCBC. 340 = ad hoc inquiry commission** (Kalelkar, Mandal), not the standing NCBC.

</details>

**Q56.** Article 350 versus 350A versus 350B — remember the three.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 350 = represent to the State or Union in any language. 350A = mother-tongue at the primary stage (States’ duty). 350B = one Special Officer for linguistic minorities for the whole of India.**

</details>
