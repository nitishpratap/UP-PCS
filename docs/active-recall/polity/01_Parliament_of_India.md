## Round 3 — Lok Sabha

**Q33.** What is the maximum constitutional strength of Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 550** under Art. **81** — **530** from the States and **20** from the Union Territories. Present elected strength is **543**. The old textbook **552** included two Anglo-Indian nominees.

</details>

**Q34.** What is the normal term of Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Five years.**

</details>

**Q35.** What is the minimum age required to become a Lok Sabha member?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 25 years** (Art. 84).

</details>

**Q36.** Who elects Lok Sabha members?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The people**, by direct election from territorial constituencies.

</details>

**Q37.** What electoral system is generally used for Lok Sabha elections?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: First-past-the-post** (simple majority) in single-member constituencies.

</details>

**Q38.** What is meant by first-past-the-post system?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The candidate with the highest votes in the constituency wins**, even without an absolute majority.

</details>

**Q39.** Who decides the delimitation of constituencies?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Delimitation Commission**, under a law of Parliament (Art. 82). Seat freeze uses the **1971** census until the first census after **2026**.

</details>

**Q40.** What is the significance of delimitation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It redraws constituency boundaries so that each seat has roughly equal population**, and it allocates seats among States.

</details>

**Q41.** What is the constitutional basis for reservation of seats for SCs and STs in Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 330.** Reservation currently continues until **25 January 2030**.

</details>

**Q42.** Can the President nominate members to Lok Sabha today?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Anglo-Indian nomination has ended.

</details>

**Q43.** Which constitutional provision earlier allowed Anglo-Indian nomination?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 331** — not more than two members.

</details>

**Q44.** Which amendment ended that provision?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The 104th Amendment.**

</details>

## Round 4 — Qualifications and disqualifications

**Q45.** What are the basic qualifications for membership of Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Citizen of India, prescribed age, and other qualifications set by law** (Art. 84). The person must also be an elector.

</details>

**Q46.** What minimum age is required for Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Lok Sabha 25. Rajya Sabha 30.**

</details>

**Q47.** Can a person be a member of both Houses simultaneously?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** If elected to both, the person must choose within **10 days** or the Rajya Sabha seat is vacated by rule.

</details>

**Q48.** Can a person be a member of Parliament and a State Legislature simultaneously?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The Parliament seat is vacated if the State seat is not given up within **14 days**.

</details>

**Q49.** What is an office of profit?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A government office that brings financial gain**, other than an office exempted by Parliament. It is a ground of disqualification under Art. **102**.

</details>

**Q50.** What are the major grounds for disqualification of an MP?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Office of profit. Unsound mind. Undischarged insolvent. Not a citizen / allegiance to a foreign State. Disqualification under a parliamentary law (RPA). Absence for **60 days**. Defection (Tenth Schedule). *Lily Thomas*: conviction of **two years or more** disqualifies at once.

</details>

**Q51.** Which Article deals with disqualification on grounds other than defection?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 102.**

</details>

**Q52.** Which Article deals with disqualification on the ground of defection?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 102(2) read with the Tenth Schedule.** The 52nd Amendment inserted the Schedule.

</details>

**Q53.** Who decides questions of disqualification of MPs?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: For Art. 102 (except defection), the President.** For the Tenth Schedule, the **Speaker or Chairman**, subject to judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan*).

</details>

**Q54.** What is the role of the President in disqualification?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President decides Art. 102 questions**, but only after obtaining the **Election Commission’s opinion**, which is binding (Art. 103).

</details>

**Q55.** What is the role of the Election Commission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Its opinion binds the President** on Art. 102 disqualification (except defection).

</details>

## Round 5 — Sessions

**Q56.** Who summons Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President** (Art. 85).

</details>

**Q57.** What is the maximum permissible gap between two sessions of Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Six months.**

</details>

**Q58.** Who can prorogue Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President.**

</details>

**Q59.** Who can dissolve Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President**, normally on the Prime Minister’s advice.

</details>

**Q60.** Can Rajya Sabha be dissolved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q61.** What is the difference between adjournment, adjournment sine die, prorogation and dissolution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Adjournment ends a sitting for a stated time (presiding officer).** Adjournment sine die ends a sitting without a date. **Prorogation** ends a **session** (President). **Dissolution** ends the **Lok Sabha** itself.

</details>

**Q62.** What happens to pending Bills when Lok Sabha is dissolved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bills pending in the Lok Sabha lapse.** Bills passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha also lapse. A Bill pending only in Rajya Sabha (not passed by LS) does **not** lapse. Prorogation never lapses a Bill.

</details>

**Q63.** Does prorogation cause all pending Bills to lapse?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Notices may lapse. Bills do not.

</details>

**Q64.** Does dissolution of Lok Sabha affect Bills pending in Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Bill originating in Rajya Sabha and still pending there does not lapse.** A Bill already passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha **does** lapse.

</details>

**Q65.** What happens to a Bill pending in Lok Sabha when Lok Sabha is dissolved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It lapses.**

</details>

**Q66.** What happens to a Bill passed by Lok Sabha but pending in Rajya Sabha when Lok Sabha is dissolved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It lapses.**

</details>

## Round 6 — Officers

**Q67.** Who elects the Speaker of Lok Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Lok Sabha from among its members** (Art. 93).

</details>

**Q68.** Who elects the Deputy Speaker?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

</details>

**Q69.** What are the major powers of the Speaker?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Presides over the House and joint sittings. Casting vote. Certifies a Money Bill. Decides Tenth Schedule cases. Maintains order. Interprets rules. Adjournment and quorum calls.

</details>

**Q70.** Who decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (Art. 110(3)).

</details>

**Q71.** Is the Speaker’s decision on Money Bill certification subject to judicial review?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 110(3) says the Speaker’s decision is final.** Courts have still examined characterisation in limited cases (*Aadhaar*). It is not a complete legal vacuum.

</details>

**Q72.** Does the Speaker vote in the House normally?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No**, not in the first instance.

</details>

**Q73.** When can the Speaker exercise a casting vote?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: When there is a tie.**

</details>

**Q74.** Can the Speaker continue in office after Lok Sabha is dissolved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes, until immediately before the first meeting of the new House.**

</details>

**Q75.** Who can remove the Speaker?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Lok Sabha**, by a resolution (Art. 94). Fourteen days’ notice is required.

</details>

**Q76.** What majority is required to remove the Speaker?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A majority of all the then members of the House** (effective majority).

</details>

**Q77.** Who is the Chairman of Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Vice-President**, ex officio.

</details>

**Q78.** Who presides over Rajya Sabha in the absence of the Chairman?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Deputy Chairman.** If both are absent, a member from the Panel of Vice-Chairpersons.

</details>

**Q79.** How is the Deputy Chairman removed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: By a resolution of the Rajya Sabha** passed by a majority of all the then members, after 14 days’ notice.

</details>

**Q80.** Does the Chairman of Rajya Sabha have the same powers as the Lok Sabha Speaker?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Similar presiding powers, but not the same office.** The Chairman is not a member, does not certify Money Bills, and does not preside over a joint sitting (the Speaker does).

</details>

## Round 7 — Privileges

**Q81.** What are parliamentary privileges?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Special rights of each House, its committees and members**, so that they can function freely (Art. 105).

</details>

**Q82.** Are parliamentary privileges explicitly listed in one comprehensive law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** They remain largely undefined in a single statute. The 44th Amendment froze them as on **20 June 1979** until defined by law.

</details>

**Q83.** Which Article deals with parliamentary privileges?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 105.** State legislatures: Art. **194**. Courts generally cannot inquire into proceedings (Art. **122**).

</details>

**Q84.** What is freedom of speech in Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A member may speak in the House without fear of court action for that speech or vote** (Art. 105(1)–(2)), subject to the Constitution and the rules of the House.

</details>

**Q85.** Is parliamentary speech subject to the same restrictions as ordinary freedom of speech under Article 19?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Article 105 is a separate privilege. It is still subject to the Constitution and House rules, not the 19(2) list as such.

</details>

**Q86.** Can an MP be arrested for something said in Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not in a court for words spoken or votes given in the House.** Privilege does not bar arrest for a **criminal** offence outside. Civil arrest is barred during session plus **40 days** before and after.

</details>

**Q87.** What is breach of privilege?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An act that attacks the authority, dignity or immunity of the House or a member.**

</details>

**Q88.** Who has the power to decide whether a question of privilege should be taken up?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker or Chairman**, often after the Privileges Committee. *Raja Ram Pal*: judicial review is not entirely barred if the House acts without jurisdiction or mala fide.

</details>

## Round 8 — Types of Bills

**Q89.** What is an Ordinary Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Bill that is neither a Money Bill nor a Constitution Amendment Bill.** It may originate in either House (Art. 107). Joint sitting is possible.

</details>

**Q90.** What is a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Bill dealing only with the matters listed in Article 110(1)** — tax, borrowing, Consolidated Fund, and related items.

</details>

**Q91.** What is a Financial Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Bill that deals with money but is not confined to Art. 110.** **Category I (117(1))** = Art. 110 matters plus other provisions. **Category II (117(3))** = expenditure from the Consolidated Fund.

</details>

**Q92.** What is the difference between a Money Bill and a Financial Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Money Bill is only Art. 110 matters; Rajya Sabha cannot amend; no joint sitting.** Financial Bill I originates only in Lok Sabha but Rajya Sabha can amend and joint sitting is possible. Financial Bill II may originate in either House.

</details>

**Q93.** Which Article defines a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 110.** The 14-day Rajya Sabha procedure is **Article 109**.

</details>

**Q94.** Who certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha.**

</details>

**Q95.** Can a Money Bill originate in Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Only in the Lok Sabha, and only on the President’s recommendation.

</details>

**Q96.** Can Rajya Sabha amend a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It cannot amend on its own.** It may only **recommend** amendments. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject them.

</details>

**Q97.** How long can Rajya Sabha keep a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 14 days.** If not returned, it is deemed passed by both Houses.

</details>

**Q98.** Is the Lok Sabha bound to accept Rajya Sabha’s recommendations on a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q99.** Is there a joint sitting for a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q100.** Is there a joint sitting for a Constitutional Amendment Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Each House must pass it separately.

</details>

## Round 9 — Ordinary Bill and joint sitting

**Q101.** Where can an Ordinary Bill originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Either House.**

</details>

**Q102.** Can an Ordinary Bill originate in Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q103.** When can a joint sitting of Parliament be called?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: For an Ordinary Bill in deadlock** (Art. 108): rejection by the other House, disagreement on amendments, or more than **six months** without passage by the other House.

</details>

**Q104.** Who summons a joint sitting?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President.**

</details>

**Q105.** Who presides over a joint sitting?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha.** In the Speaker’s absence, the Deputy Speaker, then the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.

</details>

**Q106.** What are the circumstances that can lead to a joint sitting?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The other House rejects the Bill, disagrees on amendments, or does not pass it for more than six months.**

</details>

**Q107.** Is joint sitting applicable to a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q108.** Is joint sitting applicable to a Constitutional Amendment Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q109.** How many joint sittings have occurred in India’s parliamentary history?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Three** — Dowry Prohibition Bill **1961**, Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill **1978**, POTA Bill **2002**.

</details>

**Q110.** Can the President initiate a joint sitting?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President summons it** when the Art. 108 conditions exist, on ministerial advice. The President does not create the deadlock.

</details>

## Round 12 — Financial devices

**Q132.** What is a Vote on Account?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Advance grant for a part of the financial year** until the full Budget is passed (Art. 116).

</details>

**Q133.** Why is Vote on Account needed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: So the government can spend after 31 March** before Demands for Grants and the Appropriation Bill are completed.

</details>

**Q134.** What is a Vote of Credit?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A blank-cheque grant** for an unexpected demand whose details cannot be stated (Art. 116).

</details>

**Q135.** What is an Exceptional Grant?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A grant for a special purpose forming no part of the current service of the year** (Art. 116).

</details>

**Q136.** What is a Supplementary Grant?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Additional money for a service already in the Budget** when the amount is insufficient (Art. 115).

</details>

**Q137.** What is an Additional Grant?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Money for a **new service** not contemplated in the Budget** (Art. 115).

</details>

**Q138.** What is an Excess Grant?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Regularisation of money already spent beyond the grant**, after the year, usually after PAC (Art. 115).

</details>

**Q139.** What is a Token Grant?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A token sum (₹1) to authorise a new service** when funds can be found by reappropriation.

</details>

**Q140.** Which financial device is used when expenditure on a new service is not provided for in the Budget?

<details>

## Round 13 — Parliamentary devices

**Q141.** What is Question Hour?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The first hour of a sitting**, when members ask questions of ministers.

</details>

**Q142.** Is Question Hour mentioned explicitly in the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is in the **Rules of Procedure**.

</details>

**Q143.** What is an Unstarred Question?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A written answer; no supplementary oral questions.**

</details>

**Q144.** What is a Starred Question?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An oral answer, marked with a star.** Supplementaries may be asked.

</details>

**Q145.** What is a Short Notice Question?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A question on an urgent public matter** with shorter notice than the usual period.

</details>

**Q146.** What is Zero Hour?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The time immediately after Question Hour** when members raise matters without prior notice. An Indian innovation (**1962**).

</details>

**Q147.** Is Zero Hour mentioned in the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is not even in the Rules as a named device.

</details>

**Q148.** What is an Adjournment Motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A motion to adjourn the House to discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance.** It is a Lok Sabha device and implies censure if admitted and carried.

</details>

**Q149.** What type of issue can be raised through an Adjournment Motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A definite, urgent, public matter of recent occurrence.**

</details>

**Q150.** What is a No-Confidence Motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A motion that the House has no confidence in the Council of Ministers.** Needs support of **50** members to be admitted.

</details>

**Q151.** Against whom is a No-Confidence Motion moved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Against the Council of Ministers as a whole.**

</details>

**Q152.** In which House can a No-Confidence Motion be moved?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Only in the Lok Sabha.**

</details>

**Q153.** Does the Constitution explicitly mention the No-Confidence Motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It follows from **Article 75(3)** collective responsibility, and is set out in the Rules.

</details>

**Q154.** What happens if the government loses a No-Confidence Motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Council of Ministers must resign.**

</details>

**Q155.** What is a Censure Motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A motion censuring a minister, a policy, or the government on a specific issue.** It need not say “no confidence.”

</details>

**Q156.** What is the difference between a Censure Motion and a No-Confidence Motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No-confidence is against the whole Council and, if passed, brings the government down.** Censure is specific; the government need not always resign unless it treats the vote as confidence.

</details>

## Round 14 — Committees

**Q157.** What is the purpose of parliamentary committees?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To scrutinise expenditure, legislation and administration in smaller expert groups**, because the full House cannot do all the work.

</details>

**Q158.** Which committee is considered the watchdog of public expenditure?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Public Accounts Committee (PAC).**

</details>

**Q159.** What is the composition of the Public Accounts Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 22 members — 15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha.**

</details>

**Q160.** Is the Finance Minister a member of PAC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** A minister is not a member.

</details>

**Q161.** Can a Rajya Sabha member be a member of PAC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, seven members.

</details>

**Q162.** Who is traditionally appointed Chairman of PAC?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A member from the Opposition**, appointed by the Speaker.

</details>

**Q163.** What is the role of the Estimates Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It examines Budget estimates and suggests economies and alternative policies.** It does not examine CAG reports in the PAC sense.

</details>

**Q164.** Can Rajya Sabha members be members of the Estimates Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is **Lok Sabha only**.

</details>

**Q165.** What is the composition of the Estimates Committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 30 members**, all from the Lok Sabha. It is the largest of the three financial committees.

</details>

**Q166.** What is the Committee on Public Undertakings?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A committee of 22 (15 LS + 7 RS)** that examines the working of public undertakings.

</details>

**Q167.** What is the role of the Department-related Standing Committees?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They examine Bills, Demands for Grants and long-term policy of ministries.** There are **24** DRSCs, typically **31** members (21 LS + 10 RS).

</details>

**Q168.** What is the difference between a standing committee and an ad hoc committee?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Standing committees are permanent and reconstituted periodically.** Ad hoc committees are temporary (Select, Joint, and inquiry committees) and die with their task.

</details>

</details>

**Q178.** What is a No-Confidence Motion?

<details>

</details>

</details>
