## Round 1 — President: framework

**Q1.** Which Part of the Constitution deals with the Union Executive?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Part V** (Arts. 52–78). It covers the President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers and Attorney-General.

</details>

**Q2.** Which Articles deal with the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Articles 52 to 62.**

</details>

**Q3.** Which Article establishes the office of the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 52.** There shall be a President of India.

</details>

**Q4.** Is the President the real executive or the nominal constitutional executive?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Nominal / de jure / constitutional head.** The real executive is the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

</details>

**Q5.** Who exercises the real executive power at the Union level?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.**

</details>

**Q6.** In whose name are the executive actions of the Government of India taken?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President’s name** (Art. 53 / Art. 77).

</details>

**Q7.** What is the constitutional basis for the President’s executive power?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 53** vests Union executive power in the President. Article **74** says it is exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.

</details>

**Q8.** What is the term of office of the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Five years** (Art. 56).

</details>

**Q9.** Can a President be re-elected?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes** (Art. 57).

</details>

**Q10.** Is there any constitutional limit on the number of times a person can become President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Re-election is allowed any number of times.

</details>

## Round 2 — Election of the President

**Q11.** Who elects the President of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: An Electoral College** of elected MPs of both Houses plus elected MLAs of the States and of Delhi and Puducherry (Art. 54).

</details>

**Q12.** Which members of Parliament participate in the Presidential election?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Only elected members** of the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha.

</details>

**Q13.** Do nominated MPs participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** They do vote in the **Vice-President’s** election and in **impeachment**.

</details>

**Q14.** Do elected members of State Legislative Assemblies participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q15.** Do members of Legislative Councils participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q16.** Do elected members of Union Territory legislatures participate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Only Delhi and Puducherry**, added by the **70th Amendment, 1992**. Other UT assemblies are not in Article 54.

</details>

**Q17.** Which Union Territories currently have elected assemblies relevant to the Presidential electoral college?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Delhi and Puducherry.** J&K has a legislature but is not listed in Article 54 as amended by the 70th.

</details>

**Q18.** Why is the Presidential election called an indirect election?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The people do not vote directly.** Elected legislators form the Electoral College.

</details>

**Q19.** What voting system is used for the election of the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**, by secret ballot (Art. 55).

</details>

**Q20.** What is meant by proportional representation by means of single transferable vote?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Electors rank candidates.** Surplus and eliminated votes transfer until a candidate reaches the quota (more than half of valid votes).

</details>

**Q21.** Is voting in the Presidential election conducted by secret ballot?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q22.** Why was the system of weighted votes adopted?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: To keep uniformity among States and parity between the Union and the States as a whole** (Art. 55), despite unequal State populations and House sizes.

</details>

**Q23.** How is the value of an MLA’s vote calculated?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: (Population of the State ÷ total elected MLAs) ÷ 1000.** Population is frozen at the **1971 census** until the first census after 2026.

</details>

**Q24.** How is the value of an MP’s vote calculated?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Total value of votes of all MLAs of all States ÷ total number of elected MPs.**

</details>

**Q25.** What is the constitutional objective behind maintaining balance between the votes of MPs and MLAs?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parity between the Union and the States as a whole**, plus uniformity of representation among States.

</details>

## Round 3 — Qualifications

**Q26.** What are the constitutional qualifications required to become President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Citizen of India, at least 35 years, qualified for the Lok Sabha, and not holding an office of profit** (Art. 58).

</details>

**Q27.** What minimum age is required?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 35 years.**

</details>

**Q28.** Must the President be a citizen of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q29.** Must a Presidential candidate be qualified to become a member of the Lok Sabha or the Rajya Sabha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Qualified for the Lok Sabha**, not the Rajya Sabha. The Vice-President’s benchmark is the Rajya Sabha.

</details>

**Q30.** Can a sitting MP or MLA continue in that position after becoming President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The seat is vacated (Art. 59).

</details>

**Q31.** What is an office of profit?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A government office that brings financial gain or advantage**, other than the office of President itself.

</details>

**Q32.** Can the President hold an office of profit under the Union or a State Government?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No** (Art. 59).

</details>

**Q33.** Can the President be a member of Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Parliament still consists of the President plus the two Houses (Art. 79), but the President is not a sitting member of either House.

</details>

<details>

## Round 5 — Impeachment

**Q42.** Which Article deals with impeachment of the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 61.**

</details>

**Q43.** On what ground can the President be impeached?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Violation of the Constitution only.** The phrase is not defined.

</details>

**Q44.** Can the President be impeached for violation of the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** That is the only ground.

</details>

**Q45.** Who can initiate impeachment proceedings?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Either House of Parliament.** The charge needs signatures of at least **one-fourth** of that House’s total membership and **14 days’** notice.

</details>

**Q46.** Can impeachment proceedings originate in either House?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q47.** What special majority is required to pass an impeachment resolution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Two-thirds of the total membership** of the House.

</details>

**Q48.** What is the role of the other House?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It investigates the charge.** If it also passes the resolution by two-thirds of total membership, the President is removed.

</details>

**Q49.** Does the President have the right to appear and defend himself or herself?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q50.** Is impeachment a judicial or political process?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A quasi-judicial procedure** in Parliament. No President has ever been impeached.

</details>

**Q51.** Can State legislatures participate in the impeachment of the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** Reverse trap: MLAs vote in the **election**, not in impeachment. Nominated MPs vote in **impeachment**, not in the election.

</details>

## Round 7 — President and Bills

**Q70.** What are the options available to the President when a Bill is presented for assent?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Assent, withhold assent, or (except a Money Bill) return it once for reconsideration** (Art. 111).

</details>

**Q71.** Can the President withhold assent to an ordinary Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** That is the **absolute veto**.

</details>

**Q72.** Can the President return an ordinary Bill for reconsideration?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** That is the **suspensive veto**.

</details>

**Q73.** Can the President return a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** The President may only assent or withhold assent.

</details>

**Q74.** What happens if Parliament passes a returned ordinary Bill again?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President must give assent.**

</details>

**Q75.** Is the President constitutionally bound to give assent after reconsideration?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes**, if the Bill is passed again, with or without amendments.

</details>

**Q76.** What is meant by absolute veto?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Withholding assent so that the Bill dies.**

</details>

**Q77.** What is meant by suspensive veto?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Returning the Bill for reconsideration.** If Parliament repasses it, the veto falls.

</details>

**Q78.** What is meant by pocket veto?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Neither assenting, returning, nor rejecting**, so the Bill stays pending. Article 111 sets **no time limit**. Zail Singh used it on the 1986 Postal Bill.

</details>

**Q79.** Does the Indian Constitution explicitly mention the term pocket veto?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is a description of the silence in Article 111. India has **no qualified veto** (that is the USA).

</details>

**Q80.** Can the President exercise veto over a Constitutional Amendment Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No**, after the **24th Amendment, 1971**.

</details>

**Q81.** Is the President constitutionally required to give assent to a Constitutional Amendment Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

## Round 8 — Ordinance

**Q82.** Which Article gives the President ordinance-making power?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 123.** The Governor’s parallel is Article **213**.

</details>

**Q83.** When can the President promulgate an ordinance?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: When both Houses are not in session, or when only one House is in session**, and immediate action is needed.

</details>

**Q84.** Can an ordinance be issued when Parliament is in session?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not when both Houses are in session.** Article 123 bars an ordinance only in that case.

</details>

**Q85.** Does Parliament need to be completely out of session for an ordinance to be issued?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** If even one House is not in session, an ordinance can still be issued.

</details>

**Q86.** How long does an ordinance remain effective after Parliament reassembles?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Six weeks** from reassembly, unless earlier approved or disapproved.

</details>

**Q87.** What happens if Parliament does not approve the ordinance?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It ceases to operate** after six weeks of reassembly, or sooner if disapproved.

</details>

**Q88.** Can an ordinance have the same force as an Act of Parliament?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** It cannot do what Parliament cannot do.

</details>

**Q89.** Can an ordinance amend the Constitution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.**

</details>

**Q90.** Can an ordinance violate Fundamental Rights?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** An ordinance is “law” under Article 13 and is void if it abridges Fundamental Rights.

</details>

**Q91.** Is the President’s ordinance-making power an independent power?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** It is exercised on the advice of the Council of Ministers. Re-promulgation without laying is a fraud on the Constitution (*D.C. Wadhwa*; *Krishna Kumar Singh*).

</details>

**Q92.** Whose advice does the President ordinarily act upon while promulgating an ordinance?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.**

</details>

## Round 10 — Pardoning power

**Q98.** Which Article gives the President the power to grant pardons?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 72.**

</details>

**Q99.** What are the five forms of pardoning power?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Pardon, commutation, remission, respite and reprieve.**

</details>

**Q100.** What is a pardon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It fully absolves the offender** from the sentence and conviction.

</details>

**Q101.** What is a commutation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It substitutes a lighter form of punishment.**

</details>

**Q102.** What is a remission?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It reduces the period of the sentence without changing its character.**

</details>

**Q103.** What is a respite?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A lesser sentence because of special facts**, such as pregnancy.

</details>

**Q104.** What is a reprieve?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A temporary stay of the execution of a sentence.**

</details>

**Q105.** Can the President pardon a death sentence?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q106.** Can the President pardon a court-martial sentence?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.**

</details>

**Q107.** Can the President pardon a sentence under a State law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not generally for ordinary State-law offences (that is the Governor under Art. 161).** The President **can** pardon a **death sentence** even when the offence is under State law.

</details>

**Q108.** What is the difference between the pardoning powers of the President and the Governor?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The President can pardon death sentences and court-martial sentences.** The Governor cannot pardon death (only commute, remit or suspend) and has no court-martial power. The Governor covers State-law offences only.

</details>

**Q109.** Is the President’s pardoning power subject to judicial review?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes, on limited grounds** (*Epuru Sudhakar*). It is exercised on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

</details>

</details>

**Q118.** What are the qualifications required to become Vice-President?

</details>

**Q119.** What is the term of office of the Vice-President?

<details>

**Ans: Five years** (Art. 67).

</details>

**Q120.** To whom does the Vice-President submit resignation?

<details>

**Ans: The President.**

</details>

**Q121.** How can the Vice-President be removed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A Rajya Sabha resolution passed by effective majority, agreed to by the Lok Sabha**, after **14 days’** notice (Art. 67). No specified ground.

</details>

**Q122.** Is the removal procedure for the Vice-President the same as impeachment of the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** There is no formal impeachment of the Vice-President.

</details>

**Q123.** Who acts as President when both the President and Vice-President offices are vacant?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Chief Justice of India**, under the 1969 Act (then the senior-most Supreme Court judge).

</details>

</details>

**Q142.** What happens to the Council of Ministers if the Lok Sabha passes a vote of no-confidence?

**Q145.** What is the difference between the Council of Ministers and the Cabinet?

**Q148.** Which Constitutional Amendment gave constitutional recognition to the term Cabinet?
