## Level 1 — One-line recall

**Q1.** Which Article establishes the State Legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 168.**

</details>

**Q2.** What does a State Legislature consist of?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Governor + Legislative Assembly.** In some States there is also a **Legislative Council**. The Governor is a **part of** the legislature.

</details>

**Q3.** What is the Lower House of a State Legislature called?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha).**

</details>

**Q4.** What is the Upper House of a State Legislature called?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad).** It exists only in bicameral States.

</details>

**Q5.** What is the minimum age for membership of the Legislative Assembly?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 25 years** (Art. **173**).

</details>

**Q6.** What is the minimum age for membership of the Legislative Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 30 years** (Art. **173**).

</details>

**Q7.** What is the normal term of the Legislative Assembly?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 5 years** from the date of its first meeting (Art. **172**). During a National Emergency it may be extended by one year at a time.

</details>

**Q8.** Is the Legislative Council a permanent House?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** It is **never dissolved**. It **can be abolished** under Art. **169**.

</details>

**Q9.** What fraction of Legislative Council members retire every two years?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: One-third.** The term of a member is **6 years**.

</details>

**Q10.** Who summons and prorogues the State Legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Governor** (Art. **174**). The Governor may also dissolve the Legislative Assembly.

</details>

**Q11.** What is the maximum gap allowed between two sessions of the State Legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Six months.**

</details>

## Level 4 — Composition of Legislative Council

**Q14.** What are the constitutional size limits of a Legislative Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not more than one-third of the Legislative Assembly, and not less than 40** (Art. **171**). UP Council strength is **100**.

</details>

**Q15.** Recall the five streams that make up a Legislative Council, with fractions.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** **1/3** elected by **local bodies**. **1/12** elected by **graduates**. **1/12** elected by **teachers**. **1/3** elected by **MLAs** (from persons who are **not** MLAs). **1/6 nominated** by the Governor from literature, science, art, **cooperative movement**, and social service. Mnemonic: **L–G–T–A–N** = 1/3, 1/12, 1/12, 1/3, rest (1/6). RS nomination does **not** include cooperatives.

</details>

## Level 5 — Creation or abolition of Council

**Q16.** Which Article governs creation or abolition of a Legislative Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 169.**

</details>

**Q17.** What majority must the Legislative Assembly use in a resolution under Article 169?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Special majority** — a majority of the **total membership** plus **two-thirds of members present and voting**.

</details>

**Q18.** After the Assembly resolution, how does Parliament act?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: By ordinary law** — **simple majority**. That law is **not** an Art. **368** amendment.

</details>

## Level 6 — Money Bill in the State

**Q19.** In which House may a State Money Bill be introduced?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Legislative Assembly only**, on the Governor’s recommendation.

</details>

**Q20.** Who decides whether a State Bill is a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker of the Legislative Assembly** (Art. **199**). The decision is final.

</details>

**Q21.** Within how many days must the Legislative Council return a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 14 days.**

</details>

**Q22.** Can the Legislative Council amend a Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It may only recommend amendments.** The Assembly may accept or reject them.

</details>

**Q23.** Can there be a joint sitting on a State Money Bill?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** There is **no joint sitting** at State level for any Bill.

</details>

**Q24.** Which Articles cover the State Money Bill procedure and definition?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Articles 198 and 199.** Procedure is **198**. Definition is **199**.

</details>

## Level 7 — Ordinary Bill deadlock

**Q25.** How long may the Legislative Council delay an ordinary Bill that originated in the Assembly — first time and second time?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: First round — up to 3 months.** If the Assembly passes it again, **second round — up to 1 month**. After that the Bill is **deemed passed** by both Houses in the form adopted by the Assembly (**Art. 197**).

</details>

**Q26.** Is there a joint sitting to resolve a deadlock between Assembly and Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: No.** States have **no Art. 108 equivalent**. The Assembly prevails under **Art. 197**. A Bill that **originates in the Council** and is rejected by the Assembly **dies**.

</details>

## Level 8 — Financial control

**Q27.** What is the constitutional name of the State Budget, and which Article?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Annual Financial Statement — Article 202.**

</details>

**Q28.** Which House votes on Demands for Grants in a State?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Legislative Assembly only.** The Council may **discuss** the Budget but cannot vote the demands.

</details>

**Q29.** Who effectively controls the Appropriation Bill and the Finance Bill in a State?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Legislative Assembly.** The Council of Ministers is responsible to the Assembly, so grant-voting sits there.

</details>

## Level 9 — Governor and Bills

**Q30.** Under Article 200, what four options does the Governor have when a Bill is presented?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** Give **assent**. **Withhold** assent. **Return** a **non-Money Bill** for reconsideration. **Reserve** the Bill for the **President**. If the House re-passes a returned ordinary Bill, the Governor **must** assent.

</details>

**Q31.** What happens if the Governor reserves a Bill for the President?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 201 applies.** The President may assent, withhold assent, or (for a non-Money Bill) direct the Governor to return it. Even if the House re-passes it, the **President is not bound** to assent.

</details>

## Level 10 — Ordinance power

**Q32.** Which Article gives the Governor ordinance-making power?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 213.**

</details>

**Q33.** When may the Governor issue an ordinance?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: When the Legislature is not in session**, if circumstances require immediate action. For some subjects the Governor needs the **President’s instructions**.

</details>

**Q34.** Within what time must an ordinance be approved after the Legislature reassembles?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Six weeks from reassembly.** It also ceases if both Houses pass a disapproving resolution before that.

</details>

**Q35.** Does a Governor’s ordinance have the force of law?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yes.** It has the same force and effect as an Act of the State Legislature, subject to the limits in Art. **213**.

</details>

## Level 11 — Presiding officers

**Q36.** Who are the presiding officers of the Assembly and of the Council?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Assembly — Speaker and Deputy Speaker** (Art. **178**). **Council — Chairman and Deputy Chairman** (Art. **182**).

</details>

**Q37.** When does the Speaker vote?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Not in the first instance.** The Speaker has only a **casting vote** in case of a **tie** (Art. **189**).

</details>

**Q38.** Who decides Tenth Schedule disqualification in a State House?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Speaker** (Assembly) or the **Chairman** (Council) of the House concerned. The decision is subject to judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan*).

</details>

## Level 12 — Privileges

**Q39.** Which Article grants powers, privileges and immunities to a State Legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Article 194.** In principle they are similar to Parliament’s Art. **105**, including **freedom of speech** in the House and **immunity** for speeches and votes.

</details>

## Level 13 — Conceptual

**Q40.** Why is the Legislative Council called a revising chamber?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** It can **examine, debate and suggest changes** to Bills. It cannot **permanently block** most legislation. The Assembly can override it under **Art. 197**.

</details>

**Q41.** Why is the Council weaker than the Assembly?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:** It has **no control over the executive**. It cannot introduce a **Money Bill**. It cannot vote **Demands for Grants**. It cannot ultimately **veto** an ordinary Bill that started in the Assembly.

</details>

**Q42.** How does deadlock resolution differ between Parliament and a State Legislature?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parliament** may use a **joint sitting** under Art. **108**. **States have no joint sitting.** The Assembly prevails under **Art. 197** (3 months, then 1 month).

</details>

**Q43.** Why are ministers collectively responsible to the Assembly?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Art. 164** makes the Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the **Legislative Assembly**. The ministry stays in office only while it enjoys the confidence of the **directly elected** House.

</details>
