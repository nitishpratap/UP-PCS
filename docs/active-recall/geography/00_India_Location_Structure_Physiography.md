# India — Location, Structure and Physiography

> Geography · Active recall · Extent · 82°30′E · six divisions · Bhabar–Terai · Ghats · islands

Target about 25 minutes. Answer from memory. Mark misses. Framework: **location → structure → Himalayas → plains → plateau → desert → coasts → islands**.

## A. Location and extent

**Q1.** What are India’s mainland latitude and longitude extents, and the approximate north–south and east–west lengths?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Latitude about 8°4′ N to 37°6′ N (about 3214 km north–south).** Longitude about 68°7′ E to 97°25′ E (about 2933 km east–west). Area about 3.28 million km² = **2.4%** of world land. Rank is conventionally **7th**, not 6th. India lies wholly in the northern and eastern hemispheres and is **not** wholly tropical.

</details>

**Q2.** Which latitude cuts India, through which States, and what is the Standard Meridian?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Tropic of Cancer (23°30′ N) divides roughly tropical south from subtropical–alpine north.** It passes **eight States only**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram. **Not** Uttar Pradesh. **Not** Ladakh. Standard Meridian is **82°30′ E** near **Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh**. IST = GMT + 5:30. One time zone. Chosen because it is near the country’s mid-longitude, so solar lag across about 29° of longitude (about two hours) is split.

</details>

**Q3.** Southernmost mainland versus territory; land and maritime neighbours; why the Indian Ocean location matters.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mainland south = Kanyakumari. Territory south = Indira Point on Great Nicobar.** Land neighbours: Pakistan, Afghanistan (Wakhan lore), China, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, Bangladesh. Maritime: Sri Lanka, Maldives; Andaman–Nicobar also face Myanmar/Thailand/Indonesia across seas. The Indian Ocean location controls monsoon, trade routes (Cape–Red Sea–Strait of Malacca), and strategic depth for the peninsula.

</details>

## B. Geological structure

**Q4.** What are the major geological/structural units of India? Which is oldest and most stable?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Three NCERT structural units: Peninsular Block, Himalayas and extra-peninsular mountains, Indo–Ganga–Brahmaputra Plain.** The **Peninsular Shield / Plateau** is the oldest stable crust (gneiss, granite, schist). It is faulted and eroded, **not** recently folded like the Himalaya. The plains are a foredeep of Himalayan alluvium. The Himalaya is the **young** fold belt from the India–Eurasia collision.

</details>

**Q5.** How did the Himalayas form? What was the Tethys Sea? Why “young fold mountains”?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Indian Plate collided with Eurasia (Tertiary, still rising in zones).** Marine sediments of the closed **Tethys Sea** were folded and thrust. “Young” means Tertiary age, steep relief, and continuing uplift compared with the old Aravalli. Faults and thrusts (Main Central Thrust, Main Boundary / Himalayan Frontal Fault lore, and the Great Boundary Fault of the Aravalli–Vindhyan belt) mark major crustal breaks used in structure questions.

</details>

## C. Six physiographic divisions

**Q6.** Name the six major physiographic divisions, north to south order, oldest and youngest.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Northern Mountains → Northern Plains → Peninsular Plateau → Indian Desert → Coastal Plains → Islands.** Youngest relief: Himalaya / Shiwalik class and recent Thar sands. Oldest major division: **Peninsular Plateau** (Aravalli oldest fold within it). Northern Plains sit in the foredeep. Deccan is the southern part of the Peninsular Plateau, not a separate sixth unit.

</details>

## D. Himalayan region

**Q7.** Parallel ranges north to south, and west–east regional divisions. What are the Purvanchal Hills?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Trans-Himalaya → Himadri (Greater) → Himachal (Lesser) → Shiwalik.** Trans is cold desert north of the Indus (Zaskar, Ladakh, Kailash; Karakoram north-west with K2). Himadri is crystalline, fossil-less, snow. Himachal has marine fossils. Shiwalik is youngest, with duns. West to east coaching: Punjab / Kashmir Himalaya → Kumaon → Nepal → Assam / Eastern Himalaya, then **Purvanchal** (Patkai, Naga, Mizo, Mishmi) as the NE bend. Garo–Khasi–Jaintia are **Meghalaya Plateau**, not Himalayan fold.

</details>

**Q8.** Why do the Himalayas matter for climate, rivers, agriculture, biodiversity and security?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Climatic wall: block cold Central Asian winds; force monsoon rain on southern slopes.** Source of Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra snow and glacier water. Alluvium builds the plains for agriculture. Altitude bands create huge biodiversity. Strategic frontier with China, Nepal and Bhutan.

</details>

## E. Northern Plains

**Q9.** How were the Northern Plains formed, by which rivers, and what are the regional belts?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Himalayan foredeep filled by Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra sediments.** About 2400 km long; wider in the west. Regional belts include Punjab Plain, Ganga Plain and Brahmaputra Plain (plus Rajasthan Plain / Thar in some maps). South of the Shiwaliks the micro-belts run **Bhabar → Terai → Bhangar / Khadar**.

</details>

**Q10.** Differentiate Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar and Khadar.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bhabar is a narrow pebble belt (about 8–16 km) where streams sink into porous gravel.** Terai is marshy south of Bhabar where streams re-emerge (about 15–30 km; stronger in the wetter east). **Bhangar** is older alluvium with kankar, above flood level. **Khadar** is newer floodplain alluvium, renewed and more fertile. Never swap Bhangar and Khadar. Duars of West Bengal–Assam are a separate foothill tract from the UP Terai.

</details>

## F. Peninsular Plateau

**Q11.** Central Highlands versus Deccan Plateau. Name Malwa, Bundelkhand and Chota Nagpur.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Peninsular Plateau has two parts: Central Highlands (north of the Narmada–Son) and Deccan Plateau (south).** Central Highlands include Malwa (lava/regur west-central), Bundelkhand and Baghelkhand. **Chota Nagpur** is the mineral-rich eastern plateau (Jharkhand–Odisha belt). Deccan is the southern triangular tableland, about 600–900 m class height, **sloping east**, so most rivers flow to the Bay of Bengal.

</details>

**Q12.** Major Central Highlands ranges, and why the Deccan slopes east?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Aravalli (oldest; Guru Shikhar), Vindhya north of Narmada, Satpura south of Narmada; Mahadeo–Maikal continue east toward Chota Nagpur.** Western Ghats stand high on the west; the plateau surface falls eastward toward the Eastern Ghats and Bay of Bengal.

</details>

---

## G. Western and Eastern Ghats

**Q13.** Compare Western and Eastern Ghats. Highest peak? Meeting hills?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Western Ghats: higher, continuous wall, orographic rain, west-facing escarpment, few gaps (Palghat widest).** Eastern Ghats: lower, discontinuous, eroded, cut by east-flowing rivers. Rivers: west coast short and swift; peninsula’s long rivers cross Eastern Ghats to the Bay. They meet near the **Nilgiri** (Doddabetta in Tamil Nadu). Highest Western Ghats / South India peak: **Anaimudi (2695 m), Kerala**. Cardamom Hills continue south.

</details>

---

## H. Desert and coastal plains

**Q14.** Thar Desert: character and rainfall reason.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Pleistocene–recent sand sheet west of the Aravalli (Marusthali core; Bagar fringe).** Low rainfall because of the Aravalli rain-shadow relative to the Bay branch, distance from the Arabian Sea moisture path, and subtropical high / dry winds. Keep Thar as a **separate** sixth division for UPPCS even if some maps fold it into the Rajasthan Plain.

</details>

**Q15.** Western versus Eastern Coastal Plains: width, divisions, deltas.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: West coast is generally **narrower** (Western Ghats hug the sea). East coast is broader with large deltas.** West divisions: **Konkan** (Maharashtra–Goa; coast of submergence), **Kannad**, **Malabar** (Kerala; emergence, kayals). East: **Northern Circars** (Mahanadi–Krishna), **Coromandel** (to Kanyakumari; emergence). Large deltas (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) form on the east because long east-flowing rivers dump sediment on a wide, gentle shelf. Do not call the **whole** west coast submerged — only Konkan is the classic drowned coast.

</details>

## I. Islands

**Q16.** Andaman and Nicobar versus Lakshadweep. Active volcano?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Andaman and Nicobar = Bay of Bengal, volcanic/tectonic, continuation of the Arakan–Purvanchal arc; subduction zone.** Lakshadweep = Arabian Sea, **coral** atolls. **Barren Island** (Andaman Sea) is India’s only **active** volcano. Narcondam is dormant/extinct. Channels: 10° separates Andaman from Nicobar; 9° separates Minicoy from the rest of Lakshadweep.

</details>

## Map and rapid chain

**Q17.** Mentally place the map spine without notes.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: North: Himalayas → Northern Plains → Peninsular Plateau.** West: Aravalli → Thar → Western Ghats → Arabian Sea. East: Eastern Ghats → coastal plain → Bay of Bengal. South: Nilgiri → Cardamom → Indian Ocean. Lakshadweep in Arabian Sea; Andaman and Nicobar in Bay of Bengal.

</details>

**Q18.** Complete the rapid chain.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Latitude 8°4′–37°6′ N. Longitude 68°7′–97°25′ E. Tropic = eight States (not UP). Standard Meridian 82°30′ E. Oldest = Peninsular Shield. Youngest fold = Himalaya. Six divisions = Mountains, Plains, Plateau, Desert, Coasts, Islands. Himalaya N→S = Trans–Himadri–Himachal–Shiwalik. Plains = Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra alluvium. Bhabar = streams sink. Terai = streams re-emerge, marsh. Bhangar = old. Khadar = new, fertile. Plateau = Central Highlands + Deccan. W Ghats continuous/high; E Ghats discontinuous. West coast narrow; east coast broad with deltas. Lakshadweep = coral. Andaman and Nicobar = volcanic/tectonic.**

</details>
