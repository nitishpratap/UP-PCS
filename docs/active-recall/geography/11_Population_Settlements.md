## A. Distribution and density

**Q1.** What factors shape India’s population distribution, and why is it uneven?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Terrain, climate, soil, water, minerals, industry, transport and history.** Crowded: Indo-Gangetic Plain and many coasts (flat land, water, fertile soil, farming and cities). Sparse: Himalaya, Thar, North-East hills and dry interior pockets (relief, cold, aridity, forest, weak infrastructure). Minerals and industry pull people to belts such as Chotanagpur.

</details>

**Q2.** What is population density, and how do arithmetic, physiological and agricultural density differ?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Density = people relative to land.** Arithmetic = total population ÷ total area (India 2011 = **382**). Physiological = population ÷ **net sown** area. Agricultural = agricultural population ÷ net sown area. Highest density **state** = **Bihar**. Lowest = **Arunachal Pradesh**. Delhi is a high-density UT, not a “lowest density state” answer.

</details>

## B. Growth

**Q3.** Population growth, natural versus actual growth, and the three components of change.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Growth is change in numbers over time.** Natural growth = **CBR − CDR**. Induced / migration balance is separate. Actual growth = natural growth ± net migration. Three components: births, deaths, migration.

</details>

**Q4.** Twentieth-century phases, why **1921** matters, explosion, and why growth later slowed.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 1901–1921 stagnant / low; 1921–1951 steady; then rapid post-Independence growth; later decades slower rates.** **1921** is the **Great Divide** — first modern-census decade of declining growth rate. Explosion = Stage-2 style death fall with births still high (health, food, post-1947 gains). Recent decline: falling fertility (education, contraception, urbanisation, NPP framing); Census 2011 decadal growth **17.64%**.

</details>

## C. Composition

**Q5.** Age composition, population pyramid, sex ratio and literacy.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Age bands commonly 0–14, 15–59, 60+.** Dependency = young + aged on workers. A pyramid plots age–sex. Broad base = high fertility / expansive. Narrow base and ageing top = declining or stationary. **Sex ratio** = females per 1,000 males (India 2011 = **943**; CSR 0–6 = **919**). Literacy = ability to read and write with understanding, age **7+** (2011 = **74.04%**). Kerala leads literacy and sex ratio among states; Bihar lags literacy among states.

</details>

## D. Demographic transition and dividend

**Q6.** Demographic Transition Theory stages, India’s place, and demographic dividend.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Thompson (Notestein awareness).** Stage 1 high birth and death. Stage 2 death falls first → **rapid growth**. Stage 3 birth falls. Stage 4 low birth and death. India is framed past pure Stage 2, in later expanding / declining-growth transition. **Demographic dividend** = large **15–59** working-age share. Benefit needs education, skills, jobs and health — numbers alone do not deliver it.

</details>

## E. Migration

**Q7.** Types of migration, push versus pull, main internal streams, and effects.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Migration = change of usual residence.** Internal vs international; immigration = in, emigration = out. Push = drought, poverty, conflict, unemployment at origin. Pull = jobs, education, marriage, urban services at destination. Largest Indian stream: **rural → rural** (often female marriage). Rural → urban drives city growth. Effects: remittances and labour supply, but also urban pressure, family separation and regional imbalance.

</details>

## F. Urbanisation and town types

**Q8.** Urbanisation versus urban growth versus sprawl; metro, megacity, conurbation, megalopolis, satellite town.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Urbanisation = rising urban share of total population.** Urban growth = absolute rise in urban numbers. Sprawl = low-density outward spread. Metropolitan / UA = large functional city region. Megacity = often ≥10 million (UN framing). Conurbation = merged continuous built-up cities. Megalopolis = chained metro regions. Satellite town = planned or subordinate town near a metro (e.g. NCR satellites). Census 2011 urban share **31.16%**; million UAs = **53**.

</details>

**Q9.** Census town versus statutory town.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Statutory town** is notified by law (municipality, corporation, cantonment, notified town area).** **Census town** must meet all three: population ≥ **5,000**, density ≥ **400**/km², and ≥ **75%** of male main workers in non-agriculture.

</details>

## G. Rural and urban settlements

**Q10.** Rural settlement types and the geography that shapes them.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Clustered / nucleated → semi-clustered → hamleted → dispersed.** Compact common on fertile plains (and Rajasthan around scarce water; also defence clustering in Bundelkhand / Nagaland lore). Dispersed common in **North-East hills and forests** and high Himalaya. Water, relief, soil and security decide wet-point / dry-point and spacing. **Do not** key “Thar = dispersed” — NCERT Rajasthan is **clustered** at wells/oases.

</details>

**Q11.** Town functions, hierarchy and Central Place Theory in one line.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Functions: administrative, industrial, commercial, religious, tourist, transport, garrison, mining.** Hierarchy rises from small service centres to Class I / million cities. Larger cities offer more specialised services. Central Place Theory (Christaller awareness): towns exist to supply goods and services to surrounding areas at nested ranges.

</details>

## H. Urban problems and sustainability

**Q12.** Slums, sprawl, heat island, flooding, and sustainable urbanisation.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rapid in-migration plus costly formal housing → informal slums.** Sprawl eats farmland, lengthens trips and raises service costs. Heat island = city warmer than countryside (concrete, traffic, less green). Urban flooding rises from paved surfaces, clogged drains and encroached wetlands. Challenges: housing, water, sanitation, solid waste, congestion, air pollution. Sustainable urbanisation = compact growth, public transport, green cover, resilient drainage and inclusive housing.

</details>

## Map reasons and rapid chain

**Q13.** Why high or low population in these regions?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Ganga Plain — alluvium, water, farming, cities. Kerala — coast, high literacy, plantation and dense settlement lore. Rajasthan desert — aridity and sparse resources (settlements still cluster at water). Himalaya — steep cold terrain. North-East hills — forest, relief, sparse infrastructure. Metro regions — jobs, services and migration pull.**

</details>

**Q14.** Complete the rapid chain.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Density formula = pop / area (arithmetic). 1921 = Great Divide. Change = births, deaths, migration. Sex ratio = females / 1,000 males. Pyramid = age–sex graph. DTT = four stages; explosion in Stage 2. Dividend = 15–59 working age. Push = origin hardships. Pull = destination attractions. Rural→urban = urbanisation driver. Compact = plains / water points. Dispersed = hills / forests (NE). Census town = 5,000 + 75% non-agri males + 400/km². Statutory = notified. Sprawl = low-density outward growth. Heat island = city warmer than rural fringe.**

</details>
