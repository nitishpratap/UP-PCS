## A. Himalayas — north to south

**Q1.** How are the Himalayas classified from north to south? Name the major parallel ranges.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Trans-Himalaya → Greater Himalaya (Himadri) → Lesser Himalaya (Himachal) → Shiwalik (Outer Himalaya).**

</details>

**Q2.** What is the difference between the Trans-Himalaya, Greater Himalaya, Lesser Himalaya and Shiwalik?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Trans-Himalaya lies north of the Indus, granitic, a cold desert.** Himadri is crystalline, fossil-less, permanently snowed. Himachal is sedimentary with marine fossils. Shiwalik is the youngest, unconsolidated, with human remains.

</details>

**Q3.** Which is the highest and most continuous Himalayan range?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Greater Himalaya / Himadri**, generally above 6000 m.

</details>

**Q4.** What are the major ranges of the Trans-Himalaya? Which one is particularly important in India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Zaskar, Ladakh and Kailash.** Karakoram sits in the extreme north-west with **K2**. K2 is **not** on Himadri.

</details>

**Q5.** What is the Himalayan mountain arc, and approximately from where to where does it extend?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: About 2400 km from the Indus gorge in the west to the Dihang gorge in the east.**

</details>

**Q6.** Why are the Himalayas considered young fold mountains? What type of plate interaction formed them?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They rose from the Indian–Eurasian collision** (about 50 million years ago, Tertiary). They are still rising in zones.

</details>

**Q7.** What is the difference between Himadri, Himachal and Shiwalik in altitude, structure and relief?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Himadri is the snow wall above 6000 m, crystalline and fossil-less.** Himachal is 3700–4500 m, marine fossils, Pir Panjal and Dhauladhar, hill stations. Shiwalik is 900–1100 m, outermost, duns such as Dehra Dun.

</details>

**Q8.** Which Himalayan range contains many of the highest peaks of the Indian region?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Himadri** — Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi, Kamet.

</details>

## B. Peaks and passes

**Q9.** Name important UPPCS peaks of the Indian Himalayan region and their State or region.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kanchenjunga is in Sikkim. Nanda Devi and Kamet are in Uttarakhand. Kangto is in Arunachal. Sandakphu sits on the Darjeeling–Nepal side.** Namcha Barwa is **not** in India.

</details>

**Q10.** Which is the highest peak entirely within India, as locked in the notes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kanchenjunga, 8586 m**, on Himadri in Sikkim. Nanda Devi (7816 m) is the second.

</details>

**Q11.** Which is the highest peak associated with Sikkim?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kanchenjunga.**

</details>

**Q12.** Which important peak is associated with Uttarakhand?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Nanda Devi.** Kamet is the other Himadri lock for the State.

</details>

**Q13.** Match Zoji La, Nathu La, Shipki La, Bomdi La and Lipulekh with their locations.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Zoji La = Ladakh / J&K (Srinagar–Leh). Nathu La = Sikkim (India–Tibet). Shipki La = Himachal Pradesh (Sutlej, India–Tibet). Bomdi La = Arunachal (near Bomdila town). Lipulekh = Uttarakhand**, not Ladakh.

</details>

**Q14.** Which of these passes connect India with Tibet or China, and which are important for trade or pilgrimage?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Nathu La and Jelep La (Sikkim) and Shipki La (HP) are India–Tibet trade corridors.** Lipulekh, Niti and Mana in **Uttarakhand** serve Kailash–Mansarovar and other Himalayan routes. Nathu La was reopened for trade in 2006.

</details>

**Q15.** Why are Himalayan passes strategically and economically important?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They are the only usable gaps through the wall** — defence, trade, pilgrimage, and road links such as Srinagar–Leh via Zoji La.

</details>

## C. Regional Himalayas (west to east)

**Q16.** What are the three major regional divisions of the Himalayas from west to east?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Western, Central and Eastern Himalaya.** Finer teaching split: Punjab → Kumaon → Nepal → Sikkim–Darjeeling–Bhutan → Arunachal.

</details>

**Q17.** What is meant by Punjab Himalaya, Kumaon Himalaya, Nepal Himalaya and Assam Himalaya?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Punjab Himalaya = Kashmir and Himachal (Indus to Satluj). Kumaon Himalaya = Uttarakhand (Satluj to Kali). Nepal Himalaya = Kali to Teesta. Assam Himalaya = Teesta to Dihang**, including Bhutan–Arunachal.

</details>

**Q18.** Which rivers broadly mark the boundaries between these regional divisions?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Indus, Satluj, Kali, Teesta and Dihang.**

</details>

**Q19.** Which Himalayan region contains Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Punjab Himalaya.** Kashmir Valley is sandwiched by Pir Panjal on the south and Himadri / Zanskar on the north.

</details>

**Q20.** Which Himalayan region includes Uttarakhand?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kumaon Himalaya.**

</details>

**Q21.** Which Himalayan region lies between the Kali and Teesta rivers?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Nepal Himalaya.**

</details>

## D. Eastern and North-Eastern hills

**Q22.** What happens to the Himalayas after the Dihang Gorge?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The arc bends south as the Purvanchal Hills.**

</details>

**Q23.** What are the Purvanchal Hills?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The north-eastern fold hills beyond Dihang — Patkai, Naga, Mizo and Mishmi.** This is **not** eastern Uttar Pradesh.

</details>

**Q24.** Name the major hill ranges of the Purvanchal.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Patkai Bum, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills, Mizo / Lushai Hills, and Mishmi Hills.**

</details>

**Q25.** Match Patkai Bum, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills and Mizo / Lushai Hills with their States.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Patkai Bum = Arunachal–Myanmar. Naga Hills = Nagaland (Saramati 3841 m). Manipur Hills = Manipur. Mizo / Lushai = Mizoram (Phawngpui / Blue Mountain).**

</details>

**Q26.** Why are the North-Eastern hills described as an extension of the Himalayan system despite their different orientation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They are the same young fold belt, turned south after Dihang**, so they run more north–south than the main Himalayan east–west arc.

</details>

**Q27.** Which hills form an important barrier between India and Myanmar?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Patkai Bum, Naga Hills and the Mizo Hills** along the eastern frontier. Pangsau Pass is an Arunachal–Myanmar lock.

</details>

## E. Western and Eastern Ghats

**Q28.** Compare the Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats on continuity, altitude, slope, rivers and rainfall.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Western Ghats are a continuous high wall from the Tapi gap to Kanyakumari, steep to the west, and force orographic rain.** Eastern Ghats are lower, discontinuous, and cut by east-flowing rivers.

</details>

**Q29.** Which is generally higher and more continuous — Western Ghats or Eastern Ghats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Western Ghats.** Highest of the Eastern Ghats is about Jindhagada ~1690 m.

</details>

**Q30.** Why do the Western Ghats receive heavy rainfall on their western slopes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They stand across the south-west monsoon**, so the windward west is wet and the Deccan to the east is rain-shadow.

</details>

**Q31.** Name the important sections of the Western Ghats from north to south.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Sahyadri in Maharashtra, then the Nilgiri, then Anamalai south of Palghat Gap, then Cardamom Hills.** Gaps include Thal Ghat, Bhor Gap and Palghat Gap.

</details>

**Q32.** Which are the important peaks of the Western Ghats, and where are they?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Anaimudi (2695 m) is in Kerala on the Anamalai–Cardamom complex.** Doddabetta (2637 m) is Tamil Nadu’s highest, on the Nilgiri.

</details>

**Q33.** Which is the highest peak of the Western Ghats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Anaimudi, 2695 m.**

</details>

**Q34.** Which is the highest peak of South India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Anaimudi.** It is both the Western Ghats high point and the South India high point.

</details>

**Q35.** What are the Nilgiri Hills, and where are they located?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Hills of Tamil Nadu and Kerala.** Doddabetta is the high point. Ooty sits here.

</details>

**Q36.** Which hills lie at the junction of the Western and Eastern Ghats?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Nilgiri Hills**, where Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats and the southern hills meet.

</details>

## F. Other peninsular hills

**Q37.** Where is the Aravalli Range located?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Gujarat–Rajasthan–Delhi**, about 800 km. The Delhi Ridge is the north-east stump.

</details>

**Q38.** In which direction do the Aravallis run, and why are they among India’s oldest mountain systems?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: South-west to north-east.** They are ancient Precambrian fold remnants, heavily eroded. UPPCS lock: Aravalli = oldest, Himadri = youngest among the usual options.

</details>

**Q39.** Which is the highest peak of the Aravallis?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Guru Shikhar, 1722 m**, Rajasthan (Mount Abu).

</details>

**Q40.** Locate Vindhya, Satpura, Maikal, Rajmahal and Meghalaya Hills.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Vindhya = north of the Narmada in Madhya Pradesh. Satpura = south of the Narmada (Dhupgarh, Pachmarhi). Maikal = east of Satpura–Mahadeo toward Amarkantak. Rajmahal = Jharkhand volcanic trap. Meghalaya Hills = Garo–Khasi–Jaintia plateau**, peninsular, not Himalayan fold.

</details>

**Q41.** What is the relationship between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They run east–west, parallel, with the Narmada rift between them.** Central India west to east is **Satpura → Mahadeo → Maikal → Chhotanagpur**.

</details>

**Q42.** Which river flows through the rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Narmada.** The Tapi follows a Satpura gorge to the south of that pair. Amarkantak sources the Narmada and the Son.

</details>

**Q43.** What are the major hills of Meghalaya?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Garo in the west, Khasi in the centre, Jaintia in the east.** They are separated from Chotanagpur by the **Malda gap**.

</details>

**Q44.** Which Meghalaya hill is the easternmost?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Jaintia Hills.**

</details>

**Q45.** Which Meghalaya hill is associated with Shillong?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Khasi Hills.** Shillong Peak is 1965 m. Mawsynram and Cherrapunji sit here, not on Garo or Jaintia.

</details>

## Map master recall

**Q46.** From Kashmir to Arunachal Pradesh, name the major Himalayan regional divisions in order.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Punjab Himalaya → Kumaon Himalaya → Nepal Himalaya → Sikkim–Darjeeling–Bhutan → Arunachal (Assam Himalaya), then the south bend as Purvanchal after Dihang.**

</details>

**Q47.** From north-west India to south India, locate Aravalli, Vindhya, Satpura, Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats and Nilgiri.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Aravalli SW–NE in Rajasthan. Vindhya north of Narmada. Satpura south of Narmada. Western Ghats along the west coast. Eastern Ghats along the east coast, broken. Nilgiri at their southern junction.**

</details>

**Q48.** Which major Indian mountain systems are young fold mountains, and which are old or residual?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Young fold = Himalaya, Karakoram, Purvanchal.** Old / residual = Aravalli, and the worn peninsular block (Vindhya, Satpura, Ghats as escarpments on that block). Meghalaya is a peninsular plateau, not a Himalayan fold.

</details>

**Q49.** Which mountain systems act as major climatic barriers in India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Himalaya block cold Central Asian winds and lift monsoon rain on southern slopes.** The Western Ghats lift the south-west monsoon. The Aravalli help the Thar rain-shadow on their leeward side.

</details>

**Q50.** Mentally mark Himalayas, Karakoram, Shiwalik, Aravalli, Vindhya, Satpura, Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, Nilgiri, Patkai, Naga, Mizo and Meghalaya Hills.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Karakoram = extreme NW. Himalaya = Indus to Dihang. Shiwalik = outer southern belt. Purvanchal = Patkai–Naga–Mizo after Dihang. Meghalaya = Garo–Khasi–Jaintia south of the Brahmaputra, not Purvanchal. Peninsular chain as in Q47.**

</details>

## Ten-minute chain

**Q51.** Reproduce the four chains without a map.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:**

Himalayas: Trans-Himalaya → Himadri → Himachal → Shiwalik → Purvanchal.

Peninsular: Aravalli → Vindhya → Satpura → Western Ghats → Eastern Ghats → Nilgiri.

North-East: Patkai → Naga → Manipur → Mizo.

Locks: Narmada between Vindhya and Satpura. Western Ghats = orographic rain. Aravalli = ancient. Nilgiri = Ghats junction. Dihang = bend into Purvanchal.

Peak match: TN Doddabetta · RJ Guru Shikhar · NL Saramati · MP Dhupgarh · KL Anaimudi · UK Nanda Devi · SK Kanchenjunga.

Pass match: Nathu La Sikkim · Shipki La HP · Lipulekh Uttarakhand · Zoji La Ladakh · Bomdila Arunachal.

</details>
