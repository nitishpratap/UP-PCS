## A. Basic concepts

**Q1.** What is a drainage system, and what is a drainage basin?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A drainage system is the network of streams that drain a region.** A drainage basin is the whole catchment of a large river.

</details>

**Q2.** What is a watershed, and how is it different from a drainage basin?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A watershed is a smaller catchment inside a basin**, or the divide between basins. The basin is the full river catchment.

</details>

**Q3.** What are the major factors controlling India’s drainage?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Structure, slope, rock type, and climate.** About 77% of area drains to the Bay of Bengal. About 23% drains to the Arabian Sea. Inland basins are about 8% of area (Luni, Ghaggar).

</details>

**Q4.** What are the two broad categories of Indian rivers by origin and character?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Himalayan rivers and Peninsular rivers.**

</details>

**Q5.** Compare Himalayan and Peninsular rivers on origin, flow, length, valley, meanders, and mouth.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Himalayan rivers are young, perennial (snow plus rain), often antecedent, with deep gorges then meanders and large deltas.** Peninsular rivers are old, graded, mostly rain-fed and seasonal, and consequent on the eastward slope. Most east-flowing ones build deltas. Narmada and Tapi build estuaries.

</details>

**Q6.** What are the major drainage patterns found in India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dendritic, trellis, radial, rectangular, centripetal, and parallel.**

</details>

**Q7.** Explain dendritic, trellis, radial, rectangular and centripetal patterns, with one Indian example each.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dendritic is tree-like on uniform rock (Ganga–Godavari plains). Trellis is right-angle joins on folded ridges (2024 fact). Radial spreads from a dome (Amarkantak, Girnar). Rectangular follows joints and faults (Himalayan stretches). Centripetal drains into a closed basin (Imphal / Loktak).** Parallel torrents drop down the Western Ghats.

</details>

## B. Indus system

**Q8.** Where does the Indus originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Near Kailash / Mansarovar in Tibet.** It is antecedent. It cuts a Ladakh gorge and reaches the Arabian Sea near Karachi. Only about 710 km is in India.

</details>

**Q9.** Trace the Indus through India and name the country where it ultimately flows.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Tibet → Ladakh → Pakistan → Arabian Sea.** Shyok and Zanskar join in Ladakh.

</details>

**Q10.** Name the five major tributaries of the Indus (Panj Ab).

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej.** They meet as Panjnad above Mithankot, then join the Indus.

</details>

**Q11.** Which Indus tributaries flow through India, and which are mainly Pakistan’s under the Treaty?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: All five have Indian stretches. Beas is almost wholly in India.** Under the **1960 Indus Waters Treaty**, India uses **Ravi, Beas, Sutlej**. Pakistan uses **Indus, Jhelum, Chenab**.

</details>

**Q12.** Arrange Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej from west to east.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Jhelum → Chenab → Ravi → Beas → Sutlej.** Memory: JCRBS from west to east.

</details>

**Q13.** Where does the Jhelum originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Verinag.** It flows through Wular Lake and joins the Chenab.

</details>

**Q14.** Where does the Chenab originate, and which two rivers form it?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Near Bara Lacha La, from Chandra plus Bhaga.** Baglihar and Salal stand on the Chenab.

</details>

**Q15.** Where does the Beas originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Near Rohtang.** Pandoh Dam is on the **Beas**, not the Ravi.

</details>

**Q16.** Where does the Sutlej originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rakas Tal, Tibet.** It is trans-Himalayan. Bhakra is on the Sutlej.

</details>

**Q17.** What is the significance of the Indus Waters Treaty, 1960?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It splits the six rivers: eastern three to India, western three to Pakistan.**

</details>

**Q18.** Which rivers are the eastern rivers and western rivers under that Treaty?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Eastern = Ravi, Beas, Sutlej. Western = Indus, Jhelum, Chenab.**

</details>

## C. Ganga system

**Q19.** Where does the Bhagirathi originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Gangotri.** Tehri is Bhagirathi plus Bhilangana.

</details>

**Q20.** Where does the Alaknanda originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Near Satopanth / Bhagirath Kharak.**

</details>

**Q21.** Where do the Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet, and what is the river called after that?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Devprayag.** After that confluence the river is the **Ganga**.

</details>

**Q22.** Name major Ganga tributaries from the Himalayan (left) side and the Peninsular (right) side.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Left / north: Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda.** Right: Yamuna, Son, Damodar. **Jonk is a Mahanadi tributary, not Ganga.**

</details>

**Q23.** Where does the Yamuna originate, and where does it meet the Ganga?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Yamunotri (Bandarpunch).** Sangam at **Prayagraj**. It is the Ganga’s largest tributary.

</details>

**Q24.** Name the major tributaries of the Yamuna, west to east on the right bank.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chambal → Sind → Betwa → Ken.** Hindon is a **left-bank** Yamuna stream. Tons joins near Kalsi. Betwa meets at **Hamirpur**, not Prayagraj. Ken meets at Banda. Chambal meets at Etawah.

</details>

**Q25.** How is the Chambal related to the Yamuna?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chambal is a right-bank tributary of the Yamuna**, not of the Ganga directly. Banas joins the Chambal from the Aravalli. Chambal is the usual **superimposed** example.

</details>

**Q26.** What is the significance of the Ganga–Brahmaputra delta, and why is the Ganga system important for settlement?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Padma + Jamuna + Meghna build the Sundarbans arcuate delta, the world’s largest.** The Ganga basin is India’s largest (about 26% of area). Perennial water and fertile floodplain support dense farming and cities. Farakka feeds the Hooghly. In Bangladesh the Ganga is the **Padma**.

</details>

## D. Brahmaputra

**Q27.** Where does the Brahmaputra originate, and what is it called in Tibet, Arunachal and Assam?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chemayungdung / Angsi, Tibet, as Yarlung Tsangpo.** In Arunachal it is **Siang / Dihang**. After Dibang and Lohit join, it is the **Brahmaputra** in Assam. In Bangladesh it is the **Jamuna**, not the Yamuna.

</details>

**Q28.** Why does the Brahmaputra form a braided channel in Assam, and why does it flood?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A huge silt load in a wide, gentle Assam valley splits the channel.** Heavy monsoon, snow-melt, and a braided bed cause floods. Teesta was captured from the Ganga.

</details>

**Q29.** Name important Indian tributaries of the Brahmaputra. What is Majuli?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Subansiri, Kameng, Manas, Sankosh, Teesta** on the northern side. **Majuli** is the great river island on the Brahmaputra.

</details>

**Q30.** How does the Brahmaputra connect with the Ganga system?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: In Bangladesh, Jamuna meets Padma, then Meghna, and reaches the Bay through the Sundarbans.** Barak becomes Meghna. Barak is **not** inland drainage.

</details>

## E. Peninsular rivers

**Q31.** Why do most Peninsular rivers flow to the Bay of Bengal, while Narmada and Tapi flow west?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The plateau slopes high west to low east, so consequent rivers run east.** Narmada and Tapi occupy **rift valleys** between Vindhya and Satpura and reach the Arabian Sea.

</details>

**Q32.** What is a rift valley, and how is it linked to the Narmada?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A fault-bounded trough.** The Narmada uses the rift between Vindhya (north) and Satpura (south). Damodar is the “fault valley drainage” tag. Tapi is the Narmada’s twin.

</details>

**Q33.** Where do the Narmada and Tapi originate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Narmada = Amarkantak (Maikal). Tapi = Multai on the Satpura, not the Western Ghats.** Narmada is the longest west-flowing peninsular river. Surat sits near the Tapi mouth.

</details>

**Q34.** Name major tributaries of the Narmada and of the Tapi.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Narmada: Hiran (right bank), Tawa (left bank).** Tapi: Purna and Girna are the usual named tributaries.

</details>

**Q35.** Which major Peninsular rivers form estuaries, and which form large east-coast deltas?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Estuaries = Narmada, Tapi (and Mandovi).** Deltas = Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri. West coast is steep and silt-poor on the rift rivers. East coast has a wide shelf.

</details>

## F. East-flowing rivers

**Q36.** Where does the Godavari originate, and why is it called Dakshin Ganga?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Trimbakeshwar, Nashik** — only about 80 km from the Arabian Sea, then it flows east. It is the **longest peninsular river** and the **largest peninsular basin**.

</details>

**Q37.** Name the major tributaries of the Godavari.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Left: Penganga, Wardha and Wainganga meet as Pranhita; also Indravati and Sabari.** Right: **Manjra**. Pranhita is **not** a Mahanadi tributary.

</details>

**Q38.** Where does the Krishna originate, and what are its major tributaries?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Just north of Mahabaleshwar.** Right: Ghataprabha, Malaprabha, Tungabhadra. Left: Bhima, Musi. Srisailam is on the **Krishna**, not Tungabhadra.

</details>

**Q39.** Where does the Kaveri originate, and what are its major tributaries?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Talakaveri on Brahmagiri, Coorg.** Hemavati, Kabini, Bhavani, Amravati. Mettur is on the Kaveri. The Kaveri delta is **moribund**.

</details>

**Q40.** Where does the Mahanadi originate, and what are its major tributaries?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Sihawa / Dandakaranya, Chhattisgarh.** Left: Seonath, Hasdeo, Mand, Ib. Right: Ong, Tel, **Jonk**. Hirakud is at Sambalpur.

</details>

**Q41.** Arrange Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri from north to south.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mahanadi → Godavari → Krishna → Kaveri.**

</details>

## G. Lakes, deltas, patterns

**Q42.** What is the difference between a delta and an estuary? Why east vs west?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A delta is a silt-built coast. An estuary is a tidal funnel.** East-flowing rivers dump silt on a wide shelf. Narmada and Tapi carry little silt through hard rift rock on a steep west coast.

</details>

**Q43.** Which is the largest delta, and what is the Sundarban?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Ganga–Brahmaputra (Sundarbans) arcuate delta is the world’s largest.** Bird’s-foot is the **Mississippi**, not the Ganga. Sundarban is the mangrove tract of that delta.

</details>

**Q44.** What are Chilika, Pulicat and Kolleru in drainage or coastal geography?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chilika (Odisha) is India’s largest east-coast lagoon, on the Mahanadi–Utkal coast.** Pulicat is the second lagoon (Andhra–Tamil Nadu), barred by Sriharikota. **Kolleru is a freshwater lake on the Godavari–Krishna delta**, not the lagoon king.

</details>

**Q45.** Which drainage pattern fits the Ganga–Brahmaputra plains, a central highland, folded ridges, and the Narmada–Son region?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Plains = dendritic. Central highland / Amarkantak = radial (Narmada west, Son to Ganga). Folded ridges = trellis. Narmada–Son = structure-guided rift plus radial node at Amarkantak.**

</details>

**Q46.** What is antecedent drainage, and why are Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra discussed with it?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The river is older than the uplift and saws a gorge through the rising Himalaya.** set: **Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra**. Discordant drainage cuts across structure.

</details>

**Q47.** What is superimposed drainage?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The river inherits an older course onto newer structure.** **Chambal** is the usual peninsular example.

</details>

## Map traces

**Q48.** Trace Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra, and the four east plus two west Peninsular rivers without a map.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:**

Indus: Tibet → Ladakh → Jhelum–Chenab–Ravi–Beas–Sutlej (W→E) → Pakistan → Arabian Sea.

Ganga: Bhagirathi + Alaknanda at Devprayag → Yamuna (Chambal–Sind–Betwa–Ken) plus left-bank Himalayan tribs → Padma in Bangladesh.

Brahmaputra: Tsangpo → Siang/Dihang → Assam (Majuli) → Jamuna → Padma–Meghna.

West: Narmada and Tapi to the Arabian Sea. East: Mahanadi → Godavari → Krishna → Kaveri to the Bay.

</details>

</details>
