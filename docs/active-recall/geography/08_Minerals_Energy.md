## A. Basics

**Q1.** How do metallic vs non-metallic, and ferrous vs non-ferrous, differ? Why are minerals exhaustible?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Metallic ores yield metals (iron, copper, bauxite). Non-metallic ores do not (limestone, mica).** Ferrous ores contain iron (iron ore, manganese, chromite). Non-ferrous ores do not (copper, bauxite, gold). Geological formation is slow, so stocks are **non-renewable** at human timescales.

</details>

**Q2.** Which geological regions hold India’s minerals, and why is the Chota Nagpur Plateau the mineral heartland?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Most major minerals lie **east of the Mangalore–Kanpur line**. The northern plains are almost empty of ores.** Chota Nagpur (Jharkhand–Odisha–West Bengal, parts of Chhattisgarh) is India’s **Ruhr**: iron, coal, manganese, bauxite, mica, copper, chromite. Other belts: Central (CG–MP–TG–AP–MH), South-Western Plateau (KA–Goa; little coal except Neyveli lignite), North-Western Aravalli (Cu–Zn–mica–oil), Himalaya/Assam (Tertiary coal and oil). Petroleum sits in **sedimentary basins**, not the crystalline shield. India is **rich in ferrous ores, poor in non-ferrous metals except bauxite**.

</details>

## B. Coal

**Q3.** What are the ranks of coal, which rank feeds thermal power, and what is coking coal?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Peat → lignite → bituminous → anthracite.** Non-coking bituminous feeds **thermal power** (about 80% of generation lore). **Coking coal** makes metallurgical coke for blast furnaces. **Jharia (Jharkhand)** is the coking fact. Uttar Pradesh coal is **non-coking Singrauli–Sonbhadra**, not Jharia. Neyveli is **lignite, Tamil Nadu**.

</details>

**Q4.** Where are Gondwana coalfields, and which fields fact to which States?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Gondwana coal is about 97% of Indian coal, in Damodar–Son–Mahanadi–Godavari valleys.** Tertiary coal is in the North-East. **Jharia = Jharkhand** (coking, mine fires). **Raniganj = West Bengal**. **Talcher = Odisha** (Mahanadi). **Korba = Chhattisgarh**. **Singrauli = Madhya Pradesh–Uttar Pradesh** (Son valley), **not** northern Chhattisgarh. Ramgarh is Jharkhand. Singareni is Godavari. Maolong is **Meghalaya**, not Arunachal.

</details>

**Q5.** Opencast vs underground mining, and the main environmental costs of coal?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Opencast strips the surface (cheaper, more land scar). Underground follows seams (costlier, subsidence, fires).** Costs: land loss, mine fires, fly ash, sulphur oxides, carbon dioxide, and groundwater damage.

</details>

## C. Petroleum and gas

**Q6.** Name India’s main petroleum basins and the Digboi / Mumbai High / Cambay / KG facts.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Oil and gas sit in sedimentary basins.** **Digboi / Naharkatiya = Assam** (oldest onshore lore). **Mumbai High = offshore Arabian Sea** (largest offshore crude). **Cambay / Ankleshwar = Gujarat**. **KG Basin = Andhra Pradesh and offshore** (gas-heavy). Also Cauvery and Barmer (Rajasthan). Black Revolution = petroleum.

</details>

**Q7.** How does natural gas differ from petroleum, and why is it cleaner?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Petroleum is liquid hydrocarbons. Natural gas is mainly **methane** (CNG is methane).** LPG is **propane/butane**, not CNG. Gas burns with less ash and sulphur than coal or heavy oil. Uses: power, fertiliser, city gas, CNG transport, petrochemicals.

</details>

## D. Iron and other metals

**Q8.** Types of iron ore, which is richest in iron, and the four belts / map facts?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Magnetite has about **70% Fe** (highest grade). Hematite has about 50–60% Fe and is **most used**.** Leaders: **Odisha and Chhattisgarh**. Belts: Odisha–Jharkhand; Durg–Bastar–Chandrapur; Ballari–Chitradurga; Maharashtra–Goa. **Keonjhar–Mayurbhanj / Gurumahisani = Odisha. Bailadila = Chhattisgarh. Bellary–Chitradurga / Kudremukh = Karnataka. Singhbhum / Noamundi = Jharkhand.** Concentration is on the **Peninsular shield**. Steel needs iron plus coal and manganese.

</details>

**Q9.** Manganese, bauxite and copper: States, uses, and the Khetri fact?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Manganese (Odisha, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh) is a steel alloy / deoxidiser. Bhilwara is **not** manganese.** Bauxite is the aluminium ore, linked to laterite. **Odisha** leads (Panchpatmali). Also Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh (Katni), Jharkhand. BALCO is Korba. HINDALCO is Renukoot (UP). **Copper: Khetri = Rajasthan.** Also Singhbhum (Jharkhand) and Malanjkhand (Madhya Pradesh). Chitradurga is **not** a copper city.

</details>

## E. Non-metallic

**Q10.** Limestone and mica: belts, uses, and why mica still matters?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Limestone is bulky, so **cement plants sit near quarries** (UP Chunar–Dalla lore).** Mica belts: **Koderma–Gaya–Hazaribagh** (Jharkhand), Nellore (Andhra Pradesh), Bhilwara/Ajmer (Rajasthan). Use: electrical **dielectric** and insulation. India no longer dominates world output, but the mineral remains a strategic insulator in electrical goods.

</details>

## F. Conventional energy and nuclear

**Q11.** Conventional energy mix: why coal dominates thermal power, and thermal vs hydro vs nuclear?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Conventional = coal, oil, gas, nuclear. Non-conventional = solar, wind, tidal, biogas, geothermal.** Thermal burns coal/gas (cheap base load, fly ash, non-renewable). Hydro uses falling water (Himalaya and Western Ghats relief). Nuclear uses fission (no daily fuel trains; waste and siting). India leans on coal because reserves, existing plants, and 24-hour supply beat monsoon-variable hydro.

</details>

**Q12.** Why hydropower suits the Himalaya and Western Ghats, and what are the costs of large dams?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Steep gradients and high rainfall give head.** Advantages: renewable electricity, peak power, irrigation and flood control. Costs: submergence, displacement, silt, seismic risk, and downstream ecology. **Shivanasamudra is hydel. Kalpakkam is nuclear.**

</details>

**Q13.** Nuclear stations by State, uranium vs thorium, and where each is found?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Tarapur = Maharashtra. Kakrapar = Gujarat. Kudankulam and Kalpakkam = Tamil Nadu. Kaiga = Karnataka. Rawatbhata = Rajasthan. Narora = Uttar Pradesh.** Uranium fuel: **Jaduguda, Jharkhand**. Thorium/monazite: **Kerala–Tamil Nadu beach sands** for the long three-stage programme. Nuclear and geothermal are **not** solar-derived. Nuclear is **not** biomass.

</details>

## G. Renewables and transition

**Q14.** Solar, wind, biomass, geothermal and tidal: where is the potential?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Solar: arid west — **Rajasthan and Gujarat** (clear skies, high insolation).** Wind: **Tamil Nadu (Muppandal), Gujarat (Kutch), also Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan (Jaisalmer).** Biomass: gobar, crop residue, wood — **not** coal. Geothermal: Himalayan hot-spring belts (Puga lore). Tidal: **Khambhat greater than Kutch**. Diu was the first UT with 100% solar lore.

</details>

**Q15.** Energy security vs energy transition, storage, and green hydrogen?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Energy security is reliable, affordable supply. Energy transition is shifting the mix off fossils.** Challenges: land, grid, intermittency, minerals, and finance. **Storage** (batteries, pumped hydro) holds surplus solar/wind for night and calm days. **Green hydrogen** is hydrogen from electrolysis powered by renewables, aimed at steel, fertiliser and heavy transport.

</details>

<details>

Cambay = Gujarat. Bailadila = CG iron. Khetri = RJ copper. Bauxite lead = Odisha. Limestone = cement. Uranium = Jaduguda JH. Thorium = KL–TN sands. Solar = Rajasthan/Gujarat.
