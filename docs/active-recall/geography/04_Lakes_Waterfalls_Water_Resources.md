## A. Lake concepts

**Q1.** What are the major types of lakes in India based on origin?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Tectonic, glacial (tarns and moraine-dammed), oxbow, lagoon, crater or meteorite, wind/playa, landslide or dun, and artificial reservoirs.** Chemistry (fresh, saline, brackish) is a second filter.

</details>

**Q2.** Differentiate tectonic, glacial, oxbow, lagoon and artificial lakes.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Tectonic lakes sit in a crustal warp (Wular). Glacial lakes fill cirques or sit behind moraine (Ladakh and Himachal tarns). Oxbow lakes are cut-off meanders (Ganga plain, Kabartal). Lagoons sit behind a coastal bar (Chilika, Pulicat). Artificial lakes are dam reservoirs (Gobind Sagar, Hirakud, Tehri).**

</details>

**Q3.** Why are lakes important for storage, irrigation, flood control, hydroelectricity, ecology and tourism?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They hold water in the dry season, feed canals, cushion floods, create head for turbines, host fish and birds, and draw tourists.** Wular and Kolleru store. Bhakra and Hirakud reservoirs irrigate and generate. Chilika and Loktak are ecology locks. Dal is tourism, not the size king.

</details>

**Q4.** What is the difference between a natural lake and a reservoir?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A natural lake fills a hollow made by earth, ice, river or sea.** A reservoir is **man-made** behind a dam. Gobind Sagar is a reservoir. Wular is natural.

</details>

## B. Important lakes

**Q5.** Match Wular, Dal, Pangong Tso, Tso Moriri, Sambhar, Chilika, Pulicat, Kolleru, Loktak and Vembanad with their States.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Wular and Dal = Jammu and Kashmir. Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri = Ladakh. Sambhar = Rajasthan. Chilika = Odisha. Pulicat = Andhra Pradesh–Tamil Nadu. Kolleru = Andhra Pradesh. Loktak = Manipur. Vembanad = Kerala.**

</details>

**Q6.** Which is India’s largest freshwater lake by surface area?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Wular**, on the Jhelum in Jammu and Kashmir. Dal is the tourism lake, not the size king.

</details>

**Q7.** Which is India’s largest brackish-water lagoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chilika**, Odisha, east coast. If a stem says **inland** saline, pick **Sambhar**, not Chilika.

</details>

**Q8.** Which is India’s largest inland salt lake?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Sambhar**, Rajasthan (Jaipur–Ajmer–Nagaur). Salt pans and flamingos.

</details>

**Q9.** Which lake is famous for floating islands or phumdis?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Loktak**, Manipur.

</details>

**Q10.** Which lake is associated with Keibul Lamjao National Park?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Loktak.** Keibul Lamjao is the only floating national park. It holds the Sangai. It is **not** on Wular or Dal.

</details>

**Q11.** Which lake is an important wintering ground for migratory birds and lies in Odisha?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chilika.** Nalabana is the bird island.

</details>

**Q12.** What is special about Kolleru Lake?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is a freshwater wetland between the Godavari and Krishna deltas.** It is **not** the east-coast lagoon king.

</details>

**Q13.** What is special about Vembanad Lake?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is Kerala’s longest backwater** and a Ramsar wetland. Sasthamkotta is Kerala’s large freshwater lake, a different lock.

</details>

**Q14.** What are lagoon lakes? Name two important Indian examples.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Coastal water bodies barred from the sea by a sand spit.** **Chilika** (largest) and **Pulicat** (second, Sriharikota).

</details>

**Q15.** Which important lakes are associated with Jammu and Kashmir or Ladakh?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Wular and Dal in Kashmir. Pangong Tso, Tso Moriri and Tso Kar in Ladakh.** High tarns can burst as GLOFs if ice or debris dams fail.

</details>

**Q16.** Why are Himalayan lakes particularly important in the context of glaciers and climate change?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Retreat of glaciers enlarges moraine-dammed lakes.** Unstable dams raise **GLOF** risk in Himachal, Uttarakhand, Sikkim and Ladakh.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

<details>

## D. Waterfalls

**Q23.** What geological conditions favour waterfalls?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Youthful rivers, a knickpoint, hard rock over soft rock, a fault scarp, or rejuvenation.** Peninsular Ghats and plateau edges are classic. Himalayan gorges also drop.

</details>

**Q24.** Match Jog, Dudhsagar, Athirappilly, Shivanasamudra, Hogenakkal, Chitrakote, Dhuandhar and Hundru with their States.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Jog = Karnataka. Dudhsagar = Goa. Athirappilly = Kerala. Shivanasamudra = Karnataka. Hogenakkal = Tamil Nadu–Karnataka border. Chitrakote = Chhattisgarh. Dhuandhar = Madhya Pradesh. Hundru = Jharkhand.** India’s **highest** fall is **Kunchikal** (Varahi, Karnataka), not Jog.

</details>

**Q25.** Which waterfall is associated with the Sharavathi River?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Jog (Gersoppa)**, Karnataka. Sharavati is **west-flowing** to the Arabian Sea. It is **not** a Cauvery tributary. Jog is famous, not India’s tallest.

</details>

**Q26.** Which famous waterfall is located on the Mandovi river system?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dudhsagar**, Goa.

</details>

**Q27.** Which waterfall is often called the “Niagara of India”?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Chitrakote** on the **Indravati** in Chhattisgarh. A horseshoe drop, not India’s highest.

</details>

**Q28.** Which waterfall is associated with the Narmada River?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dhuandhar** at Bhedaghat, Jabalpur (Mahakoshal, not Malwa).

</details>

**Q29.** Which waterfall is associated with the Kaveri and is important for hydropower?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Shivanasamudra** in Karnataka. Hogenakkal is the Kaveri smoking-falls lock on the Tamil Nadu border. India’s first hydro station (Shivanasamudra, 1902) is the coaching lock.

</details>

**Q30.** Why are waterfalls particularly common on the Peninsular plateau?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rivers drop off the Western Ghats and plateau edges** where hard basalt or gneiss meets a scarp. Rejuvenation adds knickpoints (Hundru on Subarnarekha, Jog on Sharavati).

</details>

**Q31.** Why are waterfalls important for hydroelectric power?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A sudden head (height) gives potential energy without a very long canal.** Koyna, Idukki and Shivanasamudra sit on Ghats or plateau drops. Himalayan hydro uses high head plus perennial flow.

</details>

## E. Water resources

**Q32.** What are India’s major sources of water?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rain and snow, rivers, lakes and reservoirs (surface), and groundwater.** India has about **18%** of world people, **4%** of water, and **2.4%** of land.

</details>

**Q33.** Differentiate surface water and groundwater.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Surface water is rivers, lakes and reservoirs.** Groundwater is the water in aquifers, tapped by wells and tubewells. Utilisable totals are about **690 BCM surface** and **433 BCM groundwater** (about **1123 BCM** together).

</details>

**Q34.** What are the major uses of water in India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Irrigation, drinking and domestic supply, industry, hydropower, and ecology.** Agriculture takes the lion’s share.

</details>

**Q35.** Which sector consumes the largest share of India’s freshwater?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Agriculture / irrigation.** About **89%** of extracted **groundwater** also goes to irrigation.

</details>

**Q36.** Why is India facing increasing water stress despite substantial water resources?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Population, uneven monsoon, over-irrigation, pollution, and falling per-capita availability.** The Indo-Gangetic plains hold much of the groundwater. The peninsula and rain-shadow west are drier.

</details>

**Q37.** What is meant by water scarcity?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Demand exceeds reliable local supply**, by season or by year. Physical scarcity (Thar, rain-shadow) differs from economic scarcity (poor storage even where rain is high).

</details>

**Q38.** What factors cause regional and seasonal variation in water availability?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Monsoon concentration in June–September, Western Disturbance winter rain only in the north-west, orography, and rain-shadow.** Tamil Nadu’s big share is the north-east monsoon. Himalayan snow feeds perennial rivers.

</details>

**Q39.** Why is groundwater particularly important for Indian agriculture?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It now waters more than 60% of irrigated area**, especially tubewells in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh. Canals dominate parts of the Ganga plain. Tanks were the old peninsula tool.

</details>

**Q40.** What are the major problems of excessive groundwater extraction?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Falling water tables, over-exploited blocks, fluoride and arsenic, and land subsidence in pockets.** Free farm power in Punjab–Haryana encourages pumping. Arsenic is a Ganga-belt lock (UP, Bihar, West Bengal).

</details>

## F. Conservation

**Q41.** What is rainwater harvesting?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Catching roof or field runoff in tanks, pits or aquifers** instead of letting it run to waste.

</details>

**Q42.** Why is rainwater harvesting particularly important in water-stressed regions?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rain is short and intense.** Local storage and recharge beat long canals in Thar, Bundelkhand and rain-shadow Deccan.

</details>

**Q43.** What is watershed management?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Treating a small catchment as one unit** — bunds, trenches, vegetation, check dams — so soil and water stay on the slope.

</details>

**Q44.** What is the difference between watershed development and river-basin management?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A watershed is a small upland catchment.** A river basin is the whole river from source to sea. PMKSY–WDC works at watershed scale. Interlinking and NWP talk at basin scale.

</details>

**Q45.** What is groundwater recharge?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Putting water back into the aquifer** through percolation, instead of only pumping it out. Atal Bhujal Yojana is the community-recharge scheme in stressed districts.

</details>

**Q46.** How do check dams, percolation tanks and recharge wells help?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They slow runoff, spread it, and let it sink.** Check dams sit on gullies. Percolation tanks are shallow ponds. Recharge wells inject into the aquifer.

</details>

**Q47.** What is micro-irrigation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Drip and sprinkler systems that give water near the root**, under PMKSY “more crop per drop.”

</details>

**Q48.** Differentiate drip irrigation and sprinkler irrigation.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Drip feeds each plant through emitters (orchards, vegetables, sugarcane). Sprinklers spray like rain (wheat, fodder, uneven land).** Both beat flood irrigation’s 30–40% efficiency.

</details>

**Q49.** Why can micro-irrigation improve water-use efficiency?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Less evaporation and seepage than flood irrigation**, so the same drop grows more crop.

</details>

**Q50.** What is interlinking of rivers, and what are its benefits and concerns?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Moving surplus-basin water to deficit basins by canals.** Claimed gains are irrigation, flood relief and navigation. Concerns are ecology, displacement, cost and interstate conflict. **Ken–Betwa** is the link under implementation (Bundelkhand, Madhya Pradesh–Uttar Pradesh).

</details>

## G. River-valley projects

**Q51.** What is a multipurpose river-valley project, and what are its purposes?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: One dam complex for irrigation, power, flood control, and often drinking water or navigation.** A **dam stores**. A **barrage** (Farakka) mainly **diverts**.

</details>

**Q52.** Match Bhakra–Nangal, Tehri, Hirakud, Sardar Sarovar, Nagarjuna Sagar, Damodar Valley and Tungabhadra with their rivers.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bhakra–Nangal = Sutlej (Gobind Sagar). Tehri = Bhagirathi. Hirakud = Mahanadi. Sardar Sarovar = Narmada (Gujarat). Nagarjuna Sagar = Krishna. DVC = Damodar (Tilaiya, Konar, Maithon, Panchet). Tungabhadra Dam = Tungabhadra.** Srisailam is also Krishna, not Tungabhadra. Pandoh is Beas.

</details>

**Q53.** Bhakra–Nangal, Tehri, Hirakud, Sardar Sarovar and Nagarjuna Sagar — river and State lock?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Sutlej (Himachal–Punjab). Bhagirathi (Uttarakhand, rock-fill). Mahanadi (Odisha). Narmada (Gujarat; Indira Sagar is Narmada in Madhya Pradesh). Krishna (Andhra–Telangana).**

</details>

**Q54.** Why are large dams debated, and what problems do they bring?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Displacement, siltation, downstream drying, seismic risk, and forest loss** sit against irrigation and power gains. Tehri and Sardar Sarovar are the social-debate icons. Koyna is the seismicity awareness lock.

</details>
