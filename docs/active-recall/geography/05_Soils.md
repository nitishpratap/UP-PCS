## A. Formation

**Q1.** What is soil, and what are the major factors of soil formation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Soil is the loose, living surface layer that can grow plants.** Jenny’s **CLORPT** set is climate, living organisms, relief, parent rock and time. Ancient Indian names: Urvara = fertile, Usara = sterile.

</details>

**Q2.** What is the difference between pedogenesis and weathering?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Weathering breaks rock into debris.** Pedogenesis turns that debris into a **profile** with organic matter, clays and horizons. Weathering is a step. Soil formation is the whole process.

</details>

**Q3.** How do parent rock, climate, relief, organisms and time influence soil?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Parent rock sets minerals (basalt → regur; granite → red). Climate sets leaching or salt. Relief sets thickness and erosion. Organisms add humus. Time matures horizons.** Residual soils sit on bedrock. Alluvium is transported.

</details>

**Q4.** What is a soil profile? Name the major horizons.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A vertical cut from surface to rock: O → A (topsoil) → E (eluviation out) → B (illuviation in) → C → R.** Young khadar has weak horizons. Mature residual soil shows clear bands. Edaphic means a **soil** factor, not climate.

</details>

**Q5.** What is the difference between soil texture and soil structure?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Texture is the sand–silt–clay mix.** Structure is how those particles clump (crumbs, blocks, plates). Sand drains. Clay holds. Loam is the farm lock.

</details>

**Q6.** Why is soil renewable but potentially exhaustible?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: New soil forms slowly. Erosion, usar and mining can strip it faster than nature rebuilds it.**

</details>

## B. Major groups

**Q7.** What are the major soil groups of India (ICAR eight)?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Alluvial, black/regur, red and yellow, laterite, arid, saline/usara, peaty, and forest/mountain.**

</details>

**Q8.** Which soil type covers the largest area of India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Alluvial**, about **40%**. Uttar Pradesh is about **90%** alluvial.

</details>

**Q9.** Which soil is mainly associated with the Deccan Trap region?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Black soil / regur.** It is **not** a Himalayan soil.

</details>

**Q10.** Which soil is strongly associated with river deposits?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Alluvial soil** of the Indo-Gangetic–Brahmaputra plains, east-coast deltas, and the Rajasthan corridor into Gujarat.

</details>

**Q11.** Which soil is particularly associated with high rainfall and intense leaching?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Laterite.** High heat plus heavy rain leach silica and lime. It is **rich in iron and aluminium** oxides, not poor in them.

</details>

**Q12.** Where are desert or arid soils mainly found?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Western Rajasthan (Thar).** Red-brown sandy soils, low nitrogen and humus. Phosphate is often normal. Kankar sits below.

</details>

**Q13.** Where are forest and mountain soils mainly found?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Himalaya and other hill slopes.** Thin on steep slopes. Valley loams are better. Snow-zone Himalayan soils are often acidic.

</details>

## C. Alluvial

**Q14.** What is the origin of alluvial soil, and where is it distributed?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is transported river alluvium**, extra-peninsular. Indo-Gangetic–Brahmaputra plains, east-coast deltas, and a Gujarat–Rajasthan corridor.

</details>

**Q15.** What is the difference between Bhangar and Khadar, and which is more fertile?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Khadar is new floodplain alluvium, finer and more fertile, renewed by floods.** Bhangar is older alluvium above the flood, coarser, with **more kankar**. Do not give kankar to Khadar. Bhur is sandy ridges in the west Gangetic belt.

</details>

**Q16.** What are the major nutrient traits of alluvial soil?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rich in potash and often lime. Poor in phosphorus. Low in nitrogen and humus** unless manured. Sand generally decreases west to east.

</details>

**Q17.** Which major crops sit on alluvial soil, and why does it matter in the Indo-Gangetic Plain?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rice, wheat, sugarcane, pulses, oilseeds and jute** (east). Flat, irrigable, flood-renewed Khadar plus canals and tubewells make it India’s grain core.

</details>

**Q18.** How do Bhabar and Tarai differ from Bhangar and Khadar?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bhabar is the pebbly belt south of the Shiwaliks where streams disappear.** Tarai is south of Bhabar where streams re-emerge, marshy and nitrogen-rich. They are foothill belts, not the Bhangar–Khadar pair.

</details>

## D. Black soil

**Q19.** What is black soil also called, and how is it tied to Deccan Trap?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Regur.** It is residual on **basaltic Deccan Trap**. Deep black sits on the upper Godavari–Krishna and north-west Deccan. Poor in nitrogen, phosphorus and organic matter.

</details>

**Q20.** What gives black soil its colour, and what are its major properties?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Titaniferous magnetite and organic compounds in basalt clay darken it.** Montmorillonite clay **shrinks and swells**, so it self-ploughs. It holds moisture. It is **not** peaty Kerala marsh.

</details>

**Q21.** Why is black soil suitable for cotton, and why does it crack when dry?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Moisture retention carries the crop through dry spells.** Shrink–swell clay opens **deep cracks** in the dry season, which also aerate the profile. Cotton is the crop lock. Jowar, wheat, linseed and citrus also sit here.

</details>

## E. Red, yellow, laterite, arid, mountain

**Q22.** Why is red soil red, and where are red and yellow soils found?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Iron oxides stain crystalline parent rock.** Yellow is the **hydrated** form. Belts: Deccan (outside deep trap), Western Ghat piedmont, Odisha–Chhattisgarh–Jharkhand, parts of Tamil Nadu and the north-east. Do not call every red soil laterite.

</details>

**Q23.** Is red soil naturally highly fertile? Which crops work with fertiliser?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is relatively poor**, especially in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus. With manure it grows millets, pulses, groundnut, potato, tobacco and some cotton.

</details>

**Q24.** What is laterisation, and why are laterite soils nutrient-poor yet rich in Fe/Al?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Hot, wet leaching removes silica and lime.** Iron and aluminium oxides stay, so the soil is **rich in Fe/Al** (bricks) but poor in plant food. Belts: Ghats, Rajmahal, Vindhya–Satpura–Malwa, north-east. Red laterite of Tamil Nadu–Andhra–Kerala suits **cashew**, tea and coffee with management.

</details>

**Q25.** What are arid or desert soils and forest or mountain soils?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Arid soils = western Rajasthan, sandy, low humus.** Mountain soils = thin on slopes, thicker in valleys, often acidic under snow forest. **Karewa** of Kashmir is lacustrine and grows saffron, almond and apple. Peaty soils of Kuttanad and Sundarbans are organic marsh, **not** regur.

</details>

## F. Problems and conservation

**Q26.** What is soil erosion, and what are the major types?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Removal of topsoil by water, wind or ice.** Water sequence: **sheet → rill → gully → ravine / badland**. Wind deflation = Thar. Coastal waves hit Kerala–Tamil Nadu–Odisha–West Bengal–Gujarat. Jhum accelerates it in the north-east and Ghats.

</details>

**Q27.** Differentiate sheet erosion, gully erosion and wind erosion.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Sheet is a thin uniform peel. Gully is channels too deep to plough. Wind lifts dry sand (deflation).** Ravines are extreme gullies.

</details>

**Q28.** What is ravine formation, and which region is famous for it?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Deep badland dissection.** The **Chambal** belt, including Agra–Etawah–Jalaun in Uttar Pradesh, is the exam ravine lock.

</details>

**Q29.** What human activities accelerate soil erosion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Deforestation, overgrazing, jhum, ploughing up and down the slope, and mining.**

</details>

**Q30.** What is soil conservation, and how do contour ploughing, terraces, strip cropping, shelter belts and afforestation help?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Keeping soil in place and fertile.** Contour ploughing follows the slope line. Terraces cut steep land. Strip cropping alternates cover. **Shelter belts stop wind.** Afforestation binds the slope. **Check dams** stop **gullies**. Do not swap shelter belt with check dam.

</details>

**Q31.** What is land degradation, and what is salinisation or alkalisation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Land degradation is a lasting fall in productive capacity.** Saline soils hold Na–K–Mg salts (**reh**, Rann of Kutch crust). Sodic/usar soils have exchangeable sodium and pH above 8.5. **Gypsum** for alkali. **Lime** for acid. Do not swap those two.

</details>

**Q32.** How can excessive irrigation degrade soil?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Waterlogging lifts salts into the root zone** in canal tracts of western–central Uttar Pradesh (usar, kallar, thur). Drain plus leach. Do not keep flooding without drainage.

</details>

## Map and rapid chain

**Q33.** Dominant soil: Indo-Gangetic Plain, Deccan Plateau, Rajasthan, high-rainfall Ghats, Odisha–Chhattisgarh–Jharkhand, Himalaya.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Alluvial. Black (regur) on trap, red on crystalline edges. Arid/desert. Laterite. Red and yellow, with laterite patches. Mountain/forest.** Uttar Pradesh Bundelkhand: Mar/Kabar = black; Parua/Rakar = red.

</details>

**Q34.** Complete the rapid locks.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Largest group = alluvial. Bhangar = old, kankar. Khadar = new, more fertile. Black parent = Deccan Trap basalt. Black crop = cotton. Red colour = iron oxide. Laterite process = leaching. Arid region = western Rajasthan. Sheet = thin peel. Gully = channels. Ravines = Chambal badlands. Salinisation = salts from waterlogging / arid evaporation.**

World Soil Day is **5 December**, not 5 June.

</details>
