## A. Basic characteristics

**Q1.** What type of climate does India have, and why is it called a tropical monsoon climate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A tropical monsoon climate** — most of India is tropical or subtropical, and winds reverse with the season. It is **not** wholly tropical. The Himalaya adds a cold-temperate belt.

</details>

**Q2.** What are the major factors controlling India’s climate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Latitude, the Himalaya, altitude, land–sea contrast, pressure and winds, distance from the sea, and relief** (Western Ghats, Aravalli). Jets, ENSO and IOD change year-to-year strength.

</details>

**Q3.** How do latitude, the Himalayas, altitude, pressure, winds, distance from the sea and relief influence Indian climate?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Latitude sets tropical heat.** The Himalaya blocks cold Central Asian winds and lifts monsoon rain. Altitude cools (lapse about 6.5 °C per 1000 m). Coasts stay moderate. The interior is continental. Western Ghats make Konkan wet and the Deccan dry.

</details>

**Q4.** Why does India experience a seasonal reversal of winds?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Summer thermal low over north-west India and a north-shifted ITCZ pull moist south-west winds.** Winter thermal high over the north-west sends dry north-east and north-west winds.

</details>

**Q5.** What is meant by the monsoon mechanism?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Land–sea heating, the northward shift of the ITCZ, and cross-equatorial flow.** The Himalaya helps. It is **not** the only cause.

</details>

**Q6.** Why is the Indian monsoon considered highly variable?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Onset, breaks, ENSO, IOD and jet position change year to year.** There is no one-to-one El Niño drought rule.

</details>

## B. Seasons

**Q7.** What are the four broad seasons recognised by the IMD?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Cold weather, hot weather, south-west monsoon, and retreating monsoon.**

</details>

**Q8.** What are the approximate months of each season?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Cold = December–February. Hot = March–May. South-west monsoon = June–September. Retreating = October–November.** North-east monsoon on the south-east coast runs October–December.

</details>

**Q9.** What are the major characteristics of the cold weather season?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Dry north-east winds, fog and frost on the plains, and Western Disturbance rain and snow in the north-west.** January is coldest in the north-west and Himalaya.

</details>

**Q10.** What is the role of Western Disturbances during winter?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Extra-tropical cyclones in the subtropical westerly jet** from the Mediterranean–Caspian–Atlantic region. They bring winter rain and snow. They are **not** the retreating monsoon.

</details>

**Q11.** Which regions receive winter rainfall from Western Disturbances?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Punjab, Haryana, Himachal, Jammu and Kashmir, Uttarakhand, western Uttar Pradesh, and northern Rajasthan.** Intensity **decreases west to east**.

</details>

**Q12.** Why is winter rainfall particularly important for rabi crops in north-west India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It waters rabi wheat.** It can also bring hail, fog and avalanche risk.

</details>

**Q13.** What are the major characteristics of the hot weather season?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: March–May heat, a building thermal low, Loo on the plains, and pre-monsoon storms.** June is hottest in north-west Rajasthan and Gujarat.

</details>

**Q14.** What are Loo, Kal Baisakhi / Nor’westers and Mango Showers, and where do they occur?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Loo is a hot dry dusty wind on the RJ–PB–HR–UP–Bihar plains in May–June. It is not a thunderstorm.** Kal Baisakhi is a violent April–May thunderstorm over West Bengal, Assam, Odisha, Bihar and Jharkhand. Mango showers are April–May rains in **Kerala and Karnataka**, not Punjab.

</details>

**Q15.** Why do pre-monsoon thunderstorms occur particularly over eastern and north-eastern India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Strong heating plus Bay moisture trigger Nor’westers.** They are **not** Western Disturbances.

</details>

## C. Southwest monsoon

**Q16.** What causes the onset of the south-west monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The ITCZ shifts north, a thermal low sits over north-west India, and moist cross-equatorial flow arrives.** IMD normal onset at Kerala is about **1 June**. Rajasthan is last, about **15 July**. Delhi is about **29 June**.

</details>

**Q17.** What is the significance of the ITCZ in the Indian monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is an equatorial low that shifts north over India in summer.** The monsoon trough is its continental arm. A south-stuck ITCZ means a weak monsoon. It is **not** a high.

</details>

**Q18.** How does differential heating of land and sea contribute to monsoon circulation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Land heats faster, so a summer low over north-west India pulls ocean air.** In winter the land cools and a high reverses the flow.

</details>

**Q19.** What is the role of the Tibetan Plateau in the summer monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Summer heating shifts the subtropical westerly jet north of Tibet.** The Tropical Easterly Jet then sets up near 14° N and supports the south-west monsoon.

</details>

**Q20.** What are the two major branches of the south-west monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Arabian Sea branch and the Bay of Bengal branch.** Together they give about 75–90% of India’s annual rain.

</details>

**Q21.** Trace the path of the Arabian Sea branch.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It strikes the Western Ghats.** Konkan and Malabar are wet. The **leeward Deccan is dry**. Tamil Nadu also lies in this rain shadow.

</details>

**Q22.** Trace the path of the Bay of Bengal branch.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It soaks north-east India first, then curves along the Himalaya into the Ganga plain, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.** Depressions often track west-north-west. The Tamil Nadu coast lies **parallel** to this branch, so it stays **dry in the south-west monsoon**.

</details>

**Q23.** Why does the monsoon arrive first in Kerala?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is the first Indian landfall of the Arabian Sea branch**, about 1 June. The front then moves north at roughly ten days per degree of latitude.

</details>

**Q24.** Why does rainfall decrease from east to west across the northern plains?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The Bay branch loses moisture as it moves inland.** Western Uttar Pradesh still gets more **winter** rain from Western Disturbances than eastern Uttar Pradesh.

</details>

**Q25.** Why does the western coast receive heavy monsoon rainfall?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Orographic lift of the Arabian Sea branch on the Western Ghats.** Konkan and Goa are the wet-wall class.

</details>

## D. Rainfall distribution

**Q26.** Why do Mawsynram and Cherrapunji receive exceptionally high rainfall?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Bay branch plus orography on the Khasi Hills.** They are the wettest class stations.

</details>

**Q27.** Why does the Deccan Plateau lie partly in a rain-shadow region?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It sits leeward of the Western Ghats** after the Arabian Sea branch has dropped its rain.

</details>

**Q28.** Why does Rajasthan receive very little rainfall?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It is far from both monsoon branches, leeward of the Aravalli, and under subtropical-high influence.** Jaisalmer is a driest-class station (under 25 cm).

</details>

**Q29.** Why does rainfall generally decrease from the Himalayan foothills towards western Rajasthan?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Moisture and orographic lift fade westward** from the Bay branch and the Himalayan foot toward the Thar.

</details>

**Q30.** Which areas of India receive very high, moderate, low and winter rainfall?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Very high = Khasi–Mawsynram, Western Ghats windward, north-east. Moderate = Ganga plain (about 100–150 cm). Low = Thar, leeward Deccan, Ladakh. Winter = north-west from Western Disturbances. Tamil Nadu’s big share is north-east monsoon, not all-India winter rain.**

</details>

**Q31.** Which side of the Western Ghats receives heavy rainfall, and which side is rain-shadow?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Windward west is wet. Leeward east (Deccan) is rain-shadow.**

</details>

**Q32.** What is the significance of the rain-shadow effect in peninsular India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: It makes the interior Deccan drier (Köppen Aw / BSh) than the Konkan–Malabar Am coast.** Tamil Nadu is also in the Arabian Sea branch’s rain shadow in summer.

</details>

## E. Monsoon dynamics

**Q33.** What is meant by a burst of monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The sudden heavy rain after May heat.** Temperature then falls.

</details>

**Q34.** What is a break in the monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A dry spell in the main season** when the trough leaves the Ganga plain.

</details>

**Q35.** Why do monsoon breaks occur?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The monsoon trough shifts onto the Himalaya.** Central India then dries. The foothills stay wet.

</details>

**Q36.** What is the monsoon trough?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A low-pressure axis along the Himalayan foot and the Ganga plain.** It is **not** a high.

</details>

**Q37.** How does the position of the monsoon trough affect rainfall in northern India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Over the Ganga plain = active rain. Over the Himalaya = break on the plains, wet foothills.**

</details>

**Q38.** What is the Somali Jet, and how is it related to the Indian monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A strong cross-equatorial low-level jet off Somalia** that feeds the south-west monsoon.

</details>

**Q39.** What is the Madden–Julian Oscillation, and how can it influence monsoon rainfall?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A 30–60 day tropical pulse of enhanced and suppressed convection.** A wet MJO phase over the Indian Ocean can favour an active spell. A dry phase can favour a break.

</details>

**Q40.** What is the role of El Niño and La Niña in Indian monsoon variability?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: El Niño (warm Peru) tends to weaken the monsoon and cuts Peruvian plankton.** La Niña tends to strengthen it. A **positive IOD can offset** El Niño. There is no automatic drought.

</details>

## F. Retreating / north-east monsoon

**Q41.** Why does the south-west monsoon begin to withdraw from India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Land cools after September, the thermal low fades, and the ITCZ shifts south.**

</details>

**Q42.** From which region does monsoon withdrawal generally begin?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: North-west India / Rajasthan in September.** Extreme south is last. Kerala onset was first. Withdrawal is roughly the reverse march.

</details>

**Q43.** What is the retreating monsoon season?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: September–November withdrawal, October heat, and a peak of Bay of Bengal cyclones.** Do not call Western Disturbances the retreating monsoon.

</details>

**Q44.** Why does Tamil Nadu receive a large proportion of its annual rainfall during the north-east / retreating monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: North-east winds pick up Bay moisture and strike the Coromandel coast.** About **half** of Tamil Nadu’s rain is this season. The south-west monsoon is dry there because the coast is parallel to the Bay branch and in the Arabian Sea rain shadow.

</details>

**Q45.** What is the role of Bay of Bengal cyclones during the retreating monsoon?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They peak in October–November** and can drench the east coast, including Tamil Nadu and Andhra.

</details>

**Q46.** Why heavy rainfall on the western coast?

<details>

**Ans: Orographic Arabian Sea branch on the Western Ghats.**

</details>

**Q47.** Why low rainfall in western Rajasthan?

<details>

**Q48.** Why exceptionally high rainfall in Meghalaya?

<details>

</details>

**Q49.** Why winter / retreating-monsoon rainfall in Tamil Nadu?

<details>

</details>

**Q50.** Why winter rainfall in Punjab–Haryana–western Uttar Pradesh?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Western Disturbances.** Not the north-east monsoon.

</details>

**Q51.** Why is the Deccan interior a rain-shadow?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Leeward of the Western Ghats.**

</details>

**Q52.** Why very high rainfall in north-eastern India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: First strike of the Bay of Bengal branch plus hill orography.**

</details>

## Ten-minute chain

**Q53.** Reproduce the season–monsoon chain.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans:**

Winter: Western Disturbances → north-west India → rabi rain.

Summer: Heating → low pressure → ITCZ north → south-west monsoon.

South-west monsoon: Arabian Sea branch + Bay of Bengal branch.

Monsoon: Onset → burst → active/break (trough) → withdrawal.

Retreating: Withdrawal → north-east winds → Bay cyclones → **Tamil Nadu rain**.

Trap: **TN dry in SW, wet in NE.** **WD ≠ retreating monsoon.** **Loo ≠ Kal Baisakhi.** **ITCZ / trough = low.**

</details>
