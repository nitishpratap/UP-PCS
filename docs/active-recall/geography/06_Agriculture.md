## A. Framework

**Q1.** What major factors influence Indian agriculture, and how do relief, soil, climate, water and technology shape patterns?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Monsoon, soil, relief, irrigation, seeds, labour and markets.** Plains and alluvium favour cereals. Black soil favours cotton. Hills favour tea. Irrigation lets rice leave the high-rain belt. HYV and machines raise intensity (GCA/NSA × 100).

</details>

**Q2.** Differentiate subsistence, commercial, plantation, intensive, extensive and mixed farming.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Subsistence feeds the family. Commercial sells a surplus. Plantation is a large estate of one perennial crop (tea, coffee, rubber, spices). Intensive uses much labour and inputs on small plots. Extensive uses large area and less labour per hectare. Mixed farming pairs crops with livestock.**

</details>

**Q3.** What is multiple cropping, and how does it differ from crop rotation?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Multiple cropping grows more than one crop on the same field in a year (kharif then rabi).** Rotation sequences different crops across years to rest nutrients. They can be used together.

</details>

## B. Seasons

**Q4.** What are India’s three cropping seasons, with sowing windows and flagship crops?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kharif June–October with the south-west monsoon: rice, maize, jowar, bajra, ragi, cotton, jute, groundnut, soy, tur.** Rabi October–March: wheat, barley, gram, mustard, peas, linseed (Western Disturbances help the north-west). Zaid March–June: melon, cucumber, fodder, vegetables. Cane is long-duration. Tea, coffee and rubber are perennial.

</details>

**Q5.** Why is kharif tied to the south-west monsoon? Which crops can span more than one season?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kharif needs monsoon heat and water at sowing.** Rice and maize can be extra-seasonal with irrigation (boro rice in Assam–West Bengal–Odisha). Wheat stays rabi. Cotton stays kharif.

</details>

## C. Food crops

**Q6.** What climate does rice need, where is it grown, and why can irrigation extend it?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: About 20–27 °C and often more than 100 cm of water, on clayey alluvium.** Belts: Ganga–Brahmaputra, east coast, Kerala, irrigated Punjab–Haryana–west UP. India is world **#2**. Aus, Aman and Boro are the Bengal seasons. Azolla is a rice biofertiliser. Golden rice is vitamin A. Assam is **not** a wheat belt.

</details>

**Q7.** What climate does wheat need, and why is it concentrated in northern and north-western India?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Cool growing weather, bright ripening, about 50–75 cm rain or irrigation, loam.** Two belts: Ganga–Satluj plains and Deccan black soil. Lead States: **Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana**. Rabi crop. India is world **#2**.

</details>

**Q8.** Why are millets climate-resilient, and how do jowar, bajra and ragi differ?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: They need less water and tolerate heat and poor soils.** Jowar’s lead State is **Maharashtra**. Bajra’s is **Rajasthan** (dry/semi-arid). Ragi’s is **Karnataka**. Ragi is **calcium-rich**. 2023 was the International Year of Millets (Shree Anna). Maize is mostly kharif food-and-feed.

</details>

## D. Cash and plantation crops

**Q9.** Cotton: climate, soil, season, regions, and why black soil?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kharif. Above 21 °C, 50–100 cm rain, about 210 frost-free days, black/regur moisture.** Maharashtra, Gujarat, Telangana. White gold. Ahmedabad is in the cotton region. The traditional mill centre is **Mumbai**.

</details>

**Q10.** Jute: climate, region, and why the Ganga–Brahmaputra delta?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 25–35 °C, more than 150 cm rain, alluvial floodplain, retting in slow water.** **West Bengal** is the fact. Uttar Pradesh–jute is **wrong**. Golden fibre. India is world **#1**.

</details>

**Q11.** Sugarcane: water need, climate, and Uttar Pradesh versus Maharashtra?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: 21–27 °C and 75–150 cm rain **or irrigation**. It is a long-duration, water-hungry crop.** Uttar Pradesh leads **quantity**. Maharashtra leads **productivity** and cooperatives. Price is **FRP** (plus SAP), **not** cereal MSP.

</details>

**Q12.** Tea, coffee and rubber: climate and regions.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Tea 20–30 °C, 150–300 cm, well-drained slopes: Assam, West Bengal (Darjeeling), Nilgiri.** Coffee 15–28 °C, 150–250 cm, shade: **Karnataka then Kerala then Tamil Nadu**. Rubber 25–35 °C, more than 200 cm: **Kerala**. Gujarat–tea is a trap.

</details>

**Q13.** What is plantation agriculture, and which Indian crops sit in that class?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: A large estate of one perennial crop, hired labour, processing on site.** Tea, coffee, rubber and many spices. They need humid tropics. Tea was the 2024 plantation tag.

</details>

## E. Irrigation

**Q14.** Why is irrigation essential, and how do canals, wells/tubewells and tanks compare?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: The monsoon is seasonal and variable.** Canals dominate the Indo-Gangetic command (Bhakra, Upper Ganga). Tubewells dominate Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh groundwater. Tanks suit the hard-rock peninsula (Tamil Nadu, interior Karnataka, Telangana).

</details>

**Q15.** What is micro-irrigation, and why drip versus sprinkler?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Drip and sprinkler under PMKSY “more crop per drop.”** Drip feeds the root (horticulture, cane). Sprinklers spray like rain (wheat, uneven land). Both beat flood irrigation.

</details>

## F. Green Revolution

**Q16.** What was the Green Revolution, when, which crops and which regions?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Mid-1960s package of HYV seed, irrigation and fertiliser.** Wheat and rice gained first. First phase: **Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh** (Trans-Gangetic wheat–rice). Lerma Rojo, Sonora 64, IR-8. Swaminathan in India. Borlaug’s Nobel is **Peace**.

</details>

**Q17.** What were the achievements, negatives, and regional disparities of the Green Revolution?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Grain self-sufficiency and buffer stocks.** Negatives: water mining, chemical load, soil fatigue, and a wheat–rice monoculture. Rain-fed east and peninsula lagged because they lacked irrigation and HYV fit. The **Evergreen / Second Green Revolution** means sustainable, eastern and rain-fed productivity (Swaminathan), not another Punjab-only wheat boom.

</details>

## G. Inputs, MSP, problems, sustainability

**Q18.** What are the major agricultural inputs? What is MSP, who recommends it, and is it a legal guarantee for every crop?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Seed, water, fertiliser, credit, power, machines, labour.** **CACP recommends MSP. The Cabinet decides.** About **22 mandated crops**. It is **not** a legally guaranteed price for every crop in every market. Cane uses **FRP**. NITI and RBI do **not** recommend MSP.

</details>

**Q19.** What is procurement, and how do MSP, procurement price and market price differ? How does PDS link to agriculture?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Procurement is FCI/State agencies buying at MSP (mainly rice and wheat) for the PDS.** Market price is what traders pay. If the market is above MSP, farmers sell outside. PDS moves procured grain to ration shops.

</details>

**Q20.** What are the major problems of Indian agriculture?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Monsoon dependence, small and **fragmented** holdings, disguised unemployment, uneven yields, post-harvest loss, weak storage and cold chains, and marketing gaps.** Fragmentation wastes time and blocks machines. Disguised unemployment means extra family labour with near-zero extra output.

</details>

**Q21.** Organic, natural, precision and climate-smart farming — plus diversification and agroforestry.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Organic avoids synthetics (Sikkim is the first fully organic State). Natural farming stresses on-farm inputs. Precision uses GIS/GPS for site-specific doses. Climate-smart farming seeks yield plus resilience plus lower emissions.** Diversification off paddy–cane eases Punjab–Haryana groundwater. Agroforestry mixes trees with crops for soil, fodder and income.

</details>

## Map and ten-minute chain

**Q22.** Map belts: rice, wheat, cotton, jute, sugarcane, tea, coffee, rubber — and why.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Rice = east and irrigated north-west (heat + water). Wheat = UP–PB–HR rabi loam. Cotton = MH–GJ–TG black soil. Jute = West Bengal humid delta. Cane = UP quantity, MH yield. Tea = Assam–Darjeeling–Nilgiri slopes. Coffee = Karnataka shade. Rubber = Kerala wet tropics.**

</details>

**Q23.** Complete the rapid chain.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: Kharif = monsoon sowings. Rabi = winter wheat–mustard–gram. Zaid = summer vegetables. Rice = hot and wet. Wheat = cool then bright. Cotton = black soil. Jute = Bengal delta. Tea = wet slopes. Coffee = shade, 15–28 °C. Cane = heavy water / FRP. Green Revolution = wheat and rice. MSP = CACP recommends, Cabinet decides. Groundwater = IG plains / west UP. Tanks = peninsula.**

India #1 in milk, pulses, jute. Often #2 in rice, wheat, cane.

</details>
