## Parliament — the frame (Art. 79–81)

- **Art. 79:** Parliament of the Union = the **President** + **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) + **Lok Sabha** (House of the People).
- Bicameral. President is an **integral part** of Parliament (assent, summons, address) even though he sits in neither House.
- **Art. 98:** each House has its own **Secretariat**, independent of the executive; **Secretary-General** is the administrative head.

### Special powers of Rajya Sabha (LS does **not** have these)

| Article | Power |
|---------|--------|

## Qualification & Disqualification (Arts. 84, 101–103)

### Qualification (Art. 84)
- Citizen of India; oath under **Third Schedule** (Art. 99).
- **25** (LS) / **30** (RS); other qualifications as prescribed by law (**RPA, 1951** — e.g. elector in any constituency for LS; for RS, elector in the State, except nominated).

### Vacation of seats (Art. 101)
- Dual membership: if elected to both Houses, must choose; otherwise both seats vacant as per law.
- **Resignation** to the Speaker / Chairman.
- **Absence for 60 days** without permission of the House → House **may** declare the seat vacant (Art. **101(4)**). The 60 days do **not** count periods when the House is prorogued or adjourned for more than 4 consecutive days.

### Disqualification (Art. 102) — five heads
1. Holds an **office of profit** under the Union/State (other than an office exempted by Parliament).
2. Of **unsound mind**, so declared by a competent court.
3. **Undischarged insolvent**.
4. Not a citizen / has voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship / acknowledges allegiance to a foreign State.
5. Disqualified by **any law** made by Parliament (**RPA 1951** — conviction ≥2 years, corrupt practices, etc.) **and** the **Tenth Schedule** (defection).

- **Decision:** President, **after consulting the Election Commission** — EC’s opinion is **binding** (Art. 103). (Defection → **Speaker / Chairman** decides — 10th Schedule; *Kihoto Hollohan*.)

### Office of Profit
- Not defined in the Constitution. Tests (SC): whether the government **appoints**, **pays**, and **controls** the holder, and whether the office could influence independence.
- Parliament may **exempt** offices by law — **Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1959**.
- Ministers’ offices are **not** offices of profit for this purpose (express exception in Art. 102).

> **Exam note:** Office-of-profit disqualification is decided by the **President + EC** (Art. 103). **Defection** is decided by the **Speaker/Chairman**. Do not mix the two forums.

## Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Pro Tem Speaker

| Office | Lock |
|--------|------|
| **Speaker (Art. 93)** | Elected by LS from among its members. Remains in office **even after dissolution** until the **first meeting of the new LS**. |
| **Removal (Art. 94)** | Resolution with **14 days’ notice**, passed by a **majority of all the then members** of LS (effective majority). Speaker **cannot preside** while the resolution is under consideration. |
| **Casting vote** | Votes only to **break a tie** (not in the first instance). |
| **Powers** | Decides **Money Bill** (Art. 110 — Go 2); presides over **joint sitting** (Art. 108); 10th Schedule defection; maintains order; adjourn/suspend sitting. |
| **Deputy Speaker** | Also elected from among LS members; performs Speaker’s duties in his absence. |
| **Panel of Chairpersons** | Nominated by the Speaker; preside when both Speaker and Deputy are absent — **not** a constitutional office. |

**Pro Tem Speaker**
- Appointed by the **President** (usually the **senior-most** member — convention, not a constitutional rule).
- Administers oath to new members and **conducts the election of the Speaker**. Office ends once the Speaker is elected.

## Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

- The **Vice-President is the *ex-officio* Chairman** (Art. 64, 89). He is **not a member** of RS.
- Votes only a **casting vote** (except when the resolution is for his own removal — then he cannot preside).
- When the VP acts as / discharges the functions of the **President**, he **does not** perform Chairman’s duties; the **Deputy Chairman** presides.
- **Deputy Chairman** is a **member**; removed under Art. **90** by a majority of **all the then members** of RS, after 14 days’ notice.

## Sessions — Summoning, Prorogation, Adjournment, Dissolution (Art. 85)

| Device | Who | What it ends |
|--------|-----|----------------|
| **Summon** | **President** | Starts a session. Maximum gap between two sessions: **6 months** (so at least **two sessions a year**). |
| **Adjournment** | **Presiding officer** | Ends a **sitting** (hours / days). Business pending **does not lapse**. |
| **Adjournment *sine die*** | Presiding officer | Ends a sitting **indefinitely** (no date fixed). |
| **Prorogation** | **President** | Ends a **session**. Pending notices (questions, motions) lapse; **Bills do not lapse** merely by prorogation. |
| **Dissolution** | **President** | Ends the **life of the Lok Sabha** only. RS cannot be dissolved. |

**Lapsing on dissolution of LS** *(Bills fully in Go 2 — one lock here)*  
- A Bill pending in LS **lapses**. A Bill passed by LS and pending in RS **lapses**. A Bill pending in RS **not passed by LS does not lapse**. A Bill passed by both Houses and pending assent **does not lapse**.

**Lame Duck Session**  
Last session of the **outgoing** Lok Sabha **after** a new Lok Sabha has been elected (members who will not return are the “lame ducks”).

> **Exam note:** Adjournment ≠ prorogation ≠ dissolution. Only the **President** prorogues/dissolves; only the **presiding officer** adjourns.

## Quorum (Art. 100)

- **Quorum** to constitute a meeting of either House = **one-tenth (1/10)** of the **total number of members** of that House.
- If at any time there is no quorum, the presiding officer must **adjourn** the House or **suspend** the meeting until there is a quorum.
- Decisions: unless the Constitution provides otherwise, by a **majority of members present and voting**. The Speaker/Chairman does **not** vote in the first instance; **casting vote** on equality.

---

## Joint Sitting (Art. 108)

**When** (deadlock on an **Ordinary Bill**):
1. The other House **rejects** the Bill, **or**
2. The Houses finally **disagree** on amendments, **or**
3. More than **six months** elapse from receipt of the Bill by the other House without it being passed (period when a House is prorogued/adjourned >4 days does not count).

The **President** summons the joint sitting if the Bill has not already lapsed by dissolution.

| Point | Lock |
|-------|------|
| Article | **108** — **not 109** (109 = Money Bill special procedure → Go 2) |
| Which Bills | **Ordinary Bills only** |
| **Never** | **Money Bill**, **Appropriation Bill**, **Constitutional Amendment Bill** (Art. 368 requires each House separately) |
| Who presides | **Speaker**; if absent, **Deputy Speaker** of LS; if both absent, **Deputy Chairman** of RS. The **Chairman (VP) does not preside**. |
| Majority | Simple majority of members **present and voting** of the two Houses as one body |
| Quorum | **1/10** of the **total membership of both Houses** |

**Joint sittings actually held (3):** Dowry Prohibition Bill **1961**; Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill **1978**; POTA Bill **2002**.

## Parliamentary Privileges (Art. 105 / States: 194)

| Privilege | Lock |
|-----------|------|
| **Freedom of speech** | In Parliament; subject to the Constitution and the rules of the House. **No court** for words spoken or votes given (Art. 105(2)). |
| **Publication** | True reports of proceedings are protected (44th clarified this after *Searchlight*). |
| **Codification** | Originally = House of Commons until defined by Parliament. **44th Amendment:** privileges shall be those existing on **20 June 1979** until defined by law. Parliament has **not** comprehensively defined them. |
| **Breach / contempt** | Each House is the **guardian** of its privileges; can reprimand, admonish, imprison (till the end of the session). |
| **Art. 122** | Courts shall not inquire into the validity of parliamentary proceedings on the ground of irregularity of procedure. (*Raja Ram Pal* — judicial review is not entirely barred in extreme cases.) |

Individual privileges (freedom from arrest in civil cases 40 days before/after a session; exemption from jury service) vs **collective** privileges (right to publish debates, exclude strangers, punish contempt).

## Whip

- **Extra-constitutional** party office. Issues instructions on how to vote / attend.
- Defiance of a whip can attract **Tenth Schedule** disqualification (except in a few exempted votes).
- Chief Whip of the ruling party is typically a **Minister of Parliamentary Affairs**.

---

## Parliamentary Secretariat (Art. 98)

- Separate secretariats for LS and RS. Recruited independently; **Speaker / Chairman** controls.
- **Secretary-General** of each House — chief advisor on procedure; remains in office across dissolutions of LS.

## Mains link (one frame)

- **Why a second chamber that cannot be dissolved?** RS is meant to represent **States** and provide **continuity** + a cooling chamber. Its **special powers (249, 312)** are the constitutional compensation for **not** being the confidence chamber — a lock that also explains why Money Bills and no-confidence stay with the Lok Sabha (Go 2).
