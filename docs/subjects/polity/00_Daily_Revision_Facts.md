1. The **Constitution of India** is the lengthiest written constitution in the world; originally adopted on **26 November 1949** with **395 Articles, 22 Parts, and 8 Schedules**, it currently comprises **25 Parts and 12 Schedules** (with about 470 Articles in numerical continuity).
2. Under **Article 1**, India is described as a **'Union of States'** rather than a federation, signifying that the Indian federation is not the result of an agreement among States and that no State has the right to secede from the Union.
3. The **Preamble** declares India to be a **'Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic'**; the terms **'Socialist'**, **'Secular'**, and **'Integrity'** were added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee.
4. The Preamble secures to all citizens: **Justice** (Social, Economic, and Political — 3 types); **Liberty** (Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith, and Worship — 5 types); **Equality** (Status and Opportunity — 2 types); and promotes **Fraternity** assuring individual dignity and national unity/integrity.
5. In ***Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)***, a 13-judge bench ruled (7:6) that the Preamble is an integral **part of the Constitution** (overruling the 1960 *Berubari Union* opinion) and formulated the **Basic Structure Doctrine**.
6. Major Constitutional Borrowings: **UK** provided Parliamentary Government, Rule of Law (Art. 14), Single Citizenship, Cabinet system, Prerogative Writs, Bicameralism, and the office of the Speaker.
7. **USA** provided **Fundamental Rights** (Part III), Independence of Judiciary, **Judicial Review** (Art. 13), **Impeachment of the President** (Art. 61), Removal of SC/HC Judges, and the office of the Vice-President.
8. **Ireland** provided the **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)** (Part IV), the method of **Presidential Election** (PR-STV), and nomination of **12 members to Rajya Sabha** by the President.
9. **Canada** provided a Federation with a **strong Centre**, vesting of **Residuary Powers in the Centre** (Art. 248), appointment of State Governors by the Centre, and the **Advisory Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court** (Art. 143).
10. **Australia** provided the **Concurrent List** (7th Schedule), Freedom of Trade, Commerce and Intercourse (Part XIII), and the mechanism of **Joint Sitting of Parliament** (Art. 108).
11. **Germany (Weimar Constitution)** provided the provision for **suspension of Fundamental Rights during National Emergency** (Art. 359). Note: The emergency framework itself is largely drawn from the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
12. **USSR (Russia)** provided **Fundamental Duties** (Art. 51A, Part IVA) and the ideals of Social, Economic, and Political Justice in the Preamble; **France** provided the Republic and the ideals of Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity.
13. **South Africa** provided the procedure for **Amendment of the Constitution** (Art. 368) and the election of members of the Rajya Sabha by elected MLAs.
14. **Schedules Master Summary (TEARS OF OLD PM):** 1st (28 States & 8 UTs); 2nd (Salaries of President, Governors, Judges, CAG — *Ministers are NOT in 2nd Sch*); 3rd (Forms of Oaths — *President, VP, and Governors take oath under Arts. 60, 69, 159, NOT 3rd Sch*); 4th (Rajya Sabha seats: UP leads with 31 seats).
15. **Schedules 5 & 6:** The **5th Schedule** governs Scheduled Areas in 10 States; the **6th Schedule** provides Autonomous District Councils in **4 North-Eastern States: AMTM** (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram; *Manipur is NOT included*).
16. **Schedules 7 to 12:** 7th (Union: 100, State: 61, Concurrent: 52 entries); 8th (22 Languages); 9th (Validation of Acts, added by 1st Amd 1951; post-1973 laws subject to judicial review per *Coelho case*); 10th (Anti-Defection Law, 52nd Amd 1985); 11th (Panchayats: **29 subjects**, 73rd Amd 1992); 12th (Municipalities: **18 subjects**, 74th Amd 1992).
17. **Eighth Schedule Language Additions:** Originally 14 languages; **Sindhi** added by 21st Amd (1967); **Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali** (KMN) added by 71st Amd (1992); **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** (BDMS) added by 92nd Amd (2003). *English and Bhojpuri are NOT in the 8th Schedule*.
18. **Citizenship (Articles 5–11, Part II):** India has **single citizenship**; the Citizenship Act of **1955** provides 5 modes of acquisition (Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation) and 3 modes of loss (Renunciation, Termination, Deprivation).
19. **Fundamental Rights exclusively for Citizens:** Articles **15, 16, 19, 29, and 30** are available ONLY to Indian citizens; all other Fundamental Rights (Arts. 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 25, 26, 27, 28) apply to both citizens and foreigners (except enemy aliens).
20. **Article 12 & 13:** Article 12 defines 'State' for Part III purposes (including statutory and non-statutory authorities like LIC, ONGC, SAIL); Article 13 establishes **Judicial Review**, voiding any law violative of Fundamental Rights (*Doctrines of Severability and Eclipse*).
21. **Right to Equality (Articles 14–18):** Art. 14 (Equality before Law — UK negative concept; Equal Protection of Laws — US positive concept); Art. 15 (No discrimination *only* on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth; 15(4) backward classes, 15(6) 10% EWS); Art. 16 (Equal opportunity in public employment); Art. 17 (Abolition of Untouchability — absolute right); Art. 18 (Abolition of Titles).
22. **Article 19 (Six Democratic Freedoms):** 19(1)(a) Speech & Expression; 19(1)(b) Peaceful assembly without arms; 19(1)(c) Associations/Unions/Cooperatives; 19(1)(d) Free movement throughout India; 19(1)(e) Reside and settle; 19(1)(g) Trade/Occupation. The right to property under 19(1)(f) was repealed by the **44th Amendment, 1978**.
23. **Article 20 (Three Criminal Safeguards):** Protection against **ex-post-facto criminal laws** (20(1)), protection against **Double Jeopardy** (20(2)), and protection against **Self-Incrimination** (20(3) — applies only to accused in criminal proceedings).
24. **Article 21 & 21A:** Article 21 guarantees protection of life and personal liberty; expanded in ***Maneka Gandhi (1978)*** to require 'Due Process of Law' (just, fair, and reasonable procedure) and in ***Puttaswamy (2017)*** to include the **Right to Privacy**. **Article 21A** (Right to Education ages 6–14) was added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**.
25. **Non-Suspendable Rights:** Under **Article 359** (as amended by 44th Amd 1978), the enforcement of **Articles 20 and 21 CANNOT BE SUSPENDED** even during a National Emergency.
26. **Prerogative Writs (Art. 32 vs Art. 226):** Dr. Ambedkar described **Article 32** as the 'Heart and Soul' of the Constitution. The Supreme Court issues writs only for Fundamental Rights, whereas the High Court under **Article 226** can issue writs for both Fundamental Rights and ordinary legal rights (making Art. 226 broader in scope).
27. **The Five Writs:** **Habeas Corpus** ('To have the body of', against both public and private entities); **Mandamus** ('We Command', against public officials for statutory duties; *cannot issue against President or Governor*); **Prohibition** (preventive against lower courts); **Certiorari** (quashes illegal orders of judicial/quasi-judicial/administrative bodies); **Quo-Warranto** ('By what authority', challenges illegal tenure in substantive public office).
28. **Key Directive Principles (Part IV):** Art. 39A (Equal Justice & Free Legal Aid — 42nd Amd); Art. 40 (Village Panchayats); Art. 43B (Cooperatives — 97th Amd); Art. 44 (Uniform Civil Code); Art. 45 (Early childhood care up to age 6 — 86th Amd); Art. 48A (Environment & Wildlife — 42nd Amd); Art. 50 (Separation of Judiciary from Executive).
29. **Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Art. 51A):** Added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**; non-justiciable; originally 10 duties; the **11th duty** (education of child aged 6–14 by parent/guardian) was added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**.
30. **Presidential Election (Articles 54 & 55):** Electoral College consists of: (1) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament; (2) Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies; (3) Elected members of Delhi and Puducherry Assemblies (70th Amd 1992). *Nominated MPs/MLAs and ALL Legislative Council members (MLCs) DO NOT VOTE*.
31. **Vice-Presidential Election (Article 66):** Electoral College consists of **ALL members of both Houses of Parliament (both Elected and Nominated)**. State MLAs and MLCs have NO role in the Vice-President's election.
32. **Impeachment of President (Article 61):** Quasi-judicial process initiated in either House on grounds of 'Violation of the Constitution'; requires 14 days' written notice signed by 1/4th members; must be passed by a **special majority of not less than 2/3rds of the TOTAL MEMBERSHIP** of each House.
33. **Pardoning Power (Art. 72 vs Art. 161):** The **President (Art. 72)** can grant pardons in all cases including **Death Sentences** and **Court-Martial (military)** sentences; the **Governor (Art. 161)** has no power to pardon death sentences or court-martial punishments.
34. **Ordinance Promulgation (Art. 123 vs Art. 213):** The President (Art. 123) and Governor (Art. 213) can issue ordinances only when the legislature is in recess; an ordinance ceases to operate **6 weeks** after the reassembly of the legislature (maximum duration is 6 months + 6 weeks).
35. **Council of Ministers Size (91st Amendment, 2003):** Total number of Ministers including the Prime Minister (or Chief Minister in States) shall not exceed **15% of the total strength** of the Lok Sabha (or State Legislative Assembly); in States, the minimum number of ministers cannot be less than **12** (Art. 164(1A)).
36. **Joint Sitting of Parliament (Article 108):** Summoned by the President to resolve deadlocks on Ordinary or Financial Bills; presided over by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha** (or Deputy Speaker / Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha; *Chairman of Rajya Sabha NEVER presides*). *NO joint sitting is permissible for Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills*.
37. **Money Bill (Article 110):** Introduced only in Lok Sabha with prior presidential recommendation; the Speaker's decision that a bill is a Money Bill is final; Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend it, and must return it within **14 days**.
38. **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG, Article 148):** Appointed by the President; tenure is **6 years or up to 65 years of age**; removed on the same grounds and manner as a Supreme Court Judge; audits accounts of Union and States; reports submitted to PAC.
39. **Finance Commission (Article 280):** Quasi-judicial body constituted by the President every 5th year; consists of a **Chairman and 4 other members**; recommends devolution of net tax proceeds between Union and States.
40. **Election Commission of India (Article 324):** Consists of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and Election Commissioners; CEC is removed in the same manner and grounds as a Supreme Court Judge. *Elections to Panchayats and Municipalities are conducted by the State Election Commission (Arts. 243K & 243ZA), NOT by the ECI*.
41. **All-India Services (Article 312):** The **Rajya Sabha** has the exclusive constitutional power to pass a resolution by a **2/3rd majority of members present and voting** declaring it necessary in the national interest to create one or more new All-India Services.
42. **Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII):** **Art. 352** (National Emergency: War, External Aggression, Armed Rebellion — approved within 1 month by special majority); **Art. 356** (President's Rule: failure of constitutional machinery — approved within 2 months by simple majority; max 3 years); **Art. 360** (Financial Emergency — never imposed).
43. **Constitutional Amendment (Article 368, Part XX):** Requires Special Majority (majority of total membership + 2/3rd of members present and voting); federal provisions require additional ratification by legislatures of not less than half the States; the President **must give assent** to an amendment bill (24th Amd 1971).
44. **Warrant of Precedence (1979 Notification):** 1. President → 2. Vice-President → 3. Prime Minister → 4. Governors (within their States) → 5. Former Presidents → 6. CJI and Lok Sabha Speaker (Equal Rank) → 7. Union Cabinet Ministers, Chief Ministers (within States), Leaders of Opposition → 7A. Bharat Ratna awardees → 9. Supreme Court Judges → 9A. CEC, CAG, UPSC Chairman → 11. Attorney General for India, Cabinet Secretary.
45. **Uttar Pradesh Political Structure:** Vidhan Sabha has **403 seats** (84 reserved for SC, 2 for ST); Vidhan Parishad has **100 seats**; Lok Sabha representation is **80 seats** (17 SC, 0 ST); Rajya Sabha representation is **31 seats** (highest in the country).

## 4. Warrant of Precedence (Order of Precedence, 1979 Notification)

*Frequently tested in arrange-order questions in UPPCS/UKPCS:*

1. **Rank 1**: **President of India**
2. **Rank 2**: **Vice-President of India**
3. **Rank 3**: **Prime Minister of India**
4. **Rank 4**: **Governors of States** (within their respective States)
5. **Rank 5**: **Former Presidents**
   - *Rank 5A*: **Deputy Prime Minister** (if any)
6. **Rank 6**: **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** and **Speaker of Lok Sabha** (*Both have equal rank!*)
7. **Rank 7**: **Cabinet Ministers of the Union**, **Chief Ministers of States** (within respective states), Deputy Chairman of NITI Aayog (former Planning Commission), **Leaders of Opposition in Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha**
   - *Rank 7A*: **Holders of Bharat Ratna**
8. **Rank 8**: Ambassadors Extraordinary & Plenipotentiary, Chief Ministers (outside their respective states), Governors (outside their respective states)
9. **Rank 9**: **Judges of the Supreme Court of India**
   - *Rank 9A*: **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)**, **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)**, Chairperson of UPSC
10. **Rank 10**: Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha, Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha, Deputy Chief Ministers, Ministers of State of the Union
11. **Rank 11**: **Attorney General for India**, Cabinet Secretary, Lieutenant Governors (within their UTs).

## 5. Parliamentary Terms, Motions & Working Procedure

| Parliamentary Term | Core Meaning & Procedural Rules |
|---|---|
| **Question Hour** | First hour of every parliamentary sitting (11:00 AM to 12:00 PM):  <br>• **Starred Questions**: Oral answers required; **supplementary questions CAN be asked** (green paper).  <br>• **Unstarred Questions**: Written answers required; **NO supplementary questions allowed** (white paper).  <br>• **Short Notice Questions**: Relates to matter of urgent public importance; asked with less than 10 days notice; answered orally (light pink paper). |
| **Zero Hour** | **Unique Indian parliamentary innovation (introduced in 1962)**; starts immediately after Question Hour (12:00 PM); members raise urgent matters without prior notice; **not mentioned in Rules of Procedure**. |
| **Adjournment Motion** | Introduced in **LOK SABHA ONLY** to draw attention to a definite matter of urgent public importance; requires support of **at least 50 members** to be admitted; sitting adjourned for debate ≥ 2.5 hours; involves censure of government. |
| **No-Confidence Motion** | Moved in **LOK SABHA ONLY** under Rule 198; requires support of **at least 50 members**; no specific grounds needed; if passed, Council of Ministers must resign. |
| **Censure Motion** | Moved in Lok Sabha against specific ministers or entire council for specific policies; requires specific grounds; if passed, government is not obligated to resign immediately but must seek confidence. |
| **Calling Attention Motion** | Indian parliamentary innovation (since 1954); calling attention of a Minister to a matter of urgent public importance and seeking an authoritative statement. Mentioned in Rules of Procedure. |
| **Cut Motions** | Moved during Budget debate (Lok Sabha only):  <br>1. **Disapproval of Policy Cut**: Amount reduced to **Re 1** (shows complete policy rejection).  <br>2. **Economy Cut**: Amount reduced by a specific lump sum.  <br>3. **Token Cut**: Amount reduced by **Rs 100** to express specific grievance. |
| **Guillotine** | When time allotted for discussion on demands for grants concludes, all remaining undiscussed demands are put to vote immediately without debate. |
| **Lame-Duck Session** | Refers to the last session of the existing Lok Sabha after a new Lok Sabha has been elected. Those existing MPs who failed to get re-elected are called "Lame Ducks". |
| **Quorum** | Minimum number of members required to be present to conduct a sitting: **1/10th of total membership** of the House (55 in Lok Sabha, 25 in Rajya Sabha) per **Article 100(3)**. |

## 6. Parliamentary Committees (Must-Memorize Facts)

## 8. High-Frequency Articles Must-Revise Daily

### A. Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts. 12–35)
- **Art. 12**: Definition of "State" (includes Union/State Governments, Parliament/Legislatures, Local authorities, other statutory authorities like LIC, ONGC).
- **Art. 13**: Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights shall be void (*Doctrine of Judicial Review*, *Doctrine of Severability*, *Doctrine of Eclipse*).
- **Art. 14**: Equality before law (British origin, negative concept) & Equal protection of the laws (American origin, positive concept).
- **Art. 15**: Prohibition of discrimination **ONLY** on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth.
  - *Art. 15(4)*: Special provisions for SC/ST/OBC (1st Amd 1951).
  - *Art. 15(6)*: 10% EWS reservation in educational institutions (103rd Amd 2019).
- **Art. 16**: Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment.
  - *Art. 16(4)*: Reservation for backward classes not adequately represented.
  - *Art. 16(6)*: 10% EWS reservation in public employment (103rd Amd 2019).
- **Art. 17**: **Abolition of Untouchability** (Absolute right; enforced via Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955).
- **Art. 18**: Abolition of titles (Bharat Ratna/Padma awards are decorations, not titles — *Balaji Raghavan Case 1996*).
- **Art. 19**: Protection of **Six Democratic Freedoms**:
  1. *19(1)(a)*: Freedom of speech and expression (includes freedom of press, right to remain silent).
  2. *19(1)(b)*: Freedom to assemble peaceably and without arms.
  3. *19(1)(c)*: Freedom to form associations, unions, or co-operative societies (97th Amd).
  4. *19(1)(d)*: Freedom to move freely throughout the territory of India.
  5. *19(1)(e)*: Freedom to reside and settle in any part of the territory of India.
  6. *19(1)(g)*: Freedom to practice any profession, trade, or business.
  - *(Note: 19(1)(f) Right to acquire, hold, and dispose of property was REPEALED by 44th Amd 1978).*
- **Art. 20**: Protection in respect of conviction for offences:
  - *20(1)*: No ex-post-facto criminal law.
  - *20(2)*: No double jeopardy (cannot be prosecuted twice for the same offence).
  - *20(3)*: No self-incrimination (cannot be compelled to be a witness against oneself).
- **Art. 21**: **Protection of Life and Personal Liberty** (*Procedure Established by Law → Due Process of Law* via *Maneka Gandhi Case 1978*; encompasses Right to Privacy - *Puttaswamy 2017*, Right to clean environment, Right to livelihood, Right to sleep, Right to die with dignity / passive euthanasia - *Common Cause 2018*).
- **Art. 21A**: **Right to Free and Compulsory Education** for children aged 6 to 14 years (Inserted by **86th Amendment 2002**; RTE Act enacted in 2009, in force 1 April 2010).
- **Art. 22**: Protection against arrest and detention (production before magistrate within 24 hours; preventive detention max 3 months without advisory board approval).
- **Art. 23**: Prohibition of traffic in human beings and **Begar** (forced labour).
- **Art. 24**: Prohibition of employment of children **below 14 years in factories, mines**, or hazardous employment.
- **Arts. 25–28**: Freedom of Religion:
  - *Art. 25*: Freedom of conscience, free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.
  - *Art. 26*: Freedom to manage religious affairs.
  - *Art. 27*: Freedom from payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion.
  - *Art. 28*: Freedom from attendance at religious instruction in state-funded educational institutions.
- **Arts. 29–30**: Cultural and Educational Rights of Minorities:
  - *Art. 29*: Protection of language, script, and culture of minorities.
  - *Art. 30*: Right of religious and linguistic minorities to establish and administer educational institutions.
- **Art. 32**: **Right to Constitutional Remedies**: Power of Supreme Court to issue 5 Writs (**Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto**); Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the **"Heart and Soul of the Constitution"**.

> **NON-SUSPENDABLE RIGHTS:** Under **Article 359 (as amended by 44th Amd 1978)**, the President **CANNOT suspend the enforcement of Articles 20 and 21** even during a National Emergency!

### B. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP, Part IV, Arts. 36–51)
- **Art. 38**: State to secure a social order for the promotion of welfare of the people (minimize inequalities).
- **Art. 39(b) & (c)**: Equitable distribution of material resources of the community to subserve common good (39b); prevention of concentration of wealth (39c) — *Articles 39(b) and 39(c) take precedence over Articles 14 and 19 per Art. 31C!*
- **Art. 39A**: **Equal justice and free legal aid** (Added by 42nd Amd 1976; Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 / NALSA).
- **Art. 40**: **Organization of Village Panchayats** (Gandhian principle).
- **Art. 41**: Right to work, to education, and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness.
- **Art. 42**: Provision for just and humane conditions of work and **Maternity relief**.
- **Art. 43**: Living wage and decent standard of life for workers.
- **Art. 43A**: **Participation of workers in management of industries** (Added by 42nd Amd 1976).
- **Art. 43B**: Promotion of **Co-operative societies** (Added by 97th Amd 2011).
- **Art. 44**: **Uniform Civil Code (UCC)** for all citizens (**Goa** had Portuguese civil code; **Uttarakhand became the first state in post-independence India to pass a UCC bill in 2024!**).
- **Art. 45**: Provision for early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of **6 years** (Amended by 86th Amd 2002).
- **Art. 48**: Organization of agriculture and animal husbandry; prohibiting slaughter of cows, calves, and milch cattle.
- **Art. 48A**: **Protection and improvement of environment, and safeguarding of forests and wildlife** (Added by 42nd Amd 1976).
- **Art. 50**: **Separation of Judiciary from the Executive** in the public services of the State.
- **Art. 51**: Promotion of international peace and security.

## 12. Landmark Supreme Court Judgments (Chronological Table)

| Case Name & Year | Key Issue / Doctrine Established |
|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad Case (1951)** | Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights under Article 368. |
| **Golaknath Case (1967)** | 11-Judge bench; ruled that **Fundamental Rights are transcendental and sacrosanct**; Parliament CANNOT amend or take away Fundamental Rights. |
| **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)** | **13-Judge bench (largest ever, 7:6 verdict on 24 April 1973)**; upheld Parliament's power to amend the Constitution, BUT established the **"Basic Structure Doctrine"** — Parliament cannot alter the basic framework/features of the Constitution. |
| **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)** | Struck down 39th Amendment (Article 329A); held that **Free and Fair Elections and Judicial Review are part of the Basic Structure**. |
| **Maneka Gandhi Case (1978)** | Reinterpreted Article 21; replaced rigid "Procedure Established by Law" with **"Due Process of Law"**; procedure must be *just, fair, and reasonable*. Golden Triangle: Articles 14, 19, and 21 are interrelated. |
| **Minerva Mills Case (1980)** | Struck down clauses of 42nd Amendment; held that **limited amending power and judicial review are basic features**; Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of **balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs**. |
| **Waman Rao Case (1981)** | Reaffirmed Basic Structure Doctrine; set cutoff date of **24 April 1973** for applying Basic Structure scrutiny to the 9th Schedule. |
| **Shah Bano Case (1985)** | Upheld divorced Muslim woman's right to maintenance under Section 125 CrPC; urged enactment of a Uniform Civil Code. |
| **Indira Sawhney Case (1992)** (*Mandal Case*) | 9-Judge bench; upheld 27% OBC reservation; introduced **"Creamy Layer"** exclusion; capped **total reservations at 50%**; no reservation in promotions. |
| **S.R. Bommai Case (1994)** | 9-Judge bench; curbed misuse of **Article 356 (President's Rule)**; held that **Secularism is part of the Basic Structure**; floor test in Assembly is the only valid test of majority. |
| **Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)** | Formulated **Vishaka Guidelines** to prevent sexual harassment of women at workplaces (precursor to POSH Act 2013). |
| **I.R. Coelho Case (2007)** | 9-Judge bench; held that all laws inserted into the **9th Schedule after 24 April 1973 are subject to judicial review** if they violate Fundamental Rights forming part of the Basic Structure. |
| **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy Case (2017)** | 9-Judge bench unanimously held that the **Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right** intrinsically protected under Article 21. |
| **Navtej Singh Johar Case (2018)** | Decriminalized consensual homosexual acts between adults by reading down **Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)**. |
| **Jarnail Singh Case (2018)** | Held that the **"Creamy Layer" principle applies to SC/ST** when extending reservation in promotions. |
| **Janki Janak v. UOI (2022)** | Upheld the constitutional validity of the **103rd Constitutional Amendment (10% EWS reservation)** by 3:2 majority. |

## 13. State Reorganization Chronology (Post-Independence India)

1. **1953**: **Andhra State** created (first linguistic state, separated Telugu-speaking areas of Madras after 56-day fast unto death of **Potti Sreeramulu**; Kurnool capital).
2. **1956**: **States Reorganisation Act 1956** (Fazal Ali Commission with H.N. Kunzru and K.M. Panikkar); created **14 States and 6 Union Territories**.
3. **1960**: **Bilingual Bombay State bifurcated into Maharashtra and Gujarat** (Gujarat became the 15th state).
4. **1961**: **Dadra & Nagar Haveli** (10th Amd) and **Goa, Daman & Diu** (12th Amd) integrated via Operation Vijay.
5. **1962**: **Pondicherry** (Puducherry) integrated via 14th Amd (ceded by French in 1954).
6. **1963**: **Nagaland** created as 16th state (incorporating Naga Hills-Tuensang Area).
7. **1966**: **Punjab bifurcated** into Punjab (Punjabi) and **Haryana** (17th state, Hindi) on recommendation of **Shah Commission**; **Chandigarh** made common UT.
8. **1971**: **Himachal Pradesh** elevated from UT to 18th State.
9. **1972**: North-Eastern reorganization: **Manipur (19th), Tripura (20th), and Meghalaya (21st)** became full states; Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh created as UTs.

## 15. The Five Types of Writs (Articles 32 & 226)

*Power to issue prerogative writs: Supreme Court (**Article 32** — only for Fundamental Rights) and High Courts (**Article 226** — for Fundamental Rights AND ordinary legal rights; HC writ jurisdiction is wider than SC!)*

| Writ Name | Literal Latin Meaning | Purpose & Grounds for Issue | Against Whom Issued | Exceptions (Cannot be Issued Against) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** (बन्दी प्रत्यक्षीकरण) | **"To have the body of"** | Remedy against **unlawful detention or illegal custody**; court orders the detaining authority to produce the detained person before it to examine legality of detention | **Both Public Authorities and Private Individuals** | 1. Detention is lawful  <br>2. Proceeding is for contempt of court/legislature  <br>3. Detention is by competent court  <br>4. Outside court's territorial jurisdiction |
| **Mandamus** (परमादेश) | **"We Command"** | Order issued to a public official/body directing them to **perform an official public duty** which they have failed or refused to perform | **Public officials, Public Corporations, Inferior Courts, Tribunals, Government** | 1. **Private individuals or private bodies**  <br>2. Discretionary duties (not mandatory)  <br>3. To enforce private contracts  <br>4. **President of India or State Governors** (Art. 361)  <br>5. Chief Justice of HC acting in judicial capacity |
| **Prohibition** (प्रतिषेध) | **"To Forbid"** | Issued by a higher court to a lower court/tribunal to **prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction** or usurping a jurisdiction not vested in it; preventive in nature | **Judicial and Quasi-Judicial Authorities only** | Administrative authorities, legislative bodies, and private individuals/bodies |
| **Certiorari** (उत्प्रेषण) | **"To be Certified" / "To be Informed"** | Issued by a higher court to a lower court/tribunal to **quash an order already passed** in excess of jurisdiction, lack of jurisdiction, or violation of natural justice; both **preventive and curative** | **Judicial, Quasi-Judicial, and Administrative Authorities** (SC expanded to administrative bodies in 1991) | Legislative bodies and private individuals/bodies |
| **Quo-Warranto** (अधिकार-पृच्छा) | **"By What Authority or Warrant"** | Issued to enquire into the **legality of the claim of a person to a public office**; prevents illegal usurpation of a substantive public office | **Substantive Public Offices of a permanent nature created by statute/Constitution** | Ministerial offices (PM/Minister), private offices.  <br>*(Can be sought by ANY interested person, not necessarily the aggrieved party!)* |
