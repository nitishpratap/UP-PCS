## 1. Parts of the Indian Constitution (All 25 Parts Cheat-Sheet)

*Originally 22 Parts in 1950; currently 25 Parts (counting sub-parts: IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA; Part VII repealed).*

| Part | Roman | Subject Matter Covered | Article Span | Key Prelims Takeaway |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Part I** | **I** | **The Union and its Territory** | **Arts. 1–4** | Art. 1: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"; Art. 3: Parliament alters state boundaries/names by simple majority |
| **Part II** | **II** | **Citizenship** | **Arts. 5–11** | Single citizenship (borrowed from UK); Citizenship Act 1955 |
| **Part III** | **III** | **Fundamental Rights** | **Arts. 12–35** | **"Magna Carta of India"**; borrowed from US Bill of Rights; justifiable |
| **Part IV** | **IV** | **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)** | **Arts. 36–51** | Borrowed from Ireland; non-justiciable; fundamental in the governance of the country |
| **Part IVA** | **IV-A** | **Fundamental Duties** | **Art. 51A** | Added by **42nd Amendment 1976** (Swaran Singh Committee); 10 originally, 11th added by **86th Amendment 2002**; non-justiciable |
| **Part V** | **V** | **The Union Government** | **Arts. 52–151** | Ch. 1: Executive (52–78), Ch. 2: Parliament (79–122), Ch. 3: President's Legislative Power (123), Ch. 4: Union Judiciary (124–147), Ch. 5: CAG (148–151) |
| **Part VI** | **VI** | **The State Governments** | **Arts. 152–237** | Governor (153–162), State Legislature (168–212), Governor's Ordinance (213), High Courts (214–232), Subordinate Courts (233–237) |
| *Part VII* | *VII* | *States in Part B of First Schedule* | *Art. 238* | **REPEALED** by **7th Constitutional Amendment Act 1956** |
| **Part VIII** | **VIII** | **The Union Territories** | **Arts. 239–242** | Administration of UTs by President through Administrator / Lieutenant Governor; Art. 239AA: Special status to NCT of Delhi (69th Amd 1991) |
| **Part IX** | **IX** | **The Panchayats** | **Arts. 243 to 243-O** | Inserted by **73rd Amendment 1992**; 3-tier Panchayati Raj; 11th Schedule (29 functional items) |
| **Part IXA** | **IX-A** | **The Municipalities** | **Arts. 243P to 243ZG** | Inserted by **74th Amendment 1992**; Urban Local Bodies; 12th Schedule (18 functional items) |
| **Part IXB** | **IX-B** | **The Co-operative Societies** | **Arts. 243ZH to 243ZT** | Inserted by **97th Amendment 2011** (partially struck down in 2021 for state cooperatives, holds for multi-state) |
| **Part X** | **X** | **The Scheduled and Tribal Areas** | **Arts. 244–244A** | 5th & 6th Schedules administration |
| **Part XI** | **XI** | **Relations between the Union and the States** | **Arts. 245–263** | Ch. 1: Legislative Relations (245–255), Ch. 2: Administrative Relations (256–263); Art. 263: Inter-State Council |
| **Part XII** | **XII** | **Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits** | **Arts. 264–300A** | Consolidated Fund (266), Contingency Fund (267), GST Council (279A), Finance Commission (280); **Art. 300A**: Right to Property (Legal/Constitutional right, NOT fundamental right!) |
| **Part XIII** | **XIII** | **Trade, Commerce and Intercourse within India** | **Arts. 301–307** | Freedom of inter-state trade and commerce |
| **Part XIV** | **XIV** | **Services under the Union and the States** | **Arts. 308–323** | All India Services (Art. 312 created by Rajya Sabha); UPSC & State PSCs (Arts. 315–323) |
| **Part XIVA** | **XIV-A** | **Tribunals** | **Arts. 323A–323B** | Added by **42nd Amendment 1976** (Swaran Singh Committee); 323A: Administrative Tribunals (CAT/SAT), 323B: Other Tribunals |
| **Part XV** | **XV** | **Elections** | **Arts. 324–329A** | Art. 324: Election Commission of India; Art. 326: Universal Adult Suffrage |
| **Part XVI** | **XVI** | **Special Provisions Relating to Certain Classes** | **Arts. 330–342A** | Reservation of seats in Lok Sabha & State Assemblies (SC: 330/332; ST: 330/332); NCSC (338), NCST (338A), NCBC (338B) |
| **Part XVII** | **XVII** | **Official Language** | **Arts. 343–351** | Ch. 1: Official Language of Union (343 - Hindi in Devanagari script), Ch. 2: Regional Languages (345), Ch. 3: Judiciary (348), Ch. 4: Special Directives (350A: Primary instruction in mother tongue, 350B: Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, 351: Promotion of Hindi) |
| **Part XVIII** | **XVIII** | **Emergency Provisions** | **Arts. 352–360** | Art. 352: National Emergency, Art. 356: President's Rule (State Emergency), Art. 360: Financial Emergency |
| **Part XIX** | **XIX** | **Miscellaneous** | **Arts. 361–367** | Art. 361: Immunity of President and Governors from criminal prosecution |
| **Part XX** | **XX** | **Amendment of the Constitution** | **Art. 368** | Constituent power of Parliament to amend the Constitution (borrowed from South Africa) |
| **Part XXI** | **XXI** | **Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions** | **Arts. 369–392** | Special provisions for states: **Art. 371 (Maha & Guj), 371A (Nagaland), 371B (Assam), 371C (Manipur), 371D & E (Andhra/Telangana), 371F (Sikkim), 371G (Mizoram), 371H (Arunachal), 371-I (Goa), 371J (Karnataka - Hyderabad-Karnataka region)** |
| **Part XXII** | **XXII** | **Short Title, Commencement & Repeals** | **Arts. 393–395** | Short title: "Constitution of India" |

## 2. All 12 Schedules (The Master Mnemonic: TEARS OF OLD PM)

| Schedule | Mnemonic Letter | Subject Matter | Key Details / High-Yield Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **T** | **Territories** | Names of the 28 States and 8 Union Territories and their territorial extents |
| **2nd** | **E** | **Emoluments & Salaries** | Salaries, allowances, and pensions of: President, Governors, Lok Sabha Speaker/Deputy Speaker, Rajya Sabha Chairman/Deputy Chairman, SC/HC Judges, CAG. **PM and Ministers are NOT in the 2nd Schedule!** |
| **3rd** | **A** | **Affirmations & Oaths** | Forms of Oaths for: Union/State Ministers, MPs/MLAs, SC/HC Judges, CAG. **President (Art. 60), Vice-President (Art. 69), and Governors (Art. 159) have oaths under separate articles, NOT in the 3rd Schedule!** |
| **4th** | **R** | **Rajya Sabha Seat Allocation** | Allocation of seats in the Council of States to States and UTs. **Uttar Pradesh has the highest: 31 seats; Uttarakhand has 3 seats.** |
| **5th** | **S** | **Scheduled Areas & Tribes** | Administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in 10 states (excluding the 4 states of the 6th Schedule); Tribes Advisory Council |
| **6th** | **O** | **Other Tribal Areas** | Administration of Tribal Areas in **4 North-Eastern States: AMTM = Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram** (Autonomous District Councils; **Manipur is NOT in the 6th Schedule!**) |
| **7th** | **F** | **Federal Lists (Division of Powers)** | Three legislative lists:  <br>1. **Union List**: Originally 97, now **100 subjects**  <br>2. **State List**: Originally 66, now **61 subjects**  <br>3. **Concurrent List**: Originally 47, now **52 subjects** (Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Wild animals & birds, Administration of justice transferred by 42nd Amd) |
| **8th** | **O** | **Official Languages** | **22 recognized languages** (Originally 14):  <br>• 1967 (21st Amd): Added **Sindhi**  <br>• 1992 (71st Amd): Added **Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali** (KMN)  <br>• 2003 (92nd Amd): Added **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** (BDMS)  <br>**Traps: English and Bhojpuri are NOT in the 8th Schedule!** |
| **9th** | **L** | **Land Reforms & Validation** | Added by **1st Amendment 1951** to protect land reform laws from judicial review; **I.R. Coelho Case (2007)**: SC ruled laws placed in 9th Schedule post **24 April 1973** (Kesavananda judgment date) are **open to judicial review** |
| **10th** | **D** | **Defection (Anti-Defection Law)** | Added by **52nd Amendment 1985**; disqualification on ground of defection; decided by Presiding Officer; subject to judicial review (**Kihoto Hollohan 1992**); split exemption of 1/3 deleted by 91st Amd 2003; merger requires **2/3rd members** |
| **11th** | **P** | **Panchayati Raj** | Added by **73rd Amendment 1992**; specifies **29 functional subjects** within the purview of Panchayats (Article 243G) |
| **12th** | **M** | **Municipalities** | Added by **74th Amendment 1992**; specifies **18 functional subjects** within the purview of Municipalities (Article 243W) |

## 3. High-Frequency Articles Must-Revise Daily

### A. Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts. 12–35)
- **Art. 12**: Definition of "State" (includes Union/State Governments, Parliament/Legislatures, Local authorities, other statutory authorities like LIC, ONGC).
- **Art. 13**: Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights shall be void (*Doctrine of Judicial Review*, *Doctrine of Severability*, *Doctrine of Eclipse*).
- **Art. 14**: Equality before law (British origin, negative concept) & Equal protection of the laws (American origin, positive concept).
- **Art. 15**: Prohibition of discrimination **ONLY** on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth.
  - *Art. 15(4)*: Special provisions for SC/ST/OBC (1st Amd 1951).
  - *Art. 15(6)*: 10% EWS reservation in educational institutions (103rd Amd 2019).
- **Art. 16**: Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment.
  - *Art. 16(4)*: Reservation for backward classes not adequately represented.
  - *Art. 16(6)*: 10% EWS reservation in public employment (103rd Amd 2019).
- **Art. 17**: **Abolition of Untouchability** (Absolute right; enforced via Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955).
- **Art. 18**: Abolition of titles (Bharat Ratna/Padma awards are decorations, not titles — *Balaji Raghavan Case 1996*).
- **Art. 19**: Protection of **Six Democratic Freedoms**:
  1. *19(1)(a)*: Freedom of speech and expression (includes freedom of press, right to remain silent).
  2. *19(1)(b)*: Freedom to assemble peaceably and without arms.
  3. *19(1)(c)*: Freedom to form associations, unions, or co-operative societies (97th Amd).
  4. *19(1)(d)*: Freedom to move freely throughout the territory of India.
  5. *19(1)(e)*: Freedom to reside and settle in any part of the territory of India.
  6. *19(1)(g)*: Freedom to practice any profession, trade, or business.
  - *(Note: 19(1)(f) Right to acquire, hold, and dispose of property was REPEALED by 44th Amd 1978).*
- **Art. 20**: Protection in respect of conviction for offences:
  - *20(1)*: No ex-post-facto criminal law.
  - *20(2)*: No double jeopardy (cannot be prosecuted twice for the same offence).
  - *20(3)*: No self-incrimination (cannot be compelled to be a witness against oneself).
- **Art. 21**: **Protection of Life and Personal Liberty** (*Procedure Established by Law $\to$ Due Process of Law* via *Maneka Gandhi Case 1978*; encompasses Right to Privacy - *Puttaswamy 2017*, Right to clean environment, Right to livelihood, Right to sleep, Right to die with dignity / passive euthanasia - *Common Cause 2018*).
- **Art. 21A**: **Right to Free and Compulsory Education** for children aged 6 to 14 years (Inserted by **86th Amendment 2002**; RTE Act enacted in 2009, in force 1 April 2010).
- **Art. 22**: Protection against arrest and detention (production before magistrate within 24 hours; preventive detention max 3 months without advisory board approval).
- **Art. 23**: Prohibition of traffic in human beings and **Begar** (forced labour).
- **Art. 24**: Prohibition of employment of children **below 14 years in factories, mines**, or hazardous employment.
- **Arts. 25–28**: Freedom of Religion:
  - *Art. 25*: Freedom of conscience, free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.
  - *Art. 26*: Freedom to manage religious affairs.
  - *Art. 27*: Freedom from payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion.
  - *Art. 28*: Freedom from attendance at religious instruction in state-funded educational institutions.
- **Arts. 29–30**: Cultural and Educational Rights of Minorities:
  - *Art. 29*: Protection of language, script, and culture of minorities.
  - *Art. 30*: Right of religious and linguistic minorities to establish and administer educational institutions.
- **Art. 32**: **Right to Constitutional Remedies**: Power of Supreme Court to issue 5 Writs (**Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto**); Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the **"Heart and Soul of the Constitution"**.

> **NON-SUSPENDABLE RIGHTS:** Under **Article 359 (as amended by 44th Amd 1978)**, the President **CANNOT suspend the enforcement of Articles 20 and 21** even during a National Emergency!

### B. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP, Part IV, Arts. 36–51)
- **Art. 38**: State to secure a social order for the promotion of welfare of the people (minimize inequalities).
- **Art. 39(b) & (c)**: Equitable distribution of material resources of the community to subserve common good (39b); prevention of concentration of wealth (39c) — *Articles 39(b) and 39(c) take precedence over Articles 14 and 19 per Art. 31C!*
- **Art. 39A**: **Equal justice and free legal aid** (Added by 42nd Amd 1976; Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 / NALSA).
- **Art. 40**: **Organization of Village Panchayats** (Gandhian principle).
- **Art. 41**: Right to work, to education, and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness.
- **Art. 42**: Provision for just and humane conditions of work and **Maternity relief**.
- **Art. 43**: Living wage and decent standard of life for workers.
- **Art. 43A**: **Participation of workers in management of industries** (Added by 42nd Amd 1976).
- **Art. 43B**: Promotion of **Co-operative societies** (Added by 97th Amd 2011).
- **Art. 44**: **Uniform Civil Code (UCC)** for all citizens (**Goa** had Portuguese civil code; **Uttarakhand became the first state in post-independence India to pass a UCC bill in 2024!**).
- **Art. 45**: Provision for early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of **6 years** (Amended by 86th Amd 2002).
- **Art. 48**: Organization of agriculture and animal husbandry; prohibiting slaughter of cows, calves, and milch cattle.
- **Art. 48A**: **Protection and improvement of environment, and safeguarding of forests and wildlife** (Added by 42nd Amd 1976).
- **Art. 50**: **Separation of Judiciary from the Executive** in the public services of the State.
- **Art. 51**: Promotion of international peace and security.

### C. Union & State Executive / Legislature Comparisons
| Constitutional Matter | Union Article (President / Parliament) | State Article (Governor / State Legislature) | Key Distinction / Comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Office of Head** | **Art. 52**: President of India | **Art. 153**: Governor of a State | 7th Amd 1956: Same person can be appointed Governor for two or more states |
| **Executive Power** | **Art. 53**: Vested in President | **Art. 154**: Vested in Governor | Governor is appointed by President (Canadian model); holds office during pleasure |
| **Pardoning Power** | **Art. 72**: President of India | **Art. 161**: Governor of a State | **President alone can pardon Death Sentences and Court Martial (Military) sentences.** Governor can suspend/remit, but cannot grant full pardon for death sentence |
| **Council of Ministers** | **Art. 74**: CoM to aid & advise | **Art. 163**: CoM to aid & advise | Governor has broader constitutional discretionary powers (Art. 163(2)) |
| **Appointment of PM/CM** | **Art. 75**: PM appointed by President | **Art. 164**: CM appointed by Governor | CoM size capped at **max 15%** of Lower House membership (91st Amd 2003); State min = 12 |
| **Top Legal Officer** | **Art. 76**: **Attorney General for India** | **Art. 165**: **Advocate General for the State** | AG has right to speak in both Houses of Parliament but **NO right to vote** (Art. 88) |
| **Joint Sitting** | **Art. 108**: Joint Sitting of Parliament | **NO PROVISION** for Joint Sitting in State Legislature! | Summoned by President; presided over by **Lok Sabha Speaker** (simple majority) |
| **Money Bill Definition** | **Art. 110**: Money Bill in Parliament | **Art. 199**: Money Bill in State Assembly | Speaker's decision is final; Rajya Sabha / Legislative Council has only **14 days** to suggest |
| **Annual Budget** | **Art. 112**: Annual Financial Statement | **Art. 202**: Annual Financial Statement | Word "Budget" is nowhere used in the Constitution! |
| **Ordinance Power** | **Art. 123**: President | **Art. 213**: Governor | Can only be promulgated when legislature is not in session; max lifespan: **6 weeks from reassembly** (absolute maximum lifespan = 6 months + 6 weeks) |
| **Assent to Bills** | **Art. 111**: President's Assent | **Art. 200**: Governor's Assent | Governor can reserve bill for consideration of President (**Art. 201**) |
| **Creation/Abolition of Council** | — | **Art. 169**: Legislative Council | State Assembly passes resolution by special majority $\to$ Parliament approves by simple majority |

## 4. Landmark Constitutional Amendments (The Complete PCS Sheet)

| Amendment Act & Year | Prime Minister / Context | Landmark Provisions / Changes Made |
|---|---|---|
| **1st (1951)** | J.L. Nehru | Added **9th Schedule** to protect land reform laws from judicial review; placed reasonable restrictions on freedom of speech (public order, friendly relations with foreign states, incitement to an offence) |
| **7th (1956)** | J.L. Nehru | Implemented **States Reorganisation Act 1956** (Fazal Ali Commission); abolished Part A, B, C, D classification; created 14 States & 6 UTs; allowed common High Court for two or more states; allowed same person as Governor of multiple states |
| **24th (1971)** | Indira Gandhi | Affirmed Parliament's power to amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights; made it **obligatory for the President to give assent** to a Constitutional Amendment Bill |
| **42nd (1976)** | Indira Gandhi (**Swaran Singh Committee**) | Known as the **"Mini-Constitution"**:  <br>1. Preamble: Added words **"SOCIALIST, SECULAR, INTEGRITY"**  <br>2. Added **Part IVA (Fundamental Duties, Art. 51A)** with 10 duties  <br>3. Added **Part XIVA (Tribunals, Arts. 323A–323B)**  <br>4. Added DPSPs: Arts. **39A** (Free legal aid), **43A** (Workers' participation), **48A** (Environment)  <br>5. Transferred **5 subjects from State List to Concurrent List**: Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Protection of Wild Animals & Birds, Administration of Justice |
| **44th (1978)** | Morarji Desai (Janata Party) | Undid emergency excesses of 42nd Amendment:  <br>1. Replaced "Internal Disturbance" with **"Armed Rebellion"** in Art. 352  <br>2. Mandated that President can declare National Emergency only on **written advice of the Cabinet**  <br>3. **Right to Property deleted from Fundamental Rights** (Art. 19(1)(f) & Art. 31 deleted; made a legal right under **Art. 300A**)  <br>4. Provided that **Articles 20 and 21 CANNOT be suspended** during emergency |
| **52nd (1985)** | Rajiv Gandhi | Added **10th Schedule (Anti-Defection Law)**; disqualified MPs/MLAs defecting from their political party |
| **61st (1988)** | Rajiv Gandhi | **Lowered voting age from 21 years to 18 years** for Lok Sabha and Legislative Assembly elections (amended **Art. 326**) |
| **69th (1991)** | P.V. Narasimha Rao | Conferred special status on Union Territory of Delhi as **National Capital Territory of Delhi (NCT)** with Legislative Assembly and 7-member Council of Ministers (inserted **Art. 239AA**) |
| **73rd (1992)** | P.V. Narasimha Rao | Accorded constitutional status to **Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)**; inserted **Part IX (Arts. 243 to 243-O)** and **11th Schedule (29 subjects)**; 33% mandatory reservation for women |
| **74th (1992)** | P.V. Narasimha Rao | Accorded constitutional status to **Urban Local Bodies (Municipalities)**; inserted **Part IXA (Arts. 243P to 243ZG)** and **12th Schedule (18 subjects)** |
| **86th (2002)** | A.B. Vajpayee | 1. Inserted **Article 21A** (Right to free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 as a Fundamental Right)  <br>2. Substituted Article 45 (Early childhood care)  <br>3. Added **11th Fundamental Duty** under **Art. 51A(k)** |
| **91st (2003)** | A.B. Vajpayee | 1. Capped the size of the Council of Ministers to **maximum 15%** of the total strength of Lok Sabha / State Legislative Assembly (minimum 12 in states)  <br>2. **Deleted 1/3rd split provision** from 10th Schedule (only 2/3rd merger protected) |
| **99th (2014)** | Narendra Modi | Established National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC); **struck down as unconstitutional by Supreme Court in 2015** (*Fourth Judges Case*), restoring the Collegium system |
| **101st (2016)** | Narendra Modi | Introduced **Goods and Services Tax (GST)**; inserted **Art. 246A, 269A, and 279A** (GST Council chaired by Union Finance Minister); in force from **1 July 2017** |
| **102nd (2018)** | Narendra Modi | Accorded constitutional status to the **National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)**; inserted **Articles 338B and 342A** |
| **103rd (2019)** | Narendra Modi | Provided **10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)** in educational admissions and civil posts; amended **Articles 15(6) and 16(6)** |
| **104th (2020)** | Narendra Modi | Extended reservation of seats for **SC and ST in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies for another 10 years (until 2030)**; **discontinued the nomination of 2 Anglo-Indian members** in Lok Sabha and 1 in Assemblies |
| **105th (2021)** | Narendra Modi | Clarified that State Governments maintain the power to prepare and maintain their own list of **Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs / OBCs)** |
| **106th (2023)** | Narendra Modi | ***Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam***: Provides **33% reservation for women in Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies, and NCT of Delhi Assembly** (inserted Arts. 330A, 332A, 239AA(2)(b)); effective after the next delimitation exercise |

## 5. Constitutional vs Statutory vs Executive Bodies

| Body Name | Category | Governing Article / Parent Act | Head / Composition | Appointed By | Removal / Tenure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Election Commission of India (ECI)** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 324** | Chief Election Commissioner + 2 Election Commissioners | President (per 2023 Act: panel of PM, LoP, Cabinet Min) | Same grounds and manner as Supreme Court Judge (6 yrs or 65 yrs) |
| **Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)** | **Constitutional** | **Arts. 315–323** | Chairman + Members (usually 9–11) | President | 6 yrs or 65 yrs; removed by President on SC enquiry under Art. 317 |
| **Finance Commission** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 280** | Chairman + 4 Members | President every 5th year | Recommends devolution of net tax proceeds between Union & States |
| **Comptroller & Auditor General (CAG)** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 148** | Single-member office | President | 6 yrs or 65 yrs; removed like a Supreme Court Judge; **"Guardian of the Public Purse"** |
| **Attorney General for India** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 76** | Single-member office | President | Holds office **during the pleasure of the President**; must be qualified to be an SC Judge |
| **Advocate General for the State** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 165** | Single-member office | Governor | Holds office **during the pleasure of the Governor**; qualified to be an HC Judge |
| **National Commission for SC (NCSC)** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 338** | Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson + 3 Members | President | 3 years tenure |
| **National Commission for ST (NCST)** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 338A** | Separated by 89th Amd 2003; Chairperson + 4 | President | 3 years tenure |
| **National Commission for BC (NCBC)** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 338B** | Accorded constitutional status by 102nd Amd 2018 | President | 3 years tenure |
| **GST Council** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 279A** | Chaired by Union Finance Minister; members: MoS Finance + State Finance Ministers | - | Voting weight: Centre = 1/3rd, States = 2/3rd; 3/4th majority needed to pass decisions |
| **Inter-State Council** | **Constitutional** | **Art. 263** | Set up in 1990 (**Sarkaria Commission**); chaired by Prime Minister | - | CMs of all states + 6 Union Cabinet Ministers |
| **National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)** | **Statutory** | **Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA) 1993** | Retired CJI or Judge of SC (Chairperson) + Members | President on panel (PM, Speaker, Home Min, LoP LS, LoP RS, Dy Chairman RS) | 3 years or 70 years |
| **Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)** | **Statutory** | **CVC Act 2003** (set up 1964 on **Santhanam Committee**) | Central Vigilance Commissioner + max 2 Vigilance Commissioners | President on panel (PM, Home Minister, LoP LS) | **4 years or 65 years** (Unique tenure!) |
| **Central Information Commission (CIC)** | **Statutory** | **Right to Information (RTI) Act 2005** | Chief Information Commissioner + max 10 ICs | President on panel (PM, LoP LS, Union Cabinet Min) | Set by Central Govt or 65 yrs |
| **Lokpal of India** | **Statutory** | **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013** | Chairperson (CJI/SC Judge/eminent person) + max 8 Members | President on panel (PM, Speaker LS, LoP LS, CJI or nominee, eminent jurist) | 5 years or 70 years |
| **National Green Tribunal (NGT)** | **Statutory** | **NGT Act 2010** | Chairperson (retired SC judge or HC CJ) + Judicial/Expert members | Central Govt in consultation with CJI | 5 years; not eligible for reappointment |
| **NITI Aayog** | **Non-Constitutional (Executive Body)** | Created by **Union Cabinet Resolution on 1 Jan 2015** (replacing Planning Commission 1950) | Chairperson: Prime Minister; Vice-Chairperson + CEO + Ex-officio members | PM appoints | Think tank; promotes Cooperative & Competitive Federalism |

## 6. Panchayati Raj Committees Chronological Ladder

| Committee Name | Year | Landmark Recommendation / Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** | **1957** | Recommended **Three-Tier Panchayati Raj System**:  <br>1. Gram Panchayat (Village)  <br>2. Panchayat Samiti (Block - executive body)  <br>3. Zilla Parishad (District - advisory body)  <br>**First implemented in Nagaur (Rajasthan) on 2 October 1959** by J.L. Nehru; second state was **Andhra Pradesh** |
| **K. Santhanam Committee** | **1963** | Panchayati Raj finances and budgetary powers |
| **Ashok Mehta Committee** | **1977** (Janata Govt) | Recommended **Two-Tier System**:  <br>1. **Mandal Panchayat** (group of villages, pop. 15,000–20,000)  <br>2. **Zilla Parishad** (District executive body)  <br>Advocated official participation of political parties in elections |
| **G.V.K. Rao Committee** | **1985** (Planning Commission) | Critiqued bureaucratic takeover, describing PRIs as **"Grass without Roots"**; recommended strengthening Zilla Parishad and creating post of District Development Commissioner (DDC) |

## 7. Landmark Supreme Court Judgments (Chronological Table)

| Case Name & Year | Key Issue / Doctrine Established |
|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad Case (1951)** | Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights under Article 368. |
| **Golaknath Case (1967)** | 11-Judge bench; ruled that **Fundamental Rights are transcendental and sacrosanct**; Parliament CANNOT amend or take away Fundamental Rights. |
| **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)** | **13-Judge bench (largest ever, 7:6 verdict on 24 April 1973)**; upheld Parliament's power to amend the Constitution, BUT established the **"Basic Structure Doctrine"** — Parliament cannot alter the basic framework/features of the Constitution. |
| **Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)** | Struck down 39th Amendment (Article 329A); held that **Free and Fair Elections and Judicial Review are part of the Basic Structure**. |
| **Maneka Gandhi Case (1978)** | Reinterpreted Article 21; replaced rigid "Procedure Established by Law" with **"Due Process of Law"**; procedure must be *just, fair, and reasonable*. Golden Triangle: Articles 14, 19, and 21 are interrelated. |
| **Minerva Mills Case (1980)** | Struck down clauses of 42nd Amendment; held that **limited amending power and judicial review are basic features**; Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of **balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs**. |
| **Waman Rao Case (1981)** | Reaffirmed Basic Structure Doctrine; set cutoff date of **24 April 1973** for applying Basic Structure scrutiny to the 9th Schedule. |
| **Shah Bano Case (1985)** | Upheld divorced Muslim woman's right to maintenance under Section 125 CrPC; urged enactment of a Uniform Civil Code. |
| **Indira Sawhney Case (1992)** (*Mandal Case*) | 9-Judge bench; upheld 27% OBC reservation; introduced **"Creamy Layer"** exclusion; capped **total reservations at 50%**; no reservation in promotions. |
| **S.R. Bommai Case (1994)** | 9-Judge bench; curbed misuse of **Article 356 (President's Rule)**; held that **Secularism is part of the Basic Structure**; floor test in Assembly is the only valid test of majority. |
| **Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)** | Formulated **Vishaka Guidelines** to prevent sexual harassment of women at workplaces (precursor to POSH Act 2013). |
| **I.R. Coelho Case (2007)** | 9-Judge bench; held that all laws inserted into the **9th Schedule after 24 April 1973 are subject to judicial review** if they violate Fundamental Rights forming part of the Basic Structure. |
| **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy Case (2017)** | 9-Judge bench unanimously held that the **Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right** intrinsically protected under Article 21. |
| **Navtej Singh Johar Case (2018)** | Decriminalized consensual homosexual acts between adults by reading down **Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)**. |
| **Jarnail Singh Case (2018)** | Held that the **"Creamy Layer" principle applies to SC/ST** when extending reservation in promotions. |
| **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)** | Upheld the constitutional validity of the **103rd Constitutional Amendment (10% EWS reservation)** by 3:2 majority. |

## 10. Eleven Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A)

Added by the **42nd Amendment 1976** (Swaran Singh). The **11th** duty was added by the **86th Amendment 2002**. Duties are **non-justiciable**.

1. Abide by the Constitution, respect its ideals, the National Flag, and the National Anthem.
2. Cherish and follow the noble ideals of the freedom struggle.
3. Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
4. Defend the country and render national service when called.
5. Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
6. Value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture.
7. Protect and improve the natural environment, forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife, and have compassion for living creatures.
8. Develop the scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform.
9. Safeguard public property and abjure violence.
10. Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity.
11. **51A(k):** Parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to the child between **6 and 14** (86th Amendment).

## 11. Emergency Comparison (Arts. 352 / 356 / 360)

| Feature | National (352) | State / President’s Rule (356) | Financial (360) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | War, external aggression, **armed rebellion** (44th Amd; was “internal disturbance”) | Failure of constitutional machinery | Threat to financial stability or credit |
| Written Cabinet advice | **Yes** (44th Amd) | On GG/Governor report or otherwise | Yes |
| Lok Sabha approval | 1 month, then 6-month extensions | 2 months, then 6-month extensions (max 3 years with conditions) | 2 months |
| FR | **20 and 21 cannot be suspended** (44th); 19 suspends automatically only for war/external aggression | No automatic FR suspension | No FR suspension |
| Centre–State | Centre can give executive directions on any matter | State executive dismissed; Parliament may legislate for the state | Centre can direct money bills / salary cuts |
| Landmark case | — | **S.R. Bommai (1994)** — floor test; secularism; judicial review of 356 | Never used so far |

> **Logic:** After the **44th Amendment**, the President **cannot** suspend enforcement of **Articles 20 and 21** even in a National Emergency.

## 16. Five Writs (Art. 32 / 226)

| Writ | Literal sense | When it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | “To have the body” | Produce a detained person; tests illegal detention |
| **Mandamus** | “We command” | Orders a public authority to do a **public duty**; not against President/GG as such, private persons, or to enforce a contract |
| **Prohibition** | “To forbid” | Higher court stops a **lower court** from exceeding jurisdiction (**preventive**) |
| **Certiorari** | “To be certified” | Higher court **quashes** a lower court’s order already passed (**curative**) |
| **Quo Warranto** | “By what authority” | Challenges a person’s right to hold a **public office** |

> **Logic:** **Prohibition** is preventive. **Certiorari** is curative. **Art. 32** is itself a Fundamental Right (SC). **Art. 226** is wider (HC can issue writs for any legal right, not only FRs). Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 the **heart and soul** of the Constitution.

## 17. Basic Structure, Majorities, President & Judiciary

**Basic Structure (Kesavananda 1973) — usual list:** supremacy of the Constitution; sovereign, democratic, republican form; secularism; federalism; separation of powers; judicial review; rule of law; free and fair elections; independence of the judiciary; harmony of FR and DPSP; limited amending power.

### Three amendment tracks
| Track | Use |
|---|---|
| **Simple majority of Parliament** | Arts. **2–4** (states), **169** (Legislative Council) — **outside** Art. 368 |
| **Special majority (Art. 368)** | Most amendments (majority of total membership **and** 2/3 of members present and voting) |
| **Special + half the States** | Federal provisions (President election, Union–State lists, SC/HC, representation of States) |

### President — daily pegs
| Head | Correct |
|---|---|
| Election | **Art. 54–55**; elected MLAs + LS + RS; **single transferable vote**; weighted votes |
| Impeachment | **Art. 61**; 14-day notice; 2/3 of **total membership** of each House |
| Veto | Absolute, suspensive, **pocket** (no time limit on ordinary bills) |
| Ordinance | **Art. 123**; only when House(s) not in session; lasts **6 weeks from reassembly** |
| Money Bill | **Art. 110**; only on LS Speaker’s certificate |
| Death sentence pardon | **Art. 72** only (full pardon); Governor **161** cannot fully pardon death |

### Supreme Court & High Court
| Head | Correct |
|---|---|
| SC strength | **34** (2019); retire at **65**; resign to **President** |
| First CJI | **H.J. Kania**; SC from **28 Jan 1950** |
| **Art. 131** | Original jurisdiction (Centre–State) |
| **Art. 136** | Special Leave Petition |
| **Art. 137** | Review |
| **Art. 143** | Advisory; **not binding** |
| **Art. 226 vs 32** | HC writs **wider** (any legal right) |

**Preamble keywords:** Sovereign, **Socialist, Secular** (42nd), Democratic, Republic; Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. Preamble is **part of the Constitution** (*Kesavananda*) but **not enforceable**.
