## State Executive — the frame

- **Part VI (Arts. 153–167).** Every State has a Governor (**Art. 153**). The State Executive = **Governor + Chief Minister + Council of Ministers + Advocate General**.
- Parliamentary (Westminster) system at the State: Governor is the **nominal / de jure** head; the **real / de facto** executive is the CoM headed by the CM.
- **Art. 154:** executive power of the State is **vested in the Governor**, exercised by him directly or through officers subordinate — in practice on CoM advice (**Art. 163**), except where the Constitution gives him **discretion**.
- Office is **borrowed from Canada** (appointed by the Centre), **not** the USA (where governors are elected). The Constituent Assembly dropped an elected Governor.
- **Art. 361 immunities** (same scheme as the President): no court for **official** acts; **no criminal proceedings** while in office; civil proceedings only after **2 months’ notice**.

> **Exam note:** The Governor is a **constituent part of the State legislature** (Art. 168) just as the President is of Parliament — even though he sits in neither House.

## Governor — appointment, term, conditions (Arts. 153–162)

**Nominated constitutional head** (Canada model, not USA): appointed by the President; 5-year outer term at pleasure; same person **can** hold two or more States.

- **153:** Office of Governor. **Proviso (7th Amendment, 1956):** the **same person** may be appointed Governor of **two or more States**.
- **154:** Executive power of the State vested in the Governor.
- **155:** Governor is **appointed** by the **President** by warrant under his hand and seal — **not elected**.
- **156:** Term **5 years**; holds office **during the pleasure of the President**; may resign to the President. No impeachment procedure.
- **157:** Citizen of India; completed **35 years**.
- **158:** Shall **not** be a member of Parliament or a State legislature (seat vacated if appointed); no other office of profit; official residence; emoluments **charged on the State CFI** (shared if he is Governor of two States).
- **159:** Oath before the **Chief Justice of the High Court** (in his absence, the senior-most HC judge).
- **160:** President may make provision for the discharge of the Governor’s functions in any contingency.
- **161:** Pardoning power — **no death sentence, no court-martial** (those are the President, Art. **72**).
- **162:** **Extent** of the executive power of the State (co-extensive with the State List / Concurrent List, subject to the Union).

**Conventions (Sarkaria) the Centre is supposed to follow (often broken):**
- Governor should be an **outsider** (not from the State).
- Should be **detached from active politics**.
- **Chief Minister should be consulted** before appointment.
- Punchhi (2010) wanted a **fixed 5-year tenure** and removal only by a process akin to impeachment — **not implemented**.

**Tenure locks**
- 5 years is the **outer** limit; he continues until a successor enters (so the office is never vacant).
- Pleasure of the President = **no security of tenure**, **no grounds** required, **no impeachment**. *BP Singhal* (2010): removal cannot be **wholly arbitrary**; it is justiciable on limited grounds.
- Emoluments determined by **Parliament**; **charged on the State CFI**; cannot be diminished during the term. If he is Governor of two States, the States **share** the cost.

### Governor — powers

**Five heads** of the same office: executive, legislative, financial, judicial, plus Art. **371…** special responsibility.

- **Executive:** All State executive action in his **name** (166). Appoints CM, other ministers, **Advocate General**, State Election Commissioner, Chairman & members of the **State PSC** (removed by the **President**, not the Governor — trap). Nominates **1/6** of the LC.
- **Legislative:** Summons / prorogues the House(s); **dissolves the LA** (not the LC). Addresses the first session of the year / after each general election (**Art. 176**). Sends messages. Decides on Bills (**200**). Promulgates **ordinances (213)**.
- **Financial:** Money Bill / demand for grants only on his **recommendation**. Causes the State **Budget (Art. 202)** to be laid. Contingency Fund of the State at his disposal. Constitutes the **State Finance Commission** (Art. 243-I).
- **Judicial:** **Pardon, reprieve, respite, remission, commutation** under **161** for offences against **State law** — **not** death, **not** court-martial. Consults the HC on appointment of **district judges** (Art. 233 — detail in Judiciary).
- **Special (371…):** Discretionary “special responsibility”: e.g. **Maharashtra & Gujarat** (development boards), **Nagaland 371A** (law and order involving religious/social practices), **Arunachal 371H**, **Karnataka 371J**, **Andhra/Telangana 371D**.

**Art. 164 extra lock:** States of **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha** *shall* have a minister in charge of **tribal welfare**.

> **Exam note:** **Art. 155 = appointment, not removal.** SPSC members are **appointed by the Governor** but **removed by the President** (after SC enquiry) — do not mix.

## Discretionary powers — absolute vs situational (Art. 163)

**Art. 163(1):** CoM with the CM at the head to **aid and advise** the Governor, **except** insofar as he is by or under the Constitution required to act in his **discretion**.  
**Art. 163(2):** if any question arises whether a matter is discretionary, the **Governor’s decision is final** and cannot be called in question.

| Kind | Meaning | Typical cases |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **Constitutional / absolute discretion** | The Constitution **itself** requires the Governor to act on his own | Reserving a Bill for the President (**200**); recommending **President’s Rule (356)**; seeking information from the CM (**167**); special responsibility in **Art. 371** States (e.g. Nagaland, Arunachal, Maharashtra/Gujarat development boards, etc.) |
| **Situational discretion** | The Constitution is silent; political reality forces a choice | **Hung Assembly** — whom to invite as CM; dismissing a CoM that has **lost the House** but will not resign; dissolving the LA when the CM has lost majority / advises dissolution after losing a floor test |

**Constitutional (absolute) discretion — full list**
1. Reserving a Bill for the President (**Art. 200**).  
2. Recommending **President’s Rule** (**Art. 356**).  
3. Seeking information from the CM (**Art. 167**).  
4. **Special responsibility** under **Arts. 371–371J** (and 6th Schedule, some governors).  
5. Deciding, under **Art. 163(2)**, whether a matter *is* discretionary.

**Situational discretion — full list**
1. Appointing the CM when **no party has a clear majority** (hung).  
2. Dismissing the CoM when it has **lost the LA** but will not resign.  
3. Dissolving the LA when the CM has lost (or never had) majority and no alternative government is possible.  
4. Advising the President on whether 356 is warranted after a political breakdown.

**Case locks**
- ***S.R. Bommai* (1994):** majority **on the floor**; a ministry with LA majority **cannot** be dismissed; 356 is justiciable.
- ***Rameshwar Prasad* (2006):** dissolving Bihar LA **without a floor test** was unconstitutional.
- ***Nabam Rebia* (2016):** Governor’s discretion is **not a general reservoir of power**; he cannot use it to **thwart** the Assembly (e.g. advancing a session to defeat a Speaker).

> **Exam note:** “The Governor always acts on advice” is **false**. Art. 163 **expressly** carves out discretion, and 163(2) makes his classification **final** — but *Nabam Rebia* says that finality is **not** a blank cheque.

## Hung Assembly & dismissal of the Council of Ministers

**Hung Assembly:** no party / pre-poll alliance has a clear majority after the LA election. Governor’s call is **situational discretion** — majority on the **House floor** (*Bommai*).

- **Governor’s call:** Invite a leader who can prove majority.
- **Floor test:** Must be held **at the earliest**; window-shopping of MLAs in Raj Bhavan is what *Bommai* condemned.
- **If CM loses the test:** CoM **must resign**. If it does not, the Governor **may dismiss** it (Art. 164 — pleasure, read with *Bommai*).
- **Dissolution:** Governor may dissolve the LA on CM advice **only if** the CM still commands (or just lost and no alternative is possible). A CM who has lost the House **cannot** be allowed to dissolve merely to avoid a rival’s claim.
- **President’s Rule:** If no government can be formed, Governor reports under **Art. 356**.

**Dismissal (Art. 164):** ministers hold office during the **Governor’s pleasure**, but pleasure is **not** a licence to sack a majority ministry. Collective responsibility is to the **Legislative Assembly**.

**If the CM resigns or dies, the whole CoM goes** (collective responsibility). The Governor may ask another leader to prove majority, or report under 356.

**Invite-order (Sarkaria / Punchhi — exam sequence)**  
(1) **Pre-poll alliance** that has a majority → (2) **largest single party** claiming support → (3) **post-poll coalition** with a majority claim → (4) **floor test at the earliest** (usually within days, not weeks).

## Reservation of Bills — Arts. 200 & 201

When a Bill passed by the State legislature is presented, the Governor **may** (Art. **200**):

| Option | Ordinary Bill | Money Bill |
|--------|---------------|------------|
| **Assent** | Yes | Yes |
| **Withhold** assent | Yes | Yes |
| **Return** for reconsideration (“as soon as possible”) | Yes | **No** |
| If House(s) **re-pass** a returned Bill | Governor **shall assent** | — |
| **Reserve** for the President | Yes | **Yes** (can reserve a Money Bill too) |

**Mandatory reservation (2nd proviso to Art. 200):** a Bill which in the Governor’s opinion would **so derogate from the powers of the High Court** as to endanger its constitutional position.

Other Bills **often** reserved (practice, not the 2nd proviso): those against Directive Principles, of grave national importance, dealing with compulsory acquisition under Art. 31A, or those the President must assent to under the Constitution.

**Art. 201 (President on a reserved Bill)**

**No time limit** — the President’s **pocket veto on State Bills**; unlike the Governor, he is **never bound** if the House re-passes.

- **Assent:** Bill becomes an Act.
- **Withhold assent:** Bill dies.
- **Direct the Governor to return** it (if not a Money Bill): State legislature reconsiders.
- **If the House(s) pass it again:** President is **still not bound** to assent.

> **Exam note:** Governor **must** assent to an ordinary Bill that he returned and the House re-passed. The **President is never so bound**. There is **no joint sitting** at State level — deadlock between LA and LC is solved by **Art. 197** (LA prevails), not by the Governor.

## Governor’s ordinance (Art. 213)

**State parallel of Art. 123** — but **213** is the only ordinance power that can require **another authority’s (President’s) instructions**.

- **When:** **Unicameral:** LA not in session. **Bicameral:** **both Houses** not in session (Laxmikanth lock).
- **Force:** Same as an Act of the State legislature; can be retrospective; cannot do what the legislature cannot do.
- **Life:** Must be laid; ceases **six weeks** from reassembly, or earlier if disapproved. Maximum theoretical life ≈ 6 months (session gap) + 6 weeks.
- **President’s instructions *compulsory*** (3 provisos): (i) a Bill that would have needed the **President’s prior sanction**, (ii) a Bill the Governor would have **reserved**, (iii) an Act of the same kind that would have been **invalid without the President’s assent**.
- **Cannot:** Amend the Constitution; encroach on the Union list; violate FRs.

> **Exam note:** Compare **123 (President)** — no “instructions” from anyone. **213** is the only ordinance power that can require **another authority’s (President’s) instructions**. *D.C. Wadhwa* (re-promulgation = fraud) applies to States too.

## Chief Minister & Council of Ministers (Arts. 163–164, 167)

**Real executive:** CM + CoM collectively responsible to the **LA only**; size cap is the **91st Amendment**.

- **Appointment:** Governor appoints the CM; other ministers **on the CM’s advice** (Art. 164).
- **Who can be CM:** Normally the leader of the majority in the **LA**. Can be from the **LC** (rare). A person who is **not a member** of either House can be CM/minister but must become one within **6 months** (164(4)).
- **10th Schedule bar (164(1B)):** A member **disqualified for defection** cannot be a minister (91st).
- **Collective responsibility:** To the **Legislative Assembly only** (not the Council) — Art. 164(2). Resignation/death of the CM = **whole CoM goes**.
- **Individual responsibility:** Ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the Governor** (in practice, the CM’s).
- **Size (91st Amendment, 2003):** Total including CM **≤ 15%** of the LA; **minimum 12** including CM. The floor of 12 applies even to small Assemblies.
- **Tribal welfare minister:** **Compulsory** in **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha** (Art. 164).
- **Art. 167:** CM must **communicate** CoM decisions, furnish information, and submit a matter to the Council if the Governor so requires (parallel to Art. **78**).
- **Oath:** Art. 164(3) — before the Governor; **Third Schedule**.
- **Deputy CM:** **Not** in the Constitution (like Deputy PM) — a political rank.

**CM vs Governor (role)**

**Real vs nominal executive** — CM answers to the LA; Governor is the President’s appointee.

| CM | Governor |
|----|----------|
| Real executive; majority in **LA** | Nominal executive; **appointee** of the President |
| Can be from LA or LC | Cannot be an MP/MLA |
| Resigns to the **Governor** | Resigns to the **President** |

## Advocate General (Art. 165)

**Highest law officer of the State** (parallel to the AG of India, Art. **76**): pleasure of the **Governor**, **HC-judge** qualification.

- **Appointment:** By the **Governor**; must be qualified to be a **High Court judge** (citizen + 10 years HC advocate **or** 10 years judicial office).
- **Term / removal:** **No fixed tenure**; **no procedure**; holds office during the **pleasure of the Governor**. Remuneration also fixed by the Governor.
- **Rights (Art. 177):** Audience in all **courts in the State**; may **speak and take part** in both Houses and their committees; **not entitled to vote** *by virtue of this article*.
- **Practice:** Not a full-time government servant — may take private briefs, **not against the State**.
- **Assisted by:** Additional / Deputy Advocates General — **statutory / executive**, not in the Constitution.

> **Exam note:** AG of India = pleasure of the **President**, SC-judge qualification. Advocate General = pleasure of the **Governor**, **HC-judge** qualification. Neither is removed like a judge.

## State Legislature — unicameral vs bicameral (Arts. 168–169)

**Art. 168:** a State legislature = the **Governor** + Legislative Assembly (**Vidhan Sabha**), and, in some States, a Legislative Council (**Vidhan Parishad**). The Governor is a **part of** the legislature (assent, address, ordinance).

| | Unicameral | Bicameral |
|--|-----------|-----------|
| Houses | Only **LA** | **LA + LC** |
| How LC is created / abolished | — | **Art. 169** |
| Deadlock on an ordinary Bill | — | **Art. 197** — LA **prevails**. **No joint sitting** in a State |

**Art. 169 procedure**
1. The **Legislative Assembly** passes a resolution by **special majority** (majority of **total membership** + **2/3 of members present and voting**).
2. **Parliament** then passes a law by **simple majority**. That law is **not** deemed an Art. **368** amendment (so no special majority, no State ratification).
3. Parliament’s law may contain **supplemental, incidental and consequential** provisions (representation, sitting members, etc.).

**No joint sitting at State level (high-yield trap)**  
Unlike Parliament’s Art. **108**, a State has **no joint sitting**. If the LC rejects / amends / sits on an ordinary Bill that started in the LA: the LA may pass it again; after the LC has had **1 month** on the second round, the Bill is **deemed passed by both Houses** in the form adopted by the LA (**Art. 197**). A Bill that **originates in the LC** and is rejected by the LA **dies**.

## Legislative Assembly (Art. 170, 172, 174–180)

**Vidhan Sabha:** directly elected; **max 500, min 60**; **5 years**; **can be dissolved**; UP = **403** (largest).

- **Strength (Art. 170):** **Max 500, min 60** elected. Parliament may by law alter. Smaller Assemblies exist by special provision (Goa 40, Sikkim 32, Mizoram 40, Arunachal 60, Nagaland 60, Puducherry 30, etc.).
- **UP:** **403** elected — **largest LA in India**.
- **Election:** Direct; **FPTP**; territorial constituencies. SC/ST reservation (**Art. 332**). Anglo-Indian nomination (**333**) ended by the **104th**. Seat freeze till the census after **2026**.
- **Term (Art. 172):** **5 years** from the first meeting; **can be dissolved** earlier by the Governor. National Emergency: extend **1 year at a time**, not beyond **6 months after** the Emergency ends.
- **Age:** **25 years**.
- **Sessions (Art. 174):** Governor summons / prorogues / **dissolves LA**. Maximum gap **6 months**.
- **Quorum (Art. 189):** **10 members or 1/10** of total membership, **whichever is greater**.
- **Disqualification:** Art. **191** (same five heads as Art. 102, plus 10th Schedule). Decision: **Governor + binding EC opinion** (**Art. 192**) — *not* the Speaker, except defection.
- **Vacation:** Dual membership; resignation to Speaker; **60 days’** absence (Art. 190).
- **Privileges:** **Art. 194** (parallel to 105); 44th = as on 20 June 1979.
- **Language:** **Art. 210** — Hindi or official language of the State, or English.
- **Money Bill (Arts. 198–199):** Introduced **only in the LA**, on the **Governor’s recommendation**. Speaker’s certificate **final**. LC: **14 days**, recommendatory only.
- **Budget (Art. 202):** Annual Financial Statement of the State; Demands for Grants voted **only in the LA**. Charged on the **State CFI**: Governor’s emoluments, Speaker/Dy Speaker, Chairman/Dy Chairman of LC, HC judges, debt charges.

**Other large Assemblies (match-list):** West Bengal **294**, Maharashtra **288**, Bihar **243**, Tamil Nadu **234**, Madhya Pradesh **230**, Karnataka **224**, Rajasthan **200**.

## Legislative Council (Arts. 169, 171–173)

**Vidhan Parishad:** continuing House like RS — **never dissolved**, **can be abolished** (169); **max 1/3 of LA, min 40**; UP = **100**.

- **Size (Art. 171):** **Not more than 1/3** of the LA **and not less than 40**. (So an LA must be at least **120** for a 40-member LC to be constitutionally possible.)
- **UP LC:** **100** (1/3 of 403 would allow up to 134; Parliament/State law fixed 100).
- **Term:** **6 years**; **1/3 retire every 2 years**; **cannot be dissolved**.
- **Age:** **30 years**.
- **Election system:** **PR-STV**, secret ballot (except the Governor’s nominees).
- **Chairman / Deputy Chairman:** Elected **from among LC members** (Arts. 182–183). **Not** the Governor. Removal: 14 days + majority of all then members.
- **Does not vote for:** **President of India** (only elected MLAs do).

**Composition (Art. 171) — five streams (must add to 1)**

- **1/3:** Local bodies — municipalities, district boards and such other local authorities as Parliament specifies.
- **1/12:** **Graduates** of **three years’** standing, resident in the State.
- **1/12:** **Teachers** in educational institutions **not lower than secondary**, of three years’ standing.
- **1/3:** **MLAs**, from among persons who are **not** MLAs.
- **1/6:** **Nominated by the Governor** — literature, science, art, **co-operative movement**, social service.

> **Exam note:** LC nomination includes **co-operative movement**; RS nomination (**12**) does **not**. Graduates / teachers = **1/12 each**, not 1/3. A Bill that **starts in the LC** and is rejected by the LA **dies** (Art. 197 does not save it).

## Speaker of the Legislative Assembly (Arts. 178–181)

**Constitutional office** (Art. 178): elected from among LA members; continues after dissolution until the new House first meets.

- **Election:** From among LA members; date fixed by the Governor (first sitting) / Speaker (thereafter).
- **Term:** Continues **after dissolution** until the first meeting of the new LA.
- **Resignation:** Speaker → **Deputy Speaker**; Deputy Speaker → **Speaker**.
- **Removal (Art. 179):** **14 days’ notice** + majority of **all the then members**. Cannot preside over his own removal.
- **Casting vote:** Votes only to **break a tie**.
- **Money Bill:** Decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill (**Art. 199**) — decision **final**.
- **10th Schedule:** Decides defection (judicially reviewable — *Kihoto / Nabam Rebia*).
- **Pro Tem Speaker:** Appointed by the **Governor** (usually senior-most MLA); oath + Speaker’s election.

**Deputy Speaker** is a constitutional office (Art. 178). Convention (often broken): from the opposition. Panel of Chairmen (Rules of Procedure) preside if both are absent.

## Governor vs President (quick contrast)

**Nominated vs elected; pleasure vs impeachment; 161 cannot touch death / court-martial.**

| Point | President | Governor |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| Election | Indirect (Electoral College) | **Nominated** by the President |
| Removal | Impeachment (Art. 61) | **Pleasure** of the President (no impeachment) |
| Dual charge | One President | **Can** be Governor of two+ States |
| Pardon death / court-martial | **Yes** (72) | **No** (161) |
| Ordinance | Art. 123 (Parliament not in session) | Art. 213 (legislature not in session); **President’s instructions** in some cases |
| Discretion | Narrow (hung LS, pocket veto, one reconsideration of advice) | **Wider** — 163 + 200 + 356 + hung LA |
| Reserved Bill re-passed | — | Governor **must** then assent; **President need not** |

## UP Focus (State Government)

**Bicameral State:** LA **403** + LC **100**; Raj Bhavan Lucknow; High Court principal seat **Prayagraj**.

- **UP Legislative Assembly:** **403** elected seats (largest LA in India).
- **UP Legislative Council:** **100** (bicameral).
- **Governor of UP:** Appointed by the President; Raj Bhavan, Lucknow.
- **High Court:** Principal seat **Prayagraj** (Letters Patent **17 Mar 1866**; first sat **Agra**). **Lucknow Bench** 1948 (Oudh Chief Court 1925 merged). Not a second High Court.

## Current Affairs

**Live locks** for match / how-many / hung-house questions.

- **Ongoing:** **Six** bicameral States: AP, Bihar, Karnataka, MH, Telangana, UP (match / how-many).
- **2019:** J&K Legislative Council **abolished** with the State (Art. 169 / reorganisation trap).
- **Convention:** *Bommai* floor-test still the lock in every hung-house controversy (discretion vs majority).

## Common Traps

**Wrong stem → lock.** (Same facts as above; this is the trap list.)

- Art. 155 = removal of Governor → **Appointment**; removal = **156 pleasure**.
- One person cannot be Governor of two States → **Can** — 7th Amd proviso to **153**.
- Art. 162 = executive power of State → that’s **154**; **162 = extent**.
- Governor always on CoM advice → **except discretion** (163).
- Governor can pardon a death sentence → **No** — only the President.
- LC can be dissolved → **Never**; it can be **abolished** (169).
- LC minimum 50 / 1/4 of LA → **min 40; max 1/3 of LA**.
- CoM min 10 in a State → **12** including CM (91st).
- CoM responsible to both Houses → **LA only**.
- President must assent if a reserved Bill is re-passed → **No** — unlike the Governor under 200.
- AG removed like an HC judge → **pleasure of the Governor**.
- RS-style Chairman of LC is the Governor → LC elects its **own** Chairman from among members.
- State legislature has a joint sitting → **No** — Art. **197**, LA prevails.
- Bill starting in LC and rejected by LA is saved by 197 → **dies**.
- SPSC members removed by the Governor → **appointed** by Governor, **removed by the President**.
- Art. 192 disqualification decided by the Speaker → **Governor + binding EC** (defection excepted).
- Governor’s ordinance needs no one’s instructions → **President’s instructions** in three listed cases.
- CM must be from the Assembly → can be from the **Council**; or a non-member for **6 months**.
- Tribal welfare minister optional in all States → **compulsory** in CG, JH, MP, Odisha.

## Mains link (one frame)

- The Governor is a **constitutional head with a Union tether**: Art. 163 discretion + Art. 200 reservation + Art. 356 report are the three levers. *Bommai* narrowed the third (floor test). Arts. **200–201** still let the Centre sit on a State Bill with **no time limit** — the sharpest remaining asymmetry between Raj Bhavan and Rashtrapati Bhavan.
