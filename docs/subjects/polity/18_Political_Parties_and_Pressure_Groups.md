## Frame

Parties as a **system** + groups that **push** the system.  **who is a national party, who founded when, coalition/hung, unions/pressure groups**. UP paper loves **BSP / SP years** and **union–party match**.

## Constitutional footing

**No “Political Parties” chapter.** Parties live through **19(1)(c)** (association), **324** (ECI), **10th Schedule** (defection — 52nd / 91st). Extra-constitutional until 52nd.

- Until 1985, parties were almost entirely **extra**-constitutional.
- RPA: **s.29A** registration · **s.29B/29C** (bonds era / donation reports — bonds **struck Feb 2024**).
- Whip / defection: 52nd/1985 10th Sch; 91st merger needs 2/3.
- Funding: **s.29C** donation reports · IT **13A**. Electoral bonds **struck Feb 2024** (*ADR*). Corporate 7.5% cap was removed in 2017 — don’t write it as current law.

## Functions of a political party

- Interest **aggregation** — turns caste / region / class demands into a manifesto.
- Elite **recruitment** — supplies CMs, ministers, MPs.
- Government / opposition — majority **forms**; rest **criticises** (official opposition needs **10%** of the House).
- Political **socialisation** — cadres, campaigns, booth work.
- Link: citizen ↔ State. Without parties, FPTP still needs a **label + symbol**.

---

## Features of the Indian party system (the missing Laxmikanth card)

- **Multi-party** — not two-party (UK/US) and not one-party (China). Duverger’s FPTP→two-party **fails** here because of **region + caste + language**.
- **Dominant-party phases** — Congress **1952–67** (Kothari) · BJP **2014–24** majority. Phase ≠ system.
- **Weak ideology** (most) — catch-all. Exceptions: **Left**, **BJP–RSS** stream.
- **Personality / dynasty** — SP, DMK, NC, RJD, NCP, Congress after Nehru — favourite criticism.
- **Traditional cleavages** — caste, religion, language, region **over** class (except Left belts).
- **Regionalisation** after **1967** — State parties as **kingmakers** at the Centre (TDP, JD(U), BJD, DMK, TMC).
- **Factionalism** — split → ECI **Para 15** (name + symbol).
- **Poor inner democracy** — no binding statute. ECI guidelines on office-bearers / elections inside parties — **soft**.
- **Extra-constitutional origin** — parties older than the Republic (INC **1885**); Constitution caught up via **10th Schedule (1985)**.
- **Coalition arithmetic** — Centre **1989–2014** and again **2024**; States since **1967**.

## Registration vs recognition vs symbol

**s.29A registers; Symbols Order 1968 recognises.** Do not merge.

- **Register** — **s.29A** RPA 1951 → ECI. Result: **RUPP** — can contest, **no** reserved symbol.
- **Recognise** — **Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968**. Result: **National** or **State** party.
- **Symbol** — same Order. **Reserved** (recognised) vs **free** (RUPP + independents).
- **Split** — **Para 15** of the Order. ECI allots **name + symbol** (*Sadiq Ali*). Legislature majority is **a** test, not the only test.

**National party — any one route**

- **6%** valid votes in LS **or** Assembly elections in **four** States **and** **4** LS seats.
- **2%** of LS seats (**11** of 543) from at least **three** States.
- Recognised as a **State party in four** States.

**State party — any one route (in that State)**

- **6%** votes in that State’s Assembly **or** LS poll **and** **2** MLAs **or** **1** MP.
- **3%** of Assembly seats **or 3 seats**, whichever is more.
- **1** LS seat per **25** LS seats from that State (minimum 1).
- **8%** of total valid votes in the State (**even with zero seats** — 2011 add-on).

ECI **reviews after every LS / every State Assembly general election**. Status can be **lost** (CPI **2023**). Do **not** freeze “India always has six national parties.”

**s.29A extra locks:** apply to ECI with memorandum + **100** citizen-members; **21-day** public notice; no dual membership of another registered party. ECI may refuse. **RUPP ≠ reserved symbol.**

**Common symbol for a RUPP (not recognition):** ECI may allot one **common** free symbol in a State if the party contests a **minimum share of seats** there (practice: **5%** of Assembly seats or a notified LS minimum). Still **unrecognised**.

**Benefits of recognition:** all-India (or in-State) **reserved symbol** · star campaigners **40** vs **20** · AIR/DD time · **1 proposer** (others **10** for LS) · easier deposit refund (1/6 votes) · consultation by ECI · office land as rules allow.

## Symbols

**Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968** — ECI, **324 + RPA**. Not an Act of Parliament.

- **Reserved:** national party = **same symbol in every State**. State party = reserved **in that State**; elsewhere it is a free/other symbol.
- **Free:** independents + RUPPs pick from the State free list.
- **Para 15** split: ECI decides which faction gets **name + symbol** (*Sadiq Ali* 1972). Tests: party constitution, legislative majority, organisational majority — **not** Speaker.
- BSP–Assam: **elephant** is reserved for BSP **except Assam** (already reserved there for another party historically).
- SP = **bicycle** (not “cycle” as a separate symbol).
- A national reserved symbol is **never** a free symbol in any State.

## Multi-party system

**India is a multi-party system** (Duverger: FPTP *tends* to two parties — India is the exception because of **region, caste, language**).

- **1952–67** — Rajni Kothari’s **Congress system**: one dominant party, opposition fragmented.
- **1967** — first big **non-Congress** coalitions in States (SVD). Congress majority at Centre survives.
- **1977** — first **non-Congress** Centre — Janata (coalition).
- **1989–2014** — **coalition era** at the Centre (NF / UF / NDA / UPA).
- **2014–19 / 2019–24** — BJP **single-party majority** (still a multi-party *system*).
- **2024** — BJP **240**, short of 272. **NDA coalition** government.

**Types of party system (Sartori / textbook):** one-party · two-party · **multi-party** · dominant-party. India = **multi-party** with **bipolar coalitions** at the Centre in some decades (NDA vs UPA), **not** a two-party system.

**Not** a two-party system. **Not** a one-party State. Dominant-party **phases** ≠ one-party *system*.

## Coalition government

**Two or more parties share the Council of Ministers** (or a party governs with **outside** support).

- **Pre-poll** — alliance **before** counting (NDA, UPA as brands).
- **Post-poll** — bargain **after** a hung House.
- **Minority govt** — cabinet from one/few parties; majority on the floor from **outside** support (e.g. UF 1996–98).
- Centre markers: **1977** Janata · **1989** National Front · **1996** United Front · **1998–2004** NDA · **2004–14** UPA · **2024** NDA again without a BJP solo majority.
- Cost: Common Minimum Programme · gathbandhan instability · policy veto by a small ally.
- State: same logic. Floor test = *Bommai* (majority on the floor, not Raj Bhavan).
- Jumbo ministry: **91st Amd** — CoM ≤ **15%** of LS / Assembly — written *because* coalition cabinets ballooned.
- CMP = Common Minimum Programme — the coalition’s public contract.

**Centre coalitions (PM lock):**

- **1977** Janata — Morarji Desai (first non-Congress).
- **1989** National Front — V.P. Singh (outside Left + BJP support — unstable).
- **1996** United Front — Deve Gowda → I.K. Gujral (Congress outside).
- **1998–2004** NDA — Vajpayee (13 days in **1996** first; then 1998/99).
- **2004–14** UPA — Manmohan Singh.
- **2014 / 2019** — Modi: BJP **solo majority** (allies still in NDA).
- **2024** — Modi: BJP **240**, NDA **coalition**.

**Pre-poll vs post-poll:** NDA/UPA as **brands** are pre-poll when declared; a hung House often produces a **post-poll** gathbandhan. Pre-poll usually invited first (Sarkaria / Punchhi — hung card below).

## Hung Parliament / Assembly

**No party** has a majority of **total membership** on its own.

- LS examples: **1989**, **1996**, **1998**, **2004** (UPA assembled a majority), **2024** (BJP hung, NDA not).
- Who is invited: President (Art. **75**) / Governor (**164**) calls whoever is **most likely to command** confidence — often the **largest** party or a **pre-poll** alliance.
- Proof: **floor test**, not hotel arithmetic. *Bommai / SR Bommai* + Punchhi.
- A 13-day Vajpayee (1996) is the textbook “invited but lost the floor” lock.
- Hung ≠ President’s Rule automatically. **356** is failure of constitutional machinery, not a hung House by itself.
- Sarkaria / Punchhi **invite order** (States, same logic at Centre): (1) **pre-poll** alliance (2) **largest** party (3) **post-poll** combination that claims majority. Then **floor test** at once.
- 1996 LS: Vajpayee sworn, **lost** confidence in **13 days** — hung + invite ≠ majority.

## Pressure groups & interest groups

| | **Political party** | **Pressure / interest group** |
|--|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Aim | Win **office** | Influence **policy** without (usually) contesting |
| Accountable | To voters via elections | To members / donors |
| Programme | Wide manifesto | Narrow interest |

**Interest group** = organised around a shared interest. It becomes a **pressure group** when it **pressures** the government (lobby, strike, PIL, media, dharna). Indian papers often treat the two as **near-synonyms** — still learn the distinction.

**Almond’s four (with Indian examples):**

- **Associational** — formal, specialised. FICCI (**1927**) · CII · ASSOCHAM · NASSCOM · IMA · trade unions.
- **Institutional** — inside the State. Bureaucracy, Army, a **party’s** labour/kisan wing.
- **Non-associational** — informal caste / language / religion. Jat / Maratha / Patidar reservation agitations; linguistic groups.
- **Anomic** — spontaneous, often violent. Riots, flash bandhs — **not** a stable organisation.

**Promotional vs protective (Finer):** promotional = a cause (NBA, PUCL, Chipko). Protective = a sectional interest (FICCI, IMA, BKU).

**Methods:** lobbying · funding · strike / bandh · PIL · media · capture of advisory boards. **Not** the same as a political party’s election campaign.

**Farm 2020–21 (SKM)** = associational pressure; three farm laws **repealed Nov 2021**. Business chambers after **1991** liberalisation = classic protective groups.

**Indian map (learn 2 per row):**

- Business: **FICCI 1927** · ASSOCHAM **1920** · CII (rebrand **1992**; roots 1895) · NASSCOM **1988**.
- Labour: AITUC / INTUC / BMS / CITU — union card below.
- Farmers: AIKS **1936** · BKU · Shetkari Sanghatana (Sharad Joshi) · SKM **2020–21**.
- Students: **ABVP 1948** · NSUI · SFI · AISF.
- Professional: **IMA** · Bar Council / Bar associations · IBA.
- Religious / cultural: **RSS 1925** (cadre; not a party) · VHP · Jamaat-e-Islami.
- Environment: **Chipko 1973** · Narmada Bachao (Medha Patkar) · Silent Valley.
- Linguistic: Samyukta Maharashtra · anti-Hindi agitations (1965).

**Techniques:** lobbying ministers/MPs · financing parties · strike / bandh / rail-roko · PIL · media · capturing advisory boards · expert committees · street veto. India has **no US-style lobbying statute**.

**Role:** voice for sections the party system under-represents · policy expertise · check on majority. **Criticism:** sectional veto · money power · unaccountable · can stall Parliament from the street · sometimes anomic (riots).

**Not a pressure group:** a **recognised political party** (it contests). RSS is a **cadre organisation** that *pressures* and *feeds* a party (BJP) — exam still parks it under promotional/institutional, **not** as a registered party.

## Trade unions

**Trade Unions Act, 1926** (being folded into the labour codes).

- First union: **Madras Labour Union, 1918** — B.P. Wadia.
- Gandhi stream: **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (Majoor Mahajan), 1920** — Anasuya Sarabhai.
- First **central** federation: **AITUC, 31 Oct 1920**, Bombay. First president **Lala Lajpat Rai**.
- ILO: India founding member **1919**.
- Right to form unions: Art. **19(1)(c)**. **Strike is not a FR** (*All India Bank Employees*).
- 42nd: **43A** workers in management (DPSP).

Registration (1926 Act): originally **7** members could apply; 2001 Amd → **10% or 100** workmen, whichever less. CTUO “recognised” by the Labour Ministry on membership returns — **BMS** has often claimed the largest verified membership.

- **AITUC — 31 Oct 1920** (oldest central) — **CPI**.
- **INTUC — 1947** — **INC**.
- **HMS — 1948** — socialists.
- **UTUC — 1949** — **RSP** (not CPI / CPI-M).
- **BMS — 23 Jul 1955** — **BJP / RSS**.
- **CITU — 1970** — **CPI(M)**.

> **Exam note:** 2022 asked BMS / INTUC / UTUC / AITUC vs INC, CPI, CPI(M), BJP. **Correct affiliations:** BMS–BJP · INTUC–INC · **AITUC–CPI** · **CITU–CPI(M)** · UTUC–**RSP**. The option set has no RSP — the only option that gets **BMS and INTUC** right is **D**, which then **swaps** the two Left unions. Learn the **true** table; mark **D** if forced.

## Mains link (one frame)

India’s party system is **multi-party** because FPTP sits on **region + caste + language**. **1952–67** was a Congress *system*, not a one-party State; **1989–2014** was coalition arithmetic; **2014** majority did not abolish other parties; **2024** returned a **coalition** Union. Close: parties **contest**, pressure groups **press** — and the 2022 paper still wants **TDP/BSP/SP/TMC years** and **BMS/INTUC** affiliations from memory.
