## Integrated vs independent — the frame

India’s judiciary is **both** integrated (one hierarchy) **and** independent (separate from the other organs) — those are **not** the same claim.

- **Integrated:** **one** hierarchy: district/sessions → High Court → Supreme Court. SC is the **apex for the Union and the States**. Canadian flavour.
- **Independent:** separate from the executive/legislature (Arts. **124–147, 214–231, 50**). Not the same thing as “integrated”.
- **USA contrast:** dual system: federal courts apply federal law; state courts apply state law; US SC is **not** the regular apex of state courts. India has **no** such split.
- **Part / Articles:** SC: **Part V, Ch. IV, Arts. 124–147**. HC: **Part VI, Ch. V, Arts. 214–231**. Subordinate: **233–237**. Tribunals: **Part XIVA (323A–323B)**.
- **Art. 50:** DPSP — separate the judiciary from the executive in the **public services of the State**.

**Predecessor**
- **Federal Court of India:** **1 October 1937** (GOI Act **1935**). First CJ: Sir **Maurice Gwyer**. Appeals still went to the **Privy Council**.
- **Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act, 1949** — residual appeals ended.
- SC inaugurated **28 January 1950**. First CJI: **H.J. Kania**. Original strength **CJI + 7 = 8**.

> **Exam note:** “Independent” ≠ “integrated”. A court can be independent in a dual system (USA). India is **both**.

## Supreme Court — Arts. 124–147 (elaboration)

**Part V, Ch. IV:** the Union’s apex court — appointment, jurisdiction, and “complete justice”.

- **Art. 124:** establishment & appointment — SC of India; President appoints after consultation; age **65**; resignation to **President**; removal **124(4)**.
- **Arts. 124A–C:** NJAC — inserted by **99th** (2014); **struck down 2015** — text is dead.
- **Art. 125:** salaries — fixed by Parliament; **charged on CFI**; no disadvantage after appointment except Art. **360**.
- **Art. 126:** Acting CJI — President appoints when CJI is absent / office vacant — convention: **senior-most**.
- **Art. 127:** ad hoc judge — sitting **HC** judge, SC-qualified; CJI + President’s consent + consult that HC CJ; used when **quorum** is wanting.
- **Art. 128:** retired judges sit — retired **SC** (or HC judge qualified for SC); CJI + President’s consent + the person’s consent.
- **Art. 129:** court of record — records as evidence; punish **contempt of itself**.
- **Art. 130:** seat — **Delhi**. CJI may, with President’s approval, sit elsewhere. **No permanent outstation benches**.
- **Art. 131:** original (federal) — exclusive Union↔State / State↔State. **Not** FR, **not** private parties.
- **Art. 132:** appeal — Constitution — from HC if substantial question of law as to **interpretation of the Constitution**.
- **Art. 133:** appeal — civil — from HC; **30th Amd (1972)** dropped the old ₹20,000 floor.
- **Art. 134:** appeal — criminal — automatic if HC reverses acquittal and sentences to **death** (or withdraws and death); else on certificate.
- **Art. 134A:** certificate — **44th Amd (1978)** — HC must consider a 132/133/134 certificate at once.
- **Art. 135:** Federal Court legacy — SC may exercise jurisdiction of the old Federal Court in leftover matters.
- **Art. 136:** SLP — discretionary leave from **any** court/tribunal **except armed forces**.
- **Art. 137:** review — SC may review its own judgment.
- **Art. 138:** enlarged jurisdiction — Parliament **by law** may enlarge SC jurisdiction.
- **Art. 139:** extra writs — Parliament may confer on SC writs for purposes **other than FR**.
- **Art. 139A:** transfer of cases — SC may transfer HC cases involving the same question of law (**44th**).
- **Art. 140:** supplemental powers — Parliament may confer powers incidental to any SC jurisdiction.
- **Art. 141:** binding law — law declared by SC **binding on all courts** in India (not on the SC itself — it can overrule).
- **Art. 142:** complete justice — unique to **SC** (not HCs). Any decree/order to do complete justice; enforceable throughout India.
- **Art. 143:** advisory — President refers; **not binding**. **143(1)** Court *may* decline; **143(2)** *shall* report.
- **Art. 144:** aid of SC — all civil & judicial authorities **shall act in aid** of the SC.
- **Art. 145:** rules / benches — rules with President’s approval. **145(3):** min **5** judges for constitutional interpretation **and** for 143.
- **Art. 146:** officers & expenses — officers appointed by **CJI**. Administrative expenses **charged on CFI**.
- **Art. 147:** “Interpretation of Constitution” includes interpretation of the **GOI Act 1935** and the **Indian Independence Act 1947**.

**Language (Art. 348):** until Parliament otherwise provides, **English** is the language of the SC and of every HC. A Governor, with the President’s prior consent, may authorise **Hindi** (or the official State language) in HC proceedings; judgments still have an English version.

## Supreme Court — composition & organisation

**CJI + judges** sit at Delhi; strength is by **ordinary law**, not a 368 amendment.

- **Establishment:** **Art. 124(1)** — not 214, not 226.
- **Seat:** **Delhi** (130). No regular benches outside Delhi.
- **Strength:** Parliament **by law** (not a constitutional amendment). Original **8**. Path: 11 (1956) → 14 (1960) → 18 (1977) → 26 (1986) → 31 (2008) → **34 (2019 Act: CJI + 33)**.
- **Tenure:** until **65**. **No extension** of a sitting judge. No second term.
- **Resignation:** to the **President** (not the CJI).
- **Oath:** **3rd Schedule**, before the **President** or a person appointed by him — to bear true faith, uphold the Constitution, perform without fear or favour.
- **Acting CJI (126):** President; convention = senior-most available SC judge.
- **Ad hoc (127):** **sitting HC** judge. Quorum-gap only. Not a retired judge (that is **128**).
- **Retired sit (128):** consent of President **and** of the person. Allowances, not a fresh tenure past 65 as a “sitting” judge.
- **Salaries (125):** Parliament; **2nd Schedule** originally; **charged on CFI**; cannot be varied to disadvantage after appointment except Financial Emergency.
- **Quorum / benches:** ordinary **2–3**. Constitution Bench: **≥5**. *Kesavananda* sat **13** (largest).
- **CJI — convention:** senior-most SC judge. **Broken twice:** **1973 A.N. Ray** (superseded Shelat, Hegde, Grover after *Kesavananda*); **1977 M.H. Beg** (superseded **H.R. Khanna**). Seniority restored **1978 (Y.V. Chandrachud)**.

## Appointment & Collegium — Judges Cases

**Text of Art. 124(2):** every SC judge is appointed by the **President** (warrant under hand and seal) after **consultation** with such SC/HC judges as he deems necessary. For a judge other than the CJI, the **CJI must always be consulted**.

“Consultation” was defined by case law — **not** by the original text. The Collegium is **extra-constitutional** (judge-made).

- **First Judges** (*S.P. Gupta*, **1981**): consultation ≠ concurrence. **Executive primacy.** Transfer of HC judges without consent upheld (later narrowed).
- **Second Judges** (*SCAORA*, **1993**): consultation = **concurrence**. **CJI primacy** (CJI as *institution*, not an individual). Collegium born: CJI + **2** senior-most SC judges. Government **bound** if Collegium reiterates.
- **Third Judges** (Art. **143** reference, **1998**): **SC appointments: CJI + 4.** **HC appointments / transfers: CJI + 2.** Also consult the CJ + 2 senior colleagues of that HC; and the senior-most SC judge from the recommendee’s parent HC. **Plurality** — if **two or more** Collegium judges dissent, the name is **not** sent. CJI’s solo opinion is not “consultation”.
- **NJAC / Fourth Judges** (*SCAORA*, **16 Oct 2015**): **99th Amd + NJAC Act 2014** struck down **4:1** (Khehar majority; **J. Chelameswar** dissent). Violated **basic structure** (judicial independence). Collegium **revived**.

### NJAC as it stood (must-know composition)

- **Chair:** **CJI** (ex officio).
- **Members:** two **senior-most SC judges**; Union **Law Minister**; two **eminent persons** — chosen by **PM + CJI + LoP in LS** (or leader of the largest opposition party). **One** of the two from SC/ST/OBC/Minority/**Women**. Tenure **3 years**, no re-nomination.
- **Veto:** if **any two** members do not agree, the appointment is **not** made.

> **Exam note:** Chronology **1981 executive → 1993 CJI+2 → 1998 CJI+4 → 2014 NJAC in the text → 2015 struck down**. Collegium is **not** in the Constitution; NJAC **was**, and still fell.

**MoP after 2015:** Collegium recommends → Law Ministry / IB inputs → PM advises President. Government may **return once** with reasons; **reiteration binds** the executive.

**CJI:** outgoing CJI recommends the **senior-most**. Appointment still formally by the **President**.

## Transfer of judges (Art. 222)

- **President** may transfer an HC judge from one HC to another **after consultation with the CJI** (= Collegium after 1993/98).
- Consent of the judge is **not** required (*Union of India v. Sankalchand Sheth*; Second Judges). Transfer is **not**, in law, a punishment — but it is **justiciable** if shown to be punitive / mala fide.
- Art. 222 is **High Court only**. SC judges are **not** “transferred”.
- **Policy:** the CJ of an HC is ordinarily an **outsider** (transferred CJ). Compensatory allowance for transferred judges — **15th Amendment**.
- For a transfer, the Collegium consults the CJs of **both** HCs (from and to).

## Removal / impeachment of judges (Arts. 124(4)–(5), 217(1)(b), 218)

Same process for **SC and HC**. Popularly “impeachment”; constitutionally it is an **address to the President** — **not** Art. 61.

- **Grounds:** **proved misbehaviour** or **incapacity** only — no other ground.
- **Motion** (Judges (Inquiry) Act, **1968**): **100 LS** or **50 RS** members; Speaker/Chairman may admit or refuse.
- **Inquiry committee (3):** an **SC judge** + an **HC Chief Justice** + a **distinguished jurist**. If the committee **clears** the judge, the motion **drops**.
- **Each House:** **special majority** = majority of **total membership** + **2/3 of members present and voting** (same formula as Art. 368’s first limb, **not** a State-ratification amendment).
- **Same session:** both Houses must pass the address in the **same session**.
- **Order:** **President** issues the order of removal.
- **In-house (1997):** internal inquiry for misconduct **short of** removal — not in the Constitution.

**Never removed.**
- **V. Ramaswami** (1993) — committee found guilty; **LS motion failed** (Congress abstained).
- **Soumitra Sen** (Calcutta HC, 2011) — **RS passed**; he **resigned** before LS voted.
- **CJI Dipak Misra** (2018) — RS notice; Vice-President (Chairman) **did not admit**.

> **Exam note:** Resignation → **President**. Removal → **President after parliamentary address**. The **CJI cannot** remove a judge.

## Independence of the judiciary

Constitutional shields so the executive cannot pack, starve, or discuss the courts at will.

- **Mode of appointment:** Collegium — executive cannot pack the court alone.
- **Security of tenure:** removed only by 124(4) address.
- **Fixed service conditions:** salary **charged**; no cut after appointment except **360**.
- **Charged expenditure:** SC salaries + **Art. 146** admin expenses on **CFI**. HC **salaries on State CFI**; **pensions on CFI (India)** — Art. **221**.
- **Conduct not discussed:** Parliament **121** / State legislature **211** — except on a removal motion.
- **Ban on practice:** **124(7):** retired **SC** judge — **no court / no authority in India**. **220:** retired **HC** judge — not before **that** HC or its subordinates; **may** before SC and **other** HCs (7th Amd replaced an absolute ban).
- **Contempt:** **129 / 215**.
- **Own staff:** SC: **CJI** (146). HC: **Chief Justice** (229).
- **Jurisdiction not easily cut:** Parliament cannot take away 32; 42nd tried (32A/131A/226A) and **43rd restored**.
- **Separation:** Art. **50**; CrPC 1973 separated executive magistrates from judicial in most States.
- **Power to make rules:** SC **145** (with President’s approval); HC **225** / its own rules.

## Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court

Four buckets in the exam: **original · writ · appellate · advisory**, plus **review / SLP / other**.

### 1. Original — three different originals (do not mix)

- **Federal (Art. 131) — exclusive:** Union↔State; Union+States↔States; State↔State. Question of law/fact on which a **legal right** depends.
- **Writ (Art. 32) — not exclusive** (HCs also 226): FR enforcement only.
- **President / VP election (Art. 71) — exclusive:** inquiry & decision of the **SC**. If election later voided, acts done **before the date of the decision remain valid**. Parliament may regulate by law.

**Art. 131 — cannot be used for**
- Enforcement of **FRs** (→ 32)
- Union/State **vs a private person** (or a PSU merely because the government holds shares — *State of Bihar v. UoI*)
- Disputes arising from a **pre-Constitution treaty / agreement / sanad** (proviso to 131) — those go, if at all, under **143(2)**

**Art. 262 — water (classic trap):** Parliament **may** provide a tribunal for inter-State river disputes and **may bar** the SC and other courts. The **Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 did bar** 131. So “SC has original jurisdiction over river-water disputes” is **false**.

### 2. Writ — Art. 32
- Itself a **Fundamental Right**. Five writs **for FR only**.  
- **32(3):** Parliament may empower **any other court** to issue the same writs within its local limits — not generally used.  
- **32(4):** shall **not be suspended** except as otherwise provided (i.e. **Art. 359**).  
- **32 = FR only; 226 = FR + any other legal right.** Five writs: Habeas Corpus · Mandamus · Prohibition · Certiorari · Quo Warranto.

### 3. Appellate — Arts. 132–134A

- **Art. 132 (from HC):** civil, criminal **or** other — HC certifies a **substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution**.
- **Art. 133 (from HC):** **civil** — substantial question of law of general importance **and** HC opinion that SC should decide it. **30th Amd (1972)** removed the old **₹20,000** pecuniary floor.
- **Art. 134 (from HC):** **criminal** — (a) HC on appeal **reverses an acquittal** and sentences to **death**, or (b) HC **withdraws** a case from below and sentences to death, or (c) HC **certifies** it as fit.
- **Art. 134A:** **44th Amd (1978)** — HC **must consider** a 132/133/134 certificate as soon as the judgment is delivered.
- **Art. 134(2):** Parliament may enlarge criminal appellate power — **SC (Enlargement of Criminal Appellate Jurisdiction) Act, 1970** (life imprisonment / 10+ years also as of right in specified cases).

Certificate is **not** needed if the party goes under **136** (SLP).

### 4. Special Leave Petition — Art. 136 (widest)

- SC **may** grant leave from **any** judgment, decree, determination, sentence or order of **any court or tribunal** in India — not only “final” orders.
- **Not a right** — leave is discretionary. The widest appellate window in the Constitution.
- **Does not lie** against a court/tribunal of the **armed forces**.
- Can leapfrog the HC; can even be from a tribunal that is not a “court”.

### 5. Review — Art. 137
- SC reviews **its own** judgment (SC Rules; analogous to Order XLVII CPC): **error apparent on the face of the record**; discovery of new evidence not earlier available despite diligence; other sufficient reason.
- Not a rehearing on merits. Typically a **smaller bench**.
- **Curative petition** (after review fails) — *Rupa Ashok Hurra* (2002) — see below.

### 6. Advisory — Art. 143

| | **143(1)** | **143(2)** |
|--|-----------|-----------|
| Who | **President** (CoM advice) | **President** |
| What | Question of **law or fact** of public importance | Dispute from a **pre-Constitution treaty / agreement** (the 131-proviso matters) |
| Can SC decline? | **Yes** | **No** — “the Court **shall** … report” |
| Binding? | **Not binding** (followed in practice) | Same — still advisory |
| Bench | **≥5** if it is a substantial constitutional question (145(3)) | Same |

**Famous 143 references (match-list):** *Delhi Laws Act* (1951) · *Berubari* (1960, cession needs Amd) · *Keshav Singh* (1965, privileges) · *Presidential Poll* (1974) · *Special Courts Bill* (1978) · *Cauvery* (1992) · **Third Judges** (1998) · *Gujarat Assembly election* (2002) · *2G / natural resources* (2012). NJAC was **litigation**, not 143.

### Other / residual
- **138 / 139 / 140:** Parliament may enlarge jurisdiction, confer extra writs, grant supplemental powers.
- **139A (44th):** SC may transfer HC cases involving the **same question of law**.
- **317:** President removes a **UPSC / SPSC** member **after an SC inquiry** — not “impeachment”.
- **329 / RPA:** election petitions of **MP/MLA** lie in the **High Court**, **not** the SC. Only **President/VP** elections are SC original (**71**).

### 42nd / 43rd / 44th — judiciary

Emergency clipped judicial review; Janata restored it. **Not** a before/after pair table — three successive amendments.

- **42nd (1976):** inserted **32A** (SC barred from considering validity of **State** laws), **131A** (SC **exclusive** on **Central** laws), **226A** (HC barred from Central laws), **228A**; **narrowed 226**; made **227 administrative-only**.
- **43rd (1977):** **repealed 32A, 131A, 226A** — HCs **again** examine Central laws.
- **44th (1978):** restored **226** and **227** fully; added **134A** (certificate) and **139A** (transfer of cases).

> **Exam note:** 2019 A/R “SC has **exclusive** jurisdiction on constitutional validity of Central laws” is **false** because **43rd** killed **131A**.

## High Courts — Arts. 214–231 (elaboration)

**Part VI, Ch. V:** a High Court for each State — writs **wider** than the SC’s, age **62**.

- **Art. 214:** HC for each State — there **shall be** a High Court for each State.
- **Art. 215:** court of record — same idea as 129.
- **Art. 216:** constitution of HC — a CJ + such other judges as the **President** may appoint. **No ceiling** in the Constitution.
- **Art. 217:** appointment / tenure — President; age **62**; resign to **President**; removal via 124(4).
- **Art. 218:** removal procedure — 124(4) and 124(5) apply to HC judges.
- **Art. 219:** oath — before the **Governor** (common HC: Governor specified by the President).
- **Art. 220:** restriction on practice — after the **7th Amd**: not before **that** HC or courts subordinate; **may** before SC and other HCs.
- **Art. 221:** salaries — determined by Parliament. **Charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State.** **Pensions charged on the CFI (India)**.
- **Art. 222:** transfer — President after consulting CJI. Consent **not** required.
- **Art. 223:** Acting CJ — President; usually the senior-most HC judge when the CJ is absent / vacant.
- **Art. 224:** additional / acting judges — **Additional:** temporary increase of business or arrears; term **≤ 2 years** and must vacate at **62**. **Acting:** when a judge is absent / acting as CJ.
- **Art. 224A:** retired judges sit — **15th Amd** — CJ of the HC, with President’s previous consent, requests a retired HC judge to sit.
- **Art. 225:** existing jurisdiction — pre-Constitution jurisdiction of each HC continues, subject to the Constitution and law.
- **Art. 226:** writs — FR **+ any other purpose**. **Wider than 32.** **15th Amd:** cause of action wholly or in part in the territory.
- **Art. 227:** superintendence — all courts **and tribunals** in the territory **except armed forces**. Admin + judicial (full power **restored by 44th**; 42nd had made it admin-only).
- **Art. 228:** pull-up — HC **may withdraw** a subordinate-court case involving a substantial question of constitutional interpretation and decide it.
- **Art. 229:** officers — appointed by the **Chief Justice** of the HC. Expenses charged on the **State CFI**.
- **Art. 230:** Union territories — Parliament may extend an HC’s jurisdiction to a UT, or exclude a UT.
- **Art. 231:** common HC — Parliament **by law** (ordinary) — common HC for two or more States / for a State and a UT. **Not** a 368 amendment.

## Jurisdiction of a High Court

**226 is the engine**; **227** is superintendence — neither is a substitute for appeal.

- **Original (225 + charter/statute):** Chartered HCs: original civil for the presidency town. Company, matrimonial, admiralty, contempt, **226 writs** (the big original).
- **Writ (226):** FR **+ any other legal right**. Discretionary (laches, alternative remedy). **15th Amd:** if the **cause of action** arises wholly or in part inside the territory, the HC can issue even if the authority sits outside. **226(4):** does **not** affect Art. **32**.
- **Appellate:** from district / sessions / other courts as law provides. Letters-patent appeals in chartered HCs.
- **Supervisory (227):** all courts **and tribunals** in the territory **except armed forces**. Both **administrative and judicial** (44th). Not a substitute for appeal.
- **Court of record (215):** records + contempt of itself.
- **Control of subordinate courts (235):** posting, promotion, leave of **district judges** and the judicial service — **HC’s control**. Appointment of district judges is **233** (Governor with HC consult) — see Subordinate Courts below.
- **Art. 228:** withdraw a constitutional question from a subordinate court.

### SC writ vs HC writ

| | Art. 32 (SC) | Art. 226 (HC) |
|--|--------------|----------------|
| Nature | **Fundamental Right** | Constitutional right (**not** a FR) |
| Scope | **FR only** | FR **+ any other legal right** |
| Territorial | All-India | Territory / **cause of action** (15th) |
| Against | State (Art. 12) | State; some writs (habeas, quo warranto) can reach private persons |
| Refusal | SC ordinarily **cannot** refuse if an FR is breached | HC **may** refuse (discretion) |
| Suspend | Only **359** | Not a FR — but 226 itself was **narrowed by 42nd, restored by 44th** |

## Judicial Review

Power of courts to test **constitutionality** of legislative and executive action and strike down what is ultra vires. Itself **basic structure** (*Kesavananda* 1973; *Minerva Mills* 1980; *L. Chandra Kumar* 1997). **Not one article** — scattered.

- **Art. 13:** laws inconsistent with FRs are **void**.
- **Arts. 32 / 226:** writs — the operational engine.
- **Arts. 131 / 136 / 143:** federal original / SLP / advisory.
- **Arts. 245–246 / 251 / 254:** competence; repugnancy.
- **Art. 227:** HC superintendence of courts **and tribunals**.
- **Art. 372:** pre-Constitution laws continue **subject to** the Constitution.

**Three slices:** review of **legislation** · of **administrative action** · of **judicial decisions** (appeal / revision / 136).

**JR ≠ appeal.** Appeal rehear **merits** (error of fact/law). JR asks only: did the authority have **jurisdiction**, follow **procedure**, and stay inside the **Constitution**? Administrative-law grounds (exam shorthand): **illegality · irrationality (Wednesbury) · procedural impropriety**.

| India | USA | UK |
|-------|-----|-----|
| **Explicit** (13, 32, 226…) | *Marbury v. Madison* (implied) | **Parliamentary supremacy** — no JR of primary Acts |
| JR = **basic structure** | No “basic structure” label | — |

- ***Kesavananda* (1973):** JR of **constitutional amendments** (basic structure).
- ***Minerva Mills* (1980):** JR itself is basic structure; 42nd’s 31C over-reach struck.
- ***I.R. Coelho* (2007):** laws in the **9th Schedule** after 24 Apr 1973 still open to JR if they damage basic structure.
- ***S.P. Sampath Kumar* (1987):** CAT treated as an **effective substitute** for the HC.
- ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997):** overruled the substitute theory: **226/227 over tribunals = basic structure**. Tribunals = **courts of first instance** only.

> **Exam note:** 42nd clipped JR (**32A / 131A / 226A**); **43rd** restored. A tribunal clause that “excludes all courts except the SC” **cannot** oust the HC.

## Judicial Activism vs Judicial Restraint vs Overreach

Term **“judicial activism”**: Arthur **Schlesinger Jr., 1947** (USA). In India it is **post-Emergency**, via PIL + Art. **21** expansion + Art. **142**. **Not** in the Constitution.

| | **Activism** | **Restraint** | **Overreach** |
|--|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| Meaning | Court fills a **gap** where the other organs have failed | Court **defers**; decides only what is necessary | Court **takes over** legislative/executive policy |
| Vehicle | PIL, 142, expansive **21** | Literal reading; *A.K. Gopalan* | Continuing mandamus that runs a ministry |
| Examples | *Maneka*; *Hussainara*; *Vishaka*; *M.C. Mehta*; *Olga Tellis* | Refusal to rewrite a tax statute | Highway liquor ban / some firecracker / anthem directions (criticised as policy) |

**Art. 21 expansions that are activism-locks (match):** speedy trial · legal aid · livelihood (*Olga Tellis*) · privacy (*Puttaswamy*) · clean environment (*M.C. Mehta*) · dignity · emergency medical care (*Parmanand Katara*).

> **Exam note:** Activism can be **constitutional** (enforcing FRs). Overreach is activism that **violates separation of powers**. Restraint is a **doctrine of self-limit**, not an article. *ADM Jabalpur* is **bad restraint** (Emergency habeas).

## PIL (Public Interest Litigation)

**Not in the text.** Judge-made through **32** and **226**. USA 1960s civil-rights litigation is the cousin; India took it up **after the Emergency**.

**Traditional lis vs PIL**

| | Traditional | PIL |
|--|-------------|-----|
| Who | Aggrieved person only (**locus standi**) | Any **public-spirited** person / NGO; court may act **suo motu** |
| Form | Formal petition | Even a **letter / postcard** (**epistolary jurisdiction**) |
| Character | Adversarial | **Inquisitorial / collaborative** (court, amicus, reports) |
| Relief | Personal | For a **class** of victims who cannot come to court |
| Against | Private or State | Primarily **State / Art. 12 authorities** — not a private quarrel |

**Features (raata)**
1. Relaxed locus standi.  
2. Epistolary + **suo motu**.  
3. Inquisitorial (commissions, NHRC, CPCB reports).  
4. Preventive **and** remedial.  
5. Directed at **public injury**, not private gain.  
6. Court may appoint **amicus curiae**.  
7. Continuing mandamus (monitor compliance).  
8. Costs / dismissal for **frivolous** PILs.

**Who / what is not PIL (SC 1998 guidelines — exam “cannot”)**  
Personal / political / publicity interest · landlord–tenant · service matter of an individual · admission to a college · recovery of a loan · a dispute already in a competent court.

- ***Mumbai Kamgar Sabha* (1976):** Krishna Iyer — precursor.
- ***Hussainara Khatoon* (1979):** undertrials; **speedy trial** = Art. 21. First big PIL.
- ***Sunil Batra* (1978/80):** prisoners’ rights.
- ***PUDR* (Asiad workers, 1982):** bonded / contract labour.
- ***S.P. Gupta* (1981):** any member of the public with **sufficient interest**.
- ***Bandhua Mukti Morcha* (1984):** bonded labour; letter = petition.
- ***Olga Tellis* (1985):** livelihood of pavement dwellers.
- ***Sheela Barse* (1983):** women prisoners.
- ***M.C. Mehta* (Oleum / Ganga / Taj / CNG, 1986–):** environment; **absolute liability**; polluter pays.
- ***Vishaka* (1997):** workplace sexual harassment — guidelines till a statute.
- ***Common Cause* (various):** blood banks, roads, etc.
- **Fathers:** **P.N. Bhagwati** + **V.R. Krishna Iyer**.
- **Exam CJI (2018):** **Bhagwati**. PIL as a device took off under **Chandrachud** (*Hussainara* 1979); that name was **not** in the options.
- **Bhagwati as CJI:** **12 Jul 1985 – 20 Dec 1986**.

## Review petition & Curative petition

| | **Review (Art. 137)** | **Curative** |
|--|----------------------|--------------|
| Source | **137** + SC Rules (Order XLVII analogue) | Judge-made — ***Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra* (2002)** |
| When | Against the SC’s **own** judgment | **Only after review is dismissed** — last constitutional resort |
| Time | Ordinarily **30 days** | No separate article; still a rarity |
| Grounds | (i) error **apparent on the face of the record** (ii) new evidence not earlier available despite diligence (iii) other sufficient reason. **Not** a rehearing on merits | (i) **violation of natural justice** (not heard) (ii) **bias** of a judge who sat (iii) abuse of process of the court |

> **Exam note:** Exhaustion order: **appeal / SLP → review → curative**. There is **no** “curative article”. A second review is not the same thing as a curative.

## Subordinate Courts (Arts. 233–237)

**Part VI, Ch. VI.** The “district judiciary” — **not** “lower” in independence; only in hierarchy.

- **Art. 233(1):** district judges — **appointed by the Governor** in **consultation with the High Court**.
- **Art. 233(2):** direct recruit from the Bar — a person **not already** in Union/State service: **advocate for 7 years** **and** **recommended by the HC**. Promotees from the judicial service do **not** need 7 years at the Bar.
- **Art. 233A:** validation — **20th Amd (1966)** — certain DJ appointments made without HC consultation were validated.
- **Art. 234:** other than DJ — recruitment to the **State judicial service** (below DJ): Governor after consulting **HC and SPSC**. Two streams in practice: **direct from Bar** + **promotees**.
- **Art. 235:** **control** over district courts and courts subordinate is **vested in the High Court** — posting, promotion, leave, transfer, discipline of DJs **and** of the judicial service below DJ.
- **Art. 236 “district judge” includes:** judge of a **city civil court**; additional / joint / assistant DJ; chief judge of a **small-cause** court; **sessions / additional / assistant sessions** judge; chief (additional) presidency magistrate.
- **Art. 236 “judicial service”:** service consisting of persons filling the post of DJ and other **civil judicial** posts **inferior to DJ**.
- **Art. 237:** magistrates — Governor may **direct** that 233–235 apply to certain classes of **magistrates** (with specified exceptions).

> **Exam note:** **Appointment of DJ = Governor + HC (233). Control after appointment = HC alone (235).** The Governor has **no** disciplinary role over the district judiciary. *All India Judges’ Association* cases: uniform service conditions; Centre was asked to consider an AIJS.

### District Courts — hierarchy & work

```
SC
 └─ High Court
     └─ District & Sessions Judge     ← SAME person: civil = DJ; criminal = Sessions
         ├─ Civil Judge (Senior Division)  ≈  Chief Judicial Magistrate
         └─ Civil Judge (Junior Division)  ≈  Judicial Magistrate 1st / 2nd class
```

- **District & Sessions:** civil = principal court of **original** jurisdiction; appeals from junior civil judges. Criminal = **Sessions** trials (offences punishable with death / life / ≥10 years, as the Code provides); appeals from magistrates.
- **Civil Judge (Sr) / CJM:** higher pecuniary original civil; CJM trials up to **7 years** (CrPC).
- **Civil Judge (Jr) / JM:** lowest original civil; JM-I / JM-II petty crime.

- **CrPC 1973** separated **judicial** magistrates from **executive** magistrates (Art. **50**). Executive magistrates (DM/SDM) do **preventive** / law-and-order work, not regular trials.
- Appeals: civil Jr → Sr/DJ → HC → SC; criminal JM → Sessions → HC → SC (with 136 always available).

### Family Courts Act, **1984**
- State Government **in consultation with the HC**. Every district with population **≥ 1 million** *shall* have a Family Court (others *may*).
- Marriage, divorce, custody, maintenance, property between spouses. **Conciliation first.** Not bound by the straitjacket of the CPC; counsellors sit with the court.
- Judge = rank of a **District Judge** / equivalent.

### All-India Judicial Service (Art. **312**)
- **42nd Amd** inserted “including an **all-India judicial service**” in 312.
- Rajya Sabha resolution by **2/3 of members present and voting** → Parliament **by law**. That law **may amend Ch. VI of Part VI** and is **not** a 368 amendment.
- **Cannot** include any post **inferior to district judge** (312(3) read with 236).
- **Not created till date.** Law Commission (1st / 14th) recommended; *All India Judges’ Assn.* (1992) directed the Centre to consider it; 1993 review left timing to the executive.

## Tribunals — Part XIVA (Arts. 323A–323B)

**42nd Amendment, 1976** inserted **Part XIVA**. (2023 trap: Tribunals ≠ **Part XI**. XI = Union–State relations.)

Why tribunals: specialised, faster, take load off HCs. They are **not** a parallel Supreme Court.

| | **Art. 323A** | **Art. 323B** |
|--|---------------|---------------|
| Subject | **Administrative** tribunals — Union / State **public services** | **Other** tribunals |
| Who may legislate | **Only Parliament** | **Parliament or a State legislature** (for matters in its list) |
| Coverage | Service matters of persons appointed to Union/State public services and posts | **Full 323B list:** levy/collection/enforcement of any **tax**; foreign exchange / import-export; **industrial and labour**; **land reforms**; urban ceiling; **elections to the legislature** (not President/VP/MP election-petitions under 71/329); production/supply of **foodstuffs** and other essential goods; rent/tenancy of urban property; and connected offences |
| Text on courts | May exclude **all courts except the SC** | Same in the text |
| After *L. Chandra Kumar* | Exclusion of **HC (226/227) is void**. Tribunal = **court of first instance** → HC **Division Bench** → SC | Same logic |

**Case pair:** *Sampath Kumar* (1987) = CAT as **substitute** of the HC. *L. Chandra Kumar* (1997) = **supplement**, not substitute; JR of tribunals is **basic structure**. *Madras Bar Association* cases later insisted on judicial majority / independence of NCLT & other tribunals. **Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021** (4-year term etc.) — several clauses struck / read down; don’t treat the 2021 Act as the last word.

### CAT / SAT / JAT (Administrative Tribunals Act, **1985**)

- **CAT:** **1 Nov 1985**. Principal Bench **New Delhi**. Benches + circuit benches (originally 5; now ~19 + circuits).
- **Covers:** All-India Services, Union civil services, civil posts under the Union, and **notified** organisations (incl. NCT of Delhi staff at the Principal Bench).
- **Does not cover:** **armed forces**; officers and servants of the **Supreme Court**; **secretarial staff of Parliament** (and analogous State-legislature staff for a SAT).
- **SAT:** Central Government **on a State’s request**. Chairman / members: **President** after consulting the **Governor**.
- **JAT:** one tribunal for **two or more** States.
- **Composition:** **Chairman** (is / has been an **HC judge**) + judicial members + administrative members.
- **Appeal path now:** CAT/SAT → **HC (226/227)** → SC. Direct “only SLP” path was the **pre-1997** design.

### Other tribunals (year / parent Act — match)

- **ITAT (Income Tax):** **1941** — oldest; 323B tax.
- **CAT:** Administrative Tribunals Act **1985** (323A).
- **NGT:** **National Green Tribunal Act, 2010**. Principal Bench **Delhi**; others Pune, Bhopal, Chennai, Kolkata. Chairperson = SC/HC judge.
- **AFT:** **Armed Forces Tribunal Act, 2007** — the reason CAT **excludes** armed forces. Appeal to SC.
- **NCLT / NCLAT:** Companies Act; NCLT from **2016**. Company law + IBC.
- **DRT / DRAT:** Recovery of Debts (banks/FIs), **1993**.
- **SAT (SEBI):** Securities Appellate Tribunal — capital markets (do **not** mix with State Administrative Tribunal).
- **NCDRC:** consumer — National Commission (not a 323A body).

## Legal aid, Lok Adalat, Gram Nyayalaya, Fast Track Courts

**Art. 39A** (42nd, 1976): equal justice and **free legal aid**. Statute: **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** (came into force **9 Nov 1995** = **National Legal Services Day**).

- **NALSA:** Patron-in-Chief **CJI**. Executive Chairman: a serving SC judge nominated by the CJI.
- **State Legal Services Authority:** Patron **HC CJ**. Executive Chairman: a serving HC judge.
- **District Legal Services Authority:** **District Judge** as Chairman.
- **Taluk Legal Services Committee:** senior-most judicial officer in the taluk.

**Who gets free legal aid (1987 Act — typical list):** women and children · SC/ST · industrial workmen · victims of disaster / trafficking / mass disaster · persons in custody · persons with disability · persons with income below the notified ceiling.

### Lok Adalat vs Permanent Lok Adalat

| | **Lok Adalat** | **Permanent Lok Adalat (2002, Ch. VIA)** |
|--|----------------|------------------------------------------|
| First (independent India) | **Gujarat, 1982** (statutory only from 1987) | Statutory from the **2002** amendment |
| Cases | **Pending** in a court **and** pre-litigation | **Pre-litigation only** |
| Subject | Civil + **compoundable** criminal | **Public utility services** only: transport, posts, telegraph, power/water, sanitation, hospitals, insurance, education… |
| Pecuniary cap | **None** | **₹1 crore** |
| If no settlement | Returned to the court — **cannot** impose a decision | **May decide on merits** |
| Award | **= civil-court decree. Final. No appeal.** (226 still open) | Same |
| Bench | Sitting/retired judicial officer + 2 others | Chairperson (**qualified to be a DJ**) + 2 persons with public-utility experience |
| Court fee | **Refunded** on settlement | Pre-litigation — no court fee in the same sense |

**Types of Lok Adalat (names):** National · State · District · Taluk · Permanent · Mega / National Lok Adalat days · Mobile.

> **Exam note:** Regular Lok Adalat = **compromise only**. PLA = the one forum that can **adjudicate**. Do not mix **SAT (SEBI)** with **SAT (State Administrative Tribunal)** or with Lok Adalat.

### Gram Nyayalaya (Gram Nyayalayas Act, **2008**)

A **court** at the intermediate-Panchayat HQ — **not** a Gram Panchayat / Nyaya Panchayat.

- **Why:** Law Commission **114th Report** — justice at the doorstep (Art. 39A).
- **In force:** **2 October 2009**.
- **Seat:** HQ of the **intermediate Panchayat** (or a group of contiguous GPs). **Mobile** sittings in villages.
- **Nyayadhikari:** rank of **JM 1st class / Civil Judge (Junior Division)**. Appointed by the **State Government in consultation with the HC**.
- **Work:** both **civil (First Schedule)** and **criminal (Second Schedule)** — petty / specified. **Conciliation first**.
- **Procedure:** **not bound** by the Indian Evidence Act / straitjacket CPC–CrPC; guided by **natural justice**; summary.
- **Appeal:** **only one:** civil → **District Court**; criminal → **Sessions**. No second appeal on facts.
- **≠:** **not** a Gram Panchayat / Nyaya Panchayat. It is a **court**.

### Fast Track Courts

- **Origin:** **11th Finance Commission (2000)**; ~**1734** FTCs; long-pending **sessions** cases and undertrials.
- **Status:** **not** constitutional. Scheme judges = additional DJ / Sessions. States continue after the FC period.
- **Later:** dedicated **Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs)** from **2019** for **rape and POCSO** (DoJ + Nirbhaya Fund).

## Contempt of Court

**Inherent** in courts of record (**129 / 215**); the 1971 Act only **regulates**.

- **Constitution:** SC **129**, HC **215** — **inherent** as courts of record. Parliament may **regulate** (Entry 77 List I / Entry 14 List III) but **cannot take the power away**.
- **Statute:** **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971** (replaced 1952). Based on the **Sanyal Committee (1961)** (H.N. Sanyal, then Addl. SG).
- **Civil contempt:** wilful **disobedience** of a judgment / decree / order / undertaking to the court.
- **Criminal contempt:** publication / act that (i) **scandalises** or lowers the authority of the court, or (ii) **prejudices** a pending proceeding, or (iii) **interferes** with the administration of justice.
- **Punishment (s.12):** simple imprisonment up to **6 months**, or fine up to **₹2,000**, or both. Honest **apology** may purge.
- **Limitation (s.20):** no contempt proceeding after **one year** from the alleged act.
- **Cognizance of criminal contempt (s.15):** court **suo motu**, **or** motion by AG/SG (SC) / Advocate-General (HC), **or** any person **with written consent** of that law officer. **Suo motu needs no AG consent**.
- **2006 Amd:** **truth** is a defence if in **public interest** and bona fide.
- **Other defences:** innocent publication / distribution; fair and accurate report of a **decided** case; **fair criticism** of a judicial act; complaint in good faith to a superior.
- **Speech:** Art. **19(2)** lists **contempt of court** as a permitted restriction on 19(1)(a).
- **Subordinate courts:** **HC** punishes contempt of courts subordinate to it. A subordinate court **cannot** try its own contempt (it **refers** to the HC).
- ***SC Bar Assn.* (1998):** SC **cannot**, in contempt, **suspend an advocate’s licence** — that is for the **Bar Council**. It can punish under 129/142 but not take over professional-roll power.

> **Exam note:** 1971 Act is a **ceiling for HCs**; SC’s 129 power is **constitutional** and not confined by the Act, but the Act is used as a **guide**.

## ADR (basic)

**S. 89 CPC** (after 1999/2002 amendments): where it appears that there exist elements of a settlement, the court **shall** formulate terms and **may refer** the parties to one of five:

1. **Arbitration**  
2. **Conciliation**  
3. **Judicial settlement** including **Lok Adalat**  
4. **Mediation**  
5. (the fifth limb is judicial settlement / Lok Adalat as above — treat **five names** as: arbitration, conciliation, mediation, judicial settlement, Lok Adalat)

- **Negotiation:** parties themselves decide; binding only if they sign.
- **Mediation:** neutral **facilitates**; does **not** impose. Settlement agreement (Mediation Act **2023** gives it statutory force).
- **Conciliation:** neutral **may suggest** terms (1996 Act, Part III). Settlement agreement = arbitral award on agreed terms.
- **Arbitration:** arbitrator **adjudicates**. **Arbitral award** = decree (Arbitration and Conciliation Act **1996**, UNCITRAL model; major amds 2015 / 2019 / 2021).
- **Lok Adalat:** compromise (regular) / merits (PLA). Decree; **no appeal**.
- **Judicial settlement:** judge as settler; as a court order.

**Other locks:** Commercial Courts Act, 2015 — **pre-institution mediation** for specified commercial suits. Online / court-annexed mediation under the **2023** Act. Constitutional hook remains **Art. 39A**.

> **Exam note:** Arbitration is **adjudicatory**. Mediation / conciliation / regular Lok Adalat are **consensual**. **PLA** is the hybrid that can decide merits. **FTC is not ADR** — it is a regular court on a fast track.
