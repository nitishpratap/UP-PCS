## Regulating Act, 1773

**Governor-General of Bengal:** Warren Hastings (4-member Executive Council)

- First British Parliamentary statute to regulate East India Company territorial rule.
- Designated **Governor-General of Bengal** with a Council of four.
- Governors of **Bombay and Madras** made subordinate to Bengal in matters of war, revenue and diplomacy.
- **Supreme Court at Calcutta** established (**1774**); first Chief Justice **Sir Elijah Impey**.
- Court of Directors had to report on civil, military and revenue affairs to the British government.
- Restricted private trade and acceptance of presents by Company servants.
- Left ambiguity between Supreme Court jurisdiction and Governor-General-in-Council → later clarified by **Act of Settlement / Amending Act, 1781**.
- Did **not** create Governor-General of India (that comes in **1833**).

> **Exam note:** 1773 = GG of **Bengal** + SC Calcutta 1774; 1833 = GG of **India**.

---

## Pitt's India Act, 1784

- Created a **Board of Control** (6 members) for political supervision of Indian affairs by the British Crown/government.
- Established **Dual Government / Dual Control**:
  - **Board of Control** → political / territorial
  - **Court of Directors** → commercial
- Distinguished Company’s commercial functions from political functions more clearly.
- Used the key phrase **“British possessions in India.”**
- Reduced the Governor-General’s Council from 4 to **3** members.
- Strengthened British government supremacy over Company administration without ending Company rule.

> **Exam note:** Board = politics; Directors = trade.

---

## Charter Acts

### Charter Act, 1793
- Renewed Company charter; trade monopoly continued.
- Separated revenue administration more clearly; refined GG & Council powers.
- Commander-in-Chief not to be a member of the Council unless specially appointed.

### Charter Act, 1813
- Ended Company trade monopoly in India **except tea trade and trade with China**.
- Allowed Christian missionaries to enter India.
- Granted **₹1 lakh** annually for education / revival of literature and science.
- Asserted Crown’s sovereignty over Company territories more explicitly.

### Charter Act, 1833
- Created **Governor-General of India** (first: **Lord William Bentinck**) — all-India executive.
- Ended Company’s commercial functions completely; Company became a purely administrative/political body.
- Centralised legislation: GG-in-Council alone could legislate for all British India.
- Added a **Law Member** to the Council (**Lord Macaulay**).
- Provided for a **Law Commission** (Macaulay) to codify laws.
- Attempted to introduce open competition for civil services (not effectively implemented then).
- Attempted to include Indians in higher services in principle (Section 87 spirit) — limited in practice.

### Charter Act, 1853
- Introduced **open competition** for the **Indian Civil Service** (Macaulay Committee followed).
- Separated the **Legislative Council** from the Executive Council (local representation began in a limited way).
- Charter not renewed for a fixed 20-year period — Company rule left open-ended until 1858.
- Law Member became a full member of the Governor-General’s Executive Council.

> **Exam note:** Open competition for ICS = **1853**, not 1833 (1833 only proposed / spirit).

---

## Government of India Act, 1858

- After 1857, transferred government of India from Company to the **British Crown**.
- Created **Secretary of State for India** — a British Cabinet minister — assisted by a **Council of India** (initially 15 members).
- Governor-General also designated **Viceroy** as the Crown’s representative.
- Double government of Pitt’s system ended; Board of Control and Court of Directors abolished for Indian governance.
- Accompanied by **Queen’s Proclamation, 1 November 1858** (read at Allahabad by Canning): no further territorial annexation policy in the old style; equal treatment promises; respect for religions/customs.
- Indian administration now directly under British Parliament through the Secretary of State.

---

## Indian Councils Act, 1861

**Viceroy:** Lord Canning

- Viceroy could nominate Indians as non-official members of his expanded council; in **1862** Canning nominated three: **Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao**.
- Restored legislative powers of **Bombay and Madras** (beginning of legislative decentralisation).
- Provided for new Legislative Councils for Bengal (1862), North-Western Provinces (1886) and Punjab (1897).
- Viceroy could issue **Ordinances** valid for **6 months** in emergencies without the council’s consent.
- **Portfolio system:** Canning introduced departmental portfolios in practice (**1859**); the **1861 Act gave legal recognition** — each Executive Council member independently headed a department (replaced corporate functioning of the whole council).
- Indians associated with law-making by nomination — **no** elections, **no** responsible government.

## Indian Councils Act, 1892

**Viceroy:** Lord Lansdowne

- Increased the number of **non-official** members in Central and Provincial Legislative Councils; **official majority** retained at the Centre.
- Councils could **discuss the annual budget** and ask **questions** on matters of public interest (interpellation-type power).
- Introduced an element of **indirect election**, but the British avoided the word “election” — used **nomination on recommendation**:
  - **Central Council:** Viceroy on recommendations from Provincial Legislative Councils and Bengal Chamber of Commerce.
  - **Provincial Councils:** Governor on advice of District Boards, Municipalities, Universities, Trade Associations, Zamindars and Chambers.
- Still no power to vote out the executive; **no responsible government**.

---

## Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley–Minto Reforms)

**Viceroy:** Lord Minto II · **Secretary of State:** John Morley

- Imperial Legislative Council size raised (about **16 → 60**); official majority retained at the Centre.
- Provincial Legislative Councils enlarged; non-official majority possible in provinces; size varied.
- Members could ask **supplementary questions**, move resolutions on the budget; voting on the budget as a whole still not allowed.
- Introduced **separate electorate for Muslims** — only Muslim voters elect Muslim candidates; communal representation legalised. Lord Minto is called **“Father of Communal Electorate.”**
- First Indian appointed to the Viceroy’s Executive Council: **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** (Law Member).
- Separate representation also for Presidency Corporations, Chambers of Commerce, Universities and Zamindars.
- Aimed at association of Indians with the administration — **not** responsible government.

> **Exam note:** Muslim separate electorates begin with **1909**, not with the Communal Award of 1932.

---

## Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms)

**Viceroy:** Lord Chelmsford · Came into force **1921**

- Based on Edwin Montagu’s declaration of **20 August 1917** — gradual introduction of responsible government in India.
- Subjects classified into **Central** and **Provincial** under Devolution Rules.
- **Dyarchy in provinces** (dual rule):
  - **Reserved subjects** (e.g. police, justice, land revenue, irrigation) — Governor and Executive Councillors; **not** responsible to the legislature.
  - **Transferred subjects** (e.g. education, health, local self-government, agriculture) — Indian ministers responsible to the Legislative Council.
- At the Centre, replaced the Indian Legislative Council with a **bicameral** legislature:
  - **Council of State** (Upper House)
  - **Legislative Assembly** (Lower House)
  - Majority of members elected by **direct** election (limited franchise).
- Provided for **three Indian members** in the Viceroy’s Executive Council (excluding the Commander-in-Chief).
- Extended separate electorates to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans.
- Separated provincial budget from the central budget.
- Created office of **High Commissioner for India** in London (some functions of Secretary of State transferred).
- Provided for establishment of a **Public Service Commission** → Central PSC set up in **1926**.
- Proposed **Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal)** — formed **1921** with about **120** members (princes and representatives).
- Mandated a statutory commission after **10 years** to review the working of the Act → became the Simon Commission.

## Government of India Act, 1935

- Longest and most detailed British Indian constitutional statute; many features later entered the Constitution of India.
- Introduced **Provincial Autonomy** (in force from **1937**) — ended Dyarchy in the provinces; ministers responsible for provincial subjects.
- Proposed an **All-India Federation** of British Indian provinces + princely states — **never came into being** (states did not accede as required).
- Proposed **Dyarchy at the Centre** — **never operated**.
- Divided powers into **three lists**: Federal, Provincial and Concurrent (commonly remembered counts ~**59 / 54 / 36**).
- Residuary powers under the Act vested in the **Viceroy**.
- Provided a **Federal Court** (inaugurated **1937**) — forerunner path to the Supreme Court.
- Established **Reserve Bank of India**; separated **Burma** (1937) and **Aden** from India.
- Bicameral legislatures in six provinces; extended franchise further (still limited).
- Abolished the Council of India in London; created Advisers to the Secretary of State.
- Retained Governor’s special responsibilities / discretionary powers — real autonomy remained constrained.

> **Exam note:** Remember the trio — Autonomy **operated**; Federation and Centre Dyarchy **did not**.

## Indian Civil Service

- Higher civil service under Company/Crown known as **Covenanted Civil Service** (ICS); lower provincial service = uncovenanted.
- Early training associated with **Haileybury College** (England).
- Charter Act **1853** introduced **open competitive examination** for ICS.
- Exams long held in London; age limits and Indian entry were constant political issues.
- Indianisation of services was a major nationalist demand (Congress resolutions; Lee Commission 1924 later context).
- Under GOI Act 1919, Central Public Service Commission created (**1926**).
- After Independence, ICS continuum shaped the **IAS** (Constitutional / statutory framework later).

## Separate Electorates

- **1909 (Morley–Minto):** first introduced for **Muslims**.
- **1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford):** extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans.
- **Communal Award, 1932:** further extended separate electorates to **Depressed Classes** (among others).
- **Poona Pact, 1932:** replaced Depressed Classes’ separate electorates with **reserved seats in a joint electorate**.
- Separate electorates remained a major constitutional controversy up to Partition; criticised in Constituent Assembly debates.

## Simon Commission, 1927

- Statutory commission under **Sir John Simon** (7 members) as required by the 1919 Act.
- **All-British** composition — **no Indian member** → nationwide boycott (“Simon Go Back”).
- Lala Lajpat Rai injured in protest lathi-charge at Lahore (1928) — later died; major nationalist flashpoint.
- Report submitted **1930**: recommended abolition of Dyarchy; extension of responsible government in provinces; federation of British India and princely states; continuation of communal electorate.
- Boycott + political deadlock led toward Round Table Conferences.

## Nehru Report, 1928

- Draft constitution by an all-parties committee chaired by **Motilal Nehru** (Secretary: Jawaharlal Nehru).
- Recommended **Dominion Status** for India (not complete independence yet — debated inside Congress).
- Proposed fundamental rights, parliamentary system, and **joint electorates** (with reserved seats for minorities as formulated).
- Rejected separate electorates as a permanent communal solution.
- Muslim League rejected it → **Jinnah’s Fourteen Points (1929)**.
- Calcutta Congress (1928) accepted Dominion Status demand with a one-year ultimatum → later Lahore 1929 Purna Swaraj line.

## Round Table Conferences, Communal Award & Poona Pact

### Round Table Conferences (London)
- **1st RTC (Nov 1930–Jan 1931):** Congress absent (Civil Disobedience); princes and other parties attended.
- **Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 March 1931):** Congress agreed to join 2nd RTC; CDM suspended.
- **2nd RTC (Sep–Dec 1931):** Gandhi as sole Congress representative; also Sarojini Naidu, Madan Mohan Malaviya, etc.
- **Dr. Rajendra Prasad did NOT participate** in the 2nd RTC.
- Deadlock on communal representation.
- **3rd RTC (Nov–Dec 1932):** Congress absent again; limited attendance → White Paper (1933) → Joint Select Committee → **GOI Act 1935**.

## August Offer, Cripps Mission & Wavell Plan

### August Offer, 1940 (Viceroy Linlithgow)
- Offered dominion status after the war; expansion of Viceroy’s Executive Council; war advisory council.
- Assured that future constitution would not be framed without minority consent (League-friendly assurance).
- Congress rejected (no immediate independence / national government); League did not fully accept either.

### Cripps Mission, March 1942 (Stafford Cripps)
- Context: World War II, Japanese threat, need for Indian cooperation.
- Proposed Dominion Status after war; Constituent Assembly after war; provinces could **opt out** of the Union; defence with British during war.
- Congress rejected (no immediate transfer of power; opt-out could Balkanise); League rejected (no clear Pakistan).
- Failure → Quit India Movement (Aug 1942).

### Wavell Plan + Shimla Conference, June–July 1945
- Proposed balanced Executive Council with equal caste Hindu and Muslim representation (with some other seats).
- Shimla Conference failed mainly over League’s claim to nominate all Muslim members / parity politics.

## Cabinet Mission, Interim Government, Mountbatten Plan & Independence Act

### Cabinet Mission, 1946 (Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander)
- Rejected a fully sovereign Pakistan but proposed a **three-tier** structure and **Sections A / B / C** grouping.
- Weak Centre (defence, foreign affairs, communications); provinces/groups otherwise autonomous.
- Plan for a **Constituent Assembly** and an **Interim Government**.
- Initially accepted then politics shifted — League later withdrew acceptance of the Plan.

### Interim Government
- Announced on **24 August 1946**; assumed office **2 September 1946** (Nehru led the Executive Council).
- Muslim League later joined the Interim Government (October 1946) even after Plan disputes.

### Mountbatten Plan / 3 June Plan, 1947
- Partition of British India into **India** and **Pakistan**.
- Princely states free to accede to either dominion (paramountcy to lapse).
- Boundary commissions; power transfer by **15 August 1947**.

### Indian Independence Act, 18 July 1947
- Created two independent dominions from **15 August 1947**.
- Abolished the office of **Secretary of State for India**.
- Ended British **paramountcy** over princely states.
- Each dominion’s Constituent Assembly empowered to frame its constitution and act as legislature.
- Office of Governor-General continued temporarily for each dominion.

## Constituent Assembly

### Composition & constitution-making process
- Framed under **Cabinet Mission Plan** (not under a sovereign Independence Act initially).
- Total seats **389** = **296** (British India) + **93** (Princely States); after Partition about **299**.
- British Indian members mostly **indirectly elected** by Provincial Legislative Assemblies (proportional representation / communal seats as per plan).
- Princely representatives were nominated by rulers.
- First meeting: **9 December 1946** — temporary President **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha**.
- Permanent President: **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** — **11 December 1946**; Vice-President **H.C. Mukherjee**.
- **Objectives Resolution** moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** on **13 December 1946**; adopted **22 January 1947** — philosophical base of the Constitution (later reflected in Preamble).
- National Flag adopted by CA on **22 July 1947**.
- Worked through committees; Drafting Committee prepared the Draft Constitution; three readings in the Assembly.
- **Last meeting: 24 January 1950** — also adopted National Anthem; members signed the Constitution.
- First President of India: **Rajendra Prasad** · First Prime Minister: **Jawaharlal Nehru**.

### Adoption & Enforcement
- Constitution **adopted / enacted: 26 November 1949** (some provisions like citizenship, provisional Parliament etc. came into force immediately).
- Constitution **came into force fully: 26 January 1950** — Republic Day (chosen to honour Purna Swaraj pledge of **26 January 1930**).
- India became a Republic; Dr. Rajendra Prasad took oath as first President.

1. GG Bengal (1773) ≠ GG India (1833)
2. ICS open competition = **1853**
3. Portfolio = **1861** (not 1892/1909)
4. Muslim separate electorates start = **1909**
5. Chamber of Princes = **1919**
6. Dyarchy = **1919**; Autonomy = **1935**
7. 1935 Federation / Centre Dyarchy = never operated
8. Federal Court inauguration = **1937**
9. Communal Award = **1932** (not 1933)
10. Poona Pact = reserved seats + joint electorates
11. 2nd RTC: Gandhi yes; **Prasad no**
12. Cripps → Wavell/Shimla → Cabinet Mission
13. Interim announced **24 Aug 1946**
14. Residuary source = **Canada**; Concurrent = **Australia**
15. Rau = Adviser; Ambedkar = Drafting Chair
16. Flag **22 Jul 1947**; last CA meeting **24 Jan 1950**
17. Adopt **26 Nov 1949**; enforce **26 Jan 1950**
18. 15 Aug 1947 ≠ Republic Day
