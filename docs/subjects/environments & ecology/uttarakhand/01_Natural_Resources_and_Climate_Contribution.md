2. **Chipko** began in **1973** at **Reni (Chamoli)**. It is the classic UK people’s movement for forest protection.
3. Dehradun institutions: **FSI** (ISFR), **FRI** (forestry research), **WII** (wildlife science/training). Do not swap their jobs.
4. **NAPCC** includes the **National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem**.
5. UK rivers (Ganga–Yamuna headwaters, Bhagirathi, Alaknanda, and tributaries) feed the Gangetic water system.
6. Forests and alpine meadows act as **carbon sinks** and regulate local hydrology through interception and infiltration.
7. Glacier and snowmelt contribution to Himalayan rivers is a climate-vulnerability fact for UKPCS.
8. **Weathering** breaks rocks into sediments, clays, and soils at the Earth’s surface. Erosion then transports the loosened material.
9. National facts that UKPCS also asks: **NEERI = Nagpur 1958**; **WED = 5 June**; atmosphere **N₂ ~78%**; **EPA 1986**; **Tansley 1935** coined ecosystem; ecosystem is an **open** system.

11. Sustainable development, Agenda 21, Brundtland, and SDGs follow the same national sustainable-development spine.
12. UK biodiversity sits in the **Himalaya** biogeographic zone with high endemism and altitudinal vegetation belts.
13. Landslides and flash floods rise where deforestation, road cutting, and extreme rainfall combine — disaster–environment link.
14. Eco-tourism and hydropower both use natural capital; unmanaged expansion can degrade slopes and rivers.
15. **Article 48A / 51A(g)** and the right to a healthy environment under **Article 21** apply nationwide, including UK.
16. Protected-area names and district facts belong in the biodiversity UK chapter; this card keeps resource–climate focus.

## 1.1 Natural Resource Base of Uttarakhand

**Identity:** Forests | Water | Biodiversity | Minerals | Mountains as resource system

- Uttarakhand’s natural resource base is dominated by the **Himalayan land–water–forest system**.
- **Forests** provide timber, non-timber forest produce, wildlife habitat, carbon storage, and slope stability.
- **Water resources** include snow and glacier melt, springs, and major Ganga–Yamuna headstreams.
- Altitudinal vegetation belts from subtropical sal tracts to alpine meadows create high **biodiversity**.
- Minerals and stone quarrying exist locally, but uncontrolled extraction can worsen slope failure and dust pollution.
- Hydropower potential is high because of steep gradients, yet reservoir and tunnel projects need careful environmental clearance and disaster assessment.
- Tourism and pilgrimage economies depend on the same natural capital and can overload fragile ecosystems if unmanaged.

## 1.2 Contribution to National and International Climate Goals

**Identity:** Carbon sink | Water tower | Himalayan Mission | Vulnerability

- Himalayan forests and soils act as **carbon sinks** that support India’s NDC forest-sink target narrative.
- The region functions as a **water tower** for the Gangetic plains through snowmelt and monsoon runoff regulation.
- The **National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem** under **NAPCC** focuses on Himalayan science, livelihood resilience, and ecological security.
- Glacier retreat, changing snow patterns, and extreme rainfall raise flood and landslide risk for UK and downstream states.
- Afforestation, spring rejuvenation, and disaster-sensitive land-use planning are practical UK climate-adaptation tools.
- International climate politics (UNFCCC / Paris / Net Zero 2070) apply to India as a whole; UK’s role is through **ecosystem services** and **vulnerability reduction**, not a separate treaty track.

## 1.3 Chipko and People’s Resource Protection

**Identity:** 1973 Reni | Forest rights consciousness | Women-led protection image

- The **Chipko movement** began in **1973** at **Reni village, Chamoli district**, when villagers hugged trees to stop commercial felling.
- It became a national symbol of community forest protection and influenced later forest policy debates.
- Chipko links ecology with livelihoods: forests protect soil and water and sustain hill agriculture.
- Related UK people’s movements and forest struggles should be read with Modern India / UK history notes when the paper asks movement chronology.

## 1.4 Dehradun Institution Triangle

**Identity:** FSI ≠ FRI ≠ WII

- **Forest Survey of India (FSI, 1981)** maps forest cover and publishes **ISFR** every two years.
- **Forest Research Institute (FRI, 1906)** does forestry **research** under the ICFRE system. It does **not** publish ISFR.
- **Wildlife Institute of India (WII, 1982)** does wildlife research, officer training, and census methodology support.
- All three sit in **Dehradun**, so city-alone matching is not enough — match the **function**.

| Institute | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| FSI | Forest cover / ISFR |
| FRI | Forestry research |
| WII | Wildlife science + training |

## 1.5 Shared National Facts That UKPCS Also Asks

**Identity:** NEERI | WED | Atmosphere | EPA | Fukuoka | Weathering

- **NEERI** is at **Nagpur (1958)**, originally CPHERI — a repeated state-PCS HQ question.
- **World Environment Day** is **5 June** (Stockholm opening day, 1972).
- Dry air composition is about **Nitrogen 78%** and **Oxygen 21%**.
- India’s **Environment (Protection) Act** is **1986** (umbrella law after Bhopal).
- **Masanobu Fukuoka** is remembered for **natural farming**.
- **Weathering** is the natural breakdown of rocks into fine particles, sediments, clays, and soils. After weathering, **erosion** carries material away.
- Sustainable development, SDGs, Rio/Agenda 21, and Eco-mark follow Topics **15**, **18**, and **30**.
