## Consolidated — 15 Must-Score Facts (UK Protected Areas)

1. **Oldest National Park in India:** **Corbett National Park** was established in **1936** as *Hailey National Park* (later Ramganga NP in 1954, renamed Corbett in 1957); India’s first national park and the launch site of **Project Tiger on 1 April 1973**.
2. **Largest National Park in Uttarakhand:** **Gangotri National Park (2,390.02 sq. km)** located in **Uttarkashi**; covers Gaumukh glacier and high-altitude alpine terrain.
3. **Smallest National Park in Uttarakhand:** **Valley of Flowers National Park (87.50 sq. km)** in **Chamoli**; discovered in 1931 by British mountaineer Frank S. Smythe; Pushpawati river flows through it.
4. **Largest Wildlife Sanctuary in Uttarakhand:** **Kedarnath Wildlife Sanctuary (975.20 sq. km)** spread across **Chamoli and Rudraprayag**; created in **1972** primarily to conserve the endangered Himalayan Musk Deer.
5. **Smallest Wildlife Sanctuary in Uttarakhand:** **Mussoorie (Benog / Mountain Quail) Wildlife Sanctuary (10.82 sq. km)** in **Dehradun**; established in **1993**.
6. **Oldest Wildlife Sanctuary in Uttarakhand:** **Govind Wildlife Sanctuary (1955)** in **Uttarkashi**; covers 485.89 sq. km in the Tons river basin.
7. **Two Tiger Reserves of Uttarakhand:**
   - **Corbett Tiger Reserve:** Notified in **1973** (first in India).
   - **Rajaji Tiger Reserve:** Notified in **2015** (48th Tiger Reserve of India; second in UK).
8. **UNESCO World Natural Heritage Sites:** **Nanda Devi NP (1988)** and **Valley of Flowers NP (2005)** form **one composite UNESCO World Heritage property** in Chamoli.
9. **Single Biosphere Reserve:** **Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve** was established in **1988** (core area: Nanda Devi NP; buffer: Valley of Flowers); included in UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves (**MAB program**) in **2004**.
10. **First Ramsar Wetland Site in Uttarakhand:** **Asan Conservation Reserve (Dehradun)** was declared Uttarakhand’s **first Ramsar site in October 2020** (at confluence of Yamuna and Asan rivers; winter refuge for Ruddy Shelduck).
11. **Jhilmil Jheel Conservation Reserve (Haridwar):** Established in **2005** specifically for the conservation of the endangered **Swamp Deer (Barasingha — *Rucervus duvaucelii*)**.
12. **Musk Deer Breeding & Research Centres:**
    - **Mahroodi Musk Deer Research Centre:** Established in **1977** (in Bageshwar / Pithoragarh border).
    - **Kanchula Kharak Musk Deer Breeding Centre:** Established in **1982** near Chopta in **Chamoli district**.
    - **Askot Wildlife Sanctuary:** Established in **1986** in **Pithoragarh**; holds the highest density of Musk Deer.
13. **Rajaji National Park Amalgamation (1983):** Formed by amalgamating 3 contiguous wildlife sanctuaries: **Rajaji Sanctuary (1948)**, **Motichur Sanctuary (1964)**, and **Chilla Sanctuary (1977)**; named after Bharat Ratna C. Rajagopalachari.
14. **Snow Leopard Conservation:** Centred in **Gangotri NP**, **Govind NP**, and **Nanda Devi NP**; India's first Snow Leopard Conservation Centre was set up in **Lanka, Uttarkashi**.
15. **Total Protected Areas Tally:** Uttarakhand has **6 National Parks**, **7 Wildlife Sanctuaries**, and **4 Conservation Reserves**.

## 1. Master Table: The 6 National Parks of Uttarakhand

*UKPCS routinely tests chronological order of establishment, area comparisons, and district locations.*

```
                     Chronology of 6 National Parks
                                    │
       1936: Corbett National Park (520.82 sq. km - Pauri & Nainital)
                                    │
       1982: Nanda Devi National Park (624.60 sq. km - Chamoli)
                                    │
       1982: Valley of Flowers National Park (87.50 sq. km - Chamoli)
                                    │
       1983: Rajaji National Park (820.42 sq. km - Dehradun, Haridwar, Pauri)
                                    │
       1989: Gangotri National Park (2,390.02 sq. km - Uttarkashi)
                                    │
       1990: Govind National Park (472.08 sq. km - Uttarkashi)
```

| National Park | Year Est. | Area ($\text{km}^2$) | District(s) | Key Ecological Features & High-Yield Stems |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Corbett National Park** | **1936** | 520.82 | **Pauri Garhwal & Nainital** | **First National Park in India and Asia**; originally named *Hailey National Park* after Sir Malcolm Hailey; renamed *Ramganga NP* (1954), and *Jim Corbett NP* (1957); headquarters at **Ramnagar**; **Project Tiger launched here on 1 April 1973**; traversed by Ramganga and Mandal rivers; core valley: **Patli Dun**. |
| **Nanda Devi National Park** | **1982** | 624.60 | **Chamoli** | High-altitude wilderness surrounding Mount Nanda Devi (7,816 m); core of Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve; inscribed on **UNESCO World Natural Heritage in 1988**; fauna: Snow Leopard, Himalayan Musk Deer, Bharal. |
| **Valley of Flowers National Park**| **1982** | **87.50** *(Smallest)* | **Chamoli** | Discovered by Frank S. Smythe in 1931 (*The Valley of Flowers*); traversed by the **Pushpawati River**; home to over 500 species of alpine flowering herbs (Brahma Kamal, Blue Poppy); inscribed on **UNESCO World Heritage in 2005**. |
| **Rajaji National Park** | **1983** | 820.42 | **Dehradun, Haridwar & Pauri** | Amalgamated from 3 sanctuaries: Rajaji (1948), Motichur (1964), and Chilla (1977); named after **C. Rajagopalachari**; traversed by Ganga and Song rivers; prime habitat for Asian Elephants and Royal Bengal Tigers; **notified as 2nd Tiger Reserve of UK in 2015**. |
| **Gangotri National Park** | **1989** | **2,390.02** *(Largest)* | **Uttarkashi** | **Largest National Park in Uttarakhand**; encompasses the Gaumukh glacier, Bhagirathi origin, and high-altitude moraines; prominent fauna: Snow Leopard, Tibetan Argali, Himalayan Ibex, Musk Deer. |
| **Govind National Park** | **1990** | 472.08 | **Uttarkashi** | Encompasses the upper catchment of the **Tons River** and Har-ki-Dun valley; sanctuary portion launched **Project Snow Leopard in 1990**; rich in medicinal plants (Aconitum, Nardostachys). |

## 2. Master Table: The 7 Wildlife Sanctuaries of Uttarakhand

| Wildlife Sanctuary (WLS) | Year Est. | Area ($\text{km}^2$) | District | Key Ecological Characteristics & Species Focus |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Govind Wildlife Sanctuary** | **1955** | 485.89 | **Uttarkashi** | **Oldest wildlife sanctuary in Uttarakhand**; situated in Supin/Tons basin; Snow Leopard, Himalayan Tahr, Musk Deer, Monal. |
| **Kedarnath Wildlife Sanctuary**| **1972** | **975.20** *(Largest)* | **Chamoli & Rudraprayag** | **Largest wildlife sanctuary in Uttarakhand**; established primarily to protect the endangered **Himalayan Musk Deer (*Moschus chrysogaster*)**; Mandakini and Alaknanda upper catchments. |
| **Askot Wildlife Sanctuary** | **1986** | 600.00 | **Pithoragarh** | Dedicated Musk Deer sanctuary in eastern Kumaon bordering Nepal and Tibet; highest documented density of Musk Deer; alpine bugyals and oak forests. |
| **Sonanadi Wildlife Sanctuary** | **1987** | 301.18 | **Pauri Garhwal** | Located north of the Ramganga reservoir; serves as the vital biological corridor connecting Corbett and Rajaji national parks; dense Sal forests; Tigers, Elephants, Cheetal. |
| **Binsar Wildlife Sanctuary** | **1988** | 47.07 | **Almora** | Pristine temperate oak, rhododendron, and deodar forest atop Binsar hill (Zero Point); prominent birding sanctuary (over 200 bird species); Barking Deer, Leopard. |
| **Mussoorie (Benog) Wildlife Sanctuary**| **1993** | **10.82** *(Smallest)* | **Dehradun** | **Smallest wildlife sanctuary in Uttarakhand**; set up to conserve the critically endangered/possibly extinct **Himalayan Mountain Quail (*Ophrysia superciliosa*)**; dense Banj oak and pine woods. |
| **Nandhaur Wildlife Sanctuary**| **2012** | 269.95 | **Nainital & Champawat** | Located in the Nandhaur river basin between Gola and Sharda rivers; part of the **Terai Arc Landscape (TAL)**; vital elephant and tiger corridor. |

## 3. Conservation Reserves & Ramsar Site

| Protected Entity | Year | Area ($\text{km}^2$) | District | Conservation Significance & Exam Details |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Asan Conservation Reserve** | **2005** | 4.44 | **Dehradun** | Declared **Uttarakhand's First Ramsar Site in October 2020**; situated at the confluence of Yamuna and Asan rivers; critical winter stopover on the Central Asian Flyway for migratory waterfowl (Ruddy Shelduck, Mallard, Gadwall). |

## Practice Zone: UKPCS Format Drill

**Q1.** Which is the largest National Park in Uttarakhand in terms of geographical area?  
A. Corbett National Park  
B. Nanda Devi National Park  
C. Gangotri National Park  
D. Rajaji National Park  
*Answer:* **C** (Gangotri National Park in Uttarkashi covers 2,390.02 sq. km).

**Q2.** Which of the following Wildlife Sanctuaries in Uttarakhand covers the largest geographical area?  
A. Askot Wildlife Sanctuary  
B. Kedarnath Wildlife Sanctuary  
C. Govind Wildlife Sanctuary  
D. Sonanadi Wildlife Sanctuary  
*Answer:* **B** (Kedarnath Wildlife Sanctuary covers 975.20 sq. km in Chamoli and Rudraprayag).

**Q3.** The famous 'Kanchula Kharak' centre in Chamoli district was established in 1982 for the breeding and conservation of:  
A. Snow Leopard  
B. Himalayan Musk Deer  
C. Himalayan Monal  
D. Asian Elephant  
*Answer:* **B** (Himalayan Musk Deer).

**Q4.** In which year was the 'Asan Conservation Reserve' in Dehradun designated as the first Ramsar Wetland Site of Uttarakhand?  
A. 2015  
B. 2018  
C. 2020  
D. 2022  
*Answer:* **C** (October 2020).

**Q5.** In which year was 'Project Tiger' launched in India from Corbett National Park?  
A. 1971  
B. 1972  
C. 1973  
D. 1975  
*Answer:* **C** (1 April 1973).

**Q6.** The endangered 'Swamp Deer' (Barasingha) is preserved in which of the following conservation reserves of Uttarakhand?  
A. Asan Conservation Reserve  
B. Jhilmil Jheel Conservation Reserve  
C. Pawalgarh Conservation Reserve  
D. Naina Devi Bird Reserve  
*Answer:* **B** (Jhilmil Jheel Conservation Reserve in Haridwar).
