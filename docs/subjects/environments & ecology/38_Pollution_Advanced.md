## Consolidated — 26 Must-Score Facts

1. **Primary** air pollutants are emitted directly (SO₂, PM, CO, NOx). **Secondary** pollutants form in air (O₃, PAN, photochemical smog, acid mist).
2. **Lichens** are pollution indicators and do **not** grow well in badly polluted air.
3. AQI runs from **Good (0–50)** to **Severe (401–500)** in India’s standard banding.
4. **NCAP (2019)** targets **122** cities with about a **20–30%** particulate cut goal.
5. **London smog** is SO₂ plus smoke. **Photochemical smog** needs NOx, VOCs, and sunlight and yields ozone.
6. **Fly ash** is a classic thermal-power pollutant. **BS-VI** norms began in **2020**.
7. **SAFAR** is an IITM air-quality and weather forecast system.
8. **BOD** measures organic pollution through microbial oxygen demand. High BOD means worse water quality.
9. **COD** is usually greater than or equal to BOD. Clean water often shows DO above about **6 mg/L** and BOD below about **3 mg/L**.
10. **Eutrophication** is nutrient (N/P) overload → algal bloom → dissolved-oxygen crash.
11. The **Central Ground Water Board (CGWB)** issues the groundwater quality report. **CPCB** is not CGWB.
12. Classic arsenic hotspot teaching points to **Bangladesh**. Fluoride and nitrate pockets matter inside India.
13. Many pesticides are **not** readily biodegradable — a frequent true statement in papers.
14. Heavy-metal facts include **chromium** around Kanpur plus lead, cadmium, and mercury elsewhere.
15. Daytime noise standards (dB): Silence **50**, Residential **55**, Commercial **65**, Industrial **75**.
16. **Moradabad** was flagged among the world’s noisiest cities in UNEP Frontier reporting.
17. About **80%** of marine pollution comes from land. **MARPOL** governs ship-source pollution.
18. Plastic Waste Management Rules date from **2016**. The SUP ban push is **2022**. EPR is the producer-responsibility tool.
19. India ranks among the world’s largest plastic-waste generators (often taught as about **third**).
20. Indoor air pollution is driven by solid cooking fuels, tobacco smoke, and poor ventilation.
21. Surface-water pollution includes sewage, industrial effluent, and agricultural runoff into rivers and lakes.
22. Drinking-water quality in India is commonly referenced to **IS 10500** standards.
23. Vehicular emissions fall with cleaner fuel standards, inspection, and modal shift — BS-VI is the current fact.
24. Coastal pollution includes oil, plastics, sewage, and industrial discharge along the shoreline belt.
25. Do not call photochemical smog a simple SO₂–smoke fog. Ozone chemistry is the key.
26. SAMEER-type apps publish AQI for public warning; they do not replace NCAP city action plans.

## 38.1 Sources of Air Pollution

**Identity:** Point / non-point | Sectoral | Primary pollutants

- **Point sources** are chimneys, stacks, and pipes; **non-point sources** include roads, farms, and open burning.
- Major sectors: power, industry, vehicles, construction dust, biomass/stubble burning, and households.
- Primary pollutants include **SO₂, NOx, CO, PM, and VOCs**.
- UP winter peaks link stubble smoke, vehicles, industry, and Indo-Gangetic inversion.

> ** note:** Stubble smoke is a classic non-point seasonal source in north India.

---

## 38.2 Air Quality

**Identity:** NAAQS | Monitoring | Lichens | SAFAR

- **Air quality** is judged against CPCB **National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)**.
- Key pollutants monitored: PM2.5, PM10, SO₂, NO₂, O₃, CO, NH₃, Pb, and related species.
- **Lichens** are sensitive bio-indicators of air pollution, especially SO₂ stress.
- They indicate pollution because they are **sensitive** — they do **not** grow well in polluted air.
- **SAFAR** (System of Air Quality and Weather Forecasting and Research) is run with **IITM** support for forecast products.

## 38.3 Air Quality Index (AQI)

**Identity:** CPCB 0–500 | Health categories | Sub-indices

- India’s **AQI** (CPCB) runs from **0 to 500**.
- Categories: **0–50 Good**, **51–100 Satisfactory**, **101–200 Moderate**, **201–300 Poor**, **301–400 Very Poor**, **401–500 Severe**.
- AQI uses sub-indices for pollutants and reports the worst as the day’s AQI.
- PM2.5 and PM10 often drive winter AQI in UP NCR/plain cities.

| AQI | Category |
|-----|----------|
| 0–50 | Good |
| 51–100 | Satisfactory |
| 101–200 | Moderate |
| 201–300 | Poor |
| 301–400 | Very Poor |
| 401–500 | Severe |

> ** note:** Higher AQI = worse air.

## 38.4 National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)

**Identity:** 2019 | 122 cities | 20–30% PM reduction target

- **NCAP** launched in **2019** to improve air quality in non-attainment cities.
- Covers **122 cities** with city action plans, monitoring, and source control.
- Target framework: about **20–30% reduction in PM concentrations** over the programme timeline (baseline-linked).
- UP NCAP cities include Lucknow, Kanpur, Ghaziabad, Agra, Varanasi, Meerut, Noida, Prayagraj, Moradabad, Bareilly, and others notified over time.
- Complements BS-VI fuel, dust control, and industrial CETP/stack measures.

> ** note:** NCAP = city clean-air programme, not a groundwater body.

## 38.5 Smog

**Identity:** Smoke + fog origin | Secondary pollutant | London type

- **Smog** is a secondary air pollution mixture; classical **London smog** is linked to **SO₂ + smoke** in cold, humid air.
- Smog is a **secondary pollutant** (formed in the atmosphere), unlike CO or fly ash emitted directly.
- wording often links smog to **oxides of nitrogen and sulphur**.

## 38.6 Photochemical Smog

**Identity:** NOx + VOC + sunlight → O₃ / PAN | LA type

- **Photochemical smog** forms when **NOx and VOCs** react in strong sunlight.
- It always involves **ozone** as a key secondary product; PAN and aldehydes also appear.
- Typical of traffic-rich sunny cities (Los Angeles type); also relevant to Indian summer/pre-monsoon urban episodes.

**PYQ — UPPCS Prelims 2022, Q46**

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

**Ans: B**

</details>

> ** note:** Photochemical smog ≠ classical London SO₂ fog.

## 38.7 Industrial Emissions

**Identity:** Stacks / SPM / SO₂ | Fly ash | CETP | Kanpur tanneries

- Industry emits PM, SO₂, NOx, VOCs, and process-specific toxins.
- **Fly ash** pollution is caused by **thermal power plants**.
- Control tools: ESPs, bag filters, FGD, stacks, and **CETPs** for clusters.
- UP geography: Kanpur tanneries (chromium/organics), Singrauli thermal belt, brick kilns.

> ** note:** Fly ash = thermal power primary pollutant; smog = secondary.

## 38.8 Vehicular Emissions

**Identity:** CO / NOx / PM / VOCs | BS-VI 2020 | Urban traffic

- Vehicles emit **CO, NOx, PM, VOCs**, and CO₂.
- India moved to **BS-VI** norms nationwide from **April 2020**.
- Congestion, diesel fleets, and two-wheelers drive city NO₂/PM peaks.
- Complementary tools: EV push, PUC checks, odd-even-type episodic measures, CAFE efficiency norms.

> ** note:** BS-VI cut sulphur in fuel and tightened NOx/PM limits versus BS-IV.

---

## 38.9 Indoor Air Pollution

**Identity:** Biomass chulha | PM2.5 | Women/children exposure

- Indoor pollution comes from **biomass cookstoves**, kerosene, incense, and tobacco smoke.
- Rural indoor PM2.5 can exceed outdoor levels and heavily affects women and children.
- Clean cooking (LPG/PNG/electric) is the main mitigation path (Ujjwala-type coverage).

> ** note:** Indoor air is a household health issue, not only an outdoor AQI story.

---

## 38.10 Surface Water Pollution

**Identity:** BOD / COD / DO | Sewage / industry | Eutrophication

- Surface waters are polluted by sewage, industrial effluent, and agricultural runoff.
- **BOD** measures oxygen used by microbes decomposing organic matter — a standard **aquatic pollution assay**.
- Higher BOD means worse organic pollution; **COD ≥ BOD**.
- Clean-ish river fact: **DO > ~6 mg/L**, **BOD < ~3 mg/L**.
- **Eutrophication:** excess **N and P** → algal bloom → DO crash → fish kill.
- UP rivers: Ganga (Kanpur stretch), Gomti (Lucknow), Yamuna (Mathura–Agra influence).

> ** note:** BOD = water organic pollution test, not blood oximetry.

## 38.11 Groundwater Pollution

**Identity:** Arsenic / fluoride / nitrate | CGWB reports

- Groundwater contaminants include **arsenic, fluoride, nitrate, iron, salinity**, and industrial leachates.
- **Arsenic** contamination is classically associated with the **Bangladesh** / lower Gangetic basin story.
- **Fluoride** and **nitrate** affect many Indian districts (including UP pockets).
- The **Annual Groundwater Quality Report** is prepared by the **Central Ground Water Board (CGWB)** — not CPCB.

## 38.12 Drinking Water Pollution

**Identity:** Pathogens | Coliform | Fluorosis / arsenicosis | IS 10500

- Unsafe drinking water carries pathogens (diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid risk) and chemical toxins.
- Microbial safety uses **coliform / E. coli** indicators.
- Chronic chemical risks: **fluorosis** (fluoride), **arsenicosis** (arsenic), nitrate (blue-baby risk in infants).
- Treatment chain: source protection → filtration/disinfection → safe storage.

> ** note:** Microbial and chemical risks are different drinking-water failure modes.

---

## 38.13 Water Quality Standards

**Identity:** Water Act 1974 | BIS IS 10500 | CPCB river classes

- **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974** is the core water-pollution statute.
- **BIS IS 10500** sets drinking-water specification limits.
- CPCB classifies river stretches for designated best uses (A–E style classes).
- CPCB/SPCB enforce consent, standards, and monitoring for effluent and ambient water.

> ** note:** Drinking standard (IS 10500) ≠ ambient river class alone.

---

## 38.14 Fertilizers and Environment

**Identity:** N/P runoff | Eutrophication | Urea overuse

- Excess **nitrogen and phosphorus** fertilisers run off into water bodies.
- Result: eutrophication, nitrate groundwater rise, and N₂O climate link (Topic 37).
- Indo-Gangetic wheat–rice systems are high-input geography.

> ** note:** Fertiliser pollution is mostly nutrient overload, not heavy-metal tanning.

---

## 38.15 Pesticides and Environment

**Identity:** Persistence | Biomagnification | Not biodegradable

- Many pesticides are **persistent** and can **biomagnify** along food chains.
- UPPCS treats **pesticide** as **not a biodegradable pollutant** versus domestic waste/urine/faecal matter.

**PYQ — UPPCS Prelims 2023, Q120**

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

</details>

> ** note:** Silent Spring-era logic: persistent pesticides harm ecosystems.

## 38.16 Heavy Metal Pollution

**Identity:** Pb / Cd / Cr / Hg | Bioaccumulation | Kanpur Cr

- Heavy metals do not degrade; they **bioaccumulate**.
- Key facts: **Pb** (batteries/paint), **Cd** (batteries/industry), **Cr** (tanneries — Kanpur), **Hg** (Minamata disease link).
- Soil and sediment act as long-term sinks near industrial clusters.

| Metal | Classic fact |
|-------|--------------|
| Cr | Kanpur tanneries |
| Hg | Minamata / neurological |
| Pb | Neurotoxin; batteries |
| Cd | Kidney / bone risk |

> ** note:** Heavy metals ≠ biodegradable organics.

## 38.18 Noise Standards

**Identity:** CPCB zone limits | dB scale | Day/night

- Noise is measured in **decibels (dB)** on a logarithmic scale.
- CPCB ambient daytime limits (standard fact): **Silence 50**, **Residential 55**, **Commercial 65**, **Industrial 75** dB; night limits are lower.
- Silence zones include areas around hospitals, courts, and educational institutions.

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Teach CPCB industrial day standard as **75 dB** in general notes.

</details>

> ** note:** Silence < Residential < Commercial < Industrial.

## 38.19 Coastal Pollution

**Identity:** Land runoff | Ports / sewage | Mangrove stress

- Coastal pollution comes mainly from **land-based** sewage, industry, ports, aquaculture, and plastics.
- Damages mangroves, estuaries, turtle nesting beaches, and nearshore fisheries.
- India’s first National Centre for Marine Biodiversity context is often linked to **Jamnagar** geography in older CA/PYQ banks.

> ** note:** Coastal pollution is mostly land-origin, not only ship dumps.

---

## 38.20 Ocean Pollution

**Identity:** MARPOL | Oil / plastic / nutrients | ~80% land-based

- About **80%** of marine pollution originates on **land**.
- **MARPOL** is the key international ship-pollution convention.
- Major issues: oil spills, nutrient dead zones, and plastic/microplastic accumulation in gyres.
- Coral stress combines warming and local pollution (cross-topic with climate/coral bleaching).

> ** note:** Ocean pollution ≠ only mid-ocean dumping.

---

## 38.21 Plastic Waste Management

**Identity:** PWM Rules 2016 | SUP ban 2022 | EPR | Microplastics

- **Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016** (amended later) govern collection, recycling, and producer duties.
- **Single-Use Plastic (SUP)** ban notification effective **July 2022** for identified items.
- **EPR** makes producers responsible for end-of-life plastic.
- India is among the world’s largest plastic-waste generators (~**3rd** in common framing).
- **Microplastics** are plastic particles **< 5 mm**.

> ** note:** PWM/EPR = management law track; SUP ban = item-specific prohibition track.
