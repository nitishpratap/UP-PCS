## Consolidated — 24 Must-Score Facts

1. From bottom to top the layers run **Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere → Exosphere**.
2. The **troposphere** holds weather. Temperature **falls** with height in this layer.
3. The **stratosphere** holds the **ozone layer**. Temperature **rises** with height here.
4. The **mesosphere** is the **coldest** layer and is where most meteors burn.
5. The **thermosphere** is very hot but thin. Auroras, the ISS path, and much of the ionosphere sit here.
6. The **exosphere** is the outermost fringe and merges into space.
7. Dry air composition is about **N₂ 78%**, **O₂ 21%**, **argon ~0.93%**, and **CO₂ ~0.04%**.
8. **Nitrogen gas (N₂) is not a greenhouse gas.Nitrous oxide (N₂O)** is a greenhouse gas. Do not confuse the two.
9. **Argon is not a greenhouse gas.** Oxygen and helium are also not greenhouse gases.
10. Earth’s air is heated mainly by **long-wave terrestrial radiation** from the warmed surface, not by direct shortwave alone.
11. The Sun sends **shortwave** insolation. The surface emits **longwave** radiation that warms the air.
12. Maximum ozone concentration is in the **stratosphere**, not the troposphere.
13. The **homosphere** is well mixed to about **80 km**. Above that the **heterosphere** separates gases by mass.
14. Tropospheric ozone is a pollutant and greenhouse gas. Stratospheric ozone is the UV shield.
15. Temperature inversion and weather systems live in the troposphere. Commercial jets often cruise near the tropopause.
16. Do not place the ozone layer in the troposphere on a match list.
17. Mesosphere cold and thermosphere hot can both be true because density differs — thin hot air is not the same as surface heat.
18. Exosphere atoms can escape to space. It is not a weather layer.
19. Composition percentages are for **dry air**. Water vapour varies strongly by place and season.
20. Atmospheric studies for climate start from this layer ladder plus greenhouse-gas composition facts.
21. Ionosphere radio reflection is mainly a thermosphere–upper-atmosphere feature, not a troposphere fact.
22. Stratospheric heating with height comes largely from ozone absorbing ultraviolet radiation.
23. Never reverse the order of troposphere and stratosphere in a bottom-to-top ladder.
24. Long-wave terrestrial radiation is the standard answer for how the lower atmosphere is heated after surface warming.

## 35.1 Structure of the Atmosphere

**Identity:** Gaseous envelope | Temp-based layers | Heated from below

- The **atmosphere** is the gaseous envelope held by Earth’s gravity.
- About **99% of atmospheric mass** lies within roughly **32 km** of the surface.
- Structure is mainly classified by the **vertical temperature profile** into five layers (see §35.3–35.8).
- By composition: **homosphere** (well mixed to ~**80 km**) and **heterosphere** (gases separate by mass above that).
- Sea-level pressure ≈ **1013 mb (1 atm)**; pressure and density fall rapidly with height.
- The Sun’s **shortwave** energy mainly heats the **surface**; the surface emits **longwave infrared** that heats the lower atmosphere from below.

## 35.2 Atmospheric Composition

**Identity:** N₂ 78% | O₂ 21% | Ar ~1% | CO₂ ~0.04% | Variable gases

- **Dry air near the surface:Nitrogen ~78.08%**, **Oxygen ~20.95%**, **Argon ~0.93%**, **CO₂ ~0.04%**.
- **Permanent gases** (N₂, O₂, Ar) stay roughly constant in the homosphere.
- **Variable gases** include water vapour (0–4%), CO₂, CH₄, O₃, and aerosols.
- **N₂ is not a greenhouse gas**; **N₂O** is. Do not confuse N₂ with nitrogen oxides or nitrous oxide.
- **Argon is not a GHG** (also locked in Topic 34 GHG questions).
- **Water vapour** is the strongest natural tropospheric greenhouse gas by volume, but mainly a climate **feedback**.
- Dust/aerosols scatter light (blue sky / red sunset).

## 35.3 Atmospheric Layers

**Identity:** Five temperature layers | Pauses reverse the trend

- Five layers from surface outward: **Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Thermosphere → Exosphere**.
- Boundaries: **tropopause, stratopause, mesopause** — where the temperature trend reverses.
- Temperature does **not** fall continuously with height through all layers.

| Layer | Approx. height | Temp trend | Signature |
|-------|----------------|------------|-----------|
| Troposphere | 0–8/18 km | ↓ upward | Weather |
| Stratosphere | ~12–50 km | ↑ upward | Ozone |
| Mesosphere | ~50–85 km | ↓ upward | Meteors; coldest |
| Thermosphere | ~85–600 km | ↑ upward | Aurora / ISS |
| Exosphere | ~600 km+ | Transition | Space merge |

> ** note:** Learn **feature ↔ layer** matching, not only names.

## 35.4 Troposphere

**Identity:** Lowest layer | All weather | Lapse rate ~6.5°C/km

- The **troposphere** is the lowest layer and holds almost all weather, clouds, and storms.
- Thickness: about **8 km at poles** to **16–18 km at the equator**.
- Temperature generally **decreases with height** (environmental lapse rate ≈ **6.5°C/km**).
- Contains most of the atmosphere’s mass and nearly all water vapour.
- Ends at the **tropopause**, where the fall in temperature stops.
- UP Indo-Gangetic plains sit in the dense lower troposphere — winter **inversions** trap smog (Kanpur, Lucknow, Ghaziabad).

> ** note:** Weather = troposphere only. Ozone shield ≠ troposphere.

---

## 35.5 Stratosphere

**Identity:** Ozone layer | Temp increases with height | UV shield

- The **stratosphere** lies roughly **12–50 km** above the surface.
- Temperature **increases with height** because ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation.
- The **ozone layer (ozonosphere)** of maximum concentration sits mainly around **15–35 km** in the stratosphere.
- Stratospheric ozone protects life from harmful **UV-B/C**; tropospheric ozone is a pollutant.
- Ends at the **stratopause**.

## 35.6 Mesosphere

**Identity:** ~50–85 km | Coldest layer | Meteors burn

- The **mesosphere** sits above the stratosphere, roughly **50–85 km**.
- Temperature **decreases with height**; the **mesopause** is the **coldest** part of the atmosphere (~**−90°C**).
- Most **meteors burn up** here due to friction with sparse air.
- Noozone-layer role and little weather activity compared with the troposphere.

> ** note:** Meteors = mesosphere; ozone ≠ mesosphere.

---

## 35.7 Thermosphere

**Identity:** Very high temperature | Low density | Aurora / ISS / ionosphere

- The **thermosphere** extends roughly **85–600 km**.
- Kinetic temperature is very high, but density is so low that you would not “feel” hot.
- Contains much of the **ionosphere**, which reflects radio waves and hosts **auroras**.
- The **International Space Station** orbits in the lower thermosphere region.
- Temperature generally **increases with height** due to absorption of high-energy solar radiation.

> ** note:** Hot ≠ dense. Thermosphere heat is not like tropospheric heat you feel.

---

## 35.8 Exosphere

**Identity:** Outermost layer | Merges into space | Light gases escape

- The **exosphere** is the outermost atmospheric region (~**600 km** upward, gradually fading).
- Gas molecules can travel long paths and eventually **escape** to space (especially H and He).
- It merges into interplanetary space rather than ending at a sharp boundary.
- Highest satellite orbits relate to this outer region matching.

> ** note:** Exosphere = space transition, not the ozone layer.
