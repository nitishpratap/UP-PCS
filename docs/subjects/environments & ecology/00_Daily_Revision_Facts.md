## 1. Core Environmental Legislation in India (Exact Years & Articles)

| Legislation / Policy Name | Year of Enactment | Constitutional Backing / Key Mandate | Landmark Institutional Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indian Forest Act** | **1927** (earlier 1865, 1878) | Consolidated forest laws; categorized into **Reserved, Protected, and Village Forests** | State Forest Departments |
| **Wildlife (Protection) Act (WPA)** | **1972** (9 Sept 1972) | Framework for protecting wild animals, birds, and plants; **6 original schedules restructured into 4 schedules in 2022 Amendment** | National Board for Wildlife (NBWL, chaired by PM); NTCA; Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB) |
| **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act** | **1974** | Prevention and abatement of water pollution; maintains wholesomeness of water | Established **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)** & State PCBs |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act (FCA)** | **1980** (25 Oct 1980) | Prohibits diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes without prior central approval | Forest clearance mechanism |
| **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act** | **1981** | Prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution; **Noise pollution included as an air pollutant by 1987 Amendment** | Empowered CPCB and SPCBs to set ambient air standards |
| **Environment (Protection) Act (EPA)** | **1986** (23 May 1986) | **Umbrella legislation** enacted under **Article 253** in the wake of the **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (Dec 1984)** | Empowered Union Govt to coordinate state actions, set emission norms; CRZ notifications; EIA rules |
| **Public Liability Insurance Act** | **1991** | Mandatory public insurance for providing immediate relief to victims of accidents occurring while handling hazardous substances | Environment Relief Fund |
| **National Environment Tribunal Act** | **1995** | Strict liability for damages arising out of any accident while handling hazardous substances | Precursor to NGT |
| **Biological Diversity Act (BDA)** | **2002** | Realized commitments under CBD (1992); 3-tier structure for conservation and equitable benefit sharing | **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA, Chennai)**, State Biodiversity Boards (SBB), Biodiversity Management Committees (BMC) |
| **Forest Rights Act (FRA)** | **2006** | Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act; vests forest land rights | Gram Sabha is the statutory authority to initiate rights recognition process |
| **National Green Tribunal (NGT) Act** | **2010** (18 Oct 2010) | Created dedicated environmental tribunal under **Article 21 (Right to a healthy environment)**; mandated disposal within **6 months** | **Principal Bench: New Delhi**; 4 Regional Benches: **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, Chennai** |
| **CAMPA Act** | **2016** | Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act; manages funds collected for diversion of forest land (90% to State, 10% to Centre) | National & State Compensatory Afforestation Funds |

## 2. Environmental Movements in India (Pioneers & Locations)

| Movement | Year & Place | Key Leaders / Activists | Core Environmental Objective / Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bishnoi Movement** | **1730**, Khejarli village (Jodhpur, Rajasthan) | **Amrita Devi Bishnoi** | King Abhay Singh ordered felling of **Khejri trees** (*Prosopis cineraria*); Amrita Devi and her 3 daughters hugged trees; **363 Bishnois sacrificed their lives**; Amrita Devi Bishnoi Wildlife Protection Award instituted in memory. |
| **Chipko Movement** | **1973**, Reni village, Chamoli (**Uttarakhand**) | **Sunderlal Bahuguna**, **Chandi Prasad Bhatt** (Ramon Magsaysay awardee), **Gaura Devi** (*Chipko Mother*) | Protest against commercial logging of ash trees in Alaknanda basin; villagers hugged trees to prevent felling; coined slogan: *"Ecology is permanent economy"*; led to 15-year ban on green felling in UP/UK hills. |
| **Appiko Movement** | **1983**, Salkani, Uttara Kannada (Karnataka) | **Pandurang Hegde** | Southern version of Chipko; hugged trees in Western Ghats to save tropical forests from commercial monoculture plantations. |
| **Silent Valley Movement** | **1978**, Palakkad (Kerala) | Kerala Sastra Sahitya Parishad (**KSSP**), Romulus Whitaker | Protest against proposed **hydroelectric project on Kunthipuzha River** that would submerge the pristine moist evergreen rainforest habitat of the endangered **Lion-Tailed Macaque**; project scrapped; declared Silent Valley National Park (1984). |
| **Narmada Bachao Andolan (NBA)** | **1985**, Narmada Valley (Gujarat, MP, Maha) | **Medha Patkar**, **Baba Amte** | Mass movement against large dams on Narmada (particularly **Sardar Sarovar Dam**); demanded comprehensive rehabilitation, environmental impact assessment, and rights of displaced tribal villagers. |
| **Jungle Bachao Andolan** | **1982**, Singhbhum (Bihar / Jharkhand) | Local tribal communities | Tribals agitated against government scheme to replace indigenous, multipurpose **Sal forests** with commercially profitable **Teak (Sagwan)** plantations; known as "Greed Game Political Populism". |
| **Tehri Dam Protest** | 1980s–1990s, Tehri (**Uttarakhand**) | **Sunderlal Bahuguna** | Agitation against the construction of the mega Tehri Dam on the seismic fault lines of Bhagirathi River; multiple hunger strikes. |

## 6. National Air Quality Index (AQI)

Launched in **October 2014** under Swachh Bharat Abhiyan by CPCB:
- **Six Categories**:
  1. *Good* (0–50)
  2. *Satisfactory* (51–100)
  3. *Moderately Polluted* (101–200)
  4. *Poor* (201–300)
  5. *Very Poor* (301–400)
  6. *Severe* (401–500).
- **Eight Monitored Pollutants (Frequently Tested in PCS!)**:
  1. **Particulate Matter 10 ($PM_{10}$)**
  2. **Particulate Matter 2.5 ($PM_{2.5}$)**
  3. **Nitrogen Dioxide ($NO_2$)**
  4. **Sulphur Dioxide ($SO_2$)**
  5. **Carbon Monoxide ($CO$)**
  6. **Ozone ($O_3$)**
  7. **Ammonia ($NH_3$)**
  8. **Lead ($Pb$)**.
  *(Notice: Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$) is NOT part of the AQI 8 pollutants!)*

## 9. Fundamental Ecological Principles & Laws

### A. Lindeman’s 10% Energy Law (1942)
- Formulated by **Raymond Lindeman**.
- Only **approximately 10% of the energy** entering any trophic level is transferred and organized into organic matter at the next higher trophic level.
- 90% of energy is lost as metabolic heat, respiration, and catabolism.
- Explains why food chains rarely exceed 4 to 5 trophic levels.

### B. Ecological Pyramids (Charles Elton, 1927)
1. **Pyramid of Energy**:
   - **ALWAYS UPRIGHT, WITHOUT EXCEPTION!**
   - Energy decreases progressively at each successive trophic level according to thermodynamics.
2. **Pyramid of Biomass**:
   - **Terrestrial Ecosystems (Grassland, Forest)**: **UPRIGHT** (Producer biomass > Herbivore > Carnivore).
   - **Aquatic Ecosystems (Pond, Lake, Ocean)**: **INVERTED** (Phytoplankton standing crop biomass is much smaller than zooplankton and predatory fish).
3. **Pyramid of Numbers**:
   - **Grassland & Cropland**: **UPRIGHT** (Millions of grasses support fewer herbivores, and even fewer carnivores).
   - **Tree / Parasitic Ecosystem**: **INVERTED** (A single large tree supports hundreds of birds, which host thousands of parasites and hyper-parasites).
   - **Forest Ecosystem**: **Spindle-shaped** (Few large trees support many herbivores, which are eaten by fewer carnivores).

### C. Bioaccumulation vs Biomagnification
- **Bioaccumulation**: Increase in the concentration of a chemical pollutant in an **individual organism over time** (rate of intake > rate of excretion).
- **Biomagnification**: Progressive increase in the concentration of persistent, non-biodegradable, fat-soluble toxins (e.g., **DDT, Mercury, PCBs**) at **successively higher trophic levels** of a food chain.
  - *Classic example:* Phytoplankton ($0.00005\text{ ppm}$) $\to$ Zooplankton $\to$ Small Fish $\to$ Fish-eating birds / Falcons ($25\text{ ppm}$ causing egg-shell thinning and population collapse).

### D. Greenhouse Gases: Global Warming Potential (GWP, 100-Year Horizon)
*Ranked from highest warming impact per molecule to lowest (relative to $CO_2 = 1$):*
$$\mathbf{SF_6} \ (23,500) > \mathbf{PFCs} \ (7,000\text{--}9,000) > \mathbf{HFCs} \ (1,400\text{--}3,000) > \mathbf{N_2O} \ (273) > \mathbf{CH_4} \ (28) > \mathbf{CO_2} \ (1)$$
- **Water Vapour**: Most abundant natural greenhouse gas contributing greatest share to natural greenhouse effect (~60%), but has short atmospheric residence time.
- **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)**: Primary anthropogenic driver of global climate change due to massive volume and century-long residence time.
- **Methane ($CH_4$)**: Major sources: Paddy/rice fields, ruminant livestock enteric fermentation, landfills, wetlands, coal mining.
