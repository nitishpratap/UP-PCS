5. **Forest cover ≠ Recorded Forest Area ≠ tree cover**. Recorded forest area is a legal-notification figure near about **23%**.
6. Under the **Indian Forest Act, 1927**, **Reserved Forest** is strictest (acts banned unless permitted). **Protected Forest** is State-regulated with some customary rights possible.
7. **Champion and Seth (1968)** classify Indian forests into **16 types in 6 groups**. The largest share is **Tropical Dry Deciduous** (about **38%**).
8. The six Champion–Seth groups are Moist Tropical, Dry Tropical, Montane Subtropical, Montane Temperate, Sub-Alpine, and Alpine.
9. UP type facts: Terai = moist deciduous; Vindhyan = dry deciduous; Bundelkhand = thorn.
10. Law chronology: **IFA 1927 → FCA 1980 → NFP 1988 → JFM 1990 → FRA 2006 → CAMPA Act 2016**.
11. The **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** requires **Central** approval before diverting forest land. Compensatory afforestation is mandatory.
12. **National Forest Policy 1988** targets about **33%** national forest cover and **66%** in hills and mountains.
13. **Chipko (1973, Reni, Uttarakhand)** is the classic Himalayan forest movement fact. The **Godavarman (1996)** line broadened the meaning of “forest.”
14. Afforestation tools include **NAP (2000)**, **Green India Mission (2014)**, and **CAMPA** funds for compensatory planting.
15. **Social forestry** (NCA **1976** lineage) grows trees on **non-forest** land for local needs: farm, community, and extension forestry.
16. **Agroforestry** integrates trees with crops or livestock. India’s Agroforestry Policy is **2014**. **ICAR-CAFRI** sits at **Jhansi, Uttar Pradesh**.
17. **JFM (1990)** is State–village co-management of **degraded forest** through VSS/FPC, often with about **50%** produce share to the community.
18. **FRA 2006** recognises rights of Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers. **Individual Forest Rights** are capped around **4 ha**, with cutoff **13 December 2005**, verified by the **Gram Sabha**.
19. **FCA** controls **diversion**. **FRA** recognises **rights**. **JFM** is an executive co-management scheme — three different tools.
20. UP ISFR **2023** forest cover is about **6.24%** of State area — not a double-digit “12.8%” trap.
21. **Sonbhadra** has UP’s highest district forest cover (about **34.87%**).
22. Districts above **20%** forest cover in UP include **Chandauli** and **Shravasti** — not Bahraich at that threshold.
23. Western UP **poplar** farm forestry is a commercial social/agroforestry model.
24. FRA implementation questions for UP often point to **Sonbhadra** and **Lalitpur** Vindhya tribal belts.
25. Silvopasture, alley cropping, home gardens, windbreaks, and taungya are agroforestry system labels — know them as combinations, not as FCA diversion tools.
26. Tree cover is scattered trees outside mapped forest patches; it is not the same as forest cover.
27. NFP **1988** shifted emphasis from revenue forestry toward ecology and community needs.
28. CAMPA manages compensatory afforestation funds after diversion — it is not FRA Gram Sabha rights recognition.

## 8.1 Forests and their Types

**Identity:** Tree-dominated ecosystem | Global + legal + ISFR classes

- A **forest** is a large area where trees form the dominant life form and support layered ecosystems.
- Forests regulate climate, conserve soil and water, store carbon, and provide timber and non-timber forest produce.
- **Global forest types** include tropical rainforest, tropical deciduous, temperate, boreal, Mediterranean scrub, and mangrove.
- India has no boreal forest. Its forests range from Western Ghats evergreen to Himalayan alpine scrub.
- Under the **Indian Forest Act, 1927**, forests are classed as **Reserved Forest**, **Protected Forest**, or **Unclassed Forest**.
- **Reserved Forest** has the strictest protection. Most acts are prohibited unless permitted.
- **Protected Forest** is state-regulated forest where some grazing, collection, or customary use may continue under rules.
- **Unclassed Forest** is forest land not yet formally placed in the Reserved or Protected category.
- **ISFR forest cover** means any patch of **1 hectare or more** with **10% or more** tree canopy, regardless of ownership.
- **Very Dense Forest** has canopy density **70% or more**. **Moderately Dense Forest** has **40–70%**. **Open Forest** has **10–40%**.
- **Forest cover** is not the same as **Recorded Forest Area**. Much forest cover exists outside legally notified forest land.
- **Tree cover** counts scattered trees in orchards and farms that fall below the 1-hectare forest patch rule.
- **Mangrove and littoral swamp forests** are distinct coastal types with salt-tolerant species such as sundari in the Sundarbans.

| IFA class | Protection level |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Reserved Forest** | Strictest — hunting, grazing, felling banned unless permitted |
| **Protected Forest** | Moderate — state controls but some rights allowed |
| **Unclassed Forest** | Not yet formally classified |

> ** note:** Trap — "forest cover counts only government forest land" is **false**.

## 8.2 Forest Types in India

**Identity:** Champion & Seth 1968 | 16 types | UP Terai–Vindhyan–Bundelkhand

- **Champion and Seth (1968)** is India's standard forest type classification with **16 major types** in **6 groups**.
- The **six groups** are Moist Tropical, Dry Tropical, Montane Subtropical, Montane Temperate, Sub-Alpine, and Alpine.
- **Tropical Wet Evergreen** forest occurs where rainfall exceeds **2000 mm**, mainly in the Western Ghats and Northeast.
- **Tropical Semi-Evergreen** forest is a transition type with rainfall around **1500–2500 mm**.
- **Tropical Moist Deciduous** forest dominates the **Terai** belt with sal, teak, and semal at **1000–2000 mm** rainfall.
- **Littoral and Swamp** forest includes mangroves on the Sundarbans and Odisha coast.
- **Tropical Dry Deciduous** is India's **largest forest type** by area, covering about **38%** of forest area.
- **Tropical Thorn** forest occurs in low-rainfall zones such as **Bundelkhand** with acacia and babool at **200–800 mm**.
- **Subtropical Pine** forest is dominated by **chir pine** in the outer Himalaya between **1000–1800 m**.
- **Himalayan Moist Temperate** forest holds oak and deodar between **1500–3300 m**.
- **Sub-Alpine and Alpine scrub** occur above **3000 m** near the treeline.
- **ISFR 2023** recorded India forest cover at **21.76%** (**7,15,343 sq km**) and total green cover at **25.17%**.
- **Madhya Pradesh** has the **largest forest area** among states. **Mizoram** has the **highest percentage** forest cover.
- **Uttar Pradesh** forest cover is **6.24%** of state area. **Sonbhadra** has the highest district share at **34.87%**.
- Only **Chandauli (21.63%)** and **Shravasti (20.95%)** exceed **20%** forest cover in UP.
- **Bahraich** is Terai moist deciduous by type but its forest cover is only about **10%**, below the 20% threshold.

## 8.3 Forest Conservation

**Identity:** FCA 1980 | Chipko 1973 | NFP 1988 | CAMPA | Godavarman 1996

- **Forest conservation** protects existing forests and restores degraded ones, not just new planting.
- Major threats include deforestation, mining, forest fires, overgrazing, shifting cultivation (**jhum**), and invasive species.
- The **Chipko Movement (1973)** began at **Reni village** in Uttarakhand when villagers hugged trees to stop commercial felling.
- **Sunderlal Bahuguna** and **Chandi Prasad Bhatt** were key Chipko leaders.
- The **Tiwari Committee (1980)** recommended strict control on forest diversion and led to **FCA 1980**.
- **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** requires **prior Central Government approval** before forest land is used for non-forest purposes.
- **Compensatory afforestation** must replace diverted forest area, with payment of **net present value (NPV)** into the **CAMPA fund**.
- **CAMPA** was set up in **2009**. The **CAMPA Act, 2016** created national and state fund authorities.
- **National Forest Policy, 1988** replaced the **1952 revenue-oriented policy** and targets **33% forest and tree cover** nationally and **66% in hills and mountains**.
- In **Godavarman v. Union of India (1996)**, the Supreme Court expanded **forest** to include areas meeting the dictionary meaning, not only notified land.
- **Green India Mission (2014)** aims to improve forest quality and carbon sequestration under India's climate action plan.
- **Article 48A** (DPSP) directs the State to protect forests. **Article 51A(g)** makes environmental protection a fundamental duty of citizens.

> ** note:** FCA = **1980**. WPA = **1972**. NFP current version = **1988**, not 1952.

## 8.4 Forest Management

**Identity:** IFA 1927 | Working plans | FSI/ISFR | Concurrent List

- **Forest management** uses scientific planning to harvest forest produce without destroying regeneration capacity.
- The **Indian Forest Act, 1927** empowers states to declare Reserved and Protected Forests and regulate felling.
- A **Working Plan** is the **10-year** scientific management document for each forest division covering inventory, regeneration, and felling schedules.
- **Sustainable yield** means harvest must not exceed the forest's regeneration capacity.
- **Forest Survey of India (FSI), Dehradun** publishes the biennial **India State of Forest Report (ISFR)**.
- **Indian Council of Forestry Research and Education (ICFRE), Dehradun** coordinates forestry research and revised type classification work.
- **Recorded Forest Area** is land legally notified as forest, about **23%** of India's geographical area.
- About **27%** of India's forest cover lies **outside** Recorded Forest Area according to ISFR 2023.
- **Forests** are on the **Concurrent List**, so both Centre and states can legislate.
- **MoEFCC** sets national policy. State Forest Departments implement protection, working plans, and JFM on the ground.
- **Van Panchayats** in Uttarakhand manage local forest patches through customary community institutions.

> ** note:** ISFR is published by **FSI**, not CPCB, ICFRE, or MoEFCC directly.

## 8.5 Afforestation

**Identity:** New forest on degraded/non-forest land | NAP | GIM | compensatory planting

- **Afforestation** creates forest cover on land that had no forest or lost canopy below the 10% threshold.
- **Reforestation** replants harvested forest. **Restoration** rebuilds full ecological function.
- **Compensatory afforestation** under FCA is **mandatory** when notified forest land is diverted.
- The developer pays **net present value (NPV)** of forest services into the **CAMPA fund**.
- **National Afforestation Programme (NAP)** was launched in **2000** through **Forest Development Agencies (FDAs)**.
- **Green India Mission (2014)** targets improvement of **5 million hectares** of forest and tree cover.
- **Nagar Van Scheme (2020)** promotes urban forests in cities.
- The **Miyawaki method** creates dense urban mini-forests with native species planted close together.
- **Native species** planting is preferred under NFP 1988 and Green India Mission over exotic monocultures such as eucalyptus.
- Farm and agroforestry plantations add to **tree cover** and **Trees Outside Forests (TOF)** in ISFR, which helps plain states like UP.

> ** note:** CAMPA money is used mainly for afforestation and forest protection, not wildlife alone.

## 8.6 Social Forestry

**Identity:** NCA 1976 | Non-forest land | Fuelwood–fodder focus

- **Social forestry** grows trees on **non-forest land** to meet local fuelwood, fodder, and timber needs.
- The **National Commission on Agriculture (1976)** promoted it to address India's **fuelwood famine**.
- **Farm forestry** is trees planted by farmers on private land, such as poplar in western UP for plywood markets.
- **Community forestry** uses village commons and panchayat land for fuelwood and fodder.
- **Extension forestry** lines roadsides, canals, and institutional land with trees.
- **NFP 1988** endorsed social forestry as part of the **33% cover target** and stressed women's participation.
- Social forestry **reduces pressure** on natural forests by supplying needs locally.
- **Kerala** was an early successful social forestry state in the 1970s–80s.
- **Social forestry is not JFM**. JFM co-manages existing degraded forest, not non-forest land.
- **Social forestry is not agroforestry**. Agroforestry integrates trees with active crop or livestock systems on farmland.

> ** note:** Social forestry roots lie in **NCA 1976**, not JFM 1990.

## 8.7 Agroforestry

**Identity:** National Policy 2014 | ICAR-CAFRI Jhansi | Trees + crops/livestock

- **Agroforestry** deliberately combines trees with crops and/or livestock on the same land unit.
- The **National Agroforestry Policy, 2014** was India's first comprehensive agroforestry policy.
- **ICAR-CAFRI (Central Agroforestry Research Institute)** is located in **Jhansi, Uttar Pradesh**.
- **ICRAF (World Agroforestry Centre)** is headquartered in **Nairobi**, not India.
- Common systems include **silvopasture**, **alley cropping**, **home gardens**, and **windbreaks**.
- **Multi-Purpose Tree Species (MPTS)** such as poplar, eucalyptus, teak, and bamboo are chosen for market value and fast growth.
- Agroforestry improves soil fertility, diversifies farmer income, and adds to **tree cover** in ISFR.
- **Taungya** allows temporary farming between rows of planted trees in forest department plantations.
- FAO treats agroforestry as a **climate-smart agriculture** practice.

> ** note:** Agroforestry Policy = **2014**, not 2006. CAFRI = **Jhansi**, not Banda.

## 8.8 Joint Forest Management (JFM)

**Identity:** MoEF resolution 1990 | VSS/FPC | 50% benefit share

- **Joint Forest Management (JFM)** is a partnership between the State Forest Department and village communities.
- JFM was launched through a **MoEF resolution in June 1990**.
- It aims to **regenerate degraded forest** with community protection and shared benefits.
- Village bodies called **Van Samrakshan Samiti (VSS)** or **Forest Protection Committee (FPC)** implement JFM.
- Communities receive about **50% of net income** from regenerated forest produce after final felling.
- **Intermediate produce** such as grass, fodder, and fallen wood is often shared fully with the village during protection.
- **Micro-plans** guide silviculture, protection, and livelihood activities for each JFM unit.
- **NFP 1988** provided the policy foundation for community participation that JFM operationalized.
- India has over **100,000 JFM committees**, one of the world's largest participatory forestry programmes.
- **JFM is an executive scheme**, not a statute like FRA 2006.
- **JFM does not grant permanent land title**. FRA can grant recognized forest rights.

> ** note:** UPPCS 2021 asked JFM launch year — answer **1990**.

## 8.9 Forest Rights Act

**Identity:** FRA 2006 | ST + OTFD | Gram Sabha | IFR 4 ha cap

- The **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006** corrects historical injustice from colonial forest laws.
- **Rules were notified in 2008**.
- It covers **Scheduled Tribes** and **Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFDs)** who depend on forests.
- An **OTFD** must have depended on forest land for livelihood for at least **three generations** prior to **13 December 2005**.
- **Individual Forest Rights (IFR)** allow habitation and cultivation on forest land up to **4 hectares** per household.
- Claimants must have occupied the land as of **13 December 2005**.
- **Community Forest Rights (CFR)** cover grazing, NTFP collection, nistar, and community forest management.
- **Community Forest Resource (CFR) management rights** allow a village to protect and manage its traditional forest boundary.
- The **Gram Sabha** receives, verifies, and passes resolutions on forest rights claims.
- Claims then go to **SDLC (Sub-Divisional Level Committee)** and **DLC (District Level Committee)** for final approval.
- Rights are **heritable** but **not transferable or alienable**. Titles are registered in both spouses' names.
- **No eviction** of eligible claimants is allowed until the recognition process is complete.
- **Critical Wildlife Habitat** provisions allow relocation from protected areas only after strict scientific and consent procedures.
- In **Niyamgiri (2013)**, Gram Sabhas rejected Vedanta bauxite mining under FRA community consent powers.
- **FRA grants rights**. **FCA controls diversion**. Both can apply to the same forest landscape.

> ** note:** IFR cap = **4 ha**, not 5 or 10. Cutoff date = **13 December 2005**.

## Common Traps — Don't Fall For These

| Trap | Correct fact |
|------|----------------|
| Bahraich >20% forest | **Chandauli + Shravasti** only |
| Forest cover = RFA only | Cover exists **outside** notified forest |
| FCA enacted 1972 | **1980** |
| JFM = FRA same year | JFM **1990**, FRA **2006** |
| Social forestry = JFM | Social = **non-forest land**; JFM = degraded forest |
| FRA IFR cap 10 ha | **4 ha** |
| Agroforestry Policy 2006 | **2014** |
| ISFR by CPCB | **FSI Dehradun** |
| NFP 1952 vs 1988 | Current policy = **1988** |
| CAFRI location | **Jhansi**, not Banda |
| MP vs Mizoram | MP = **largest area**; Mizoram = **highest %** |
| RF vs Protected Forest | RF = strictest IFA class |
