## Consolidated — 68 Must-Score Facts

1. India is a **mixed economy** — public and private sectors coexist. The balance shifted toward markets after **1991**, but public enterprise did not vanish.
2. Standard school texts still call India an **underdeveloped / developing** economy on structural grounds (low per capita income historically, large agri workforce share, poverty / underemployment, technology gaps) even while absolute GDP size is large.
3. **Adam Smith** is commonly called the **Father of Economics**. **J.M. Keynes** is the father of modern **macroeconomics**, not the blanket “Father of Economics” tag.
4. **Economic growth** is a rise in real output / real income (often tracked by real GDP or per capita income).
5. **Economic development** is broader — living standards, welfare, health, education, inequality and poverty outcomes.
6. Growth in **per capita income is not a sufficient measure of development**.
7. **GDP** is the market value of all **final** goods and services produced **within the domestic territory** in a period.
8. **GNP / GNI** adjusts domestic product for **net factor income from abroad** (NFIA): roughly GNP = GDP + NFIA.
9. **NDP** = GDP − depreciation (consumption of fixed capital). **NNP** = GNP − depreciation.
10. **National Income** in older Indian teaching is often **NNP at factor cost** (NNPFC). Factor cost vs market price differs by net indirect taxes.
11. **Real GDP** uses constant (base-year) prices. **Nominal GDP** uses current prices.
12. **GDP deflator** = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. It is a broad price index of domestic output — not the usual retail-inflation key (CPI is Topic 4).
13. Three approaches to measuring national income: **product (value added)**, **income**, and **expenditure**. In theory they reconcile.
14. Intermediate goods are excluded from GDP to avoid **double counting**. Only final goods (or value added) count.
15. Problems in measuring NI in India include a large **informal / unorganised** sector, **non-monetised** consumption, data gaps and quality issues.
16. **Green GDP** tries to adjust conventional GDP for environmental degradation / natural-resource depletion.
17. Sectoral shares: historically primary → secondary → tertiary shift in structural change; services now dominate GDP share while agriculture still employs a large workforce share.
18. **Planning Commission** (1950) steered Five-Year Plans and plan-fund allocation culture. It was **extra-constitutional**.
19. **NITI Aayog** replaced the Planning Commission from **1 January 2015** by **Union Cabinet resolution**. It is a policy think tank, not a constitutional body.
20. **Prime Minister** is Chairperson of NITI Aayog. First Vice-Chairperson: **Arvind Panagariya**.
21. NITI promotes **cooperative and competitive federalism**. It does **not** allocate the old Plan funds the way the Planning Commission did.
22. NITI Governing Council includes the PM, Chief Ministers of States / UTs with legislatures, and Lt Governors of other UTs — **not State Governors** as a class.
23. **Atal Innovation Mission (AIM)** is a NITI Aayog flagship.
24. Five-Year Plans ran from the **First Plan (1951–56)** through the **Twelfth Plan (2012–17)**. After that, plan grants / FYP era gave way to NITI’s vision–strategy–action agenda style.
25. **First Plan** stressed agriculture, irrigation and power (Harrod–Domar influence teaching).
26. **Second Plan** (Mahalanobis) stressed heavy / capital-goods industry and public-sector industry.
27. **Third Plan** aimed at self-reliance / self-sufficiency teaching; wars and drought disrupted outcomes.
28. **Fourth Plan** stressed growth with stability / social justice themes after plan holiday.
29. **Fifth Plan** is linked with poverty eradication / *Garibi Hatao* teaching and the Emergency-era context.
30. **Sixth / Seventh** continued growth, modernisation and technology themes in school lists.
31. **Eighth Plan** began after reforms; liberalisation context matters more than a single slogan.
32. **Ninth Plan** — growth with social justice / equity emphasis.
33. **Tenth Plan** — monitorable targets; growth with equity teaching.
34. **Eleventh Plan (2007–12)** — **Towards Faster and More Inclusive Growth**.
35. **Twelfth Plan (2012–17)** — **Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth**.
36. **LPG reforms (1991)** under **P.V. Narasimha Rao** (Finance Minister Manmohan Singh) — liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation response to BOP crisis.
37. An earlier **first phase of liberalisation / delicensing** is often tagged to **Rajiv Gandhi** (mid-1980s). Do not erase 1991 as the systemic LPG break.
38. **NDC (National Development Council)** linked States to Plan approval in the Planning Commission era.
39. Business / trade cycle phases in school lists: expansion, peak, contraction / recession, trough / recovery.
40. Rolling plan teaching (Janata period) interrupted the fixed five-year rhythm — remember as a break, not a permanent system.
41. Transfer payments (pensions, scholarships, gifts) are **excluded** from GDP because they involve no productive contribution.
42. **Visvesvaraya Plan (1934)** was an early systematic planning proposal aiming to double national income in about ten years.
43. **National Planning Committee (1938)** was set up under Congress President Subhash Chandra Bose with **Jawaharlal Nehru** as chairman.
44. **Bombay Plan (1944)** was drafted by leading industrialists and favoured state-led heavy industrialisation.
45. **Gandhian Plan (1944)** by S.N. Agarwal stressed decentralisation and cottage industries.
46. **People's Plan (1945)** was associated with **M.N. Roy** and labour/agrarian priorities.
47. **Sarvodaya Plan (1950)** is linked with **Jayaprakash Narayan**.
48. **NDC** (National Development Council, 1952 teaching) coordinated Centre–State plan approval in the Planning Commission era.
49. **Plan Holiday (1966–69)** meant three Annual Plans after war, drought and forex stress — not a Five-Year Plan.
50. **Rolling Plan (1978–80)** under the Janata government revised targets yearly (Myrdal / Lakdawala teaching).
51. Steel plants of the Second Plan teaching set: **Bhilai** (USSR), **Durgapur** (UK), **Rourkela** (West Germany).
52. **IPR 1956** is often called the ‘Economic Constitution’ teaching tag for the public-sector industrial map.
53. 1991 crisis teaching: forex plunged to roughly **two weeks of imports**; gold was pledged abroad for emergency support.
54. **Counter-cyclical** fiscal policy expands spending / cuts taxes in a downturn; **pro-cyclical** moves with the boom.
55. NITI first CEO teaching tag: **Sindhushree Khullar**; first VC: **Arvind Panagariya**.
56. NITI flagship indices teaching desk includes **SDG India Index**, **CWMI**, and Aspirational Districts.
57. **Market price = Factor cost + Net Indirect Taxes** (indirect taxes − subsidies).
58. **GVA** measures output minus intermediate consumption; it is the building block of GDP by production.
59. Fisher–Clark structural shift teaching: primary → secondary → tertiary; India jumped toward **services** in GDP share.
60. Planning Commission was created on **15 March 1950** by executive resolution (Neogy Advisory Planning Board teaching).
61. Fifth Plan teaching twin goals: **Garibi Hatao** and self-reliance; it was terminated early by the Janata government.
62. Eighth Plan sits in the **post-1991** structural-adjustment window (Rao–Manmohan teaching).
63. NITI is **neither constitutional nor statutory**; it cannot allocate old Plan grants like the Planning Commission.
64. Business-cycle trough / depression teaching marks the lowest output and highest unemployment phase before recovery.
65. **Green GDP** subtracts environmental degradation / resource depletion costs from conventional GDP.
66. Double-counting trap: never add intermediate goods into GDP if final goods (or value added) are already counted.
67. NITI Governing Council trap: **State Governors are not members**; CMs and Lt Governors are.
68. 12th Plan is the **last** Five-Year Plan (2012–17) before the NITI vision–strategy–action style.

## 1.1 Features of the Indian economy

**The Indian economy** is a large, diverse economy with structural dualism — a modern formal segment alongside a large traditional and informal segment.

- Textbooks still classify India as a **developing / underdeveloped** economy on structural grounds (low per capita income historically, dualism, agrarian employment share).
- India is a **mixed economy**: public and private sectors both produce and invest under a market-plus-regulation frame.
- A large domestic market and demographic weight shape demand and labour supply.
- Primary, secondary and tertiary shares have shifted toward **services in GDP** while agriculture still employs a large workforce share.
- Regional imbalance, informal employment and infrastructure gaps remain structural features in teaching.
- **Adam Smith** is the usual “Father of Economics” tag; **J.M. Keynes** is the face of modern macroeconomics — not the Father of Economics.

## 1.2 Growth versus development

**Economic growth** is a rise in real output (usually real GDP or real per capita GDP).

- Growth is a **quantitative** expansion of income and production.
- **Economic development** is a broader improvement in living standards, health, education, equity and capabilities.
- Per capita income growth is **necessary but not sufficient** for development.
- Development asks who benefits, what happens to poverty and human capabilities, and whether growth is sustainable.
- Inclusive growth spreads benefits across regions, castes, genders and income groups (Topic 8 neighbour).
- Environmental sustainability enters development debate; it is not an HDI pillar (Topic 8 / 12).

## 1.3 National income — GDP family

**National income** measures the money value of goods and services produced by an economy over a period.

- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** is the value of final goods and services produced **within the domestic territory**.
- **GNP (Gross National Product)** = GDP + **net factor income from abroad**.
- **NDP / NNP** subtract **depreciation** (capital consumption) from the gross measures.
- **Nominal** figures use current prices; **real** figures use constant prices to track volume.
- The **GDP deflator** = Nominal GDP / Real GDP × 100 in teaching.
- Three methods: **production**, **income** and **expenditure** — they should reconcile in principle.
- Sectoral shares show primary / secondary / tertiary contribution to GDP.
- A practical problem in India is **non-monetised consumption** and informal output that money accounts struggle to capture.
- Under-employment, inflation and low savings are real problems, but they are not the classic NI **measurement** difficulty keyed here.
- **Green GDP** adjusts conventional GDP for environmental degradation and resource depletion in teaching.

## 1.4 Planning Commission to NITI Aayog

**Economic planning** in India meant the State setting priorities, allocating investible resources and coordinating public investment through Five-Year Plans.

- The **Planning Commission** (1950) prepared Plans and allocated Plan assistance under a centralised model.
- The Prime Minister chaired the Planning Commission; a Deputy Chairman ran day-to-day work.
- **NITI Aayog** replaced the Planning Commission from **1 January 2015**.
- NITI was created by a **Union Cabinet resolution**, not by a constitutional amendment.
- The **Prime Minister** is Chairperson of NITI Aayog — not the President.
- **Arvind Panagariya** was the first Vice-Chairperson of NITI Aayog.
- NITI promotes **cooperative and competitive federalism**; it does **not** allocate old-style Plan funds the way the Planning Commission did.
- The **Governing Council** includes Chief Ministers of States and Lt Governors of UTs without legislatures.
- **State Governors are not** part of the Governing Council — a repeated trap.
- **Atal Innovation Mission (AIM)** is a NITI flagship for innovation and entrepreneurship.

## 1.5 Five-Year Plans — theme tags

**Five-Year Plans** were the Planning Commission’s medium-term investment and priority frameworks from the 1950s until Plan-era closure.

- Early Plans emphasised agriculture, irrigation and community development, then heavy industry.
- Plan slogans are high-yield: match Plan number to theme, not the other way around.
- The **First Plan (1951–56)** focused on agriculture, irrigation and community development after Partition scarcity.
- The **Second Plan (1956–61)** followed the **Mahalanobis** heavy-industry model.
- The **Third Plan** faced drought and war stress; planning later entered **Plan Holidays** / Annual Plans in the mid-1960s teaching.
- The **Fourth / Fifth** Plans carried growth-with-stability and poverty-removal (Garibi Hatao-era) teaching tags.
- The **Eighth Plan** is associated with liberalisation-era priorities after 1991.
- The **Ninth / Tenth** Plans deepen growth and equity themes in coaching maps.
- The **Eleventh Plan** centres **inclusive growth**.
- The **Twelfth Plan** slogan is **Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth**.
- Do not swap 11th (inclusive) with 12th (adds sustainable explicitly in the keyed slogan).
- Plan-era allocation politics ended with NITI; Plan slogans still appear in Prelims stems.

## 1.6 Reforms and the cycle

**Economic reforms** liberalise controls, open trade and investment, and reshape the public–private balance.

### Reform chronology

- Mid-**1980s** delicensing and technology import easing under **Rajiv Gandhi** is the usual “first phase of liberalisation” key.
- The **1991 LPG package** under **P.V. Narasimha Rao** (with Manmohan Singh as Finance Minister) is the full structural break — crisis, IMF/World Bank engagement and systemic opening.
- **Liberalisation** cut industrial licensing and controls; **privatisation / disinvestment** reduced public ownership in selected PSUs; **globalisation** deepened trade and capital openness.
- Later waves include tax reform, financial-sector opening, GST and labour / farm reform debates — year-sensitive CA.
- Do not call 1991 the “first phase” when the stem asks for the mid-1980s wave.

### Business cycle

- Short-run output fluctuates in phases: **expansion → peak → contraction → trough**, then recovery.
- Expansion raises output, employment and investment; peaks mark high utilisation.
- Contraction / recession cuts demand and employment; troughs are the low point before revival.
- Cycles move around the long-run growth path — they do not replace structural reform teaching.

## Common Traps

* Mixed economy + underdeveloped/developing textbook tags; Keynes ≠ Father of Economics.
* Growth ≠ development; per capita income alone is not enough.
* GDP (domestic) vs GNP (+ NFIA); real vs nominal; deflator ≠ CPI key.
* NITI: 1 Jan **2015**, Cabinet resolution, **PM** Chair, first VC **Panagariya**; no old Plan-fund role; Governors ≠ GC.
* 11th = inclusive; 12th = inclusive + sustainable.
* Rajiv = early liberalisation phase; 1991 Rao = LPG package.
* Non-monetised / informal output muddies NI estimates.
* NITI ≠ Finance Commission (Art. 280).
