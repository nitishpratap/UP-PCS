Government receipts are divided into two main categoriesRevenue Receipts and Capital Receipts. Those government
receipts which create a liability for the government or reduce its financial assets are called Capital Receipts. On the other hand, Revenue Receipts, are those which neither create any additional liability on the government nor reduce its assets;
these include tax revenue and non-tax revenue (such as
interest, dividend, fees, etc.).
When the government borrows from the market, RBI, or other
financial institutions, it creates an obligation to repay the
principal and interest in the future; therefore, these receipts
create a new liability for the government and are treated as
Capital Receipts.
Under Disinvestment, the government sells its equity stake
in public sector enterprises. This reduces the government's
financial assets, and in official accounting such receipts
are also treated as Capital Receipts (especially as non-debt
capital receipts). Thus, both borrowings and disinvestment
are capital receipts.
The interest received by the Central Government on loans
given to states, public sector enterprises, etc., is classified
officially as non-tax revenue and included under Revenue
Receipts, not under Capital Receipts. Revenue receipts
neither create any new liability nor reduce the government's financial assets.
Thus, only Statements I and II are correct, while Statement
[ IIl is incorrect; therefore, the correct answer is option (a).

Direct Taxes : A direct tax is a kind of charge, which is
Imposed directly on the taxpayer and paid directly to the
government by the persons/entities on whom it imposed
A direct tax is one that cannot be shifted by the taxpayer to
someone else. Examples : Income Tax, Corporation Tax.
Property Tax, Wealth Tax, Gift Tax etc.
Indirect Taxes : An indirect tax is a tax collected by an
intermediary (such as a retail store) from the person who
bears the ultimate economic burden of the tax (such as the
customer). An indirect tax is one that can be shifted by the
taxpayer to someone else. An indirect tax may increase the
price of a good so that consumers are actually paying the tax
by paying more for the products. Examples : Customs Duty,
Central Excise Duty, Service Tax, Sales Tax, GST, Value
Added Tax (VAT) etc.

the State Governments would levy and collect SGST.
