## 1. National Income Aggregates & Macroeconomic Formulas

| Concept / Metric | Formula / Technical Definition | Key Exam Traps & Prelims Triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **GDP at Market Price** | $GDP_{MP} = C + I + G + (X - M)$ | Total market value of all final goods & services produced **within the domestic territory** in 1 year. Foreigners earning in India included; Indians earning abroad excluded. |
| **GNP at Market Price** | $GNP_{MP} = GDP_{MP} + NFIA$ | Measures income earned by **nationals/citizens** regardless of geographical location. $NFIA$ = Net Factor Income from Abroad (Factor income from abroad $-$ Factor income paid abroad). |
| **NNP at Market Price** | $NNP_{MP} = GNP_{MP} - \text{Depreciation}$ | Deducts consumption of fixed capital (depreciation). **Depreciation is also called Capital Consumption Allowance**. |
| **NNP at Factor Cost (National Income)** | $NNP_{FC} = NNP_{MP} - \text{Net Indirect Taxes (NIT)}$ | Traditional official definition of **National Income**. Net Indirect Taxes (NIT) = Indirect Taxes $-$ Subsidies. |
| **Gross Value Added (GVA)** | $GVA_{Basic} = GVA_{Factor\ Cost} + \text{Production Taxes} - \text{Production Subsidies}$ | Since 2015, India measures headline GDP as: **$GDP_{MP} = GVA_{Basic} + \text{Product Taxes} - \text{Product Subsidies}$**. Base year changed to **2011–12**. |
| **Personal Disposable Income (PDI)** | $PDI = \text{Personal Income} - \text{Direct Taxes} - \text{Miscellaneous Fees/Fines}$ | Actual income available to households for consumption expenditure and savings. |
| **Real GDP vs Nominal GDP** | $\text{Real GDP} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{GDP Deflator}} \times 100$ | Real GDP is calculated at **constant base year prices** (eliminates inflation impact). Nominal GDP is at current prices. |
| **GDP Deflator** | $\frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100$ | Most comprehensive measure of inflation in an economy because it covers **all goods and services** produced, unlike CPI/WPI which use a fixed basket. |

> **Examiner Trap:** Transfer payments (old age pensions, unemployment allowance, student scholarships) are **excluded** from National Income because no corresponding production of goods or services occurs. Second-hand goods sales are also excluded (only broker commission is included).

## 2. Five-Year Plans (FYP) & NITI Aayog Master Matrix

| Plan & Duration | Model / Architect | Core Objective / Strategy | Key Milestones & Exam Triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st FYP (1951–56)** | **Harrod-Domar Model** | Agriculture, irrigation, price stability | Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, Damodar Valley dams launched; Target 2.1%, Achieved 3.6%. |
| **2nd FYP (1956–61)** | **P.C. Mahalanobis Model** | Rapid industrialisation, Heavy & Basic Industries | Steel plants at **Bhilai** (USSR), **Rourkela** (Germany), **Durgapur** (UK); Industrial Policy Resolution 1956. |
| **3rd FYP (1961–66)** | John Sandy & Sukhamoy Chakravarty | Self-reliant & self-generating economy | **Bokaro Steel Plant** (1964, USSR), FCI & CACP set up (1965). Plan failed due to 1962 China War, 1965 Pak War, 1965–66 Severe Drought. |

| **4th FYP (1969–74)** | D.R. Gadgil Formula | Growth with stability & progressive self-reliance | **14 Major Commercial Banks Nationalised** (19 July 1969); Pokhran-I Nuclear Test (*Smiling Buddha*, 1974); 1971 Indo-Pak War. |
| **5th FYP (1974–79)** | D.P. Dhar Model | **Garibi Hatao** (Poverty Eradication) & Self-reliance | **20-Point Programme** (1975); Minimum Needs Programme (MNP); Terminated 1 year early in 1978 by Janata Govt. |
| **Rolling Plan (1978–80)** | Gunnar Myrdal concept | Annual revisions without fixed 5-year lock | Introduced by Janata Govt (Morarji Desai); ended when Congress returned in 1980. |
| **6th FYP (1980–85)** | Investment & Modernisation | Poverty eradication, economic & tech self-reliance | **NABARD established** (12 July 1982, Shivaraman Committee); **EXIM Bank** (1982); **6 more banks nationalised** (1980). |
| **7th FYP (1985–90)** | Pranab Mukherjee / Lakdawala | Food, Work & Productivity | Speed Post (1986); SEBI formed (1988); Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (JRY, 1989). |
| **Annual Plans (1990–92)** | Balance of Payments Crisis | Structural reforms & crisis stabilization | **LPG Reforms (1991)**: Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation under P.V. Narasimha Rao & Dr. Manmohan Singh. |
| **8th FYP (1992–97)** | John W. Miller Model | Human Resource Development (employment, education) | India joined **WTO** (1 Jan 1995); National Stock Exchange (NSE) operationalised. |
| **9th FYP (1997–2002)** | Social Justice & Equality | Growth with Social Justice and Equity | 50 years of Indian Independence; Asian Financial Crisis backdrop. |
| **10th FYP (2002–07)** | Target 8% GDP Growth | Doubling per capita income in 10 years, reduce poverty by 5% | National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (**NREGA**, 2005); Bharat Nirman. |
| **11th FYP (2007–12)** | C. Rangarajan / Montek Singh | **Faster and More Inclusive Growth** | Right to Education (RTE Act, 2009); National Rural Livelihoods Mission (NRLM). |
| **12th FYP (2012–17)** | Final Five-Year Plan | **Faster, More Inclusive and Sustainable Growth** | Terminated; replaced by NITI Aayog 15-Year Vision, 7-Year Strategy & 3-Year Action Agenda. |

> **NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India):** Formed on **1 January 2015** replacing the Planning Commission (15 March 1950). Non-constitutional, non-statutory extra-constitutional think tank. Chairperson: **Prime Minister**. Governing Council comprises all State Chief Ministers, UT Lt Governors/CMs. Operates on **Bottom-Up** approach and **Cooperative Federalism** (unlike top-down Planning Commission).

## 3. Monetary Policy, RBI Instruments & Money Supply Aggregates

### A. RBI Monetary Policy Instruments Master Table
| Instrument | Nature / Category | Operational Mechanism & Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** | Quantitative / Direct | Percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) commercial banks **must keep with RBI in cash**. No interest paid by RBI on CRR. Governed by RBI Act, 1934 (Sec 42). |
| **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** | Quantitative / Direct | Percentage of NDTL commercial banks **must maintain with themselves** in approved liquid assets (Cash, Gold, Govt Securities). Governed by Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (Sec 24). |
| **Repo Rate** | Quantitative / Indirect | Rate at which RBI lends short-term money to commercial banks against **government securities as collateral** (repurchase agreement). Primary policy rate. |
| **Reverse Repo Rate** | Quantitative / Indirect | Rate at which RBI borrows short-term funds from commercial banks against government securities. |
| **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)** | Quantitative / Liquidity | Introduced in April 2022 under amended RBI Act (Sec 17). Allows RBI to absorb excess liquidity from commercial banks **without providing any collateral (G-Secs)**. Replaced Reverse Repo as floor of LAF corridor. |
| **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)** | Quantitative / Liquidity | Penal rate at which scheduled commercial banks can borrow overnight funds from RBI by dipping into their SLR quota (up to prescribed limit). Ceiling of LAF corridor. |
| **Bank Rate** | Quantitative / Long-term | Rate at which RBI buys or rediscounts bills of exchange or commercial papers; benchmark for penal interest rates on non-compliance with reserve requirements. |
| **Open Market Operations (OMO)** | Quantitative / Market | Outright purchase and sale of Government Securities (G-Secs) by RBI in open market to inject or absorb durable liquidity. |
| **Moral Suasion, Credit Rationing, Margin Requirements** | Qualitative / Selective | Directives to banks to channel credit to specific priority sectors, control speculative borrowing, and regulate loan-to-value (LTV) ratios. |

### B. Money Supply Measures (Monetary Aggregates)
- **Reserve Money ($M_0$ / High Powered Money):** Currency in Circulation $+$ Bankers' deposits with RBI $+$ 'Other' deposits with RBI.
- **Narrow Money ($M_1$):** Currency with Public $+$ Demand Deposits with Banking System (Current & Savings Account - CASA) $+$ 'Other' deposits with RBI. *(Most liquid aggregate).*
- **$M_2$:** $M_1 +$ Post Office Savings Bank deposits.
- **Broad Money ($M_3$):** $M_1 +$ Time Deposits (Fixed/Recurring Deposits) with the banking system. *(Most commonly used indicator of money supply in India).*
- **$M_4$:** $M_3 +$ All deposits with Post Office Savings Organisation (excluding National Savings Certificates / NSC).
- **Liquidity Hierarchy:** $M_1 > M_2 > M_3 > M_4$.

### C. Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)
- Statutory body constituted under **Section 45ZB of RBI Act, 1934** (amended by Finance Act, 2016).
- **6 Members:** 3 from RBI (RBI Governor as ex-officio Chairperson, Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, 1 RBI officer) $+$ 3 external members appointed by Central Government.
- **Inflation Target:** Central Govt in consultation with RBI sets retail inflation target (CPI headline) at **4% with a tolerance band of $\pm 2\%$** (i.e., 2% to 6%).
- Meets at least **4 times a year**. Quorum is 4 members. Each member has 1 vote; RBI Governor has a **casting vote** in case of a tie.

## 4. Inflation Indices: CPI vs WPI Master Comparison

| Parameter | Wholesale Price Index (WPI) | Consumer Price Index (CPI - Combined) |
|---|---|---|
| **Publishing Authority** | Office of Economic Adviser (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce & Industry | National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) |

## 5. Public Finance, Deficits & Finance Commission

### A. Deficit Formulas Master Sheet
1. **Fiscal Deficit (FD):** $\text{Total Expenditure} - \text{Total Receipts (excluding borrowings)}$. It represents the total borrowing requirements of the government from all sources in that year.
2. **Revenue Deficit (RD):** $\text{Revenue Expenditure} - \text{Revenue Receipts}$. Indicates that the government is borrowing to finance day-to-day consumption rather than capital asset creation.
3. **Effective Revenue Deficit (ERD):** $\text{Revenue Deficit} - \text{Grants for creation of capital assets}$. Introduced in Union Budget 2011–12.
4. **Primary Deficit (PD):** $\text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Interest Payments}$. Measures the current year's fiscal stance excluding the burden of past accumulated debt interest.

### B. Finance Commission (Article 280) Master Matrix
- Quasi-judicial body constituted by the **President every 5 years** under Article 280.
- Recommends devolution of net proceeds of taxes between Union and States (Vertical devolution) and allocation among States (Horizontal devolution).
- **1st FC:** K.C. Neogy (1951)
- **13th FC:** Dr. Vijay Kelkar (recommending GST framework)
- **14th FC:** Dr. Y.V. Reddy (increased States' share sharply from 32% to **42%**)
- **15th FC:** **N.K. Singh** (2020–26): Devolution share set at **41%** (1% adjusted for newly created UTs of J&K and Ladakh). Criteria weights: Income Distance (45%), Population 2011 (15%), Area (15%), Forest & Ecology (10%), Demographic Performance (12.5%), Tax Effort (2.5%).
- **16th FC:** Constituted on 31 Dec 2023 under chairmanship of **Dr. Arvind Panagariya** (former Vice Chairman of NITI Aayog).

## 6. Banking & Financial Sector Milestones

- **1935 (1 April):** RBI established on recommendations of **Hilton Young Commission (1926)** under RBI Act, 1934. Initial capital ₹5 crore; nationalised on **1 January 1949**.
- **1955:** Imperial Bank of India (formed in 1921 by amalgamating Presidency Banks of Bengal, Bombay, Madras) nationalised and renamed **State Bank of India (SBI)** on recommendation of All India Rural Credit Survey Committee (AD Gorwala).
- **1969 (19 July):** **14 Major Commercial Banks** (with deposits $\ge ₹50$ crore) nationalised by Indira Gandhi Govt via Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Ordinance.
- **1975 (2 October):** First Regional Rural Bank (**Prathama Bank**, Moradabad, UP) established on recommendations of **M. Narasimham Working Group**.
- **1980 (15 April):** **6 More Commercial Banks** (with deposits $\ge ₹200$ crore) nationalised.
- **1982 (12 July):** **NABARD** established under NABARD Act, 1981 on recommendation of **B. Sivaraman Committee**.
- **1988:** SEBI set up as non-statutory body; granted statutory powers via **SEBI Act, 1992** on 30 January 1992.
- **Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Targets:** Total PSL target for Domestic Scheduled Commercial Banks and Foreign Banks with $\ge 20$ branches is **40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)**. Sub-targets: Agriculture (18%, within which 10% for Small & Marginal Farmers), Micro Enterprises (7.5%), Weaker Sections (12%). For Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) and Small Finance Banks (SFBs), total PSL target is **75% of ANBC**.

### B. Minimum Support Price (MSP) Mechanism
- Announced by Central Government on recommendations of **CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices)** before sowing season.
- Covers **22 mandated crops** $+$ Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) for **Sugarcane**:
  - 7 Cereals (Paddy, Wheat, Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Ragi, Barley)
  - 5 Pulses (Gram, Arhar/Tur, Moong, Urad, Masur)
  - 8 Oilseeds (Groundnut, Rapeseed/Mustard, Soyabean, Sesamum, Sunflower, Safflower, Nigerseed, Copra)
  - Raw Cotton, Raw Jute, De-husked Coconut
- **Cost Concepts used by CACP:**
  - $A2$: Direct paid-out costs on seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, hired labour, fuel, irrigation.
  - $A2 + FL$: $A2 +$ Imputed value of unpaid family labour.
  - $C2$: Comprehensive cost ($A2 + FL +$ rental value of owned land $+$ interest on owned capital assets).
  - Central Govt formula since Union Budget 2018–19: MSP set at **at least 1.5 times ($150\%$) of $A2 + FL$**.

## 8. Poverty Estimation Committees & Multidimensional Poverty

| Committee & Year | Consumption Basket / Criteria Used | Poverty Line Benchmark (Monthly / Daily) |
|---|---|---|
| **Y.K. Alagh (1979)** | Minimum Nutritional Calorie intake | **2,400 kcal/day** in Rural areas; **2,100 kcal/day** in Urban areas. |
| **D.T. Lakdawala (1993)** | Calorie norms linked with state-specific price indices (CPI-AL for rural, CPI-IW for urban) | State-specific poverty lines; rejected uniform national basket. |
| **Suresh Tendulkar (2009)** | Shifted from calories to mixed reference basket including **Health, Education, Clothing, Footwear** | Set per capita daily consumption at **₹27.2 in rural** and **₹33.3 in urban** areas (₹816/month rural, ₹1000/month urban at 2011–12 prices). National poverty rate: **21.9%** (2011–12). |
| **C. Rangarajan (2014)** | Independent nutritional norm (2,155 kcal rural, 2,090 kcal urban + protein/fat) + non-food expenses | Set per capita daily consumption at **₹32 in rural** and **₹47 in urban** areas (₹972/month rural, ₹1407/month urban). National poverty rate: **29.5%** (2011–12). |

> **Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) by NITI Aayog:**  
> Follows Alkire-Foster methodology. **3 Dimensions and 12 Indicators** (each dimension weighted equally at $1/3$):
> 1. **Health (1/3):** Nutrition (1/6), Child & Adolescent Mortality (1/12), Maternal Health (1/12).
> 2. **Education (1/3):** Years of Schooling (1/6), School Attendance (1/6).
> 3. **Standard of Living (1/3):** Cooking Fuel, Sanitation, Drinking Water, Electricity, Housing, Assets, Bank Account (each 1/21).
