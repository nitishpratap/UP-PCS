## Consolidated — 44 Must-Score Facts

1. **Money** is anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange, a unit of account, a store of value and a standard of deferred payment.
2. **Fiat money** has value by legal tender / trust, not by intrinsic metal content. **Commodity money** and **representative money** are older teaching types.
3. **Narrow money (M1)** in Indian teaching centres on currency with the public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI (classic list).
4. **Broad money (M3)** adds time deposits of banks to the narrow base — the most-watched aggregate in school keys.
5. **Money supply** expands when the central bank buys securities / injects liquidity and contracts when it sells securities / tightens reserves (other things equal).
6. **Commercial banks** accept deposits, give loans and create credit within reserve rules.
7. India’s banking map: **public sector banks**, private banks, foreign banks, **cooperative banks**, **Regional Rural Banks**, **Small Finance Banks**, **Payments Banks**, and Local Area Banks in older lists.
8. **Bank nationalisation**: **14** major banks on **19 July 1969**; **6** more in **1980** — directed credit, branch expansion and priority lending followed.
9. **Imperial Bank of India** was converted into the **State Bank of India** (1955 teaching) as the public-sector apex commercial bank story.
10. **Regional Rural Banks (1975)** combine local feel with commercial-bank discipline for small / marginal farmers, artisans and rural labour.
11. **NABARD (12 July 1982)** is the apex **refinance and development** bank for agriculture and rural credit — not the day-to-day retail lender of first resort in the usual key.
12. Rural-credit chronology: **RRBs (1975) → NABARD (1982) → SHG–Bank Linkage (1992) → Kisan Credit Card (1998)**.
13. **Lead Bank Scheme (1969)** assigns one bank lead responsibility for a **district** (area approach after Gadgil / Nariman teaching).
14. **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** steers bank credit to agriculture, MSME, education, housing, weaker sections and related notified categories.
15. **NPA (Non-Performing Asset)**: a loan where interest / principal stays overdue beyond the prescribed period (90-day teaching for term loans). Gross vs net NPA and provisioning are supervision keys.
16. **Payments Banks** take deposits (subject to limits), provide payments / remittance services and issue debit cards / prepaid instruments — they **cannot** give ordinary loans or issue credit cards in the classic licence design.
17. **Small Finance Banks** are full banks with a mandate to lend mainly to unserved / underserved segments (SFB licence differs from payments banks).
18. **Digital banking / UPI**: Unified Payments Interface enables real-time account-to-account payments on phones; FinTech rides on bank rails, NPCI systems and RBI authorisations.
19. **PMJDY (2014)** and business-correspondent / BC models are modern inclusion instruments after the nationalisation–RRB–Lead Bank phase.
20. **DICGC** insures bank deposits up to the notified limit per depositor per bank — a safety net, not a lending tool.
21. **RBI** was set up on **1 April 1935** under the **RBI Act, 1934**, and nationalised on **1 January 1949**.
22. Core RBI roles: **monetary authority**, **issuer of banknotes**, **banker and debt manager to government**, **bankers’ bank / lender of last resort**, **regulator / supervisor of banks**, **manager of foreign exchange** (FEMA lane).
23. **₹1 note / coin** sits with the Government of India in teaching; other banknotes are issued by RBI.
24. **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** — six members (three from RBI including the Governor as Chair; three external, appointed by the Central Government) — decides the **policy repo** and related policy stance.
25. Flexible **inflation targeting**: headline CPI **4%** with a tolerance band of **±2%** (target notified by government in consultation with RBI).
26. **Repo rate**: rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks against eligible collateral (injects liquidity when used).
27. **Reverse repo / SDF**: rates at which RBI absorbs liquidity from banks; **SDF (2022)** does so without collateral securities.
28. **Bank rate**: longer-standing penal / signal rate in teaching; do not confuse it with the overnight repo of LAF.
29. **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)**: share of NDTL kept as cash balance with RBI — earns **no** interest in the post-2007 teaching.
30. **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)**: share of NDTL held in approved liquid assets (G-Secs, cash, gold teaching) by the bank itself.
31. **MSF (Marginal Standing Facility)**: overnight borrowing window against G-Secs, including dipping into SLR within limits — a safety valve above the repo.
32. **Open Market Operations (OMO)**: RBI buys / sells government securities to inject / absorb durable liquidity.
33. **LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility)** is the daily repo / reverse-repo (and related) corridor for short-term liquidity.
34. Raising repo / CRR / SLR generally **tightens** credit; cutting them generally **eases** credit (transmission caveats remain).
35. **Financial inclusion** means affordable access to useful formal financial services for all, especially the underbanked.
36. UPPCS keys nationalisation, RRBs and **village adoption by bank branches** as inclusion steps; Lead Bank Scheme is a named district tool for the same goal.
37. **Cooperative banks** (urban and rural) are member-based; supervision is a shared RBI / Registrar story depending on type — do not erase RBI’s role after recent reforms teaching.
38. **Microfinance / SHG–bank linkage** brings group savings and credit into formal banking; NABARD pioneered the large SHG programme.
39. **MCLR / external-benchmark lending rates** improve transparency of how banks price loans — a reforms tag, not a money-supply aggregate.
40. **Basel norms** set capital adequacy and risk-management standards for banks under RBI supervision.
41. RBI does **not** collect income tax, run the population census, or formulate foreign policy — those are classic wrong options against “issue of currency”.
42. **Appreciation** of a currency is a rise in its value relative to another; **depreciation** is a fall — external-sector depth stays in Topic 9, but the word pair appears in money / forex stems.
43. **Scheduled banks** are listed in the RBI Act’s Second Schedule and enjoy certain facilities (e.g. LAF access teaching).
44. **Lender of last resort**: RBI provides emergency liquidity to solvent banks facing temporary stress — a bankers’ bank function.

## 3.1 Money — functions and aggregates

**Money** is anything widely accepted as payment for goods, services and debts.

- Classic functions: **medium of exchange**, **unit of account**, **store of value**, and **standard of deferred payment**.
- Without a medium of exchange, trade falls back toward barter and the double coincidence of wants.
- As a unit of account, money lets prices and debts be stated in a common measure.
- As a store of value, money carries purchasing power across time — inflation erodes this function.
- As a standard of deferred payment, money denominates future contracts and loans.

### Monetary aggregates (Indian teaching)

- **M1 (narrow money)** includes currency with the public plus demand deposits and other highly liquid components in the RBI teaching map.
- **M3 (broad money)** adds time deposits and related less-liquid deposits to the narrow base in school presentation.
- Liquidity ranking puts **currency** ahead of demand deposits; **time deposits** are less liquid than demand deposits.
- Growth of M3 is watched as a broad liquidity signal; the MPC still targets inflation, not a rigid money-growth rule, in modern teaching.

## 3.2 Banking structure, nationalisation, NPA, digital rails

**India’s banking map** includes public sector banks, private banks, foreign banks, cooperative banks, Regional Rural Banks, Small Finance Banks, Payments Banks and older Local Area Bank lists.

- **Public sector banks** dominate branch reach and priority-sector outreach in historical teaching.
- **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)** were created to serve rural credit needs with a local focus.
- **Cooperative banks** serve members; regulation mixes RBI and State roles.
- **Payments Banks** may take deposits and offer payments but cannot give loans or issue credit cards in the classic teaching rule.
- **Small Finance Banks** lend mainly to underserved segments under a differentiated licence.

### Nationalisation and inclusion

- Major bank nationalisation waves in teaching: **1969** (14 banks) and **1980** (6 banks).
- Nationalisation aimed to expand credit to priority sectors and unserved regions.
- The **Lead Bank Scheme (1969)** assigns a lead bank at district level for credit planning and inclusion outreach.
- Village adoption by bank branches is treated as an inclusion step alongside nationalisation and RRBs in keyed stems.

### NPA and digital rails

- A **Non-Performing Asset (NPA)** is a loan where interest or principal stays overdue beyond the regulatory norm (90 days teaching for term loans).
- High NPAs weaken bank capital and constrain fresh lending.
- **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** directs credit to agriculture, MSME, education, housing and weaker sections in teaching.
- **UPI** is India’s real-time retail payments rail linking bank accounts through a common interface.
- FinTech and digital banking expand access but raise cyber-risk and consumer-protection questions.

## 3.3 NABARD and rural credit chronology

**NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development)** is India’s apex bank for agricultural refinance and rural development finance.

- NABARD was established in **1982**.
- It refinances commercial banks, RRBs and cooperatives for farm and rural lending.
- It is **not** the central bank (that is RBI) and **not** a commercial bank like SBI.
- Land Development Banks (LDBs) are specialised long-term farm-credit institutions — not the apex refinance body.

### Rural credit chronology (must order)

- **Regional Rural Banks** were established in **1975**.
- **NABARD** followed in **1982**.
- The **SHG–Bank Linkage Programme** began in **1992**.
- The **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)** plan began in **1998**.
- Keyed order: RRB → NABARD → SHG linkage → KCC (**4, 1, 2, 3** in the UPPCS stem numbering).
- “One State, one RRB” consolidation is CA-sensitive; March 2025 teaching keyed a wave merging **26** RRBs.

## 3.4 RBI and monetary policy toolkit

**The Reserve Bank of India** is India’s central bank and monetary authority.

- RBI was established in **1935** and nationalised in **1949** in teaching.
- Core functions: issue of currency notes, banker to the government, banker to banks, custodian of forex reserves, and monetary-policy conduct.
- RBI does **not** collect income tax, conduct the population census, or formulate foreign policy.
- Only the **₹1** note / coin lane is a Finance Ministry exception in classic note-issue teaching; higher denominations are RBI-issued.

### Monetary Policy Committee and tools

- The **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** sets the policy repo rate under a flexible inflation-targeting frame.
- India’s retail inflation target is **4% CPI** with a **±2%** tolerance band in teaching.
- **Repo rate** is the rate at which RBI lends to banks against collateral — the policy rate spine.
- **Reverse repo** is the rate RBI pays when banks park funds with it.
- **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)** is the share of deposits banks must keep as cash reserves with RBI.
- **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)** is the share banks must hold in approved liquid securities.
- **Bank Rate / MSF** and open-market operations fine-tune liquidity in teaching.
- Raising repo / CRR / SLR tightens liquidity; cutting them eases it.

## Common Traps

* RBI issues notes (not tax, census, foreign policy); ₹1 is the government exception.
* MPC = **6** members, **Governor** chairs, CPI **4% ± 2%** — not Finance Minister as Chair.
* Repo injects; reverse repo / SDF absorbs; CRR = cash with RBI (no interest); SLR = liquid assets with bank.
* Payments Banks ≠ loan banks; Small Finance Banks can lend.
* Inclusion: nationalisation + RRBs + village adoption; LBS = **Lead Banking Scheme** (not Loan Banking…).
* Rural chronology: **RRB 1975 → NABARD 1982 → SHG 1992 → KCC 1998**.
* NABARD = apex refinance — not “RBI is the agri refinance apex” in the UKPCS key.
* Appreciation ≠ depreciation; exchange-rate regimes deepen in Topic 4/9 as needed.
