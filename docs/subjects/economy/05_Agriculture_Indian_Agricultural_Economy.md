## Consolidated — 42 Must-Score Facts

1. Indian agriculture is still the largest employment absorber relative to its **falling GDP share** — structural dualism continues.
2. Features: monsoon dependence, predominance of **small and marginal** holdings, land fragmentation, mixed cropping in many regions, and rising horticulture / livestock share in value.
3. **Agricultural productivity** is output per hectare (or per worker); yield gaps versus best-practice farms are a standing problem.
4. **Kharif** crops sow with the southwest monsoon (roughly June–July) and harvest in autumn (e.g. rice, maize, cotton, soybean, groundnut teaching).
5. **Rabi** crops sow in winter after monsoon and harvest in spring (e.g. wheat, barley, gram, mustard, potato teaching).
6. **Zaid** is the short summer season between Rabi and Kharif (e.g. watermelon, cucumber, fodder teaching).
7. **Food crops** feed humans / livestock (cereals, pulses, oilseeds in broad school lists). **Cash / commercial crops** are grown mainly for sale (cotton, sugarcane, jute, tobacco, oilseeds in commercial use).
8. **Plantation crops** are estate-style perennial / tree crops — **tea, coffee, rubber, spices** teaching — not wheat or maize.
9. **Horticulture** covers fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices and plantation produce — a fast-growing value segment.
10. India is a top global producer in several crops (rice, wheat, milk, pulses, spices teaching) while remaining a large **consumer** market.
11. **Irrigation** sources: canals, tubewells / wells, tanks. Tubewell density is high in the Green Revolution belt.
12. **Punjab** leads among standard options in **percentage** of cultivated area irrigated.
13. Inputs: quality **seeds**, **fertilizers** (N, P, K), **pesticides**, water, credit, power and machinery.
14. **Soil Health Card** teaching tracks nutrient status; organic carbon and micronutrients matter beyond NPK alone.
15. **Organic farming** avoids synthetic agrochemicals; **natural farming** (e.g. Zero Budget Natural Farming teaching) stresses local biological inputs and low external cost.
16. Mechanisation rises with tractor / harvester density but remains uneven across small holdings.
17. **Agricultural marketing** moves produce from farm to consumer via mandis, private trade, cooperatives, FPOs and e-platforms.
18. **APMC** mandis are state-regulated market yards; reform debates and e-NAM deepen in Topic 6.
19. **CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices)** **recommends** MSP; the **Union government / CCEA** decides and announces.
20. MSP is a floor-price support signal for mandated crops — coverage and procurement reality vary by crop and State (Topic 6 depth).
21. **Agricultural credit** flows through commercial banks, RRBs, cooperatives and NABARD refinance (NABARD identity in Topic 3).
22. **Crop insurance** (e.g. PMFBY lane) hedges yield / weather risk — scheme depth in Topic 6.
23. **FPOs (Farmer Producer Organisations)** aggregate small farmers for input purchase, marketing and bargaining power.
24. **Contract farming** pre-agrees price / quality / buyback between farmers and buyers — risk-sharing with power-asymmetry traps.
25. **Food processing** adds value, cuts wastage and links farms to urban / export demand.
26. Supply chains need storage, cold chains, transport and grading — post-harvest loss is a standing cost.
27. India both **exports** (rice, spices, buffalo meat, sugar in some years, marine products teaching) and **imports** (edible oils, pulses in deficit years, sometimes fertilizer).
28. Problems: monsoon risk, small holdings, fragmentation, soil degradation, groundwater stress, inadequate storage, weak price realisation, and disguised unemployment.
29. **Small and marginal farmers** dominate holding counts; they need credit, insurance and collective marketing more than large estates do.
30. **Agricultural labour** includes landless and near-landless workers — wages and seasonal migration matter for rural livelihoods.
31. **Indirect farm subsidies** in Indian teaching include cheap fertilizers, seeds, power and irrigation support that lower input costs without a pure cash cheque.
32. **Direct farm subsidies / transfers** put cash or income support into farmer accounts (PM-KISAN teaching) — keyed 2022 reading still treated many classic input supports as indirect.
33. WTO agri rules use **Amber / Blue / Green** box teaching — they do **not** simply “allow all direct and ban all indirect” subsidies.
34. Diversified cropping is helped by India’s tropical monsoon geography, but local water and soil still constrain choices.
35. **Mono-cropping** of water-intensive rice–wheat in northwest India raises productivity and also groundwater / residue problems.
36. Livestock and fisheries supplement farm income — mixed farming is common.
37. Land fragmentation raises cost per unit and blocks mechanisation at scale.
38. Credit overdue and informal moneylender dependence remain where formal inclusion is thin.
39. Climate shocks (drought, flood, heat, unseasonal rain) make risk management central.
40. Productivity rises need seed–water–nutrient–knowledge packages together, not one input alone.
41. Gender and unpaid family labour are large in farm work but under-counted in many statistics.
42. Topic border: revolutions, NFSA, PM schemes, detailed MSP maths → **Topic 6**.

## 5.1 Features, seasons and cropping

**Indian agriculture** remains employment-heavy, holding-fragmented and monsoon-sensitive even as its GDP share has fallen.

- A large share of the workforce still depends on farm and allied activity.
- Holdings are predominantly **small and marginal**, which raises cost and limits mechanisation scale.
- Rainfed area remains large; irrigation coverage differs sharply by State.
- Diversification into horticulture, dairy, poultry and fisheries raises value even where cereal area saturates.

### Cropping seasons

- **Kharif** (roughly June–October) depends on the southwest monsoon — rice, maize, cotton, jowar, bajra, groundnut and tur are classic kharif crops.
- **Rabi** (roughly October–March) uses cooler weather and Western Disturbances — wheat, barley, gram, mustard and linseed are classic rabi crops.
- **Zaid** (roughly March–June) covers short-duration summer crops such as melons, cucumber, fodder and vegetables.
- Sugarcane is long-duration and straddles seasons; tea, coffee and rubber are perennial plantation crops.
- Never swap wheat into kharif or cotton into rabi in a correct-pair stem.

### Cropping intensity and patterns

- **Net sown area** is land sown at least once a year.
- **Gross cropped area** counts every sowing in the year.
- **Cropping intensity** = GCA / NSA × 100.
- Mixed farming combines crops with livestock.
- Double cropping means two crops on the same land in one year.

## 5.2 Major crops and plantation agriculture

**Food grains, cash crops and plantations** form the crop map for economic stems.

- **Rice** needs hot-wet conditions and clayey alluvium; methods include transplant, broadcast and DSR teaching.
- **Wheat** is a rabi cereal needing cool growing weather and bright ripening.
- Millets (**jowar, bajra, ragi**) and maize are hardy cereals with strong dryland and feed roles.
- **Pulses** fix nitrogen through Rhizobium and remain central to protein security.
- Oilseeds (groundnut, mustard, soybean) link to the Yellow Revolution neighbour in Topic 6.
- **Cotton** is a kharif fibre crop; **jute** is the eastern golden fibre on alluvial soils.
- **Sugarcane** needs heat and moisture; UP leads quantity teaching while Maharashtra leads productivity / cooperative teaching.
- **Plantation agriculture** grows perennial estate crops such as **tea, coffee and rubber** on large organised holdings.
- Tea, coffee and rubber are the classic plantation answers; wheat, rice and maize are field food crops, not plantations.

## 5.3 Irrigation, inputs and farming systems

**Irrigation and farm inputs** decide yield stability when the monsoon fails or is uneven.

- Sources of irrigation teaching: canals, tubewells / groundwater, tanks and lift systems.
- Groundwater covers a large share of irrigated area in Indian teaching; canals matter on clayey plains.
- **Punjab** leads among common options in share of cultivated area that is irrigated.
- **Command Area Development** improves water use inside an existing command — it is not a new dam.
- Drip and sprinkler raise water-use efficiency in horticulture and dry tracts.
- Seeds, fertilisers, pesticides, credit and extension form the input package of modern farming.
- The seed chain teaching runs **Breeder → Foundation → Certified**.
- Organic farming bans synthetics; **Sikkim** is the first fully organic State in teaching.
- Precision farming uses GIS / GPS for site-specific input management.
- Mixed farming = crops + livestock; conservation agriculture = minimum tillage + residue + rotation.

## 5.4 Marketing, credit, MSP signal and farm subsidies

**Agricultural marketing** moves produce from farm to consumer; **credit** finances the crop cycle; **subsidies** alter input and output prices.

### Marketing and MSP signal

- Produce moves through mandis, private trade, cooperatives, FPOs and e-platforms.
- **CACP recommends** MSP; the **Union Cabinet / CCEA decides** — not RBI, NITI or the Finance Ministry alone.
- MSP is a policy floor against distress sale for mandated crops; it is not a guarantee that every grain is procured everywhere.
- APMC regulation and e-NAM deepen in Topic 6.

### Farm credit

- Formal credit flows through commercial banks, RRBs, cooperatives and NABARD refinance (Topic 3).
- **Kisan Credit Card** eases short-term crop credit in teaching.
- Informal moneylenders still matter where formal reach is thin — higher cost and risk of debt traps.

### Farm subsidies

- **Input subsidies** such as fertiliser are treated as **indirect** farm subsidies in the keyed teaching.
- Cheap **power and irrigation** bill relief is also an input / indirect subsidy — not a direct cash transfer in the classic key.
- WTO agriculture rules do **not** simply “allow all direct and ban all indirect” subsidies.
- Not all Indian farm support is indirect; do not mark that absolute claim as true.
