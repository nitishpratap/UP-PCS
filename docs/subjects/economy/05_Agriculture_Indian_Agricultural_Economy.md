## Consolidated — 72 Must-Score Facts

1. Agriculture and allied sectors contribute approximately **17%–18% of India's Gross Value Added (GVA)** at current prices, but engage **~45%–47% of the nation's total workforce**.
2. According to the **Agricultural Census of India** (conducted quinquennially since 1970–71), agricultural holdings are classified into 5 categories: **Marginal (< 1.00 ha)**, **Small (1.00–2.00 ha)**, **Semi-Medium (2.00–4.00 ha)**, **Medium (4.00–10.00 ha)**, and **Large (≥ 10.00 ha)**.
3. **Small and Marginal Farmers (SMFs, holding < 2.0 ha)** account for **~86.2% of all operational land holdings** in India, but operate only **~47.3% of the total operated area**.
4. **Marginal holdings (< 1.0 ha)** alone constitute **68.5%** of all agricultural land holdings in India, with an average operational size of just **0.38 hectare**.
5. The all-India average size of operational land holdings has consistently declined from **2.28 hectares in 1970–71** to **1.08 hectares in 2015–16** due to generational land subdivision and population pressure.
6. The state having the **highest average size of operational land holdings** in India is **Nagaland** (~5.06 ha) followed by **Punjab** (~3.62 ha) and **Rajasthan** (~2.73 ha); **Kerala** has among the lowest (~0.18 ha).
7. India is divided into **15 broad Agro-Climatic Zones** by the Planning Commission (1988) based on physiography, climate, soil, and cropping patterns; ICAR under NARP classifies India into **127 Agro-Climatic Zones** (and Uttar Pradesh into **9 zones**).
8. **Kharif crops (खरीफ)** are sown with the onset of the South-West Monsoon in **June–July** and harvested in **September–October**; major crops include **Rice, Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut, Soybean, Tur/Arhar, Urad, and Moong**.
9. **Rabi crops (रबी)** are sown in **October–November** (winter onset) and harvested in **March–April** (spring); major crops include **Wheat, Barley, Gram (चना), Mustard (सरसों), Linseed (अलसी), Peas (मटर), and Potato**.
10. **Zaid crops (जायद)** are short-duration summer crops grown from **March to June** between Rabi harvest and Kharif sowing; predominantly include cucurbitaceous crops: **Watermelon (तरबूज), Muskmelon (खरबूजा), Cucumber (खीरा), Bitter gourd (करेला), Moong, and leafy vegetables/fodder**.
11. India is the **world's largest producer** of **Milk, Pulses, Jute, and Spices**; and the **second-largest producer** of **Rice, Wheat, Sugarcane, Groundnut, Vegetables, Fruits, and Cotton** (after China).
12. India is the **largest exporter of Rice** in the world (accounting for ~40% of global rice trade), but is the **largest importer of Pulses and Vegetable Edible Oils**.
13. **Rice (चावल)** is the **most cultivated crop in India**, occupying the largest gross cropped area (~47 million hectares); **West Bengal** is the largest producer of Rice, while **Punjab** has the highest per-hectare productivity.
14. **Wheat (गेहूं)** is the second most important cereal crop in India, occupying ~31 million hectares; **Uttar Pradesh** is the largest producer of Wheat in terms of total absolute output, while **Punjab** records the highest yield/productivity per hectare.
15. **Shree Anna (श्री अन्न / Millets)** was championed by India, leading the UN to declare **2023 as the International Year of Millets**; **Rajasthan** is the largest producer of **Bajra** (Pearl Millet), while **Karnataka/Maharashtra** lead in **Jowar** (Sorghum).
16. Among pulses, **Gram (चना)** accounts for the largest share (~45%–50% of total pulse production in India); **Madhya Pradesh** is the largest pulse-producing state in India.
17. In oilseeds, **Soybean** and **Groundnut** lead production; **Madhya Pradesh & Maharashtra** lead in Soybean, **Gujarat** leads in Groundnut, and **Rajasthan** leads in Rapeseed & Mustard.
18. **Sugarcane (गन्ना)** is a perennial tropical cash crop; **Uttar Pradesh** is the largest producer of Sugarcane and Sugar in India, but Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu record higher sucrose content and recovery rates due to maritime tropical climate.
19. **Cotton (कपास - White Gold)** is predominantly grown in the black regur soil of the Deccan plateau; **Gujarat** and **Maharashtra** are the top producing states.
20. In Net Irrigated Area across India, the source-wise share is: **Tube-wells & Wells (~62%–64%)**, followed by **Canals (~23%–25%)**, and **Tanks (~3%–4%)**.
21. **Uttar Pradesh** possesses the largest canal network and largest tube-well irrigated area in absolute terms in India; **Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh** lead in **Tank irrigation**.
22. The **Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)** was launched in **2015** with the motto *"Har Khet Ko Pani"* and *"Per Drop More Crop"* (focused on micro-irrigation: drip and sprinkler).
23. The ideal, scientifically balanced consumption ratio of **NPK fertilizers** is **4 : 2 : 1** ($N:P:K$); in high-subsidy states like Punjab and Haryana, the actual consumption ratio has severely skewed to over **8 : 3 : 1**.
24. **Urea** is the ONLY fertilizer whose **Maximum Retail Price (MRP) is statutorily fixed** by the Central Government; the difference between the normative delivered cost and the controlled MRP is paid as subsidy directly to manufacturers.
25. **Neem Coated Urea (NCU)** was made **100% mandatory** for all indigenous and imported agricultural urea in **2015**; it prevents illegal diversion to chemical industries and improves Nitrogen Use Efficiency (NUE) by slowing degradation.
26. The **Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS)** scheme was introduced on **1 April 2010** for non-urea P&K fertilizers (**DAP, MOP, Complex fertilizers**), where government fixes an annual per-kg subsidy based on nutrient content ($N, P, K, S$), while MRPs are decontrolled.
27. India is **100% import-dependent** for **Potassic fertilizers (Muriate of Potash - MOP)** as India has no commercially exploitable potash reserves.
28. The world's first commercial **Nano Liquid Urea** and **Nano DAP** plants were established by **IFFCO** at **Kalol, Gujarat**.
29. **PM-PRANAM** (PM Programme for Restoration, Awareness, Nourishment and Amelioration of Mother Earth) was announced in Union Budget 2023–24 to incentivise states to promote alternative fertilizers and reduce chemical fertilizer usage.
30. **Sikkim** was formally declared India's and the world's **First 100% Organic State** in **January 2016**; **Lakshadweep** is the first 100% Organic Union Territory (2020).
31. **Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF)** was pioneered in India by Padma Shri **Subhash Palekar**; it relies on 4 core pillars: **Jeevamrutha** (microbial culture), **Bijamrutha** (seed treatment), **Acchadana** (mulching), and **Whapasa** (soil moisture aeration).
32. In institutional agricultural credit flow, **Commercial Banks** account for the overwhelming majority (**~75%–78%**), followed by **Regional Rural Banks (~12%–14%)** and **Cooperative Banks (~10%–12%)**.
33. Under the **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** norms of the RBI, domestic scheduled commercial banks and foreign banks with ≥20 branches must allocate **18% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)** or Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure (CEOBE) to the **Agricultural sector** (with **10% specifically sub-targeted to Small and Marginal Farmers**).
34. The **National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD)** was established on **12 July 1982** under the NABARD Act, 1981, upon the recommendations of the **B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD)**, taking over the functions of the ACD and RPCC of RBI and the ARDC.
35. The **Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)** was instituted within NABARD in **1995–96** to finance rural infrastructure projects of State Governments using the PSL shortfall of commercial banks.
36. The **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)** scheme was formulated by NABARD in **August 1998** on the recommendations of the **R.V. Gupta Committee** to provide timely, flexible short-term credit to farmers for crop production, post-harvest expenses, and working capital.
37. In 2018–19, the Government of India extended the **Kisan Credit Card (KCC) facility to Fisheries and Animal Husbandry farmers** to meet their working capital requirements.
38. The **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)** was launched in **February 2016** (effective Kharif 2016), replacing NAIS and MNAIS; it charges a uniform low premium of **2.0% for Kharif crops**, **1.5% for Rabi crops**, and **5.0% for Annual Commercial & Horticultural crops**, with the balance actuarial premium subsidized 50:50 by the Centre and States.
39. The **Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)** was established in **January 1965** (initially as the *Agricultural Prices Commission* until renamed in 1985); it functions as an advisory body under the Ministry of Agriculture.
40. The CACP recommends Minimum Support Prices (MSP) for **22 mandated agricultural crops** (14 Kharif, 6 Rabi, 2 commercial: Jute and Copra) and Fair & Remunerative Price (FRP) for **Sugarcane**; the final decision is taken by the **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)** chaired by the Prime Minister.
41. The **National Commission on Farmers (NCF)**, set up in 2004 under **Prof. M.S. Swaminathan**, recommended in its 5th report (2006) that the MSP should be fixed at least **50% above the comprehensive cost of production (Cost C2 + 50%)**.
42. The Central Government officially calculates the 1.5x MSP benchmark against the **Cost A2 + FL** formula (Paid-out costs + imputed family labor value), whereas farmer unions demand MSP based on the higher **Cost C2** (which also includes imputed land rent and interest on owned capital assets).
43. **Electronic National Agriculture Market (e-NAM)** was launched on **14 April 2016** as a pan-India electronic trading portal networking existing APMC mandis to create a unified national market for agricultural commodities; it is managed by the **Small Farmers' Agri-Business Consortium (SFAC)**.
44. The Central Sector Scheme for **Formation and Promotion of 10,000 Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs)** was launched in **2020** with a total budgetary outlay of ₹6,865 crore to achieve economies of scale for small and marginal farmers.
45. Under the **Model APMC Act 2003** and the **APLM Act 2017**, states are encouraged to allow private wholesale markets, direct purchase from farmers, establishment of e-trading platforms, and exemption of fruits and vegetables from mandi fees.
46. The **APEDA** (Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) was established by an Act of Parliament in **December 1985** under the **Ministry of Commerce and Industry** to promote export of agricultural and processed food products.
47. **MPEDA** (Marine Products Export Development Authority) was constituted in **1972** under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry to promote marine and seafood exports.
48. **Operation Flood (ऑपरेशन फ्लड)** was launched in **1970** by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under **Dr. Verghese Kurien** (Father of White Revolution in India), transforming India from a milk-deficient nation into the world's largest milk producer through the Anand cooperative pattern (AMUL).
49. In the **WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA)**, domestic agricultural subsidies are categorized into three color-coded boxes: **Green Box** (non-trade distorting, allowed without limit, e.g. R&D, pest control, environment), **Blue Box** (direct payments under production-limiting programs, allowed), and **Amber Box** (trade-distorting market price support and input subsidies, subject to reduction commitments).
50. Under WTO Amber Box rules, developing countries like India are entitled to a **de-minimis limit of 10%** of the total value of agricultural production; the **Peace Clause** agreed at the **Bali Ministerial Conference (2013)** protects India's food procurement programs under NFSA against legal challenges by developed nations even if the 10% ceiling is breached.

51. Net Sown Area of India is about **140 million hectares**; Gross Cropped Area is about **195–200 million hectares**.
52. Cropping intensity ≈ **GCA/NSA × 100** — national teaching near **~140%**; Punjab is among the highest.
53. Alluvial soil is India's most widespread soil type (~**43%**); black **regur** is the classic **cotton** soil of the Deccan.
54. Laterite soils suit **tea, coffee, rubber, cashew** more than foodgrain belts.
55. India is the world's **largest groundwater user** (~25% of global extraction); tubewells dominate net irrigated area (~**62–64%**).
56. Canals are about **23–25%** of net irrigated area; tanks about **3–4%** (Tamil Nadu / Andhra lead tank irrigation).
57. PMKSY motto pair is **Har Khet Ko Pani** and **Per Drop More Crop** (micro-irrigation).
58. Aus, Aman and Boro are **three seasonal rice** crops in West Bengal / Assam / Odisha belts.
59. Sugarcane is a long-duration crop spanning seasons; cotton is primarily Kharif.
60. Foundation seed comes from breeder seed and carries a **white** tag in seed-class teaching.
61. Gorwala All-India Rural Credit Survey (**1951/54**) recommended creating **SBI** for rural banking thrust.
62. **Prathama Bank** (Moradabad, UP, **2 Oct 1975**) was India's first Regional Rural Bank.
63. Short-term cooperative credit ladder: **PACS** (village) → **DCCB** (district) → **StCB** (state).
64. First Constitutional Amendment **1951** inserted **Article 31B** and the **Ninth Schedule** to protect land-reform laws.
65. **Operation Barga (1978)** recorded sharecroppers in **West Bengal**.
66. UP Consolidation of Holdings Act **1953** made Chakbandi compulsory in the State.
67. Green Revolution HYVP launch teaching parks in the **Plan Holiday** window (**1966–69**), not Second Plan.
68. Norman Borlaug's semi-dwarf wheat work was at **CIMMYT, Mexico**.
69. Foodgrain production teaching peaks near **330 MT** class records in recent Survey desks (edition-bound).
70. Horticulture output often **exceeds** foodgrain tonnage in recent Survey snapshots (edition-bound).
71. Topic border: MSP / FCI / NFSA / colour revolutions deepen in **Topic 6**.
72. Match **producer vs productivity** leaders (e.g. UP wheat output vs Punjab wheat yield) before picking keys.

## 5.1 Agrarian Structure and Operational Holdings

- **Primary Sector Contribution:** Agriculture, forestry, and fishing contribute approximately **17%–18% of India's Gross Value Added (GVA)** at current basic prices. Despite its declining economic share in GDP (from ~54% in 1950–51), it remains the backbone of the rural economy, directly employing **~45%–47% of the Indian workforce** according to the Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS).
- **The Land Holding Paradigm:** The structure of land ownership in India is characterised by acute **fragmentation** and dominance of small, economically unviable plots.
- **Agricultural Census Findings (2015–16):**
  - **Marginal Farmers (< 1 ha):** Constitute **68.5%** of all operational holdings (operating ~24.0% of area).
  - **Small Farmers (1–2 ha):** Constitute **17.7%** of holdings (operating ~23.0% of area).
  - **Combined Small & Marginal Farmers (SMFs, < 2 ha):** Constitute **86.2%** of all holdings in India, controlling **47.3%** of total operated area.
  - **Medium & Large Farmers (> 4 ha):** Make up less than **4.4%** of holdings, but control over **30.9%** of operated land.
  - **Average Operational Size:** Declined from **2.28 ha in 1970–71** to **1.15 ha in 2010–11**, and further to **1.08 ha in 2015–16**.
- **Economic Implications of Fragmentation:**
  1. Prevents economies of scale in farm operations.
  2. Severely limits mechanisation (e.g. combine harvesters cannot navigate fragmented strips).
  3. High transaction costs for institutional credit delivery.
  4. Farmers remain vulnerable to climate volatility, price shocks, and local moneylenders.

> **Logic:** Remember the cut-off thresholds for UPPSC: Marginal is $<1	ext{ ha}$; Small is $1-2	ext{ ha}$; Semi-Medium is $2-4	ext{ ha}$; Medium is $4-10	ext{ ha}$; Large is $\ge 10	ext{ ha}$. Over 86% of Indian farmers are Small or Marginal!

## 5.2 Cropping Patterns & Major Crops in India

- **Cropping Pattern (शस्य प्रतिरूप):** Refers to the proportion of area under different crops at a given point in time in a region.
- In India, **food grains (खाद्यान्न)** occupy approximately **65%–68% of the gross cropped area**, reflecting the subsistence orientation of the agrarian structure, while **commercial/cash crops (नकदी फसलें)** occupy ~32%–35%.
- **Gross Cropped Area (GCA) vs Net Sown Area (NSA):**
  - **Net Sown Area (NSA):** The total physical land area sown with crops at least once in an agricultural year (~139–140 million hectares in India).
  - **Gross Cropped Area (GCA):** Total area sown once as well as more than once in the year (~198–200 million hectares).
  - **Cropping Intensity (शस्य सघनता):** Calculated as:
    $$\text{Cropping Intensity} = \left( \frac{\text{Gross Cropped Area}}{\text{Net Sown Area}} \right) \times 100$$
    India's average cropping intensity is approximately **~142%–143%**. **Punjab** records the highest cropping intensity (>190%), while rainfed dryland states record lower ratios (~115%–125%).

### Detailed Crop Profiles & Leading State Producers

#### 1. Rice (चावल / Paddy - *Oryza sativa*)
- **Status:** Primary staple food crop of India; occupies the largest gross cropped area (~47 million hectares).
- **Agro-climatic Needs:** Warm and humid tropical climate; mean temperature $22^{\circ}\text{C} - 32^{\circ}\text{C}$; annual rainfall $100 - 200\text{ cm}$; deep clayey or loamy alluvial soils with water-retaining capacity.
- **Leading Producers:** **West Bengal (1st)**, **Uttar Pradesh (2nd)**, **Punjab (3rd)**.
- **Productivity Champion:** **Punjab** records the highest yield per hectare (>4,300 kg/ha) due to 100% canal and tube-well irrigation and high fertilizer consumption.
- **Aus, Aman, Boro:** In West Bengal, Assam, and Odisha, three seasonal rice crops are harvested in a single year: **Aus** (autumn rice, sown May/June), **Aman** (winter rice, sown June/July), and **Boro** (summer rice, sown Nov/Dec).

#### 2. Wheat (गेहूं - *Triticum aestivum*)
- **Status:** Second most crucial cereal crop; backbone of northern India's food security and public distribution system.
- **Agro-climatic Needs:** Temperate rabi crop; cool growing period ($10^{\circ}\text{C} - 15^{\circ}\text{C}$) and warm sunny weather during ripening ($20^{\circ}\text{C} - 25^{\circ}\text{C}$); rainfall $50 - 75\text{ cm}$; well-drained fertile loamy soils.
- **Western Disturbances (पश्चिमी विक्षोभ):** Light winter cyclonic rainfall in December–January across Punjab, Haryana, and Western UP is extremely beneficial for wheat tillering and grain filling.
- **Leading Producers:** **Uttar Pradesh (1st in absolute output ~32%–34% of national total)**, **Madhya Pradesh (2nd)**, **Punjab (3rd)**.
- **Productivity Champion:** **Punjab and Haryana** lead in yield (>4,800 kg/ha).

#### 3. Shree Anna / Millets (मोटे अनाज)
- **Crops:** **Bajra (Pearl Millet)**, **Jowar (Sorghum)**, **Ragi (Finger Millet)**, Small millets (Kodo, Kutki, Sanwa, Cheena).
- **Nutritional & Climate Significance:** Highly drought-resistant, climate-resilient C4 plants requiring minimal irrigation and chemical fertilizers; rich in dietary fiber, iron, calcium, and micronutrients.
- **Policy Push:** Championed by India, the UN General Assembly declared **2023 as the International Year of Millets (IYM)**. The Government rebranded millets as **"Shree Anna"** and designated ICAR-Indian Institute of Millets Research (IIMR), Hyderabad as a Global Centre of Excellence.
- **Leading Producers:**
  - **Bajra:** **Rajasthan (1st)**, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat.
  - **Jowar:** **Maharashtra (1st)**, Karnataka.
  - **Ragi:** **Karnataka (1st)**.

#### 4. Pulses (दलहन)
- **Status:** Vital source of plant protein in vegetarian diets; leguminous crops that fix atmospheric nitrogen through *Rhizobium* root nodules, naturally restoring soil fertility.
- **Major Crops:** **Gram (चना - ~45% of total pulses)**, **Tur/Arhar (अरहर/तूर)**, **Urad (उड़द)**, **Moong (मूंग)**, **Masoor (मसूर)**.
- **Leading Producers:** **Madhya Pradesh (1st)**, **Rajasthan (2nd)**, **Maharashtra (3rd)**.
- **Economic Paradox:** India is the **world's largest producer (25%)**, **largest consumer (27%)**, and **largest importer (14%)** of pulses.

#### 5. Oilseeds & Cash Crops
- **Oilseeds (तिलहन):** India meets ~55%–60% of its edible oil consumption through imports (predominantly Palm oil from Indonesia/Malaysia and Soybean/Sunflower oil from South America/Ukraine).
  - **Soybean:** **Madhya Pradesh & Maharashtra** (Madhya Pradesh is called *Soya State*).
  - **Groundnut:** **Gujarat (1st)**, Rajasthan.
  - **Rapeseed & Mustard:** **Rajasthan (1st)**, Madhya Pradesh, Haryana.
- **Sugarcane (गन्ना):** **Uttar Pradesh (1st in area & production)**, Maharashtra, Karnataka.
- **Cotton (कपास):** **Gujarat (1st)**, Maharashtra, Telangana.
- **Jute (जूट - Golden Fibre):** **West Bengal (1st ~75%–80% of national total)**, Bihar, Assam.

## 5.3 Irrigation Architecture and Water Management

- **The Irrigation Imperative:** More than **50% of India's Net Sown Area is rainfed (बारानी कृषि)**, making agricultural output heavily sensitive to the spatial and temporal vagaries of the Indian Summer Monsoon.
- **Irrigation Status:** Gross Irrigated Area is approximately **102–103 million hectares**, while Net Irrigated Area is **~70–73 million hectares**.

### Source-wise Share in Net Irrigated Area of India

| Irrigation Source | National Share (%) | Geographical Distribution & Leading States | Economic Advantages & Environmental Hazards |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Wells & Tube-wells (नलकूप एवं कुएं)** | **~62% – 64%** (Dominant Source) | Heavily concentrated in the Indo-Gangetic Plains: **Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Bihar**. | **Advantages:** Independent farmer control, on-demand irrigation, high application efficiency.<br>**Hazards:** Over-exploitation of aquifers, creating "dark zones" and ground subsidence; power subsidy distortion. |
| **Canals (नहरें)** | **~23% – 25%** (Second Largest) | Extensive in alluvial river plains: **Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan (Indira Gandhi Canal)**. | **Advantages:** Harnesses perennial river runoff; low recurring operational cost for farmers.<br>**Hazards:** Waterlogging (*Seep*), soil salinization and alkalinization (*Reh/Kallar*), siltation of canals. |
| **Tanks (तालाब / जलाशय)** | **~3% – 4%** | Ancient indigenous system prevalent in the hard-rock Deccan plateau: **Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka**. | **Advantages:** Eco-friendly, rainwater harvesting, recharges groundwater.<br>**Hazards:** Silt accumulation, highly dependent on local seasonal rainfall, seasonal drying. |
| **Other Sources (Lifts, Springs, Streams)** | **~7% – 9%** | Mountainous valleys, coastal deltas, wetlands: **Uttarakhand, Himachal, North-Eastern states**. | Localized small-scale mountain systems (*Kuhl* in Himachal). |

### Flagship Irrigation Schemes

#### 1. Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)
- **Launched:** **July 2015** with an overarching objective to expand cultivable area under assured irrigation and improve on-farm water use efficiency.
- **Motto:** *"Har Khet Ko Pani"* (water to every field) and *"More Crop Per Drop"* (enhancing water productivity).
- **Core Components:**
  1. **Accelerated Irrigation Benefit Programme (AIBP):** Focuses on faster completion of ongoing major and medium irrigation projects.
  2. **PMKSY - Har Khet Ko Pani:** Creating new water sources through minor irrigation (both surface and groundwater), repair, restoration and renovation (RRR) of traditional water bodies.
  3. **PMKSY - Per Drop More Crop (PDMC):** Promotes micro-irrigation technologies (**Drip Irrigation** and **Sprinkler Irrigation**) to save water (up to 40%–50%) and reduce fertilizer wastage through fertigation.
  4. **PMKSY - Watershed Development:** Ridge-to-valley soil and moisture conservation, check dams, and rainwater harvesting.

## 5.4 Agricultural Inputs: Seeds, Fertilizers, and Mechanisation

### A. Seeds (बीज) — The Fundamental Biological Catalyst
- Seed is the most vital input determining genetic crop productivity; **Seed Replacement Rate (SRR)** is the percentage of total cropped area sown with certified/quality seeds relative to farm-saved seeds.
- **Classes of Seeds in India:**
  1. **Breeder Seed (प्रजनक बीज):** Produced under direct supervision of the originating plant breeder at ICAR institutes/State Agricultural Universities; 100% genetic purity; tagged with **Golden Yellow label**.
  2. **Foundation Seed (आधार बीज):** Progeny of breeder seed produced by National Seeds Corporation (NSC) or State Seed Corporations; tagged with **White label**.
  3. **Certified Seed (प्रमाणित बीज):** Progeny of foundation seed certified by State Seed Certification Agencies and sold directly to commercial farmers; tagged with **Azure Blue label**.

### B. Chemical Fertilizers & Subsidy Mechanisms
- **Recommended NPK Ratio:** **4 : 2 : 1** ($N:P:K$). Actual consumption in several high-yield states has skewed past **8:3:1** due to disproportionate urea subsidization.
- **1. Urea Subsidy Regime:**
  - Urea is the **only fertilizer remaining under statutory price control (fixed statutory MRP)**.
  - The difference between the delivered cost of production of urea and its government-notified retail price is disbursed directly to urea manufacturers as a subsidy under the **New Urea Policy (NUP)**.
  - **100% Mandatory Neem Coated Urea (NCU, 2015):** Mandatory coating with neem oil slows nitrification in soil, preventing groundwater leaching and eliminating illegal diversion of subsidized urea to chemical, paint, and plywood industries.
- **2. Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS) Scheme (2010):**
  - Introduced in **April 2010** under the Department of Fertilizers for non-urea P&K fertilizers (**DAP, MOP, NPK complexes, Single Super Phosphate - SSP**).
  - Central Government announces a **fixed annual per-kilogram subsidy** for each nutrient ($N$, $P$, $K$, and Sulphur $S$).
  - **MRPs of P&K fertilizers are decontrolled** and determined by market dynamics and global raw material costs.
  - **Potash Import Dependence:** India possesses **zero commercially extractable reserves of Potash** and is **100% dependent on imports** for Muriate of Potash (MOP).
- **3. Nano Fertilizers:**
  - Developed and patented by IFFCO; **Nano Urea (Liquid)** and **Nano DAP**.
  - A 500 ml bottle replaces an entire 45 kg bag of conventional granular urea, cutting logistics costs, reducing soil acidification, and raising nutrient use efficiency from ~30% to over ~80%.

> **Traps:** Urea = statutorily controlled MRP; P&K (DAP, MOP) = decontrolled MRP under NBS. India has 100% import dependence for Potash (MOP)!

## 5.5 Sustainable, Organic and Natural Farming

### 1. Organic Farming (जैविक कृषि)
- Agricultural practice that avoids synthetic chemical fertilizers, chemical pesticides, growth regulators, and genetically modified organisms (GMOs).
- Relies on crop rotation, green manures, animal manures, bio-fertilizers (*Azotobacter, Rhizobium, PSB*), and biological pest control.
- **Sikkim Landmark:** Formally conferred status as the **World's First 100% Organic State** by the UN FAO in **2016** (Future Policy Gold Award).
- **Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY):** Launched in **2015** to promote cluster-based organic farming through **Participatory Guarantee System (PGS-India)** certification without incurring heavy third-party audit fees.

### 2. Natural Farming vs Organic Farming
- **Organic farming** often permits off-farm organic inputs (e.g. commercially purchased vermicompost, bio-fertilizers) and requires rigorous certification.
- **Natural farming** strictly prohibits all external purchased inputs; all soil nutrients and pest repellents must be prepared on-farm using indigenous cow dung, cow urine, and local biomass.
- **Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF):**
  - Pioneered by Padma Shri **Subhash Palekar** in Maharashtra and Karnataka.
  - **Four Pillars of ZBNF:**
    1. **Jeevamrutha (जीवामृत):** Fermented liquid microbial inoculant made of fresh indigenous cow dung, cow urine, jaggery, pulse flour, virgin soil, and water; stimulates soil earthworms and microbial activity.
    2. **Bijamrutha (बीजामृत):** Seed treatment concoction using cow dung, urine, and lime to protect young seedlings from seed-borne and soil-borne fungal pathogens.
    3. **Acchadana (आच्छादन - Mulching):** Soil, straw, and live mulching to preserve topsoil moisture and suppress weeds.
    4. **Whapasa (वापसा):** Micro-climatic soil moisture aeration condition where both air and water molecules coexist in equal balance in root zones, slashing water needs by over 80%.

## 5.6 Agricultural Marketing, Mandis and Supply Chains

- **The Traditional APMC Structure:**
  - Agricultural marketing is a **State subject** under Entry 28 of List II (State List) of the Seventh Schedule.
  - Historically, state governments established **Agricultural Produce Market Committees (APMCs)** to protect farmers from exploitation by middlemen and moneylenders.
  - **APMC Market Failures:** Over time, APMC mandis degenerated into cartels, charging steep mandi fees, multiple levies, licensing barriers for new traders, lack of transparent electronic bidding, and high post-harvest losses.
- **Model APMC Act (2003) & APLM Act (2017):**
  - Drafted by the Central Government to nudge states to allow private wholesale markets, direct purchase from farmers, single-point levy of market fee, and promotion of public-private partnerships.
- **Electronic National Agriculture Market (e-NAM):**
  - **Launched:** **14 April 2016** by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
  - **Core Architecture:** A virtual electronic trading platform that digitally integrates existing physical APMC mandis across India to create a unified national agricultural market.
  - **Nodal Implementing Agency:** **Small Farmers' Agri-Business Consortium (SFAC)** under the Ministry of Agriculture.
  - **Benefits:** Online transparent competitive bidding, assaying and quality certification facilities at mandi gate, electronic online payments directly into farmers' bank accounts, eliminating local commission agent cartels.
- **Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs):**
  - Institutional aggregation of small and marginal farmers into producer companies under the Companies Act, 2013.
  - Enhances smallholder bargaining power in purchasing farm inputs at wholesale rates and selling produce directly to institutional buyers and exporters.
  - Central Sector Scheme: **Formation and Promotion of 10,000 FPOs** launched in **2020** with a dedicated credit guarantee fund in NABARD.

## 5.7 Institutional Agricultural Credit and Rural Finance

- **Historical Perspective:** At Independence, non-institutional informal sources (village moneylenders, traders, zamindars) supplied over **93% of all rural credit** at usurious interest rates (All-India Rural Credit Survey Committee, 1954).
- Today, **formal institutional sources supply over 75%** of agricultural credit, while informal sources account for under 25%.

### Institutional Lenders Profile

1. **Commercial Banks (Public and Private Banks):**
   - Provide the lion's share (**~75%–78%**) of total institutional farm credit disbursed annually.
   - Excel in disbursing agricultural term loans (for tractors, solar pumps, cold storage).
2. **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs - क्षेत्रीय ग्रामीण बैंक):**
   - Established under the **RRB Act, 1976** based on the recommendations of the **M. Narasimham Working Group (1975)**.
   - First RRB: **Prathama Bank** (Moradabad, UP, sponsored by Syndicate Bank on 2 October 1975).
   - Equity ownership: **50% Central Govt : 15% State Govt : 35% Sponsor Bank**.
   - Account for **~12%–14%** of agricultural credit.
3. **Cooperative Credit Institutions (सहकारी ऋण संस्थाएं):**
   - Short-term rural cooperative credit structure is three-tiered:
     1. **PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies)** at the village level.
     2. **DCCB (District Central Cooperative Banks)** at the district level.
     3. **StCB (State Cooperative Banks)** at the apex state level.
   - Account for **~10%–12%** of agricultural credit, but hold the largest rural customer footprint among marginal farmers.

### Apex Institutions and Priority Lending Mandates

#### 1. NABARD (राष्ट्रीय कृषि और ग्रामीण विकास बैंक)
- **Established:** **12 July 1982** under the NABARD Act, 1981, upon the recommendations of the **B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD)**.
- **Mandate:** Apex development and refinancing bank for agriculture, small-scale industries, cottage and village industries, handicrafts, and rural crafts.
- **Equity:** 100% owned by the Government of India (RBI transferred its share to the Govt).
- **Core Facilities:**
  - Refinances Commercial Banks, RRBs, and State Cooperative Banks for short-term and long-term agricultural lending.
  - Manages the **Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)** set up in **1995–96**.
  - Operates the **Long Term Irrigation Fund (LTIF)** and **Micro Irrigation Fund (MIF)**.

#### 2. Kisan Credit Card (KCC) Scheme
- **Introduced:** **August 1998** by NABARD and RBI on the recommendations of the **R.V. Gupta Committee**.
- **Objective:** Provide adequate, timely, and cost-effective credit to farmers under a single window for:
  1. Short-term crop cultivation expenses.
  2. Post-harvest expenses and produce marketing loans.
  3. Working capital for maintenance of farm assets and allied activities.
  4. Consumption requirements of farmer households (up to 10%).
- **Interest Subvention Scheme (ISS) / Modified ISS:**
  - Benchmark short-term crop loan up to **₹3 lakh** is provided at a subsidized base interest rate of **7%**.
  - Farmers ensuring **prompt repayment** receive an additional **3% prompt repayment incentive (PRI)**, bringing the effective interest rate down to just **4% per annum**.
- **Expanded Scope (2018–19):** KCC facility extended to **Animal Husbandry, Dairy, and Fisheries** farmers for working capital needs up to ₹2 lakh at similar concessional interest rates.

## 5.8 Agricultural Risk, Insurance & Allied Economy

### A. Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)
- **Launched:** **February 2016** (operational from Kharif 2016 season) under the Ministry of Agriculture.
- **Replaced:** National Agricultural Insurance Scheme (NAIS) and Modified NAIS (MNAIS).
- **Uniform Low Premium Paid by Farmers:**
  - **2.0%** of sum insured for all **Kharif food and oilseed crops**.
  - **1.5%** of sum insured for all **Rabi food and oilseed crops**.
  - **5.0%** of sum insured for all **Annual Commercial and Horticultural crops**.
- **Subsidy Sharing:** The difference between actuarial premium rates and farmer's premium is shared **50:50 between the Central and State Governments** (90:10 for North-Eastern States).
- **Key Features:**
  - Comprehensive risk coverage: prevented sowing/planting risk, localized calamities (landslide, hailstorm, inundation), mid-season adversity, and post-harvest losses (up to 14 days for crops kept in "cut-and-spread" condition in the field).
  - Uses modern remote sensing, drones, and satellite imagery for rapid Crop Cutting Experiments (CCEs) and direct DBT claim settlements.
  - Made **voluntary for all farmers** (including loanee farmers) since Kharif 2020.

### B. Allied Agricultural Sectors

#### 1. Animal Husbandry & Dairy (पशुपालन एवं डेयरी)
- India is the **world's largest milk producer**, contributing ~24% of global milk output (~230 million tonnes in 2022–23).
- **Operation Flood (ऑपरेशन फ्लड):** Launched in **1970** by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) headed by **Dr. Verghese Kurien**; established the three-tier cooperative milk federation pattern (Anand model / AMUL).
- **Rashtriya Gokul Mission (2014):** Focused on indigenous bovine breed conservation and productivity enhancement.

#### 2. Fisheries Sector & Blue Revolution (नीली क्रांति)
- India is the **second-largest fish producing country** (after China), contributing ~8% to global fish production.
- **Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY):** Launched in **May 2020** with an outlay of ₹20,050 crore to modernize fish infrastructure, harbor landing centers, cage culture, and boost seafood exports.

## 5.9 Structural Challenges & WTO Trade Regimes

### A. Disguised Unemployment in Agriculture (प्रच्छन्न बेरोजगारी)
- A socio-economic condition where **more people are engaged in an economic activity than fundamentally necessary**.
- If surplus laborers are withdrawn from the agricultural plot, the **total physical agricultural output remains completely unchanged**.
- Therefore, the **marginal physical productivity of surplus farm labor is ZERO (or negative)**.
- Caused by lack of alternative non-farm rural industrial employment, high population growth, and joint-family tenure systems.

### B. WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA) — The Three Subsidy Boxes
- Negotiated during the Uruguay Round (1986–1994) to reduce agricultural trade distortions and export subsidies.

```
                           WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA) Subsidies
                                                │
         ┌──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                      ▼                                      ▼
    [Green Box]                            [Blue Box]                             [Amber Box]
  • Completely non-trade distorting      • Direct payments under production-    • Trade-distorting price supports
  • Allowed without any cap              limiting programs                      • MSP, fertilizer & power subsidies
  • R&D, pest control, infrastructure,   • E.g. Acreage reduction payments      • Developing country cap: 10%
    disaster relief, environment aid     • Exempted from reduction              • India protected by Bali Peace Clause
```

1. **Green Box (हरा बॉक्स):**
   - Subsidies that cause **zero or minimal trade-distorting effects**.
   - Must be publicly funded and not involve price support.
   - Examples: Agricultural research, extension training, pest control, rural infrastructure, direct decoupled income transfers, disaster relief payments, and environmental conservation programs.
   - **Status:** **Allowed without any financial ceiling**.
2. **Blue Box (नीला बॉक्स):**
   - Direct government payments to farmers under **production-limiting programs** (based on fixed area and yields or a fixed number of livestock).
   - Designed to avoid market glut.
   - **Status:** **Exempted from reduction commitments**.
3. **Amber Box (अंबर बॉक्स):**
   - All domestic support measures that directly **distort international production and trade** by affecting market prices.
   - Examples: **Minimum Support Prices (MSP)**, input subsidies on fertilizers, canal water, electricity, and seeds.
   - **Ceilings (*De-minimis* Limits):**
     - Developed nations: Capped at **5%** of their total value of agricultural production.
     - Developing nations (India): Capped at **10%** of their total agricultural production value.
4. **The Peace Clause & Bali Ministerial Conference (2013):**
   - India successfully negotiated the **Peace Clause** at the 9th WTO Ministerial Conference in Bali (2013), and made it permanent in 2014.
   - **Mandate:** Protects developing countries from being challenged or penalized under WTO dispute settlement mechanisms if their domestic food security procurement programs (such as MSP-backed procurement for the National Food Security Act, 2013) breach the 10% Amber Box *de-minimis* threshold.
