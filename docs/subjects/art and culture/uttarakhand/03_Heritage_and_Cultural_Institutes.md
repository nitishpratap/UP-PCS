## 1. World Heritage (विश्व धरोहर) & Monumental Sites in Uttarakhand

### 1.1 UNESCO World Heritage Sites (WHS)
- **Unified Property:** **Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks** constitute a single composite serial UNESCO Natural World Heritage Site (प्राकृतिक विश्व धरोहर स्थल) located in the Chamoli high Himalaya (हिमालय).
  - *Nanda Devi National Park:* Inscribed in **1988** (core zone (कोर क्षेत्र) of high-altitude alpine wilderness around Mount Nanda Devi, 7,816 m).
  - *Valley of Flowers National Park:* Inscribed as an extension in **2005** (discovered in 1931 by British mountaineer Frank S. Smythe; famed for endemic alpine blooms).
- **Exam Traps:** 
  - Kaziranga (काजीरंगा) National Park (Assam) and the Mountain Railways of India (Darjeeling, Nilgiri (नीलगिरि), Kalka-Shimla) are World Heritage sites located **outside Uttarakhand**.
  - In UKPCS Prelims 2025 (Q52), the stem tested this exact pair: the keyed correct answer was **Valley of Flowers and Nanda Devi National Park only**.

### 1.2 UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (ICH)
- **Ramman Ritual Theatre:** Inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List (प्रतिनिधि) of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity on **2 October 2009**.
- **Location:** Village **Saloor-Dungra** (सलूर-डुंगरा), Painkhanda / Joshimath block, **Chamoli district**.
- **Context:** Annual late-April spring festival dedicated to **Bhumiyal Devta** (local earth guardian spirit).
- **Key Features:** Masked dance drama enacting episodes from the *Ramayana* (रामायण) without spoken dialogues; 18 wooden masks (*Dyot Pattar* for sacred deities like Lord Narsingh; *Khyari Pattar* for historical/secular figures); martial *Mal-Nrit* representing Garhwali–Gorkha warfare.

## 2. Garhwal Painting: The Pahari (पहाड़ी) Art Heritage of Uttarakhand

```
                    Evolution of Garhwal Painting
                                  │
      May 1658: Arrival of Mughal Prince Sulaiman Shikoh at Srinagar
                 Accompanied by royal painters Shyamdas & Hardas
                                  │
      Raja Prithvipati Shah confers title & post of 'Tasbirdar' (Picture-maker)
                                  │
      Lineage: Shyamdas ➔ Hardas ➔ Hiralal ➔ Mangatram ➔ Mola Ram (1743–1833)
                                  │
      Mola Ram blossoms as supreme master (Painter, Poet, Historian, Philosopher)
                                  │
      Later exponents: Chetu and Manku (Patronized by Raja Sudarshan Shah at Tehri)
                                  │
      Rediscovery: Barrister Mukandi Lal recovers lost art; writes 'Garhwal Painting' (1969)
```

### 2.1 Origin & Historical Genesis (1658)
- In **May 1658**, Prince Sulaiman Shikoh (eldest son of Dara (दारा) Shikoh (दारा शिकोह)) fled the wrath of Aurangzeb (औरंगजेब) and sought political asylum in Srinagar Garhwal at the court of the valiant Parmar King **Raja Prithvipati Shah**.
- Accompanying the prince's royal entourage were two master Mughal miniature (लघु चित्र) painters — a father-son duo named **Shyamdas** and **Hardas**.
- Even after Sulaiman Shikoh was later betrayed and handed over to the Mughals, Shyamdas and Hardas remained in Srinagar Garhwal.
- Raja Prithvipati Shah granted them royal court patronage and conferred upon them the high administrative-artistic court post of ***Tasbirdar*** (court portraitist/picture-maker).
- This 1658 event laid the foundation of the distinctive **Garhwal Branch of the Pahari School of Painting**.

### 2.2 Mola Ram (1743–1833): The Towering Legend
- **Lineage:** Great-grandson of Hardas (Hardas $\rightarrow$ Hiralal $\rightarrow$ Mangatram $\rightarrow$ **Mola Ram**).
- **Royal Patronage:** Flourished across the reigns of four (चातुर्याम) Garhwal monarchs: **Pradip Shah**, **Lalit Shah**, **Jaikrit Shah**, and **Pradyumna Shah**.
- **Artistic Style:** Synthesized delicate Mughal realism with lyrical Pahari romanticism, Kangra (कांगड़ा) fluidity, and dramatic Himalayan landscapes (curving rivers, starry skies, blossoming Buransh (बुरांश), and delicate female figures).
- **Masterpiece Paintings:**
  - *Chandramukhi* (The Moon-Faced Maiden)
  - *Mayur-Vihar* (Peacock in Flight)
  - *Utkanthita Nayika (नायिका)* (उत्कंठिता नायिका)
  - *Abhisarika Nayika* (अभिसारिका नायिका)
  - *Mastani*
  - *Radha-Krishna Kunj Vihar*
- **Literary Masterpiece:** Mola Ram was also an accomplished poet and historian in Brajbhasha; authored ***Gadhrajvansh Kavya** (गढ़राजवंश काव्य)* (history of the Parmar dynasty of Garhwal) and *Manmath Sagar*.

### 2.3 Chetu, Manku & the Tehri (टिहरी) Emigration
- Following the Gorkha invasion of Garhwal (1804) and the subsequent (परवर्ती) British partition of Garhwal (1815), Raja **Sudarshan Shah** founded his new capital at **Tehri** (टिहरी).
- Two outstanding painter disciples of Mola Ram's school — **Chetu** and **Manku** — migrated to Tehri and received the patronage of Raja Sudarshan Shah.
- *Manku's Masterpiece:* The illustrated manuscript of Jayadeva (जयदेव)'s ***Gita Govinda** (गीत गोविंद)* (1730s/Tehri cycle).
- *Chetu's Masterpiece:* The illustrated sequence of the *Ramayana* and *Rukmini Mangal*.

### 2.4 Rediscovery by Barrister Mukandi Lal
- After the death of Mola Ram and the dispersal of the court, Garhwal painting fell into complete oblivion and was often misclassified under the Kangra or Basholi schools.
- Celebrated lawyer, freedom fighter, and art connoisseur **Barrister Mukandi Lal** (of Lansdowne (लैंसडाउन)/Pauri (पौड़ी)) spent decades collecting surviving paintings, sketches, and manuscripts from Mola Ram's descendants.
- In **1969**, the Publications Division, Government of India, published Mukandi Lal's landmark monumental book: ***Garhwal Painting***, which introduced the genius of Mola Ram and the Garhwal Pahari school to the international art world.

## 4. Notable Modern Authors & Cultural Icons of Uttarakhand

- **Pushpesh Pant:** Renowned international relations scholar, culinary historian, and Padma (पद्मा) Shri awardee from Uttarakhand.
  - *Known Authentic Works:* ***Lazzatnama** (लज़्ज़तनामा)*, ***From the King's Table to Street Food***, *India: The Cookbook*, *Victory Mantra*.
  - *Exam Trap (UKPCS 2025 Q49):* In the 2025 prelims stem asking which title is NOT written by Pushpesh Pant, the keyed incorrect title was ***Himalaya Ke Barf** (हिमालय के बर्फ)*.
- **Dr. D. D. Sharma:** Celebrated linguist from Kumaon (कुमाऊँ); received Padma Shri for encyclopedic documentation of Central Himalayan and Tibeto-Burman languages.
- **Prof. Shekhar Pathak:** Historian, geographer, and founder of the research journal *PAHAR*; led the decadal *Askot-Arakot Abhiyan* foot-march; author of *The Chipko (चिपको) Movement (चिपको आंदोलन): A People's History*.
