## 2. Classical Languages of India (Chronology & Criteria)

### A. Chronological List of Recognized Classical Languages
1. **Tamil** (Declared in **2004**) — First language to receive the status.
2. **Sanskrit** (Declared in **2005**).
3. **Telugu** (Declared in **2008**).
4. **Kannada** (Declared in **2008**).
5. **Malayalam** (Declared in **2013**).
6. **Odia** (Declared in **2014**).
7. **Marathi** (Approved by Union Cabinet on **3 October 2024**).
8. **Pali** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).
9. **Prakrit** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).
10. **Assamese** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).
11. **Bengali** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).

*(Now total **11 Classical Languages** in India!)*

### B. Eligibility Criteria (Ministry of Culture)
- High antiquity of early texts / recorded history over a period of **1500–2000 years**.
- A body of ancient literature/texts considered a valuable heritage by generations of speakers.
- Literary tradition must be **original and not borrowed** from another speech community.
- Classical language and literature being distinct from modern, there may also be a discontinuity between the classical language and its later forms or offshoots.

## 3. Temple Architecture Styles & Architectural Anatomy

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 NAGARA vs DRAVIDA ANATOMY                   │
├──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┤
│ NAGARA (North India)         │ DRAVIDA (South India)        │
│ • Curvilinear Shikhara       │ • Stepped-Pyramidal Vimana   │
│ • Amalaka & Kalasha on top   │ • Octagonal/Dome Stupika top │
│ • Raised plinth (Jagati)     │ • Elaborate boundary wall    │
│ • No huge boundary walls     │ • Towering Gateways: GOPURAM │
│ • Water tank not essential   │ • Water tank (Kalyani) inside│
│ • Rekha-Deula / Phamsana     │ • Pillared hall (Mandapas)   │
└──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

### A. Core Architectural Components (Vocabulary Goldmine)
- **Garbhagriha**: Sanctum Sanctorum (innermost chamber housing the principal deity).
- **Mandapa**: Pillared assembly hall leading to the sanctum for devotees.
- **Antarala**: Vestibule / intermediate foyer connecting Garbhagriha and Mandapa.
- **Shikhara** (North) / **Vimana** (South): Mountain-like spire rising above the Garbhagriha.
- **Amalaka**: Stone disc with fluted edges resembling a myrobalan fruit, crowning the North Indian Shikhara.
- **Kalasha**: Pitcher/pot shaped finial installed atop the Amalaka.
- **Gopuram**: Colossal, monumental decorated gateway of South Indian temples.
- **Panchayatana Style**: Main central temple surrounded symmetrically by **four subsidiary shrines** at the four corners (e.g., Dashavatara Temple Deogarh, Kandariya Mahadeva Khajuraho, Lakshmana Temple Khajuraho, Brahmeswara Temple Bhubaneswar).

## 6. Traditional Folk Theatres & Martial Arts

### A. Folk Theatres of India
- **Nautanki**: Uttar Pradesh (Hathras and Kanpur styles); musical verse dialogues in *Doha*, *Chaubola*, *Chhappai*.
- **Bhavai**: Gujarat & Rajasthan; fast-paced comic/social satire, acrobatic balance of 7–9 brass pots on head; devotion to Amba.
- **Tamasha**: Maharashtra; lively music and fast Lavani dance; Kolhati community.
- **Yakshagana**: Coastal Karnataka; vibrant masks, massive headdresses, Kannada dance-drama based on Epics.
- **Bhand Pather**: Kashmir; satirical comic theatre of traditional minstrel-actors (*Bhands*); accompanied by Surnai and Dhol.
- **Maach**: Malwa (Madhya Pradesh); musical folk theatre in open-air stages.
- **Swang**: Haryana & Western UP; dialogue recited in verse; started by Ali Bakhsh and Pt. Deep Chand.
- **Ankiya Nat / Bhaona**: Assam; one-act plays introduced by Srimanta Sankardeva; performed in Brajavali language.
- **Therukoothu**: Tamil Nadu; street theatre performing scenes from Mahabharata (Draupadi cult).
- **Jatra**: Bengal; musical theatrical performances conducted on open four-side platforms.

### B. Traditional Martial Arts of India
- **Kalaripayattu**: Kerala (*Mother of all martial arts*); practiced in training arenas called *Kalari*; uses Urumi (flexible sword).
- **Silambam**: Tamil Nadu; martial art focused on spinning and striking with a **long bamboo staff**.
- **Thang-Ta (Huyen Langlon)**: Manipur; armed martial art (*Thang* = Sword, *Ta* = Spear).
- **Gatka**: Punjab; traditional stick-fighting martial art practiced by Nihang Sikh warriors.
- **Mardani Khel**: Maharashtra; traditional armed martial art of Marathas using *Pata* (gauntlet-sword) and *Vita* (corded spear).
- **Pari-Khanda**: Bihar & Jharkhand; sword (*Pari*) and shield (*Khanda*) fighting created by Rajputs; foundation of Chhau.
- **Thoda**: Himachal Pradesh; martial archery sport played between two teams (Saathi and Paashi, symbolizing Kauravas & Pandavas).

## 7. Classical Music: Hindustani vs Carnatic & Top Gharanas

### A. Core Differences
- **Hindustani Music**: North India; influenced by Persian/Arabian music; focuses on improvisation; divided into various **Gharanas**; major forms: Dhrupad, Khayal, Thumri, Tarana, Tappa.
- **Carnatic Music**: South India; entirely indigenous; strictly structured and composition-oriented (**Kriti**); Trinity of Carnatic Music: **Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, and Syama Sastri**; Purandara Dasa is revered as the **"Pitamaha of Carnatic Music"**.

### B. Famous Hindustani Gharanas & Exponents
- **Gwalior Gharana**: Oldest Khayal Gharana; founded by Nathan Pir Baksh and Hassu-Haddu Khan; simple, open-throated singing (Vishnu Digambar Paluskar).
- **Agra Gharana**: Founded by Haji Sujan Khan / Khuda Baksh; *Nom-Tom* alap; Faiyaz Khan (*Aftab-e-Mousiqui*).
- **Kirana Gharana**: Founded by Abdul Karim Khan and Abdul Wahid Khan; deep emotional alap; **Pt. Bhimsen Joshi**, Gangubai Hangal, Prabha Atre.
- **Banaras Gharana**: Pt. Rajan-Sajan Mishra (vocal), **Ustad Bismillah Khan** (Shehnai - Bharat Ratna 2001), Pt. Samta Prasad (Gudai Maharaj - Tabla), Pt. Kishan Maharaj (Tabla).
- **Lucknow Gharana**: Renowned for Kathak (Birju Maharaj) and Tabla (Modu Khan & Bakshu Khan).
- **Maihar Gharana**: Founded by Baba Allauddin Khan; legendary instrumentalists: **Pt. Ravi Shankar** (Sitar - Bharat Ratna 1999), Ustad Ali Akbar Khan (Sarod), Annapurna Devi (Surbahar).
