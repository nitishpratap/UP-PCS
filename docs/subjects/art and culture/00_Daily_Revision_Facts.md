### B. Eligibility Criteria (Ministry of Culture)
- High antiquity of early texts / recorded history over a period of **1500–2000 years**.
- A body of ancient literature/texts considered a valuable heritage by generations of speakers.
- Literary tradition must be **original and not borrowed** from another speech community.
- Classical language and literature being distinct from modern, there may also be a discontinuity between the classical language and its later forms or offshoots.

## 3. Temple Architecture Styles & Architectural Anatomy

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 NAGARA vs DRAVIDA ANATOMY                   │
├──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┤
│ NAGARA (North India)         │ DRAVIDA (South India)        │
│ • Curvilinear Shikhara       │ • Stepped-Pyramidal Vimana   │
│ • Amalaka & Kalasha on top   │ • Octagonal/Dome Stupika top │
│ • Raised plinth (Jagati)     │ • Elaborate boundary wall    │
│ • No huge boundary walls     │ • Towering Gateways: GOPURAM │
│ • Water tank not essential   │ • Water tank (Kalyani) inside│
│ • Rekha-Deula / Phamsana     │ • Pillared hall (Mandapas)   │
└──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

### A. Core Architectural Components (Vocabulary Goldmine)
- **Garbhagriha**: Sanctum Sanctorum (innermost chamber housing the principal deity).
- **Mandapa**: Pillared assembly hall leading to the sanctum for devotees.
- **Antarala**: Vestibule / intermediate foyer connecting Garbhagriha and Mandapa.
- **Shikhara** (North) / **Vimana** (South): Mountain-like spire rising above the Garbhagriha.
- **Amalaka**: Stone disc with fluted edges resembling a myrobalan fruit, crowning the North Indian Shikhara.
- **Kalasha**: Pitcher/pot shaped finial installed atop the Amalaka.
- **Gopuram**: Colossal, monumental decorated gateway of South Indian temples.
- **Panchayatana Style**: Main central temple surrounded symmetrically by **four subsidiary shrines** at the four corners (e.g., Dashavatara Temple Deogarh, Kandariya Mahadeva Khajuraho, Lakshmana Temple Khajuraho, Brahmeswara Temple Bhubaneswar).

### B. Masterpiece Temples & Builders
| Temple Name | Location | Builder / Dynasty | Architecture Style / Key Architectural Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kailasa Temple (Cave 16)** | Ellora (Maharashtra) | **Krishna I** (Rashtrakuta, 8th c.) | **World's largest monolithic rock-cut monument**, carved top-down out of a single basalt cliff |
| **Shore Temple** | Mamallapuram (Tamil Nadu) | **Narasimhavarman II Rajasimha** (Pallava) | Structural stone temple overlooking Bay of Bengal; UNESCO WHS |
| **Brihadeshwara (Rajarajeshwara)** | Thanjavur (Tamil Nadu) | **Rajaraja I Chola** (1010 CE) | Grandest Dravida Vimana (66 m high); granite monolith Stupika (80 tonnes); shadow never falls at noon |
| **Gangaikonda Cholapuram** | Ariyalur (Tamil Nadu) | **Rajendra I Chola** (1035 CE) | Commemorated his expedition to the Ganga; feminine concave Vimana |
| **Sun Temple** | Konark (Odisha) | **Narasimhadeva I** (Eastern Ganga, 13th c.) | Designed as colossal chariot of Surya with 24 carved stone wheels & 7 horses; **"Black Pagoda"** |
| **Jagannath Temple** | Puri (Odisha) | **Anantavarman Chodaganga** (Eastern Ganga) | Rekha-Deula style; famous Ratha Yatra; **"White Pagoda"** |
| **Lingaraja Temple** | Bhubaneswar (Odisha) | Somavamshi Kings (Yayati I) | Finest example of Kalinga Nagara style (Deula, Jagamohana, Natamandapa, Bhogamandapa) |
| **Sun Temple** | Modhera (Gujarat) | **Bhima I** (Solanki / Chaulukya, 1026 CE) | Maru-Gurjara style; massive stepped water tank (**Surya Kund**) with 108 miniature shrines |
| **Kandariya Mahadeva Temple** | Khajuraho (Madhya Pradesh) | **Dhangadeva** (Chandela, 10th c.) | Largest temple at Khajuraho; Panchayatana style; 84 miniature spires (*Urushringas*) |
| **Dilwara Jain Temples** | Mount Abu (Rajasthan) | **Vimal Shah** (Vimal Vasahi 1031) & **Tejpala/Vastupala** (Luna Vasahi 1230) | Solanki style; world-renowned intricate, translucent white marble carving |
| **Hoysaleswara Temple** | Halebidu (Karnataka) | King Vishnuvardhana (Hoysala) | Soapstone (chloritic schist); stellate (star-shaped) ground plan; high-relief friezes; UNESCO WHS (2023) |
| **Chennakeshava Temple** | Belur (Karnataka) | King Vishnuvardhana (Hoysala, 1117 CE) | UNESCO WHS (2023); exquisite Madanika bracket figures |
| **Dashavatara Temple** | Deogarh (Lalitpur, UP) | Gupta Period (6th c. CE) | **Earliest surviving Panchayatana stone temple in North India**; famous Sheshashayi Vishnu panel |
| **Bhitargaon Temple** | Kanpur (UP) | Gupta Period (**5th c. CE**; house **c. 320–550**) | **Oldest surviving terracotta/brick temple** with an arch in India |

> **Dynasty spans for temple builders (from–to, not founder-year only):** Gupta **c. 320–550** (Deogarh, Bhitargaon). Pallava imperial **c. 575–897** (Shore Temple = Rajasimha). Rashtrakuta **c. 753–973** (Kailasa = Krishna I). Chola imperial **c. 850–1279** (Rajaraja I **1010**, Rajendra I **1035**). Chandela **c. 9th–13th c.** (Khajuraho). Eastern Ganga **c. 11th–15th c.** (Konark, Puri). Hoysala **c. 11th–14th c.** (Belur **1117**, Halebidu). Solanki / Chaulukya **c. 10th–13th c.** (Modhera **1026**).

## 4. Fairs and Festivals of India, Uttar Pradesh & Uttarakhand

### A. Major National Cultural Festivals
- **Hornbill Festival**: Nagaland (1–10 December at Kisama Heritage Village) — "Festival of Festivals" uniting 16 Naga tribes.
- **Bihu**: Assam (Three cycles: *Rongali / Bohag Bihu* in spring/April, *Kongali / Kati Bihu* in autumn, *Bhogali / Magh Bihu* in winter).
- **Chapchar Kut**: Mizoram (Spring festival celebrated after clearing jhum forests; Cheraw bamboo dance).
- **Losar**: Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh (Tibetan / Buddhist New Year).
- **Pushkar Fair**: Ajmer (Rajasthan) — World's largest camel and livestock trading fair on Kartik Purnima.
- **Thrissur Pooram**: Vadakkunnathan Temple, Thrissur (Kerala) — Kudamattam parasol display & caparisoned elephants.
- **Wangala Festival**: Meghalaya (Garo Hills) — "100 Drum Festival" harvest celebration honouring the Sun God Saljong.

### B. Uttar Pradesh Fairs & Festivals (UP Special Goldmine)
- **Kumbh Mela & Magh Mela**: Prayagraj (Sangam of Ganga, Yamuna, Saraswati) — Maha Kumbh held every 12 years.
- **Bateshwar Fair**: Bateshwar, Agra (on Yamuna river) — Famous cattle and animal fair; cluster of 101 Shiva temples.
- **Nauchandi Mela**: Meerut (UP) — Celebrates communal harmony; held after Holi dedicated to Nav Chandi Devi and saint Bale Miyan.
- **Shakumbhari Devi Mela**: Saharanpur (UP) — Celebrated twice a year during Navratris.
- **Devi Patan Mela**: Tulsipur, Balrampur (UP) — Famous Shaktipeeth in Terai.
- **Dadri Mela**: Ballia (UP) — Second largest cattle fair in India, named after sage Dardar Muni.
- **Govardhan Parikrama & Lathmar Holi**: Mathura and Barsana/Nandgaon.
- **Taj Mahotsav**: Agra (Shilpgram) — 10-day cultural festival in February showcasing Mughal crafts and cuisine.
- **Sulh-i-Kul Festival**: Agra — Symbolizes Hindu-Muslim fraternity and peace started by Akbar.

### C. Uttarakhand Fairs & Festivals (UKPCS Priority!)
- **Nanda Devi Raj Jat**: Chamoli (Uttarakhand) — **Held once every 12 years**; 280-km pilgrimage trek on foot from Nauti village to Homkund; guided by four-horned ram (**Chasingha Khadu**).
- **Ramman**: Saloor-Dungra village, Joshimath, Chamoli — Dedicated to Bhumiyal Devta; wooden masked dance; UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.
- **Bagwal Mela (Stone Throwing Fair)**: Devidhura, Champawat — Held on Raksha Bandhan at Varahi Devi Temple; four clans (Kham) throw stones at each other.
- **Purnagiri Mela**: Tanakpur, Champawat — Thousands of pilgrims visit during Chaitra Navratri on Kali river.
- **Uttarayani Mela**: Bageshwar (on confluence of Saryu and Gomati rivers) — Historical site where Coolie-Begar registers were dumped into Saryu in 1921!
- **Jaiti Mela**: Almora — Traditional folk songs and Jhora dance.

## 9. Traditional Folk Theatres & Puppetry of India

### A. Major Folk Theatres (Direct State Match)
| Folk Theatre | State / Region | Distinctive Features / Themes |
|---|---|---|
| **Nautanki** | **Uttar Pradesh** | Popular musical theatre; prominent gharanas in **Hathras** (classical singing style) and **Kanpur** (dramatic prose dialogues); *Doha, Chaubola, Chhand* meters accompanied by *Nakkara* drum |
| **Bhand Pather** | **Jammu & Kashmir** | Traditional satirical folk theatre of agricultural community; witty social satire, slapstick comedy, and mimicry accompanied by Surnai and Dhol |
| **Swang / Saang** | **Haryana & Western UP** | Musical folk drama with rhyming dialogues; pioneered by Pt. Deep Chand and Kishan Lal Bhat; open-air stage |
| **Rasleela & Ramlila** | **Uttar Pradesh** (Mathura–Vrindavan / Kashi) | Devotional dance-dramas depicting Krishna's childhood pastoral pastimes and Rama's life journey |
| **Bhavai** | **Gujarat & Rajasthan** | Fast-paced socio-religious folk play; male actors (*Asaita Thakar* tradition); balancing earthen pots on head; uses *Bhungal* brass horn |
| **Jatra** | **West Bengal & Odisha** | Open-air musical melodrama; originated during Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's Bhakti movement; loud declamation and heroic acting |
| **Yakshagana** | **Karnataka** (Coastal) | Grand dance-drama based on Ramayana/Mahabharata; towering decorated headgear (*Muguta*), vibrant facial makeup, high jumps and spins |
| **Therukoothu** | **Tamil Nadu** | "Street play" performed at village squares during annual Draupadi Amman festivals |
| **Koodiyattam** | **Kerala** | Classical Sanskrit temple drama; UNESCO ICH; elaborate mudras and eye expressions |
| **Ankiya Nat / Bhaona** | **Assam** | One-act plays created by **Srimanta Sankardeva**; written in Brajavali language; performed in monastic prayer halls (*Namghar*) |
| **Tamaasha** | **Maharashtra** | Energetic folk theatre combining *Lavani* dance and rapid wit; accompanied by Dholki and Halgi |
| **Dashavatar** | **Konkan / Goa / Maharashtra** | Folk theatre enacting the ten incarnations of Lord Vishnu in local temples |

## 10. Martial Arts of India

| Martial Art Form | State of Origin | Weapon / Technique / Distinctive Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Kalaripayattu** | **Kerala** | **Oldest martial art in the world** (traced to Sage Parashurama); practiced in an earthen pit (*Kalari*); weapons: *Urumi* (flexible whip-like sword), staff, shield |
