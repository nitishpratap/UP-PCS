## 1. The 8 Classical Dances of India (Recognized by Sangeet Natak Akademi)

| Dance Form | State of Origin | Key Pioneers & Revivalists | Notable Exponents | Distinctive Features, Repertoire & Exam Keywords |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bharatanatyam** | **Tamil Nadu** | **E. Krishna Iyer**, **Rukmini Devi Arundale** (founder of Kalakshetra Foundation) | Yamini Krishnamurthy, Padma Subrahmanyam, Alarmel Valli, Mrinalini Sarabhai | Regarded as the **oldest classical dance** of India, originating from ancient temple dancer (*Devadasi*) traditions where it was historically known as *Sadir* or *Dasi Attam*. It symbolizes the element of fire (*Agni*) and features the characteristic half-sitting stance termed **Araimandi**. A complete traditional recital follows the structured sequence: *Alarippu → Jatiswaram → Shabdam → Varnam → Padam → Tillana*. It is performed in the *Ekaharya* format, wherein a single performer assumes and portrays multiple roles. |
| **Kathak** | **Uttar Pradesh** | **Lady Leela Sokhey** (Madame Menaka), **Nawab Wajid Ali Shah** (patron of Lucknow Gharana) | **Pt. Birju Maharaj**, Sitara Devi, Shambhu Maharaj, Lachhu Maharaj, Damayanti Joshi | The name derives from the ancient aphorism *“Katha Kahe So Kathak Kahave”* (one who recites stories is a Kathak). The dance is characterized by intricate and swift rhythmic footwork (**Tatkar**), dazzling pirouettes (**Chakkars**), expressive gait narratives (*Gat Nikas*), and dynamic rhythmic contests (*Jugalbandi*) with the tabla. Its major gharanas include **Lucknow** (famed for grace, *Bhava*, and subtle mime), **Jaipur** (distinguished by fiery footwork, speed, and *Veer rasa*), **Banaras**, and **Raigarh**. |
| **Kathakali** | **Kerala** | **Vallathol Narayana Menon** (founder of Kerala Kalamandalam) | Kalamandalam Gopi, Guru Kunchu Kurup, Raman Kutty Nair | A grand visual dance-drama enacting epic narratives from the Ramayana and Mahabharata. It is celebrated for its elaborate facial makeup (*Chutti*) and ornate towering headgear. Character types are defined by strict symbolic facial colors: **Pacha** (green makeup, depicting divine heroes and noble kings), **Kathi** (knife-shaped red streaks on green with a white nose-knob, representing arrogant royalty and villains), **Thadi** (bearded characters: red/black for evil demons, white for pious figures like Hanuman), and **Minukku** (warm yellow/flesh tone, representing virtuous women and sages). |
| **Kuchipudi** | **Andhra Pradesh** | **Siddhendra Yogi** (creator of *Bhamakalapam*) | Yamini Krishnamurthy, Raja & Radha Reddy, Swapnasundari | Named after its village of origin, Kuchelapuram in Krishna district, this form developed as a dance-drama tradition combining spoken dialogue, classical singing, and dance. Its most celebrated virtuoso feature is the **Tarangam**, in which the dancer deftly balances upon the raised rim of a brass plate while carrying a brass water pitcher (*Kundi*) balanced steadily atop the head. |
| **Odissi** | **Odisha** | **Kelucharan Mohapatra**, Pankaj Charan Das, Deba Prasad Das | Sonal Mansingh, Sanjukta Panigrahi, Madhavi Mudgal | Deeply rooted in temple rituals, its lineage traces back to female temple dancers (*Maharis*) and juvenile male performers (*Gotipuas*). The performance repertoire is heavily inspired by Jayadeva’s devotional masterpiece, the *Gita Govinda*. It is distinguished by two fundamental body postures: the feminine, soft, three-bend deflective stance (**Tribhanga**) and the firm, square, masculine stance (**Chowk**). |
| **Sattriya** | **Assam** | **Mahapurusha Srimanta Sankardeva** (15th century) | Bhabananda Barbayan, Jatin Goswami, Ghanakanta Bora | Created as an instrument of the Neo-Vaishnavite Bhakti movement, this sacred dance form was nurtured for over five centuries inside monastic sanctuaries known as **Sattras**. Its musical and theatrical foundation rests upon *Borgeet* (devotional songs) and *Ankiya Nat* (one-act devotional plays). The Sangeet Natak Akademi officially recognized Sattriya as India's 8th classical dance in **November 2000**. |
| **Manipuri** | **Manipur** | **King Bhagyachandra** (Ningthou Ching-Thang Khomba), **Rabindranath Tagore** (introduced at Santiniketan) | Guru Bipin Singh, Jhaveri Sisters (Nayna, Suvarna, Darshana, Ranjana), Charu Mathur | Centered upon the immortal, tender love of Radha and Krishna through devotional **Raslila** performances. It is distinguished by soft, undulating, lyrical movements in which the dancer's feet never strike the floor sharply. Female dancers wear an ornate, stiff cylindrical skirt known as the **Potloi** or *Kumin*. Performances are traditionally complemented by the energetic, acrobatic drum dance called **Pung Cholom**. |
| **Mohiniyattam** | **Kerala** | **Maharaja Swathi Thirunal** of Travancore, **Vallathol Narayana Menon** | Sunanda Nair, Bharati Shivaji, Kanak Rele, Gopika Varma | Literally meaning the "Dance of the Enchantress", this dance draws mythological inspiration from Lord Vishnu’s Mohini incarnation. It is performed strictly as a solo feminine recital embodying pure **Lasya** (delicate, wave-like, swaying body movements resembling palm fronds). Performers are clad in a signature pristine off-white handloom saree embellished with a shimmering golden brocade border, known as the **Kasavu saree**. |

## 2. Classical Languages of India (Chronology & Criteria)

### A. Chronological Order of Recognition
1. **Tamil** (Declared in **2004**) — Conferred status as India's first Classical Language.
2. **Sanskrit** (Declared in **2005**).
3. **Telugu** (Declared in **2008**).
4. **Kannada** (Declared in **2008**).
5. **Malayalam** (Declared in **2013**).
6. **Odia** (Declared in **2014**).
7. **Marathi** (Approved by the Union Cabinet on **3 October 2024**).
8. **Pali** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).
9. **Prakrit** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).
10. **Assamese** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).
11. **Bengali** (Approved on **3 October 2024**).

*(India officially recognizes **11 Classical Languages** following the landmark Union Cabinet decision of October 2024.)*

### B. Eligibility Criteria (Ministry of Culture)
To qualify for status as a Classical Language, a language must satisfy the following four criteria formulated by the Ministry of Culture:
- **High Antiquity:** Recorded history and early literary texts spanning a historical antiquity of **1500 to 2000 years**.
- **Valuable Heritage:** A treasured corpus of ancient literature and texts cherished as invaluable heritage by generations of speakers.
- **Original Literary Tradition:** A literary heritage that is demonstrably authentic and original, not borrowed from another speech community.
- **Linguistic Distinctiveness:** A distinct divergence between the classical language and its modern forms or vernacular offshoots, acknowledging potential historical discontinuity across later evolutionary stages.

## 3. Temple Architecture Styles & Architectural Anatomy

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     NAGARA vs DRAVIDA ARCHITECTURE                      │
├───────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ NAGARA (North Indian Style)       │ DRAVIDA (South Indian Style)        │
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Curvilinear tower: Shikhara     │ • Stepped-pyramidal tower: Vimana   │
│ • Crowning Amalaka & Kalasha      │ • Crowning octagonal/dome Stupika   │
│ • Built on a raised plinth: Jagati│ • Enclosed by elaborate boundary wall│
│ • Monumental gateways absent      │ • Towering ornamental gates: Gopuram│
│ • Temple water tanks not mandatory│ • Temple water tank (Kalyani) inside│
│ • Sub-types: Rekha-Deula, Phamsana│ • Multi-pillared assembly Mandapas  │
└───────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### A. Core Architectural Vocabulary
- **Garbhagriha (Sanctum Sanctorum):** The innermost, unadorned sacred chamber housing the principal deity, designed to focus divine energy.
- **Mandapa:** A pillared hall situated in front of the sanctum, serving as a gathering space for devotees during rituals.
- **Antarala (Vestibule):** An intermediate foyer or transit vestibule connecting the Garbhagriha with the main Mandapa.
- **Shikhara (North) / Vimana (South):** The monumental mountain-like spire rising skyward directly over the Garbhagriha.
- **Amalaka:** A large, fluted circular stone disc resembling a myrobalan fruit, installed at the apex of a Nagara Shikhara.
- **Kalasha:** A pot- or pitcher-shaped decorative finial placed directly atop the Amalaka as the highest point of the temple.
- **Gopuram:** A towering, monumental gateway providing entrance through the perimeter walls of South Indian Dravidian temple complexes.
- **Panchayatana Style:** An architectural layout where the main central shrine is symmetrically flanked by **four smaller subsidiary shrines** at the corners (prominent examples include the Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh, and the Kandariya Mahadeva and Lakshmana temples at Khajuraho).

### B. Masterpiece Temples, Dynasties & Architectural Highlights
| Temple Name | Location | Builder Dynasty & Ruler | Architectural Style & Primary Exam Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kailasa Temple (Cave 16)** | Ellora (Maharashtra) | **Krishna I** (Rashtrakuta Dynasty, 8th century CE) | Celebrated as the **world's largest monolithic rock-cut monument**, excavated vertically from top to bottom out of a single volcanic basalt cliff face. |
| **Shore Temple** | Mamallapuram (Tamil Nadu) | **Narasimhavarman II Rajasimha** (Pallava Dynasty) | One of the earliest free-standing structural stone temples in Southern India, built on the edge of the Bay of Bengal; designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. |
| **Brihadeshwara (Rajarajeshwara)** | Thanjavur (Tamil Nadu) | **Rajaraja I Chola** (consecrated in 1010 CE) | Represents the zenith of Dravida architecture, crowned by a massive 66-metre pyramidal Vimana and an 80-tonne monolithic granite Stupika; renowned for its shadow engineering. |
| **Gangaikonda Cholapuram** | Ariyalur (Tamil Nadu) | **Rajendra I Chola** (1035 CE) | Commissioned to commemorate Rajendra I's victorious military campaign to the holy River Ganga; features a graceful, concave-curving Vimana profile. |
| **Sun Temple** | Konark (Odisha) | **Narasimhadeva I** (Eastern Ganga Dynasty, 13th century CE) | Designed in the form of an immense stone chariot dedicated to the Sun God Surya, complete with 24 carved stone wheels and 7 galloping horses; historically termed the **"Black Pagoda"**. |
| **Jagannath Temple** | Puri (Odisha) | **Anantavarman Chodaganga** (Eastern Ganga Dynasty) | A quintessential monument of the Kalinga Nagara style (Rekha-Deula); celebrated for the world-famous Ratha Yatra and historically known as the **"White Pagoda"**. |
| **Lingaraja Temple** | Bhubaneswar (Odisha) | Somavamshi Dynasty (King Yayati I) | Regarded as the most complete and magnificent specimen of Kalinga architecture, showcasing all four classic halls: Deula, Jagamohana, Natamandapa, and Bhogamandapa. |
| **Sun Temple** | Modhera (Gujarat) | **Bhima I** (Solanki / Chaulukya Dynasty, 1026 CE) | Built in the ornate Maru-Gurjara style; renowned for its grand stepped water tank (**Surya Kund**) adorned with 108 miniature shrines along its terraces. |
| **Kandariya Mahadeva Temple** | Khajuraho (Madhya Pradesh) | **Dhangadeva / Vidyadhara** (Chandela Dynasty, 10th–11th century CE) | The largest and most elaborate Hindu temple at Khajuraho; erected on a Panchayatana plan with a soaring Shikhara accompanied by 84 subsidiary spires (*Urushringas*). |
| **Dilwara Jain Temples** | Mount Abu (Rajasthan) | **Vimal Shah** (Vimal Vasahi, 1031 CE) & **Tejpala / Vastupala** (Luna Vasahi, 1230 CE) | Outstanding examples of Solanki temple craft, internationally famous for their luminous, translucent white marble ceiling carvings and filigree stonework. |
| **Hoysaleswara Temple** | Halebidu (Karnataka) | King Vishnuvardhana (Hoysala Dynasty) | Carved from soft chloritic schist (soapstone) on a stellate (star-shaped) plinth, covered in intricate, continuous narrative friezes; inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023. |
| **Chennakeshava Temple** | Belur (Karnataka) | King Vishnuvardhana (Hoysala Dynasty, 1117 CE) | Built to commemorate Hoysala military triumphs; celebrated for its star-shaped sanctum and masterfully sculpted *Madanika* bracket figures; inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023. |
| **Dashavatara Temple** | Deogarh (Lalitpur district, UP) | Gupta Period (6th century CE) | Stands as the **earliest surviving Panchayatana stone temple in Northern India**, famous for its classical relief depiction of Sheshashayi Vishnu reclining on the serpent Shesha. |
| **Bhitargaon Temple** | Kanpur district (UP) | Gupta Period (5th century CE; Gupta imperial span c. 320–550 CE) | Recognized as the **oldest surviving terracotta and brick temple** in India featuring a genuine radiating structural arch. |

> **Dynastic Spans for Key Temple Builders:** Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE: Deogarh, Bhitargaon); Imperial Pallavas (c. 575–897 CE: Shore Temple under Rajasimha); Rashtrakutas (c. 753–973 CE: Kailasa Temple under Krishna I); Imperial Cholas (c. 850–1279 CE: Rajaraja I in 1010 CE, Rajendra I in 1035 CE); Chandelas of Jejakabhukti (c. 9th–13th century CE: Khajuraho); Eastern Gangas (c. 11th–15th century CE: Konark, Puri); Hoysalas (c. 11th–14th century CE: Belur in 1117 CE, Halebidu); Solankis / Chaulukyas of Gujarat (c. 10th–13th century CE: Modhera in 1026 CE).

## 4. Fairs and Festivals of India, Uttar Pradesh & Uttarakhand

### A. Major National Cultural Festivals
- **Hornbill Festival (Nagaland):** Held annually from 1 to 10 December at the Naga Heritage Village in Kisama; acclaimed as the "Festival of Festivals", it showcases the collective traditions of all 16 recognized Naga tribes.
- **Bihu (Assam):** Celebrated across three distinct agricultural cycles: *Rongali (Bohag) Bihu* in spring (April) marking the Assamese New Year, *Kongali (Kati) Bihu* in autumn marking crop protection, and *Bhogali (Magh) Bihu* in winter marking the post-harvest feast.
- **Chapchar Kut (Mizoram):** Celebrated with great joy in spring after the completion of jhum (slash-and-burn) clearing operations; famously features the traditional *Cheraw* bamboo dance.
- **Losar (Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh):** The traditional Buddhist and Tibetan New Year festival, observed with monastic mask dances (*Cham*) and elaborate symbolic offerings.
- **Pushkar Fair (Ajmer, Rajasthan):** One of the world's largest livestock and camel trading fairs, organized during Kartik Purnima along the holy banks of Lake Pushkar.
- **Thrissur Pooram (Kerala):** Staged at the Vadakkunnathan Temple in Thrissur; renowned for competitive temple displays featuring caparisoned elephants, dynamic parasol exchanges (*Kudamattam*), and majestic traditional percussion ensembles (*Panchavadyam*).
- **Wangala Festival (Garo Hills, Meghalaya):** Known as the "100 Drum Festival", this post-harvest celebration is observed by the Garo tribe in honor of Saljong, the Sun God of fertility.

### B. Uttar Pradesh Fairs & Festivals (High-Yield State Core)
- **Kumbh Mela & Magh Mela (Prayagraj):** Organized at the sacred Triveni Sangam (confluence of the Ganga, Yamuna, and mythical Saraswati); the Maha Kumbh takes place every 12 years, with the annual Magh Mela held during the Hindu month of Magha.
- **Bateshwar Fair (Bateshwar, Agra):** A renowned historic cattle and livestock fair hosted along the Yamuna riverbank, famous for its picturesque riverside cluster of 101 Shiva temples.
- **Nauchandi Mela (Meerut):** A celebrated symbol of communal fraternity held after Holi, where pilgrims pay homage concurrently to the shrine of Nav Chandi Devi and the dargah of Sufi saint Hazrat Bale Miyan.
- **Shakumbhari Devi Mela (Saharanpur):** Organized twice every year during the auspicious Chaitra and Ashwin Navratris at the historic Shakumbhari Devi Peeth in the Shivalik foothills.
- **Devi Patan Mela (Tulsipur, Balrampur):** A major annual fair held at the sacred Devi Patan Shaktipeeth, deeply revered across the Indo-Nepal Terai region.
- **Dadri Mela (Ballia):** Regarded as India's second-largest cattle fair, historically established and named in commemoration of the revered sage Dardar Muni.
- **Govardhan Parikrama & Lathmar Holi (Mathura, Barsana & Nandgaon):** Centuries-old Braj traditions featuring circumambulation of the sacred Govardhan Hill and the joyous playful stick-and-shield Holi celebrations between the women of Barsana and the men of Nandgaon.
- **Taj Mahotsav (Agra):** A vibrant 10-day cultural extravaganza organized each February at Shilpgram near the Taj Mahal, highlighting Mughal crafts, culinary arts, and folk performances.
- **Sulh-i-Kul Festival (Agra):** Inspired by Emperor Akbar’s imperial principle of universal peace and religious tolerance (*Sulh-i-Kul*), this festival serves as a cornerstone of interfaith unity.

### C. Uttarakhand Fairs & Festivals (UKPCS Priority)
- **Nanda Devi Raj Jat (Chamoli, Uttarakhand):** Organized **once every 12 years**, this epic 280-kilometre barefoot pilgrimage spans from Nauti village to the sacred lake of Homkund at the base of Mount Trishul, led by a sacred four-horned ram (**Chasingha Khadu**).
- **Ramman (Saloor-Dungra village, Joshimath, Chamoli):** A ritualistic religious festival and folk theatre dedicated to the tutelary deity **Bhumiyal Devta**; features masked dances utilizing traditional painted wooden masks and has been inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list.
- **Bagwal Mela / Stone-Throwing Fair (Devidhura, Champawat):** Celebrated annually on Raksha Bandhan at the Maa Varahi Devi Temple, where warriors of four local clans (*Khams*) traditionally engage in stone-hurling ritual combat.
- **Purnagiri Mela (Tanakpur, Champawat):** Attracts hundreds of thousands of devotees during Chaitra Navratri to the Purnagiri Shaktipeeth perched above the Sharda (Kali) River.
- **Uttarayani Mela (Bageshwar):** Celebrated on Makar Sankranti at the holy confluence of the Saryu and Gomati rivers; carries immense modern historical importance as the site where registers of the oppressive Coolie-Begar forced labor system were dumped into the river in 1921.
- **Jaiti Mela (Almora):** A traditional cultural fair of the Kumaon region, featuring spirited performances of the circular *Jhora* dance and traditional folk ballads.

## 5. UNESCO Tangible World Heritage Sites in India (45 Total Sites)

*India currently possesses **45 inscribed UNESCO World Heritage Sites** following the historic inscription of Sarnath in **July 2026**.*

- **The Sole Mixed Heritage Site:** **Khangchendzonga National Park** (Sikkim, inscribed in 2016 for combined natural and cultural criteria).
- **Latest Landmark Inscriptions:**
  - **#45: Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath (Uttar Pradesh)** — Inscribed in **July 2026** at the 48th World Heritage Committee session in Busan. This serial cultural site encompasses the monumental **Chaukhandi Stupa** together with the excavated core remains including the Dhamek Stupa and Ashokan pillar sanctuary. It represents **Uttar Pradesh's 4th cultural World Heritage Site**.
  - **#44: Maratha Military Landscapes of India** (Inscribed in **2025**; covers 12 historic fortifications: 11 in Maharashtra and Gingee Fort in Tamil Nadu).
  - **#43: Moidams – Mound-Burial System of the Ahom Dynasty** (Charaideo district, Assam, inscribed in **2024**).
  - **#42: Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas** (Karnataka — Belur, Halebidu, and Somnathpura, inscribed in **2023**).
  - **#41: Santiniketan** (Birbhum district, West Bengal, inscribed in **2023**).
  - **#40: Dholavira: A Harappan City** (Kutch district, Gujarat, inscribed in **2021**).
  - **#39: Kakatiya Rudreshwara / Ramappa Temple** (Palampet, Mulugu district, Telangana, inscribed in **2021**).
- **Uttar Pradesh Tangible Cultural World Heritage Sites (4 Sites):**
  1. **Taj Mahal** (Agra — inscribed in 1983)
  2. **Agra Fort** (Agra — inscribed in 1983)
  3. **Fatehpur Sikri** (Agra — inscribed in 1986)
  4. **Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath** (Varanasi district — inscribed in **2026**)
- **Uttarakhand Tangible Natural World Heritage Site (1 Serial Property):**
  - **Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks** (Chamoli district, inscribed in 1988, expanded in 2005).

> **Important Distinction:** In examination questions, do not quote obsolete counts of 42 or 43 sites, nor limit Uttar Pradesh to only 3 tangible sites following the 2026 inscription of Sarnath. Furthermore, note that the inclusion of **Deepavali** (December 2025) belongs to UNESCO's **Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH)** list, which is entirely distinct from physical World Heritage monuments.

## 7. Folk Paintings of India — State-Wise Master Guide

| Traditional Painting Form | State / Region | Key Features, Media & Cultural Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanjhi** | **Uttar Pradesh** (Mathura–Vrindavan / Braj) | An exquisite traditional stencilled **paper-cutting art** practiced along the Yamuna ghats of Mathura and Vrindavan. Traditionally fashioned during Krishna festivals and Pitru Paksha; recognized with an official GI tag. |
| **Aipan** | **Uttarakhand** (Kumaon) | A sacred ritual floor and wall art created using white rice-flour paste (*Biswar*) upon an ochre-red background (*Geru*), featuring complex geometric and floral protective motifs. Holds a GI tag. |
| **Madhubani (Mithila Painting)** | **Bihar** (Mithila region) | Practiced traditionally by women using natural pigments and brushes made of twigs and nibs; characterized by vivid double-line borders and stylized motifs of deities, nature, and the wedding chamber (*Kohbar*). Holds a GI tag. |
| **Warli** | **Maharashtra** (Thane / Palghar) | An ancient tribal art executed on mud-and-cowdung plastered walls using white pigment made of rice paste and water, depicting community dancing, harvesting, and reverence for the mother goddess *Palaghata*. |
| **Pattachitra** | **Odisha** (also practiced in West Bengal) | A traditional scroll painting executed on treated cloth (*Patta*) or dried palm leaves using natural mineral colors, illustrating episodes from the life of Lord Jagannath, Krishna, and Vaishnava legends. |
| **Phad** | **Rajasthan** (Bhilwara / Shahpura) | Immense narrative cloth scroll paintings depicting the heroic exploits of folk deities such as Pabuji and Devnarayan, traditionally recited and sung by traveling priest-bards (*Bhopas*). |
| **Gond** | **Madhya Pradesh** (Mandla / Dindori) | Celebrated tribal art of the Gond community, characterized by rich textures composed of fine dots, dashes, and rhythmic lines, depicting flora, fauna, and their supreme forest divinity *Bada Dev*. |
| **Kalamkari** | **Andhra Pradesh** | Traditional hand-painted and block-printed textile art using natural vegetable dyes and a sharpened bamboo reed (*kalam*). Maintained through two prominent traditions: **Srikalahasti** (freehand temple hangings depicting epics) and **Machilipatnam** (block-printed trade designs). |
| **Pithora** | **Gujarat & Madhya Pradesh** (Rathwa & Bhil tribes) | Sacred ritual wall paintings executed inside tribal homes to invoke prosperity and fulfill vows, centered on the consecrated depiction of horses honoring the deity *Pithora Baba*. |
| **Kalighat** | **West Bengal** (Kolkata) | A 19th-century urban folk painting style that developed around the Kalighat Kali Temple, renowned for sweeping brush lines and watercolor washes depicting Hindu divinities and satirical caricatures of the colonial elite. |
| **Phulkari** | **Punjab** | Traditional folk floral embroidery executed on coarse dyed cotton cloth (*khaddar*) using vibrant untwisted silk threads (*pat*), traditionally gifted during weddings. |
| **Mandana** | **Rajasthan & Malwa** | Auspicious geometric floor and wall designs drawn with white chalk (*khadiya*) against red clay plaster to welcome deities and mark domestic festivals. |

> **Key Exam Distinction:** Do not confuse **Sanjhi** (indigenous to the Braj region of Uttar Pradesh), **Aipan** (traditional to Kumaon in Uttarakhand), and **Madhubani** (native to Bihar). Each holds a distinct geographical and cultural identity.

## 8. All 16 UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH) Elements of India

*Inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (Ministry of Culture & UNESCO):*

| # | Year Inscribed | Intangible Cultural Heritage Element | Region / Community | Primary Ritual & Cultural Significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2008** | **Koodiyattam (Kutiyattam)** | **Kerala** | India’s oldest living Sanskrit temple theatre tradition, performed in consecrated theatre halls called *Koothambalams* with the accompaniment of copper *Mizhavu* drums. |
| 2 | **2008** | **Ramlila** | **Uttar Pradesh & North India** | The traditional dramatic enactment of the Ramayana during Dussehra, epitomized by the world-famous month-long open-air Ramnagar Ramlila of Varanasi supported by the Kashi Naresh. |
| 3 | **2008** | **Tradition of Vedic Chanting** | Pan-India | The uninterrupted oral recitation of sacred Vedic hymns handed down over millennia through sophisticated mnemonic recitation techniques such as *Jatapatha* and *Ghanapatha*. |
| 4 | **2009** | **Ramman** | **Saloor-Dungra village, Chamoli (Uttarakhand)** | A ritual festival and sacred masked theatre held in honor of the tutelary deity **Bhumiyal Devta**, where villagers perform epic episodes wearing traditional wooden masks. |
| 5 | **2010** | **Chhau Dance** | **Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha** | A semi-classical martial dance tradition that encompasses three distinct regional streams: **Seraikella** (Jharkhand, masked), **Purulia** (West Bengal, large theatrical masks), and **Mayurbhanj** (Odisha, performed without masks). |
| 6 | **2010** | **Kalbelia Folk Songs and Dances** | **Rajasthan** | A sensuous, serpent-like folk dance performed by women of the nomadic Kalbelia snake-charmer community, rhythmically driven by the *Poongi* (been) and *Dhaf*. |
| 7 | **2010** | **Mudiyettu** | **Kerala** | A sacred ritual dance-drama performed in Kali temples, dramatizing the mythological battle between the goddess Kali and the demon Darika; preceded by elaborate floor paintings called *Kalam*. |
| 8 | **2012** | **Buddhist Chanting of Ladakh** | **Ladakh (Trans-Himalaya)** | The solemn rhythmic chanting of sacred Buddhist texts and philosophical teachings by Mahayana monks (Lamas) of the Gelukpa, Drukpa, and Kagyu monastic lineages. |
| 9 | **2013** | **Sankirtana** | **Manipur** | A sacred ritual performance encompassing devotional singing, drumming (*Pung*), and graceful dance, performed by Meitei Vaishnavites to mark life-cycle ceremonies and religious festivals. |
| 10 | **2014** | **Traditional Brass and Copper Craft of Utensil Making** | **Jandiala Guru, Amritsar (Punjab)** | The artisanal metal-working technique of hammering heated brass and copper plates into household vessels, practiced traditionally by the hereditary **Thathera** community. |
| 11 | **2016** | **Yoga** | Pan-India (Global) | The ancient Indian physical, mental, and spiritual discipline founded upon Patanjali’s Ashtanga Yoga; commemorated internationally each year on **21 June**. |
| 12 | **2016** | **Nawrouz (Nowruz)** | Parsi & Kashmiri communities | The ancient Persian New Year festival celebrated annually on the vernal equinox (**21 March**), signifying renewal, peace, and the rebirth of nature. |
| 13 | **2017** | **Kumbh Mela** | **Prayagraj (UP), Haridwar (UK), Ujjain (MP), Nashik (Maharashtra)** | The largest peaceful religious gathering of pilgrims on Earth, rotating across four sacred river sanctuaries (Ganga, Godavari, and Kshipra) over twelve-year cycles. |
| 14 | **2021** | **Durga Puja in Kolkata** | **Kolkata (West Bengal)** | A monumental 10-day autumn festival celebrating the triumph of Goddess Durga over Mahishasura, renowned for extraordinary public art installations, community pandals, and syncretic celebrations. |
| 15 | **2023** | **Garba of Gujarat** | **Gujarat** | A devotional, circular community dance performed during the nine nights of Navratri around a perforated earthen lamp (*Garbha Deep*) or an image of Goddess Amba. |
| 16 | **2025 (Dec)** | **Deepavali (Festival of Lights)** | Pan-India | Inscribed at the 20th session of the Intergovernmental Committee in December 2025 as India's 16th ICH element, honoring the universal celebration of light over darkness and wisdom over ignorance. |

## 9. Traditional Folk Theatres & Puppetry of India

### A. Major Folk Theatres
| Folk Theatre Form | State / Region | Key Features, Dramaturgy & Musical Accompaniment |
|---|---|---|
| **Nautanki** | **Uttar Pradesh** | Highly popular operatic folk musical theatre of Northern India; evolved two renowned styles: **Hathras** (emphasizing classical singing) and **Kanpur** (noted for crisp theatrical dialogue). Plays feature verses in *Doha, Chaubola*, and *Chhand* meters accompanied by the thunderous **Nakkara** (kettledrum). |
| **Bhand Pather** | **Jammu & Kashmir** | Traditional satirical folk theatre practiced by rural farming communities; blends witty social criticism, parodies, and slapstick comedy with music played on the *Surnai* (shehnai-like reed) and *Dhol*. |
| **Swang / Saang** | **Haryana & Western UP** | Dialogue-driven musical folk drama written in rhymed verse; pioneered by celebrated playwrights such as Pt. Deep Chand and Kishan Lal Bhat; staged on open platforms surrounded by spectators. |
| **Rasleela & Ramlila** | **Uttar Pradesh** (Mathura–Vrindavan & Ayodhya / Varanasi) | Devotional dance-dramas dramatizing the divine childhood pastimes of Lord Krishna in Braj and the heroic life journey of Lord Rama across Awadh and Varanasi. |
| **Bhavai** | **Gujarat & Rajasthan** | Fast-paced socio-religious folk theatre initiated by the 14th-century poet Asaita Thakar; features male actors balancing up to eight earthen pots on their heads while playing the long brass trumpet known as the **Bhungal**. |
| **Jatra** | **West Bengal & Odisha** | Open-air musical melodrama that emerged during the Bhakti movement under Chaitanya Mahaprabhu; characterized by grand musical prologues, impassioned dialogues, and high dramatic intensity. |
| **Yakshagana** | **Karnataka** (Coastal) | Spectacular traditional theatre based on episodes from the Hindu epics; distinguished by elaborate costumes, towering decorated headdresses (**Muguta**), vivid facial cosmetics, and high athletic leaps. |
| **Therukoothu** | **Tamil Nadu** | Traditional rural street theatre performed in village squares, historically centered on the annual festival worship of Draupadi Amman. |
| **Koodiyattam** | **Kerala** | Classical Sanskrit temple drama recognized as a UNESCO ICH masterpiece; features stylized hand gestures (*Mudras*) and highly sophisticated eye movements accompanied by *Mizhavu* drums. |
| **Ankiya Nat / Bhaona** | **Assam** | One-act Vaishnavite devotional plays created by **Srimanta Sankardeva**; composed in the literary language *Brajavali* and traditionally staged in monastic assembly halls (**Namghars**). |
| **Tamasha** | **Maharashtra** | Energetic, fast-paced secular folk theatre that evolved in military camps; combines satirical comedy, witty banter, and lively *Lavani* dance accompanied by the *Dholki*. |
| **Dashavatar** | **Konkan / Goa / Maharashtra** | Traditional temple folk theatre depicting the ten divine incarnations of Lord Vishnu, widely performed during local harvest festivities. |

### B. Traditional Indian Puppetry (Four Master Traditions)
| Puppetry Form | Traditional Name | State of Origin | Key Construction & Manipulation Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **String Puppets** | **Kathputli** | **Rajasthan** | Carved from a single block of mango wood and dressed in vibrant medieval Rajasthani garments; manipulated with strings, while the puppeteer produces sharp whistling voices using a reed whistle called a *Boli*. |
| | **Kundhei** | **Odisha** | Made of lightweight wood with long flowing skirts and no articulated legs; movements are closely attuned to the rhythms of Odissi music. |
| | **Gombeyatta** | **Karnataka** | Highly stylized wooden puppets modeled directly upon the elaborate costumes, headdresses, and characters of **Yakshagana** theatre. |
| | **Bommalattam** | **Tamil Nadu** | Combines mechanisms of **both rod and string puppetry**; represents the largest, heaviest, and most mechanically complex puppets in India, operated via a cloth ring worn on the puppeteer's head. |
| **Shadow Puppets** | **Tholu Bommalata** | **Andhra Pradesh** | Translucent leather puppets painted in glowing vegetable colors on both sides; projected against a backlit white cloth screen. |
| | **Togalu Gombeyatta** | **Karnataka** | Small, delicate leather shadow figures depicting epic scenes, operated behind an illuminated white screen. |
| | **Ravanachhaya** | **Odisha** | Made from opaque deerskin; **casts purely black-and-white shadows** on the screen without color projections and has no articulated joints, producing dramatic silhouetted forms. |
| **Glove Puppets** | **Pavakoothu** | **Kerala** | Worn directly over the puppeteer’s hand; intricately modeled with carved wooden heads and painted costumes inspired directly by **Kathakali** makeup and crowns. |
| **Rod Puppets** | **Putul Nach** | **West Bengal** | Heavy wooden dolls sculpted with shoulder joints; manipulated from beneath the stage by puppeteers holding sturdy control rods. |
| | **Yampuri** | **Bihar** | Carved entirely from single pieces of wood without joints; specifically dramatizes moral tales set in the court of Yama, the God of Death. |

## 10. Martial Arts of India

| Martial Art Form | State of Origin | Key Weapons, Principles & Distinctive Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Kalaripayattu** | **Kerala** | Celebrated as the **oldest living martial art in the world** (mythologically attributed to Sage Parashurama); practiced within a sacred subterranean earthen arena (*Kalari*). Employs strikes, grappling, and traditional weapons including the deadly flexible whip-sword (**Urumi**), staff, and buckler. |
| **Silambam** | **Tamil Nadu** | An ancient staff-fencing martial art referenced in Sangam literature such as the *Silappadikaram*; utilizes a 1.68-metre rapid-spinning **bamboo staff** (*Silambam*) to deflect attacks and strike vulnerable targets. |
| **Thang-Ta & Sarit Sarak** | **Manipur** | The traditional combat system of Meitei warriors; **Thang-Ta** emphasizes armed warfare (**Thang** = sword, **Ta** = spear), while **Sarit Sarak** comprises unarmed hand-to-hand combat and evasion. |
| **Gatka** | **Punjab** | Traditional Sikh martial art codified under the patronage of the sixth Sikh Guru, **Guru Hargobind Ji**; practitioners utilize wooden training swords (*Gatka*) and leather target shields, prominently demonstrated during the Hola Mohalla festival. |
| **Pari-Khanda** | **Bihar** | Created historically by Rajput warriors; utilizes the shield (**Pari**) and two-edged sword (**Khanda**); its footwork patterns and athletic steps form the foundational physical vocabulary of **Chhau dance**. |
| **Thoda** | **Himachal Pradesh** | A traditional martial sport rooted in **archery** that traces its lineage to the Mahabharata; practiced during Baisakhi in the Kullu and Shimla hills, where competing archers aim blunt arrows strictly at their opponents' legs below the knees. |
| **Mallakhamb** | **Madhya Pradesh & Maharashtra** | An extraordinary traditional gymnastic martial discipline in which athletes perform dynamic aerial yoga postures and acrobatic maneuvers on a vertical polished teak pole or a hanging cotton rope; declared the **State Sport of Madhya Pradesh**. |
| **Musti-Yuddha** | **Varanasi (Uttar Pradesh)** | Ancient traditional unarmed Indian pugilism and bare-knuckle boxing utilizing punches, elbow strikes, and grappling maneuvers. |
| **Mardani Khel** | **Maharashtra** (Kolhapur) | A historic Maratha armed martial art emphasizing agility, swordplay with the flexible gauntlet-sword (**Patta**), and defense using the bamboo staff (*Kathi*). |
| **Kuttu Varisai** | **Tamil Nadu** | Traditional Dravidian unarmed martial art focusing on rapid footwork, devastating animal-style strikes, and knowledge of vital pressure points (**Varma Kalai**). |

Kishan Maharaj** (Tabla), **Pt. Samta Prasad / Gudai Maharaj** (Tabla), **Girija Devi** (Queen of Thumri), Siddheshwari Devi, Rajan & Sajan Mishra |
