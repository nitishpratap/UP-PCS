## Consolidated — 45 Must-Score Facts

1. The Mauryan empire ended in **185 BCE** when general **Pushyamitra Shunga** (पुष्यमित्र शुंग) killed the last Mauryan **Brihadratha**. The **Shungas** then ruled Magadha about **185–73 BCE**, and the short **Kanva** (कण्व) house followed about **73–28 BCE**.
2. Pushyamitra’s **two ashwamedhas** are recorded in the **Ayodhya** (अयोध्या) inscription of Dhanadeva. That record is **not** Besnagar and **not** Hathigumpha.
3. Northwest invader order for the match paper is **Greeks → Sakas → Kushans**. Parthians often drop out of short lists, but they sit between Shakas and Kushans in the northwest belt.
4. **Hathigumpha** at Udayagiri (Odisha) belongs to **Kharavela** (खारवेल) of Kalinga, a Jain Mahameghavahana / Chedi king. Ashoka, Harsha, and Kanishka are the usual wrong options.
5. The **Besnagar** Garuda pillar is the **Heliodorus** Bhagavata dedication for **Vasudeva**. It is **not** Pushyamitra’s ashwamedha record.
6. The **Satavahanas** were the Deccan / Andhra house. Tradition names **Simuka** as founder, and the capitals are **Pratishthana (Paithan)** and **Amaravati** (अमरावती).
7. **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (गौतमीपुत्र शातकर्णि) defeated Shaka **Nahapana** (नहपान) of the **Kshaharata** house. The victory is recorded in the **Nasik prasasti** of **Gautami Balashri**.
8. Satavahanas nurtured **Prakrit** and pushed **public Buddhist art** (Amaravati, Karle, Nasik). **Hala** wrote the Prakrit *Gatha Saptasati*. **Yajna Sri Satakarni** put a **ship** on coins as a sea-trade stamp.
9. After Satavahana decline, the **Ikshvakus** ruled coastal Andhra from **Nagarjunakonda** (नागार्जुनकोंडा). They are a post-Satavahana Andhra house, not the mythic Ayodhya Ikshvaku line.
10. Peninsula **megaliths** are Iron Age burials with **dolmens**, **cists**, and **hero stones**, often with **black-and-red ware** and iron. Classic sites include **Brahmagiri**, **Adichanallur**, and **Maski** (मास्की).
11. The greatest Indo-Greek name is **Menander (Milinda)**. His capital is **Sagala (Sialkot)**, and the *Milinda Panha* records his debate with **Nagasena** (नागसेन). Indo-Greeks also issued the first **portrait** die-struck coins in India.
12. The first Shaka king named in India is **Maues / Moga** at Taxila–Gandhara. **Nahapana** is the greatest **Kshaharata**; **Rudradaman I** is the greatest **Kardamaka**.
13. Rudradaman’s **Junagadh / Girnar** inscription is the first long literary **Sanskrit** prose record in India. It tells how the **Sudarshana lake** was built under **Chandragupta Maurya** (चंद्रगुप्त मौर्य), adorned under **Ashoka** (अशोक), and **repaired** by Rudradaman.
14. The brief Parthian / Pahlava bridge king is **Gondophares**. The northwest Buddhist site fact for this age is **Takht-i-Bahi**.
15. Kushans rise from the **Yuezhi**. **Kujula Kadphises** founds the house; **Vima Kadphises** floods India with imperial **gold**; **Kanishka** (कनिष्क) is the greatest ruler.
16. Kushan main capital is **Purushapura (Peshawar)**. **Mathura** (मथुरा) in Uttar Pradesh is the second great Indian centre. Art pairs **Gandhara** (गांधार) Greco-Buddhist schist with **Mathura** red sandstone.
17. Coaching keys peg Kanishka’s accession to **78 CE**, the start of the **Shaka era**. Do **not** confuse it with **Vikrama Samvat 57 BCE**.
18. Kanishka convened the **Fourth Buddhist Council** in Kashmir at **Kundalvana**. **Vasumitra** is remembered as president, and **Ashvaghosha** (अश्वघोष) belongs to this age. Kanishka’s title is **Devaputra** (देवपुत्र).
19. Sangam Tamilakam’s **Muvendar** are **Chera** (bow), **Chola** (चोल) (tiger), and **Pandya** (fish). Capitals are **Vanji / Karur**, **Uraiyur**, and **Madurai** (मदुरै).
20. **Karikala** is the Sangam Chola who built the **Kallanai** on the Kaveri. He is **not** imperial **Rajaraja I** of Thanjavur.
21. Sangam seat tradition is **Madurai** under the Pandyas. Three assemblies: **1st Madurai** (Agastya / Tolkappiyar), **2nd Kapadapuram** (Agastya), **3rd Madurai** (**Nakkirar**). **Tolkappiyam** (तोल्काप्पियम्) is the earliest Tamil grammar. **Akam** is love poetry; **Puram** is war and kingship poetry.
22. Sangam ports fact as **Muziris** (Chera / Roman pepper), **Puhar** (Chola), and **Korkai** (Pandya pearl coast). West-coast Periplus ports also include **Tondi**, **Mushiri / Muziris**, and **Nelcynda**. **Arikamedu** (Periplus **Poduke**) yields Roman amphorae.
23. Later south dynasty matchs: **Mahendravarman I** (महेन्द्रवर्मन प्रथम) is **Pallava**; **Kadungon** is **Pandya**; **Amoghavarsha I** is **Rashtrakuta** (राष्ट्रकूट); **Rajaraja I** is **Chola**.
24. **Gandhara** art is Kushan northwest Greco-Buddhist. **Amaravati** art is Satavahana Andhra limestone narrative relief. Do not swap the schools.
25. Ashoka’s Rock Edict II names Chola, Pandya, Keralaputra, and Satiyaputra as **independent neighbours**, not Mauryan provinces.
26. In Uttar Pradesh, **Ayodhya** is the Pushyamitra ashwamedha fact, and **Mathura** is the Kushan second capital and Mathura-art home.
27. **Patanjali** (पतंजलि) of the *Mahabhashya* (महाभाष्य) and Kalidasa’s (कालिदास) *Malavikagnimitra* (मालविकाग्निमित्रम्) (Agnimitra (अग्निमित्र)) sit in Shunga-court tradition, but Buddhist books stay hostile to Pushyamitra even while Sanchi still grows. **Patanjali is Shunga-age — not Kanishka’s court.**
28. Satavahana personal names are often **metronymic** (Gautamiputra, Vashishthiputra). After their decline, Deccan power thins toward the third century CE.
29. **Vima Kadphises** (Kadphises II) issues the first large-scale **gold** for regular use; title **Sarvalokeshvara**. **Kujula** mainly issues **copper**. **Kanishka** puts **Buddha** (बुद्ध) on coins.
30. **Rabatak** lists Kushan cities **Saketa, Kaushambi, Pataliputra, and Champa** — **not Shravasti**. Sarnath Kanishka Buddha image inscription is dated about **81 CE** (year 3 of his era).
31. Vikrama **57 BCE** and Shaka **78 CE** differ by **135 years**. Indian national calendar **Chaitra 1** matches **22 March** (21 March in a leap year).
32. **Ashvaghosha** (*Buddhacharita*, *Saundarananda*, *Sariputraprakarana* (शारिपुत्रप्रकरण)), **Vasumitra**, **Nagarjuna** (नागार्जुन), **Parsva**, and physician **Charaka** (चरक) adorn Kanishka’s court. Chinese texts remember **Pan Chao** defeating Kanishka’s force.
33. Kushans traded via the Persian Gulf and Red Sea (**Periplus**, Arikamedu evidence) but had **no** strong naval-force tradition. Child marriage custom is often dated from the **Kushan** (कुषाण) age.
34. **Gautamiputra Satakarni** is **Ekabrahmana** / protector of the varna order (Nasik of Gautami Balashri). Satavahanas issue punch-marked, lead, copper, silver, potin — **not gold**. **Matsya Purana** gives the longest Andhra list (~30 kings).
35. Rudradaman’s Junagadh is the earliest long literary **Sanskrit / kavya-style** prose. He repaired Sudarshana **without forced labour**. **Yaudheya** coins show **Kartikeya** with a peacock.
36. **Strato II** issued **lead** coins. **Agathocles** put **Sankarshana (Balarama) and Vasudeva** on bilingual coins. Gandhara sculpture often uses **green schist**; **Bamiyan** held colossal Buddha images.
37. Sangam titles for the king include **Kon**, **Ko**, and **Mannan**. The ordinary word *vendan* also means king.
38. Sage **Agastya** is remembered as having Aryanised the south and as the **Father of Tamil Literature**. **Tolkappiyar**, author of *Tolkappiyam*, is listed among Agastya’s twelve disciples.
39. *Silappadikaram* (शिलप्पदिकारम्) is by **Ilango Adigal** (इलंगो अडिगल) (linked as grandson of Chola **Karikala**) and narrates **Kannagi** and **Kovalan**. *Tirukkural* (*Kural* / **Muppal**) by **Tiruvalluvar** (तिरुवल्लुवर) is called the Bible and **Laghuveda** of Tamil literature. *Ramavataram* (रामावतारम्) (Tamil Ramayana) is by **Kamban** (कंबन).
40. **Uraiyur** (also **Koli** / **Varanam**) on the Kaveri at Tiruchirappalli was the Sangam Chola capital and a major **cotton** centre in the *Periplus*.
41. Sanskrit *Yavanapriya* means **pepper** (काली मिर्च) — the Greek / Roman favourite spice. An **amphora** is the tall double-handled Roman jar for wine or oil found at Arikamedu.
42. **Vattakirutal** in Sangam poems is the defeated king’s ritual suicide by **fasting unto death**.
43. Sangam literature names **Chera, Chola, and Pandya** only among the great houses. **Pallava** and **Kadamba** are **not** Sangam ruling dynasties in that corpus.
44. A **Pandya** king sent an ambassador to **Rome in 26 BCE**. Pandya heartland lies south of the Kaveri around **Madurai**; the river of that core is the **Vaigai** (some MCQ banks print it as “Vengi” — do not confuse with Eastern Chalukya **Vengi**).
45. **Udiyanjiral** belongs to the **Chera** line and is often treated as the first historical Chera of the Sangam age. Chera capital memory is **Vanji / Vanchi / Karuvur (Karur)**.

## 8.1 Post-Mauryan Period

**185 BCE to Gupta rise ~320 CE | Magadha after the Mauryas | no single pan-Indian empire**

After **185 BCE** India has several power centres at once. Magadha passes to the **Shungas**. The Deccan has the **Satavahanas**. The deep south has the Sangam **Muvendar**. The northwest door sees Indo-Greeks, then Shakas, then Parthians, then Kushans.

### Pushyamitra Shunga coup (185 BCE) — Cause, Course, Result

**Victim:** last Mauryan **Brihadratha** | **Actor:** Mauryan general **Pushyamitra Shunga**

**Cause:** The Mauryan empire had weakened after **Ashoka**. The last king **Brihadratha** could not hold the army or the treasury.

**Course:** General **Pushyamitra Shunga** killed **Brihadratha** at a military review and seized Magadha. Buddhist tradition is hostile to him, but **Sanchi** (सांची) still grew in this age.

**Result:** The **Mauryan empire ended** in **185 BCE**. The **Shunga dynasty** ruled Magadha until **73 BCE**. Pushyamitra performed **two ashwamedhas**, recorded at **Ayodhya** — not Besnagar or Hathigumpha.

### How the northwest order is remembered

- Match-lists often use the short trio **Greeks → Sakas → Kushans**.
- **Parthians** sit between Shakas and Kushans in fuller teaching. Papers sometimes drop them.

### Shunga house (185–73 BCE)

- The capital is **Magadha** (मगध). **Vidisha** (विदिशा) is a second centre. Coaching memory also ties Shunga ancestry to **Ujjain** (उज्जैन).
- There is a Brahmanical revival under this house.
- Pushyamitra performs two **ashwamedha** rites. The **Ayodhya inscription** of **Dhanadeva** records those two.
- Do not place those ashvamedhas at Besnagar, Hathigumpha, or Sarnath.
- **Agnimitra** (अग्निमित्र) is Pushyamitra’s son. Kalidasa’s (कालिदास) *Malavikagnimitra* (मालविकाग्निमित्रम्) uses him.
- **Patanjali** of the *Mahabhashya* is placed in this court in tradition. He is **not** a Kanishka-court scholar.
- Buddhist books are hostile, yet Sanchi is still enlarged in this age.
- **Bharhut** (भरहुत) stupa railings are a classic **Shunga** sculpture fact.
- The last Shunga **Devabhuti** is killed by **Vasudeva Kanva**.

### Kanva house (73–28 BCE)

- The Kanvas are a short Magadha house after the Shungas.
- Puranic memory says the last Kanva **Susharma** falls to an Andhra / Satavahana founder (**Simuka**).
- After that, the north is open to later local and foreign powers.

### Two inscriptions not to swap

- Kharavela’s **Hathigumpha** is the Kalinga card. It is not Pushyamitra’s ashvamedha record.
- **Heliodorus** at Besnagar is a Bhagavata Greek ambassador. He is also not the ashvamedha record.

> [!TIP]
> **Exam Anchor [UPPCS Prelims 2018, Q91]:** **Hathigumpha inscription** of Kharavela on Udayagiri hills (Odisha) records 14 years of Kharavela’s reign; 5th year records canal diversion from Tan-suliya. *(See Q6 in Complete PYQ Bank below)*

> **Logic:** 185 = Shunga coup, not Ashoka’s death (232).

## 8.2 Satavahana Rulers

**Deccan / Andhra house | ~1st c. BCE–3rd c. CE | Puranic “Andhras”**

- The founder in tradition is **Simuka** (also Sindhuk / Shipraka in Puranas). Capitals are **Pratishthana (Paithan)** — the lasting capital — and early **Amaravati**. The title **Dakshinapathapati** (दक्षिणापथपति) means lord of the southern path. Names are often **metronymic**: Gautamiputra, Vashishthiputra.
- The **Matsya Purana** gives the longest Andhra / Satavahana king list (about **30** names).

- **Satakarni I** is the early expansion king and the first to hold the title **Satakarni**. The **Naneghat** inscription of queen **Naganika** belongs to this house.
- **Gautamiputra Satakarni** is the greatest.
### Gautamiputra Satakarni vs Nahapana — Cause, Course, Result

**Cause:** Shaka **Nahapana** (Kshaharata) controlled a belt from **Ajmer** to **Nasik** and threatened Satavahana survival.
**Course:** **Gautamiputra Satakarni** defeated Nahapana. The **Nasik prasasti** of **Gautami Balashri** records the victory. Nahapana's silver was restruck. The same record calls him **Ekabrahmana** and protector of the **varna** (वर्ण) order (no free intermixture of the four varnas).
**Result:** Satavahana power peaked in the western Deccan. Later Shaka greatness passed to the **Kardamaka** house of **Rudradaman** (रुद्रदामन). He restruck Nahapana’s silver (Jogalthembi hoard type).

| Ruler | Fact |
|-------|------|
| **Simuka** | Traditional founder after Kanva Susharma |
| **Satakarni I** | Early expansion; **Naneghat** of queen **Naganika** |
| **Gautamiputra Satakarni** | Greatest; defeats **Nahapana**; **Ekabrahmana** / varna protector; **Nasik** of **Gautami Balashri** |
| **Vashishthiputra Pulumavi** | Son and successor |
| **Hala** | *Gatha Saptasati* in Prakrit |
| **Yajna Sri Satakarni** | **Ship** on coins — sea-trade fact |

- They nurtured **Prakrit** in inscriptions and *gathas*, not Sanskrit as the court speech. **Public art** got more push: the **Amaravati** stupa (with **Ayaka / Aryaka pillars**), the **Karle** (कार्ले) chaitya, and the Nasik caves.
- Coin metals: punch-marked, **lead**, copper, bronze, silver, potin — **not gold**.
- Land grants to Brahmans and Buddhist monks start to show. Roman trade crosses their roads. Decline comes about the 3rd century CE. **Ikshvakus** at Nagarjunakonda come next in Andhra. **Vakatakas** (founder **Vindhyashakti**) rise in the Deccan after Satavahana weakening.

> [!TIP]
> **Exam Anchor [UPPCS Prelims 2021, Q52]:** Satavahanas were called **Andhra-Bhrityas** in Puranas; Prakrit was their official administrative language; maternal naming tradition (Gautamiputra) but patrilineal succession. *(See Q4 in Complete PYQ Bank below)*

> **Logic:** Gautamiputra Satakarni = **Satavahana** (सातवाहन) (Paithan). Kanishka = **Kushan**. Madurai = **Pandya**.

## 8.2a Ikshvakus and Nagarjunakonda

- After Satavahana decline (~3rd century CE), the **Ikshvakus** ruled coastal **Andhra** from **Nagarjunakonda**.
- The site is famous for **Buddhist stupa**, monastery, and **Ikshvaku inscriptions**.
- Puranas call them **Shriparvatiya**. Ikshvakus here are a **post-Satavahana Andhra** house. The mythic Ayodhya Ikshvaku line is a separate Puranic genealogy.

## 8.3 South Indian History

**Peninsula below the Vindhyas | Kalinga under Kharavela | megalithic south | Sangam Tamilakam**

This card covers three south layers. First comes **Kharavela** of Kalinga. Then come Iron Age **megaliths**. Then Ashoka’s RE II names the Tamil neighbours who stay independent.

### Kharavela — Hathigumpha Campaigns (Cause, Course, Result)

**Source:** Hathigumpha inscription, Udayagiri | **Dynasty:** Mahameghavahana / Chedi | **Religion:** Jain

**Cause:** Kalinga sought independence, trade prestige, and recovery of cult images after Nanda and Mauryan pressure.

**Course:** The inscription records recovery of a **Jina image** carried off by a **Nanda** king. It also records military expeditions toward **Magadha** and the **south**, plus gifts to monks and public works.

**Result:** Kharavela is the strongest **post-Mauryan Kalinga** ruler in epigraphy. **Hathigumpha** = Kharavela.

### Kharavela — identity facts

- The source is the **Hathigumpha** inscription at **Udayagiri** (उदयगिरि), Odisha.
- **Hathigumpha** belongs to **Kharavela** of Kalinga. Ashoka, Harsha, and Kanishka have **other** inscriptions.
- He is a Jain patron.
- Caves stand at **Udayagiri (18)** and **Khandagiri (15)**.
- Hathigumpha itself is at **Udayagiri**, not Khandagiri.
- The dynasty label is **Mahameghavahana / Chedi**.

### Megalithic south

- **Megalithic** cultures are Iron Age burial traditions of the peninsula and parts of central India.
- They predate and underlie the Sangam polities.
- Burial types include **dolmens**, **cists**, stone circles, capstones, and **hero stones (virakkal / nadukal)** for warriors.
- Pottery is often **black-and-red ware (BRW)**.
- Iron tools and weapons appear in the graves.
- Major sites include **Brahmagiri**, **Adichanallur**, **Maski**, **Hallur**, **Nevasa**, and **Piklihal**.
- Brahmagiri links megalithic layers to early historic transitions.
- **PGW** is Later Vedic **doab** (दोआब) ware. **Megaliths** are mainly **south and Deccan** Iron Age burials.
- Grave goods can include pottery, iron weapons, gold, and beads.
- Some sites show communal or family burials under large stone slabs.

### Ashoka’s southern neighbours

- Rock Edict **II** names Chola, Pandya, Keralaputra, and Satiyaputra as **independent neighbours**.
- They are **not** Mauryan provinces.

> [!TIP]
> **Exam Anchor [UPPCS Prelims 2018, Q16]:** **Kharavela** of Chedi/Mahameghavahana dynasty is the king recorded in Hathigumpha inscription. *(See Q5 in Complete PYQ Bank below)*

> **Logic:** Hathigumpha** = **Kharavela** of Kalinga.

## 8.4 Indo-Greeks

**Bactrian Greeks in India | after Alexander’s satraps | before Shakas**

- Alexander’s **326** march (Hydaspes, Sangala, Beas turn-back) belongs to the **Foreign Invasions** chapter. Indo-Greeks are the **later** Bactrian-Greek kings inside India.

- In chronology, “Greeks” means Alexander’s wave **plus** these Indo-Greeks.
- They sit **before** Shakas and **before** Kushans.
- **Greco-Bactria** breaks from the Seleucids under **Diodotus**, about **250 BCE**. **Demetrius I**, about **180 BCE**, makes the first major push into India with an elephant-scalp helmet type.
- The greatest name is **Menander (Milinda)**. His capital is **Sagala (Sialkot)**. The *Milinda Panha* records his debate with **Nagasena**. He is **not** a contemporary of Bimbisara / Buddha / Prasenjit. Sixteen Menander coins are remembered from **Bairat**.
- **Agathocles** and **Pantaleon** issue early bilingual coins with **Brahmi** (ब्राह्मी) legends and **Sankarshana (Balarama) / Vasudeva** types. **Apollodotus** is an early Indian-side silver king.
- They issue the first **portrait**, die-struck coins in India. Legends are **Greek plus Kharoshthi**. Sometimes Brahmi appears. Late Indo-Greek **Strato II** is remembered for **lead** coins.
- **Heliodorus**, ambassador of **Antialcidas**, raises the **Besnagar** (Vidisha) Garuda pillar about **113 BCE**. He calls himself a Bhagavata of **Vasudeva**.
- The *Yuga Purana* memory places **Yavanas** toward **Pataliputra** (पाटलिपुत्र). That raid belongs to the Indo-Greek age, not to Alexander in 326 BCE.
- Besnagar is **not** Pushyamitra’s two-ashwamedha record. That inscription is at **Ayodhya**.
- Indo-Greek power in the northwest ends when **Shakas** take Gandhara and Punjab.

> **Logic:** Menander = Indo-Greek, *Milinda Panha*, capital **Sagala**. **Sangala** = Alexander’s 326 battle. **Heliodorus pillar** = **Besnagar**. Pushyamitra ashwamedha = **Ayodhya**.

## 8.5 Shakas

**Indo-Scythians | after Greeks, before Kushans | title kshatrapa / mahakshatrapa**

- The Indian name **Shaka** means **Scythian** nomads pushed from Central Asia into India from about the **1st century BCE**.
- The first king named in India is **Maues / Moga** at **Taxila** (तक्षशिला) and **Gandhara**. His capital tradition points to **Sirkap**. **Azes I** often follows in coin lists.
- The **Kshaharata** line rules in the western Deccan belt. **Bhumaka** is an early name. **Nahapana** is the greatest of this house.
- Nahapana’s coins run from **Ajmer** to **Nasik**. His capital is **Minnagara**, between Ujjain and Broach.
- **Gautamiputra Satakarni** defeats Nahapana. The **Nasik prasasti** of **Gautami Balashri** records the victory. Nahapana’s silver was restruck.
- The **Kardamaka** line rules western India from about the 1st century CE. **Chashtana** is the founder named in the **Andau inscription** of 130 CE.
- **Rudradaman I** is Chashtana’s grandson and the greatest western Shaka. He rules about **130–150 CE** as **mahakshatrapa** (महाक्षत्रप).
- His **Junagadh / Girnar** inscription is the first long record in literary **Sanskrit** prose in India and the earliest specimen of **kavya** style on stone.
- The **Sudarshana lake** story sits on that rock. It was built by **Pushyagupta**, a Vaishya, under **Chandragupta Maurya**. It was adorned by **Tushaspha**, a Yavana, under **Ashoka**. A storm burst the embankment. **Rudradaman repaired it without forced labour**. He did **not** claim he founded the lake.
- Kardamaka coinage is mainly **silver** (रूपक) and **potin**; **copper** issues are rare.
- Western Shaka rule fades when **Rudrasimha III** is defeated by **Chandragupta II** (चंद्रगुप्त द्वितीय) in the 4th century CE.
- Coaching ratio for gold : silver in the Saka–Kshatrapa horizon is **1 : 35** (**UKPCS 2021**).
- **Vikrama Samvat 57 BCE** is the Vikramaditya legend at Ujjain. It is **not** the **Shaka era 78 CE** tied to Kanishka. The two eras differ by **135 years**.

> **Logic:** Junagadh is not Hathigumpha. Rudradaman is not Kharavela. Nahapana is Kshaharata, not Kardamaka.

---

## 8.6 Parthians

**Pahlavas | brief northwest bridge | between Shaka west and Kushan rise**

- The **Parthians** are called **Pahlavas** in Indian sources. They hold a **short** belt in the northwest after Shakas and before Kushan dominance.
- Their zone is **Gandhara**, **Punjab**, and **Sind** (सिंध).
- The best-known king is **Gondophares**, who rules about **19–45 CE**. His coins and inscriptions appear in the Kabul–Taxila belt.
- The site fact for Parthian-period Buddhism in the NW is **Takht-i-Bahi** near Mardan in Pakistan.
- Christian legend places **St Thomas** at Gondophares’ court. That is tradition, not a royal inscription.
- **Kujula Kadphises** of the Kushans displaces Parthian and remaining Greek power in the northwest.

> **Logic:** Gondophares is **Parthian / Pahlava**. He is not Maues (Shaka) and not Kanishka (Kushan).

## 8.7 Kushanas

**Yuezhi clan | 1st–3rd c. CE | Silk Road empire | last of the 2023 trio**

- The **Kushanas** were one clan of the **Yuezhi** nomads of north Central Asia. They moved through Bactria into Gandhara and the Punjab.
- The founder is **Kujula Kadphises**. He unifies Yuezhi clans and takes Gandhara–Punjab. His coinage is mainly **copper**.
- **Vima Kadphises** (Kadphises II) issues the first large-scale **gold** coinage for regular use in India. Titles include **Shaonanoshao** (king of kings) and **Sarvalokeshvara**.
- **Kanishka** is the greatest Kushan ruler.
- **Huvishka** and **Vasudeva I** are later great names before decline.
- The main capital is **Purushapura (Peshawar)**. **Mathura** in Uttar Pradesh is the second great Kushan centre inside India.
- At its height the empire runs from the **Oxus** / Turfan fringe to the middle **Ganga** (गंगा) plain — a dynasty whose power also sat **outside** India.
- Coins are gold **dinaras** with legends in **Greek**, **Bactrian**, and **Kharoshthi** (खरोष्ठी). Royal portraits are standard. **Yaudheya** copper coins of this age show **Kartikeya** with a peacock.
- Kushan patronage pairs **Gandhara** Greco-Buddhist sculpture (**green schist**) with **Mathura** red-sandstone Indian types. **Bamiyan** held colossal rock-cut Buddhas in the Afghan Kushan world.
- Trade ran through the Persian Gulf and Red Sea (*Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* — **anonymous** Greek merchant; Arikamedu amphorae). Evidence does **not** support a strong Kushan **navy**.
- Social coaching note: **child marriage** custom is often dated from the Kushan age, with girls’ upanayana fading.
- The Kushans are **last** in the 2023 invader trio: Greeks → Sakas → Kushans.

> [!TIP]
> **Exam Anchor [UPPCS Prelims 2023, Q23]:** Chronological order of invaders in ancient India: **Greeks (Indo-Greeks) → Sakas (Scythians) → Kushanas**. *(See Q2 in Complete PYQ Bank below)*

> **Logic:** Vima’s gold ≠ Gupta dinara as a “first” if the paper wants Kushan gold.

## 8.8 Kanishka

**Greatest Kushan | Purushapura | Shaka era 78 CE | Fourth Buddhist Council**

- **Kanishka** is the most famous Kushan ruler. His empire included Afghanistan, Gandhara, Punjab, and much of the Ganga plain.
- keys usually peg his accession to **78 CE**, the start of the **Shaka Samvat** (Shaka era). Some books use about **127 CE** for his reign peak. Prelims still want **78 CE** when they say Shaka era. Vikrama **57 BCE** and Shaka **78 CE** differ by **135 years**. National calendar **Chaitra 1 ≈ 22 March** (21 March in a leap year).
- Kanishka uses the title **Devaputra** (son of god). The **Rabatak inscription** from Afghanistan gives his genealogy and names cities **Saketa, Kaushambi, Pataliputra, and Champa** — **not Shravasti**.
- The Sarnath Buddhist image inscription of Kanishka is dated about **81 CE** (year 3 of his era).
- Kanishka convenes the **Fourth Buddhist Council** in **Kashmir** at **Kundalvana / Kundalavana**.
- **Vasumitra** is remembered as president of the council. **Ashvaghosha** writes *Buddhacharita*, *Saundarananda*, and *Sariputraprakarana*. Court circle also remembers **Parsva**, **Nagarjuna**, and physician **Charaka**. **Patanjali** is **not** of this court (Shunga age).
- Chinese texts remember Kanishka’s clash with **Pan Chao** (Ban Chao) and defeat of a large Kushan force.
- The school linked to the council is **Sarvastivada** (सर्वास्तिवाद). Mahayana Buddhism grows strongly under Kushan patronage.
- Council-king memory: **Ajatashatru** (अजातशत्रु) (1st), **Kalashoka** (2nd), **Ashoka** (3rd), **Kanishka** (4th).
- Kanishka builds a great stupa at **Peshawar**. Chinese pilgrims later describe it.
- His coins show **Buddha**, **Shiva**, **Mithra**, and Greek deities on the same series.
- Kanishka is a common **trap option** for **Hathigumpha**. That inscription belongs to **Kharavela** of Kalinga.

> **Logic:** Shaka era = **78 CE** (Kanishka). Vikrama era = **57 BCE**. **Hathigumpha** = **Kharavela** of Kalinga. Rabatak ≠ Shravasti.

## 8.9 Sangam Age

**Tamilakam south of Krishna–Tungabhadra | ~300 BCE–300 CE | literature as the state archive**

- **Sangam** (संगम) means a conference of poets or a forum of scholars. The literature composed in those assemblies is **Sangam literature**.
- The traditional seat is **Madurai** under the **Pandyas**.
- Sangam Tamilakam is **contemporary** with the Satavahanas — a **parallel** south, a different dynasty.
- Later legend counts **three Sangams**. The first two are largely mythical; the Third Sangam is the poems we still have.

| Sangam | Place | President / tradition |
|--------|-------|------------------------|
| **First** | **Madurai** | Saint **Agastya** (with **Tolkappiyar** in some lists) |
| **Second** | **Kapadapuram** | Saint **Agastya** |
| **Third** | **Madurai** | **Nakkirar** |

- Sage **Agastya** is credited with having **Aryanised** south India. Tradition says he came south from **Kashi** (काशी) at the gods’ request and later resided in the south. He is also called the **Father of Tamil Literature**.
- According to Sangam texts, the titles **Kon**, **Ko**, and **Mannan** belong to the **king**.

- **Ettuttokai** (एट्टुत्तोकै) is the eight anthologies (*Purananuru*, *Akananuru*). **Pattuppattu** (पत्तुप्पाट्टु) is the ten long songs (*Pattinappalai* is the Puhar port poem).
- ***Tolkappiyam*** is the residual text of the Second Sangam tradition and the **most ancient available** long Tamil work. It is a treatise on **grammar and poetry**, written in a formula style. Author **Tolkappiyar** is counted among the **twelve disciples** of Agastya.
- ***Silappadikaram*** is by **Ilango Adigal**, remembered as a grandson of Chola **Karikala**. It narrates the tragic story of **Kannagi** and her husband **Kovalan**.
- ***Tirukkural*** (*Kural*) by **Tiruvalluvar** is called the Bible and **Laghuveda** of Tamil literature. It is also known as **Muppal**.
- ***Manimekalai*** by **Sattanar** is the Buddhist sequel to the Kannagi epic.
- ***Ramavataram*** (Tamil Ramayana) was authored by **Kamban** (later medieval Tamil; not a Third Sangam anthology, but a high-yield Tamil classic).
- **Akam** is inner / love poetry. **Puram** is outer / war and kingship.
- Five landscapes (*tinai*): **Kurinji** (hills, Murugan, hunting), **Mullai** (pastoral forest, cattle), **Marudam** (plains, paddy), **Neydal** (coast, fishing and salt), **Palai** (dry land, separation and raids).
- **Vattakirutal** in Sangam poems is a defeated king’s **ritual suicide by starving himself to death**.

| Kingdom | Emblem | Capital | Port | Famous king |
|---------|--------|---------|------|-------------|
| **Chera** | Bow | **Vanji / Vanchi / Karur (Karuvur)** | **Muziris (Muchiri)**; also **Tondi** | **Senguttuvan**; **Udiyanjiral** (early Chera) |
| **Chola** | Tiger | **Uraiyur** | **Puhar / Kaveripattinam** | **Karikala** |
| **Pandya** | Fish | **Madurai** | **Korkai** | **Nedunjeliyan** |

- **Karikala** builds the **Kallanai** (Grand Anicut) on the Kaveri and builds up Puhar. He is **not** Rajaraja I.
- **Uraiyur** on the Kaveri in Tiruchirappalli was also called **Koli** and **Varanam**. It was a major **cotton** centre and appears in the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
- **Udiyanjiral** belongs to the **Chera** dynasty and is often treated as the first historical Chera of the Sangam age.
- **Senguttuvan** sits on the west-coast Roman pepper trade. Later memory gives him the Pattini / Kannagi cult.
- The Pandyas hold the pearl coast at Korkai and patronise the Madurai assembly.
- Pandya country lies **south of the Kaveri**, centred on Madurai and parts of the far south. The river of that core is the **Vaigai**. Some question banks print **“Vengi”** for this Pandya river — do **not** confuse it with Eastern Chalukya **Vengi** on the Andhra coast.
- A **Pandya** king sent an ambassador to the kingdom of **Rome in 26 BCE**. The Pandya house has one of the longest remembered spans in the deep south, with capital at **Madurai**.

- The king is *vendan* (also Kon / Ko / Mannan). Land tax is **vari**. Customs are **sungam**. Units are **nadu** and **ur**. Sub-chiefs are **velir**. This is not a Mauryan kumara-mahamatra machine.
- Sangam literature describes the rise of **Chera, Chola, and Pandya** only. **Pallava** and **Kadamba** are **not** Sangam Muvendar houses.

- The anonymous Greek *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* lists western ports such as **Naura**, **Tondi**, **Mushiri / Muziris**, and **Nelcynda / Nelisanda**.
- **Arikamedu** near Pondicherry is the foremost Indo–Roman station on the eastern coast. An intaglio identified with **Augustus Caesar**, plus beads and gems, support Roman contact. Dubreuil called it “a true Roman city.” In the *Periplus* it is **Poduke**.
- An **amphora** is an ancient tall jar with a long narrow neck and **two handles**, used in Rome for wine or oil.
- Black pepper was a favourite of expensive Roman cookery. In Sanskrit it is ***Yavanapriya*** — “favourite of the Yavanas / Greeks.”
- Trade guilds of later south-Indian townships include **Manigramam**, **Nanadesi** (नानादेशी), and **Valanjiyar**.

- **Keeladi** is early historic Tamil urban. Tamil-Brahmi appears on pottery and caves. Yavana in the poems means a foreign or Roman trader.

> **Logic:** Muvendar is not Pallava. Sangam texts are not the *Arthashastra* (अर्थशास्त्र). **Poduke = Arikamedu**. **Yavanapriya = pepper**, not muslin.

## 8.9a Sangam Wars and Inter-State Politics

**Chera–Chola–Pandya rivalry | Puram war poems | not the later imperial Cholas**

Sangam **Puram** poetry remembers constant wars among the **Muvendar** — **Chera**, **Chola**, and **Pandya**. These are early historic Tamil kings, not the later imperial Cholas of Rajaraja I.

### Cause

- Three crowns competed for river land, ports, and prestige in Tamilakam.
- Hill and border **velir** chiefs fought under or against the great kings.

### Course — named kings

- **Karikala** of the Cholas fought rivals and built the **Kallanai** dam on the Kaveri.
- **Nedunjeliyan** of the Pandyas appears in war poems such as the **Purananuru**.
- **Senguttuvan** of the Cheras is linked to the west-coast pepper trade.
- The same Chera house is tied to the **Kannagi / Pattini** legend in *Silappadikaram*.

### Result / custom

- Victory brought tribute, cattle, and fame in Puram verse.
- When a king lost in battle, **Vattakirutal** could follow — ritual fasting unto death.
- Do not confuse Sangam Karikala with later **Rajaraja I**.

## 8.10 Later South — 2025 match only

**Not Post-Mauryan meat | parked here because 2025 dumped it on a south-dynasty list**

- **Mahendravarman I** is a **Pallava**. He belongs to the 7th century and to Mamallapuram. He wrote *Mattavilasa Prahasana* (मत्तविलास प्रहसन).
- **Kadungon** is a **Pandya**. He belongs to the 6th-century revival after the **Kalabhra** gap.
- **Amoghavarsha I** is a **Rashtrakuta**. He belongs to the 9th century at Manyakheta. He is linked to Kannada *Kavirajamarga*.
- **Rajaraja I** is a **Chola**. He rules **985–1014**. He builds **Brihadeshwara** at Thanjavur.

### 1. Native Successors of Mauryas (Shunga, Kanva, Satavahana, Chedi)
#### A. Sunga (Shunga) Dynasty (185 BC – 73 BC) [Capital: Vidisha, MP]
- **Establishment:** Founded in **185 BC** by **Pushyamitra Sunga (Pushyamitra Shunga)**, the Brahmin Commander-in-Chief who assassinated the last Mauryan emperor Brihadratha during an army review.
- **Religious Revival:** Staunch adherent of orthodox Hinduism; revived the **Bhagavata religion** and Brahminical influence.
- **Two Ashvamedha Sacrifices:** Performed **two Ashvamedha Yajnas**; the officiating royal priest was the celebrated grammarian **Patanjali** (author of the *Mahabhashya / Mahabhasya*, who was born at **Gonarda** in Central India).
- **Sunga Art & Monuments:**
  - Built and renovated the great Buddhist stupa at **Bharhut** (in Satna, MP).
  - Constructed the fine stone **gateway railing** surrounding the great Sanchi Stupa (originally built of brick by Ashoka).
  - Other monuments of Sunga art: Vihara, Chaitya, and Stupa of **Bhaja** (near Pune), **Amaravati Stupa**, and **Nasika (Nasik) Chaitya**.
- **Succession:**
  - Succeeded by son **Agnimitra**, the romantic hero of Kalidasa's famous classical drama *Malavikagnimitra*.
  - Followed by a succession of weak rulers: **Vasumitra, Vajramitra, Bhagabhadra** (in whose 14th regnal year Greek envoy Heliodorus set up the Garuda Pillar at Besnagar), and **Devabhuti**.

#### B. Kanva Dynasty (73 BC – 28 BC) [Capital: Pataliputra]
- In **73 BC**, **Devabhuti**, the degenerate last ruler of the Sunga dynasty, was murdered by his Brahmin minister **Vasudeva**, who usurped the throne and established the **Kanva dynasty**.
- Kanva rule lasted 45 years and came to an end in **28 BC**.

#### C. Satavahana Dynasty (60 BC – 225 AD) [Capital: Pratishthana / Pratishtana / Paithan (Maharashtra)]
- **Origin & Identity:** The most prominent native successors of the Mauryas in the Deccan and Central India; identified with the **Andhras** mentioned in the Puranas.
- **Geographical Cradle:** Early Satavahana kings appeared **not in Andhra, but in Maharashtra**, where the vast majority of their earliest inscriptions and coin hoards are found.
- **Key Kings & Chronology:**
  - **Simuka (60 BC – 37 BC):** The founder of the Satavahana dynasty.
  - **Satakarni I:** 3rd ruler; expanded power and prestige by conquests across Central India; performed two Ashvamedhas.
  - **Hala:** 17th ruler; great patron of literature and author of the celebrated Prakrit erotic anthology **Gathasaptasati (Sattasai)**. His court contemporary was **Gunadhya**, author of the lost Prakrit epic **Brihatkatha (Vrihat Katha)**.
  - **Gautamiputra Satakarni (106–130 AD):** 23rd ruler; the **greatest Satavahana ruler**; revived falling Satavahana fortunes, championed orthodox Brahmanism (*Ekabrahmana*), and utterly crushed the Saka Kshatrapa **Nahapana** (recorded in the Nasik Prasasti of his mother Gautami Balashri).
  - **Vasishthiputra Sri Satakarni:** 24th ruler; married the daughter of Western Saka Kshatrapa Rudradaman I, but was defeated twice in battle by him.
  - **Yajna Sri Satakarni:** 27th ruler; the dynasty's **last great king**; recovered northern territories; his coins feature maritime ships, testifying to active naval trade.
  - **Pulamavi III:** 30th ruler; the **last Satavahana ruler**. Satavahanas were finally supplanted in the 3rd century AD by the **Ikshvakus** in eastern Andhra and **Abhiras / Vakatakas** in the west.
- **Administrative & Cultural Innovations:**
  - **Earliest Inscriptional Evidence of Land Grants (1st Century BC):** Satavahanas initiated the historic practice of donating tax-free land (*Brahmadeya*) endowed with fiscal and administrative immunities to Brahmanas and Buddhist monks, which gradually fostered feudalism.
  - **Rock-cut Architecture:** Excavated numerous rock-cut worship halls (**Chaityas**) and monasteries (**Viharas**) in NW Deccan (Maharashtra): **Nasik, Kanheri, and Karle** (Karle Chaitya is the largest and most magnificent rock-cut chaitya in India).
  - **Stupa Architecture:** Famous stupas at **Amaravati** (a world-famous sculptural treasure house) and **Nagarjunakonda**.
  - **Language & Coinage:** Official court language was **Prakrit**. Issued coinage mainly in **lead**, as well as copper, bronze, and **potin** (billon).

#### D. Cheti / Chedi Dynasty of Kalinga (1st Century BC)
- Emerged in Kalinga (modern Odisha) in the 1st century BC.
- Sole historical information is derived from the famous **Hathigumpha Inscription** (near Bhubaneswar) incised by **Kharavela**, the 3rd ruler of the dynasty.
- A devout follower and generous royal patron of **Jainism**, Kharavela constructed residential rock-cut cave dwellings for Jain monks on **Udayagiri and Khandagiri hills** near Bhubaneswar.

### 2. Foreign Successors of Mauryas (Indo-Greeks, Sakas, Parthians, Kushanas)
#### A. The Indo-Greeks (Bactrian Greeks: 2nd Century BC)
- The **first foreign conquerors of North-Western India** in the post-Mauryan era.
- **Menander (Milinda: 165 BC – 145 BC):**
  - Most renowned Indo-Greek ruler; capital at **Sagala (Sialkot)**.
  - Converted to Buddhism by Buddhist philosopher **Nagasena (Nagarjuna)**; their philosophical debate is immortalised in the Pali classic **Milindapanho** ("Questions of Milinda").
- **Numismatic Landmarks:**
  - The Indo-Greeks were the **first rulers in India to issue coins which can be definitely attributed to particular kings** (portrait coins bearing royal names and legends).
  - They were the **first to issue gold coins in India**.
- **Artistic Legacy:** Introduced Greek / Hellenistic art traditions into NW India, giving birth to the hybrid **Gandhara School of Art**.

#### B. The Sakas (Scythians: 1st Century BC – 4th Century AD)
- Nomadic tribes of Central Asia who supplanted the Indo-Greeks; established **five distinct regional branches** across India.
- **Rudradaman I (130 AD – 150 AD):**
  - The most celebrated Saka ruler in India, belonging to the **Kardamaka branch** of Western Kshatrapas.
  - Celebrated for his military victories against the Satavahanas.
  - **Lake Sudarshana:** Restored and repaired the broken dam of Lake Sudarshana in Kathiawar (originally built under Chandragupta Maurya and Ashoka) entirely from his private purse **without levying forced labour (Vishti) or emergency taxes**.
  - **Sanskrit Patronage:** Issued the celebrated **Junagadh Rock Inscription**, which is the **first-ever long inscription in chaste, classical Kavya-style Sanskrit**.
- **Other Prominent Saka Rulers:** Nahapana, Ushavadata (Ushavadeva), Chashtana, and Ghamatika.
- **Vikrama Samvat (58 BC):** In ~58 BC, an unnamed king of Ujjain (celebrated as **Vikramaditya**) decisively defeated the Sakas and founded the **Vikrama Era (58 BC)** to commemorate the liberation of Ujjain.

#### C. The Parthians (Pahlavas: 1st Century BC – 1st Century AD)
- Originating in Iran, the Parthians moved into NW India, replacing Saka power in Taxila and Arachosia.
- **Gondaphernes (Gondophares):** The most famous Parthian monarch. According to Christian tradition, **St. Thomas (the Apostle)** visited India during his reign to propagate Christianity and suffered martyrdom near Madras.

#### D. The Kushanas (1st Century AD – 3rd Century AD)
- One of the **five clans of the Yuezhi (Yeuchi)** nomadic confederacy of Central Asia.
- **Kujula Kadphises (Kadphises I):** Founded the first Kushana dynasty; struck copper coins imitating Roman coins.
- **Vema Kadphises (Kadphises II):** Second king; consolidated the realm; issued the **first large-scale imperial gold coins** with Shaivite emblems (*Maheshvara*).
- **Kanishka I (78 AD – 101 AD) — "Second Ashoka":**
  - Founded the second Kushana line; the greatest of all Kushana emperors.
  - Ascended the throne in **78 AD**, inaugurating the **Saka Era (78 AD)**, which was adopted as the National Calendar by the Government of India in 1957.
  - Twin Capitals: **Purushapura (Peshawar)** as the main imperial seat and **Mathura** as the second capital in the Gangetic plain.
  - Convened the **Fourth Buddhist Council** at **Kundalavana (Kashmir)**, where Mahayana doctrines were codified.
  - Controlled the world-famous **Silk Route** linking China through Central Asia to Iran and the Roman West, yielding colossal toll revenues.
  - Kushanas were the **first rulers in India to issue gold coins on a wide and regular scale**.
  - Court Luminaries: Patronised a galaxy of intellectual giants: **Parsva, Vasumitra, Ashvaghosha, Nagarjuna, Charaka** (royal physician and author of *Charaka Samhita*), and **Mathara**.
- **Vasudeva I:** The last prominent Kushana monarch.

### 4. The Sangam Period (1st – 3rd Century AD)
#### A. The Three Early Kingdoms (Muvendar Master Matrix)
| Kingdom | Emblem | Inland Capital | Coastal Capital / Ports | First Known Ruler | Greatest / Most Famous Ruler |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Chera** | **Bow** | **Vanji / Karayur** | **Muzris** and **Tondi** | **Udiyanjeral (Udiyangeral)** | **Senguttuvan (Red Chera)** |
| **The Chola** | **Tiger** | **Uraiyur** (famed cotton emporium) | **Puhar / Kaveripattanam** | **Elara** | **Karikala** |
| **The Pandya** | **Fish** | **Madurai** (on Vaigai river; Sangam venue) | **Korkai / Colchoi** (famed for pearl fishery) | **Mudukudumi** | **Nedunjeliyan (Nendujelian)** |

#### B. Dynastic Peculiarities
- **The Cheras:**
  - Occupied central and northern Kerala and the Kongu region of Tamil Nadu.
  - Main ports: **Muzris** (Cranganore) and **Tondi**. The Roman Empire established **two military cohorts (regiments) at Muzris** and constructed a **Temple of Augustus at Muzris**.
  - **Udiyanjeral:** Earliest known ruler; tradition credits him with feeding the combatants of the Kurukshetra war.
  - **Senguttuvan (Red Chera):** Greatest ruler; invaded North India and crossed the Ganges; established the **Pattini Cult** (divine worship of Kannagi as the goddess of chastity).
- **The Cholas (Cholamandalam):**
  - Located between the Pennar and Vellar rivers (modern Thanjavur and Tiruchirappalli).
  - Inland capital **Uraiyur** was an internationally renowned centre for trade in **cotton textiles**.
  - Coastal capital & primary port was **Puhar (Kaveripattanam)**.
  - **Elara:** Conquered Sri Lanka and ruled it for 50 years.
  - **Karikala ("The Man with Charred Leg"):** Greatest Chola king; founded Puhar; constructed **160 km of flood embankments along the river Kaveri** using the labour of **12,000 Sri Lankan prisoners of war**.
  - Maintained a formidable naval fleet.
- **The Pandyas:**
  - First noticed by **Megasthenes**, who recorded that their kingdom was ruled by a woman and celebrated for **pearls**.
  - Capital **Madurai** on the **Vaigai river**; chief port **Korkai (Colchoi)**.
  - Dispatched diplomatic embassies to Roman emperors **Augustus** and **Trajan**.
  - Mentioned in the *Ramayana* and *Mahabharata*.
  - **Nedunjeliyan:** Greatest king; defeated a coalition of Cholas and Cheras at Talaiyalanganam. He erroneously executed Kovalan on false theft charges, causing the fiery destruction of Madurai by the curse of **Kannagi**.

#### C. Sangam Administration & Social Geography
- **The Crown:** King was the supreme administrative centre; styled **Ko, Mannan, Vendan, Korravan, or Iraivan**. The royal assembly was called **Avai**.
- **Panchamahasabha (Fivefold Ministerial Council):**
  1. **Amaichchar:** Ministers
  2. **Purohitar:** Priests
  3. **Dutar:** Envoys / Ambassadors
  4. **Senapatiyar:** Military Commanders
  5. **Orar:** Spies / Intelligence Agents
- **Territorial Hierarchy:** **Mandalam / Nadu** (Province) → **Ur** (Town) → **Perur** (Big Village) → **Sirur** (Small Village).
- **Settlement Typology:** **Pattinam** (coastal port town), **Puhar** (harbour area), **Cheri** (suburb of a town).
- **Revenue Administration (Taxes):**
  - **Karai:** Land tax.
  - **Irai:** Tribute from vassals and war booty.
  - **Ulgu:** Customs duties and tolls collected at ports.
  - **Iravu:** Extraordinary demand / forced royal contribution.
  - **Variyam:** A tax-yielding territorial revenue division.
  - **Variyar:** Royal tax collector.
- **Land Fertility:** Proverbial agricultural surplus: *"The space in which an elephant could lie down produced enough grain to feed seven persons."*

#### D. The Three Sangams (Assemblies of Tamil Poets)
| Sangam | Venue | Chairman | Surviving Literature | Royal Patrons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Sangam** | **Ten-Madurai** (Southern Madurai; submerged by the sea) | Sage **Agastya (Agattiyar)** | None (all texts perished) | 89 Pandya Kings (attended by 549 poets) |
| **2nd Sangam** | **Kapatapuram / Alvai** (submerged by the sea) | **Agastya** (founder); **Tolkappiyar** (subsequent chairman) | **Tolkappiyam** (by Tolkappiyar — earliest extant Tamil grammar) | 59 Pandya Kings |
| **3rd Sangam** | **North Madurai** | **Nakkirar** | *Ettutogai*, *Pattupattu*, *Patinenkilkanakku*, etc. | 49 Pandya Kings |

#### E. Corpus of Sangam Literature & Epics
- **Melkanakku (18 Major Works — Narrative):** Comprises **Ettutogai** (Eight Anthologies) and **Pattupattu** (Ten Idylls).
- **Kilkanakku (18 Minor Works — Didactic):** Comprises **Patinenkilkanakku** (Eighteen Didactic Texts).
- **Tirukkural (Kural / Muppal):**
  - Composed by **Tiruvalluvar**; part of Patinenkilkanakku.
  - Universally acclaimed as **"The Bible of Tamil Land"** and *Laghu Veda*; celebrated manual on ethics, statecraft, and marital love.
- **Great Tamil Epics:**
  1. **Silappadikaram ("The Story of the Anklet"):** Authored by Jain prince **Ilango Adigal**; narrates the tragic story of Kovalan, his devoted wife Kannagi, and courtesan Madhavi of Puhar; hailed as the **"Iliad of Tamil Poetry"**.
  2. **Manimekalai:** Authored by grain merchant **Sittalai Sattanar**; direct literary sequel to Silappadikaram; chronicles the adventures and spiritual conversion of Manimekalai (daughter of Kovalan and Madhavi); deeply tinged with **Buddhist philosophy**.
  3. **Sivaga Sindamani (Jivaka Chintamani):** Composed by Jain monk **Tiruttakkadevar**; masterwork deeply tinged with **Jain ideals**.
  4. **Bharatam:** Composed by **Perudevanar** (earliest Tamil rendition of the Mahabharata).

#### F. Panchatinai (Five Eco-zones of Tamilakam)
| Tinai (Eco-zone) | Landscape / Ecology | Primary Occupation | Typical Inhabitants |
