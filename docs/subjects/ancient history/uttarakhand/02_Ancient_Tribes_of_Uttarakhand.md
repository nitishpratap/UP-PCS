## Confused Pairs

| Pair | Key |
|------|-----|
| Tribe name vs dynasty capital | **Kuninda** is a people/polity; **Kartikeyapur** is a later capital seat |
| Kirata vs Khasa | Both are Himalayan ethnonyms; do not force one shared capital on either without a source |
| Yaudheya vs Kuninda | Yaudheya = famous **plains republic/gana**; Kuninda = **foothill** coin power rooted in UK–HP |
| Literary tribe vs coin polity | Kirata/Khasa prove early **naming**; Kuninda/Yaudheya prove early **political economy** |
| Yaudheya vs Yadava | Ancient **gana** ≠ medieval Yadava of Devagiri |

---

## Must-Score Facts

1. “Ancient tribes” = literary Himalayan ethnonyms + early coin polities.
2. **Kirata** and **Khasa** are ethnonym cards, not one-capital dynasties.
3. **Kuninda** is the first strong Uttarakhand-linked political name for Prelims.
4. **Yaudheya** is a **gana**; do not call it a Chandela-style monarchy.
5. Kartikeyapur is later kingdom geography, not a prehistoric tribe capital.

---

## 2.1 How this head works

- The syllabus line **“Ancient tribes of Uttarakhand”** expects Himalayan ethnonyms from epics and Puranas **plus** the first historic coin powers.
- Prefer one-sentence identity for each name: who they were, where they sat, and what source proves them.
- Do not dump medieval Chand or Parmar houses into this chapter.

---

## 2.2 Kirata

**Kirata | Himalayan belt | epic–Puranic ethnonym**

- Epic and Puranic geography place **Kirata** peoples in the northern mountain world.
- Coaching memory treats them as early hill dwellers of the Himalayan fringe, not as a single dated kingdom with one capital list.
- Use Kirata as a **peopling and naming** card, not as a substitute for Kuninda coins.

---

## 2.3 Khasa

**Khasa | central Himalaya | durable ethnonym**

- **Khasa** survives as a long Himalayan identity label in later traditions of Kumaon and Garhwal.
- Traditions sometimes link Khasa groups to early hill settlement and later local polities, but papers usually want the **ethnonym**, not a forged king-list.
- Keep Khasa separate from Kartikeyapur capital geography.

---

## 2.4 Tangana and other epic hill names

- Mahabharata geography also remembers northern peoples such as the **Tangana** (and neighbouring epic hill names in coaching lists).
- These names show that the hills already sat inside north Indian literary geography before clear dynastic chronicles.
- Treat them as **literary tribes**, not as full administrative dynasties with dated inscriptions.

---

## 2.5 From ethnonym to historic polity

- After the Mauryan and post-Mauryan churn, **Kuninda** coinage marks the first clear political economy of the central Himalayan foothills.
- **Yaudheya** power sits mainly in the Sapta-Sindhu / Haryana–Punjab–north Rajasthan belt, but hill coin finds show trade and contact with Garhwal–Kumaon.
- Full teaching cards for Kuninda and Yaudheya sit in [Topic 3](03_Kuninda_and_Yaudheya.md).
- Later kingdom-scale power begins with **Kartikeyapur / Katyuri**, taught in [Topic 4](04_Kartikepur_Dynasty.md).

## UKPCS Practice

**Q1.** Which of the following is the earliest well-attested coin-issuing polity linked to the Uttarakhand foothills?

A. Chand dynasty  
B. Parmar of Garhwal  
C. Kuninda  
D. Gorkha

**Logic:** Separates literary tribe labels and medieval houses from the first coin polity.

**Ans: C.** Kuninda coinage marks the early historic foothill polity. Chand and Parmar are medieval; Gorkha is modern.

</details>

**Q2.** With reference to ancient Uttarakhand, which pairing is correct?

A. Kirata — capital fixed at Baijnath in every inscription  
B. Khasa — only a twentieth-century census label with no older memory  
C. Yaudheya — warrior gana tradition of the plains fringe with hill coin finds  
D. Kuninda — identical with the later Chand dynasty of Almora  

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Logic:** Ethnonym vs gana vs medieval dynasty traps.

**Ans: C.** Yaudheya is an ancient warrior gana; hill finds show contact, not a Chand-style monarchy. Kirata/Khasa are ethnonyms without a forced Baijnath capital; Kuninda is earlier than Chand.

</details>

**Q3.** Which statement is correct?

A. Kartikeyapur is the first prehistoric tribe named in the Rigveda  
B. Literary Kirata and Khasa names prove early Himalayan peopling and naming  
C. Yaudheya and Yadava of Devagiri are the same house  
D. All ancient UK tribes are known only from Ashokan edicts  

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Logic:** What literary ethnonyms can and cannot prove.

**Ans: B.** Epic and Puranic names show early peopling and naming. Kartikeyapur is later kingdom geography; Yaudheya ≠ Yadava; Ashokan edicts do not catalogue every hill ethnonym.

</details>

## Common Traps

1. **Kuninda = Katyuri** — centuries and political form apart.
2. **Yaudheya capital = Almora** — plains gana with hill **finds**, not an Almora palace.
3. **Kirata / Khasa = one shared capital list** — ethnonyms, not forced city keys.
4. **Yaudheya = Yadava of Devagiri** — different ages and houses.
5. **Ancient tribes = only Chand and Parmar** — those are medieval UK cards.
