## 3. Panch Prayag & Indian River Drainage System

### A. The Panch Prayag of Uttarakhand (Order along Alaknanda River)
*Memorize downstream order from source towards plains:*
1. **Vishnuprayag**: **Alaknanda** + **Dhauliganga** (Chamoli)
2. **Nandaprayag**: **Alaknanda** + **Nandakini** (Chamoli)
3. **Karnaprayag**: **Alaknanda** + **Pindar** (Chamoli)
4. **Rudraprayag**: **Alaknanda** + **Mandakini** (Rudraprayag)
5. **Devprayag**: **Alaknanda** + **Bhagirathi** → Official **GANGA** begins here! (Tehri Garhwal)

### B. Ganga River Tributaries (Left vs Right Bank)
- **Left Bank Tributaries (West to East)**:
  1. **Ramganga** (originates in Dudhatoli range, Pauri Garhwal; flows through Jim Corbett NP; meets Ganga at Kannauj)
  2. **Gomati** (originates in plains at **Gomat Taal / Fulhar Lake, Pilibhit, UP** — *only Himalayan/Ganga tributary originating in the plains!*; Lucknow & Jaunpur on its banks; joins Ganga near Saidpur, Ghazipur)
  3. **Ghaghra / Karnali** (originates from Mapchachungo glacier near Mansarovar; joined by Sarda/Kali; **largest tributary by water volume**; joins Ganga at Chhapra, Bihar)
  4. **Gandak** (originates in Nepal Himalayas; joins Ganga at Sonpur / Hajipur, Bihar)
  5. **Kosi** (*Sorrow of Bihar*; 7 streams / Saptakoshi; notorious for course-shifting; joins at Kursela, Katihar)
  6. **Mahananda** (originates in Darjeeling hills; **last left-bank tributary of Ganga in India**).
- **Right Bank Tributaries**:
  1. **Yamuna** (longest tributary, 1376 km; Yamunotri glacier, Bandarpunch peak; joins at Prayagraj Triveni Sangam; its tributaries: Chambal, Sind, Betwa, Ken, Tons, Hindon)
  2. **Son** (originates from **Amarkantak Plateau, MP**; flows north-eastwards through Sonbhadra UP; joins Ganga near Danapur/Patna, Bihar)
  3. **Punpun** (originates in Palamu, Jharkhand; joins Ganga at Fatuha near Patna).

### C. Peninsular Drainage: West-Flowing vs East-Flowing
- **West-Flowing (into Arabian Sea — form Estuaries, NO deltas)**:
  - **Narmada** (1312 km): Amarkantak plateau (MP); flows through **Rift Valley** between Vindhyas (North) and Satpuras (South); forms **Dhuandhar Falls** at Marble Rocks (Bhedaghat, Jabalpur); Sardar Sarovar Dam.
  - **Tapi / Tapti** (724 km): Multai (Betul district, MP); flows in rift valley south of Satpuras; Ukai Dam & Kakrapar Project (Gujarat).
  - **Sabarmati**: Dhebar Lake (Aravalli Range, Udaipur); flows through Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar into Gulf of Khambhat.
  - **Mahi**: Vindhyan hills (Dhar, MP); **CUTS THE TROPIC OF CANCER TWICE!**
  - **Luni**: Originates near Ajmer (Aravallis) as Sagarmati; **ends in the marshy Rann of Kutch (Inland drainage, never reaches the sea)**; sweet water up to Balotra, then saline.
  - **Sharavati**: Shimoga (Karnataka); forms famous **Jog Falls (Gersoppa / Mahatma Gandhi Falls)**.
  - **Periyar & Bharathappuzha (Ponnani)**: Kerala.
- **East-Flowing (into Bay of Bengal — form large Deltas)**:
  - **Mahanadi** (851 km): Sihawa hills (Dhamtari, Chhattisgarh); flows through Odisha; Hirakud Dam.
  - **Godavari** (1465 km — **Dakshin Ganga / Vriddha Ganga**): Trimbakeshwar (Nashik, Maharashtra); **longest river in Peninsular India**; Tributaries: Pravara, Manjra, Penganga, Wainganga, Wardha, **Pranhita** (largest), Indravati, Sabari.
  - **Krishna** (1401 km): Mahabaleshwar (Maharashtra); Tributaries: Koyna, Ghataprabha, Malaprabha, Bhima, **Tungabhadra** (largest), Musi (Hyderabad on its banks), Munneru.
  - **Kaveri** (800 km — **Ganga of the South**): Talakaveri (Brahmagiri Range, Coorg, Karnataka); flows year-round (perennial due to SW & NE monsoons); creates river islands: **Srirangapatna, Shivanasamudra, and Srirangam**; Tributaries: Hemavati, Harangi, Shimsha, Arkavathi (left); Lakshmantirtha, Kabini, Bhavani, Noyyal, Amaravati (right).

## 7. Major Sea Ports & National Waterways of India

### A. 13 Major Ports of India
- **Western Coast**:
  1. **Deendayal Port (Kandla)** (Gulf of Kutch, Gujarat): Tidal port; free trade zone (SEZ); handles crude oil & grains.
  2. **Mumbai Port** (Maharashtra): Largest natural port and harbour in India.
  3. **Jawaharlal Nehru Port (JNPT / Nhava Sheva)** (Navi Mumbai): **Largest container port in India**; fully mechanized.
  4. **Mormugao Port** (Goa - Zuari estuary): Premier port for iron ore exports from Goa.
  5. **New Mangalore Port** (Karnataka): Handles iron ore exports from Kudremukh.
  6. **Cochin Port** (Kerala - Willingdon Island in Vembanad Lake): Natural harbour; International Container Transshipment Terminal (Vallarpadam).
- **Eastern Coast**:
  7. **V.O. Chidambaranar Port (Tuticorin)** (Tamil Nadu - Gulf of Mannar): Artificial deep-sea port; pearl fishery.
  8. **Chennai Port** (Tamil Nadu): **Oldest artificial harbour on the east coast**; second busiest.
  9. **Kamarajar Port (Ennore)** (Tamil Nadu): **First corporatized port in India** (public company).
  10. **Visakhapatnam Port** (Andhra Pradesh): **Deepest landlocked and protected port in India** (Dolphin’s Nose hill protects from storms).
  11. **Paradip Port** (Odisha - Mahanadi delta): Deep-water port exporting iron ore to Japan.
  12. **Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port (Kolkata–Haldia)** (West Bengal): **Only riverine major port in India** (on Hooghly River); twin dock system (Kolkata & Haldia).
- **Island**:
  13. **Port Blair Port** (Andaman & Nicobar Islands).

### B. Core National Waterways (NW) of India
- **NW-1**: **Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly River System** from **Prayagraj to Haldia** (1,620 km) — **Longest National Waterway in India**; passes through UP, Bihar, Jharkhand, WB.
- **NW-2**: **Brahmaputra River** from **Sadiya to Dhubri** (891 km) in Assam.
- **NW-3**: **West Coast Canal** from **Kottapuram to Kollam** (205 km) in Kerala (includes Champakara & Udyogmandal canals).
- **NW-4**: **Krishna–Godavari Rivers & Canals** from Kakinada to Puducherry (1,078 km).
- **NW-5**: **Brahmani River, Deltaic channels of Mahanadi & East Coast Canal** (588 km) in Odisha & WB.
- **NW-16**: **Barak River** from Lakhipur to Bhanga (121 km) in Assam.

## 11. Ocean Currents & Planetary Local Winds of the World

### A. Major Ocean Currents (Warm vs Cold Master Matrix)
*Rule of Thumb: Currents moving from Equator to Poles = **Warm**; Currents moving from Poles to Equator = **Cold**.*

| Ocean Basin | Warm Ocean Currents | Cold Ocean Currents | High-Yield Prelims Exam Takeaways |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atlantic Ocean** | • **Gulf Stream** (powerful warm current)  <br>• **North Atlantic Drift** (keeps NW European ports ice-free year-round)  <br>• **Florida Current**  <br>• **Antilles Current**  <br>• **Brazil Current** (South Atlantic)  <br>• **Guinea Current** (West Africa) | • **Labrador Current** (flows from Arctic past Greenland)  <br>• **Canary Current** (North Africa coast)  <br>• **Benguela Current** (South-West Africa coast; creates **Namib & Kalahari Deserts**)  <br>• **Falkland / Malvinas Current** (Patagonia coast)  <br>• **East Greenland Current** | • **Grand Banks of Newfoundland**: Confluence of warm **Gulf Stream** and cold **Labrador Current** produces dense fog and creates world's richest cod-fishing ground.  <br>• **Sargasso Sea**: Clockwise North Atlantic gyre creates a calm, landless sea surrounded by currents, filled with *Sargassum* seaweed. |
| **Pacific Ocean** | • **Kuroshio (Japan Current)** (warm counterpart of Gulf Stream)  <br>• **North Pacific Drift**  <br>• **East Australian Current** (flows south along eastern Australia)  <br>• **Alaska Current** (keeps Alaskan coast ice-free)  <br>• **Tsushima Current** (Sea of Japan) | • **Oyashio (Kuril Current)** (cold current from Bering Strait)  <br>• **California Current** (West coast of USA)  <br>• **Peru / Humboldt Current** (flows along Chile & Peru; high upwelling, rich in anchovies; suppressed during **El Niño**)  <br>• **Okhotsk Current** | • Meeting of warm **Kuroshio** and cold **Oyashio** creates dense fog and prime fishing grounds off Japan.  <br>• **Atacama Desert** (driest non-polar desert) created by the desiccating effect of the cold **Humboldt/Peru Current**. |

## 12. Structure of Atmosphere & Key Meteorological Phenomena

| Atmospheric Layer | Height Range | Temperature Trend | Landmark Phenomenon / Exam Triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Troposphere** | **0 to 18 km** (Equator: ~18 km; Poles: ~8 km) | **Decreases with altitude**  <br>(Lapse Rate: **6.5°C per km** or **1°C per 165 m**) | • Thickest at Equator due to strong vertical convectional currents.  <br>• Contains **~75% to 80% of total atmospheric mass** and **almost ALL atmospheric water vapour and dust particles**.  <br>• **ALL weather phenomena** (clouds, rainfall, lightning, thunder, cyclones, fog) occur EXCLUSIVELY in this layer.  <br>• Upper boundary is the **Tropopause** (temperature drops to -80°C over Equator and -45°C over Poles). |
| **2. Stratosphere** | **From Tropopause up to 50 km** | **Increases with altitude**  <br>(from -60°C up to 0°C at Stratopause) | • **Contains the Ozone Layer (Ozonosphere)** between **15 km and 35 km** (absorbs harmful solar ultraviolet UV-B and UV-C radiation).  <br>• Completely free from clouds and turbulent weather; ideal condition for flying **commercial supersonic jet aircraft**.  <br>• High-altitude **Subtropical Jet Streams** blow near the tropopause.  <br>• Rare iridescent **Nacreous Clouds (Mother-of-Pearl clouds)** form in polar winter stratosphere. |
| **3. Mesosphere** | **50 km to 80 km** | **Decreases rapidly with altitude**  <br>(reaches **-100°C at Mesopause**) | • **COLDEST LAYER OF THE ATMOSPHERE** (-100°C to -130°C).  <br>• **Meteors burn up** in this layer upon entering from space due to friction with thin gases.  <br>• Rare **Noctilucent Clouds** (night-shining polar clouds composed of ice crystals on meteor dust) appear here. |
| **4. Thermosphere / Ionosphere** | **80 km to 400+ km** | **Increases rapidly with altitude**  <br>(exceeds 1,500°C due to intense solar radiation absorption) | • Contains electrically charged ionized gas particles (**Ions**).  <br>• **Reflects terrestrial radio waves back to Earth**, enabling wireless global radio communication:  <br>  - **D-layer (60–90 km)**: Reflects low-frequency radio waves, absorbs high-frequency; disappears at night.  <br>  - **E-layer / Kennelly-Heaviside Layer (90–130 km)**: Reflects medium-frequency radio waves.  <br>  - **F-layer / Appleton-Barnett Layer (140–400 km)**: Reflects high-frequency short radio waves.  <br>• **Auroras** (polar light displays caused by solar wind hitting charged atmospheric gases):  <br>  - **Aurora Borealis** (Northern Lights, Arctic)  <br>  - **Aurora Australis** (Southern Lights, Antarctic)  <br>• **International Space Station (ISS)** orbits in this layer (~400 km). |
| **5. Exosphere** | **>400 km to outer space** (~10,000 km) | Extremely high temperature, but imperceptible due to ultra-low density | • Outermost rarefied fringe of Earth's atmosphere merging into interplanetary space.  <br>• Dominated by light gases: **Hydrogen and Helium**.  <br>• Artificial communication and weather satellites orbit in higher exosphere. |
