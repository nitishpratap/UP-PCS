## Consolidated — 50 Must-Score Facts

1. Uttar Pradesh has the largest **Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)** in India with **403 elected members**.
2. Prior to the creation of Uttarakhand on 9 November 2000, the UP Legislative Assembly had **425 seats**.
3. The **UP Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)** has **100 seats**, the largest Upper House among Indian states.
4. Uttar Pradesh is represented by **80 Lok Sabha Members** and **31 Rajya Sabha Members**, the highest for any state.
5. In the Lok Sabha, **17 seats** are reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC) and **0 seats** for Scheduled Tribes (ST) from UP.
6. In the UP Legislative Assembly, **84 seats** are reserved for SCs and **2 seats** (Obra and Duddhi) for STs.
7. Under the 91st Constitutional Amendment Act (2003), the Council of Ministers in UP is capped at **60 ministers** (15% of 403).
8. The value of an MLA's vote from Uttar Pradesh in the Presidential Election is **208**, the highest among all Indian states.
9. **Smt. Sarojini Naidu** was the **First Woman Governor** of Uttar Pradesh and the first woman Governor in India (1947–1949).
10. **Pandit Govind Ballabh Pant** was the **First Chief Minister** of Uttar Pradesh (1950–1954).
11. **Smt. Sucheta Kripalani** became the **First Woman Chief Minister** of Uttar Pradesh and India in October 1963.
12. **Chaudhary Charan Singh** served as the first non-Congress Chief Minister of UP (1967) and later became Prime Minister of India.
13. The **High Court of Judicature at Allahabad** was established on **17 March 1866** at Agra before moving to Allahabad in **1869**.
14. The **Lucknow Bench** of the Allahabad High Court was created in **1948** by amalgamating the Chief Court of Oudh.
15. The Allahabad High Court has a sanctioned strength of **160 Judges**, making it the largest High Court in India.
16. The **first Chief Justice** of Allahabad High Court was **Sir Walter Morgan** (1866).
17. The first Indian Chief Justice of Allahabad High Court post-independence was **Justice Bidhu Bhushan Malik**.
18. Uttar Pradesh is administratively divided into **18 Divisions (Mandals)** and **75 Districts (Zilas)**.
19. The headquarters of **Devipatan Division** is located at **Gonda**.
20. The headquarters of **Chitrakoot Division** is located at **Banda**.
21. The largest administrative divisions by number of districts (6 districts each) are **Lucknow, Kanpur, and Meerut**.
22. **Hapur** is the smallest district of UP by geographic area (660 km²), while **Lakhimpur Kheri** is the largest (7,680 km²).
23. The **Uttar Pradesh Public Service Commission (UPPSC)** was established on **1 April 1937** with its headquarters at **Prayagraj**.
24. Members of UPPSC are appointed by the **Governor**, but can only be removed by the **President of India** (Article 317).
25. The functions of UPPSC can be extended exclusively by the **UP State Legislature** under Article 321 of the Constitution.

30. The tenure of the UP Lokayukta was extended from 6 years to **8 years** by an amendment in 2012.
31. The base statute for rural local self-government in UP is the **UP Panchayat Raj Act, 1947**.
32. Intermediate and district rural bodies operate under the **UP Kshettra Panchayats and Zila Panchayats Act, 1961**.

35. The political head of a Gram Panchayat is the **Gram Pradhan**, directly elected by the Gram Sabha.
36. The **Gram Sabha** consists of all persons registered in the electoral rolls of a village within the Panchayat area.

49. The head of the administrative hierarchy and civil services in Uttar Pradesh is the **Chief Secretary**.

## Teaching cards

### 2.1 The State Executive & Civil Administration

#### A. The Governor of Uttar Pradesh (Articles 153–161)
- The Governor is the constitutional and executive head of the State.
- **Appointment & Qualifications**: Appointed by the President of India under Article 155 for a normal term of 5 years (holds office during the pleasure of the President). Must be a citizen of India and at least 35 years of age.
- **Oath of Office**: Administered by the Chief Justice of the **Allahabad High Court** (Article 159).
- **Historical Milestones**:
  - **Smt. Sarojini Naidu**: Appointed on 15 August 1947, becoming the **First Woman Governor of any State in Independent India**. She served until her death in office on 2 March 1949 at Lucknow.
  - **Sri Hormasji Peroshaw Mody**: Served as Governor from 1949 to 1952 during the formal renaming of the state from United Provinces to Uttar Pradesh on 24 January 1950.

#### B. The Chief Minister and Council of Ministers (Articles 163–167)
- The real executive authority is vested in the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister.
- **Size Limitation (91st Amendment Act, 2003)**:
  - Article 164(1A) mandates that the total number of Ministers, including the Chief Minister, in the Council of Ministers in a State shall **not exceed 15%** of the total number of members of the Legislative Assembly.
  - For Uttar Pradesh ($403 	ext{ MLAs} 	imes 15\% = 60.45$), the maximum permissible size of the UP Council of Ministers is **60 Ministers**. The constitutional minimum floor is 12 ministers.
- **Historical Leadership in UP**:
  - **Pandit Govind Ballabh Pant**: First Premier of United Provinces (1937–1939, 1946–1950) and the **First Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh** (26 January 1950 – 27 December 1954). Conferred Bharat Ratna in 1957.
  - **Dr. Sampurnanand**: Second CM (1954–1960); pioneer of Varanasi Sanskrit University (Sampurnanand Sanskrit University).
  - **Smt. Sucheta Kripalani**: Took office on 2 October 1963, becoming the **First Woman Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh and in India** (served until March 1967).
  - **Chaudhary Charan Singh**: First non-Congress Chief Minister of UP (1967 and 1970); later 5th Prime Minister of India (champion of peasant rights / *Kisan Diwas*).
  - **Mayawati**: First Dalit woman Chief Minister of any Indian state; served as UP CM across 4 separate terms.

#### A. Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)
- **Total Assembly Seats**: **403**.
- **Historical Trajectory**:
  - United Provinces Assembly in 1950 had 430 seats.
  - Delimitation in 1967 reduced it to 425 seats.
  - On **9 November 2000**, with the creation of Uttarakhand (carving out 13 hill districts and 22 assembly constituencies), the UP Assembly was reconstituted to **403 elected seats** (and 1 nominated Anglo-Indian seat, which was subsequently discontinued via the 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019).
- **Seat Reservations**: Under Article 332, **84 seats** are reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC) and **2 seats** (Obra and Duddhi in Sonbhadra) are reserved for Scheduled Tribes (ST).

#### B. Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)
- Uttar Pradesh has a **bicameral legislature** (one of only 6 states: UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana).
- **Total Council Seats**: **100**.
- **Constitutional Limits (Article 171)**: Maximum strength cannot exceed one-third of the total membership of the Legislative Assembly ($403 / 3 = 134$), and minimum strength cannot be less than 40.
- **Composition of the 100 Seats**:
  1. **36 Members (36%)**: Elected by electorates consisting of members of Municipalities, Zila Panchayats, and Kshetra Panchayats (Local Authorities constituencies).
  2. **36 Members (36%)**: Elected by the members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs) using proportional representation by means of a single transferable vote.
  3. **10 Members (10%)**: Nominated by the **Governor** having special knowledge/practical experience in Literature, Science, Art, Co-operative Movement, and Social Service.
  4. **9 Members (9%)**: Elected by registered Graduates of at least 3 years' standing from Graduates constituencies.
  5. **9 Members (9%)**: Elected by Teachers of at least 3 years' standing in secondary/higher educational institutions from Teachers constituencies.

### 2.3 The Judiciary: High Court of Judicature at Allahabad

#### A. Genesis and Evolution
- Established by Royal Letters Patent on **17 March 1866** under the High Courts Act, 1861, as the **High Court of Judicature for the North-Western Provinces** at **Agra**.
- Shifted from Agra to **Allahabad (Prayagraj)** in **1869**.
- Designated officially as the **High Court of Judicature at Allahabad** on 11 March 1919.
- **The Lucknow Bench**:
  - The Chief Court of Oudh was established at Lucknow in 1925.
  - By the **United Provinces High Courts (Amalgamation) Order, 1948**, the Chief Court of Oudh was amalgamated into the High Court of Allahabad, creating the permanent **Lucknow Bench**.
- **Jurisdiction Post-2000**:
  - With the enactment of the UP Reorganisation Act, 2000, 13 northern districts fell under the newly established **Uttarakhand High Court at Nainital** (established 9 November 2000).

#### B. Bench Strength and Jurisdiction
- **Sanctioned Judicial Strength**: **160 Judges** (Permanent: 119, Additional: 41), making it the **largest High Court in India and the world by judicial manpower**.
- **First Chief Justice**: Sir Walter Morgan (1866–1871).
- **First Indian Chief Justice (Post-Independence)**: Justice Bidhu Bhushan Malik (1947–1955).

### 2.5 Local Self-Government in Uttar Pradesh

#### A. Rural Local Bodies (Panchayati Raj)
- Enacted through the **UP Panchayat Raj Act, 1947** (for Gram Panchayats) and the **UP Kshettra Panchayats and Zila Panchayats Act, 1961** (for intermediate and district tiers), conforming to the **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992**.
- **Three-Tier Structure**:
  1. **Gram Panchayat (Village Level)**: Headed by directly elected **Gram Pradhan**. All adults registered on electoral rolls form the **Gram Sabha**.
  2. **Kshetra Panchayat (Block Level)**: Headed by **Block Pramukh** (elected by elected members of Kshetra Panchayat / BDCs). Executive officer is **Block Development Officer (BDO)**.
  3. **Zila Panchayat (District Level)**: Apex tier headed by **Zila Panchayat Adhyaksha** (elected by elected Zila Panchayat ward members). Executive officer is Chief Development Officer (CDO) / Apar Mukhya Adhikari (AMA).
- **Reservation Policy**:
  - Constitutional floor is 33.3% (1/3rd) for women; Uttar Pradesh statutory provisions guarantee **50% reservation for women** across PRIs and ULBs.
  - Reservations for SCs and STs proportional to their population; 27% reservation for Backward Classes (OBCs).

#### B. Urban Local Bodies (Municipalities)
- Enacted through the **UP Municipalities Act, 1916** and **UP Municipal Corporation Act, 1959**, conforming to the **74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992**.
- **Three Categories of Urban Bodies**:
  1. **Nagar Nigam (Municipal Corporation)**: For large urban areas with populations exceeding 5 lakhs. Headed politically by a directly elected **Mayor (Mahapaur)**; administrative executive is the **Municipal Commissioner (Nagar Ayukt - IAS)**.
     - **17 Nagar Nigams in UP**: Lucknow, Kanpur, Prayagraj, Varanasi, Agra, Ghaziabad, Meerut, Bareilly, Aligarh, Moradabad, Gorakhpur, Jhansi, Saharanpur, Firozabad, Ayodhya, Mathura-Vrindavan, Shahjahanpur (17th Nagar Nigam).
  2. **Nagar Palika Parishad (Municipal Council)**: For smaller urban areas (populations 1 lakh to 5 lakhs). Headed by an elected President (*Parishad Adhyaksha*); executive officer is Executive Officer (EO).
  3. **Nagar Panchayat (Town Area)**: For transitional areas from rural to urban.
- **The Industrial Township Exception (Article 243Q)**:
  - Under the Proviso to Article 243Q(1), an urban area specified as an **Industrial Township** provided with municipal services by a statutory development authority is exempt from constituting a Municipal Corporation.
  - Examples: **NOIDA** (New Okhla Industrial Development Authority), **Greater Noida**, and **YEIDA** (Yamuna Expressway Industrial Development Authority) operate under the UP Industrial Area Development Act, 1976.
