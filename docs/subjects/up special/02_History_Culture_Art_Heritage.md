## Consolidated — 50 Must-Score Facts

1. **8 of the 16 Mahajanapadas** of the 6th century BCE were located in present-day Uttar Pradesh.
2. The **Sohgaura Copper Plate Inscription** in Gorakhpur is the earliest known record of state drought relief and famine storehouses in India.
3. Lord Buddha delivered his first sermon (**Dharmachakrapravartana**) at **Sarnath (Varanasi)** in the presence of 5 ascetics.
4. Lord Buddha spent the maximum number of rainy-season retreats (**25 Varshavas**) at **Shravasti (Sahet-Mahet)**.
5. Lord Buddha attained **Mahaparinirvana** at **Kushinagar (Kasia)** in 483 BCE on the banks of the Hiranyavati River.
6. The **Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath** was officially adopted as the **National Emblem of India** on 26 January 1950.
7. **Prayag Prashasti** (Allahabad Pillar) was composed in classical Sanskrit by court poet **Harishena** eulogizing Samudragupta.
8. The **Bhitari Pillar Inscription** in Ghazipur district records Emperor **Skandagupta's** crushing defeat of the invading Hunas.
9. **Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh (Lalitpur)** is one of the earliest surviving Gupta stone panchayatana temples.
10. **Bhitargaon Temple in Kanpur** is the oldest surviving terraced brick temple with a shikhara and true arches in India.
11. **Kannauj** was the imperial capital of Emperor **Harshavardhana** and the prize of the 8th–10th century **Tripartite Struggle**.
12. **Agra** was founded by Sultan **Sikandar Lodi in 1504**, who shifted the Sultanate capital there in **1506**.
13. **Jaunpur** was founded by **Feroz Shah Tughlaq in 1359** in memory of Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan) and nicknamed **"Shiraz-e-Hind"**.

15. **Akbar** established his ceremonial capital at **Fatehpur Sikri** in honour of Sufi Saint **Sheikh Salim Chishti**.

18. **Taj Mahal** was designed by chief architect **Ustad Ahmad Lahori** and completed in 1648 on the banks of the Yamuna.
19. The Nawabi dynasty of Awadh was established in **1722 by Saadat Khan (Burhan-ul-Mulk)**.
20. Nawab **Asaf-ud-Daula shifted the capital of Awadh from Faizabad to Lucknow in 1775**.
21. Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula constructed the **Bara Imambara** (with its famous Bhul-bhulaiya) and **Rumi Darwaza** in 1784 as famine relief projects.
22. Awadh was annexed by Lord Dalhousie on **13 February 1856** on grounds of alleged **"misgovernance"** (James Outram Report).

26. **Rani Lakshmibai (Manikarnika)** was martyred fighting British forces under General Hugh Rose at Gwalior in June 1858.
27. **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah**, who spearheaded the resistance in Faizabad, was hailed as the "Lighthouse of Rebellion".
28. **Khan Bahadur Khan** established a parallel administration during the 1857 Revolt in **Bareilly (Rohilkhand)**.
29. **Maulvi Liaquat Ali** led the 1857 uprising in **Prayagraj (Allahabad)** from Khusro Bagh.
30. Queen Victoria's **Proclamation of 1858** was read out by Lord Canning on **1 November 1858 at Minto Park, Allahabad**.
31. A total of **9 Annual Sessions** of the Indian National Congress were hosted in Uttar Pradesh prior to 1947.
32. **Sarojini Naidu** became the **First Indian Woman President** of the Indian National Congress at the **Kanpur Session (1925)**.
33. The historic **Lucknow Session of INC (1916)** presided by **A.C. Mazumdar** saw the signing of the **Lucknow Pact**.
34. The **UP Kisan Sabha** was founded in **February 1918** by Gauri Shankar Mishra, Indra Narain Dwivedi, and Madan Mohan Malaviya.
35. The **Oudh Kisan Sabha** was established in **October 1920 at Pratapgarh** under the leadership of **Baba Ramchandra**.
36. The **Eka Movement (1921–22)** was a radical peasant uprising in Hardoi, Bahraich, and Sitapur led by **Madari Pasi**.
37. The **Chauri Chaura incident** occurred on **4 February 1922** in Gorakhpur, leading Mahatma Gandhi to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement on **12 February 1922**.
38. The **Kakori Train Action** was carried out on **9 August 1925** near Lucknow by revolutionaries of the HRA.
39. **Ram Prasad Bismil** was martyred at **Gorakhpur Jail**, **Ashfaqulla Khan** at **Faizabad Jail**, **Roshan Singh** at **Naini (Prayagraj) Jail**, and **Rajendra Lahiri** at **Gonda Jail** in Dec 1927.
40. **Chandrashekhar Azad** attained martyrdom fighting British police at **Alfred Park, Prayagraj on 27 February 1931**.
41. **Chittu Pandey ("Sher-e-Ballia")** established the first **Parallel Government** (*Swaraj Sarkar*) at Ballia during the Quit India Movement in August 1942.
42. **Charkula Dance** is a traditional folk dance of the **Braj (Mathura) region** where women balance a wooden pyramid with **108 lit lamps**.
43. **Rai Dance** is a peacock folk dance performed by the Beriya community of **Bundelkhand**.

48. **Bateshwar Fair (Agra)** is the largest livestock and camel fair in Western UP, held on the banks of Yamuna.

### 2.1 Ancient Uttar Pradesh: Mahajanapadas, Mauryan, Gupta and Harsha Eras

#### A. The Eight Mahajanapadas in Uttar Pradesh (6th Century BCE)
Out of the 16 Mahajanapadas mentioned in the Buddhist text *Anguttara Nikaya* and Jain text *Bhagavati Sutra*, **8 Mahajanapadas** were located within the territory of present-day Uttar Pradesh:

│                    8 MAHAJANAPADAS OF UTTAR PRADESH                        │
├─────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┤

├─────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Kashi        │ Varanasi                     │ Varanasi & Surroundings   │
│ 2. Kosala       │ Shravasti / Saket (Ayodhya)  │ Ayodhya, Gonda, Bahraich  │

#### B. The Buddhist Heritage Circuit in Uttar Pradesh
Uttar Pradesh is the cradle of Buddhism where Lord Gautama Buddha spent the majority of his monastic life:
1. **Sarnath (Varanasi)**:
   - Known anciently as *Mrigadava* (Deer Park) and *Isipatana*.
   - Site of the **First Sermon (Dharmachakrapravartana)** delivered to the five ascetics (Pancha Vaggiya).
   - Monolithic Ashoka Pillar with the **Four-Lion Capital** (adopted as the National Emblem of India in 1950) with the *Dharmachakra* (24 spokes on the National Flag).
   - **Dhamek Stupa** (built in Gupta period over Mauryan foundations) and **Chaukhandi Stupa**.
2. **Shravasti (Sahet-Mahet)**:
   - Capital of Kosala Kingdom situated on the banks of Achiravati (Rapti) River.
   - Buddha spent **25 Varshavas (rainy season retreats)** here (19 in Jetavana Monastery donated by merchant Anathapindika and 6 in Pubbarama donated by Vishakha).
   - Performed the Great Miracle (*Mahapratiharya*) to confound non-believers.
3. **Kushinagar (Kasia)**:
   - Capital of the Mallas on the banks of Hiranyavati River.
   - Site of Buddha's **Mahaparinirvana** at the age of 80 in 483 BCE.
   - **Parinirvana Temple** housing a 6.10-metre monolith statue of the Reclining Buddha in red sandstone.
   - **Ramabhar Stupa** (Mukutbandhana Chaitya), the exact cremation site of Buddha.
4. **Sankisa (Farrukhabad)**:
   - Located on the banks of Ikshumati (Kalinadi) River.
   - Believed to be the place where Buddha descended from *Trayastrimsa Heaven* down a triple ladder after preaching Dhamma to his mother Queen Maya.
5. **Kapilavastu (Piprahwa-Ganwaria, Siddharthnagar)**:
   - Childhood home and palace of Prince Siddhartha; excavation of ancient stupa revealed inscribed soapstone relic caskets containing bone fragments of the Buddha.
6. **Kaushambi**:
   - Visited by Buddha in the 6th and 9th years after enlightenment; site of the ancient **Ghoshitarama Monastery**.

#### C. Inscriptions and Architecture of Imperial Dynasties
- **Prayag Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription)**: Inscribed on an Ashokan pillar by court poet **Harishena** in classical Sanskrit using Champu kavya style; records the military conquests and imperial genealogy of **Samudragupta** ("Napoleon of India"). Inscriptions of Ashoka, Samudragupta, and later Jahangir coexist on this single monolith.
- **Bhitari Pillar Inscription (Ghazipur)**: Describes the valour of **Skandagupta**, detailing his crushing defeat of the White Huna invaders, saving northern India from destruction.
- **Gupta Brick and Stone Architecture**:
  - **Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh (Lalitpur)**: Built in the Panchayatana style; showcases exquisite high-relief sculptures of *Gajendramoksha*, *Nara-Narayana*, and *Sheshashayi Vishnu*.
  - **Bhitargaon Brick Temple (Kanpur Nagar)**: Unique surviving terraced Gupta brick temple with an early curvilinear shikhara and true arches.

### 2.2 Medieval Uttar Pradesh: Sultanate, Sharqi Dynasty, Mughals and Awadh Nawabs

#### A. Jaunpur and the Sharqi Sultanate (1394–1479 CE)
- Founded by **Feroz Shah Tughlaq in 1359** in memory of his cousin Muhammad bin Tughlaq (whose real name was **Jauna Khan**).
- Became an independent kingdom under **Malik Sarwar (Khwaja-i-Jahan)** who assumed the title *Sultan-us-Sharq* (King of the East) in 1394.
- Reached its cultural zenith under **Ibrahim Shah Sharqi (1402–1440)**, earning Jaunpur the glorious epithet **"Shiraz-e-Hind"** (The Shiraz of India) for its outstanding patronage of Islamic scholarship, Arabic-Persian theology, and arts.
- **Architectural Highlights**:
  - **Atala Masjid (1408 CE)**: Massive rectangular sloping propylons (pylons), arched central gateways, and absence of minarets.
  - **Jama Masjid** and **Lal Darwaza Masjid** (built by Queen Raji Bibi in 1450).

#### B. The Mughal Period in Uttar Pradesh
1. **Agra as the Imperial Capital**:
   - **Sikandar Lodi** founded the city of Agra in **1504** and shifted the Delhi Sultanate capital to Agra in **1506**.
   - **Babur** established the Mughal dynasty after the 1st Battle of Panipat (1526); buried initially at **Aram Bagh (Ram Bagh)** in Agra (oldest Mughal garden in India) before being relocated to Kabul.
   - **Akbar** constructed the massive **Agra Fort** (1565–1573) in red sandstone, featuring the Jahangiri Mahal and Akbari Mahal.
   - **Shah Jahan** added white marble structures inside Agra Fort (Diwan-i-Khas, Diwan-i-Aam, Moti Masjid, Khas Mahal, Musamman Burj).
   - **Taj Mahal (1631–1648)**: Built by Shah Jahan on the banks of Yamuna as a mausoleum for Mumtaz Mahal; chief architect **Ustad Ahmad Lahori**. UNESCO World Heritage Site (1983).
   - **Itimad-ud-Daulah's Tomb (Agra)**: Built by Empress **Nur Jahan** for her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg (1622–1628); the first Mughal monument built entirely of white marble and the pioneer of delicate **Pietra Dura** (parchin kari) inlay work ("Baby Taj").
   - **Akbar's Tomb, Sikandra (Agra)**: Commenced by Akbar and completed by Jahangir (1613); distinct five-tiered pyramidal structure without a central dome.
2. **Fatehpur Sikri (City of Victory)**:
   - Built by Akbar between **1571 and 1585** on the ridge of Sikri to honour Sufi Saint **Sheikh Salim Chishti**, whose blessings led to the birth of Prince Salim (Jahangir).
   - **Buland Darwaza (1575/1601)**: 54-metre high majestic triumphal archway built to commemorate Akbar's historic conquest of Gujarat.
   - Other monuments: White marble **Tomb of Salim Chishti** with delicate jali lattice screens, **Panch Mahal** (5-storey pyramidal wind tower based on Buddhist vihara plan), **Ibadat Khana** (Hall of Inter-religious Debates, 1575), and **Diwan-i-Khas** (with its massive central carved pillar).

#### C. The Nawabs of Awadh (1722–1856 CE)
- **Saadat Khan (Burhan-ul-Mulk)** appointed Governor (Subedar) of Awadh by Mughal Emperor Muhammad Shah Rangeela in **1722**; established hereditary autonomy.
- **Safdarjung (1739–1754)**: Became Wazir of the Mughal Empire; his grand mausoleum is in Delhi.
- **Shuja-ud-Daula (1754–1775)**: Fought alongside Mir Qasim and Shah Alam II in the **Battle of Buxar (1764)**; signed the humiliating **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** with Robert Clive.
- **Asaf-ud-Daula (1775–1797)**: **Shifted the capital from Faizabad to Lucknow in 1775**. Constructed the architectural marvels of Lucknow:
  - **Bara Imambara (Asafi Imambara)** (1784) with its celebrated **Bhul-bhulaiya** (labyrinth) and unsupported central arched hall (50m × 16m × 15m).
  - **Rumi Darwaza** (1784) and **Asafi Mosque**.
- **Ghazi-ud-Din Haidar (1814–1827)**: Formally assumed the title of **"King of Awadh" (Badshah)** in 1819 with British encouragement, severing formal ties with the Mughal Emperor.
- **Wajid Ali Shah (1847–1856)**: The 10th and last Nawab of Awadh. Great patron of Kathak dance, Thumri vocal music, and Urdu theatre (*Indar Sabha*); wrote under the pen-name *Akhtarpiya*.
- **Annexation of Awadh (13 February 1856)**: Lord Dalhousie annexed Awadh on grounds of **"alleged misgovernance and administrative misrule"** based on the biased report of British Resident **James Outram** (not under the Doctrine of Lapse). Wajid Ali Shah was exiled to Metiabruz (Kolkata).

### 2.3 Freedom Struggle and Revolutionary Movements in Uttar Pradesh

#### A. The Revolt of 1857 in Uttar Pradesh
- **Outbreak**: On **10 May 1857**, soldiers of the 3rd Native Cavalry at Meerut mutinied against greased cartridge orders, broke open prisons, killed British officers, and marched to Delhi.
- **Lucknow**:
  - Led by **Begum Hazrat Mahal** (wife of exiled Nawab Wajid Ali Shah).
  - Her minor son **Birjis Qadr** was proclaimed Nawab.
  - British Resident Sir Henry Lawrence was killed during the siege of Lucknow Residency. Recaptured by Sir Colin Campbell in March 1858.
- **Kanpur**:
  - Led by **Nana Sahib (Dhondu Pant)**, adopted son of last Peshwa Baji Rao II.
  - Assisted by military commander **Tantia Tope (Ramachandra Pandurang)** and political advisor **Azimullah Khan**.
- **Jhansi**:
  - Led by **Rani Lakshmibai (Manikarnika)** after Lord Dalhousie refused to recognize her adopted son Damodar Rao under the Doctrine of Lapse.
  - British commander Sir Hugh Rose remarked: *"Here lay the woman who was the only man among the rebels."*
- **Other UP Epicentres**:
  - **Faizabad**: Led by **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah** (known as the "Lighthouse of Rebellion" for organizing unified guerrilla warfare).
  - **Bareilly (Rohilkhand)**: Led by **Khan Bahadur Khan**.
  - **Prayagraj (Allahabad)**: Led by **Maulvi Liaquat Ali**.
  - **Mathura**: Led by Devi Singh; **Bijnor**: Mohammad Khan; **Banda**: Nawab Ali Bahadur; **Baraut (Baghpat)**: Shah Mal.

#### C. Peasant & Revolutionary Movements in UP
1. **UP Kisan Sabha (February 1918)**: Founded at Prayagraj by Gauri Shankar Mishra and Indra Narain Dwivedi, with the backing of Pt. Madan Mohan Malaviya.
2. **Oudh Kisan Sabha (October 1920)**: Formed at Pratapgarh by **Baba Ramchandra**, Jawaharlal Nehru, and Mata Badal Pande to fight exorbitant rents (*nazrana*) and illegal evictions (*bedakhli*).
3. **Eka Movement (1921–1922)**: Centred in Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur, and Barabanki under peasant leader **Madari Pasi**. Peasants took religious vows refusing to pay more than recorded rents.
4. **Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922)**:
   - Protesting peasants at Chauri Chaura market (Gorakhpur district) were fired upon by police.
   - Enraged mob surrounded and torched the police station, killing 22 policemen.
   - Mahatma Gandhi suspended the nationwide Non-Cooperation Movement on **12 February 1922** (Bardoli Resolution).
5. **Kakori Train Action (9 August 1925)**:
   - Planned and executed by the **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)** under Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri, Sachindra Bakshi, and Chandrashekhar Azad.
   - Revolutionaries held up the 8-Down passenger train at Kakori near Lucknow to seize British government railway funds for purchasing arms.
   - **Martyrdom of the 4 Leaders (December 1927)**:
     - **Pt. Ram Prasad Bismil**: Hanged at **Gorakhpur Jail** (Famous lines: *"Sarfaroshi ki tamanna ab hamare dil mein hai"*).
     - **Ashfaqulla Khan**: Hanged at **Faizabad (Ayodhya) Jail** (First Muslim revolutionary hanged for conspiracy against British rule).
     - **Thakur Roshan Singh**: Hanged at **Malaka Jail (Naini, Prayagraj)**.
     - **Rajendra Nath Lahiri**: Hanged at **Gonda Jail** (on 17 Dec 1927, two days ahead of schedule).
6. **Martyrdom of Chandrashekhar Azad (27 February 1931)**:
   - Commander-in-Chief of HSRA surrounded by British police at **Alfred Park (now Chandrashekhar Azad Park, Prayagraj)**; fought valiantly with a single pistol until shooting himself with the last bullet to uphold his vow never to be captured alive.
7. **Ballia Independent Government (August 1942)**:
   - During the Quit India Movement, **Chittu Pandey ("Sher-e-Ballia")** led a mass uprising, stormed the district jail, took control of the administration, and established the **First Parallel Government (Swaraj Sarkar)** in India for a week.

### 2.4 Classical Music, Gharanas, Folk Dances, Fairs and Handicrafts

#### A. The Gharana System of Uttar Pradesh
1. **Lucknow Gharana (Kathak & Tabla)**:
   - **Kathak**: Founded by Ishwari Prasad Mishra; patronized by Nawab Wajid Ali Shah. Epitomized by Pandit Birju Maharaj, Lachhu Maharaj, Shambhu Maharaj, Sitara Devi, and Damayanti Joshi.
   - **Tabla**: Founded by Modhu Khan and Bakshu Khan; characterized by the delicate use of the ring finger and *Meend* (pitch bending).
2. **Banaras Gharana (Music, Tabla & Dance)**:
   - **Shehnai**: Bharat Ratna **Ustad Bismillah Khan**.
   - **Tabla**: Pt. Kishan Maharaj, Pt. Samta Prasad (Gudai Maharaj), Pt. Anokhelal Mishra; known for vigorous *Banaras Baaj*.
   - **Thumri / Dadra / Chaiti / Kajri**: **Girija Devi** ("Queen of Thumri"), Rasoolan Bai, Badi Moti Bai, Siddheshwari Devi.
   - **Vocal Classical**: Pt. Rajan and Sajan Mishra (Dhrupad/Khayal).
3. **Kirana Gharana (Shamli / Muzaffarnagar)**:
   - Founded by **Ustad Abdul Karim Khan** and **Ustad Abdul Wahid Khan**.
   - Made internationally renowned by **Pandit Bhimsen Joshi** (Bharat Ratna), Gangubai Hangal, and Sawai Gandharva.
   - Known for its purity of notes (*Swara Shuddhi*) and slow development of Ragas.
4. **Agra Gharana (Rangeela Gharana)**:
   - Traced to Haji Sujan Khan (court musician of Akbar); rejuvenated by **Ustad Faiyaz Khan** (received the title *Aftab-e-Mousiqi* from Maharaja of Mysore).
   - Blend of vigorous Dhrupad-style Alap with lyrical Khayal.

#### B. Folk Music and Folk Dance Traditions of UP
- **Folk Dance Spectrum**:
  - **Charkula (Braj / Mathura)**: Iconic dance where women balance 108 lit earthen lamps on a multi-tiered wooden pyramid over their heads.
  - **Rai Dance (Bundelkhand)**: Performed by women of the Beriya community to the rhythm of Mridang; mimics the gait of a peacock.
  - **Karma Dance (Sonbhadra & Mirzapur)**: Ancient tribal group dance of Kharwar, Kol, and Baiga communities worshipping nature.
  - **Dhobiya Dance (Purvanchal)**: Performed by the washerman community on joyous occasions, depicting the relationship between a washerman and his donkey.
  - **Chholiya Dance**: Traditional martial sword dance performed in the Kumaon-bordering areas.
  - **Natwari Dance (Purvanchal)**: Performed by the Ahir/Yadav community during Janmashtami and Holi, tracing footsteps of Lord Krishna.
  - **Pai Danda (Bundelkhand)**: Stick dance mimicking the battles of Lord Krishna.
- **Folk Song Genres**:
  - **Alha (Bundelkhand)**: Heroic ballads sung in *Banaffari* dialect recounting the valour of Alha and Udal.
  - **Kajri (Mirzapur / Varanasi)**: Rainy season folk song celebrating the onset of monsoon and Radha-Krishna devotion.
  - **Birha (Purvanchal)**: Narrative songs depicting social struggles and mythological themes sung primarily by the Ahir community.
  - **Rasiya / Hori / Phag (Braj)**: Romantic, devotional songs celebrating Holi and Radha-Krishna in Brajbhasha.
  - **Sohar (Awadh / Purvanchal)**: Traditional celebratory songs sung upon the birth of a child.

#### C. Major Fairs & Festivals of Uttar Pradesh
- **Kumbh Mela & Magh Mela (Prayagraj)**:
  - Held at the holy Triveni Sangam (confluence of Ganga, Yamuna, and mythical Saraswati).
  - Maha Kumbh held every 12 years; Kumbh Mela inscribed on **UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2017)**.
- **Bateshwar Fair (Agra)**: Held at Bateshwar on the banks of Yamuna; sacred pilgrimage with 101 Shiva temples and Northern India's premier livestock/camel/cattle fair on Kartik Purnima.
- **Dadri Fair (Ballia)**: Second largest cattle fair in India, held annually in honour of Sage Bhrigu on Kartik Purnima.
- **Nauchandi Fair (Meerut)**: Celebrated after Holi; unique symbol of Hindu-Muslim harmony encompassing Navchandi Temple and Bale Miyan Dargah.
- **Dewa Sharif Fair (Barabanki)**: Held at the mausoleum of Sufi Saint Waris Ali Shah during Kartik month.
- **Shakumbhari Devi Fair (Saharanpur)**: Massive Navratri fair at the ancient Shakumbhari temple.
- **Govardhan Parikrama & Lathmar Holi (Mathura / Barsana)**: World-famous Holi festival where women of Barsana playfully strike men of Nandgaon with wooden sticks (*Lathis*).
- **Ramnagar Ramlila (Varanasi)**: Ancient 31-day open-air theatrical Ramlila patronized by Kashi Naresh; inscribed on **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage List (2008)**.
