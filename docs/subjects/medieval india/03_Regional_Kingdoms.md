## 3.1 Sharqi (Sharki) Kingdom — Jaunpur

**Period:** **1394–1484** | **Capital:** **Jaunpur** (eastern UP) | **Founder:** **Malik Sarwar** (Malik-us-Sharq)

- After Timur weakened Delhi (**1398**), **Malik Sarwar** declared independence at Jaunpur around **1394**.
- Core territory: **eastern UP** — Jaunpur, Ghazipur, Banaras belt; claimed reach from Aligarh to Darbhanga.
- Jaunpur was called **"Shiraz of the East"** for Persian literary patronage.
- Key rulers: Malik Sarwar → Mubarak Shah → **Shamsuddin Ibrahim Shah** (peak building) → Mahmud → Hussain Shah (last).
- **Sharqi architecture:** massive gateways, huge arches, bold stonework — **Atala Masjid**, **Lal Darwaza Masjid**, **Jami Masjid (Jaunpur)**.
- **Malik Muhammad Jaisi** composed **Padmavat** in the Jaunpur cultural milieu.
- **Bahlul Lodi** annexed Jaunpur in **1484** after prolonged Lodi–Sharqi struggle.

> **Exam note:** **Lal Darwaza–Jaunpur** is **correct** (2018 Q19). Trap: **Tin Darwaza–Ahmedabad** — Tin Darwaza is at **Bidar Fort**; Ahmedabad has **Teen Darwaza**.

## 3.3 Kashmir under Zain-ul-Abidin

**Reign:** **1420–1470** | **Title:** **Bud Shah** (Great Sultan)

- Predecessor **Sikandar Shah (1389–1413)** had forced conversions, temple destruction, and cow slaughter under **Suha Bhatt**.
- Zain-ul-Abidin **abolished jaziya**, banned cow slaughter, **restored temples**, and permitted reconversion.
- Appointed **Sriya Bhatt** as Minister of Justice and court physician.
- Patronised **Persian, Kashmiri, Sanskrit, Tibetan** learning; commissioned **Mahabharata** and **Rajatarangini** translations.
- Promoted **shawl weaving**, papermaking, and crafts; built dams, canals, and **Zaina Lanka** in **Wular Lake**.
- Defeated Ladakh Mongols; expanded influence over Baltistan, Jammu, Rajauri.

> **Exam note:** **UPPCS 2023 Q36** — who abolished **jaziya AND cow slaughter** → **Zain-ul-Abidin only**.

## 3.4 Vijayanagara Empire

**Founded:** **1336** | **Founders:** **Harihara I & Bukka I** (Sangama) | **Capital:** **Hampi** (Tungabhadra)

| Dynasty | Key rulers | Period |
|---------|------------|--------|
| **Sangama** | Harihara I, Bukka I, Deva Raya II | 1336–1485 |
| **Saluva** | Saluva Narasimha | 1485–1505 |
| **Tuluva** | **Krishnadevaraya**, Achyuta Raya | 1505–1570 |
| **Aravidu** | Tirumala, Venkata II | 1570–1646 |

- Founded in response to **Delhi Sultanate** expansion under **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** and Deccan instability.
- Chronic wars with **Bahmani Kingdom** over **Raichur doab** (Krishna–Tungabhadra).
- Peak under **Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529)**; decline accelerated under **Aliya Rama Raya** regency.
- **1565 Talikota:** Hampi sacked; **Aravidu** ruled diminished kingdom from **Penukonda/Chandragiri**.
- Foreign accounts: **Nuniz**, **Paes**, **Duarte Barbosa** describe Hampi's wealth.
- Monuments: **Vitthala Temple**, **Virupaksha**, **Hazara Rama** at Hampi.

> **Exam note:** Founders = **Harihara & Bukka**, not Krishnadevaraya.

## 3.5 Vijayanagara Administration

- King = **dharma protector** and commander-in-chief; cabinet of great officers (titles varied by period).
- **Nayankara/amara system:** king granted **amaram lands** to **nayakas** for **military service**, tribute, and administration — precursor to post-1565 **Nayak kingdoms** (Madurai, Tanjore).
- Provinces (**rajyas**) under governors; below: **nadu** clusters and villages with local assemblies.
- **Land revenue** = main income; temple lands (**devadana**), **agrahara**, peasant holdings.
- Army: **elephants, cavalry, infantry, artillery** (16th c.); Portuguese supplied horses and firearms.
- **Mahanavami/Dasara** = royal festival displaying power, tribute, and military might.
- Temple grants recorded on **mandapa pillars** at Virupaksha and Vitthala.

> **Exam note:** **Nayankara** = Vijayanagara — not Delhi **iqta** or Mughal **jagir** (similar logic, different polity).

## 3.6 Bahmani Kingdom

**Founded:** **1347** | **Founder:** **Hasan Gangu** (throne name **Alauddin Bahman Shah**)

- Hasan Gangu rebelled against **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**; declared independence at **Gulbarga**.
- Early capital **Gulbarga**; **Ahmad Shah I Wali** shifted to **Bidar (~1429)**.
- Kingdom divided into **eight tarafs** (provinces) under **tarafdars**.
- Rival of **Vijayanagara** over **Raichur doab** — war and marriage alliances alternated.
- **Mahmud Gawan** (Persian wazir under Muhammad Shah III) expanded to Orissa coast; introduced land surveys and strict accounting.
- Gawan executed **1481** after forged treason letter — administrative peak ended.
- **Deccani vs Afaqi** noble factions paralysed court.
- Last sultan **Kalimullah**; kingdom split **1518** into five Deccan Sultanates.

> **Exam note:** **Hasan Gangu** founded Bahmani — **Mahmud Gawan** was minister, not founder.

## 3.7 Deccan Sultanates

**Five successor states (from ~1518):**

| Sultanate | Dynasty | Capital | Lock |
|-----------|---------|---------|------|
| **Bijapur** | Adil Shahi | Bijapur | **Gol Gumbaz**; **Ibrahim Adil Shah II** — **Kitab-i-Nauras** |
| **Golkonda** | Qutb Shahi | Golkonda → Hyderabad | **Charminar (1591)**; diamond trade |
| **Ahmadnagar** | Nizam Shahi | Ahmadnagar | **Burhan-e-Masir** chronicle |
| **Bidar** | Barid Shahi | Bidar | **Tin Darwaza** (Bidar Fort) |
| **Berar** | Imad Shahi | Ellichpur | Smallest; absorbed early |

- **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** founded **Hyderabad (1591)** with **Charminar**.
- Four sultanates (**Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golkonda, Bidar**) united against Vijayanagara at **Talikota (1565)**.
- Mughal annexations: Ahmadnagar **1636**, Bijapur **1686**, Golkonda **1687** (**Abul Hasan Qutb Shah** last ruler).

> **Exam note:** **Kitab-i-Nauras = Ibrahim Adil Shah II (Bijapur)** — not Krishnadevaraya (2020 Q44).

## 3.8 Krishnadevaraya

**Reign:** **1509–1529** | **Dynasty:** **Tuluva** | **Capital:** **Hampi**

- **Battle of Raichur (1520):** defeated **Ismail Adil Shah** of Bijapur; captured Raichur fort — military peak.
- Defeated **Prataparudra** of Orissa/Gajapati; secured Krishna boundary.
- Used **Portuguese** contacts for horses and guns.
- Authored **Amuktamalyada** (Telugu epic); patronised **Ashtadiggajas** (eight great Telugu poets).
- **Allasani Peddana** (Manucharitramu) and **Tenali Ramakrishna** at his court.
- Personal **Vaishnava** devotion but patronised **Shaiva** temples too.
- Died **1529**; brother **Achyuta Raya** followed; later **Rama Raya** regency led to Talikota.

> **Exam note:** **Amuktamalyada** = Krishnadevaraya (Telugu). **Kitab-i-Nauras** = Ibrahim Adil Shah II (Bijapur).

---

## 3.9 Battle of Talikota (1565)

**Date:** **23 January 1565** | **Site:** **Rakkasa-Tangadi** (near Talikota)

- **Aliya Rama Raya** (regent) meddled in Deccan Sultanate successions and alienated all four major states.
- Coalition: **Bijapur (Ali Adil Shah I)**, **Ahmadnagar (Hussain Nizam Shah I)**, **Golkonda (Ibrahim Qutb Shah)**, **Bidar (Ali Barid)**.
- **Rama Raya** captured and **beheaded** on battlefield — Vijayanagara army collapsed.
- **Hampi** looted for ~six months; capital abandoned.
- **Aravidu** dynasty retreated to **Penukonda/Chandragiri**; **nayakas** became independent (Madurai, Tanjore, Gingee).
- End of Vijayanagara as a **great power** — not same as empire's nominal survival till 1646.

> **Exam note:** **Talikota 1565** ≠ **Raichur 1520** (Krishnadevaraya's victory). **Rama Raya** killed, not Krishnadevaraya (d. 1529).

## 3.10 Bahmani Administration

| Office | Function |
|--------|----------|
| **Wazir** | Prime minister — **Mahmud Gawan** held this |
| **Mustaufi** | Finance/accounts |
| **Mir Jumla** | Foreign trade/customs |
| **Tarafdar** | Provincial governor of **taraf** |

- **Eight tarafs** = provincial divisions; weak tarafdars later became breakaway sultanates.
- **Persianate centralised** bureaucracy adapted from Delhi Sultanate.
- **Mahmud Gawan's reforms:** land measurement, strict accounting, cash salaries, merit appointments.
- **Riyaz-ul-Insha** = collection of **Mahmud Gawan's letters/state papers**.
- **Riyaz-us-Salatin** = history of **Bengal** — NOT Gawan (2023 Q33 trap).
- **Burhan-e-Masir** = history of **Ahmadnagar** (Bahmani successor).
- **Mirat-e-Sikandari** = **Gujarat** victory narrative.

> **Exam note:** **2023 Q33** → Mirat-e-Sikandari(4), Burhan-e-Masir(2), Riyaz-us-Salatin(1), Riyaz-ul-Insha(3) = **A (4-2-1-3)**.

## Common Traps — Don't Fall For These

1. **Tin Darwaza–Ahmedabad is wrong** — Tin Darwaza = **Bidar**; Ahmedabad = Teen Darwaza.
2. **Lal Darwaza–Jaunpur is correct** — do not mark it wrong in NOT-matched lists.
3. **Riyaz-us-Salatin ≠ Riyaz-ul-Insha** — Bengal vs Gawan's letters (2023 Q33).
4. **Kitab-i-Nauras ≠ Amuktamalyada** — Ibrahim Adil Shah II vs Krishnadevaraya.
5. **Krishnadevaraya ≠ Vijayanagara founder** — Harihara & Bukka founded it.
6. **Talikota ≠ Raichur** — 1565 defeat vs 1520 Krishnadevaraya victory.
7. **Rama Raya died at Talikota** — Krishnadevaraya died **1529**.
8. **Mahmud Gawan ≠ Bahmani founder** — Hasan Gangu founded Bahmani.
9. **Bijapur ≠ Bidar** — different Deccan centres; Gol Gumbaz at Bijapur.
10. **Bahlul Lodi annexed Jaunpur (1484)** — not Sikandar Lodi.
11. **Gol Gumbaz is at Bijapur** — not Hampi or Bidar.
12. **Nayankara = Vijayanagara** — not Bahmani taraf system.
13. **Jaunpur is in UP** — only Topic-3 capital inside Uttar Pradesh.
14. **Five Deccan Sultanates** — not Jaunpur (Sharqi was annexed earlier).
15. **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah** — last Golkonda ruler when Mughals took 1687.
