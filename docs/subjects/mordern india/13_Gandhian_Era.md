## 13.1 Overview & Indian National Movement Chronology (Gandhian Phase)

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Gandhian Era** | Roughly **1915–1948**, when Gandhi's leadership, satyagraha, and constructive programme shaped mass nationalism |
| **Satyagraha** | Non-violent resistance based on truth and willingness to suffer, not hatred of the opponent |
| **Mass nationalism** | Politics that mobilises peasants, workers, students, and women beyond elite constitutionalism |

### Causes — Why the Gandhian Phase Matters

1. Gandhi returned from South Africa with tested satyagraha methods and *Hind Swaraj* ideas.
2. World War I, Rowlatt, and Jallianwala destroyed faith in British justice for millions.
3. Home Rule and Extremism had prepared the ground; Gandhi supplied a mass moral method.
4. Hindu–Muslim cooperation around Khilafat briefly widened the national front.
5. Chronology questions dominate UPPCS — year order is as important as narrative.

### Overview — How It Works

- Topic 13 is a **chronology + event** paper: learn each satyagraha/pact as Causes → Course → Results, then drill year chains.
- Early local satyagrahas (**1917–18**) build Gandhi's Indian credibility before all-India leadership.
- **1919** is the moral rupture year (Rowlatt + Jallianwala).
- **1920–22** is NCM–Khilafat; **Chauri Chaura** is the withdrawal trap.
- **1923–29** covers Swarajists, Simon, Nehru Report, and Lahore Purna Swaraj.
- **1930–34** covers Dandi/CDM, RTCs, Gandhi–Irwin, Communal Award, Poona Pact.
- Quit India / INA / Cabinet Mission belong mainly to Topic 14 — mention only for chronology edges.
- Trap: "Gandhian Era begins with Quit India." It begins with return/**1915** and early satyagrahas.
- Trap: "Purna Swaraj declared in 1928 Nehru Report." Nehru Report = dominion draft; Purna Swaraj = **Lahore 1929**.
- Trap: "Communal Award = 1933." It is **16 August 1932**.

## 13.2 Gandhi in South Africa

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Gandhi in South Africa (1893–1914)** | Period when Gandhi developed satyagraha against racial discrimination |
| **Phoenix / Tolstoy Farm** | Ashram experiments linked to simple living and satyagraha training |
| **Hind Swaraj (1909)** | Gandhi's critique of modern civilisation and statement of swaraj ethics |

### Causes

1. Racial humiliation (including the Pietermaritzburg incident memory) pushed Gandhi into public defence of Indian rights.
2. Discriminatory laws on registration, immigration, and taxes targeted Indians in Natal/Transvaal.
3. Indian traders and indentured labourers needed an organised voice.
4. Gandhi's legal training and Gujarati community links gave him an organisational base.
5. Exposure to Tolstoy, Ruskin, and Thoreau shaped non-violent method.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Gandhi arrives in South Africa as a lawyer and faces racial discrimination.
2. He organises Indian opinion through petitions, the Natal Indian Congress stream, and the paper *Indian Opinion*.
3. He experiments with community living at Phoenix Settlement and later Tolstoy Farm.
4. Satyagraha campaigns challenge registration certificates, immigration curbs, and related disabilities.
5. In **1909** he writes **Hind Swaraj**, outlining civilisational critique and true swaraj.
6. Prolonged struggle ends with a negotiated settlement under Smuts–Gandhi understandings.
7. Gandhi leaves South Africa (**1914**) and returns to India in **1915**.
8. South African methods become the template for Champaran–Kheda–Ahmedabad and later all-India satyagraha.

### Gandhi in South Africa — How It Works

- South Africa is where **satyagraha** is invented in practice, not merely theorised in India.
- *Hind Swaraj* is the ideological seed-text of the Gandhian Era (full book matching also appears in Topic 10).
- Phoenix/Tolstoy farms link constructive living to political struggle — a preview of constructive programme.
- Gandhi returns as an Empire-loyal reformer in tone, then radicalises after Rowlatt–Jallianwala.
- Trap: "Satyagraha begins only at Dandi 1930." Method begins in **South Africa**.
- Trap: "Hind Swaraj written after Champaran." It is **1909**, before return.
- Trap: "Gandhi returned in 1919." Return is **1915**.
- Trap: "South Africa struggle was only about indenture abolition in one year." It was a long multi-issue campaign.

> **Exam note:** Pair **South Africa → satyagraha method** and **Hind Swaraj 1909 → ideology**.

## 13.3 Gandhian Ideology

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Gandhian Ideology** | Ethical-political programme of truth, non-violence, swaraj, swadeshi, and trusteeship |
| **Ahimsa** | Non-violence as active love/force, not passive cowardice |
| **Swaraj** | Self-rule of individual and nation; moral self-discipline plus political freedom |
| **Sarvodaya** | Welfare of all, especially the weakest |

### Gandhian Ideology — How It Works

- Core triad: **Truth (Satya)**, **Non-violence (Ahimsa)**, and **Satyagraha** as method.
- Swaraj is both political independence and self-control; *Hind Swaraj* attacks blind modern industrialism.
- Swadeshi and boycott link ethics to economics — use Indian goods, strengthen village industries.
- Trusteeship asks wealth-holders to act as trustees for society rather than absolute owners.
- Ends-and-means unity: impure means corrupt goals — key A/R theme.
- Religion in politics for Gandhi meant moral conscience, not theocratic rule.
- Mass contact required simple living, vernacular speech, and symbols (khadi, salt).
- Trap: "Gandhi rejected all modern tools always." He used trains, press, and law strategically while criticising modern civilisation's ethics.
- Trap: "Ahimsa = doing nothing." Satyagraha is active non-violent resistance.
- Trap: "Swaraj = only parliamentary seats." For Gandhi it includes village and personal swaraj.

> **Exam note:** Ideology questions often hide inside *Hind Swaraj*, khadi, trusteeship, and means–ends statements.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- Text: *Hind Swaraj* **1909**
- Method: Satyagraha
- Symbols: khadi, charkha, salt
- Ideal: Sarvodaya / trusteeship
- Means = ends ethic

### PYQs — Ideology

1. **(UPSC / standard pattern)** *Hind Swaraj* is associated with → **M.K. Gandhi / 1909**.

2. **(Standard A/R pattern)** Gandhi insisted means must be pure because impure means corrupt ends → classic ideology reason.

### Definitions

| **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)** | Indigo cultivators' struggle in Bihar against tinkathia-type planter compulsion |
| **Kheda Satyagraha (1918)** | Gujarat peasant struggle for revenue remission after crop failure |
| **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)** | Mill-workers' wage struggle; Gandhi's fast as pressure tool |

### Master Overview Table

| Satyagraha | Year | Region | Core issue | Key aide memory |
|------------|------|--------|------------|-----------------|
| Champaran | **1917** | Bihar | Indigo / planter oppression | Raj Kumar Shukla |
| Kheda | **1918** | Gujarat | Land revenue remission | Local peasant leaders; Patel rises |
| Ahmedabad | **1918** | Gujarat | Plague-bonus / wages | Anasuya Sarabhai |

### 13.4.1 Champaran Satyagraha (1917)

#### Causes

1. European planters forced peasants into indigo under oppressive tinkathia-like arrangements.
2. Falling indigo demand and planter exactions left cultivators indebted and coerced.
3. Local appeals reached Gandhi through **Raj Kumar Shukla**.
4. Gandhi sought a verified inquiry rather than remote petition politics.
5. Success here would prove satyagraha's Indian applicability after South Africa.

#### Course — Step by Step

1. Gandhi arrives in Champaran after Shukla's persistence.
2. He begins recording peasant statements despite official obstruction.
3. Authorities order him to leave; he refuses and courts arrest — moral victory.
4. Government appoints an inquiry; Gandhi joins the process.
5. Evidence of planter abuses mounts through village documentation.
6. Settlement reduces planter compulsion and eases peasant burdens.
7. Gandhi's aides and local volunteers spread the method of non-violent inquiry.
8. Champaran becomes Gandhi's first major Indian satyagraha success.

#### Champaran — How It Works

- Champaran is the **first** major Indian satyagraha — year lock **1917**.
- **Tinkathia** = peasants forced to grow indigo on **3/20** of their holdings — classic definition trap.
- Issue is **indigo/planter**, not salt or Rowlatt.
- Raj Kumar Shukla is the peasant contact tag; aides include **Rajendra Prasad, J.B. Kripalani, Mazharul Haq, Brajkishore Prasad**.
- Method mix: investigation + readiness for jail + negotiated settlement; tinkathia pressures ease after inquiry.
- Trap: "Champaran = 1918 with Kheda." Champaran is **1917**.
- Trap: "Tinkathia means only a cash fine of three kathas." It is forced **indigo acreage (3/20)**.
- Trap: "Champaran = Ahmedabad mill strike." Different class and issue.
- Trap: "Champaran started Quit India." Totally different decade.

> **Exam note:** Champaran = **1917 + tinkathia (3/20) + indigo + Bihar + Shukla**.

#### Results

| Result | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|

## 13.13 Civil Disobedience Movement & Dandi March

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34)** | Mass defiance of selected colonial laws, launched with the Salt Satyagraha |
| **Dandi March (12 Mar–6 Apr 1930)** | Gandhi's salt march from Sabarmati to Dandi |
| **Dharasana Satyagraha** | Later salt-works raid; reported by **Web Miller** |

### Causes

1. Purna Swaraj required a mass programme beyond resolutions.
2. Salt tax symbolised unjust colonial monopoly affecting the poor.
3. Gandhi chose a simple illegal act everyone could understand and copy.
4. Failure of dominion negotiations/Irwin vagueness pushed confrontation.
5. Congress organisational machinery was ready after Simon–Lahore mobilisation.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Gandhi sends demands to Irwin; unmet, he plans salt satyagraha.
2. On **12 March 1930**, Dandi March begins from Sabarmati with chosen volunteers.
3. On **6 April 1930**, Gandhi makes salt at **Dandi**, breaking the salt law.
4. Nationwide salt marches, forest-law breaches, no-tax strains, and boycotts spread.
5. Government represses with arrests; movement still expands socially (including women).
6. **Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 Mar 1931)** suspends CDM for RTC participation.
7. After 2nd RTC failure, CDM resumes; repression intensifies.
8. Movement is finally withdrawn around **April/May 1934**.

### 13.13.1 Dandi March — Focus Block

1. Salt is a universal necessity; tax hits even the poorest.
2. Illegal salt-making is an easy-to-copy civil breach.
3. March creates a moving public drama across Gujarat villages.
4. Gandhi needs a moral issue broader than elite constitutional debate.
5. Launch must follow Purna Swaraj's Independence Day pledge momentum.

#### Course — Step by Step

1. Route planned from Sabarmati Ashram toward coastal Dandi.
2. March begins **12 March 1930** with disciplined volunteers.
3. Village meetings spread khadi, temperance, and anti-untouchability messages.
4. Press coverage nationalises the march daily.
5. Arrival at Dandi and salt-making on **6 April 1930** inaugurate mass CDM.
6. Parallel salt actions erupt on coasts and inland symbolic breaches follow.
7. Arrests begin; movement continues under Congress/local leadership.
8. Dharasana later shows brutal police response reported by **Web Miller**.

1. RTC communal deadlock left British to impose a settlement.

## Common Traps — Don't Fall For These

1. **Trap:** Communal Award in 1933. **Correct:** **16 August 1932** — **2019 Q19**.

2. **Trap:** Poona Pact before Gandhi–Irwin Pact. **Correct:** Irwin **Mar 1931**; Poona **Sep 1932** — **2025 Q7**.

3. **Trap:** End of CDM before Poona Pact. **Correct:** Poona 1932; CDM ends **1934**.

4. **Trap:** Tagore resigned from Viceroy's Council; Nair returned knighthood. **Correct:** **Tagore = knighthood**; **Nair = resignation**.

5. **Trap:** Many Councillors resigned over Jallianwala (Shafi and others). **Correct:** **Only Sankaran Nair** — **2025 Q135**.

6. **Trap:** Rajendra Prasad attended 2nd RTC. **Correct:** He did **not** — **2020 Q24**.

7. **Trap:** Nehru Report demanded Purna Swaraj. **Correct:** **Dominion Status (1928)**; Purna Swaraj = Lahore **1929**.

8. **Trap:** Nehru Report chaired by Jawaharlal. **Correct:** **Motilal Nehru**.

9. **Trap:** Dandi began on 26 January 1930. **Correct:** 26 Jan = Independence Day observance; Dandi starts **12 March**.

10. **Trap:** Web Miller reported Chauri Chaura. **Correct:** **Dharasana** — **2021 Q82**.

11. **Trap:** Chauri Chaura is outside UP. **Correct:** **Gorakhpur, UP**.

12. **Trap:** NCM = CDM. **Correct:** NCM 1920–22 boycott; CDM 1930–34 law-breaking (salt).

13. **Trap:** Champaran = 1918 with Kheda. **Correct:** Champaran **1917**.

14. **Trap:** Simon Commission had Indian members. **Correct:** **All-white**.

15. **Trap:** Congress attended all RTCs. **Correct:** Gandhi at **2nd** as sole Congress rep; absent in 1st/3rd.

16. **Trap:** Swaraj Party founded in 1930. **Correct:** **1923** (Das–Motilal).

17. **Trap:** Khilafat = Pakistan movement. **Correct:** Ottoman Caliphate defence.

18. **Trap:** Hind Swaraj written after Champaran. **Correct:** **1909** in South Africa period.

19. **Trap:** Gandhi–Irwin Pact = Poona Pact. **Correct:** Different years, issues, counterparts.

20. **Trap:** First Indian satyagraha = Dandi. **Correct:** First major Indian = **Champaran 1917**; method from South Africa.

21. **Trap:** Karachi 1931 = Purna Swaraj session. **Correct:** Purna Swaraj = **Lahore 1929**; Karachi = **Fundamental Rights + Economic Programme (Patel)**.

22. **Trap:** Tinkathia = random indigo rumour. **Correct:** Forced indigo on **3/20** of land.

23. **Trap:** Poona Pact kept separate electorates and only added a few seats. **Correct:** Separate electorates dropped; reserved seats **71 → 148** in joint electorates.

24. **Trap:** Gandhi–Irwin Pact gave immediate Dominion Status. **Correct:** Truce + RTC seat + limited salt/prisoner relief — **not** dominion.

25. **Trap:** Vedaranyam salt march = Gandhi personally. **Correct:** **C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji)** in Tamil Nadu.

26. **Trap:** Vaikom Satyagraha unrelated to Gandhi. **Correct:** Gandhi visited/participated (UPPCS **2021 Both**).

27. **Trap:** Belgaum 1924 President was Nehru. **Correct:** **Gandhi** (his only Congress Presidentship).

28. **Trap:** 11 Points were accepted by Irwin so Dandi was unnecessary. **Correct:** Points ignored → Dandi/CDM launched.
