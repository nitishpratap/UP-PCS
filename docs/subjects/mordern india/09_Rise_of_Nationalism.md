## 9.1 Rise of Nationalism — Overview

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Indian nationalism (early phase)** | Political consciousness seeking Indian rights, representation, and later self-rule under/against colonial state |
| **Political association** | Organised body for petitions, meetings, and public opinion before mass satyagraha politics |
| **Economic nationalism** | Critique of colonial economy (drain, deindustrialisation, poverty) as politics |

### Causes — Why Nationalism Rose

1. Modern education and English created a shared public sphere across regions.
2. Press, railways, and unified administration made all-India communication possible.
3. Racial discrimination in jobs, courts, and clubs humiliated the educated class.
4. Economic drain, famines, and deindustrialisation supplied material grievances.
5. Lord Lytton’s reactionary measures and Ilbert Bill controversy sharpened political anger.
6. Pre-Congress associations trained Indians in organised constitutional politics.

### Rise of Nationalism — How It Works

- Early nationalism was first a **city-educated** movement of lawyers, journalists, and teachers, not yet a peasant mass movement.
- It grew through **associations → Congress → Moderate–Extremist debate → mass movements** (later topics).
- Economic critique (Naoroji, Ranade, R.C. Dutt) turned poverty into a political charge against empire.
- Political method initially meant petitions, memorials, speeches, and the British public opinion strategy.
- Extremists later argued that petitions without pressure were futile; Swadeshi and boycott became weapons.
- UPPCS tests foundations: **who founded which body**, **who never became president**, **book years**, and **Moderate ideology A/R**.
- Trap: "Nationalism began only in 1920 with Gandhi." Organised nationalism is nineteenth-century.
- Trap: "INC was the first Indian political body." Many associations preceded 1885.
- Trap: "All early nationalists wanted immediate complete independence in 1885." Early Congress demanded reforms within empire.
- Trap: "Tilak was the first Congress President." He never presided.

> **Exam note:** Build triangles: **association↔founder↔year** and **leader↔role↔trap**.

### Results

| Result | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| All-India political platform | INC after 1885 |
| Economic critique tradition | Drain theory politics |
| Moderate–Extremist split/reunion | Method debate inside nationalism |
| Trained leadership | Later mass movements inherited cadres |

### Exam Facts (raata)

- INC: **1885 Bombay**

## 9.2 Political Organisations before INC

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **East India Association (1866)** | London body by Dadabhai Naoroji to inform British opinion on India |
| **National Indian Association (1870)** | Britain-based association associated with Mary Carpenter for India-related social/educational links |
| **Indian Society (1872)** | London Indian students’ society (Ananda Mohan Bose linked in exam memory) |
| **Indian Association (1876)** | Calcutta political body by Surendranath Banerjee and Ananda Mohan Bose |
| **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870)** | Deccan public association for local grievances and representation |
| **Madras Mahajan Sabha (1884)** | Madras Presidency political association |
| **Bombay Presidency Association (1885)** | Bombay elite political association (Mehta, Telang, Tyabji circle) |

### Causes

1. Indians needed organised channels to reach British officials and British public opinion.
2. Regional educated elites wanted bodies for civic rights, jobs, and local grievances.
3. London became a lobbying theatre because Parliament sat there.
4. Calcutta, Bombay, Madras, and Poona each built presidency/local platforms.
5. Failure of purely local petitions pushed leaders toward all-India coordination.

### Course — Step by Step

1. **1866:** Dadabhai Naoroji founds **East India Association** in London.
2. **1870:** **National Indian Association** is established in Britain (Mary Carpenter).
3. **1870:** **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** organises Deccan public politics.
4. **1872:** **Indian Society** forms among Indian students in London.
5. **1876:** Surendranath Banerjee and Ananda Mohan Bose found **Indian Association** in Calcutta — the strongest pre-Congress political body in Bengal.
6. Banerjee’s **Indian National Conference** (1883, 1885) experiments with all-India meetings.
7. **1884:** **Madras Mahajan Sabha** is founded.
8. **1885:** **Bombay Presidency Association** appears in the same founding season as INC.

### Pre-INC Organisations — How It Works

- Memorise the **2024 four-item order** first: East India Association → National Indian Association → Indian Society → Indian Association.
- Then add the syllabus triad of presidency bodies: Poona Sarvajanik Sabha, Madras Mahajan Sabha, Bombay Presidency Association.
- Indian Association mattered because it ran political campaigns on civil services age, local rights, and national conferences.
- East India Association mattered because it placed the Indian case inside Britain.
- These bodies were schools of constitutional politics that made the 1885 Congress possible.
- Trap: "Indian Association founded in 1866." That year is East India Association.
- Trap: "East India Association founded by Surendranath Banerjee." Founder = **Naoroji**.
- Trap: "Bombay Presidency Association long before INC." It is **1885**, same year zone as Congress.
- Trap: "No serious politics existed before 1885." Pre-INC associations were active for two decades.

> **Exam note:** **2024 Q15 = 1,2,3,4**; pair each body with founder and city.

## 9.3 Establishment of the Indian National Congress

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Indian National Congress (INC)** | All-India political organisation founded in **1885** |
| **A.O. Hume** | Retired British ICS officer who organised the first Congress |
| **W.C. Bonnerjee (Banerjee)** | First President of INC (Bombay session 1885) |
| **Safety Valve Theory** | View that Hume founded Congress to safely vent Indian discontent |

### Causes

1. Pre-Congress associations proved Indians could organise politically but lacked one national body.
2. Educated Indians wanted a yearly all-India forum for demands and networking.
3. Hume and liberal British friends feared unrest without a constitutional outlet.
4. Indian leaders wanted peaceful pressure for civil service, councils, and economic justice.
5. The timing after Lytton-era reaction and Ilbert controversy favoured a national platform.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Hume circulates the idea of a national conference of educated Indians.
2. On **28 December 1885**, the first session meets at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay.
3. **W.C. Bonnerjee** becomes the first President; about 72 delegates attend.
4. Early Congress demands include Indianisation of services, expansion of councils, and reduction of military expenditure.
5. Sessions rotate among cities, creating an all-India ritual of politics.
6. Moderates dominate the first two decades.
7. Extremists rise after Partition of Bengal / Swadeshi (Topic 11 detail).
8. Surat Split **1907** and Lucknow reunion **1916** reshape Congress politics.

### Establishment of INC — How It Works

- Congress began as an **elite constitutional body**, not a mass satyagraha machine.
- Hume’s role is real; Indian political readiness from pre-INC associations is equally real. Do not reduce 1885 to only a British conspiracy or only a sudden Indian invention.
- **Safety Valve Theory** is an interpretation, not the only reading. Nationalist historians stress Indian initiative using Hume’s organisational help.
- First-session facts: **1885, Bombay, W.C. Bonnerjee**.
- Early policy = reform within empire + economic critique, not Quit India.
- Trap: "Dadabhai Naoroji was the first Congress President." First = **W.C. Bonnerjee**; Naoroji was President later (and thrice).
- Trap: "INC founded in Calcutta 1885." First session is **Bombay**.
- Trap: "Hume was the first President." Hume was organiser; President was Bonnerjee.
- Trap: "Congress demanded complete independence in 1885." That demand crystallises much later (1929 Lahore etc.).

> **Exam note:** **1885 Bombay + Bonnerjee + Hume organiser**; Safety Valve = debated theory.

### Results

| Result | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| National political centre | All-India leadership stage |
| Annual sessions culture | Continuity of nationalist debate |
| Moderate programme base | Petitions and public opinion politics |
| Later mass inheritance | Gandhi era inherits Congress name/structure |

| **W.C. Bonnerjee** | First President |

## 9.5 Early Congress Policies

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Constitutional agitation** | Politics through petitions, meetings, press, and council work |
| **3 P’s of Moderates** | Petition, Prayer, Protest (classic schoolbook shorthand) |
| **Economic critique** | Demand to change colonial fiscal/commercial policy |

### Early Congress Policies — How It Works

- Early Congress asked for **Indianisation of civil services**, simultaneous ICS exams, and higher age limits.
- It demanded expansion of legislative councils and more Indian representation.
- It criticised military spending, land revenue harshness, and drain of wealth.
- It supported civil liberties and opposed racial discrimination in justice and jobs.
- Method was loyal criticism: expose "Un-British" wrongs while valuing British political ideals.
- Extremists later said policy without sanctions (boycott/Swadeshi) was toothless.
- Trap: "Early Congress had no economic programme." Drain and poverty were central.
- Trap: "Early Congress = revolutionary terrorism." Wrong stream.
- Trap: "Early Congress rejected all council entry." Council reform was a Moderate demand.
- Trap: "Policy stayed unchanged till 1947." Methods and goals radicalised after Swadeshi/Gandhi eras.

> **Exam note:** Early policy = **councils + services + economic justice + constitutional methods**.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- ICS Indianisation demand
- Council expansion
- Cut military expenditure
- Drain/poverty critique
- Constitutional methods first

## 9.6 Moderates (Naram Dal)

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Moderates** | Early Congress leaders believing in gradual reform through constitutional means |
| **Providence / modernisation view** | Belief that British rule could modernise India if conducted justly |
| **Un-British rule** | Phrase for colonial practices that violated British liberal principles |

### Causes

1. Faith in British liberalism and the power of public opinion in Britain.
2. Social base in professional middle classes comfortable with petitions and law.
3. Fear that violent politics would invite repression and destroy hard-won forums.
4. Success of Victorian reform language in earlier campaigns.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Moderates dominate Congress from 1885 into the early 1900s.
2. Leaders include Dadabhai Naoroji, Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee (early phase), and others.
3. They document drain, poverty, and administrative injustice in books and speeches.
4. They lobby British liberals and use Congress sessions as annual pressure points.
5. After Partition of Bengal, Extremists challenge Moderate leadership and methods.
6. Surat Split **1907** formally divides the camps.
7. Moderates continue constitutional work; Extremists push Swadeshi politics.
8. Lucknow **1916** reunites Congress streams under wartime politics.

### Moderates — How It Works

- Moderates did **not** love every British act. They often saw British connection as potentially modernising (**providence** idea) while attacking exploitation as betrayal of British ideals (**Un-British rule**).
- **2024 Q135:** Assertion about providence/modernisation is **true**. Reason about complaining against Un-British rule is also a standard true characterisation, but it does **not correctly explain** why they saw rule as providential modernisation. Answer: **Both true; R is not the correct explanation of A**.
- Their method: evidence, petition, publicity — not mass boycott first.
- Gokhale is the classic Moderate: Servants of India Society, Banaras President 1905, mentor image opposite Tilak.
- Trap: "Moderates wanted immediate violent overthrow." False.
- Trap: "Moderates never criticised economic drain." They pioneered that critique.
- Trap: "A and R mean R explains A in 2024." Key is **R does not explain A**.
- Trap: "Un-British rule means they hated Britain’s existence in India totally." It means they wanted British principles applied truly.

> **Exam note:** Master the **2024 Moderates A/R** wording exactly.

## 9.7 Extremists (Garam Dal)

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Extremists** | Nationalists demanding stronger pressure — Swadeshi, boycott, passive resistance, Swaraj emphasis |
| **Lal–Bal–Pal** | Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal |
| **Surat Split (1907)** | Open rupture between Moderates and Extremists |

### Causes

1. Moderate petitions seemed too slow against Curzon’s Partition and repression.
2. Swadeshi enthusiasm needed militant leadership.
3. Cultural-religious mobilisation (festivals, vernacular press) widened the base beyond elites.
4. Leaders like Tilak argued Swaraj required fearless assertion, not only memorials.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Extremist politics crystallises around Swadeshi (detail in Topic 11).
2. Lal–Bal–Pal become the popular triad of assertive nationalism.
3. Conflict over Congress presidency and methods peaks at **Surat 1907**.
4. Extremists face arrests, deportations, and press prosecutions.
5. Tilak’s long imprisonment weakens open Extremist organisation for a time.
6. Home Rule leagues (Tilak, Besant) revive assertive politics around **1916**.
7. Lucknow session reunites Congress; Extremists re-enter the main stream.
8. Later Gandhi mass politics transforms the method debate again.

### Extremists — How It Works

- Extremists were not all bomb revolutionaries. Many were **political Extremists** using boycott/Swadeshi/passive resistance inside public politics.
- Revolutionary terrorism is a related but distinct stream (Topic 11).
- Compared with Moderates, Extremists trusted Indian popular energy more than British goodwill.
- Surat Split is the organisational symbol of the quarrel; Lucknow 1916 is the repair.
- Trap: "Extremists = only HSRA revolutionaries." HSRA is later revolutionary; Extremists are early-20th-century Congress radicals.
- Trap: "Tilak supported Age of Consent Act." He **opposed** it.
- Trap: "Extremists rejected Swaraj." They popularised assertive Swaraj language.
- Trap: "Surat Split was Congress vs Muslim League." It was **Moderate vs Extremist**.

> **Exam note:** Extremists = **Lal–Bal–Pal + Surat 1907 + assertive methods**.

## 9.8 Bal Gangadhar Tilak

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856–1920)** | Extremist nationalist leader of Maharashtra; called **Lokmanya** |
| **Kesari / Maratha** | His newspapers (Marathi / English) |
| **Home Rule League (Tilak)** | 1916 league demanding self-government |

### Causes of Tilak’s Politics

1. He believed Moderate petitions could not awaken national will.
2. He used Ganapati and Shivaji festivals to mobilise popular culture into politics.
3. Press prosecutions and arrests radicalised his confrontation with the Raj.
4. He wanted Swaraj as a clear political right, not a distant reward for loyalty.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Tilak builds Kesari/Maratha as militant public opinion tools from Pune.
2. He opposes the **Age of Consent Act (1891)** as colonial interference in Hindu society (while Malabari supports it).
3. He becomes a pillar of Extremist politics with Lajpat Rai and Bipin Pal.
4. After Surat Split he remains the Maharashtra Extremist magnet.
5. Imprisoned (including Mandalay years), he returns to politics later.
6. In **1916** he starts Home Rule League work alongside Annie Besant’s league.
7. He declares the famous line associated with him: **“Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it.”**
8. He dies in **1920**, still without ever having presidied a Congress session.

### Tilak — How It Works

- Tilak is the syllabus’s named Extremist. Learn him as politician + journalist + Home Rule leader.
- Highest-yield negative fact: **he never presidied an INC session** (**2021 Q50**), unlike Gokhale, Lajpat Rai, and Subhas Bose.
- On social legislation, he sided against Malabari’s Age of Consent campaign — tested in 2024.
- His newspapers Kesari (Pune) are press classics (Topic 7 overlap).
- *Gita Rahasya* is his philosophical-political book (full book Qs in Topic 10).
- Trap: "Tilak was Congress President at Surat." He **never** became President.
- Trap: "Tilak supported Age of Consent Act." He **opposed** it.
- Trap: "Tilak founded INC." Hume/Bonnerjee founding generation; Tilak is later Extremist.
- Trap: "Tilak = Moderate like Gokhale." They are the classic opposite pair.

> **Exam note:** Tilak = **Lokmanya + never President + Kesari + Home Rule + anti-Consent**.

## 9.9 Dadabhai Naoroji

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Dadabhai Naoroji (1825–1917)** | Early nationalist; **Grand Old Man of India**; Drain theorist |
| **Drain of Wealth** | Theory that Britain systematically drained India’s wealth |
| **Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901)** | His famous book — **2021 Q139** |

### Causes / Why He Mattered

1. He connected Indian poverty to colonial economic structure, not Indian “laziness.”
2. He used London politics and Parliament to voice India.
3. He bridged economic research and Congress leadership.
4. He gave Moderates an intellectual core beyond mere loyalty rhetoric.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Builds career as intellectual, businessman, and publicist across India and Britain.
2. **1866:** Founds **East India Association** in London.
3. Becomes a leading Drain of Wealth theorist through papers and speeches.
4. Serves as Congress President in **1886, 1893, and 1906**.
5. In **1901** publishes **Poverty and Un-British Rule in India**.
6. Enters British Parliament (first Asian MP in Commons memory) amplifying Indian claims.
7. At the 1906 Calcutta session, the Swaraj idea enters Congress vocabulary in a new way.
8. Remains the moral-intellectual grandfather figure of early nationalism.

### Dadabhai Naoroji — How It Works

- Naoroji is both **organisation founder** (East India Association) and **economic theorist** (Drain).
- Book year **1901** is a direct Prelims fact — do not mark 1900/1902/1903.
- “Un-British rule” in the book title feeds Moderate ideology questions: critique of betrayal of British principles.
- He is a Moderate statesman, not an Extremist street mobiliser like Tilak.
- Trap: "Poverty and Un-British Rule published in 1885." Year is **1901**.
- Trap: "Naoroji founded Indian Association." That is Banerjee–Bose, 1876.
- Trap: "Naoroji never presidied Congress." He presidied **three** times.
- Trap: "Drain Theory invented by Tilak." Classic association is **Naoroji** (also R.C. Dutt et al.).

> **Exam note:** Naoroji = **Drain + 1901 book + East India Association + thrice President**.

### Results

| Economic nationalism canon | Poverty as political proof |
| London advocacy | Imperial public sphere |

### Exam Facts (raata)
