## 2.0 One clear picture — how the Company became an empire

British rule in India did **not** begin as a planned land empire. It grew in **phases**. Hold this map first; every later card plugs into it.

| Phase | Rough years | Method | Exam lock |
|-------|-------------|--------|-----------|
| Commercial foothold | to c.1750 | Factories under local leave | Surat, Calcutta, Madras |
| Political breakthrough | **1757–65** | Puppet Nawab → **Diwani** | Plassey → Buxar → Allahabad |
| Dual / direct Bengal | **1765–72** | Take revenue, dodge blame → then direct charge | Clive creates; Hastings ends Dual Govt |
| Ring Fence | **1770s–90s** | Buffer states around Bengal | Awadh as shield; **not** Subsidiary Alliance |
| Non-intervention | Cornwallis / Shore | Avoid costly wars when possible | Brief pause before Wellesley |
| Subsidiary Alliance | **Wellesley 1798–1805** | Troops + subsidy + Resident | First = **Hyderabad 1798** |
| Paramountcy | **Lord Hastings** era | Smash rivals; claim overlordship | Nepal, Pindaris, Third Maratha |
| Annexation drive | **Dalhousie 1848–56** | Lapse + war + “misrule” | Jhansi/Nagpur vs **Awadh 1856** |

**Why the English won (Spectrum locks, keep short):**

- Better firearms, drill and paid standing troops.
- Steady war finance from Bengal revenue after **1765**.
- Indian powers were divided; the Company picked allies and paid traitors.
- A second line of commanders (Clive, Coote, Lake, Arthur Wellesley) fought for the Company, not only for one king.

**What this topic does *not* dig into:** day-to-day Permanent Settlement detail and full Governor-General reform lists live with administration notes. Here the lock is **expansion**.

> **Exam note:** Start every answer from the **phase**. Plassey is not Diwani. Subsidiary is not Lapse. Warren Hastings is not Lord Hastings.

## 2.1 Bengal on the eve — Nawabs and Company friction

**Richest Mughal subah** | capital **Murshidabad** | Company base **Fort William, Calcutta**

- Bengal’s wealth rested on **textiles, saltpetre, indigo and land revenue**.
- Under Emperor **Farrukhsiyar’s farman of 1717**, the Company gained major duty-free trade privileges in Bengal for a fixed annual payment. Later Nawabs fought the private misuse of those **dastaks**.
- Conflict grew when English private traders used **dastaks** (duty-free passes) beyond Company goods.
- The Nawabs wanted customs control. The Company wanted fort rights and trade privilege.
- **Murshid Quli Khan** (from **1717**) was the first strong independent-minded Nawab. He made **Murshidabad** the capital.
- **Shujauddin** followed him. Then came **Sarfaraz Khan**.
- **Alivardi Khan** killed Sarfaraz and ruled **1740–56**.
- Alivardi fought the Maratha **Bargis**. He was the last strong Nawab before the Plassey crisis.
- **Siraj-ud-Daulah (1756–57)** was Alivardi’s grandson. Court rivals included the banker **Jagat Seth**, **Omichand** and **Rai Durlabh**.
- Immediate sparks included English fortification of **Fort William** without leave, asylum to Krishnadas, and fear of the French in the **Seven Years’ War**.
- In **June 1756** Siraj took Calcutta. The **Black Hole** episode belongs to that capture (Holwell’s famous story; numbers are debated, the event is not).
- Clive and Admiral Watson recaptured Calcutta. The **Treaty of Alinagar (February 1757)** restored English privileges. Peace lasted only until Plassey.

| Nawab | Approx. | Exam tag |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Murshid Quli Khan | from 1717 | First strong Nawab; Murshidabad |
| Shujauddin | after Murshid Quli | Bridge ruler before Sarfaraz |
| Sarfaraz Khan | brief | Killed by Alivardi |
| Alivardi Khan | 1740–56 | Last strong Nawab; fought Bargis |
| Siraj-ud-Daulah | 1756–57 | Calcutta 1756; fell at Plassey |
| Mir Jafar | from 1757 | First puppet after Plassey |
| Mir Qasim | 1760–63 | Munger; equal duties; Buxar ally |
| Najm-ud-Daulah | after 1765 | Pensioned figurehead under Diwani |

> **Exam note:** Independent Nawabi ends at Plassey. **Diwani is 1765, not 1757**. The **1717 farman** explains the dastak quarrel; it is not Diwani.

## 2.2 Battle of Plassey, 23 June 1757

**Palashi (Nadia, Bengal)** on the Bhagirathi | **Robert Clive** | Nawab **Siraj-ud-Daulah**

- Clive’s force was small, about **3,000**. Siraj’s army looked large on paper, about **50,000**.
- The English won by **conspiracy**, not by a long pitched battle. Fighting lasted only hours on one afternoon.
- Secret allies of Clive included **Mir Jafar**, **Rai Durlabh**, banker **Jagat Seth**, and **Omichand**.
- **Omichand** was deceived by Clive’s forged or dual treaty.
- Rain soaked Siraj’s gunpowder. That helped the English, but conspiracy was the main cause.
- Siraj was captured and later killed on orders linked to Mir Jafar’s side (**Miran** is the usual name in the story).
- **Mir Jafar** became Nawab. The Company received the **zamindari of the 24 Parganas** and huge presents.
- Plassey founded a **political empire** through a puppet. It did **not** give the **Diwani**.
- It made the English the decisive military power in Bengal and opened the road to northern politics.

> **Exam note:** Plassey = **1757**, **Clive**, **Mir Jafar**. Diwani = **1765** after Buxar.

## 2.3 From Mir Qasim to Buxar and the Treaty of Allahabad

### Mir Qasim and the break with the Company

- Mir Jafar could not satisfy endless Company demands. In **1760** the Company replaced him with son-in-law **Mir Qasim**.
- Under the **1760 arrangement**, Mir Qasim cleared dues, promised war help in the south, and accepted friendship with the Company. Mir Jafar was pensioned.
- Mir Qasim shifted the capital to **Munger (Monghyr)** to keep distance from Calcutta.
- He reformed the army and bureaucracy. He tried to abolish internal duties so that **Indian and English traders paid equally**.
- Private English misuse of **dastaks** made equal duties a clash of principles. War followed.
- After the **Patna massacre of 1763**, the English reinstalled **Mir Jafar**. Mir Qasim fled and joined Awadh and the Emperor.

### Battle of Buxar, 22 October 1764

**Buxar (Bihar)** | English commander **Major Hector Munro** | Clive was in England

- The English faced a **triple alliance**.
- **Mir Qasim** stood for Bengal’s displaced Nawabi.
- **Shuja-ud-Daula** stood for **Awadh**.
- **Shah Alam II** was the Mughal Emperor.
- The coalition was routed. Buxar was a **real field victory**, unlike Plassey’s conspiracy win.
- Victory made the Company the strongest power in northern India, not only in Bengal.
- After the war the restored Mir Jafar also handed **Midnapore, Burdwan and Chittagong** revenues toward Company army costs. That was still short of full Diwani.

### Treaty of Allahabad, August 1765

Clive returned in **1765** and settled two linked treaties at Allahabad / the Prayagraj belt.

| Party | Terms |
|-------|-------|
| **Shah Alam II** | Granted **Diwani** of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa; about **₹26 lakh/year**; lived under Company protection at Allahabad |
| **Shuja-ud-Daula** | Kept Awadh after large indemnity (about **₹50 lakh**); **Allahabad and Kora (Kara)** went to the Emperor under English control; Banaras zamindar **Balwant Singh** secured |
| **Bengal Nawab** | **Najm-ud-Daulah** became a pensioned figurehead |

- **Diwani** = right to collect **land revenue**.
- Spectrum also notes a large Company claim linked to **nizamat** functions (defence, police, justice) alongside the Diwani farman. In practice Dual Government blurred both.
- Political empire now had a **legal Mughal stamp**. Revenue of the richest provinces financed later wars.

> **Exam note:** Munro fought; Clive settled. Allahabad = **1765**. Buxar is **not** a Bengal-only battle.

## 2.4 Bengal administration after Diwani

- **Diwani (1765)** meant the Company collected revenue of **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**.
- **Nizamat** (police and criminal justice) stayed in the Nawab’s **name**.
- In practice the Company also controlled Nizamat by nominating the deputy and keeping the army.
- **Dual Government (1765–72)** was Clive’s design: take the profit, avoid the blame for day-to-day rule.
- Administration collapsed. The **Bengal Famine of 1770** is the catastrophe of this dual system.
- In **1772 Warren Hastings** ended Dual Government. The Company took **direct** civil and revenue charge.
- **Clive** governed Bengal **1758–60** and again **1765–67** (Diwani and Dual Government).
- **Verelst** and **Cartier** governed while Dual Government continued, including the famine years.
- **Hastings (1772–85)** ended Dual Government, used Ring Fence, faced Banaras **1781**, and fought the First Maratha War.
- **Cornwallis (1786–93)** later brought the **Permanent Settlement of Bengal** (administration detail lives with revenue notes).

> **Exam note:** Clive **created** Dual Government; Hastings **ended** it in **1772**, not in 1857.

## 2.5 Robert Clive

**Company writer at Madras from 1743** | defence of **Arcot 1751** | **Plassey 1757** | Diwani settlement **1765**

- He rose in the Carnatic struggle and became the architect of English power in Bengal.
- He designed the **Mir Jafar** conspiracy that won Plassey.
- In his second Bengal term he negotiated the **Treaty of Allahabad, 1765**, and set up Dual Government.
- He did **not** command at Buxar.
- Parliament later inquired into his presents and jagir.
- He died in **1774**.

> **Exam note:** Clive = Plassey + Diwani frame. Hastings = end of Dual Government + Ring Fence + Banaras + Salbai.

## 2.6 Warren Hastings (expansion only)

**Governor of Bengal from 1772** | first **Governor-General of Bengal from 1773** (Regulating Act)

- In **1772** he ended Dual Government. The Company took direct revenue and civil charge.
- His **Ring Fence** policy used **Awadh** as a buffer around Bengal. This was **not** yet the Subsidiary Alliance.
- He fought the **Rohilla War in 1774** with Shuja-ud-Daula against the Rohillas of Rohilkhand.
- The **First Anglo-Maratha War** ended with the **Treaty of Salbai, 1782**.
- The **Banaras Rebellion of 1781** was his clash with Raja **Chait Singh**.
- The **Asiatic Society of Bengal** was founded in **1784** by **Sir William Jones**.
- Hastings **declined** the presidentship in Jones’s favour.
- He was impeached in Britain from **1788 to 1795** over Chait Singh, the Rohillas and presents. He was **acquitted**.

> **Exam note:** Keep this card for expansion locks — Dual Government **1772**, Ring Fence, Salbai, Banaras. Full office/reform lists sit with Governor-General notes.

## 2.7 British expansion policy — full ladder

There was **not** one policy from start to finish. Name the **Governor-General** and the **tool**.

| Policy | Who | What it meant | Exam example |
|--------|-----|---------------|--------------|
| Commercial leave | Early Company | Factories under farman / local grant | Surat, Calcutta |
| Puppet + Diwani | Clive | Install Nawab; then take revenue | Mir Jafar; Allahabad 1765 |
| Ring Fence | Warren Hastings | Buffer allies around Bengal | Awadh as shield |
| Non-intervention | Cornwallis / John Shore | Avoid expensive forward wars when possible | Pause before Wellesley |
| Subsidiary Alliance | **Wellesley (1798–1805)** | British force + subsidy/cession + Resident; ruler keeps internal admin | **Hyderabad 1798** first |
| Paramountcy | **Lord Hastings** | Company as overlord; crush remaining rivals | Nepal; Pindaris; Third Maratha |
| Doctrine of Lapse | **Dalhousie from 1848** | No natural heir → annex | Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur |
| Misrule annexation | Dalhousie | Annex for “bad government” | **Awadh 1856** (not Lapse) |
| Masterly Inactivity | John Lawrence | Caution on Afghanistan after First Afghan War | Watch frontier; avoid occupation |
| Proud Reserve | Lytton | Forward Afghan policy in the 1870s | Second Afghan War context |

- Wellesley fought the **Fourth Mysore War** and the **Second Maratha War**. He was recalled in **1805** because the wars cost too much.
- **Lord Hastings** (not Warren Hastings) fought Nepal, the Pindaris and the Third Maratha War.
- **Ellenborough** annexed **Sindh in 1843**.
- **Hardinge** fought the First Sikh War.
- **Dalhousie** annexed **Punjab in 1849** and **Awadh in 1856**.
- The **Vellore Mutiny (1806)** sits between Wellesley’s recall and the Nepal war on chronology charts.

> **Exam note:** Wellesley did **not** invent Lapse. Dalhousie–Lapse is a standing match lock.

## 2.8 Subsidiary Alliance

**Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)** | perfected from earlier French practice (Dupleix’s idea, British system)

- The Indian ruler kept **internal administration**.
- He lost the right to war and peace and could not host other Europeans without British leave.
- He paid a **subsidy** or **ceded land** instead, and accepted a **British Resident**.
- The Company posted a subsidiary force “for his protection.” In practice the Resident ran foreign policy.

| State | Year |
|-------|------|
| **Hyderabad (Nizam)** | **1798 — first** |
| **Mysore (Wodeyar)** | **1799** after Tipu |
| **Tanjore (Thanjavur)** | **1799** (Wellesley period) |
| **Awadh** | **1801** (ceded Rohilkhand / Doab tracts) |
| **Peshwa** | **1802 Treaty of Bassein** |
| Sindhia / Bhonsle | after Second Maratha War |
| Many Rajput states | under Lord Hastings after 1818 |

- The alliance looked cheaper than annexation at first.
- Wellesley also pressed hard on south Indian courts. **Tanjore** entered subsidiary terms, and the **Carnatic** Nawabi was effectively reduced under Company control in the same forward phase.
- The subsidy later ruined treasuries. **Awadh’s path to 1856** is the classic exam example.
- **Wellesley was recalled in 1805** because expansion wars were too expensive.

> **Exam note:** First subsidiary = **Hyderabad 1798**, not Jhansi. Hyderabad was **never** annexed by Lapse.

## 2.9 Doctrine of Lapse and Awadh 1856

- **Dalhousie** used Lapse from **1848**.
- If a dependent state had **no natural heir**, an adopted son was **not recognised**.
- The state then **lapsed** to the Company.

| State | Year | Note |
|-------|------|------|
| **Satara** | **1848** | First major Lapse case |
| Jaitpur, Sambalpur | 1849 | Smaller Lapse annexations |
| **Baghat** | 1850 | Hill state; annexed then later restored in the Lapse story |
| **Jhansi** | **1853** | Gangadhar Rao died; **Damodar Rao** (Lakshmibai’s adopted son) **rejected** |
| **Nagpur** | **1854** | Major Lapse annexation |
| **Awadh** | **1856** | **Wajid Ali Shah**; **misgovernance**, **not** Lapse |

- Some lists also name **Udaipur** among threatened Lapse cases. Exam focus stays on **Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur**, and the **Awadh ≠ Lapse** trap.
- Awadh had already been hollowed by the **1801** subsidiary cessions.
- In **1856** Resident **Outram**’s reports and Dalhousie’s “misrule” charge led to annexation. **Wajid Ali Shah** was deposed and sent toward Calcutta.
- Lapse and “misrule” together fed the anger that later exploded in **1857**, but this card stops at annexation.

> **Exam note:** Dalhousie did **not** recognise Lakshmibai’s heir. Awadh **1856 ≠** Lapse.

## 2.10 Anglo-Mysore Wars and Mysore State

- After Vijayanagara’s fall at **Talikota (1565)**, a **Wodeyar** kingdom rose in Mysore (from early 17th century).
- **Hyder Ali** seized real power in **1761**. He had already set up a modern armoury at **Dindigal in 1755**.
- **Tipu Sultan** succeeded in **1782**. He used rockets, kept French contacts, and was called the Tiger of Mysore.
- Tipu also pushed state reforms in trade, sericulture and a new calendar. Exams still test wars and treaties first.
- The capital was **Seringapatam (Srirangapatna)**.
- There were **four** Anglo-Mysore Wars. Fear of French help to Tipu shaped Wellesley’s urgency in the Fourth War.

| War | Years | Key names | End |
|-----|-------|-----------|-----|
| **I** | 1767–69 | Hyder near Madras; Nizam/Maratha angles | **Treaty of Madras 1769** (status quo) |
| **II** | 1780–84 | **Pollilur 1780** (Hyder/Tipu win); **Porto Novo 1781** (**Eyre Coote**); Hyder dies **1782** | **Treaty of Mangalore 1784** (prisoners restored; status roughly restored) |
| **III** | 1790–92 | Tipu attacked **Travancore**; **Cornwallis** led; Marathas and Nizam with English | **Treaty of Seringapatam 1792** — lost about **half** territory + ₹3 crore; sons as hostages |
| **IV** | **1799** | **Wellesley**; Harris/Stuart; Nizam allied | Tipu killed **4 May 1799**; **Krishna Raja Wodeyar III** restored under **subsidiary**; English took Kanara, Coimbatore, Wynad and Seringapatam belt; Nizam got Gooty etc. |

> **Exam note:** Madras ≠ Mangalore. Tipu did **not** survive 1799. First Mysore comes after the Second Anglo-French War and long before the First Sikh War.

## 2.11 Anglo-Maratha Wars

- The Maratha confederacy had five main houses.
- The **Peshwa** sat at **Poona**.
- **Sindhia** sat at **Gwalior**.
- **Holkar** sat at **Indore**.
- **Bhonsle** sat at **Nagpur**.
- The **Gaekwad** sat at **Baroda**.
- **Chauth** was the Maratha claim of **one-fourth** of land revenue for “protection”. It is **not** the Subsidiary Alliance.
- **Sardeshmukhi** was an extra levy of about **10 percent**.
- After **Panipat 1761**, Madhav Rao I revived Maratha power, but later houses drifted apart. Division helped the Company.

- After the Third War the Peshwaship ended. Maratha houses became dependent princes under British paramountcy.

**Why the Marathas lost (short Spectrum locks):**

- The confederacy was divided. Houses fought each other as often as they fought the Company.
- Superior Company artillery, drill and Bengal war finance after Diwani.
- After Bassein the Peshwa himself became a British client, splitting Maratha politics from inside.

## 2.12 Sikh Empire, Anglo-Sikh Wars, Treaty of Lahore, Punjab Annexation

- **Ranjit Singh** unified the Sikh misls. He died in **1839**.
- His capital was **Lahore**.
- European officers such as **Ventura** and **Allard** trained his army.
- He set up the **Adalat-i-Ala**, the supreme court, at **Lahore**, not Amritsar.
- The empire stretched from the **Khyber** to the **Sutlej**.
- By the **Treaty of Amritsar (25 April 1809)** with the Company, Ranjit accepted the **Sutlej** as the southern limit of his push. That kept peace while he lived.
- After **1839** succession chaos (Kharak Singh, Nau Nihal Singh, Sher Singh and court factions) opened the door to war. The Khalsa army remained powerful but politics collapsed.

| War | Years | GG | Battles | Result |
|-----|-------|-----|---------|--------|
| **I** | 1845–46 | **Hardinge** | **Mudki** (18 Dec 1845), Ferozeshah, Aliwal, **Sobraon** | **Treaty of Lahore 1846** + **Bhairowal 1846** |
| **II** | 1848–49 | **Dalhousie** | Multan revolt (**Mulraj**); Ramnagar; **Chillianwala** (British setback); **Gujrat** (21 Feb 1849) | **Punjab annexed 29 Mar 1849** |

**Treaty of Lahore, 1846**

- The Sikhs ceded the **Jullundur Doab** (land between Beas and Sutlej).
- Kashmir was ceded and then sold to **Gulab Singh** for **₹75 lakh** (Treaty of Amritsar with Gulab Singh, **1846** — do not confuse with Ranjit’s **1809** Amritsar treaty).
- The Sikhs paid an indemnity and accepted a British resident.
- The treaty did **not** annex Punjab.
- The later **Treaty of Bhairowal (1846)** tightened British control over the minor Maharaja **Duleep Singh**.

**Annexation, 1849**

- **Duleep Singh** was deposed and sent to England.
- Punjab became a **Chief Commissionership**.
- **Henry Lawrence** and **John Lawrence** ran the “Punjab School” of administration.

> **Exam note:** **1809 Amritsar ≠ 1846 Lahore ≠ 1849 annexation**. Mudki = First Sikh, not Mysore.

## 2.13 Anglo-Nepal War and Treaty of Sugauli, 1816

- The war lasted **1814–16**.
- The Governor-General was **Lord Hastings**, not Warren Hastings.
- The Gurkhas had expanded into **Garhwal, Kumaon and Sikkim**.
- **David Ochterlony** faced **Amar Singh Thapa**.
- **Nalapani** is remembered for **Balbhadra Kunwar**.

**Treaty of Sugauli (1816)**

- Nepal ceded **Kumaon** and **Garhwal**.
- It also ceded **Sikkim west of the Kali** and strips of the **Tarai**.
- Nepal remained **independent**. It was **not annexed**.
- A British Resident sat at Kathmandu.
- The British gained hill tracts later associated with **Shimla, Ranikhet and Nainital**.
- **Kathmandu was not obtained.**
- Gurkha recruitment into the British army began after the war.

> **Exam note:** Kathmandu trap is standard. Sugauli ≠ Lahore.

## 2.14 Anglo-Burmese Wars

- The **First Anglo-Burmese War (1824–26)** was fought under **Amherst**, not Dalhousie.
- It ended with the **Treaty of Yandabo, 1826**. The Company gained **Assam, Manipur, Arakan and Tenasserim**.
- **Maha Bandula** was killed at Danubyu in **1825**.
- The **Second War (1852)** under **Dalhousie** annexed **Pegu / Lower Burma**.
- The **Third War (1885)** deposed **Thibaw**. Upper Burma was taken and Burma became a province of British India.

> **Exam note:** First Burma War is **not** Dalhousie’s. Basic date lock = **Yandabo 1826**.

## 2.15 Sindh Annexation, 1843

- Sindh was ruled by the **Talpur Amirs**.
- The Governor-General was **Ellenborough**, not Dalhousie. The field commander was **Sir Charles Napier**.
- After the **First Afghan War** the British wanted a secure Indus supply route to the north-west.
- Earlier pressure included commercial treaties and navigation on the Indus. Conquest was gradual, then sudden in 1843.
- The **Battle of Miani (17 February 1843)** and **Dabo (24 March 1843)** decided the war.
- Sindh was annexed in **1843**. Napier’s reported telegram **“Peccavi”** (“I have sinned”) is folklore, not a treaty name.

> **Exam note:** Sindh is **1843**. Punjab is **1849**. Ellenborough / Napier, not Dalhousie.

## 2.16 Pindari campaign and Vellore Mutiny

### Pindaris (Lord Hastings)

- The **Pindaris** were irregular plunder bands based mainly in Malwa / Central India.
- They raided Company and allied territories. **Lord Hastings** decided to destroy them.
- The **Pindari campaign (1817–18)** ran alongside the **Third Anglo-Maratha War**.
- Ending the Pindaris and the Peshwa together completed British paramountcy in central India.

### Vellore Mutiny, 1806

- The **Vellore Mutiny (10 July 1806)** broke out in the Madras Presidency soon after Wellesley’s recall.
- Sepoys rose against new dress and caste-religious regulations in the fort at **Vellore**.
- Tipu’s family was confined there. The rising failed quickly.
- It is an **expansion-era chronology lock** (after Wellesley **1805**, before Nepal **1814**), not the Revolt of 1857.

> **Exam note:** Nana Fadnavis d. **1800** → Wellesley recalled **1805** → Vellore **1806** → Anglo-Nepal **1814–16**.

## 2.17 Neighbouring frontiers — Afghan, Bhutan, Tibet, NW policy

Spectrum keeps these with expansion / paramountcy. Hold the locks; deep viceroy reform detail sits elsewhere.

### Anglo-Afghan Wars (chronology + policy)

| War | Years | GG / policy tag | End lock |
|-----|-------|-----------------|----------|
| **First** | **1839–42** | **Auckland**; forward move against Dost Muhammad / Russian fear | Disaster of the retreat from Kabul; Company failure |
| **Second** | **1878–80** | **Lytton**; **Proud Reserve** / forward Afghan line | Treaty of Gandamak phase; Abdur Rahman later settled |
| **Third** | **1919** | After World War I | Afghanistan’s independence in foreign affairs |

- First Afghan War is the standard UPPCS chronology companion to Mysore / Sikh wars.
- **John Lawrence’s Masterly Inactivity** meant caution on Afghanistan after the First War lessons — watch the frontier, avoid costly occupation.
- **Lytton’s Proud Reserve** was a more forward Afghan policy in the 1870s.

### Anglo-Bhutanese

- Border friction in Assam / Duars led to war in the 1860s.
- The **Treaty of Sinchula (1865)** settled the First Anglo-Bhutan War. Britain took the **Duars**; Bhutan kept independence with a subsidy arrangement.

### Anglo-Tibetan

- The **Younghusband mission (1904)** forced a British expedition to Lhasa under Curzon’s frontier anxiety.
- Tibet was not annexed as a British province. The lock is **mission / treaty pressure**, not a Plassey-style conquest.

> **Exam note:** Do not place First Afghan War under Dalhousie. Masterly Inactivity ≠ Proud Reserve.

## 2.18 Banaras Rebellion and Akbar Fort, Prayagraj

### Banaras / Chait Singh, 1781

- Raja **Chait Singh** of Banaras clashed with **Warren Hastings**.
- Hastings demanded extra tribute of about **₹23 lakh** and a troop levy.
- Hastings arrested him at **Shivala**. Guards rescued him.
- **Major Popham** crushed the revolt.
- Chait Singh fled to **Gwalior**. Benares came under closer Company control.
- The episode became a charge in Hastings’s **impeachment**. It is **not** part of 1857.

### Akbar Fort, Prayagraj

- This is the Mughal fort at the **Ganga–Yamuna sangam** at Allahabad / Prayagraj.
- It is **not** the Delhi or Agra fort.
- The **Treaty of Allahabad, 1765** was settled in this belt.
- **Shah Alam II** lived for a time under Company protection here.
- The Company also tightened control of the Doab.
