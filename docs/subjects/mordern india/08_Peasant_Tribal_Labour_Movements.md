### Sanyasiâ€“Fakir Revolt (~1763â€“1800)

**Period:** ~1763â€“1800 (peak after the **Bengal famine of 1770**)  
**Region:** Bengal and Bihar  
**Leaders:** **Majnu Shah** (Fakir), **Musa Shah**, **Bhawani Pathak**, **Devi Chaudhurani**

### Causes
- Hindu sanyasis and Muslim fakirs traditionally moved in armed bands, collecting alms and customary contributions from villages.
- After Plassey the Company treated these bands as a threat to order and revenue.
- Company rules restricted their movement, taxed pilgrim routes, and cut their right to collect village contributions.
- The **Bengal famine of 1770** left peasants desperate. Many uprooted cultivators joined the bands for survival.

### Course
- Rebels attacked Company *kuthis* (trading posts), government treasuries and loyal zamindars across Bengal and Bihar.
- The rising ran through **Warren Hastingsâ€™** years and continued in waves into the 1780sâ€“90s.
- Company troops fought a long, scattered campaign of hit-and-run raids rather than one decisive battle.

### Results
- The rising was gradually crushed only towards the end of the eighteenth century as Company control tightened.
- **Bankim Chandra Chatterjeeâ€™s** novel *Anandamath* (1882) is set against this background.
- The song **Vande Mataram** comes from that novel.
- In the **2025 Q25** chronology set this is the **earliest** of the four revolts.

> **Exam note:** Sanyasi is **late eighteenth century**, not 1859. It comes **before** Indigo, Kuka and Pabna.

### Kuka (Namdhari) Revolt (~1872)

**Year (exam):** **~1872** (Malerkotla action)  
**Region:** Punjab (Bhaini Sahib, Ludhiana; Malerkotla)  
**Leaders:** **Baba Ram Singh**; the sect was earlier shaped by **Balak Singh**

### Causes
- The **Namdhari / Kuka** movement began as a Sikh socio-religious reform preaching simplicity, cow protection, boycott of British goods, and opposition to caste display.
- Baba Ram Singh built a strong following around his centre at **Bhaini Sahib**; followers were called Kukas from their cry in kirtan.
- Cow-slaughter by butchers, seen as an attack on Sikh-Hindu sentiment, turned the sect's reform zeal into direct action.

### Course
- In **1872** a Kuka band attacked butchers at **Malerkotla** after cow-slaughter incidents.
- British officer **Cowan** responded by having dozens of Kukas **blown from guns** without a proper trial.

### Results
- Baba Ram Singh was deported to **Rangoon**, ending the movement's organised leadership.
- Place Kuka **after Indigo (1859â€“60)** and **before Pabna (1873)** â€” the **2025 Q25** chronology lock.

> **Exam note:** Kuka â‰  1857. Kuka â‰  Pabna. Year tag for chronology is **1872**.

## 8.2 Peasant Movements and Revolts

- Peasant movements grew from **rent hikes**, eviction (*bedakhli*), **indigo contracts**, and unpaid labour (*begar*).
- Later they also grew from Kisan Sabha, Congress and left politics.
- Early cases often used **leagues and courts**, as at Pabna.
- Later cases used **satyagraha**, as at Champaran, Kheda and Bardoli, or **sharecropper strikes**, as at Tebhaga.

### Pabna Agrarian League (1873â€“85)

**Year:** **1873â€“85**  
**Region:** Yusufshahi pargana, **Pabna** district, Bengal  
**Leaders:** **Ishan Chandra Roy**, **Khoodi Mollah**, **Shambhu Nath Pal**

### Causes
- Zamindars raised rents illegally above the recorded rate, forged occupancy papers, and threatened eviction of tenants who resisted.

### Course
- Peasants formed an **agrarian league** in **1873**. They refused illegal cesses, went to court, and used village solidarity rather than arms.
- The movement stayed largely **legal and organised** â€” not a tribal war and not an indigo-factory riot.
- Indian publicists such as **R.C. Dutt** and sections of the Bengal press backed the ryots' cause.

### Results
- Pressure from this long struggle fed into the **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885**, which gave occupancy tenants some legal protection.
- **2019 Q18** matches Pabna with **1873â€“85**. **2025 Q25** places Pabna **last** among Sanyasiâ€“Indigoâ€“Kukaâ€“Pabna.

> **Exam note:** Pabna = **zamindar rent**, Bengal, **1873â€“85**. Deccan 1875 = **moneylenders**. Indigo 1859 = **European planters**.

### Eka Movement (1921â€“22)

**Year:** **1921â€“22** (2019 list uses **1922**)  
**Region:** Northern Awadh â€” **Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur** (also Unnao / Kheri belt)  
**Leader:** **Madari Pasi**

### Causes
- Awadh tenants faced high rents, illegal **nazrana** (premium), unpaid **begar**, and **bedakhli** (eviction) at the hands of taluqdars.

### Course
- *Eka* means **unity**. Peasants swore â€” often on Ganga water â€” to pay **only the recorded rent**, to take a **receipt**, and not to leave the holding.
- Congress and Khilafat volunteers helped launch the movement. When it turned more militant, Congress pulled back.
- **Madari Pasi**, a **Pasi** (depressed-caste) leader, kept a strong local command after Congress withdrew support.

### Results
- The rising was suppressed by **1922**. It is **not** the 1918 UP Kisan Sabha and **not** Tebhaga.

> **Exam note:** Eka = **Awadh 1921â€“22 + Madari Pasi**. Dwivedi is **1918 UP Kisan Sabha**, not Eka.

### Champaran Satyagraha (1917)

**Year:** **1917**  
**Region:** Champaran, Bihar  
**Issue:** **Tinkathia** indigo (peasants had to plant indigo on **3/20** of the holding)

### Causes
- European planters still forced peasants into the **tinkathia** indigo contract long after the 1859 Bengal Indigo Revolt.

### Course
- **Rajkumar Shukla** brought **Gandhi** to Champaran. This became Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India**.
- Gandhi held a public inquiry and recorded peasant statements documenting planter abuses.

### Results
- The inquiry broke the tinkathia system.
- The **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918** ended the old planter compulsion.
- Do **not** merge this with the **Indigo Revolt of 1859â€“60** (Bengal, Biswas brothers).

> **Exam note:** Champaran **1917** = Gandhi + tinkathia + Bihar. Indigo Revolt **1859â€“60** = Bengal + Biswas brothers.

### Kheda Satyagraha (1918)

**Year:** **1918**  
**Region:** Kheda, Gujarat  
**Leaders:** Gandhi, **Vallabhbhai Patel**, Indulal Yagnik

### Causes
- Crop failure and plague made the fixed revenue demand unjust; peasants asked for **remission**.

### Course
- It ran as a **no-revenue** satyagraha â€” a dress rehearsal in method for **Bardoli 1928**.
- Patel's rural organisational skill was first seen here, building village-level discipline for withholding revenue.

### Results
- The government granted partial relief on the revenue demand.

> **Exam note:** Kheda **1918** â‰  Bardoli **1928**. Both are Gujarat revenue satyagrahas; Bardoli is the bigger Patel card.

### Oudh (Awadh) Kisan Sabha (October 1920)

**Year:** **October 1920**, Pratapgarh  
**Leader:** **Baba Ramchandra** (mass mobiliser); Congress and Home Rule volunteers helped organise  
**Demands:** end to *nazrana* (illegal premium), reduced *begar*, security of tenure against *bedakhli*

### Causes
- Awadh tenants faced illegal *nazrana* premiums, unpaid *begar*, and insecurity of tenure through *bedakhli* eviction by taluqdars.
- This is a **separate body** from the **1918 United Provinces Kisan Sabha** of Dwivedi and Gauri Shankar Mishra. Do not merge the two founding dates.

### Course
- **Baba Ramchandra**, a former indentured labourer returned from Fiji, held huge Awadh village meetings using Ramayana-style oral recitation to draw crowds.
- The **UP Provincial Congress Committee** took formal notice and linked the Sabha to the wider Non-Cooperation mobilisation of 1920â€“21.
- A young **Jawaharlal Nehru** toured Pratapgarh, Rae Bareli and Faizabad villages in these months and later called the experience his political education, but he did **not found** the Sabha.
- Militancy grew faster than Congress control; taluqdar houses were looted in places, and Congress leaders tried to keep the movement non-violent and constitutional.

### Results
- The more radical **Eka Movement (1921â€“22)** under **Madari Pasi** grew partly out of this same Awadh discontent when Congress pulled back from the sharper edge of tenant anger.

> **Exam note:** **1918 UP Kisan Sabha** (Dwivedi) â†’ **October 1920 Oudh Kisan Sabha** (Ramchandra) â†’ **1921â€“22 Eka** (Madari Pasi). Three different Awadh-belt bodies, three different years.

### All India Kisan Sabha (1936)

- Formed at the **Lucknow Congress session, 1936**, as a national peasant platform.
- **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati** became the central AIKS leader (Bihar / Bhumihar peasant base).
- N.G. Ranga, Indulal Yagnik and left Congressmen were associated.
- Demands included zamindari abolition, occupancy rights, and an end to illegal cesses.
- Sahajanand is a **1936** figure. He did **not** found the **UP Kisan Sabha in 1918**.

> **Exam note:** AIKS **1936** = Sahajanand. UP Kisan Sabha **1918** = **Indra Narayan Dwivedi**.

## 8.4 Indigo Revolt (1859â€“60)

**Year:** 1859â€“60  
**Region:** Bengal â€” **Nadia**, Jessore, Pabna, Khulna, Murshidabad  
**Leaders:** **Digambar Biswas** and **Bishnu Biswas** of Chaugacha, Nadia  
**Viceroy:** Canning

### Causes
- European planters forced ryots to grow indigo on their **best plots** at a price that did not cover cost.
- Planters gave cash advances (*dadon*) that trapped ryots in endless debt. Refusal to sow brought beatings, cattle seizure, and eviction.

### Course
- In **1859** the Biswas brothers of Nadia refused to sow indigo. Village after village followed their lead.
- Ryots stopped planting, socially boycotted planter agents, and in places attacked indigo factories.
- The **Hindu Patriot** under **Harish Chandra Mukherjee** publicised planter crimes; missionaries also wrote against the system.
- **Dinabandhu Mitra** wrote the play ***Nil Darpan*** (1860) dramatising the ryots' suffering.
- **Rev. James Long** was fined and jailed for publishing the English translation of the play.

### Results
- The government appointed the **Indigo Commission, 1860** (Seton-Karr), which held that indigo was grown **only under force**.
- Forced indigo collapsed in large parts of Bengal â€” this is treated as a **successful peasant revolt**.
- It remained a **ryot versus European planter** fight, distinct from a tribal forest war and from Champaran 1917.

> **Exam note:** Indigo = **1859â€“60 Bengal + Biswas brothers + Commission 1860**. 2025 Q25: between Sanyasi and Kuka. 2025 Q127: after Awadh 1856, before Second Afghan War 1878.

## 8.5 Deccan Riots (1875)

**Year:** **1875**  
**Region:** **Poona** and **Ahmednagar** districts (Maharashtra Deccan)  
**Target:** Moneylenders (*sahukars* / Marwari and Gujarati creditors) â€” **not** indigo factories

### Causes
- Ryotwari revenue was high and had to be paid in cash even after a bad harvest.
- The **American Civil War** created a **cotton boom**; when it ended, prices crashed and peasants could not repay loans taken during the boom.
- Moneylenders took land, ornaments and enforced **debt bonds**, and courts usually sided with creditors.

### Course
- Riots began in **May 1875** at **Supa** and spread through the Poonaâ€“Ahmednagar belt (Koregaon and nearby villages).
- Crowds attacked *sahukar* houses and **burnt bond papers**. The aim was to wipe out the written debt, not random massacre.

### Results
- The **Deccan Riots Commission** inquired into the causes.
- The **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act, 1879** tried to check usury and ease court procedure for ryots.
- Deccan 1875 stayed a short **debt riot**, unlike Pabna's long **rent-league** struggle or Bardoli's **1928 no-tax satyagraha**.

> **Exam note:** Deccan **1875** = moneylenders + Relief Act **1879**. Not 1857, not Birsa, not Bardoli.

## 8.6 Santhal Rebellion (1855â€“56)

**Year:** **1855â€“56**  
**Region:** **Damin-i-Koh** / Rajmahal hills (Bhagalpurâ€“Rajmahal belt; later **Santhal Parganas**)  
**Leaders:** brothers **Sidhu, Kanhu, Chand and Bhairav** Murmu (exam tag: **Sidhu and Kanhu**)

### Causes
- The Company had settled Santhals in **Damin-i-Koh (1832)** to clear forest and pay rent.
- Outsiders called ***dikus*** â€” zamindars, *mahajans*, traders, police and railway contractors â€” seized Santhal land and trapped them in debt.
- Courts and police backed the exploiters, and traditional village headmen lost their customary authority.

### Course
- On **30 June 1855** at **Bhognadih**, Sidhu and Kanhu declared the **Hul** (rebellion), claiming a divine command to end *diku* rule.
- Rebel bands attacked moneylenders, zamindars and symbols of Company power. The rising spread fast across the Santhal belt.
- The British declared martial law and crushed the Hul by **1856**; Sidhu was killed and Kanhu was captured.

### Results

## 8.15 Bonded Labour Practices

- **Bonded labour** is work tied by **debt or custom** so the worker cannot leave freely. The loan never clears; the family often inherits the debt.
- ***Begar*** is unpaid labour taken by landlords, taluqdars or officials. It was a core Awadh and tribal grievance (Eka, Tana Bhagat, Kol, Munda).
- Indigo ***dadon*** (advances) locked Bengal ryots into unfree crop contracts. That is crop bondage, not a mill wage.
- Deccan *sahukars* and Santhal *mahajans* used **bond papers** as weapons. Burning those papers in 1875 was an attack on bondage, not random loot.
- Plantation and *arkatti* recruitment in Assam also used advances and penal contracts (a related unfree-labour story).
- **Article 23** of the Constitution prohibits traffic in human beings and *begar* / similar forms of forced labour.
- The **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976** is the main statute after independence. It frees bonded labourers and extinguishes the debt.
- **AITUC (1920)** organised factory wage workers. It did **not** abolish village bondage.

> **Exam note:** Bondage here = **debt, indigo advances, *diku* moneylenders, begar**. Not the same as INTUC/AITUC mill unions. Act = **1976**, not 1920.
