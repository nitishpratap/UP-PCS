## 5.1 Revolt of 1857

- The Revolt of 1857 was a widespread uprising against **East India Company** rule. It lasted from **1857 to 1858**.
- It started among **sepoys** (Indian soldiers) and then spread to peasants, taluqdars, artisans and displaced Indian rulers.
- The strongest area was **North and Central India**, especially present-day **Uttar Pradesh**.
- The British called it a **Sepoy Mutiny**. Later nationalist writers called it the **First War of Independence**.
- **V.D. Savarkar** used that nationalist name in his book *The Indian War of Independence* (**1909**).
- It was **not** a war of the whole of India. **Punjab, Madras, Bombay, Hyderabad** and most of **South India** stayed quiet or helped the British.
- There was **no single national command** and no modern political party behind it.

> **Exam note:** The revolt did **not** begin at Delhi. The mass outbreak was at **Meerut on 10 May 1857**.

---

## 5.2 Causes

**Political**

- The **Doctrine of Lapse** (Dalhousie) annexed states with no natural heir. **Jhansi, Satara and Nagpur** are the stock examples.
- **Awadh** was annexed in **1856** on the charge of **misrule**, not under Lapse. Nawab **Wajid Ali Shah** was sent to Calcutta.
- These annexations angered rulers, their soldiers and the people who had lived under them.

**Economic**

- Land revenue was high and had to be paid in **cash**.
- Traditional handicrafts declined under cheap British imports.
- In Awadh, many **taluqdars** lost estates after annexation. Peasants also suffered.

**Social and religious**

- Missionary activity created fear of forced conversion.
- Social laws such as abolition of **sati** and widow remarriage were read by many as an attack on custom.
- The army’s new **Enfield** rifle used a cartridge rumoured to be greased with **cow and pig fat**.

**Military**

- Indian sepoys were paid less and promoted less than British soldiers.
- The **General Service Enlistment Act, 1856** required new recruits to serve overseas. Crossing the sea was against the caste belief of many high-caste sepoys of the Bengal Army.
- The greased cartridge became the **immediate spark**. It was **not** the only cause.

> **Exam note:** Awadh 1856 ≠ Doctrine of Lapse. Greased cartridge = spark, not the full explanation.

## 5.3 Beginning of Revolt

- On **29 March 1857**, sepoy **Mangal Pandey** of the **34th Native Infantry** attacked British officers at **Barrackpore**.
- Pandey was hanged on **8 April 1857**. The 34th Native Infantry was later disbanded.
- At **Meerut**, sepoys who refused the new cartridges were court-martialled and sentenced on **9 May 1857**.
- On **10 May 1857** the Meerut sepoys mutinied, freed their comrades, killed officers, and marched towards Delhi. This is treated as the **start of the revolt**.
- On **11–12 May 1857** the rebels seized **Delhi** and proclaimed **Bahadur Shah II (Zafar)** as emperor.
- From May–June 1857 the revolt spread to **Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi, Bareilly, Allahabad, Faizabad** and parts of **Bihar**.

| Date | Place | What happened |
|------|--------|----------------|
| **29 March 1857** | Barrackpore | Mangal Pandey incident |
| **10 May 1857** | Meerut | Mass outbreak |
| **11–12 May 1857** | Delhi | City seized; Zafar proclaimed emperor |
| **4 June 1857** | Lucknow | Rising (correct pair in 2023 Q40) |
| **June 1857** | Jhansi | Rising — **not 11 May** |

> **Exam note:** Barrackpore is the **spark**. Meerut is the **beginning**. UPPCS 2023 Q40 unmatched pair = **Jhansi — 11 May 1857**.

## 5.5 Leaders of Revolt

The six named leaders of the syllabus have their own headings below. These are the **other** names that UPPCS puts in match and option traps.

- **Bahadur Shah Zafar** was the last Mughal emperor. Rebels made him the **symbolic** head at Delhi. After Delhi fell he was tried, exiled to **Rangoon**, and died in **1862**.
- **Bakht Khan**, a Rohilla officer from the Bareilly contingent, organised the **actual fighting** at Delhi. Zafar did not command the army in the field.
- **Khan Bahadur Khan** led the revolt at **Bareilly** in Uttar Pradesh (**2023 Q46**). He is not the leader of Lucknow.
- **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah** (the Maulvi of Faizabad) led at **Faizabad** and also fought in the Lucknow region.
- **Maulvi Liaquat Ali** led at **Allahabad**.
- **Azimullah Khan** was the political adviser of **Nana Sahib**.
- **Birjis Qadr**, son of Begum Hazrat Mahal, was seated as the young ruler at Lucknow.

**British commanders (exam pairs)**

- **John Nicholson** led the assault that recaptured **Delhi** in **September 1857**. He was killed in the assault. Hodson captured Zafar.
- **Henry Lawrence** defended the **Lucknow Residency** and died during the siege.
- **Havelock** and later **Colin Campbell** operated at **Kanpur** and in the relief of **Lucknow**. **Outram** also took part in the Lucknow operations.
- **Sir Hugh Rose** led the campaign against **Jhansi, Kalpi and Gwalior**.

> **Exam note:** Bareilly’s leader is **Khan Bahadur Khan**. Do not put Hazrat Mahal, Nana Sahib or Kunwar Singh at Bareilly.

## 5.6 Important Places

Centre, leader and start-date are in the table above. These are the other places that still get asked.

- **Lucknow Residency** was besieged for months. **Henry Lawrence** was killed there.
- **Bibighar** at Kanpur is linked to the massacre during Nana Sahib’s control of the city.
- After Jhansi fell, **Lakshmibai** and **Tantia Tope** moved to **Kalpi** and then to **Gwalior**.
- **Gwalior** was the capital of Sindhia. The Rani died near Gwalior at **Kotah-ki-Serai** on **17/18 June 1858**.
- **Arrah** was the main siege town in Kunwar Singh’s Bihar campaign.
- **Tantia Tope** was hanged at **Shivpuri** on **18 April 1859**.
- **Bahadur Shah Zafar** was exiled to **Rangoon**.
- **Allahabad** saw harsh repression under Colonel Neill. The **Queen’s Proclamation** was read here on **1 November 1858**.

> **Exam note:** Lakshmibai died at **Gwalior in 1858**, not at the first fall of Jhansi in 1857.

## 5.7 Consequences

- The **Government of India Act, 1858** ended East India Company rule. India was placed under the **British Crown**.
- A **Secretary of State for India** was created in London. The Governor-General was also given the title **Viceroy**.
- **Lord Canning**, who was already Governor-General during the revolt, became the **first Viceroy**.
- The **Queen’s Proclamation** was issued on **1 November 1858** and read at **Allahabad**.
- The Proclamation promised: no more aggressive annexation, no interference in Indian religions, and equal treatment of Indians in service — in words, not always in practice.
- The army was reorganised (Peel Commission line): more British soldiers, mixed recruitment from different regions and groups, and an end to the old high-caste Bengal Army pattern.
- The Doctrine of Lapse was no longer used as a regular annexation tool. Princely treaties were stressed.
- The Mughal court at Delhi was finished. Later colonial writing often blamed Muslims specially for the revolt.
- Later nationalists treated 1857 as the beginning of the freedom struggle.

**Why the revolt failed**

- There was no all-India plan and no modern central leadership.
- Weapons and organisation were weaker than the Company’s.
- Many princes helped the British — **Patiala, Jind, Hyderabad, Nepal**, and Sikh chiefs in Punjab.
- The British used the **telegraph** and **railways** to move troops.
- Large parts of the South and Punjab did not join.

> **Exam note:** Crown rule begins in **1858**, not on 10 May 1857. Canning, not Dalhousie, was the first Viceroy.

## 5.8 Rani Lakshmibai

- She was the Rani of **Jhansi**. Her husband was **Gangadhar Rao**.
- They adopted **Damodar Rao**. **Dalhousie** refused to recognise him and annexed Jhansi under the **Doctrine of Lapse**.
- Statement 2 of **2024 Q148** is therefore **false**: Dalhousie did **not** accept her adopted son as heir.
- In 1858 she fought the British force under **Sir Hugh Rose**.
- After Jhansi fell she joined Tantia Tope, fought at Kalpi and Gwalior, and died on **17/18 June 1858** near **Gwalior**.

> **Exam note:** The **Rani Jhansi Regiment** of the INA was raised by **Subhas Chandra Bose**. It is not an 1857 fact.

---

## 5.9 Nana Sahib

- He was the adopted son of the last Peshwa, **Baji Rao II**, and lived at **Bithoor**.
- The British stopped his pension. This is a standard political grievance of 1857.
- He became the political leader of the revolt at **Kanpur**.
- **Tantia Tope** was his military commander. **Azimullah Khan** was his adviser.
- After the British recaptured Kanpur he fled towards **Nepal**. His later end is not clearly known.

> **Exam note:** Nana Sahib is **Kanpur**, not Bareilly.

---

## 5.10 Tantia Tope

- He was the main general of **Nana Sahib** at Kanpur.
- After Kanpur he fought with **Rani Lakshmibai** at **Kalpi** and **Gwalior**.
- He then carried on a guerrilla war.
- He was captured after betrayal and hanged at **Shivpuri** on **18 April 1859**.

> **Exam note:** Tantia Tope is a general, not the Nawab of Awadh and not the leader of Bareilly.

---

## 5.11 Kunwar Singh

- He was an aged zamindar of **Jagdishpur** in **Bihar** (Shahabad / Arrah region).
- He led the 1857 rising there against the British.
- He was wounded in later fighting and died on **26 April 1858**.
- **2024 Q148** statement 1 is **true**: he did lead the rebellion from Jagdishpur.

> **Exam note:** Kunwar Singh belongs to **Bihar**, not to Lucknow, Bareilly or Jhansi.

## 5.12 Begum Hazrat Mahal

- She was the wife of Nawab **Wajid Ali Shah** of Awadh, who had been deposed in 1856.
- During 1857 she led the revolt at **Lucknow** and in Awadh.
- Her son **Birjis Qadr** was placed on the masnad at Lucknow.
- After Lucknow fell she took shelter in **Nepal**.

> **Exam note:** Begum Hazrat Mahal is **Lucknow / Awadh**, not Bareilly.

## 5.13 Mangal Pandey

- He was a sepoy of the **34th Native Infantry** stationed at **Barrackpore**.
- On **29 March 1857** he attacked British officers. This is the first famous spark of 1857.
- He was hanged on **8 April 1857**.
- The 34th Native Infantry was later disbanded.
- His act is **not** the same as the Meerut rising of **10 May 1857**.

> **Exam note:** Mangal Pandey = **Barrackpore, 29 March**. Meerut = **10 May**.

## 5.14 Role of Awadh

- **Dalhousie** annexed Awadh in **1856**. The Resident was **Outram**.
- The official reason was **misgovernance**, not the Doctrine of Lapse.
- Nawab **Wajid Ali Shah** was sent to Calcutta.
- A large number of Bengal Army sepoys came from Awadh, so annexation hit the army directly.
- **Taluqdars** lost land after the new settlement. That is why Awadh saw a big **civilian** revolt around Lucknow, not only a sepoy mutiny.
- **2025 Q127** places “Acquisition of Awadh by the British” **before** the Indigo Revolt of 1859.

## 5.15 Role of Uttar Pradesh

Present-day Uttar Pradesh (then the North-Western Provinces plus Awadh) was the **main theatre** of 1857.

- **Meerut** — mass outbreak on **10 May 1857**.
- **Lucknow** — capital of Awadh; leader **Begum Hazrat Mahal**; rising on **4 June 1857**.
- **Kanpur** — **Nana Sahib** and **Tantia Tope**.
- **Bareilly** — **Khan Bahadur Khan**.
- **Allahabad** — **Maulvi Liaquat Ali**; later the Queen’s Proclamation of **1 November 1858**.
- **Faizabad** — **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah**.
- **Banaras** and Allahabad also saw British counter-terror under officers such as **Neill**. Rebel hold there was shorter.

In this region sepoys, peasants and taluqdars rose together more than in the South or Punjab.

> **Exam note:** Kunwar Singh is **Bihar**. Jhansi is **Bundelkhand**. Both are asked with UP centres, but they are not UP towns.

## Common Traps — Don't Fall For These

1. **Jhansi started 11 May 1857** → **June 1857** (2023 Q40 = **D**).
2. **Bareilly = Hazrat Mahal / Nana / Kunwar** → **Khan Bahadur Khan** (2023 Q46).
3. **Dalhousie accepted Lakshmibai’s heir** → **false** (2024 Q148 = **Only 1**).
4. **Revolt began at Delhi** → mass outbreak **Meerut 10 May**.
5. **Awadh annexed by Lapse** → **misrule, 1856**.
6. **Awadh annexed in 1857–58** → **1856**, a **cause**.
7. **Kunwar Singh = Jhansi/UP** → **Jagdishpur, Bihar**.
8. **Nana = Bareilly** → **Kanpur**.
9. **Zafar ran the army at Delhi** → **Bakht Khan** = field command.
10. **First Viceroy = Dalhousie** → **Canning**, 1858.
11. **Company ended on 10 May 1857** → **GOI Act 1858**.
12. **Rani died at Jhansi in 1857** → **Gwalior, June 1858**.
13. **INA Rani Jhansi Regiment = 1857** → **Bose / Azad Hind**.
14. **Pandey hanged at Meerut 10 May** → Barrackpore spark; hanged **8 April**.
15. **1857 = whole of India** → South, Punjab, many princes stayed out.
