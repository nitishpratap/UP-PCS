## 5.1 Long arc of people’s movements

- Hill society’s protests run from colonial **Coolie-Begar** and forest restrictions to Praja Mandal, Chipko, and the separate-state agitation.
- Social-reform strands such as **Dola-Palki** sit beside labour and forest struggles.
- The syllabus head “people’s movements … and other related aspects” covers both ecological movements and **Uttarakhand statehood** politics.

---

## 5.2 Coolie-Begar as a people’s movement

- Forced porterage under colonial rule produced a mass hill campaign centred on Kumaon.
- Leadership memory: **Badri Dutt Pandey** and associates; success associated with **Bageshwar**.
- Full meeting-place and Parishad chronology stay in Topic 4; here remember it as the classic early people’s struggle of the hills.

---

## 5.3 Dola-Palki movement

**Causes:** Upper-caste custom denied lower-caste bridegrooms the dignity of a bridal **palki (palanquin)** and related wedding honour in parts of Garhwal and Kumaon.

**Course:** Reformers and local activists organise against the insult of the **dola-palki** restriction and press for equal wedding dignity.

**Results:** The movement becomes a standard social-reform card of the hills and shows that “people’s movements” are not only anti-colonial or ecological.

---

## 5.4 Forest satyagraha and anti-liquor strands

**Forest satyagraha — Causes:** Colonial forest laws shrink village use of timber, fodder, and pasture.

**Course:** Hill communities defy reserved-forest rules through collective entry, grazing, and cutting remembered as forest satyagraha, especially in the Gandhian decades.

**Results:** The memory feeds later ecological politics and the Chipko vocabulary of village forest rights.

**Anti-liquor — Causes:** Temperance ideals and social harm of liquor shops in hill bazaars.

**Course:** Picketing and boycott of liquor shops ride with Non-cooperation / CDM-style constructive work.

**Results:** Anti-liquor remains a recurring social-movement strand beside forest and statehood politics.

---

## 5.5 Chipko movement — Cause, Course, Result

**Causes:** Commercial felling and contractor logging threaten Himalayan village forests, fuelwood, and watershed stability in Garhwal (then Uttar Pradesh).

**Course:**

- The modern Chipko wave opens at **Mandal (Chamoli) in 1973**.
- **Chandi Prasad Bhatt** organises through the **Dasholi Gram Swarajya Mandal (DGSM)** and village resistance to contractor axes.
- On **26 March 1974** at **Reni**, **Gaura Devi** leads village women who embrace trees and block loggers.
- **Sunderlal Bahuguna** carries Chipko’s message across the hills and into all-India environmental politics.
- Method memory: hugging trees (**chipko**) to stop felling.

**Results:** Large commercial clear-felling faces a lasting political check in the central Himalaya. Chipko becomes the textbook people’s ecological movement of Uttarakhand and must not be swapped with Karnataka’s later **Appiko**.

---

## 5.6 Uttarakhand Kranti Dal and statehood politics

**Causes:** Hill districts argue that plains-centred Uttar Pradesh neglects Himalayan development, identity, and administration.

**Course:**

- The **Uttarakhand Kranti Dal (UKD)** forms in **1979** as a regional party demanding a separate hill state.
- UKD and later broad fronts keep the separate-state demand alive through the 1980s and early 1990s.
- By **1994** the demand becomes a mass andolan with student–youth leadership and statewide marches.

**Results:** Regional-party politics and street mobilisation together push New Delhi and Lucknow toward creating a new hill state in **2000**.

---

## 5.7 Uttarakhand movement, 1994 — incident ratta

**Causes:** Demand for a separate hill state, administrative neglect narratives, and mobilisation after years of regional politics.

**Course (1994 flashpoints):**

- **Khatima incident — 1 September 1994**, remembered as **Black Day**, after police firing on statehood protesters.
- **Mussoorie incident — 2 September 1994** — firing the day after Khatima; do not accept **3 September** as the keyed date.
- Activists then push the campaign toward Delhi and the national capital’s attention.
- On the return route, the **Rampur Tiraha** episode near **Muzaffarnagar** unfolds on the night of **1–2 October 1994**.
- Coaching memory stresses police / PAC repression of busloads of Uttarakhand protesters at the Tiraha, with killings, beatings, and sexual violence reported in the andolan narrative.

**Results:** The October outrage nationalises the movement. Pressure continues until the separate state is created in **2000**.

---

## 5.8 State formation — Uttaranchal to Uttarakhand

**Causes:** Sustained hill agitation, UKD / multiparty pressure, and the 1994 martyrdom memory make a separate state politically unavoidable.

**Course:** Parliament carves the new state from Uttar Pradesh.

**Results:**

- The state is born on **9 November 2000** under the name **Uttaranchal**.
- The name changes to **Uttarakhand** with effect from **1 January 2007**.
- Keep the birth date and the rename year as two separate ratta cards.

## Common Traps

1. **Mussoorie = 3 September 1994** — **2 September**.
2. **Black Day = Muzaffarnagar** — Black Day pairs with **Khatima / 1 Sep**.
3. **Chipko = Coolie-Begar** — different centuries and issues.
4. **Bahuguna alone = Reni** — Reni village lead is **Gaura Devi**.
5. **State born as Uttarakhand in 2000** — birth name is **Uttaranchal**; rename **2007**.
6. **Appiko = Chamoli** — Appiko is Karnataka; Chipko is the Himalaya.
