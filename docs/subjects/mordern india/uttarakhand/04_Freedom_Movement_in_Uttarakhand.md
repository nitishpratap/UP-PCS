## 4.1 1857 and Uttarakhand

**Causes:** News of the plains rising and local anger against Company rule reach Kumaon and Garhwal. Hill society is not a single front — some belts rise while other garrisons stay loyal.

**Course:**

- In the **Champawat / Lohaghat** belt, **Kalu Mehra (Kalu Singh Mahara)** organises local fighters remembered as **Krantiveer / Bandukchi**.
- Coaching memory places ambushes around **Kali, Sui, and Gumdesh**, and a raid that burns British barracks at **Lohaghat (Chandmari)**.
- Associates remembered with him include **Anand Singh Fartyal** and **Bishan Singh Karayat**.
- British columns under the Kumaon administration crush the rising; Bastia is a standard defeat memory.

**Results:** The hill rising fails, but **Kalu Mehra** remains the named 1857 memory for Uttarakhand coaching. National leaders and centres stay in the shared 1857 chapter; this card only plants the hill echo.

---

## 4.2 Newspapers and nationalist opinion

- **Almora Akhbar** is the enduring nineteenth-century vernacular paper of Almora and a long critic of **begar**.
- **Badri Dutt Pandey** edits **Almora Akhbar** in the 1910s and turns it into a sharper nationalist voice.
- After the paper is shut under colonial pressure (about **1918**), he founds the weekly **Shakti** at **Almora** on **Vijayadashami, 15 October 1918**.
- **Shakti** carries Coolie-Begar reporting and Parishad politics into the early 1920s abolition campaign.
- Keep the press card beside Coolie-Begar: journalism → mass meeting at **Bageshwar** → rollback of forced labour.

---

## 4.3 Kumaon Parishad and early organisation

- The **Kumaon Parishad** organises hill political opinion from **1916** at **Almora**.
- Session venues in coaching order: Almora, Haldwani, Kotdwar, then the **fourth conference at Kashipur (1920)**.
- The Parishad links local grievances (begar, forests, representation) to the wider nationalist wave.

---

## 4.4 Home Rule at Dehradun

- In **1918** a branch of the **Home Rule League** opens at **Dehradun**.
- UKPCS 2025 keys the founder as **Swami Vicharanand Saraswati**.
- **Dayanand Saraswati** is chronologically impossible (d. 1883).
- **Tara Dutt Gairola** is a Garhwal reformer distractor on neighbouring stems, not this Home Rule key.

---

## 4.5 Coolie-Begar and Badri Dutt Pandey

### Causes

- **Coolie-Begar** is forced porterage and unpaid labour that colonial hill offices extract from villages.
- Road parties, officials on tour, and forest work all draw on this unpaid hill labour.

### Course

- In **1918**, **Badri Dutt Pandey** informs **Mahatma Gandhi** about the system; Series B provisional key places the meeting at **Calcutta**.
- The newspaper **Shakti** and Parishad leaders carry the campaign through **1920**.
- At the **Uttarayani** fair at **Bageshwar** in **January 1921**, thousands gather with **Badri Dutt Pandey**, **Hargovind Pant**, and others.
- Crowds throw begar registers into the **Saryu** as a public refusal of forced labour.
- The movement merges with Non-Cooperation pressure in the hills.

### Results

- The United Provinces authorities abolish the begar system for Kumaon in the early **1920s**.
- Coolie agencies, wages, and freer fuel–fodder rules follow as partial administrative relief.
- Gandhi’s later **1929** Kumaon visit is a separate chronology trap — do not use it as the 1918 meeting place.

## 4.6 Garhwal social–political associations

- **Tara Dutt Gairola** establishes the **Garhwal Union** and the **Garhwal Hitkarini Sabha**.
- These bodies sit with early twentieth-century Garhwali reform and nationalist networking.
- Keep them distinct from the Kumaon Parishad founding card (Almora 1916).

---

## 4.7 Peshawar incident and Chandra Singh Garhwali (1930)

**Causes:** Civil Disobedience reaches the North-West Frontier. **Khudai Khidmatgars** under **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** fill the streets of **Peshawar**. Colonial commanders order Indian troops to fire on an unarmed crowd.

**Course:**

- Men of the **Garhwal Rifles** under **Chandra Singh Garhwali (Vir Chandra Singh Garhwali)** refuse the order at **Peshawar (1930)**.
- The refusal is remembered with the **Qissa Khwani** repression memory of that year.
- Garhwali and fellow soldiers face court-martial, imprisonment, and dismissal.

**Results:** The episode becomes a national CDM symbol and a core Uttarakhand freedom memory. Pair it with Ghaffar Khan’s Red Shirts, but keep the refuse-to-fire name as **Chandra Singh Garhwali**.

---

## 4.8 Sridev Suman and the Tehri Praja Mandal

**Causes:** **Tehri** is a princely estate, not a British district. Subjects demand civil rights, responsible government, and an end to arbitrary princely rule — the same Praja Mandal wave that runs through many Indian states.

**Course:**

- The **Tehri Rajya Praja Mandal** organises the estate’s nationalist and responsible-government politics in the late 1930s.
- **Sridev Suman (Sri Dev Suman)** is the iconic organiser and prisoner of that struggle.
- Arrested by the Tehri state, he begins a prolonged **hunger strike** in jail.
- He dies in custody on **25 July 1944** and is remembered as the hunger-strike martyr of Tehri.

**Results:** Suman’s death hardens opposition to the Tehri monarchy and keeps the Praja Mandal demand alive into the merger years. Pair the estate origin card in [Tehri Estate](03_Tehri_Estate.md) with this freedom card.

---

## 4.9 Nagendra Saklani and the Tehri endgame

**Causes:** After 1947 the Tehri monarchy still resists full popular rule. Peasant and Praja Mandal radicals push for liberation of pockets of the estate.

**Course:**

- **Nagendra Saklani** works with peasant leaders such as **Daulat Ram** in the Kadakot stream and later at **Kirtinagar**.
- On **10–11 January 1948**, people raise the Tricolour and declare people’s administration at **Kirtinagar**.
- Royal forces try to retake the town; **Nagendra Saklani** and **Molu Bhardari** are killed on **11 January 1948**.

**Results:** The double martyrdom accelerates the collapse of Tehri princely power. The estate merges into the United Provinces / later Uttar Pradesh map (**1949** coaching memory). Do not swap Saklani’s 1948 death with Suman’s 1944 hunger strike.

---

## 4.10 Non-cooperation, CDM, and Quit India in the hills

- **Non-cooperation** echoes through Parishad politics, boycott language, and early anti-begar organisation in Kumaon and Garhwal.
- **Civil Disobedience** shows as forest-law defiance, liquor picketing, and the Peshawar / Garhwali card of **1930**.
- **Quit India (1942)** brings arrests of hill workers and keeps underground Congress / Praja Mandal networks alive into the 1940s.
- This is a short bridge card — full national CCR stays in the shared Gandhian chapters; here only plant the hill echo.

## Common Traps

1. **Dayanand founded Home Rule Dehradun** — died 1883.
2. **Fourth Parishad at Almora** — Almora is founding; fourth is Kashipur.
3. **Gandhi 1929 visit = 1918 Begar meeting** — different events.
4. **Raturi / Nautiyal founded Garhwal Union** — Tara Dutt Gairola.
5. **Shakti = Almora Akhbar** — Shakti is the 1918 successor weekly.
6. **Ghaffar Khan refused to fire at Peshawar** — Red Shirts leader; refuse-to-fire = Chandra Singh Garhwali.
7. **Saklani = 1944 hunger strike** — that card is Sridev Suman.
