## 1.1 Causes — why the Gorkhas entered the hills

- After consolidating power in Nepal under the Shah–Thapa system, Gorkha armies pushed west into the central Himalaya.
- Fragmented hill polities of **Kumaon (Chand)** and **Garhwal (Parmar)** invited successive campaigns.
- Control of trade routes, revenue villages, and strategic ridge lines between the Kali and the Yamuna drew Nepal’s expansion.
- By about **1790**, Gorkha power replaces Chand rule in Kumaon in the standard coaching chronology.

---

## 1.2 Course — occupation and the Anglo-Gorkha War

- Through the 1790s and early 1800s the Gorkhas occupy large parts of Kumaon and Garhwal and collect hill revenue.
- Harsh revenue demands and forced labour memories later feed British and nationalist narratives of Gorkha rule.
- Border friction with the East India Company after the Company’s rise in the Doab and Rohilkhand leads to the **Anglo-Gorkha War (1814–1816)**.
- In the **Dehradun military campaign of 1814**, **Major General Gillespie** leads the English column and dies assaulting **Nalapani (Kalanga)** fort.
- **Balbhadra Singh Thapa** defends Nalapani; the fort falls only after prolonged fighting.
- **Ochterlony** operates on the western / Sutlej front and forces the decisive military pressure that brings Nepal to terms.

---

## 1.3 Results — Sugauli and the new map

- The **Treaty of Sugauli** ends the war: Nepal cedes territories west of the **Kali** among other clauses.
- **Kumaon** and **eastern Garhwal** pass under British administration.
- **Sudarshan Shah** receives the restored western Garhwal nucleus that becomes the **Tehri** princely estate.
- Gorkha political rule in Uttarakhand ends; Gorkha cultural and military memory remains in coaching traps about forts and titles.

---

## 1.4 Administration ratta — courts under Gorkha rule

- The judge under Gorkha administration is called **Vichari** (also spelled Bichari).
- Associated judicial functionaries are remembered as **Sabha**.
- **Divya** means trial by ordeal (fire, water, and similar tests), not the name of a prompt-justice officer.
- UKPCS 2025 tested this three-statement set: Vichari and Sabha correct; Divya-as-officer wrong.

---

## Must-Score Facts

1. Gorkha entry into Kumaon ≈ **1790**; war with Company **1814–16**.
2. Gillespie dies at **Nalapani**; Balbhadra defends; Ochterlony ≠ Dehradun column.
3. Sugauli → British Kumaon / E. Garhwal + Tehri for Sudarshan Shah.
4. Judge = **Vichari**; workers = **Sabha**; **Divya** = ordeal.
5. Do not place Chand Char Aal facts inside Gorkha admin cards.

## Common Traps

1. **Divya = officer for prompt justice** → ordeal, not a post.
2. **Ochterlony died at Nalapani** → Gillespie did; Ochterlony fought elsewhere.
3. **Whole of Garhwal became British district in 1815** → Tehri remained princely.
4. **Gorkha rule begins in 1814** → 1814 is the Company war; occupation began earlier (~1790 in Kumaon).
