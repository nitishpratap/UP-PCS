## Consolidated — 45 Must-Score Facts

1. Blood is a specialized fluid connective tissue consisting of **~55% liquid Plasma** and **~45% cellular Formed Elements**; slightly alkaline with a normal physiological **pH of 7.35–7.45**; total blood volume in a healthy adult is **5 to 6 litres** (~7–8% of body weight).
2. Blood plasma is 90–92% water; plasma proteins include **Albumin** (maintains colloidal osmotic / oncotic pressure, ~4 g/dL), **Globulins** ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ — $\gamma$-globulins act as circulating **antibodies**), and **Fibrinogen** (essential for blood clotting); **$\text{Serum} = \text{Plasma} - \text{Clotting Factors (Fibrinogen)}$**.
3. **Erythrocytes (Red Blood Cells / RBCs)**: Normal count is **5.0–5.5 million / $\mathbf{mm^3}$** in males and 4.5–5.0 million / $\text{mm}^3$ in females; mature mammalian RBCs are **biconcave, circular, and enucleated** (lack nucleus, mitochondria, and Golgi bodies; **Camel and Llama are exceptions: their RBCs are oval and nucleated**).
4. Absence of mitochondria forces RBCs to generate ATP exclusively through **anaerobic glycolysis**, preventing them from consuming any of the oxygen they transport.
5. RBCs are produced in the red bone marrow (**Erythropoiesis**); stimulated by the glycoprotein hormone **Erythropoietin (EPO)** secreted by renal juxtaglomerular cells in response to hypoxia.
6. The average lifespan of a human red blood cell is **$\mathbf{\sim 120\text{ days}}$**; worn-out aged RBCs are destroyed by macrophages in the **Spleen**, which is universally designated as the **"Graveyard of RBCs"** (and acts as a reserve "Blood Bank").
7. **Haemoglobin ($Hb$)**: Conjugated chromoprotein; normal concentration is **$14–17\text{ g/dL}$** in adult men and **$12–15\text{ g/dL}$** in women; consists of the protein globin and 4 iron-porphyrin **Haem** groups containing **ferrous iron ($\mathbf{Fe^{2+}}$)**; imparts the red colour to blood.
8. Each molecule of haemoglobin can bind a maximum of **4 molecules of Oxygen ($O_2$)** to form bright red **Oxyhaemoglobin**; deoxygenated blood is darker bluish-red.
9. **Methemoglobin & Blue Baby Syndrome**: When ferrous iron ($Fe^{2+}$) of haemoglobin is oxidized into ferric iron ($Fe^{3+}$), it forms **Methemoglobin**, which cannot bind or carry oxygen; infants ingesting groundwater contaminated with **Nitrates ($NO_3^-$)** suffer from **Methemoglobinemia (Blue Baby Syndrome)**, presenting with acute cyanosis and chocolate-brown blood.
10. **Leukocytes (White Blood Cells / WBCs)**: Total count is **$6,000–8,000/\mathbf{mm^3}$**; nucleated, lack haemoglobin; divided into **Granulocytes** and **Agranulocytes**; the normal ratio of RBC to WBC is **$600:1$**.
11. **Granulocytes**:
    - **Neutrophils (60–65%)**: Most numerous; multi-lobed nucleus; primary phagocytic first-responders that engulf bacteria and cell debris.
    - **Eosinophils / Acidophils (2–3%)**: Bilobed nucleus; release histaminase; counts surge in **allergic reactions and intestinal helminth parasitic infections**.
    - **Basophils (0.5–1%)**: Least numerous; secrete **Histamine** (vasodilator in inflammation), **Serotonin**, and **Heparin** (natural anticoagulant).
12. **Agranulocytes**:
    - **Monocytes (6–8%)**: Largest WBCs; bean/kidney-shaped nucleus; enter tissues to transform into massive phagocytic **Macrophages**.
    - **Lymphocytes (20–25%)**: Large rounded nucleus; responsible for specific adaptive immunity: **B-lymphocytes** (produce humoral antibodies) and **T-lymphocytes** (mature in the thymus, execute cell-mediated immunity).
13. **Thrombocytes (Blood Platelets)**: Normal count is **$\mathbf{1.5–4.5\text{ lakh}}/\mathbf{mm^3}$**; non-nucleated protoplasmic cell fragments budded from giant marrow **Megakaryocytes**; lifespan 7–10 days; aggregate at wound sites and release **Thromboplastin** to trigger blood clotting; depleted drastically in dengue fever.
14. **Blood Clotting (Coagulation Cascade)**:
    - Step 1: Ruptured tissues and platelets release **Thromboplastin / Thrombokinase**.
    - Step 2: Prothrombin is converted to active **Thrombin** in the presence of **Calcium ions ($\mathbf{Ca^{2+}}$ / Factor IV)** and thrombokinase.
    - Step 3: Thrombin converts soluble **Fibrinogen** into insoluble **Fibrin polymer threads**, forming a network that traps blood cells to produce a clot (**Thrombus**).
15. **Vitamin K** is indispensable for hepatic synthesis of four essential clotting factors: **Factor II (Prothrombin), Factor VII, Factor IX, and Factor X**.
16. **Heparin** is a natural conjugated polysaccharide anticoagulant secreted by basophils and mast cells; prevents intravascular clotting inside healthy blood vessels.
17. In clinical blood banks, blood clotting in collection bags is prevented by adding calcium-chelating chemical anticoagulants: **Sodium Citrate, EDTA, or CPD (Citrate Phosphate Dextrose)**.
18. **ABO Blood Group System**: Discovered by Austrian biologist **Karl Landsteiner (1900–1901)**; blood groups ($A, B, AB, O$) are determined by specific oligosaccharide **antigens on the surface of RBC membranes** and complementary **antibodies in blood plasma**:
    - **Group A**: Has A antigen on RBCs; anti-B antibody in plasma.
    - **Group B**: Has B antigen on RBCs; anti-A antibody in plasma.
    - **Group AB**: Has **both A and B antigens** on RBCs; **NEITHER anti-A nor anti-B antibody** in plasma.
    - **Group O**: Has **NEITHER A nor B antigen** on RBCs; **BOTH anti-A and anti-B antibodies** in plasma.
19. Transfusion rules: **O Negative ($\mathbf{O^-}$)** is the **Universal Donor** for red-cell transfusions (lacks A, B, and Rh antigens, so donor RBCs cannot be attacked by recipient antibodies); **AB Positive ($\mathbf{AB^+}$)** is the **Universal Recipient** (lacks all anti-A, anti-B, and anti-Rh antibodies in plasma, so cannot attack transfused red cells).
20. **Rh Factor (Rhesus Antigen / Antigen D)**: Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner and Alexander S. Wiener (1940)** in the Rhesus macaque monkey; present on RBCs of ~85% of humans (Rh-positive); ~15% are Rh-negative.
21. **Erythroblastosis Fetalis (Haemolytic Disease of the Newborn)**: Occurs when an **Rh-negative mother** conceives an **Rh-positive fetus**; fetal Rh antigens leak into maternal blood during first delivery, sensitizing the mother to produce maternal anti-Rh antibodies (**IgG class**); during a subsequent Rh-positive pregnancy, maternal anti-Rh IgG crosses the placenta and hemolyses fetal erythrocytes, causing severe fatal anaemia, kernicterus, and hydrops fetalis; prevented by administering **Anti-D Rh-immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)** to the mother within 72 hours of delivering an Rh-positive baby.
22. **Bombay Blood Group (hh Phenotype)**: Rare blood phenotype discovered in Mumbai by **Dr. Y. M. Bhende (1952)**; individuals lack the universal precursor **H-gene**, so cannot biosynthesise A or B antigens; tests as Group O in routine ABO grouping, but serum contains anti-A, anti-B, and **anti-H antibodies**; can donate blood to anyone in the ABO system, but can **receive blood ONLY from another Bombay blood group donor**.
23. The human heart is a hollow, muscular, myogenic organ situated in the mediastinum of the thoracic cavity between the lungs, tilted slightly to the left; enclosed within a double-walled fibro-serous sac called the **Pericardium** filled with lubricating pericardial fluid.
24. The human heart contains **$\mathbf{4\text{ chambers}}$**: two thin-walled superior receiving chambers called **Atria (Auricles)** and two thick-walled inferior pumping chambers called **Ventricles**.
25. The **Left Ventricle** possesses the thickest, most muscular myocardial wall (about 3 times thicker than the right ventricle) because it must pump blood at high pressure into the **Aorta** across the entire systemic body circuit.
26. Internal Heart Valves:
    - **Tricuspid Valve**: Possesses 3 muscular cusps; guards the right atrio-ventricular orifice between the right atrium and right ventricle.
    - **Bicuspid Valve (Mitral Valve)**: Possesses 2 muscular cusps; guards the left atrio-ventricular orifice between the left atrium and left ventricle.
    - **Semilunar Valves**: Guard the exit of the right ventricle into the **Pulmonary Artery** (Pulmonary semilunar valve) and the exit of the left ventricle into the **Aorta** (Aortic semilunar valve); prevent backflow into ventricles during diastole.
27. Valve cusps are tethered to papillary muscles in the ventricular walls by tough, inextensible fibrous cords called **Chordae Tendineae ("Heart Strings")**, preventing cusps from inverting into atria during high-pressure ventricular systole.
28. **Nodal Conduction System (Myogenic Autonomic Rhythmicity)**:
    - **SA Node (Sino-Atrial Node / "Heart of Hearts")**: Located in the upper-right corner of the right atrium near the entry of the superior vena cava; generates intrinsic rhythmic action potentials at **70–75 beats/minute**; functions as the natural **Pacemaker of the Heart**.
    - **AV Node (Atrio-Ventricular Node)**: Located in the lower-left corner of the right atrium near the inter-atrial septum; acts as a relay station, introducing a crucial **0.1-second delay** to allow atria to empty completely before ventricular contraction begins; called the **Pacesetter**.
    - **Bundle of His (AV Bundle)**: Specialized muscle fascicle descending down the interventricular septum, bifurcating into Right and Left Bundle Branches.
    - **Purkinje Fibres**: Microscopic conduction fibres spreading rapidly throughout the ventricular myocardium to trigger coordinated contraction from the cardiac apex upward.
29. If the natural SA node pacemaker fails, an artificial battery-powered **Electronic Pacemaker** (invented by Wilson Greatbatch, containing a lithium-iodine battery) is surgically implanted under the skin to stimulate the heart electrically.
30. The **Cardiac Cycle** lasts **$\mathbf{0.8\text{ seconds}}$** at a normal resting rate of 72 beats/minute:
    - **Atrial Systole**: 0.1 second (atria contract, topping up ventricles).
    - **Ventricular Systole**: 0.3 second (ventricles contract at high pressure, pumping blood into aorta and pulmonary artery).
    - **Joint Diastole (Complete Cardiac Diastole)**: 0.4 second (all four chambers relax simultaneously and refill passively).
31. **Stroke Volume**: Volume of blood pumped out by each ventricle during each individual cardiac cycle = **$\mathbf{\sim 70\text{ mL}}$**.
32. **Cardiac Output**: Volume of blood pumped by each ventricle per minute:
    $$\text{Cardiac Output} = \text{Stroke Volume (70 mL)} \times \text{Heart Rate (72 bpm)} \approx \mathbf{5040\text{ mL/min}} \approx \mathbf{5\text{ Litres/min}}$$
33. **Heart Sounds Auscultated via Stethoscope**:
    - **First Heart Sound ("LUBB")**: Low-pitched, booming, longer duration (~0.15s); produced by the sudden, simultaneous **closure of the Atrioventricular Valves (Tricuspid and Bicuspid)** at the onset of ventricular systole.
    - **Second Heart Sound ("DUPP")**: Higher-pitched, sharp, shorter duration (~0.10s); produced by the rapid **closure of the Semilunar Valves** at the onset of ventricular diastole.
    - **Logic:** Heart sounds are produced by the snapping shut of heart valves, **not** by the contraction of muscle tissue itself.
34. **Electrocardiogram (ECG)**: Graphical recording of the electrical vector voltages generated by the heart during cardiac cycles; invented by Dutch physician **Willem Einthoven (Father of ECG, Nobel Prize 1924)**:
    - **P Wave**: Represents **Atrial Depolarization** (electrical excitation spreading from SA node across atria, preceding atrial contraction).
    - **QRS Complex**: Represents **Ventricular Depolarization** (rapid electrical spread triggering ventricular systole).
    - **T Wave**: Represents **Ventricular Repolarization** (return of ventricles from excitation to resting electrical state).
35. **Blood Pressure (BP)**: Lateral hydrostatic pressure exerted by circulating blood against the elastic walls of major systemic arteries; measured in **mm Hg** using a **Sphygmomanometer** placed over the brachial artery at the level of the heart:
    - **Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP)**: Peak arterial pressure during left ventricular contraction = **$\mathbf{120\text{ mm Hg}}$**.
    - **Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP)**: Minimum baseline arterial pressure during ventricular relaxation = **$\mathbf{80\text{ mm Hg}}$**.
    - **Standard Normal Resting BP**: **$\mathbf{120/80\text{ mm Hg}}$**.
    - **Pulse Pressure**: $\text{Systolic} - \text{Diastolic} = 120 - 80 = \mathbf{40\text{ mm Hg}}$.
    - **Hypertension**: Sustained resting arterial pressure $\ge 140/90\text{ mm Hg}$; damages kidneys, retina, and coronary vessels.
36. **Arteries vs Veins Anatomical Distinctions**:
    - **Arteries**: Carry blood **away** from the heart under high pulsatile pressure; walls are thick, muscular, and rich in elastic fibres (thick tunica media); deep-seated; **lack internal valves**; carry bright red oxygenated blood (**Exception: The Pulmonary Artery carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs**).
    - **Veins**: Carry blood **toward** the heart under low continuous pressure; walls are thin with a wide lumen; superficial; **contain crescent-shaped Semilunar Valves** to prevent backflow of blood under gravity; carry dark deoxygenated blood (**Exception: The Pulmonary Veins carry fully oxygenated blood from the lungs into the left atrium**).
    - **Capillaries**: Microscopic microscopic vessels whose walls consist of a single layer of endothelial cells; site of all nutrient, gas, and metabolic exchange between blood and tissue interstitial fluid.
37. **Double Circulation in Humans**: Blood passes through the heart **twice** to complete one full systemic circuit:
    - **Pulmonary Circuit**: $\text{Right Ventricle} \xrightarrow[\text{Deoxygenated}]{\text{Pulmonary Artery}} \text{Lungs} \xrightarrow[\text{Oxygenated}]{\text{Pulmonary Veins}} \text{Left Atrium}$.
    - **Systemic Circuit**: $\text{Left Ventricle} \xrightarrow[\text{Oxygenated}]{\text{Aorta}} \text{Body Tissues} \xrightarrow[\text{Deoxygenated}]{\text{Venae Cavae}} \text{Right Atrium}$.
38. **The Hepatic Portal System**: Specialized venous pathway in which venous blood draining the stomach, pancreas, spleen, and intestines does **not** return directly to the inferior vena cava, but is collected by the **Hepatic Portal Vein** and delivered to capillary sinusoids of the **Liver**; ensures absorbed toxins, glucose, and nutrients are processed and detoxified before reaching the systemic heart.
39. **Coronary Circulation**: The heart myocardium does not absorb nutrients or oxygen directly from blood passing through its chambers; it is supplied exclusively by the **Right and Left Coronary Arteries** (the very first branches arising from the root of the ascending aorta); coronary venous blood returns directly to the right atrium via the **Coronary Sinus**.
40. **Cardiovascular Pathology**:
    - **Atherosclerosis**: Deposition of cholesterol, calcium, and fibrous tissue forming hard atheromatous plaques within arterial walls, narrowing the lumen.
    - **Arteriosclerosis**: Hardening and loss of elasticity of arterial walls due to calcification.
    - **Angina Pectoris**: Severe acute substernal chest pain radiating to the left shoulder and arm caused by transient cardiac ischemia (inadequate oxygen supply to heart muscle).
    - **Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack)**: Sudden acute thrombotic occlusion of a coronary artery, causing irreversible ischemic necrosis (death) of a patch of myocardium.
41. **Lymphatic System & Lymph (Tissue Fluid / Interstitial Fluid)**:
    - As blood flows through arterial capillary beds, hydrostatic pressure forces fluid, water-soluble nutrients, and ions out through capillary pores into intercellular spaces, forming **Interstitial / Tissue Fluid**.
    - Interstitial fluid drained by specialized blind-ended **Lymphatic Capillaries** is termed **Lymph**.
    - **Chemical Composition of Lymph**: Straw-coloured, transparent fluid identical to blood plasma **minus red blood cells, platelets, and large plasma proteins (albumin/fibrinogen)**; contains abundant **Lymphocytes**.
42. **Circulation of Lymph**: Lymphatic capillaries coalesce into lymphatic vessels resembling veins with numerous valves; drain into two large terminal trunks: the **Right Lymphatic Duct** (drains right upper quadrant) and the **Thoracic Duct (Left Lymphatic Duct)** (drains remaining 75% of body); both empty directly into the **Subclavian Veins** at the base of the neck.
43. **Lymph Nodes**: Small oval glandular encapsulated filter organs distributed along lymphatic channels (in neck, armpits, groin, mesentery); filter lymph, trap circulating bacteria, viruses, and metastasizing cancer cells; packed with lymphocytes and plasma cells that mount adaptive immune responses.
44. **The Spleen (Pliha / तिल्ली)**:
    - Largest single mass of lymphatic tissue in the human body; located in the left hypochondriac region of the abdomen beneath the diaphragm.
    - Contains **Red Pulp** (vascular sinusoids that filter blood, remove old worn-out erythrocytes and blood-borne pathogens) and **White Pulp** (lymphoid sheaths housing T and B lymphocytes).
    - Functions: **Graveyard of RBCs**, **Blood Bank** (can contract to release stored red blood cells into systemic circulation during acute haemorrhage), and major antibody-producing lymphoid organ.
45. **Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)**: Emergency life-saving procedure comprising chest compressions (depth 5–6 cm at a rate of 100–120 compressions/minute) combined with artificial rescue breaths to manually preserve blood circulation to the brain and vital organs during cardiac arrest.

## 5.1 Cellular Elements of Blood & Haemostasis

- **The Liquid Connective Matrix (Plasma)**:
  - Straw-coloured, viscous fluid constituting ~55% of total blood volume.
  - Plasma proteins play fundamental roles in homeostasis:
    - **Serum Albumin (~60% of total plasma protein, 3.5–5.0 g/dL)**: Synthesised exclusively by the liver; provides 80% of total **colloid osmotic pressure (oncotic pressure $\sim 25\text{ mm Hg}$)** that holds water inside the vascular lumen, preventing plasma from extravasating into interstitial spaces. In liver cirrhosis or severe protein malnutrition, albumin synthesis collapses, causing widespread fluid retention (**Ascites and Oedema**).
    - **Serum Globulins (~36% of plasma protein)**: $\alpha$ and $\beta$ globulins act as transport carrier proteins (e.g. transferrin carries iron; ceruloplasmin carries copper); **$\gamma$-globulins (Immunoglobulins: IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD, IgE)** are synthesised by plasma B-cells and act as specific circulating defensive antibodies.
    - **Fibrinogen (~4% of plasma protein)**: Soluble high-molecular-weight clotting precursor synthesised by the liver.
- **Erythrocyte Morphometrics & Haemoglobin Chemistry**:
  - Mature human erythrocytes are non-nucleated biconcave discs ($7.2\text{ microns}$ diameter, $2.2\text{ microns}$ thick); biconcave geometry increases surface-area-to-volume ratio by 30%, maximizing gas diffusion efficiency and allowing flexibility to deform through narrow $5\text{-micron}$ capillary lumens.
  - **Erythropoiesis**: In early embryos, RBCs are formed in the **yolk sac**; from the 2nd to 7th month of gestation, formation shifts to the **liver and spleen**; from the 7th month onward and throughout adult life, formation is restricted exclusively to **red bone marrow** (in vertebrae, ribs, sternum, pelvis, and proximal ends of femur/humerus).
  - Normal destruction of RBCs: Aged, fragile RBCs are engulfed by reticuloendothelial macrophages of the spleen and liver; the globin protein is degraded into free amino acids; iron is salvaged and stored in the liver as **Ferritin**; the iron-free porphyrin ring is degraded into yellow-green bile pigments: **Biliverdin** $\rightarrow$ **Bilirubin**.
- **Leukocyte Differential Count & Diagnostic Signatures**:
  - **Neutrophils (60–65%)**: Nucleus has 3–5 interconnected lobes; cytoplasm has fine neutral-staining granules; motile via amoeboid diapedesis; active phagocytes.
  - **Eosinophils (2–3%)**: Bilobed spectacle-shaped nucleus; coarse spherical granules that stain bright pinkish-red with acidic dyes (Eosin); release histaminase to dampen allergic reactions; attack parasitic worms by secreting cytotoxic major basic protein (MBP).
  - **Basophils (0.5–1%)**: S-shaped nucleus; coarse irregular granules that stain intense dark blue with basic dyes (Methylene Blue); liberate **Histamine** during acute hypersensitivity (Type I anaphylaxis).
  - **Monocytes (6–8%)**: Largest leukocyte ($15–20\text{ microns}$); kidney-shaped or indented nucleus; circulate in blood for 24 hours before entering connective tissues to become **Macrophages** (e.g. Kupffer cells in liver, Alveolar macrophages in lungs, Microglia in brain, Osteoclasts in bone).
  - **Lymphocytes (20–25%)**: Small ($7–10\text{ microns}$); spherical, intensely staining nucleus surrounded by a thin rim of basophilic cytoplasm:
    - **B-Lymphocytes**: Mature in bone marrow; differentiate into antibody-secreting **Plasma Cells** upon antigen stimulation (Humoral Immunity).
    - **T-Lymphocytes**: Mature in the **Thymus** gland; differentiate into **Cytotoxic T-cells ($CD8^+$)** which destroy virus-infected and cancer cells, and **Helper T-cells ($CD4^+$)** which orchestrate the entire immune response (Cell-Mediated Immunity).
- **Thrombocytes & Haemostasis (The Clotting Cascade)**:
  - Platelets adhere to exposed sub-endothelial collagen via von Willebrand factor, activate, and release ADP and Thromboxane A2 to form an initial **Primary Platelet Plug**.
  - The intrinsic and extrinsic coagulation pathways converge on the common pathway:
    $$\text{Factor X} \xrightarrow[\text{Thrombokinase}]{\mathbf{Ca^{2+}}} \text{Active Factor Xa}$$
    $$\text{Prothrombin (Factor II)} \xrightarrow[\mathbf{Ca^{2+}}]{\text{Factor Xa + Va}} \mathbf{Thrombin (Factor IIa)}$$
    $$\text{Soluble Fibrinogen (Factor I)} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{Thrombin}} \mathbf{Insoluble Fibrin Monomers} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{Factor XIIIa}} \mathbf{Stable Cross-Linked Fibrin Clot}$$
  - Clot retraction occurs as platelets contract via thrombosthenin, squeezing out a clear pale-yellow fluid called **Serum**.

## 5.2 Cardiac Anatomy, Electrophysiology & Haemodynamics

- **Anatomical Organisation of the Human Heart**:
  - Weighs approximately $280–340\text{ grams}$ in adult males and $230–280\text{ grams}$ in females; approximately the size of an individual's clenched fist.
  - Heart wall comprises three distinct tissue coats:
    1. **Epicardium (Visceral Pericardium)**: Outer thin protective serous layer.
    2. **Myocardium**: Middle thick contractile layer composed of branching **Cardiac Muscle Fibres** connected by transverse **Intercalated Discs** containing low-resistance **Gap Junctions**, allowing the entire chamber to contract as a functional electrical syncytium.
    3. **Endocardium**: Inner smooth lining of simple squamous endothelium continuous with the endothelial lining of systemic blood vessels.
- **Internal Partitioning & Flow Architecture**:
  - The right and left atria are separated by the **Interatrial Septum**, which displays an oval shallow depression called the **Fossa Ovalis** (remnant of the embryonic *Foramen Ovale*, through which blood bypassed the unexpanded fetal lungs).
  - The right and left ventricles are separated by the thick, muscular **Interventricular Septum**.
  - **Blood Flow Sequence Through Chambers**:
    $$\text{Venae Cavae} \rightarrow \text{Right Atrium} \xrightarrow[\text{Valve}]{\text{Tricuspid}} \text{Right Ventricle} \xrightarrow[\text{Semilunar}]{\text{Pulmonary}} \text{Pulmonary Artery} \rightarrow \text{Lungs}$$
    $$\text{Lungs} \xrightarrow{\text{Pulmonary Veins}} \text{Left Atrium} \xrightarrow[\text{Mitral}]{\text{Bicuspid}} \text{Left Ventricle} \xrightarrow[\text{Semilunar}]{\text{Aortic}} \text{Aorta} \rightarrow \text{Systemic Organs}$$
- **Electrophysiology of the Cardiac Pacemaker**:
  - Cardiac muscle is **myogenic**: the electrical impulse originates intrinsically within specialized auto-rhythmic nodal cardiac muscle cells without requiring nervous stimulation.
  - **SA Node (Pacemaker)**: Possesses the fastest rate of spontaneous diastolic depolarization due to unique unstable resting membrane potential caused by slow inward leak of sodium ($Na^+$) ions via "funny channels" ($I_f$).
  - Autonomic Regulation:
    - **Sympathetic Nervous System (Cardiac Accelerator Nerves)**: Releases **Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine)**; binds to $\beta_1$-adrenergic receptors, increasing SA node firing rate, cardiac contractility, and cardiac output.
    - **Parasympathetic Nervous System (Vagus Nerve / CN X)**: Releases **Acetylcholine (ACh)**; binds to muscarinic receptors, hyperpolarizing the SA node and **slowing the heart rate (Bradycardia)**.
- **Physical Dynamics of Blood Pressure & Measurement**:
  - Blood pressure is highest in the Ascending Aorta (~$120\text{ mm Hg}$), declines progressively across large systemic arteries, drops sharply across high-resistance **Arterioles** (the primary site of peripheral vascular resistance), falls to $15–30\text{ mm Hg}$ in capillaries, and drops to near zero ($0–4\text{ mm Hg}$) in the right atrium (Central Venous Pressure / CVP).
  - **Clinical Sphygmomanometry**: An inflatable cuff is wrapped around the upper arm and inflated above systolic pressure to occlude the brachial artery; as the valve is released slowly, blood begins to spurt through the partially compressed vessel, generating turbulent tapping sounds audible through a stethoscope — these are the **Sounds of Korotkoff**. The first audible tap denotes **Systolic BP**; the complete disappearance of sound denotes **Diastolic BP**.

## Ghatnachakra Extra Drill: Cardiovascular Electrophysiology, Blood Elements & Transfusion
- **Cardiovascular Anatomy & Landmark Milestones**:
  - **Heart Mass & Cavity**: Fist-sized muscular organ weighing **250–300 grams**, situated in the **mediastinum** (middle chest compartment between lungs).
  - **Pericardium & Fluid**: Double-walled fibroserous sac containing **pericardial fluid**, which cushions the heart from external concussive trauma and provides friction-free lubrication during cardiac cycles.
  - **Cardiac Chambers**: 4 chambers = 2 thin-walled Atria (receiving) + 2 thick-walled Ventricles (pumping).
  - **Natural Pacemaker (SA Node)**: **Sinoatrial Node**, located in the upper subepicardial wall of the right atrium near superior vena cava entrance; initiates spontaneous electrical action potentials that pace the heartbeat.
  - **Heart Rate & Cycle**: Normal resting rate = **72–75 beats per minute (BPM)**; slows to 40–50 BPM during sleep. **Tachycardia** = resting rate >100 BPM; **Bradycardia** = resting rate <60 BPM.
  - **Rest Period of Heart**: The heart **rests between two successive heartbeats** (specifically during general ventricular and atrial diastole).
  - **Double Circulation**: In a single complete circuit, blood passes through the human heart **twice** (pulmonary circuit to oxygenate blood + systemic circuit to distribute blood), maximizing oxygenation efficiency and homeothermic heat control.
  - **Pioneering Cardiac Milestones**:
    - **William Harvey (1628)**: First accurately described systemic blood circulation and cardiac pumping mechanics.
    - **Dr. Christiaan Barnard (Dec 3, 1967)**: Performed the world's first successful human heart transplant (on patient Louis Washkansky at Groote Schuur Hospital, Cape Town, South Africa).
    - **Jarvik-7**: First permanent artificial mechanical heart, invented by **Robert Jarvik**; successfully implanted in patient Barney Clark in 1982.
- **Hemodynamics, Blood Pressure & Vessel Physiology**:
  - **Normal Blood Pressure**: **120/80 mm Hg** (Systolic pressure = 120 mm Hg during ventricular contraction; Diastolic pressure = 80 mm Hg during ventricular filling).
  - **Instrument**: Measured using a **Sphygmomanometer** (mercury column device) applied to the brachial artery, accompanied by a stethoscope to detect Korotkoff sounds.
  - **Atmospheric Pressure Comparison Fact**: Because clinical sphygmomanometers measure gauge pressure above atmospheric pressure, the actual absolute blood pressure inside human arteries is **greater than atmospheric pressure** (Absolute pressure $\approx 760 + 120 = 880 \text{ mm Hg}$).
  - **Age Dynamics**: Blood pressure generally **increases with age** due to progressive collagen cross-linking and arteriosclerotic loss of arterial wall elasticity.
  - **Arteries vs Veins Architecture**:
    - **Arteries**: Carry oxygenated blood under high pressure away from heart; thick tunica media with abundant elastic/muscular fibres; **contain no internal valves** (except aortic and pulmonary semilunar valves at ventricular exits). *Exception:* **Pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood to lungs.
    - **Veins**: Carry deoxygenated blood under low hydrostatic pressure toward heart; thin walls; possess internal **semilunar valves** to prevent retrograde backflow. *Exception:* **Pulmonary veins** are the **only veins carrying oxygenated blood** (from lungs to left atrium).
    - **Coronary Arteries**: Direct branches of the ascending aorta that supply oxygenated blood to the **myocardium** (heart muscle itself); occlusion causes acute myocardial infarction (heart attack).
    - **Nitric Oxide (NO) Vasodilation**: Endogenous gas synthesized by endothelial cells from L-arginine; diffuses into vascular smooth muscle, activating guanylyl cyclase to cause profound **vasodilation**, lowering systemic vascular resistance and blood pressure.
- **Blood Fluid Matrix & Formed Elements Dynamics**:
  - **Blood Physical Characteristics**: Fluid connective tissue; volume in a normal 70 kg adult = **5 to 6 Litres** (~7% of total body weight); specific gravity ~1.060; slightly alkaline with a strictly buffered pH of **7.35 to 7.45**.
  - **Plasma (~55% of blood volume)**: 91–92% water; 7–8% plasma proteins (**Albumin** maintains colloid oncotic pressure, **Fibrinogen** for haemostasis, **Globulins** for immune defense).
  - **Red Blood Cells (Erythrocytes)**:
    - Biconcave enucleated discs lacking nucleus, mitochondria, and ribosomes (maximizes intracellular space for haemoglobin packaging). **Mammalian Exception:** Camels and Llamas have oval, nucleated erythrocytes!
    - Adult count: ~5.0 to 5.5 million/$\mu L$; lifespan = **100–120 days**.
    - **Erythropoiesis**: Continual formation in **red bone marrow** (in liver/spleen in the embryo); stimulated by renal **Erythropoietin (EPO)**.
    - **Spleen**: Functions as the **"Blood Bank"** (stores reservoir of RBCs) and **"Graveyard of RBCs"** (macrophages phagocytose and recycle senescent erythrocytes).
    - **Haemoglobin**: Iron-bearing ($Fe^{2+}$) respiratory metalloprotein; normal range = **12–16 g/100 ml**; 1 g Hb binds **1.34 ml of $O_2$**.
    - **Earthworm Invertebrate Exception**: In earthworms (*Pheretima*, Phylum Annelida), **haemoglobin is dissolved freely in the blood plasma** rather than enclosed in corpuscles.
    - **Myoglobin**: Monomeric oxygen-storage heme protein present in skeletal muscle; possesses higher oxygen affinity than haemoglobin.
  - **White Blood Cells (Leukocytes)**:
    - Nucleated cells defending against pathogens; count = **4,000–11,000/$\mu L$**; diameter = **~0.007 mm ($7 \mu m$)**.
    - **Granulocytes**: **Neutrophils** (60–65%, 2–5 lobed nucleus, phagocytosis), **Eosinophils** (2–4%, bilobed nucleus, allergy/parasites), **Basophils** (0.5–1%, secrete anticoagulant **heparin** and vasodilator **histamine**).
    - **Agranulocytes**: **Lymphocytes** (20–25%, B-cells produce antibodies, T-cells cell-mediated immunity; **IgM** is the first antibody synthesized in primary infection) and **Monocytes** (4–8%, largest WBCs, differentiate into tissue macrophages).
    - **Leukemia**: Malignant neoplastic proliferation of immature, non-functional white blood cells in bone marrow.
  - **Platelets (Thrombocytes) & Haemostasis**:
    - Enucleated cytoplasmic discs; count = **1.5 to 4.5 lakh/$\mu L$**; lifespan = 8–10 days.
    - **Clotting Cascade**: Damaged tissue/platelets release thromboplastin $ \rightarrow$ activates prothrombin to **Thrombin** $ \rightarrow$ Thrombin converts soluble **Fibrinogen** into insoluble **Fibrin** meshwork.
    - **Alum Haemostasis**: Potash alum contains trivalent $Al^{3+}$ ions that rapidly neutralize the electronegative colloidal charges of blood plasma proteins, precipitating them to form an instantaneous physical clot over cuts and wounds.
    - **Artificial Plastic Blood**: Oxygen-carrying biocompatible plastic substitute developed at the **University of Sheffield, Britain**; stored as an easily transportable paste for emergency battlefield resuscitation regardless of blood type.
- **ABO Blood Groups, Rh Factor & Transfusion Immunology**:
  - Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner (1900)**; Rh factor discovered with **Alexander Wiener (1937)** in *Macaca mulatta* (Rhesus monkey).
  - **Antigens (Agglutinogens)**: Glycoproteins on RBC surfaces (Antigen A, Antigen B).
  - **Antibodies (Agglutinins)**: Immunoglobulins in plasma (anti-A / $\alpha$, anti-B / $\beta$).
  - **Universal Donor**: **Blood Group O (specifically O-negative, $O^-$)**: RBCs possess neither A, B, nor Rh antigens; can be safely infused into any recipient in critical emergency trauma without prior cross-matching.
  - **Universal Recipient**: **Blood Group AB (specifically AB-positive, $AB^+$)**: Plasma contains neither anti-A, anti-B, nor anti-Rh antibodies.
  - **Mendelian Inheritance of ABO Blood Groups**: Proved by Felix Bernstein (1924); governed by multiple alleles ($I^A, I^B, i$).
  - **Erythroblastosis Fetalis**: Hemolytic anemia of the newborn occurring when an **Rh-negative mother** conceives an **Rh-positive fetus** from an Rh-positive father. Maternal anti-Rh IgG antibodies cross the placenta during subsequent pregnancies, lysing fetal RBCs.

## Common Traps & Negative-Marking Eliminators

1. **RBC vs WBC lifespan**: RBC ~**120 days**; many WBCs are short-lived.
2. **Universal donor/recipient**: O negative ≈ universal donor; AB positive ≈ universal recipient — Rh matters.
3. **Pacemaker**: **SA node** is the natural pacemaker — not AV node (AV is relay).
4. **Pulmonary artery**: Carries **deoxygenated** blood — the classic vessel trap.
5. **Pulmonary vein**: Carries **oxygenated** blood to the left atrium.
6. **Systole vs diastole**: Systole = contraction; diastole = relaxation.
7. **Lymph**: Returns tissue fluid; contains lymphocytes — it is **not** the same as blood plasma with RBCs.
8. **Haemophilia**: Clotting-factor disorder — not a low-RBC anaemia by definition.
9. **Blood group antigen**: Group A has A antigen; antibodies are anti-B in plasma.
10. **ECG waves**: P = atrial depolarisation; QRS = ventricular depolarisation; T = ventricular repolarisation.
