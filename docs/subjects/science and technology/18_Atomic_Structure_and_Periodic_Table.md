## Consolidated — 50 Must-Score Facts

1. **Subatomic Mass & Charge Hierarchy:**
   - **Electron:** Discovered by **J.J. Thomson (1897)** via cathode ray discharge tubes. Charge $= -1.602 \\times 10^{-19}\\text{ C}$; Mass $= 9.109 \\times 10^{-31}\\text{ kg} = 0.000548\\text{ u}$ (approx. **$1/1836\\text{th}$ of proton mass**). Charge-to-mass ratio ($e/m$) $= 1.7588 \\times 10^{11}\\text{ C/kg}$.
   - **Proton:** Observed as canal rays/anode rays by Eugen Goldstein (1886) and identified as the hydrogen nucleus by **Ernest Rutherford (1919)**. Charge $= +1.602 \\times 10^{-19}\\text{ C}$; Mass $= 1.6726 \\times 10^{-27}\\text{ kg} = 1.00727\\text{ u}$.
   - **Neutron:** Discovered by **James Chadwick in 1932** by bombarding beryllium foils with alpha particles ($^9_4Be + ^4_2He \\to ^{12}_6C + ^1_0n$). Charge $= 0$ (neutral); Mass $= 1.6749 \\times 10^{-27}\\text{ kg} = 1.00866\\text{ u}$. **The neutron is the heaviest subatomic particle in an ordinary atom.**
2. **Rutherford's Gold Foil $\\alpha$-Scattering Experiment (1911):** Bombarded a thin gold foil ($100\\text{ nm}$ thick) with fast $\\alpha$-particles:
   - Most $\\alpha$-particles passed straight through undeflected $\\implies$ **most of the atomic volume is empty space**.
   - A few deflected at large angles, and roughly 1 in 20,000 bounced back ($180^\\circ$) $\\implies$ all positive charge and nearly all atomic mass is concentrated in a tiny central core called the **Nucleus**.
   - Nuclear diameter is $\\approx 10^{-15}\\text{ m}$ ($1\\text{ Fermi}$), whereas atomic diameter is $\\approx 10^{-10}\\text{ m}$ ($1\\text{ \\AA}$). The atom is $100,000\\text{ times larger}$ than its nucleus.
3. **Bohr's Quantum Postulates (1913):**
   - Electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain discrete, non-radiating circular orbits called **stationary states**.
   - Angular momentum of revolving electron is an integral multiple of $h / 2\\pi$:
     $$L = mvr = \\frac{nh}{2\\pi} \\quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \\dots)$$
   - Radiation is emitted or absorbed only when an electron jumps from one stationary orbit to another: $\\Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = h\\nu$.
4. **Four Quantum Numbers:**
   - **Principal Quantum Number ($n = 1, 2, 3, \\dots$ / K, L, M, N):** Dictates main energy level, electron shell, and average distance from nucleus. Max electrons in shell $= 2n^2$.
   - **Azimuthal / Orbital Angular Momentum ($l = 0\\text{ to } n-1$):** Dictates orbital shape ($l=0 \\to s$ spherical; $l=1 \\to p$ dumbbell; $l=2 \\to d$ double-dumbbell; $l=3 \\to f$ complex).
   - **Magnetic Quantum Number ($m_l = -l\\text{ to } +l$):** Dictates spatial orientation of orbitals in 3D space. Number of orbitals in a subshell $= 2l + 1$.
   - **Spin Quantum Number ($m_s = +1/2\\text{ or } -1/2$):** Dictates intrinsic electron spin orientation (clockwise or counter-clockwise).
5. **Aufbau Principle:** Electrons occupy orbitals of the **lowest available energy level first** before occupying higher levels. Governed by the **$(n + l)$ rule**:
   - Orbitals with lower $(n + l)$ value fill first (e.g., $4s$ with $n+l = 4+0=4$ fills before $3d$ with $n+l = 3+2=5$).
   - If two orbitals share the same $(n + l)$ value, the one with lower $n$ fills first ($3d$ fills before $4p$).
6. **Pauli's Exclusion Principle (1925):** **No two electrons in the same atom can possess the exact same set of all four quantum numbers.** Consequently, an orbital can hold a maximum of **two electrons**, and they must have **opposite (antiparallel) spins** ($\uparrow\downarrow$).
7. **Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity:** Electron pairing in degenerate orbitals belonging to the same subshell ($p, d, f$) **cannot occur until each orbital is singly occupied with parallel spins** ($\uparrow$, $\uparrow$, $\uparrow$).
8. **Anomalous Electronic Configurations of Chromium and Copper:**
   - **Chromium ($Z=24$):** Expected $[Ar] 3d^4 4s^2$; Actual $= [Ar] 3d^5 4s^1$.
   - **Copper ($Z=29$):** Expected $[Ar] 3d^9 4s^2$; Actual $= [Ar] 3d^{10} 4s^1$.
   - *Reason:* **Completely filled ($d^{10}$) and exactly half-filled ($d^5$) subshells possess extra thermodynamic stability** due to symmetrical electron distribution and maximum quantum exchange energy.
9. **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element having the **same atomic number ($Z$) but different mass numbers ($A$)**. Identical chemical properties, different nuclear stability.
   - Hydrogen Isotopes: Protium ($^1_1H$, 0 neutrons), Deuterium ($^2_1H$, 1 neutron), Tritium ($^3_1H$, 2 neutrons, radioactive).
10. **Isobars:** Atoms of different chemical elements having the **same mass number ($A$) but different atomic numbers ($Z$)** (e.g., $^{40}_{18}Ar$ and $^{40}_{20}Ca$). Different chemical and physical properties.
11. **Isotones:** Atoms having the **exact same number of neutrons ($N = A - Z$)** but different atomic numbers (e.g., $^{14}_6C$ and $^{16}_8O$, both having 8 neutrons).
12. **Isoelectronic Species:** Atoms, molecules, or ions containing the **exact same total number of electrons** (e.g., $N^{3-}, O^{2-}, F^-, Ne, Na^+, Mg^{2+}, Al^{3+}$, each having 10 electrons).
    - *Ionic Radius Trend:* Among isoelectronic ions, **ionic radius decreases as positive nuclear charge ($Z$) increases**:
      $$N^{3-} (1.71\\text{ \\AA}) > O^{2-} (1.40\\text{ \\AA}) > F^- (1.33\\text{ \\AA}) > Na^+ (1.02\\text{ \\AA}) > Mg^{2+} (0.72\\text{ \\AA}) > Al^{3+} (0.53\\text{ \\AA})$$
13. **Dobereiner's Law of Triads (1829):** Arranged elements in groups of three with similar chemical properties. The atomic weight of the middle element is approximately the arithmetic mean of the other two:
    - Triad 1: Lithium ($7$), Sodium ($23$), Potassium ($39$) $\\to (7+39)/2 = 23$.
    - Triad 2: Calcium ($40$), Strontium ($88$), Barium ($137$).
    - Triad 3: Chlorine ($35.5$), Bromine ($80$), Iodine ($127$).
14. **Newlands' Law of Octaves (1864):** English chemist John Newlands arranged elements in order of increasing atomic weight; every **eighth element showed properties similar to the first**, analogous to musical musical notes (Sa, Re, Ga, Ma...). **Valid only up to Calcium ($Z=20$)**.
15. **Mendeleev's Periodic Law (1869):** Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev stated: *"The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their **Atomic Weights**."*
    - Contained 63 elements arranged in 8 Groups and 6 Periods.
    - Correctly predicted undiscovered elements and left gaps: **Eka-Boron (Scandium)**, **Eka-Aluminum (Gallium)**, and **Eka-Silicon (Germanium)**.
    - Flaws: Inverted atomic weights ($Te$ before $I$, $Co$ before $Ni$); no fixed position for Hydrogen; anomalous placement of isotopes.
16. **Modern Periodic Law (Henry Moseley, 1913):** English physicist Henry Moseley measured characteristic X-ray frequencies ($\nu$) emitted by elements under electron bombardment, discovering $\\sqrt{\\nu} = a(Z - b)$. Proved that **Atomic Number ($Z$) is more fundamental than atomic weight**:
    *"The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their **Atomic Numbers**."*
17. **Architecture of the Modern Long Form Periodic Table:** Based on Bohr's electronic configurations:
    - **18 Vertical Columns** called **Groups**.
    - **7 Horizontal Rows** called **Periods**.
    - Divided into 4 blocks ($s, p, d, f$) based on the subshell receiving the differentiating valence electron.
18. **Period Breakdown:**
    - Period 1: Very short (2 elements: H, He).
    - Period 2 & 3: Short (8 elements each: Li to Ne; Na to Ar).
    - Period 4 & 5: Long (18 elements each: K to Kr; Rb to Xe).
    - Period 6: Very long (32 elements: Cs to Rn; includes Lanthanides $Z=57\\text{--}71$).
    - Period 7: Incomplete/complete (32 elements: Fr to Og; includes Actinides $Z=89\\text{--}103$).
19. **Magic Numbers in the Periodic Table:** The electron capacities of successive shells that confer exceptional nuclear and electronic stability: **2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32**.
20. **Groups and Chemical Families:**
    - Group 1: **Alkali Metals** ($Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr$) — configuration $ns^1$; highly reactive, soft, low melting points, store in kerosene.
    - Group 2: **Alkaline Earth Metals** ($Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra$) — configuration $ns^2$.
    - Groups 3 to 12: **Transition Metals ($d$-block)** — partially filled $d$-orbitals; exhibit variable oxidation states, form colored catalytic compounds.
    - Group 15: **Pnictogens** ($N, P, As, Sb, Bi$).
    - Group 16: **Chalcogens** ($O, S, Se, Te, Po$) — 'ore-forming' elements.
    - Group 17: **Halogens** ($F, Cl, Br, I, At$) — 'salt-producers'; $ns^2 np^5$; highly electronegative.
    - Group 18: **Noble Gases / Inert Gases** ($He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn, Og$) — completely filled $ns^2 np^6$ (except He $1s^2$); zero valency, highest ionization energies.
21. **Inner Transition Elements ($f$-block):** Placed in two separate rows at the bottom:
    - **Lanthanides (4f series):** 14 elements from Cerium ($Z=58$) to Lutetium ($Z=71$), following Lanthanum ($Z=57$).
    - **Actinides (5f series):** 14 elements from Thorium ($Z=90$) to Lawrencium ($Z=103$), following Actinium ($Z=89$). **All actinides are radioactive.**
22. **Lanthanide Contraction:** The steady, progressive decrease in atomic and ionic radii of Lanthanide elements ($La^{3+} \\to Lu^{3+}$) with increasing atomic number.
    - *Cause:* Extremely poor shielding effect of diffused inner $4f$ electrons against increasing nuclear charge ($Z_{\\text{eff}}$), pulling the outer $6s$ shell inward.
    - *Consequence:* Elements of the 2nd and 3rd transition series have virtually identical atomic radii: **Zirconium ($Zr, 1.60\\text{ \\AA}$) and Hafnium ($Hf, 1.59\\text{ \\AA}$)**, and **Niobium ($Nb$) and Tantalum ($Ta$)**, making their chemical separation difficult.
23. **Atomic Radius Periodic Trend:**
    - **Across a Period (Left to Right):** **Decreases**. Principal shell $n$ remains constant while nuclear charge ($Z$) increases, pulling electrons closer.
    - **Down a Group (Top to Bottom):** **Increases**. New electron shells are added, increasing screening/shielding.
    - Smallest neutral atom: **Helium ($He$)** / Hydrogen.
    - Largest neutral atom: **Cesium ($Cs$)** / Francium.
24. **Ionization Energy / Enthalpy ($IE$):** Minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound valence electron from an isolated neutral gaseous atom in its ground state:
    $$X(g) + IE_1 \\longrightarrow X^+(g) + e^-$$
    - **Across a Period:** **Increases** (higher $Z_{\\text{eff}}$, smaller radius).
    - **Down a Group:** **Decreases** (larger radius, valence electron further away).
    - Highest $IE_1$ in the Periodic Table: **Helium ($He$, $2372\\text{ kJ/mol}$)**.
    - Lowest $IE_1$ in the Periodic Table: **Cesium ($Cs$, $376\\text{ kJ/mol}$)**.
25. **Ionization Energy Anomalies (High-Yield UPPCS Traps):**
    - **$IE_1(\\text{Beryllium}) > IE_1(\\text{Boron})$:** $Be$ ($1s^2 2s^2$) has a fully filled stable $2s$ subshell, requiring more energy to remove an electron than $B$ ($1s^2 2s^2 2p^1$), where removing the single $2p$ electron leaves a stable $2s^2$ core.
    - **$IE_1(\\text{Nitrogen}) > IE_1(\\text{Oxygen})$:** $N$ ($1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$) has an exactly half-filled $2p$ subshell with extra quantum stability, making its first ionization energy higher than $O$ ($1s^2 2s^2 2p^4$).
26. **Electron Gain Enthalpy / Electron Affinity ($EA$):** Energy released when an electron is added to an isolated neutral gaseous atom:
    $$X(g) + e^- \\longrightarrow X^-(g) + \\text{Energy}$$
    - **Across a Period:** Generally becomes more negative / **increases**.
    - **Down a Group:** Generally **decreases**.
    - **The Fluorine vs Chlorine Trap:** **Chlorine ($Cl$) has a HIGHER electron affinity than Fluorine ($F$)** ($Cl = -349\\text{ kJ/mol}$ vs $F = -328\\text{ kJ/mol}$). Fluorine's tiny $2p$ subshell has extremely high electron density; incoming electrons suffer severe inter-electronic repulsion, lowering affinity. Sequence: **$Cl > F > Br > I$**.
27. **Electronegativity ($EN$):** The relative tendency of an atom in a chemical molecule to attract the shared pair of bonding electrons toward itself. Dimensionless property measured on the **Pauling Scale**.
    - **Across a Period:** **Increases** from alkali metals to halogens.
    - **Down a Group:** **Decreases**.
    - **Most Electronegative Element:** **Fluorine ($EN = 4.0$)**, followed by **Oxygen ($3.5$)**, **Nitrogen ($3.0$)**, and **Chlorine ($3.0$)**.
    - Least Electronegative / Most Electropositive: **Cesium / Francium ($0.7$)**.
28. **Valency Across a Period:** Valency with respect to hydrogen increases from **$1\\text{ to } 4$ and then decreases back to $0$** ($NaH=1, MgH_2=2, AlH_3=3, SiH_4=4, PH_3=3, H_2S=2, HCl=1, Ar=0$). Valency with respect to oxygen increases from $1\\text{ to } 7$ ($Na_2O \\to Cl_2O_7$). Valency remains constant down a group.
29. **Metallic vs Non-Metallic Character:**
    - Metallic character (electropositivity, ability to lose electrons) **decreases across a period** and **increases down a group**.
    - Non-metallic character (electronegativity, ability to gain electrons) **increases across a period** and **decreases down a group**.
    - Nature of Oxides: Metallic oxides are **basic** ($Na_2O, CaO$); non-metallic oxides are **acidic** ($SO_2, CO_2, P_2O_5$); metalloid/intermediate oxides are **amphoteric** ($Al_2O_3, ZnO, PbO$).
30. **Diagonal Relationship:** Light elements of the second period share surprising chemical similarities with diagonally adjacent elements of the third period due to nearly identical charge-to-size ratios (ionic potential):
    - **Lithium ($Li$) resembles Magnesium ($Mg$)**: Both form normal oxides, decompose carbonates on heating, and have insoluble fluorides.
    - **Beryllium ($Be$) resembles Aluminum ($Al$)**: Both form amphoteric oxides and covalently bonded chlorides that sublime.
    - **Boron ($B$) resembles Silicon ($Si$)**: Both form covalent polymers, acidic oxides, and hydrides that spontaneously ignite.
31. **Metalloids (Semimetals):** Elements with border-line properties between metals and non-metals, lining the staircase divide: **Boron (B), Silicon (Si), Germanium (Ge), Arsenic (As), Antimony (Sb), Tellurium (Te), and Polonium (Po)**.
32. **Physical State of Elements at Room Temperature ($25^\\circ\\text{C}$):**
    - **Liquids (Only Two Elements):** **Mercury ($Hg$, metal)** and **Bromine ($Br_2$, non-metal)**. Elements melting slightly above room temperature: Gallium ($29.7^\\circ\\text{C}$), Cesium ($28.4^\\circ\\text{C}$), and Francium ($27^\\circ\\text{C}$).
    - **Gases (11 Elements):** Hydrogen ($H_2$), Nitrogen ($N_2$), Oxygen ($O_2$), Fluorine ($F_2$), Chlorine ($Cl_2$), and all six Noble Gases ($He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn$).
    - **Solids:** All remaining $\approx 105\\text{ elements}$.
33. **Most Abundant Elements in the Earth's Crust (by Weight):**
    $$\\text{Oxygen } (46.6\\%) > \\text{Silicon } (27.7\\%) > \\text{Aluminum } (8.1\\%) > \\text{Iron } (5.0\\%) > \\text{Calcium } (3.6\\%) > \\text{Sodium } (2.8\\%) > \\text{Potassium } (2.6\\%) > \\text{Magnesium } (2.1\\%)$$
    - **Most abundant element in crust:** **Oxygen ($46.6\\%$, non-metal)**.
    - **Most abundant metalloid in crust:** **Silicon ($27.7\\%$)**.
    - **Most abundant metal in crust:** **Aluminum ($8.1\\%$)**, followed by Iron ($5.0\\%$).
34. **Most Abundant Elements in the Human Body (by Weight):**
    $$\\text{Oxygen } (65\\%) > \\text{Carbon } (18.5\\%) > \\text{Hydrogen } (9.5\\%) > \\text{Nitrogen } (3.2\\%) > \\text{Calcium } (1.5\\%) > \\text{Phosphorus } (1.0\\%)$$
    - **Most abundant element in human body:** **Oxygen ($65\\%$)**.
    - **Most abundant metal in human body:** **Calcium ($1.5\\%$)**, located primarily in bones and teeth as hydroxyapatite.
35. **Most Abundant Element in the Entire Universe:** **Hydrogen ($\approx 75\\%$)**, followed by **Helium ($\approx 24\\%$)**.
36. **Most Abundant Gas in the Earth's Atmosphere:** **Nitrogen ($78.08\\%$)**, followed by **Oxygen ($20.95\\%$)**, **Argon ($0.93\\%$, most abundant noble gas)**, and **Carbon Dioxide ($0.04\\%$)**.
37. **Lightest and Densest Elements:**
    - **Lightest element & gas:** **Hydrogen ($H_2$)**.
    - **Lightest metal:** **Lithium ($Li$, density $0.534\\text{ g/cm}^3$)**, floats on water and petroleum.
    - **Densest element / metal:** **Osmium ($Os$, density $22.59\\text{ g/cm}^3$)**, closely followed by Iridium ($Ir, 22.56\\text{ g/cm}^3$).
38. **Hardest Natural vs Synthetic Substances:**
    - Hardest natural substance: **Diamond** (allotrope of pure Carbon, Mohs hardness 10).
    - Hardest metal: **Tungsten / Chromium**.
    - Highest melting point of all elements: **Carbon (sublimes at $\approx 3825^\\circ\\text{C}$)**. Highest melting point among metals: **Tungsten ($3422^\\circ\\text{C}$)**.
39. **Lowest Melting Point Element:** **Helium ($-272.2^\\circ\\text{C} = 0.95\\text{ K}$ at $25\\text{ atm}$ pressure)**; cannot freeze into solid at standard atmospheric pressure even at absolute zero.
40. **Coinage Metals:** Group 11 transition elements used historically for minting currency: **Copper (Cu), Silver (Ag), and Gold (Au)**.
41. **Noble Metals:** Metals that resist oxidation, corrosion, and acid attack: **Gold (Au), Platinum (Pt), Silver (Ag), and Rhodium (Rh)**. Dissolve only in **Aqua Regia** ($3:1$ concentrated $HCl$ and $HNO_3$).
42. **Strategic Metals:** Metals essential for national defense, aerospace, and energy independence: **Titanium (Ti, 'Metal of the Future'), Zirconium (Zr), Chromium (Cr), Manganese (Mn)**.
43. **Liquid Metal at Room Temperature:** **Mercury ($Hg$, atomic number 80)**. Has low melting point ($-38.83^\\circ\\text{C}$) due to relativistic contraction of the filled $6s^2$ shell resisting metallic bonding.
44. **Best Electrical Conductors:** **Silver ($Ag$) > Copper ($Cu$) > Gold ($Au$) > Aluminum ($Al$)**. Silver is not used for domestic power lines solely due to high cost.
45. **Poor Conductors Among Metals:** **Lead ($Pb$)** and **Mercury ($Hg$)** are the poorest conductors of heat and electricity among metals.
46. **Alkali Metals Stored in Liquid:** Sodium ($Na$) and Potassium ($K$) react violently with atmospheric moisture and oxygen ($2Na + 2H_2O \\to 2NaOH + H_2 \\uparrow$ with explosive heat), catching fire. They are preserved by immersing in **kerosene oil**.
47. **White Phosphorus Storage:** White phosphorus ($P_4$) has a very low ignition temperature ($30^\\circ\\text{C}$) and undergoes spontaneous combustion in air, emitting dense white smoke. It is **stored submerged under water** (in which it is insoluble).
48. **Liquid Non-Metal:** **Bromine ($Br_2$, atomic number 35)** is the only non-metallic element that exists as a fuming reddish-brown liquid at room temperature ($25^\\circ\\text{C}$).
49. **Non-Metal Exhibiting Metallic Luster:** **Iodine ($I_2$)** forms dark violet-black lustrous crystals that sublime upon gentle heating into violet vapors. **Diamond** and **Graphite** also exhibit distinctive luster.
50. **Non-Metal That Conducts Electricity:** **Graphite** (allotrope of Carbon). Each carbon atom is $sp^2$ hybridized, bonded to three neighbors in planar hexagonal sheets, leaving one **delocalized free $\\pi$-electron** per atom to drift freely under electric potential.

## Confused Pairs

| Concept A | Concept B | Razor-Sharp Distinguishing Criteria & Exam Traps |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electron Affinity** | **Electronegativity** | Electron affinity is an **absolute thermodynamic energy released** when an electron is added to an isolated neutral gaseous atom ($X + e^- \\to X^-$; unit: $\\text{kJ/mol}$). Electronegativity is a **relative qualitative tendency** of a bonded atom to attract shared electron pairs in a chemical bond (dimensionless Pauling scale). Highest EA = **Chlorine**; Highest EN = **Fluorine**. |
| **Atomic Number ($Z$)** | **Mass Number ($A$)** | Atomic number ($Z$) equals the **number of protons** in the nucleus (defines the element; integer without units). Mass number ($A$) equals total count of nucleons (**protons + neutrons**). Atoms with same $Z$ and different $A$ are **Isotopes**; atoms with same $A$ and different $Z$ are **Isobars**. |
| **Orbits (Bohr)** | **Orbitals (Wave Mechanics)** | Orbit is a deterministic 2D circular planar path of an electron around the nucleus (violates Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle; max electrons $= 2n^2$). Orbital is a 3D probabilistic spatial region where probability of finding an electron is maximum ($>90\\%$; max electrons $= 2$). |
| **Mendeleev's Law** | **Modern Periodic Law** | Mendeleev: Properties are periodic functions of **Atomic Weights**. Modern (Moseley): Properties are periodic functions of **Atomic Numbers ($Z$)**. Moseley resolved Mendeleev's cobalt-nickel and tellurium-iodine inversions. |
| **Abundance: Crust vs Body** | **Most Abundant Metal** | In Earth's crust: Most abundant element is **Oxygen ($46.6\\%$)**; most abundant metal is **Aluminum ($8.1\\%$)**. In human body: Most abundant element is **Oxygen ($65\\%$)**; most abundant metal is **Calcium ($1.5\\%$)**. |
| **Liquid Metal** | **Liquid Non-Metal** | Liquid metal at room temperature is **Mercury ($Hg$, metal)**. Liquid non-metal at room temperature is **Bromine ($Br_2$, non-metal)**. |
| **$s$-Block** | **$d$-Block** | $s$-block: Groups 1 and 2; valence electrons in outermost $s$-subshell; fixed valency ($+1, +2$); highly electropositive metals. $d$-block: Groups 3 to 12; electrons filling inner $(n-1)d$ subshell; variable oxidation states ($Fe^{2+}, Fe^{3+}$); form colored coordination complexes. |
| **Lanthanides** | **Actinides** | Lanthanides: Filling $4f$ subshell; $Z=58\\text{--}71$; only Promethium ($Pm$) is radioactive. Actinides: Filling $5f$ subshell; $Z=90\\text{--}103$; **ALL actinides are radioactive**. |

## 1.1 Structure of the Atom: Classical Models to Quantum Wave Mechanics

### Evolution of Atomic Models
- **Dalton's Atomic Theory (John Dalton, 1808):** Matter consists of indivisible, indestructible atoms. Atoms of the same element are identical in mass and properties. (Overturned by the discovery of subatomic particles and isotopes).
- **Thomson's Plum Pudding Model (1898):** Uniform sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded like raisins in a pudding.
- **Rutherford's Planetary Nuclear Model (1911):** Discovery of the nucleus. Failed to explain why accelerating orbiting electrons do not continuously radiate electromagnetic energy and spiral into the nucleus within $10^{-8}\\text{ s}$ (Maxwell's electrodynamics).
- **Bohr's Quantum Postulates (1913):** Introduced non-radiating stationary orbits where angular momentum is quantized: $mvr = nh/2\\pi$. Successfully explained the hydrogen spectral emission lines (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett, Pfund series).
- **De Broglie's Matter Waves (1924):** $\\lambda = h/p = h/mv$. Dual particle-wave nature of electrons.
- **Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle (1927):** $\\Delta x \\cdot \\Delta p \\ge h / 4\\pi$. Impossible to determine simultaneous trajectory; replaced deterministic orbits with probabilistic 3D **Orbitals**.

---

## 1.2 Quantum Numbers, Shells and Electronic Configuration

### Orbital Quantum Numbers & Shapes
- **Principal ($n = 1, 2, 3, 4$):** Shell designation (K, L, M, N). Total orbitals in shell $= n^2$; Max electrons $= 2n^2$.
- **Azimuthal ($l = 0\\text{ to } n-1$):** Subshell and shape.
  - $l = 0$ ($s$ orbital): Spherical, non-directional; can hold 2 electrons.
  - $l = 1$ ($p$ orbital): Dumbbell-shaped ($p_x, p_y, p_z$); can hold 6 electrons.
  - $l = 2$ ($d$ orbital): Double-dumbbell ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}, d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$); can hold 10 electrons.
  - $l = 3$ ($f$ orbital): Complex multi-lobed; 7 orbitals holding 14 electrons.
- **Rules Governing Electron Filling:**
  - **Aufbau Principle:** Orbitals fill in order of increasing $(n + l)$ energy: $1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p < 7s$.
  - **Pauli Exclusion:** Max 2 electrons per orbital with opposing spins.
  - **Hund's Rule:** Degenerate orbitals fill singly with parallel spins before pairing.

---

## 1.3 Atomic Number, Mass Number, Isotopes and Isobars

### Nuclear Classification Summary
- **Atomic Number ($Z$):** Number of nuclear protons.
- **Mass Number ($A$):** Sum of protons and neutrons ($Z + N$).
- **Isotopes:** Same $Z$, different $A$ ($^1_1H, ^2_1H, ^3_1H$).
- **Isobars:** Same $A$, different $Z$ ($^{40}_{18}Ar$ and $^{40}_{20}Ca$).
- **Isotones:** Same neutron count ($N = A - Z$).
- **Isoelectronic:** Same total electron count ($O^{2-}, F^-, Na^+, Mg^{2+}$).

---

## 1.4 Evolution of the Periodic Table: Mendeleev to Moseley

### Historic Milestones
- **Mendeleev's Table (1869):** Arranged 63 elements by **atomic weight**. Predicted Gallium ($Ga$) as *Eka-Aluminum* and Germanium ($Ge$) as *Eka-Silicon*.
- **Moseley's Modern Law (1913):** Shifted basis to **Atomic Number ($Z$)**, eliminating anomalies. Long form table has **7 Periods and 18 Groups**.

---

## 1.5 Master Analysis of Periodic Trends and Anomalies

### Core Trends Across Period vs Down Group
- **Atomic Radius:** Decreases across period $\\to$; increases down group $\\downarrow$.
- **Ionization Energy ($IE$):** Increases across period $\\to$; decreases down group $\\downarrow$.
  - *Anomalies:* $Be > B$ (full $2s^2$) and $N > O$ (half-filled $2p^3$).
- **Electron Affinity ($EA$):** Increases across period $\\to$; decreases down group $\\downarrow$.
  - *Anomaly:* **Chlorine has higher affinity than Fluorine** ($Cl > F > Br > I$) due to inter-electronic repulsions in tiny fluorine.
- **Electronegativity ($EN$):** Increases across period $\\to$; decreases down group $\\downarrow$. Peak is **Fluorine (4.0)**.

---

## 1.6 Metals, Non-Metals, Metalloids and Body Composition

### Chemical Demarcation
- **Metals:** Electropositive, form basic oxides, malleable, ductile, high electrical conductivity.
- **Non-Metals:** Electronegative, form acidic oxides, brittle insulators (except graphite).
- **Metalloids:** Borderline semiconductors: B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po.
- **Elemental Abundance:**
  - Earth's Crust: Oxygen ($46.6\\%$) > Silicon ($27.7\\%$) > Aluminum ($8.1\\%$, most abundant metal) > Iron ($5.0\\%$) > Calcium ($3.6\\%$).
  - Human Body: Oxygen ($65\\%$) > Carbon ($18.5\\%$) > Hydrogen ($9.5\\%$) > Nitrogen ($3.2\\%$) > Calcium ($1.5\\%$, most abundant metal).

  - other properties.
  • Atoms cannot be created or destroyed.
  • Compounds are formed when two or more different
  - types of atoms combine.
  • A chemical reaction is a rearrangement of atoms.
  - Dalton's theory provided a scientific basis for understanding
  - why elements combine in certain proportions to form
  - compounds. It also explained the law of constant composition,
  - conservation of mass, and the law of multiple proportions.

## Common Traps

| Concept | Trap / Confusion Point | Examiner's Angle | Correct Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electron Affinity vs Electronegativity** | Assuming Fluorine has the highest electron affinity. | Most tested chemistry trap in PCS/IAS. | **Chlorine has the highest electron affinity** ($Cl > F > Br > I$); **Fluorine has the highest electronegativity**. |
| **Abundance: Element vs Metal** | Answering Iron or Silicon as the most abundant metal in crust. | Crust composition trap. | **Oxygen is the #1 element** ($46.6\%$); **Aluminum is the #1 metal** ($8.1\%$). |
| **Most Abundant Body Metal** | Answering Iron because of hemoglobin blood volume. | Human biochemistry trap. | **Calcium is the #1 metal in the body** ($1.5\%$ of body mass, $1\text{ kg}$ in bones); Iron is only $\approx 4\text{ grams}$. |
| **Liquid Non-Metal** | Confusing Mercury with Bromine. | State of matter trap. | **Mercury is a liquid metal**; **Bromine is the only liquid non-metal**. |
| **Nitrogen vs Oxygen $IE$** | Assuming Oxygen has higher ionization energy because it is to the right. | Subshell stability trap. | **Nitrogen has higher $IE_1$ than Oxygen** due to its stable half-filled $2p^3$ subshell. |
| **Heaviest Subatomic Particle** | Assuming the proton is heavier than the neutron. | Nucleon mass trap. | The **Neutron is the heaviest** ($1.6749 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$ vs proton $1.6726 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$). |
| **Isobars vs Isotones** | Confusing same mass number with same neutron count. | Nuclear terminology trap. | **Isobars = Same mass number ($A$)**; **Isotones = Same neutron count ($N = A - Z$)**. |
| **Chromium Configuration** | Writing $3d^4 4s^2$. | Transition metal configuration trap. | Actual configuration is **$[Ar] 3d^5 4s^1$** (half-filled $d$-subshell stability). |
| **Doped Semiconductor Charge** | Assuming N-type is negatively charged. | Solid state physics trap. | Doped semiconductors (both N-type and P-type) are **strictly electrically neutral**. |
| **Sodium Storage** | Storing sodium in water instead of kerosene. | Chemical safety trap. | Sodium explodes in contact with water; must be stored in **kerosene oil**. |
