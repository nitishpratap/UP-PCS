1. Animals are eukaryotic, multicellular, heterotrophic organisms lacking cell walls and photosynthetic pigments; store reserve food as **glycogen and fat**.
2. **Porifera** (sponges) exhibit cellular level of organisation, have thousands of minute incurrent pores (**Ostia**) and a single large excurrent opening (**Osculum**), and maintain a unique water canal system lined by flagellated **Choanocytes / Collar cells**.
3. **Cnidaria (Coelenterata)** possess stinging capsules called **Cnidoblasts / Nematocysts** for defence and prey capture; show radial symmetry and tissue-level organisation; exhibit alternation of generation (**Metagenesis**) between sessile cylindrical **Polyp** and free-swimming umbrella-like **Medusa** (e.g. *Hydra*, *Aurelia* jellyfish, *Physalia* Portuguese man-of-war). Corals (*Anthozoa*) secrete massive $CaCO_3$ skeletons of organic origin. *Hydra* has **no blood but respires** via simple diffusion.
4. **Platyhelminthes** (flatworms) are triploblastic, acoelomate, dorso-ventrally flattened worms; excrete and osmoregulate via specialized **Flame cells (Protonephridia)** (e.g. *Planaria*, *Taenia solium* pork tapeworm, *Fasciola hepatica* liver fluke). Tapeworms are **hermaphrodites** (monoecious).
5. **Aschelminthes / Nematoda** (roundworms) possess a **Pseudocoelom** (false body cavity); sexually dimorphic (separate sexes); examples: *Ascaris lumbricoides* (intestinal roundworm), *Wuchereria bancrofti* (filarial worm causing elephantiasis transmitted by *Culex* mosquito), *Dracunculus medinensis* (guinea worm, a nematode, NOT an annelid!).
6. **Annelida** (segmented worms) exhibit true **metameric segmentation**, a closed circulatory system, and excrete via coiled tubular **Nephridia**; earthworms breathe cutaneously across moist glandular skin; possess **4 to 5 pairs of lateral hearts** (aortic arches in segments 7–11 or 7, 9, 12, 13).
7. Earthworm (*Pheretima posthuma*) blood is **red** because haemoglobin (**erythrocruorin**) is dissolved in the blood plasma; **lacks red blood cells (RBCs)**; known as the "friend of the farmer" for aerating soil and increasing organic matter.
8. **Arthropoda** is the **largest phylum** of the animal kingdom (over two-thirds of all named species, >1 million insects); characterized by **chitinous exoskeleton**, jointed appendages, open circulatory system with a haemocoel, and excretion via **Malpighian tubules** (insects), **Green glands / Antennal glands** (crustaceans/prawns), or **Coxal glands** (spiders/scorpions).
9. **Insects (Class Insecta / Hexapoda)**: 3 main body segments (**Head, Thorax, Abdomen**), **3 pairs of legs (6 legs)** attached to the thorax, usually 2 pairs of wings, 1 pair of antennae, tracheal respiration via spiracles. Examples: Cockroach, Honeybee, Mosquito, Butterfly, Cicadas, Froghoppers (spittlebugs), Pond skaters (*Gerridae*), Termites (white ants, Isoptera).
10. **Arachnids (Class Arachnida)**: 2 body regions (**Cephalothorax and Abdomen**), **4 pairs of walking legs (8 legs)**, **zero wings and zero antennae**; mouth appendages are **chelicerae** (fangs) and **pedipalps**. Examples: Spiders, Scorpions, Ticks, Mites. Ticks and mites have unsegmented fused bodies.
11. **Spider Silk (Gossamer Silk)**: Protein fibre spun by spiders, renowned for being both strong and elastic (often described as stronger than steel). Spiders practice **sexual cannibalism** (female kills and eats the male before, during, or after copulation).
12. **Peacock Tarantula (*Poecilotheria metallica*)**: Critically Endangered, arboreal spider endemic to deciduous forests of Andhra Pradesh / Pakkam Malai; it is an **insectivorous arachnid, NOT a crustacean**.
13. Insect blood is termed **Haemolymph**; it is **colourless / white** in cockroaches because it completely lacks respiratory pigments like haemoglobin; does not transport oxygen (oxygen is delivered directly to cells by tracheae).
14. **Mosquito Mouthparts & Posture**: Only **female mosquitoes suck blood** (mouthparts modified to pierce and suck: labium sheath, labrum feeding tube, paired mandibles and maxillae forming piercing stylet); **males have mouthparts adapted to suck flower/plant juices**. Females have larger wings. **Female *Anopheles* rests at an angle (~45°)** to the surface with abdomen pointing up; ***Culex* rests parallel** to the surface.
15. **Bioluminescence in Glowworms**: Larvae and adults of *Arachnocampa luminosa* / fireflies emit blue-green light from abdominal light organs via the enzyme **Luciferase** acting on luciferin substrate.
16. **Beneficial vs Harmful Insects**: *Bombyx mori* (silk), *Laccifer / Kerria lacca* (lac resin), *Apis mellifera* (honey/wax) are beneficial. ***Helicoverpa armigera*** (cotton bollworm/corn earworm) is a polyphagous destructive agricultural pest.
17. **Parasitoid species**: Larvae develop inside/on a host and ultimately kill it (e.g. parasitic wasps, parasitic flies, certain carabid beetles). Centipedes are generalist predators; termites are detritivores.
18. **Detritivores & Filter Feeders**:
19. **Ant Biology**: Ants inject **Formic acid** ($HCOOH$, simple carboxylic acid) when they bite. Ants have the **largest brain relative to body size** in the animal kingdom. Leafcutter ants (*Attini*) actively **cultivate fungi** for food.
20. **Mollusca** is the second largest animal phylum; soft-bodied, unsegmented animals usually enclosed in a calcareous shell ($CaCO_3$); possess a sensory rasping tongue-like feeding organ called **Radula** and a fleshy muscular foot; true pearls are harvested from the marine pearl oyster (***Pinctada vulgaris***, class Bivalvia/Pelecypoda).
21. **Cephalopods**: **Octopus (Devilfish)** has 8 arms with suckers, camera eyes, and no external shell. **Colossal Squid (*Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni*)** and **Giant Squid (*Architeuthis dux*)** are the largest living invertebrates.
22. **False Fish vs True Fish (Must-Score Fact)**:
23. **Fishes in Frozen Lakes**: Lakes freeze only at the surface; because water reaches maximum density at $4^\circ\text{C}$, the bottom water remains liquid ($>0^\circ\text{C}$), allowing fish and aquatic life to survive under the ice shield. Fish die outside water because gills collapse and cannot absorb gaseous oxygen.
24. **Echinodermata**: Exclusively marine spiny-skinned animals; exhibit secondary radial symmetry (bilateral in free-swimming larvae, pentamerous radial in adults); possess an ambulacral **Water Vascular System** operating suction-cup **Tube feet (Podia)**; **all echinoderms are NOT viviparous — they are typically oviparous with external fertilisation**.
25. **Animal heart chambers**: **Fish** = 2 chambers (1 atrium + 1 ventricle; single venous circulation); **Amphibians & most Reptiles** = 3 chambers (2 atria + 1 ventricle; mixed blood tolerated); **Crocodiles, Birds & Mammals** = **4 chambers** (2 atria + 2 ventricles; complete double circulation).
26. **Amphibian Metamorphosis**: Tadpoles are aquatic and herbivorous; metamorphosis into carnivorous frogs involves **dramatic shortening of the intestine** (controlled by thyroid hormone) to process meat diets. Frogs are cold-blooded and breathe via skin and lungs.
27. **Reptiles & Snakes**:
28. **Poisonous Species Across Taxa**: Poisonous species occur among **butterflies** (African Giant Swallowtail, Monarch — sequester plant alkaloids), **fishes** (Puffer fish Tetraodontidae with tetrodotoxin; Reef stonefish most venomous fish), and **frogs** (poison dart frogs with skin alkaloid secretions).
29. **Herbivorous Exceptions**: Adult **Green sea turtles** (*Chelonia mydas*) are strictly herbivorous; **Parrot fish** graze on algae; **Dugong / Sea cow** is a large herbivorous marine mammal. (Dogs are carnivores/omnivores, NOT herbivores).
30. **Flightless Birds & Aves**: **Kiwi** (*Apteryx*) is a small flightless bird endemic to New Zealand. **White Leghorn** is an exotic Mediterranean breed of fowl (chicken).
31. **Nocturnal Animals**: Highly developed hearing, smell, and adapted eyesight: Mosquitoes, Bats, Owls, Kiwis, Aye-aye (largest nocturnal primate using echolocation), **Malabar civet** (*Viverra civettina*). (Lion-tailed macaque is strictly diurnal; Sambar deer is crepuscular/nocturnal).
32. **Poikilothermic (Cold-blooded)**: Fish, Amphibians, Reptiles (body temperature fluctuates with environment).
33. **Homeothermic (Warm-blooded)**: Birds and Mammals (maintain stable internal body temperature via homeostasis).
34. **Respiratory organs across animal taxa**: Cutaneous (Earthworms, Frogs), Branchial (Fish, Tadpoles), Tracheal (Insects), Book lungs (Spiders, Scorpions), Book gills (Horseshoe crab *Limulus*), Pulmonary (Adult frogs, Reptiles, Birds, Mammals via blowholes in Cetaceans).
35. **Kangaroo rat** (*Dipodomys*) can survive its entire life without drinking water; produces the **most concentrated, almost solid urine** among all mammals and relies on metabolic water.
36. **Camels**: Store water in their **intestine** (can drink $>100\text{ litres}$ at once) and store energy as **fat in the hump**; have broad padded 2-toed feet; lack an Omasum (pseudoruminant).
37. **Gallbladder Absence**: Animals that do **not possess a gallbladder** include: all members of the deer family (except musk deer), equines (horses, zebras), camels, giraffes, elephants, rhinoceroses, whales, rats, and doves.
38. **Hooves vs Claws**: Horses, zebras, deer, cattle have hooves; **Jackal (*Canis aureus*)** belongs to Canidae and has **padded feet with nails/claws, NO hooves**.
39. **Nilgai (*Boselaphus tragocamelus*)**: Largest Asian **antelope** (family Bovidae, deer-like appearance, permanent hollow horns); NOT a cow.
40. **Wild Animal Scientific Names**: Leopard = ***Panthera pardus***; Lion = *Panthera leo*; Tiger = *Panthera tigris*; Snow Leopard = *Panthera uncia*; Jaguar = *Panthera onca*; Asiatic wild ass = *Equus hemionus*; Barasingha = *Cervus duvaucelii*; Chinkara = *Gazella bennettii*.
41. **Big Cat Behaviour**: Lions have **no specific breeding season**; **Cheetahs do NOT roar** (chirp, purr, growl); both male and female leopards mark territory by scent/urine.
42. **Predator Defense Mechanisms**: **Hedgehog** rolls into a tight ball with quills pointing outwards; **Pangolin** rolls into an armoured ball with sharp overlapping scales; **Marmots do NOT roll up** (they live in burrows and hide).
43. **Indian Squirrels**: Primarily arboreal, **build nests in trees** (not burrows in ground), hoard/bury nuts and seeds in ground, and are **omnivorous**.
44. **Primate Intelligence & Tool Use**: **Orangutan** uses sticks as tools to scrape insects from tree holes, poke logs for honey, and open *Neesia* fruits. Chimpanzee, Parrot, and Dolphin have been taught language with success; dogs cannot learn language. Humans evince a unique desire to learn and self-express.
45. **Apes vs Monkeys**: Apes (Superfamily Hominoidea) are tailless: Gibbons (lesser apes), Chimpanzees, Gorillas, Orangutans, Humans (great apes). **Gibbon** is the smallest, cleanest, gentlest ape. **Langurs** have long tails and are Old World monkeys (Colobinae), NOT apes.
46. **Brain Capacity**: Modern human average cranial capacity is **~1,350 cc** (range 1,000–2,000 cc); fossil **Cro-Magnon man had ~1,600 cc** (larger than modern average). **Ant has the largest brain in proportion to its body size**. Sperm whale has largest absolute brain (~7 kg).
47. **Panda**: Giant Panda (*Ailuropoda melanoleuca*) belongs to the **Bear family (Ursidae)**.
48. **Dingo (*Canis lupus dingo*)**: Australian wild dog that **cannot bark**; instead, it howls like a wolf.
49. **Sloth bear**: The shaggy bear commonly seen performing on Indian streets.
50. **Otter**: Semiaquatic carnivorous mammal (subfamily Lutrinae, family Mustelidae); fierce, aggressive predatory behaviour.

## 2.1 Animal Nutrition & Digestive Strategies

- **Digestive System Adaptations Across Feeding Guilds**:
  - **Herbivores**: Consume high-fibre plant matter rich in tough structural **cellulose**. Cellulose requires prolonged microbial fermentation, necessitating a **long and anatomically complex digestive tract**:
    - **Ruminants (Foregut Fermenters)**: Cattle, buffalo, sheep, goat, deer, giraffe.
    - **Non-ruminant Herbivores (Hindgut Fermenters)**: Horses, zebras, rabbits, elephants. Fermentation occurs in a massive, elongated **Caecum and Colon** located after the stomach and small intestine; less efficient than foregut fermentation (rabbits practice **coprophagy / caecotrophy** to re-ingest soft faeces for nutrient absorption).
  - **Carnivores**: Consume nutrient-dense, easily digestible animal protein and fats; possess a **short, straight, simple digestive tract** with a reduced or rudimentary caecum (human caecum terminates in the vestigial vermiform appendix).
  - **Omnivores**: Moderate intestinal length adapted for diverse dietary items (humans, bears, pigs, crows).
- **The Ruminant Compound Stomach Architecture**:
  - Consists of four distinct chambers arranged sequentially:
    1. **Rumen (Paunch)**: The largest chamber (occupies ~80% of total stomach volume). Acts as a massive anaerobic fermentation chamber holding up to 150–200 litres; populated by billions of symbiotic anaerobic bacteria, protozoa, and fungi that break $\beta\text{-1,4-glycosidic bonds}$ of cellulose into **volatile fatty acids** (acetate, propionate, butyrate).
    2. **Reticulum (Honeycomb)**: Inner lining shows hexagonal honeycomb-like cells; traps heavy foreign objects (nails, wire); coordinates regurgitation of coarse fibrous boli back into the mouth for re-chewing as **cud**.
    3. **Omasum (Manyplies / Bible)**: Contains numerous thin muscular leaves arranged like book pages; reabsorbs water, bicarbonate, and residual volatile fatty acids.
    4. **Abomasum (True Stomach)**: The only glandular, enzymatic compartment; secretes gastric juice containing **Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)** and **Pepsin** to digest microbial proteins and dietary matter.
- **The Camel Exception (Pseudoruminant Anatomy)**:
  - Camels and llamas chew the cud and possess active ruminal microbial fermentation, but **completely lack the Omasum (third stomach compartment)**; their stomach is strictly 3-chambered.
  - The camel's hump is composed entirely of **fat / adipose tissue**, not water; cellular oxidation of 1 gram of fat yields **1.07 grams of metabolic water**, but metabolic water generation requires oxygen intake which causes net respiratory water loss. Camels conserve water primarily through:
    - Highly concentrated, crystalline urine and extremely dry faeces.
    - Oval, nucleated RBCs capable of swelling up to 240% without haemolysing.
    - Wide tolerance of core body temperature fluctuations ($34^\circ\text{C}$ to $41^\circ\text{C}$), eliminating the need for sweating during daytime desert heat.

## 2.2 Respiration & Circulatory Dynamics in Animals

- **Evolution of Animal Respiratory Systems**:
  - **Cutaneous Respiration**: Gas exchange directly across thin, vascular, moist body surface:
    - **Earthworms and Leeches**: Lack specialised lungs or gills; mucus and coelomic fluid keep the skin constantly moist; atmospheric $O_2$ dissolves in moisture and diffuses directly into sub-epidermal blood capillaries. If an earthworm's skin dries out, it suffocates and dies.
    - **Amphibians (Frogs)**: Frogs respire cutaneously during underwater submersion, winter sleep (**Hibernation**), and summer sleep (**Aestivation**).
  - **Branchial Respiration (Gills)**:
    - Specialized vascularised external or internal outfoldings adapted for extracting dissolved oxygen from water:
    - Bony fish possess 4 pairs of gills housed in branchial chambers beneath a protective bony flap called the **Operculum**.
    - Cartilaginous fish (sharks, rays) possess 5–7 pairs of exposed gill slits lacking an operculum.
    - Counter-current exchange mechanism: Blood flows across gill lamellae in the opposite direction to water flow, ensuring maximum oxygen diffusion gradient (~80% extraction efficiency).
  - **Tracheal Respiration (Direct Cellular Ventilation in Insects)**:
    - Air enters the body through lateral paired muscular valves called **Spiracles** (usually 10 pairs: 2 thoracic, 8 abdominal).
    - Spiracles lead into branched cuticular tubes called **Tracheae**, which further divide into fluid-filled microscopic **Tracheoles** penetrating directly into individual muscle cells.
    - **Crucial consequence**: Insect blood (haemolymph) **plays zero role in oxygen transport**; oxygen diffuses in the gas phase directly to cellular mitochondria.
  - **Book Lungs & Book Gills**:
    - **Book Lungs**: Terrestrial arachnids (spiders, scorpions) possess internal chambers containing parallel thin vascular plates arranged like the leaves of a closed book.
    - **Book Gills**: Marine horseshoe crabs (***Limulus*** — an arthropod "living fossil") possess external respiratory plates arranged like pages.
  - **Pulmonary Respiration (Lungs)**:
    - Found in terrestrial vertebrates: adult amphibians (buccopharyngeal + pulmonary), reptiles, birds, and mammals.
    - **Cetaceans (Whales and Dolphins)**: Aquatic placental mammals; lack gills; surface regularly to forcefully exhale humid warm air and inhale fresh air through modified dorsal nostrils called **Blowholes**; can hold breath underwater for up to 90 minutes due to massive muscle stores of **Myoglobin**.

- **Comparative Vertebrate Heart & Circulation**:

| Class | Heart Chambers | Circulation Type | Nature of Blood Flowing Through Heart |
|-------|----------------|------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Pisces (Fish)** | **2 Chambers** (1 Atrium + 1 Ventricle) | **Single Circulation** | Exclusively deoxygenated **Venous Blood** (pumps blood to gills for oxygenation, then directly to tissues) |
| **Amphibia (Frogs)** | **3 Chambers** (2 Atria + 1 Ventricle) | **Incomplete Double** | Oxygenated (left) and deoxygenated (right) blood mix inside the single ventricle |
| **Reptilia (Lizards, Snakes, Turtles)** | **3 Chambers** (Incompletely 4) | **Incomplete Double** | Ventricle has an incomplete interventricular septum; partial mixing occurs |
| **Crocodilia (Crocodiles, Alligators)** | **4 Chambers** (2 Atria + 2 Ventricles) | **Complete Double** | Complete anatomical separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood (**Exception among reptiles**) |
| **Aves (Birds)** | **4 Chambers** (2 Atria + 2 Ventricles) | **Complete Double** | Complete separation; right systemic aortic arch persists |
| **Mammalia (Mammals)** | **4 Chambers** (2 Atria + 2 Ventricles) | **Complete Double** | Complete separation; left systemic aortic arch persists |

## 2.3 The Invertebrate Spectrum: Phyla & Signatures

- **Phylum Porifera (Sponges / छिद्रधारी जन्तु)**:
  - Most primitive multicellular animals; lack true tissues, organs, nerves, and muscles; asymmetrical or radially symmetrical.
  - Body wall consists of outer **Pinacoderm** and inner **Choanoderm** separated by a gelatinous matrix (**Mesohyl**) containing amoebocytes and mineral **spicules** ($CaCO_3$ or silica) or organic **spongin fibres**.
  - **Water Canal System**: Water enters through thousands of incurrent pores (**Ostia**) into a central cavity (**Spongocoel**) and exits through a terminal excurrent aperture (**Osculum**). Flagellated **Choanocytes (Collar cells)** generate water currents, trap microscopic food, and drive respiration and excretion.
  - Examples: *Sycon* (*Scypha*), *Spongilla* (freshwater sponge), *Euspongia* (commercial bath sponge), *Euplectella* ("Venus flower basket", gifted as a wedding symbol of eternal union in Japan).
- **Phylum Cnidaria / Coelenterata (दंश कोशिका जन्तु)**:
  - Aquatic, mostly marine, diploblastic, radially symmetrical animals with a central gastrovascular cavity (**Coelenteron**) opening via a single mouth/anus on a hypostome.
  - Possess specialized stinging cells called **Cnidoblasts / Nematocysts** containing a coiled, venomous inverted thread (toxin: **Hypnotoxin**, a mix of proteins and phenols); function in offence, defence, and anchorage.
  - Exhibit **Polymorphism**:
    - **Polyp**: Cylindrical, sessile, asexual form (e.g. *Hydra*, *Adamsia* sea anemone).
    - **Medusa**: Umbrella-shaped, free-swimming, sexual form with marginal tentacles (e.g. *Aurelia* jellyfish).
    - **Metagenesis (Alternation of Generation)**: In *Obelia*, polyps produce medusae asexually by budding, and medusae produce polyps sexually via fertilised eggs.
  - Coral reefs are formed by massive colonies of stony corals (e.g. *Corallium rubrum* / red coral) that secrete massive skeletons of pure **Calcium Carbonate ($CaCO_3$)**.
- **Phylum Platyhelminthes (Flatworms / चपटे कृमि)**:
  - Triploblastic, dorso-ventrally flattened, bilateral, acoelomate worms; organ-level organisation; incomplete gut (no anus).
  - Excretion and osmoregulation performed by specialized ciliated **Flame Cells (Solenocytes)**.
  - **Parasitic Adaptations**: Hooks, suckers, thick protective tegument, loss of digestive system, massive reproductive capacity:
    - ***Taenia solium*** (Pork Tapeworm): Lives in human small intestine; head (**scolex**) has 4 suckers and a rostellum with chitinous hooks; body consists of repeating segments called **proglottids**; causes Taeniasis and Cysticercosis.
    - ***Fasciola hepatica*** (Sheep Liver Fluke): Endoparasite in bile ducts of sheep and cattle; intermediate host is a freshwater snail (*Lymnaea*).
    - ***Planaria***: Free-living freshwater flatworm celebrated for its **immense, unmatched capacity of regeneration**.
- **Phylum Aschelminthes / Nematoda (Roundworms / गोलकृमि)**:
  - Triploblastic, bilateral, unsegmented, vermiform worms with a cylindrical tapering body; possess a **Pseudocoelom** derived from the blastocoel.
  - Complete digestive tract with a muscular, pumping pharynx.
  - Show pronounced **Sexual Dimorphism**: Females are significantly longer and straight; males are shorter with a curved posterior tail bearing copulatory **pineal spicules**.
  - Examples:
    - ***Ascaris lumbricoides***: Giant human intestinal roundworm; transmitted through water/food contaminated with embryonated eggs.
    - ***Wuchereria bancrofti***: Filarial worm; adult parasites obstruct lymph vessels of lower limbs and scrotum, causing massive chronic swelling called **Elephantiasis (Filariasis)**; transmitted by the bite of female ***Culex*** mosquitoes.
    - ***Ancylostoma duodenale*** (Hookworm) and ***Enterobius vermicularis*** (Pinworm).
- **Phylum Annelida (Segmented Worms / वलयी कृमि)**:
  - Triploblastic, coelomate, bilateral animals characterized by internal and external **Metameric Segmentation**; body covered by a thin moist non-cellular cuticle.
  - Locomotion via chitinous **Setae** (earthworms) or muscular lateral paddle-like **Parapodia** (marine *Nereis*).
  - Excretion and osmoregulation via segmentally arranged coiled tubes called **Nephridia**.
  - Closed circulatory system; blood contains haemoglobin (**erythrocruorin**) dissolved in plasma (lacks RBCs).
  - Examples:
    - ***Pheretima posthuma*** (Indian Earthworm): Hermaphrodite; clitellum in segments 14–16; possesses 4–5 pairs of lateral hearts; improves soil fertility.
    - ***Hirudinaria granulosa*** (Leech): Sanguivorous ectoparasite; secretes **Hirudin** anticoagulant.
- **Phylum Arthropoda (Jointed-Leg Animals / संधिपाद जन्तु)**:
  - Largest animal phylum (over two-thirds of all named animal species, >1 million insects described); segmented body with jointed appendages and an external **Chitinous Cuticle** shed via **Ecdysis / Moulting**.
  - Open circulatory system: haemolymph pumped into a **Haemocoel**.
  - Sensory organs: Compound eyes with **Ommatidia** (mosaic vision), antennae, and statocysts.
  - Excretion: **Malpighian tubules** (insects), **Green glands / Antennal glands** (prawns/crustaceans), **Coxal glands** (arachnids).
  - **Insects (Hexapoda) vs Arachnids (Arachnida) — Fundamental Differentiation**:
    - **Class Insecta**: Body divided into 3 tagmata (**Head, Thorax, Abdomen**); **3 pairs of walking legs (6 legs)** attached to the thorax; usually 2 pairs of wings; 1 pair of antennae; tracheal respiration. Examples: Cockroach, Butterfly, Honeybee, Mosquito, Housefly, Cicadas, Froghoppers, Pond skaters, Termites.
    - **Class Arachnida**: Body divided into 2 regions (**Cephalothorax and Abdomen**); **4 pairs of walking legs (8 legs)**; **zero wings and zero antennae**; mouthparts consist of 1 pair of **chelicerae** (pincer-like fangs) and 1 pair of **pedipalps** (sensory/grasping). Examples: Spiders, Scorpions, Ticks, Mites.
    - **Ticks and Mites (Subclass Acari)**: Appendages jointed, body bilaterally symmetrical and triploblastic; individuals appear as an unsegmented single body mass (abdomen fused with cephalothorax).
    - **Peacock Tarantula / Gooty Tarantula (*Poecilotheria metallica*)**: Sighted in 2019 in Pakkam Malai Reserve Forest (expanding known range ~370 km south of Eastern Ghats); Critically Endangered, **arboreal (tree-dwelling), insectivorous spider, NOT a crustacean**.
    - **Spider Silk (Gossamer Silk)**: Protein fibre spun from abdominal spinnerets; miraculously combines high tensile strength (often compared to steel) with high elasticity.
    - **Sexual Cannibalism in Spiders**: Phenomenon where the female spider kills and consumes the male before, during, or after copulation (strong males occasionally escape to mate again).
- **Phylum Mollusca (Soft-Bodied Animals / कोमल शरीरी जन्तु)**:
  - Second largest animal phylum; unsegmented soft body usually enclosed within a calcareous shell ($CaCO_3$) secreted by a fleshy fold called the **Mantle (Pallium)**.
  - Body divided into **Head**, ventral **Muscular Foot**, and dorsal **Visceral Hump** with a rasping tongue-like **Radula**.
  - Key Representatives:
    - ***Pinctada vulgaris*** (Pearl Oyster, Class Bivalvia): Irritation from foreign particles induces mantle epithelium to secrete concentric sheets of nacre (aragonite $CaCO_3$ + conchiolin), forming genuine pearls.
    - ***Octopus*** (Devilfish, Class Cephalopoda): 8 flexible arms with suckers, bilaterally symmetrical, complex camera eyes, ink sac for defensive smoke screen; **no shell**.
    - **Squids**: **Colossal Squid (*Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni*)** is the heaviest invertebrate; **Giant Squid (*Architeuthis dux*)** is the longest invertebrate (up to 18 metres).
- **Phylum Echinodermata (Spiny-Skinned Animals / शूलचर्मी जन्तु)**:
  - Exclusively marine deuterostomes; adults show **pentamerous radial symmetry**, larvae are bilateral; possess a calcareous endoskeleton of ossicles/spines.
  - **Water Vascular (Ambulacral) System**: Hydraulic network operating suction-cup **Tube Feet (Podia)** for locomotion and food capture.
  - **Logic:** **All echinoderms are NOT viviparous — they are typically oviparous with external fertilisation and indirect development with free-swimming larvae.**
  - Examples: *Asterias* (Starfish), *Echinus* (Sea urchin), *Holothuria* (Sea cucumber), *Ophiura* (Brittle star).
- **Feeding Strategies — Detritivores vs Filter Feeders**:
  - **Detritivores**: Heterotrophs that feed on dead and decaying organic matter (detritus), aiding remineralisation: **Earthworms, Millipedes, Woodlice, Dung flies, Slugs**. (Seahorses and Jellyfish are secondary consumers/predators, NOT detritivores).
  - **Filter Feeders (Suspension Feeders)**: Strain suspended particles and plankton from water via specialized filtering structures: **Oysters, Clams, Sponges, Baleen Whales, Flamingos**. Oysters draw water through gills and automatically filter nutrients, acting as vital aquatic ecosystem engineers.

## 2.4 Insects: Vectors, Products & Economic Roles

- **Insect Anatomy, Respiration & Haemolymph**:
  - Head (compound eyes, antennae, feeding mouthparts), Thorax (3 segments, 3 pairs of legs, wings), Abdomen (viscera).
  - **Insect Blood (Haemolymph)**: Colourless / white in cockroaches because it completely lacks respiratory pigments; carries nutrients and uric acid, but **zero oxygen**. Respiration is carried out entirely via lateral **Spiracles** leading into branching **Tracheae**.
- **Mosquito Biology — Gender Differences & Resting Posture**:
  - **Diet & Mouthparts**:
    - **Female Mosquitoes**: Mouthparts adapted to **pierce and suck blood** from warm-blooded birds and mammals (ectoparasite); labium acts as a sheath, labrum forms the central feeding tube, and paired mandibles and maxillae form a sharp piercing stylet. Females require blood proteins to develop eggs.
    - **Male Mosquitoes**: Mouthparts lack piercing stylets; adapted strictly to **suck floral nectar and fruit juices**.
    - Females generally have noticeably **larger wings and body sizes** than males.
  - **Resting Posture Differentiation**:
    - **Female *Anopheles***: Body rests at an **angle of ~45° to the substratum** with its abdomen pointing upward into the air.
    - **Female *Culex***: Body rests **parallel to the surface of the substratum**.
- **Bioluminescence & Enzyme Luciferase**:
  - Glowworms (*Arachnocampa luminosa*) and fireflies emit blue-green light from abdominal light organs through the enzyme **Luciferase** oxidising luciferin in the presence of ATP and $Mg^{2+}$.
- **Beneficial vs Harmful / Destructive Insects**:
  - **Beneficial Insects**: *Bombyx mori* (silk), *Kerria lacca* (lac resin), *Apis mellifera* (honey, beeswax, pollination).
  - **Destructive Crop Pest**: ***Helicoverpa armigera*** (Cotton bollworm / American bollworm / Corn earworm); polyphagous pest attacking cotton, tomato, maize, and $>100$ crops, causing severe economic damage.
- **Parasitoids vs Predators vs Detritivores**:
  - **Parasitoids**: Lay eggs on or inside a host organism; developing larvae feed on the host's tissues and ultimately kill it: **Parasitic Wasps (Ichneumonids, Braconids), Parasitic Flies (Tachinids), and Carabid Ground Beetles**.
  - **Generalist Predators**: Centipedes (carnivorous arthropods with poison claws).
  - **Detritivores**: Termites (**White Ants**, infraorder Isoptera; eusocial insects that consume dead wood, cellulose, and leaf litter).
- **Ant Biology**:
  - Ants inject **Formic acid ($HCOOH$)**, a simple carboxylic acid, when stinging/biting.
  - Ants have the **largest brain in proportion to body size** in the entire animal kingdom.
  - **Fungus Farming in Ants**: Leafcutter ants (tribe *Attini*) cut foliage to actively cultivate specialized fungal gardens in underground colonies as their exclusive food source.
- **Other Notable Insects**:
  - **Cicadas (Superfamily Cicadoidea, Hemiptera)**: Sound-producing insects (>3,000 species) with stout bodies and clear wings.
  - **Froghoppers (Superfamily Cercopoidea, Hemiptera)**: Powerful jumping insects; plant-sucking nymphs produce protective foam shelters (**spittlebugs**).
  - **Pond Skaters / Water Striders (Family Gerridae, Hemiptera)**: Insects anatomically adapted to run/skate across the water surface film using surface tension, capturing insects that fall onto water.
- **Insects as Disease Vectors**:

| Vector Insect | Causative Pathogen | Disease Transmitted | Pathogen Nature |
|---------------|-------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Female *Anopheles* Mosquito** | *Plasmodium vivax / falciparum* | **Malaria** | Sporozoan Protozoan |
| **Female *Aedes aegypti* Mosquito** | Dengue virus (Flavivirus), Chikungunya, Zika | **Dengue**, **Chikungunya**, **Yellow Fever**, **Zika** | RNA Virus |
| **Female *Culex quinquefasciatus*** | *Wuchereria bancrofti* | **Filariasis (Elephantiasis)** | Nematode Roundworm |
| **Sandfly (*Phlebotomus argentipes*)** | *Leishmania donovani* | **Kala-azar (Visceral Leishmaniasis / Dum-Dum Fever)** | Flagellated Protozoan |
| **Tsetse Fly (*Glossina palpalis*)** | *Trypanosoma brucei* | **African Sleeping Sickness** | Flagellated Protozoan |
| **Housefly (*Musca domestica*)** | *Vibrio cholerae*, *Salmonella typhi*, *Entamoeba* | **Cholera**, **Typhoid**, **Amoebic Dysentery** | Mechanical Vector (Bacteria / Protozoa) |
| **Rat Flea (*Xenopsylla cheopis*)** | *Yersinia pestis* (Pasteurella) | **Bubonic Plague (Black Death)** | Bacterium |

## 2.5 Fish, Amphibians, Reptiles & Mammalian Exceptions

- **Class Pisces — True Fish vs False Fish**:
  - **True Fish Characteristics**: Cold-blooded aquatic chordates, streamlined body with scales, breathe via gills, swim via paired/unpaired fins, 2-chambered venous heart.
  - **Cartilaginous Fish (Chondrichthyes)**: Skeleton made purely of cartilage; **contain ZERO true bones** (Shark fish = 0 bones); ventral mouth; placoid scales; exposed gill slits without operculum; no air bladder: *Scoliodon* (Dogfish / Dog Shark), *Pristis* (Sawfish), *Torpedo* (Electric ray), *Trygon* (Stingray).
  - **Bony Fish (Osteichthyes)**: Skeleton of bone; terminal mouth; cycloid scales; 4 pairs of gills covered by a bony **Operculum**; possess an **Air Bladder / Swim Bladder** regulating buoyancy:
    - *Hippocampus* (**Seahorse** — bony fish, vertical swimming, male carries fertilised eggs in brood pouch).
    - *Clarias* (**Catfish** — order Siluriformes, prominent whisker-like sensory barbels).
    - ***Tor putitora* (Golden Mahseer)**: Endangered cyprinid **fish** of rapid Himalayan rivers (frequently confused in PSCs as a bird; it is a true fish!).
    - *Exocoetus* (Flying fish), Pipefish, Paddlefish, Guitarfish, Globefish.
  - **Master Fact — Animals Named "Fish" That Are NOT True Fish**:
    - **Starfish (*Asterias*)**: Phylum Echinodermata (no gills, fins, or scales).
    - **Jellyfish (*Aurelia*)**: Phylum Cnidaria / Coelenterata (diploblastic medusa).
    - **Silverfish (*Lepisma*)**: Phylum Arthropoda, Class Insecta (wingless nocturnal insect).
    - **Cuttlefish (*Sepia*)**: Phylum Mollusca, Class Cephalopoda (internal shell).
    - **Hagfish (*Myxine*)**: Jawless cyclostome chordate (lacks true jaws and paired fins).
    - **Devilfish (*Octopus*)**: Phylum Mollusca, Class Cephalopoda (8-armed mollusc).
    - **Crayfish**: Phylum Arthropoda, Class Crustacea (freshwater decapod crustacean).
  - **Why Fishes Survive Under Frozen Lakes**:
    - In winter, ambient atmospheric temperature falls below $0^\circ\text{C}$, freezing the surface layer into ice. Ice is a poor conductor of heat and floats on water.
    - Water has anomalous density, reaching maximum density at **$4^\circ\text{C}$**. Denser water at $4^\circ\text{C}$ sinks to the bottom, keeping bottom layers liquid ($>0^\circ\text{C}$), allowing fish and aquatic life to thrive beneath the frozen surface.
    - When taken out of water, fish suffocate because delicate gill filaments stick together, collapsing the respiratory surface area and preventing oxygen diffusion.
- **Class Amphibia (Dual-Life Vertebrates)**:
  - Ectothermic (cold-blooded); smooth, moist, glandular skin **completely devoid of scales**; 3-chambered heart (2 atria + 1 ventricle); tolerates partial mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.
  - Breathe through moist skin (**cutaneous**) and lungs (**pulmonary**).
  - **Metamorphosis**: Herbivorous aquatic tadpole larva undergoes abrupt structural changes controlled by **Thyroid Hormone**:
    - Gills disappear and lungs develop.
    - Tail is resorbed.
    - **Intestine dramatically shortens in length** (transitioning from a long herbivorous gut processing plant matter to a compact carnivorous gut digesting flesh/insects).
- **Class Reptilia (Creeping / Crawling Vertebrates)**:
  - First truly terrestrial vertebrates; ectothermic; dry cornified skin covered by epidermal **scales or scutes**; lay cleidoic shelled eggs.
  - Heart is **3-chambered** with an incomplete interventricular partition; **Crocodiles, Alligators, and Gharials possess a fully 4-chambered heart**.
  - **Snakes & Snake Venom Dynamics**:
    - Limbless reptiles (loss of limbs explained by Lamarck's use-and-disuse principle).
    - **Poisonous Fangs**: Modified **maxillary teeth** (usually 1 pair) attached to venom ducts; hollow or grooved like hypodermic needles.
    - **Venom Glands**: **Homologous to salivary glands** of vertebrates; venom is chemically modified saliva containing 20+ protein toxins and enzymes.
    - **Venom Types**:
      - **Neurotoxic & Cardiotoxic**: Cobra (*Naja naja*) and Krait (*Bungarus caeruleus*); paralyzes respiratory muscles and attacks neuromuscular junctions.
      - **Vasculotoxic & Haemotoxic**: Russell's Viper (*Daboia russelii*) and Saw-scaled Viper (*Echis carinatus*); destroys vascular endothelium, lyses RBCs, and disrupts blood clotting.
    - **King Cobra (*Ophiophagus hannah*)**: Longest venomous snake ($~3.6\text{ m}$); the **ONLY snake in the world that builds an above-ground nest of leaves**; female deposits 20–50 eggs and guards the mound until hatching; diet consists almost exclusively of **other snakes** (rat snakes, kraits, small pythons).
    - **Python (Family Pythonidae)**: Giant non-venomous constrictor snake.
    - **Green Anaconda (*Eunectes murinus*)**: Viviparous snake (gives birth to live young rather than laying eggs).
- **Poisonous Organisms Across Classes**:
  - Toxic species exist among **Butterflies** (African Giant Swallowtail, Monarch — sequester cardiac glycosides from larval host plants), **Fish** (Puffer fish contains lethal tetrodotoxin; Reef stonefish is most venomous fish), and **Frogs** (Poison dart frogs store batrachotoxin alkaloids in skin).
- **Class Aves (Birds / Feathered Bipeds)**:
  - Warm-blooded (homeothermic); 4-chambered heart; pneumatic bones; toothless beak; right aortic arch.
  - **Kiwi (*Apteryx*)**: Small flightless bird endemic to New Zealand.
  - **White Leghorn**: World's leading commercial egg-laying breed of fowl (chicken).
  - Indian Nightjar, Spoonbill, White Ibis belong to Aves.
- **Class Mammalia & Mammalian Exceptions**:
  - Warm-blooded, mammary glands, body hair, diaphragm, 7 cervical vertebrae, 4-chambered heart.
  - **Blue Whale (*Balaenoptera musculus*)**: Largest animal ever known ($33\text{ m}$, $>180\text{ tonnes}$); lifespan 80–90 years; aquatic mammal.
  - **Sperm Whale (*Physeter macrocephalus*)**: Largest of the **toothed whales** ($55\text{ ft}$, $35–45\text{ tonnes}$); carnivorous predator; possesses the **largest absolute brain in the animal kingdom (~7 kg)**.
  - **Cetaceans (Whales, Dolphins, Porpoises)**: Breathe air via dorsal nostrils (**Blowholes**); warm-blooded; nurse calves with milk. **Ganges River Dolphin (*Platanista gangetica*)** is India's National Aquatic Animal.
  - **Semiaquatic Mammals**: Seal, Sea Lion, Walrus (order Carnivora, pinnipeds). **Sea cow (Dugong)** is a giant herbivorous aquatic mammal (order Sirenia).
  - **Bats (Order Chiroptera)**: **Only mammals naturally capable of true sustained powered flight** via skin membrane wings (**patagium**). Smallest mammal: **Kitti's hog-nosed bat (bumblebee bat)** ($29–34\text{ mm}$); largest bat: Flying fox (wingspan $1.7\text{ m}$). **Indian Flying Fox (*Pteropus giganteus*)** is frugivorous (eats fruits and nectar), NOT blood-sucking.
  - **Egg-Laying Mammals (Prototheria / Monotremes)**: Lay soft leathery eggs; have cloaca and mammary glands without nipples: **Duck-billed Platypus** (*Ornithorhynchus anatinus*) and **Spiny Anteater / Echidna** (*Tachyglossus*).
  - **Pouched Mammals (Metatheria / Marsupials)**: Give birth to immature altricial young nurtured in abdominal pouch (**Marsupium**): Kangaroo, Koala, Opossum, Wombat, Tasmanian devil, Wallaby, Bandicoot. **Marsupials are NOT naturally found in India**; they occupy diverse habitats (deserts, forests, savannas), NOT exclusively montane grasslands.
  - **Desert Adaptations**:
    - **Kangaroo Rat (*Dipodomys*)**: Can live its entire life without drinking water; produces the **most concentrated, almost solid urine** among mammals; lives on metabolic water from oxidized seeds.
    - **Camel**: Stores water in its **intestine** (drinks $>100\text{ litres}$ at once) and stores energy as fat in its hump; broad padded 2-toed feet; pseudoruminant (lacks omasum).
  - **Absence of Gallbladder**: Animals lacking a gallbladder include: all members of the **deer family (except musk deer)**, **equines (horses, zebras)**, camels, giraffes, elephants, rhinoceroses, whales, doves, and rats.
  - **Hooves vs Claws**: Horses, cattle, deer have hooves; **Jackal (*Canis aureus*)** belongs to Canidae and has **padded feet with claws, NO hooves**.
  - **Nilgai (*Boselaphus tragocamelus*)**: Largest Asian **antelope** (family Bovidae, hollow permanent horns); NOT a cow.
  - **Big Cat Wildlife Facts**:
    - Scientific names: Leopard (***Panthera pardus***), Lion (*Panthera leo*), Tiger (*Panthera tigris*), Snow Leopard (*Panthera uncia*), Jaguar (*Panthera onca*).
    - Lions have **no specific breeding season** (breed year-round in captivity, every 2 years in wild).
    - **Cheetahs do NOT roar** (they growl, chirp, purr, and meow).
    - Male and female leopards proclaim territory via **scent-marking and urine spraying**.
  - **Self-Defense Mechanisms**:
    - **Hedgehog**: Spiny mammal that rolls into a tight ball with muscular control pointing quills outward.
    - **Pangolin (Scaly Anteater)**: Rolls into an armoured ball with sharp overlapping keratin scales protecting face.
    - **Marmots**: Large herbivorous ground squirrels; **do NOT roll up**; escape predators by diving into underground burrows.
  - **Indian Squirrels**: Arboreal; **build nests (dreys) in trees** (not burrows in ground); bury/hoard nuts and seeds in soil; strictly **omnivorous** (eat nuts, fruits, insects, bird eggs).
  - **Primate Evolution & Behaviour**:
    - **Apes vs Monkeys**: Apes (Superfamily Hominoidea) are **tailless**: Gibbons (lesser apes), Chimpanzees, Gorillas, Orangutans, Humans (great apes). **Gibbon** is the smallest, gentlest man-like ape. **Langurs** have long tails and are Old World monkeys (Colobinae), NOT apes.
    - **Tool Use**: **Orangutan** uses sticks to scrape insects from tree holes, poke logs for honey, and open irritating *Neesia* fruits.
    - **Language**: Chimpanzee, Parrot, and Dolphin have been trained to use language symbols with success; **dogs cannot learn language**.
    - **Human Distinctiveness**: Humans are the only animals evincing a conscious desire to learn, philosophise, and express complex symbolic thought.
    - **Cranial Capacity**: Modern human average cranial cavity is **~1,350 cc** (range 1,000–2,000 cc); fossil **Cro-Magnon man had ~1,600 cc**. **Ant has the largest brain in proportion to its body weight**.
    - **Giant Panda**: Member of the bear family (**Ursidae**).
    - **Dingo (*Canis lupus dingo*)**: Australian wild dog that **cannot bark**; howls like a wolf.
    - **Sloth bear**: Common street performing bear in India.
    - **Otter**: Semiaquatic mustelid (subfamily Lutrinae); aggressive aquatic predator.
  - **Summary of Mammalian Sub-Classes**:
    1. **Prototheria (Monotremes / Egg-Laying)**: Platypus (*Ornithorhynchus*) and Spiny Anteater (*Echidna*).
    2. **Metatheria (Marsupials / Pouched)**: Kangaroo, Koala, Opossum, Wombat (abdominal marsupium; not naturally found in India).
    3. **Eutheria (Placental)**: Whales (*Balaenoptera*), Bats (*Pteropus*), Elephants, Primates (nourished in utero via chorio-allantoic placenta).

## 2.6 Silk, Sericulture & Animal Products

- **Biology of Silk & The Silkworm Lifecycle**:
  - Silk is a continuous animal protein filament spun by the mature caterpillar larva of silk moths during cocoon construction:
  - **Life Cycle Stages**:
    1. **Egg**: Female moth lays 300–500 eggs.
    2. **Larva (Caterpillar)**: Voracious feeder on host leaves; undergoes 4 moults through 5 instars over 25–30 days.
    3. **Pupa / Chrysalis**: Fully grown 5th instar larva stops feeding, anchors itself, and rotates its head in a figure-8 motion, extruding a liquid secretion from paired labial silk glands through a spinneret. The liquid solidifies upon contact with air into a continuous silk filament ($1000–1500\text{ metres}$ long), forming an oval protective shell called the **Cocoon**.
    4. **Adult Moth**: In nature, the pupa metamorphoses into a moth that secretes an alkaline fluid to dissolve cocoon threads and emerges, rendering the broken thread unusable for reeling.
  - **Stifling & Reeling**: In commercial silk production, live pupae inside cocoons are killed by hot air, steam, or baking before emergence (**Stifling**); cocoons are then soaked in hot water to soften sericin gum, and continuous fibres from multiple cocoons are wound together onto reels (**Reeling**).
- **Chemical Nature of Natural Silk**:
  - **Fibroin (75–80%)**: Insoluble crystalline core structural protein composed predominantly of the amino acids **Glycine, Alanine, and Serine** in anti-parallel $\beta$-pleated sheets; gives silk its exceptional tensile strength and softness.
  - **Sericin (20–25%)**: Amorphous, water-soluble globular sticky gelatinous protein that coats and cements the two fibroin filaments together.
  - **Logic:** Natural silk is a pure protein and therefore **always contains Nitrogen** (~17%); cotton contains zero nitrogen because it is pure plant carbohydrate (cellulose). Spider dragline silk is also an exceptionally strong protein.
- **The Four Commercial Silks of India**:
  - **Mulberry Silk**: Reared on cultivated White Mulberry (*Morus alba*); softest, finest, white or creamy yellow; accounts for **~70% of total Indian silk production**; **Karnataka is #1** (Mysuru, Mandya, Ramanagara), followed by Andhra Pradesh and West Bengal. Central Silk Board headquarters is located at **Bengaluru**.
  - **Tasar Silk (Tussar)**: Wild forest vanya silk obtained from ***Antheraea mylitta***; caterpillars feed on leaves of Arjun (*Terminalia arjuna*) and Asan (*Terminalia tomentosa*); coarse, copper-brown, porous; **Jharkhand is #1** (encapsulating Santhal Parganas and Chaibasa), followed by Chhattisgarh and Odisha.
  - **Muga Silk**: Exclusive to the state of **Assam**; produced by ***Antheraea assamensis*** feeding on aromatic leaves of Som (*Machilus bombycina*) and Soalu (*Litsaea polyantha*); famous for its **natural brilliant golden-yellow luster**, exceptional durability, and glossy texture (improves with washing); held by Assam as a protected Geographical Indication (GI Tag).
  - **Eri Silk (Endi)**: Spun silk produced by ***Samia ricini*** feeding on Castor leaves (*Ricinus communis*); white or brick-red; thermal properties comparable to wool; called **"Ahimsa Silk"** because the cocoon is open-ended, allowing the adult moth to emerge naturally without boiling the live pupa. Primary states: Assam and Meghalaya.

    - **Nagori**: Nagaur (Rajasthan); agile, light-footed trotting bullocks.
    - **Malvi**: Malwa plateau (Madhya Pradesh); massive draught power.
    - **Kangayam**: Coimbatore (Tamil Nadu); short, stout draught animal.
  - **Dual-Purpose Breeds (Work Bullocks + Fair Milk Yield)**:

    - **Ongole**: Prakasam and Guntur (Andhra Pradesh); large muscular humped bull; prized internationally for heat and tropical tick resistance.
  - **Exotic Dairy Breeds**:
    - **Holstein-Friesian**: Netherlands; black and white patches; **highest volume milk yielder in the world** ($6000–8000\text{ litres}$ per lactation); milk fat is moderate (~3.4–3.6%).

  - **Jaffarabadi**: Gir forest, Saurashtra (Gujarat); massive heavy body, forehead shows a prominent frontal shield, horns droop down and curl outward; yields high milk volumes.
  - **Surti**: Anand, Kheda, Vadodara (Gujarat); medium-sized, sickle-shaped horns with white markings.
  - **Mehsana**: Mehsana, Banaskantha (Gujarat); synthesized stabilized cross of Murrah bull $\times$ Surti female; early maturing and regular breeder.
  - **Nili-Ravi**: Ferozepur (Punjab) and Pakistan; distinctive walled / glass-white eyes (**"Panch Kalyani"**: white switch, white forehead, white muzzle, white legs, white eyes).

  - **Jamunapari Goat**: Etawah (Chambal ravines, UP); largest Indian dairy goat breed; white coat with tan patches, long drooping ears ($25–30\text{ cm}$), convex Roman nose giving a parrot-mouth appearance; dual-purpose (milk and meat).
  - **Barbari Goat**: Urban and peri-urban plains of UP, Haryana, and Delhi; small, compact, short erect ears, highly prolific (twins/triplets common); ideal for stall-fed intensive dairying ("City Goat").
  - **Black Bengal Goat**: West Bengal, Bihar, Odisha; small, black coat; prized for high prolificacy (kidding twice a year) and world-class tender chevon meat and fine leather.
  - **Changthangi Goat (Pashmina Goat)**: High-altitude arid plateaus of Changthang (Ladakh, $>4000\text{ metres}$ altitude); winter undercoat yields ultra-fine, warm, luxurious **Pashmina Cashmere** fibre ($12–14\text{ microns}$ diameter).
  - **Merino Sheep**: Native to Spain, commercialised in Australia; finest, softest apparel wool in the world with high crimp.
  - **Marwari Sheep**: Rajasthan desert; black face, coarse carpet wool, hardy walker.
  - **Nellore Sheep**: Andhra Pradesh; tallest sheep breed in India; hairy coat, raised exclusively for meat (mutton).
- **Poultry Science & Breeds**:
  - **Kadaknath (Kalamasi)**: Indigenous chicken of Jhabua and Dhar districts of Madhya Pradesh; skin, beak, wattles, tongue, meat, bones, and visceral peritoneum are all **jet black** due to intense deposition of the pigment **Melanin** (fibromelanosis); meat is rich in protein (~25%), iron, and essential amino acids, with very low fat and cholesterol; awarded a GI Tag.
  - **Aseel**: Indigenous to Andhra Pradesh and Telangana; massive muscular frame, erect posture, strong beak; celebrated for fighting stamina and ferocious courage; raised for meat and gamecock sport.
  - **White Leghorn**: Small, light-framed Mediterranean exotic bird; premier commercial egg-laying machine (layer), producing 280–300 white eggs per year.

## 2.8 Wildlife Conservation, Vultures & Diclofenac Crisis

- **The Indian Vulture Crisis & Veterinary Diclofenac**:
  - Between the early 1990s and 2007, populations of India's native *Gyps* vultures (White-rumped vulture *Gyps bengalensis*, Indian vulture *Gyps indicus*, and Slender-billed vulture *Gyps tenuirostris*) plummeted by **over 99%** (from tens of millions to a few thousand), representing the fastest catastrophic population crash of any bird species in recorded history.
  - **Causative Agent**: Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug (NSAID) **Diclofenac**, widely administered to cattle and buffaloes as a routine analgesic and painkiller for mastitis and arthritis.
  - **Pathology**: Scavenging vultures feeding on the carcasses of treated livestock within 48–72 hours of administration ingested toxic doses; vultures lack the enzyme to metabolise diclofenac, leading to acute kidney failure, catastrophic hyperuricaemia, and extensive chalky white precipitation of uric acid crystals on internal organs (**Visceral Gout**), causing death within days.
  - **Regulatory Ban**: Government of India formally **banned veterinary manufacture, sale, and use of Diclofenac in 2006**. In **2023**, the central government further banned veterinary use of **Ketoprofen and Aceclofenac** (which metabolises into diclofenac in cattle). **Meloxicam and Tolfenamic acid** are safe vulture-friendly alternatives.
  - Conservation actions: Vulture Conservation and Breeding Centres (VCBC) established at Pinjore (Haryana), Buxa (WB), and Rani (Assam) by BNHS.
- **Asiatic Lion Conservation in Gir**:
  - The Asiatic Lion (*Panthera leo persica*) is genetically distinct from African lions (smaller mane in males, longitudinal fold of skin along the belly).
  - Survives in the wild strictly in the **Gir National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary** and surrounding agro-pastoral landscapes of Saurashtra (Junagadh, Amreli, Bhavnagar, Gir Somnath in Gujarat).
  - Population increased from a critical bottleneck of ~20 individuals in the early 20th century to **891 individuals in May 2025** (up from 674 in 2020); Project Lion is active for habitat expansion (Barda Wildlife Sanctuary proposed as second home).
- **IUCN Red List Categories**:
  - **Extinct (EX)**: Beyond reasonable doubt that the last individual has died (e.g. Dodo, Passenger Pigeon, Tasmanian Tiger / Thylacine, Indian Cheetah declared extinct in India in 1952).
  - **Extinct in the Wild (EW)**: Survives only in cultivation, captivity, or as a naturalized population outside historic range.
  - **Critically Endangered (CR)**: Faces an extremely high risk of extinction in the wild (e.g. Great Indian Bustard, Gharial, White-rumped Vulture, Hangul / Kashmir Stag).
  - **Endangered (EN)**: Very high risk of wild extinction (e.g. Asiatic Lion, Royal Bengal Tiger, Asian Elephant, Gangetic Dolphin, Snow Leopard, Red Panda).
  - **Vulnerable (VU)**: High risk of endangerment in the wild (e.g. One-horned Rhinoceros, Nilgiri Tahr).

## Common Traps & Negative-Marking Eliminators

1. **Pearl source**: Pearl comes from a **mollusc** (oyster), not an insect — silk, honey, and lac are insect products.
2. **Animals named fish that are not fish**: Jellyfish, starfish, cuttlefish, crayfish, shellfish — none are true fishes.
3. **Cold-blooded vs warm-blooded**: Amphibians and reptiles are **poikilotherms**; birds and mammals are **homeotherms**.
4. **Marsupial vs placental**: Kangaroo is marsupial; whale and bat are placental mammals.
5. **Natural silk**: Pure protein, so it **contains nitrogen**; cotton has none.
6. **Honey bee caste**: Queen and workers are female; drones are male — drones develop from **unfertilised eggs** (parthenogenesis).
7. **Lac insect**: *Kerria lacca* on host trees; lac is a **resinous secretion**, not a plant gum.
8. **Snake locomotion**: Snakes have no limbs; movement uses **ribs and ventral scales**, not "legs that folded away" as a living trait.
9. **Bird bones**: Pneumatic (air-filled) bones reduce weight — do not mark them as "solid heavy bones".
10. **Ruminant stomach**: Four chambers — rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum; **abomasum** is the true glandular stomach.
