1. The structural and functional unit of the nervous system is the **Neuron**; adult neurons reside permanently in the quiescent $G_0$ phase of the cell cycle and **do not divide** (lack active centrioles).
2. A typical neuron consists of a **Cell Body (Soma/Cyton)** containing Nissl's granules, short branched receptive **Dendrites**, and a single long conducting **Axon** terminating in synaptic knobs; myelin sheath in the peripheral nervous system is secreted by **Schwann cells**.
3. Two neurons communicate across a microscopic gap called a **Synapse** via chemical messengers called **Neurotransmitters** (e.g. **Acetylcholine**, Noradrenaline, Dopamine, GABA); electrical sparks do not jump across ordinary synapses.
4. The central nervous system (CNS) is wrapped in three protective fibro-vascular membranes called **Meninges**: outer tough **Duramater**, middle web-like **Arachnoid mater**, and inner delicate vascular **Piamater** (Mnemonic: **DAP**); space between arachnoid and piamater is filled with shock-absorbing **Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)**.
5. **Cerebrum**: Largest part of the human brain (~80% of brain mass); seat of intelligence, conscious thought, memory, reasoning, voluntary motor control, speech (Broca's area), and sensation; right and left cerebral hemispheres are joined internally by a broad transverse curved band of white nerve fibres called the **Corpus Callosum**.
6. **Hypothalamus**: Situated at the base of the diencephalon; acts as the body's **Thermostat** (regulates core body temperature); controls hunger, thirst, satiety, sleep-wake cycles, emotional responses (anger, fear, pleasure via the limbic system), and regulates the pituitary gland via releasing and inhibiting neurohormones.
7. **Cerebellum**: Second largest part of the brain; displays an internal tree-like branching pattern of white matter called **Arbor Vitae** ("Tree of Life"); coordinates voluntary muscular contractions, maintains posture, and controls **physical equilibrium and body balance**; alcohol intoxication temporarily depresses the cerebellum, causing muscular uncoordination and a staggering gait.
8. **Medulla Oblongata**: Continues inferiorly into the spinal cord; houses vital autonomous reflex centres controlling **respiratory rhythm, cardiac rate, arterial blood pressure (vasomotor centre)**, as well as protective reflexes: swallowing, coughing, sneezing, vomiting, and salivation.
9. Peripheral nerves: Humans possess **$\mathbf{12\text{ pairs}}$ of Cranial Nerves** (arising from brain; Vagus nerve / CN X is the longest and most widely distributed) and **$\mathbf{31\text{ pairs}}$ of Spinal Nerves** (8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal; all spinal nerves are mixed nerves).
10. Autonomic Nervous System (ANS): **Sympathetic System** prepares the body for intense physical exertion ("Fight or Flight": dilates pupils, accelerates heart, dilates bronchi, inhibits gut peristalsis); **Parasympathetic System** restores calm ("Rest and Digest": constricts pupils, slows heart, stimulates digestive peristalsis and salivation).

## 6.1 Nervous System, Synaptic Transmission & Brain Topography

### Master Architecture: Neurons, Nerves & Autonomic Divisions
- **The Neuron (Structural & Functional Unit)**:
  - Longest specialized cells in the animal kingdom; generate and propagate electrochemical nerve impulses. Composed of **Soma (cell body)**, **Dendrites** (receptive inputs), and a single **Axon** (conductive output terminating in synaptic boutons).
  - Neurons communicate across intercellular gaps called **Synapses** via chemical neurotransmitters (**Acetylcholine, Norepinephrine, Dopamine, Serotonin**).
  - **Phylogenetic Distribution Fact**: All multicellular animals possess neurons **except Sponges (Porifera)** and Placozoans. Plants completely lack neurons.
- **Peripheral Nerve Geography**:
  - **Cranial Nerves**: **12 pairs** originating directly from the ventral base of the brain and brainstem, innervating structures of the head, neck, and viscera (e.g. Vagus nerve X).
  - **Spinal Nerves**: **31 pairs** of mixed nerves emerging bilaterally from the spinal cord segments:
    - **8 Cervical pairs** ($C_1–C_8$)
    - **12 Thoracic pairs** ($T_1–T_{12}$)
    - **5 Lumbar pairs** ($L_1–L_5$)
    - **5 Sacral pairs** ($S_1–S_5$)
    - **1 Coccygeal pair** ($Co_1$)
- **Autonomic Nervous System (ANS) Dual Antagonism**:
  - Regulated centrally by the **Hypothalamus** to govern involuntary homeostatic processes:
    - **Sympathetic Division ("Fight or Flight")**: Activated during stress, emergency, and intense exercise; increases heart rate and cardiac contractility, dilates bronchioles, dilates pupils, inhibits gastrointestinal peristalsis and salivation, diverts blood flow from skin and viscera to skeletal muscles; utilizes **Norepinephrine / Noradrenaline** (adrenergic fibres).
    - **Parasympathetic Division ("Rest and Digest")**: Activated in relaxed, quiescent states; slows heart rate, constricts pupils, stimulates digestive secretions, peristalsis, and glycogen synthesis; mediated primarily via the **Vagus nerve (CN X)**; utilizes **Acetylcholine** (cholinergic fibres).
  - **Enteric Nervous System (ENS)**: Semi-autonomous intrinsic neural meshwork in the gastrointestinal submucosal and myenteric plexuses; termed the **"Second Brain"**.
- **Brain Topography & Functional Specialization**:
  - **Cerebrum (Telencephalon)**: Largest cerebral mass divided into right and left cerebral hemispheres; controls conscious intellect, abstract reasoning, voluntary motor control, speech, sensory interpretation (vision, auditory, olfactory, somatosensory), and memory storage (specifically in the **Hippocampus**).
  - **Cerebellum ("Little Brain")**: Located in posterior cranial fossa beneath occipital lobes; coordinates complex voluntary movements, regulates muscle tone, and maintains **equilibrium, posture, and motor balance**.
  - **Medulla Oblongata**: Most inferior segment of brainstem transitioning directly into the spinal cord; indispensable autonomic regulatory centre governing **heart rate, blood pressure (vasomotor centre), respiration, swallowing, vomiting, coughing, and sneezing** (bulbar reflex centre).
  - **Hypothalamus (Master Visceral Control)**:
    - Governs **Thermoregulation** (biological thermostat maintaining normal internal core temperature at **98.6°F / 37.0°C / 310 K**).
    - Contains the primary **Satiety, Hunger, and Thirst centres**.
    - Regulates sleep-wake circadian patterns, sexual libido, and orchestrates pituitary endocrine secretion.
  - **Absence of Pain Receptors in Brain**: Brain parenchyma is entirely **devoid of nociceptors (pain receptors)**; thus, neurosurgical incisions into brain tissue provoke zero direct pain sensation!
  - **Reflex Arcs**: Rapid, involuntary protective responses (e.g. withdrawing a hand from a scalding surface); sensory afferents synapse directly onto interneurons and motor efferents within the **spinal cord**, initiating immediate motor withdrawal without awaiting slower cognitive processing by the cerebral cortex.
  - **Homeothermy vs Poikilothermy**:
    - **Endothermic Homeotherms (Warm-blooded)**: Mammals and birds; maintain a constant core internal body temperature independently of ambient external climate.
    - **Ectothermic Poikilotherms (Cold-blooded)**: Reptiles, amphibians, and fishes; core temperature fluctuates passively with environmental temperature.

### High-Yield Common Traps & Landmark Biochemistry Facts
- **Insulin Chemical Architecture**: A 51-amino acid anabolic peptide consisting of two polypeptide chains linked by disulfide bridges; contains a coordinated **Zinc ($Zn$)** ion. Extracted botanically from the tuberous roots of **Dahlia** or produced via recombinant DNA technology (*Humulin*).
- **Oxytocin ("Love / Cuddle / Parturition Hormone")**:
  - Stimulates forceful rhythmic contractions of the myometrium during childbirth via positive feedback loops.
  - Elicits contraction of myoepithelial cells surrounding mammary alveoli, causing **milk ejection (let-down)** in nursing mothers.
  - **Agronomic & Veterinary Misuse**: Illegally injected into dairy cows and buffaloes to force milk descent into udders, and sprayed onto cucurbitaceous vegetables (pumpkins, watermelons, bottle gourds) to induce rapid unnatural enlargement.
- **Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**: Secreted by placental syncytiotrophoblasts immediately upon blastocyst implantation; prevents involution of the corpus luteum, maintaining high progesterone levels during early pregnancy; rapidly excreted in maternal urine, serving as the biological marker for **clinical urine pregnancy tests**.
- **Ghrelin**: Secreted primarily by neuroendocrine cells of the empty gastric fundus; acts directly on the arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus to stimulate **appetite and hunger**.
- **Pheromones (Ectohormones)**: Volatile biochemicals secreted by exocrine glands into the external environment to transmit chemical messages to conspecifics. Classic example: **Bombykol**, a potent sex attractant secreted by female silkworm moths (*Bombyx mori*) capable of attracting males across kilometers.

## 6.3 Human Reproduction, Gametogenesis & Embryonic Development

### Master Physiological Facts & Reproductive Biology
- **Forms of Reproduction**:
  - **Asexual Reproduction**: Offspring produced from a single organism without gametic fusion; produces genetically identical clones.
  - **Sexual Reproduction**: Fusion of two haploid gametes ($n = 23$) formed via **meiosis** (spermatogenesis in male, oogenesis in female) to form a single diploid zygote ($2n = 46$).
  - **Viviparous Nature**: Humans give birth to living young (viviparous) with **internal fertilization** occurring inside the maternal reproductive tract.

### Male Reproductive Anatomy & Spermatogenesis
- **Testes**: Primary male sex organs; housed outside the abdominal pelvic cavity within the **scrotum** (maintains a scrotal temperature $2.0\text{--}2.5^\circ\text{C}$ lower than basal core body temperature, required for viable spermatogenesis).
- **Seminiferous Tubules**: Functional structural units of testes where spermatozoa are generated via spermatogenesis.
- **Sertoli Cells ("Nurse / Sustentacular Cells")**: Somatic cells lining seminiferous tubules; provide essential nutrition and structural support to developing germ cells during spermatogenesis; activated by **FSH (Follicle-Stimulating Hormone)** from adenohypophysis and express surface FSH receptors.
- **Leydig Cells (Interstitial Cells)**: Nestled in connective tissue between seminiferous tubules; synthesize and secrete **Testosterone** (androgen) under stimulation by **LH / ICSH**; drives spermatogenesis and male secondary sexual characteristics (deepening of voice, facial/body hair, muscularity).
- **Accessory Ducts & Glands**:
  - **Epididymis**: Site of sperm maturation and physiological storage.
  - **Vas Deferens (Sperm Duct)**: Ascends into pelvic cavity, loops over urinary bladder to join ejaculatory duct.
  - **Seminal Vesicles**: Secrete alkaline seminal fluid rich in fructose (energy source for sperm motility) and prostaglandins.
  - **Prostate Gland**: Single accessory exocrine gland surrounding prostatic urethra; secretes alkaline enzymatic fluid enhancing sperm motility and neutralizing vaginal acidity.
  - **Male Urethra**: Shared terminal conduit serving dual function: discharge of seminal fluid (ejaculation) and urine (longer than female urethra).

### Female Reproductive Anatomy & Oogenesis
- **Ovaries**: Paired primary female reproductive organs situated within lower pelvic abdominal cavity; produce female gametes (**ova/eggs**) and secrete steroid hormones (**Estrogen** and **Progesterone**). Each ovary cyclically releases one mature ovum alternately each month (**Ovulation**).
- **Fallopian Tubes (Oviducts / Uterine Tubes)**: Paired ciliated muscular conduits extending from ovaries to uterine fundus; fimbriae catch ovulated secondary oocyte; **ampullary-isthmic junction is the anatomical site where fertilization normally takes place**.
- **Uterus (Womb)**: Pear-shaped thick muscular organ (composed of outer perimetrium, thick smooth muscle myometrium, and inner vascular glandular endometrium); site of blastocyst **implantation** and fetal gestation.
- **Cervix**: Muscular ring at lower uterine pole; canal dilates dramatically during parturition under oxytocin and relaxin influence.
- **Vagina**: Fibromuscular canal receiving penis during copulation and functioning as birth canal during delivery.
- **Female Urethra**: Strictly restricted to urinary voiding (anatomically distinct from genital canal).

### Germ Layers & Tissue Fate Mapping
- **Ectoderm (Outer Layer)**:
  - Nervous system (Brain, spinal cord, motor/sensory peripheral nerves).
  - Sensory organs (Retina, lens, inner ear).
  - Integumentary appendages (Epidermis of skin, hair follicles, nails, mammary glands).
- **Mesoderm (Middle Layer)**:
  - Musculoskeletal tissues (All skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscles; bones, cartilage, axial skeleton).
  - Cardiovascular system (**Heart**, blood vessels, lymphatic channels, blood cells, bone marrow).
  - Urogenital system (Kidneys, ureters, gonads—testes/ovaries, reproductive ducts).
  - Adrenal cortex, spleen, and serous membranes (pleura, pericardium, peritoneum).
- **Endoderm (Inner Layer)**:
  - Epithelial mucosal lining of gastrointestinal tract and respiratory tract (pharynx, trachea, bronchi, lungs).
  - Visceral digestive and endocrine glands (Liver parenchyma, pancreas, thyroid, parathyroids).
  - Urinary bladder lining and parts of urethra.

### Extraembryonic Membranes & Placental Support System
- **Amnion**: Innermost thin protective sac enclosing developing embryo; filled with **amniotic fluid**.
  - **Amniotic Fluid Functions**: Cushions fetus against external mechanical shock/pressures, maintains stable microthermal environment, allows musculoskeletal motility, and **prevents embryonic desiccation and structural adhesions**.
- **Chorion**: Outermost extraembryonic membrane; projects **chorionic villi** into maternal endometrium to establish the fetal portion of the **Placenta**; mediates critical gas and nutrient exchange.
- **Allantois**: Early vascularized membrane contributing to umbilical blood vessels; handles nitrogenous liquid wastes and facilitates respiratory gas transfer in early embryogenesis.
- **Yolk Sac**: Site of primitive hematopoiesis (early red blood cell formation) prior to hepatic development; contributes to initial gut tube formation.
- **Placenta**: Specialized fetomaternal organ providing nutrition, respiratory gas exchange ($O_2/CO_2$), and metabolic waste removal; produces pregnancy-sustaining hormones (**hCG, Progesterone, Estrogen, Human Placental Lactogen - hPL**). Connected to fetal navel via the **umbilical cord** (containing 2 umbilical arteries and 1 umbilical vein).
- **Parturition Dynamics**: Normal gestation period is **~38 weeks (266 days) post-conception (~40 weeks / 9 months 10 days post-LMP)**. Labor initiation involves fetal-maternal endocrine cascades triggering posterior pituitary release of **Oxytocin**, inducing forceful rhythmic myometrial contractions and progressive cervical dilation.

## 6.4 Skeletal System, Cartilage, Joints & Muscular Dynamics

### Master Comparative Architecture: Bones, Cartilage, Ligaments & Tendons
| Connective Tissue | Cellular / Matrix Components | Vascularity & Innervation | Mechanical Function & Clinical Focus |
|-------------------|------------------------------|---------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Bone (Osseous Tissue)** | Osteocytes, Osteoblasts, **Osteoclasts**; Matrix = **Collagen fibres (organic)** + **Calcium phosphate / Hydroxyapatite (inorganic, ~70%)** | Richly vascularized (Haversian canals), heavily innervated | Rigid structural framework; mineral reservoir ($Ca, P$); houses red bone marrow for **hematopoiesis**. |
| **Cartilage** | **Chondrocytes** (isolated in lacunae); Matrix = Glycosaminoglycans, proteoglycans, collagen & elastin | **Avascular & Aneural** (nourishment via diffusion only; heals very slowly) | Resilient, flexible cushion covering bone ends at synovial joints; forms structural shape of ear pinna, external nose tip, epiglottis, and tracheal rings. |
| **Ligaments** | Dense regular fibrous connective tissue rich in **elastic collagen fibres** | Poorly vascularized | **Connects Bone to Bone**; stabilizes joints, prevents abnormal dislocation. |
| **Tendons (Sinew)** | Dense parallel bundles of **white non-elastic collagen fibres** | Poorly vascularized | **Attaches Muscle to Bone**; withstands high mechanical tensile stress during contraction. |

### Long Bone Architecture & Bone Marrow Dynamics
- **Structural Regions**:
  - **Diaphysis (Shaft)**: Hollow cylinder of dense compact bone designed to resist bending; inner central medullary cavity contains **Yellow Bone Marrow** (rich in adipocytes/triglycerides for energy storage).
  - **Epiphyses (Flared Ends)**: Cancellous (spongy) bone composed of a trabecular lattice bathed in **Red Bone Marrow**, the primary site of **hematopoiesis** (production of erythrocytes/RBCs, leukocytes/WBCs, and thrombocytes/platelets).
- **Osteoclasts vs Osteoblasts**:
  - **Osteoblasts**: Bone-forming cells that secrete osteoid (unmineralized collagen matrix) and promote calcium phosphate crystallization.
  - **Osteoclasts**: Multinucleated giant cells derived from monocyte-macrophage lineage; secrete acid and lysosomal enzymes to resorb and break down calcified bone matrix. Essential for constant skeletal remodeling, blood calcium homeostasis, and fracture repair.

### Axial & Appendicular Skeleton Master Facts
- **Total Adult Bones**: **206** (Infant skeleton contains ~300 bones, which fuse during development).
  - **Axial Skeleton (80 bones)**:
    - **Skull (28 or 29 bones)**: 8 cranial (protects brain), 14 facial (mandible is the only movable skull bone), 6 auditory ossicles (**Malleus / hammer (2), Incus / anvil (2), Stapes / stirrup (2 — smallest bone in human body, located in middle ear)**), and 1 **Hyoid bone** (isolated U-shaped bone in the throat).
    - **Vertebral Column (26 adult bones, derived from 33 fetal vertebrae)**: 
      - Cervical vertebrae: **7 (C1 to C7)**. **C1 is Atlas**, **C2 is Axis**. The **Atlantoaxial joint** between Atlas and Axis is a **Pivot (rotary) synovial joint**, permitting horizontal turning/rotation of the head.
      - Thoracic vertebrae: **12**.
      - Lumbar vertebrae: **5** (largest, bears torso weight).
      - Sacrum: **1** (5 fused vertebrae).
      - Coccyx: **1** (4 fused tail vertebrae).
    - **Thoracic Rib Cage (25 bones)**: **Sternum (1 breastbone)** + **12 pairs of ribs (24 total)**:
      - **True Ribs (Pairs 1–7)**: Attached posteriorly to thoracic vertebrae and anteriorly *directly* to the sternum via costal cartilage.
      - **False Ribs (Pairs 8–10)**: Attached posteriorly to vertebrae, but anteriorly join the costal cartilage of the 7th rib rather than the sternum directly.
      - **Floating Ribs (Pairs 11–12)**: Attached posteriorly to vertebrae but have *no anterior connection*; remain unattached anteriorly.
  - **Appendicular Skeleton (126 bones)**:
    - **Pectoral Girdle (4)**: Clavicle / Collarbone (2) + Scapula / Shoulder blade (2).
    - **Upper Limbs (60)**: Humerus (2), Radius & Ulna (4), Carpals / Wrist bones (16: 8 in each wrist), Metacarpals / Palm (10), Phalanges / Fingers (28: 14 per hand, 2 in thumb, 3 each in other digits).
    - **Pelvic Girdle (2)**: Os Coxae / Hip bones (2: fusion of ilium, ischium, and pubis).
    - **Lower Limbs (60)**: **Femur / Thigh bone (2 — longest, heaviest, and strongest bone in human body)**, Patella / Kneecap (2), Tibia & Fibula / Shank (4), Tarsals / Ankle (14: 7 per ankle), Metatarsals / Foot (10), Phalanges / Toes (28: 14 per foot).

### Exoskeleton, Keratin & Nails Physiology
- **Exoskeleton Structures**: Mammalian hair, nails, horns, hooves, and claws are composed of fibrous **$\alpha$-keratin**. Reptilian scales and bird feathers contain **$\beta$-keratin**.
- **Painless Nail & Hair Cutting**: Nails and outer hair shafts consist of densely compacted dead keratinized epithelial cells entirely devoid of blood vessels and nerve endings; hence, cutting them causes zero pain.

### Clinical Pathology of Joints & Orthopedic Surgery
- **Arthritis Classifications**:
  - **Osteoarthritis (OA)**: Most prevalent joint disorder; non-inflammatory wear-and-tear degeneration of the articular cartilage cushioning bone ends, causing painful bone-on-bone friction, osteophyte (spur) formation, and joint stiffness.
  - **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)**: Chronic systemic autoimmune disorder wherein the body's immune cells attack the **synovial membrane**, causing severe inflammation, pannus formation, erosion of opposing articular surfaces, and symmetrical joint deformities (fingers, wrists, knees).
  - **Gout (Metabolic Arthritis)**: Caused by chronic hyperuricemia (elevated blood uric acid from purine breakdown); results in the supersaturation and precipitation of needle-like **Monosodium urate crystals** within joint synovial cavities (most frequently the first metatarsophalangeal joint of the big toe), causing acute excruciating inflammatory flares.
- **Arthroplasty**: Surgical orthopedic reconstruction or total replacement of a diseased, damaged, or ankylosed joint using synthetic metallic, ceramic, or polyethylene prostheses (most commonly **Total Hip Replacement** or Total Knee Replacement).

### Chemical & Elemental Composition of Human Body
- **Water Percentage**:
  - Adult Male: **60–65%** | Adult Female: **55–60%** (lower water % due to higher proportion of subcutaneous adipose tissue) | Newborn Infants: **75–78%** (drops to 65–70% by 1 year).
  - **Organ Water Distribution**: Lungs (~83%), Muscles & Kidneys (~79%), Brain & Heart (~73%), Skin (~64%), **Bones (~31%)**.
  - Daily requirement: Adult male ~3.0 Litres, adult female ~2.2 Litres.
- **Master Elemental Abundance in Human Body**:
  - 6 elements comprise **98.7%** of human body mass:
    1. **Oxygen (O)**: **65.0%** (Most abundant element in human body, primarily as $H_2O$)
    2. **Carbon (C)**: **18.5%** (Structural backbone of all organic molecules)
    3. **Hydrogen (H)**: **9.5%**
    4. **Nitrogen (N)**: **3.2%** (Constituent of amino acids, proteins, DNA/RNA)
    5. **Calcium (Ca)**: **1.5%** (Most abundant mineral; primary cation in bone mineral)
    6. **Phosphorus (P)**: **1.0%** (Nucleic acids, ATP, calcium phosphate)
  - Remaining Macro-elements (1.2%): Potassium ($K$: 0.4%), Sulphur ($S$: 0.3%), Sodium ($Na$: 0.2%), Chlorine ($Cl$: 0.2%), Magnesium ($Mg$: 0.1%).
  - **Total Body Content Ranking of Metals**: $\text{Copper } (Cu) < \text{Iron } (Fe) < \text{Sodium } (Na) < \text{Potassium } (K)$.

### Human Dentition & Teeth Mineralization
- **Diphyodont Dentition**: Humans develop two successive sets of teeth:
  - **Deciduous (Milk / Primary) Teeth**: **20 teeth** (appear at ~6 months; 8 incisors, 4 canines, 8 primary molars; premolars are absent in milk dentition).
  - **Permanent Teeth**: **32 adult teeth** (16 in maxilla/upper jaw, 16 in mandible/lower jaw: 8 incisors, 4 canines, 8 premolars, 12 molars).
  - **Monophyodont Teeth**: The **12 permanent molars** (including the 4 wisdom teeth) erupt only once and are never replaced.
- **Tooth Enamel**: The shiny white outer layer covering the crown; the **hardest substance in the human body**. Composed of crystalline **Hydroxyapatite** (calcium phosphate mineral, $Ca_{10}(PO_4)_6(OH)_2$). Maintained by Vitamins A, C, D, calcium, and trace **Fluoride** (which converts hydroxyapatite to acid-resistant fluorapatite).
- **Dental Plaque**: A complex oral biofilm consisting of **food debris + saliva glycoproteins + bacterial colonies + mouth organic acids** adhering tenaciously to enamel surfaces.

### Muscular System Physiology & Extremes
- **Biomechanical Rule**: **Muscles can only pull (contract); they can NEVER push a bone!** Movement is produced by coordinated action of **antagonistic pairs** (e.g. contracting biceps flexes the elbow while relaxing triceps; contracting triceps extends the elbow).
- **Physical Changes during Contraction**: Muscle fibres become **shorter, thicker, and stiffer**.
- **Longest Muscle**: **Sartorius** ("tailor's muscle"), a strap-like muscle running diagonally across the anterior compartment of the thigh from the anterior superior iliac spine to the medial tibia.
- **Smallest Muscle**: **Stapedius**, just ~1–2 mm long, located within the tympanic cavity of the middle ear; dampens excessive vibrations of the stapes footplate to protect the inner ear from loud acoustic trauma.
- **Strongest Muscle**: **Masseter** (jaw chewing muscle, based on bite force per unit weight); can exert an occlusal biting force exceeding **90 kg (200 lb)** on molars.

## Common Traps & Negative-Marking Eliminators

1. **Myopia correction**: **Concave** lens; hypermetropia = **convex** lens.
2. **Thyroxine element**: Needs **iodine** — deficiency → goitre.
3. **Insulin source**: **Beta cells** of islets of Langerhans — alpha cells make glucagon.
4. **Adrenaline**: Emergency hormone from adrenal **medulla**; cortex makes steroids.
5. **Neuron impulse**: Travels axon terminal via synapse with neurotransmitters — not continuous cytoplasm jump in chemical synapses.
6. **Male vs female gamete**: Sperm is motile and tiny; ovum is larger and non-motile.
7. **Fertilisation site**: Usually **fallopian tube** (ampulla), not uterus.
8. **Bone vs cartilage**: Bone is hard with osteocytes; cartilage is flexible with chondrocytes — ear pinna is cartilage.
9. **Ball-and-socket vs hinge**: Shoulder/hip = ball-and-socket; elbow/knee = hinge.
10. **Reflex arc**: Sensory → interneuron (often) → motor — brain is not required for simple spinal reflexes.
