<summary><strong>🎯 Consolidated — 50 Must-Score Facts</strong> <span class="st-toggle-hint">(Click to Expand)</span></summary>

1. **Carbon ($Z=6$)** exhibits exceptional chemical versatility due to two foundational properties: **Catenation** (the unique ability of carbon atoms to link together via strong covalent bonds to form long open chains, branched structures, or rings) and **Tetravalency** (sharing four valence electrons to form 4 covalent bonds).
2. The $C-C$ single bond dissociation enthalpy is exceptionally high (**$348\text{ kJ/mol}$**), imparting extraordinary thermodynamic stability to organic chains (unlike Silicon, whose $Si-Si$ bond energy is only $222\text{ kJ/mol}$).
3. **Hybridization States of Carbon**:
   - $sp^3$ (Tetrahedral, bond angle $109^\circ 28'$, all 4 are single $\sigma$-bonds, e.g., Methane, Diamond, Alkanes).
   - $sp^2$ (Trigonal planar, bond angle $120^\circ$, one double bond containing $1\sigma + 1\pi$, e.g., Ethene, Graphite, Graphene, Benzene).
   - $sp$ (Linear, bond angle $180^\circ$, one triple bond containing $1\sigma + 2\pi$, e.g., Ethyne/Acetylene).
4. **Alkanes** ($C_n H_{2n+2}$): Saturated hydrocarbons with single bonds. Formerly termed **paraffins** (Latin *parum affinis* = little affinity) due to their chemical inertness toward acids and bases under ambient conditions.
5. **Methane ($CH_4$)**: The simplest alkane; known historically as **Marsh Gas** because it is generated by anaerobic methanogenic archaebacteria in stagnant swamps, waterlogged paddy fields, ruminant animal digestive tracts (cattle cud-chewing), and decomposing municipal landfill sites. It is the primary constituent of Natural Gas ($\approx 85–95\%$) and Biogas/Gobar Gas ($\approx 55–70\%$).
6. **Alkenes** ($C_n H_{2n}$): Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond ($C=C$). Known as **olefins** (oil-forming).
7. **Ethylene / Ethene ($C_2H_4$)**: The only gaseous **plant hormone** that naturally promotes fruit ripening and leaf abscission; also serves as the chemical monomer for manufacturing polyethylene/polythene.
8. **Alkynes** ($C_n H_{2n-2}$): Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon triple bond ($C \equiv C$).
9. **Acetylene / Ethyne ($C_2H_2$)**: Burns in pure oxygen to produce an intensely hot **Oxy-acetylene flame ($\approx 3200^\circ\text{C}$)** used for industrial cutting and welding of steel.
10. **Artificial Fruit Ripening**: Industrial ripening often uses **Calcium Carbide ($CaC_2$)**, which reacts with atmospheric moisture to release acetylene gas ($CaC_2 + 2H_2O \to Ca(OH)_2 + C_2H_2 \uparrow$). *Must-Score*: Commercial calcium carbide typically contains toxic trace impurities of arsenic hydride (arsine, $AsH_3$) and phosphorus hydride (phosphine, $PH_3$), leading FSSAI to ban its use in food trade.
11. **Benzene ($C_6H_6$)**: The archetypal aromatic hydrocarbon. Proposed by August Kekulé in 1865 as a planar hexagonal ring of 6 $sp^2$ carbon atoms with alternating double bonds. Thermodynamically stabilized by resonance with a delocalized cloud of $6\pi$ electrons complying with **Hückel's Rule ($4n+2$ $\pi$-electrons, where $n=1$)**.
12. **Allotropes of Carbon**: Pure elemental carbon exists as crystalline allotropes (**Diamond, Graphite, Fullerenes, Carbon Nanotubes, Graphene**) and amorphous forms (Coal, Charcoal, Lampblack/Carbon Black, Coke).
13. **Diamond**: Each carbon is $sp^3$ hybridized and bonded tetrahedrally to 4 other carbons in a rigid 3D framework ($C-C$ bond length $1.54\text{ \AA}$). It is the hardest known natural substance (Mohs 10), an electrical insulator, but an exceptional thermal conductor.
14. **Graphite**: Each carbon is $sp^2$ hybridized and bonded to 3 others in planar hexagonal rings ($C-C$ bond length $1.42\text{ \AA}$). Layers are held by weak van der Waals dispersion forces ($3.35\text{ \AA}$ gap). Delocalized $\pi$ electrons allow it to **conduct electricity**. It is soft, slippery, and acts as a **solid dry lubricant**.
15. **Thermodynamic Stability of Carbon**: **Graphite is the thermodynamically most stable allotrope of carbon** under standard ambient conditions ($298.15\text{ K}, 1\text{ bar}$); its standard enthalpy of formation is defined as zero ($\Delta_f H^\circ = 0$). Diamond is metastable ($\Delta_f H^\circ = +1.9\text{ kJ/mol}$).
16. **Fullerenes ($C_{60}$, Buckminsterfullerene)**: Discovered in 1985 by Harold Kroto, Robert Curl, and Richard Smalley. Spherical cage resembling a soccer ball ("buckyball"), composed of **20 hexagonal rings and 12 pentagonal rings**.
17. **Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs)**: Discovered by Sumio Iijima in 1991. Cylindrical tubules of rolled graphene sheets with diameters on the nanometer scale; possess tensile strength $>50$ times that of steel and high thermal/electrical conductivity.
18. **Graphene**: Isolated in 2004 by Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov at Manchester University using adhesive tape exfoliation of graphite (Nobel Prize in Physics, 2010). A single atom-thick, two-dimensional honeycomb sheet of $sp^2$ carbon; nearly transparent ($97.7\%$ light transmission), ballistic electron conductor, and over $100\text{ times}$ stronger than structural steel.
19. **Black Gold**: Synthesized in 2019 by scientists at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR, Mumbai) by rearranging the inter-nanoparticle spacing of gold nanoparticles; absorbs visible and near-infrared solar light, acting as a solar water desalination absorber and $CO_2$ conversion catalyst.
20. **Chronology of Landmark Advanced Materials**:
    $$\text{Kevlar (1965)} \to \text{Fullerenes (1985)} \to \text{Carbon Nanotubes (1991)} \to \text{Graphene (2004)} \to \text{Black Gold (2019)}$$
21. **Polymer**: A high-molecular-weight macromolecule formed by the repetitive chemical linkage of small, reactive monomer molecules ($M_w \approx 10^3\text{ to }10^7\text{ u}$).
22. **Addition (Chain-Growth) Polymers**: Formed by the repeated addition of unsaturated monomer units (containing $C=C$ or $C \equiv C$) without the elimination of any small byproduct molecules (e.g., Polyethylene, Polypropylene, PVC, Polystyrene, Teflon).
23. **Condensation (Step-Growth) Polymers**: Formed by repeated condensation reactions between bi-functional or poly-functional monomers, accompanied by the **elimination of small byproduct molecules** such as water ($H_2O$), alcohol ($CH_3OH$), or ammonia ($NH_3$) (e.g., Nylon-6,6, Terylene/Dacron, Bakelite, Melamine).
24. **Classification Based on Intermolecular Forces**:
    - **Elastomers**: Possess the **weakest intermolecular forces** (van der Waals forces). Polymer chains are randomly coiled and held together by occasional cross-links, allowing them to stretch under tension and snap back to original shape (e.g., Natural rubber, Vulcanized rubber, Buna-S, Buna-N, Neoprene).
    - **Fibres**: Possess the **strongest intermolecular forces** (extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding or strong dipole-dipole interactions). Long, linear chains pack tightly into crystalline arrays, providing high tensile strength and high melting points (e.g., Nylon-6,6, Terylene, Kevlar, Silk, Wool).
    - **Thermoplastics**: Possess **intermediate intermolecular forces** between elastomers and fibres. Linear or slightly branched polymers that **soften on heating and harden upon cooling reversibly**; can be repeatedly melted, remoulded, and recycled (e.g., Polyethylene, PVC, Polystyrene, Teflon).
    - **Thermosetting Plastics**: Form extensive **three-dimensional cross-linked network polymers**. During moulding, heating triggers irreversible chemical cross-linking. Once set, they **cannot be remelted, softened, or reshaped**; excessive heating causes degradation. They are completely non-recyclable (e.g., Bakelite, Melamine-formaldehyde, Urea-formaldehyde).
25. **Polyethylene / Polythene**:
    - **Low-Density Polyethylene (LDPE)**: Polymerized under extreme pressure ($1000–2000\text{ atm}$) and temperature ($200–350^\circ\text{C}$) via a free-radical mechanism. Possesses extensive branched chains that prevent close packing, yielding low density and high flexibility; used for squeeze bottles, packaging films, and flexible tubing.
    - **High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE)**: Polymerized under low pressure ($6–7\text{ atm}$) and moderate temperature ($60–70^\circ\text{C}$) in the presence of a **Ziegler-Natta Catalyst** (triethylaluminium and titanium tetrachloride, $Al(C_2H_5)_3 + TiCl_4$). Consists of linear chains that pack tightly into crystalline structures, providing high density, rigidity, and chemical resistance; used for heavy buckets, dustbins, pipes, and milk crates.
26. **Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC)**: Formed by the addition polymerization of **Vinyl Chloride ($CH_2=CHCl$)**. An electrical insulator used for drainage pipes, electrical wire coatings, raincoats, and vinyl flooring.
27. **Teflon (PTFE, Polytetrafluoroethylene)**: Formed by the addition polymerization of **Tetrafluoroethylene ($CF_2=CF_2$)** under high pressure with persulphate catalyst:
    $$n(CF_2=CF_2) \to [-CF_2-CF_2-]_n$$
    Possesses high thermal stability (operating range up to $260^\circ\text{C}$), chemical inertness against boiling aqua regia and strong alkalis, and an exceptionally low coefficient of friction; used as a **non-stick coating on kitchen cookware**, low-friction machinery bearings, and chemical valve gaskets.
28. **Bakelite (Phenol-Formaldehyde Resin)**: The first fully synthetic commercial plastic, invented in 1907 by Belgian-American chemist **Leo Baekeland**:
    - A **thermosetting cross-linked polymer** formed by the condensation of **Phenol ($C_6H_5OH$)** with **Formaldehyde ($HCHO$)** in the presence of an acid or base catalyst.
    - The initial linear intermediate polymer is **Novolac** (used in paints and wood lacquers). Heating Novolac with excess formaldehyde produces the rigid, cross-linked infusible solid Bakelite.
    - An electrical insulator resistant to heat and chemicals; standard material for **electrical switches, plugs, sockets, distributor caps**, and heat-insulated handles of frying pans and pressure cookers.
29. **Melamine-Formaldehyde Resin**: A thermosetting polymer formed by the condensation of Melamine (2,4,6-triamino-1,3,5-triazine) with Formaldehyde. Highly durable and impact-resistant; used to manufacture **unbreakable household crockery, dinnerware**, and decorative laminate countertops.
30. **Polystyrene**: Addition polymer of **Styrene (phenylethene, $C_6H_5-CH=CH_2$)**. In its foamed expanded form, it is commercially known as **Thermocol**; used as thermal insulation in refrigerators and packaging material for fragile electronics.
31. **Rayon ("Artificial Silk")**:
    - A **semi-synthetic regenerated cellulose fibre**, NOT a synthetic petrochemical plastic or modified starch!
    - **Manufacturing**: Natural cellulose (from wood pulp or cotton linters) is dissolved in caustic soda ($NaOH$) and carbon disulphide ($CS_2$) to form a viscous cellulose xanthate solution ("viscose"), which is extruded through fine spinnerets into a bath of dilute sulphuric acid ($H_2SO_4$). The acid regenerates pure cellulose as continuous filaments.
    - Absorbs moisture, drapes like silk, and blends well with cotton or wool.
32. **Nylon-6,6**:
    - The first fully synthetic commercial textile fibre, synthesized in 1935 by **Wallace Carothers** at DuPont.
    - A **condensation polyamide** formed by the reaction of **Adipic Acid** ($6\text{ carbons}$, $HOOC-(CH_2)_4-COOH$) with **Hexamethylenediamine** ($6\text{ carbons}$, $H_2N-(CH_2)_6-NH_2$) at $280^\circ\text{C}$ under pressure:
      $$n HOOC(CH_2)_4COOH + n H_2N(CH_2)_6NH_2 \to [-CO(CH_2)_4CONH(CH_2)_6NH-]_n + 2n H_2O$$
    - Joined by peptide-like **amide linkages ($-CO-NH-$)**. High tensile strength, elasticity, and abrasion resistance; used for toothbrush bristles, tyre cords, ropes, climbing gear, and parachutes.
33. **Nylon-6 (Perlon)**: Formed by the ring-opening polymerization of the cyclic monomer **Caprolactam** (a 7-membered ring containing 6 carbon atoms) when heated with water at $260^\circ\text{C}$.
34. **Terylene / Dacron (Polyester)**:
    - Formed by the condensation polymerization of **Ethylene Glycol ($CH_2OH-CH_2OH$)** with **Terephthalic Acid ($p-HOOC-C_6H_4-COOH$)** at $200^\circ\text{C}$ with zinc acetate–antimony trioxide catalyst.
    - Joined by **ester linkages ($-COO-$)**. Crease-resistant, quick-drying, and resistant to moths and chemical attack; blended with cotton to make **terycot** and with wool to make **terywool**.
35. **Polyethylene Terephthalate (PET)**: The chemical equivalent of Dacron used for blowing rigid, transparent, recyclable beverage bottles, food jars, and synthetic fleece jackets.
36. **Kevlar (Poly-para-phenylene terephthalamide)**:
    - An aromatic polyamide (**aramid fibre**) synthesized in 1965 by American chemist **Stephanie Kwolek** at DuPont.
    - Monomers: **1,4-phenylenediamine** and **terephthaloyl chloride**.
    - Liquid crystalline polymer chains align parallel, held together by dense networks of intermolecular hydrogen bonds between amide groups and $\pi-\pi$ aromatic stacking.
    - Possesses an extraordinary tensile strength-to-weight ratio (**$\approx 5\text{ times}$ stronger than structural steel on an equal weight basis**); widely used for **bulletproof tactical vests, combat helmets, military armor plating**, cut-resistant gloves, and aerospace structural composites.
37. **Lexan / Polycarbonate**: A high-impact, optically transparent thermoplastic polyester formed from bisphenol A and phosgene; used for **bulletproof transparent security windows**, riot shields, and astronaut helmet visors.
38. **Polymethyl Methacrylate (PMMA / Plexiglass / Lucite / Perspex)**: Formed by the addition polymerization of methyl methacrylate; a transparent, shatterproof thermoplastic substitute for glass used in airplane cockpit canopies, illuminated signs, and dental prosthetics.
39. **Natural Rubber**:
    - A natural addition polymer extracted from the milky latex of the rubber tree (*Hevea brasiliensis*).
    - Chemically, it is strictly **cis-1,4-polyisoprene**:
      $$n(CH_2=C(CH_3)-CH=CH_2) \to [-CH_2-C(CH_3)=CH-CH_2-]_n$$
    - Monomer: **Isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene)**.
    - Because of its all-*cis* stereochemical configuration, polymer chains cannot pack closely and remain coiled, conferring high elasticity. In contrast, the all-*trans* isomer is **Gutta-percha**, which is non-elastic, hard, and crystalline.
40. **Vulcanization of Rubber**:
    - Discovered serendipitously in 1839 by **Charles Goodyear**.
    - Raw natural rubber is sticky and soft at warm temperatures ($>33^\circ\text{C}$), brittle at low temperatures ($<10^\circ\text{C}$), and soluble in non-polar organic solvents (benzene, petrol).
    - **Mechanism**: Heating raw rubber with **$3–5\%$ elemental Sulphur** and basic activators ($ZnO$) at $140^\circ\text{C}$.
    - Sulphur atoms form stable **disulphide cross-links ($-S-S-$)** across the reactive double bonds of adjacent polyisoprene chains, locking them into a three-dimensional elastic network.
    - Vulcanized rubber resists abrasion, remains elastic across $-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $100^\circ\text{C}$, does not absorb water, and is used for **heavy-duty automobile tyres**. If sulphur content is increased to $30–35\%$, extremely hard, non-elastic **Ebonite (vulcanite)** is formed.
41. **Neoprene (Polychloroprene)**:
    - The first synthetic commercial elastomer (invented at DuPont, 1930).
    - Monomer: **Chloroprene (2-chloro-1,3-butadiene)**:
      $$n(CH_2=C(Cl)-CH=CH_2) \to [-CH_2-C(Cl)=CH-CH_2-]_n$$
    - Highly resistant to petroleum oils, greases, gasoline, and atmospheric ozone oxidation; widely used for manufacturing fuel delivery hoses, automotive engine gaskets, conveyor belts, and diving wetsuits.
42. **Buna-S (SBR, Styrene-Butadiene Rubber)**:
    - A synthetic copolymer formed by the addition polymerization of **75% 1,3-Butadiene and 25% Styrene** in the presence of sodium catalyst ("Bu" = Butadiene, "Na" = Natrium/Sodium catalyst, "S" = Styrene).
    - High abrasion resistance and mechanical toughness; primarily used for automotive road tyres.
43. **Buna-N (NBR, Nitrile Rubber)**:
    - A synthetic copolymer of **1,3-Butadiene and Acrylonitrile ($CH_2=CH-CN$)**.
    - Resistant to the swelling action of petrol, oils, and organic solvents; used for oil seals, fuel tank linings, and laboratory safety gloves.
44. **Natural Protein Fibres**:
    - **Silk**: Natural structural animal protein fibre secreted by silkworms (*Bombyx mori*). Composed of **Fibroin** ($\approx 75\%$, rich in glycine, alanine, and serine forming pleated $\beta$-sheets) bound together by an outer gummy protein sheath called **sericin** ($\approx 25\%$).
    - **Wool**: Natural protein fibre harvested from sheep fleece. Composed of **$\alpha$-Keratin**, a fibrous protein with an $\alpha$-helical secondary structure stabilized by hydrogen bonds and extensive **cystine disulphide bridges ($-S-S-$)**; burning wool releases a characteristic pungent odor of burning sulphur.
45. **Cellulose**: The most abundant organic polymer on Earth. A linear homopolysaccharide composed of **$\beta$-D-glucose** units joined by **$\beta-1,4$ glycosidic bonds**. Humans cannot digest cellulose because our digestive tract lacks the enzyme cellulase.
46. **Starch**: Plant storage carbohydrate consisting of **$\alpha$-Amylose** (water-soluble, linear $\alpha-1,4$ polymer) and **Amylopectin** (water-insoluble, branched $\alpha-1,4$ and $\alpha-1,6$ polymer). Gives a characteristic deep blue-black color with iodine solution.
47. **PHBV (Poly-$\beta$-hydroxybutyrate-co-$\beta$-hydroxyvalerate)**: A commercially significant **biodegradable aliphatic polyester** synthesized by bacterial fermentation (*Ralstonia eutropha*) from glucose and propionic acid; degrades completely in soil and landfill environments via microbial enzymes.
48. **Glyptal**: A cross-linked polyester resin formed by the condensation of Ethylene Glycol with Phthalic Acid; used in manufacturing heavy-duty industrial paints, lacquers, and electrical building primers.
49. **Urea-Formaldehyde Resin**: Thermosetting adhesive resin used for manufacturing particle boards, decorative laminated sheets, and unbreakable plastic bottle closures.
50. **Plasticizer**: Low-molecular-weight organic liquids (e.g., dioctyl phthalate, DOP) added to rigid polymers like PVC to lower glass transition temperature ($T_g$) and impart mechanical softness and flexibility (used for garden hoses, synthetic leather).

| Confused Pair | Concept A | Concept B | Razor-Sharp Distinguishing Line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thermoplastic vs. Thermosetting Plastic** | **Thermoplastic**: Linear/branched polymer; **softens on heating and hardens on cooling reversibly** (Polyethylene, PVC, Teflon). Can be remelted and recycled. | **Thermosetting**: 3D cross-linked network; **hardens permanently on initial heating** via chemical cross-links (Bakelite, Melamine). Cannot be remelted; decomposes on overheating. | **Recyclability & Heat Response**: Thermoplastics melt cleanly upon heating and are recyclable; Thermosets do not melt and char without reshaping. |
| **Elastomer vs. Fibre** | **Elastomer**: Has the **weakest intermolecular forces** (van der Waals); coiled chains with sparse cross-links; stretches and retracts (Rubber, Neoprene). | **Fibre**: Has the **strongest intermolecular forces** (H-bonding, dipole-dipole); linear crystalline chains; high tensile strength, minimal stretch (Nylon, Terylene). | **Intermolecular Force Magnitude**: Elastomers have minimal intermolecular attraction, allowing stretch; Fibres have maximal intermolecular attraction, resisting elongation. |
| **Rayon vs. Nylon** | **Rayon**: **Semi-synthetic regenerated cellulose fibre** derived chemically from natural wood pulp/cotton. | **Nylon**: **Fully synthetic polyamide fibre** synthesized from petroleum-derived petrochemical monomers (adipic acid + diamine). | **Raw Material Origin**: Rayon requires plant cellulose as its starting backbone; Nylon is synthesized entirely from petrochemical monomers. |
| **Natural Rubber vs. Neoprene** | **Natural Rubber**: Polymer of hydrocarbon **Isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene)**; degrades in oil and gasoline. | **Neoprene**: Synthetic polymer of chlorinated monomer **Chloroprene (2-chloro-1,3-butadiene)**; highly resistant to oil and gasoline. | **Chlorine Atom Presence**: Neoprene has a chlorine substituent replacing the methyl group ($CH_3 \to Cl$), which confers superior solvent resistance. |
| **LDPE vs. HDPE** | **LDPE (Low Density)**: Highly branched chains; prepared at high pressure ($1000–2000\text{ atm}$); flexible, low melting point (squeeze bottles). | **HDPE (High Density)**: Strictly linear chains; prepared at low pressure with **Ziegler-Natta catalyst**; rigid, high melting point (buckets, pipes). | **Chain Branching & Catalyst**: LDPE is branched (free radical, ultra-high pressure); HDPE is linear (Ziegler-Natta catalyst, low pressure). |
| **Bakelite vs. Melamine** | **Bakelite**: Condensation resin of **Phenol + Formaldehyde**; dark brown/amber color; used for electrical switches and cookware handles. | **Melamine**: Condensation resin of **Melamine (triazine) + Formaldehyde**; light-colored/white; used for unbreakable dinner plates and decorative laminates. | **Monomer Backbone**: Bakelite is phenolic; Melamine uses a nitrogen-rich heterocyclic triazine ring. |
| **Graphene vs. Fullerene** | **Graphene**: Single **flat 2D planar sheet** of $sp^2$ carbon packed in an open honeycomb lattice. | **Fullerene ($C_{60}$)**: **Hollow 3D spherical closed cage** composed of 20 hexagons and 12 pentagons. | **Dimensionality & Shape**: Graphene is an infinite 2D open planar sheet; Fullerene is a discrete 3D closed molecular soccer ball. |
| **Silk Fibroin vs. Wool Keratin** | **Silk (Fibroin)**: Rich in small amino acids (glycine, alanine); forms anti-parallel $\beta$-pleated sheets; smooth, non-elastic. | **Wool (Keratin)**: Rich in sulphur-containing cysteine; forms coiled $\alpha$-helical structures held by disulphide bonds; crimped, highly elastic. | **Secondary Protein Conformation**: Silk is composed of $\beta$-sheets (no stretch); Wool is composed of coiled $\alpha$-helices with disulphide cross-links (stretchy). |

## Detailed Theory Notes

### 5.1 Carbon Bonding, Hybridization & Hydrocarbon Families

#### 1. Catenation and Carbon's Unique Position
Carbon occupies Group 14 and Period 2 of the Periodic Table. Its electronic configuration is $1s^2 2s^2 2p^2$.
- **Catenation**: The unique property of elements to form covalent bonds with atoms of the same element, generating extended chains, branched networks, and rings.
  $$\text{Catenation Capability: } C \gg Si > Ge \approx Sn > Pb$$
  The exceptional catenation of carbon stems from its small atomic radius ($77\text{ pm}$), which allows effective nuclear overlap and generates a high bond energy ($348\text{ kJ/mol}$). Silicon forms $Si-Si$ bonds, but their lower energy ($222\text{ kJ/mol}$) and reactivity toward oxygen make long silicon chains unstable under ambient conditions.

```
       H   H
       |   |
   H - C - C - H   (Ethane: sp³, single bond, σ only, bond angle 109.5°)
       |   |
       H   H

       H   H
        \ /
         C = C     (Ethene: sp², double bond, 1σ + 1π, bond angle 120°)
        / \
       H   H

   H - C ≡ C - H   (Ethyne: sp, triple bond, 1σ + 2π, bond angle 180°)
```

#### 2. Master Hydrocarbon Taxonomy
1. **Alkanes ($C_n H_{2n+2}$)**:
   - Saturated aliphatic hydrocarbons. Every carbon is $sp^3$ hybridized with single $\sigma$-bonds.
   - Chemically unreactive at room temperature; undergo free-radical substitution reactions under UV light (e.g., chlorination: $CH_4 + Cl_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu} CH_3Cl + HCl$).
   - Methane ($CH_4$) accounts for $>85\%$ of natural gas and is generated by methanogenic archaebacteria in oxygen-deprived environments (paddy fields, wetlands, cattle rumens).
2. **Alkenes ($C_n H_{2n}$)**:
   - Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a $C=C$ double bond ($1\sigma + 1\pi$).
   - The $\pi$-bond is formed by sideways lateral overlap of unhybridized $p$-orbitals and is susceptible to electrophilic addition reactions (hydrogenation, halogenation).
   - Ethene ($C_2H_4$) is an endogenous plant hormone promoting fruit ripening and the monomer for polyethylene.
3. **Alkynes ($C_n H_{2n-2}$)**:
   - Unsaturated hydrocarbons with a $C \equiv C$ triple bond ($1\sigma + 2\pi$).
   - Acetylene ($C_2H_2$) burns in pure oxygen to yield an intensely hot flame ($\approx 3200^\circ\text{C}$), utilized in oxy-acetylene welding. Terminal alkynes have weakly acidic acetylenic protons that react with heavy metal salts ($AgNO_3, Cu_2Cl_2$) to form insoluble explosive acetylides.
4. **Aromatic Hydrocarbons (Arenes)**:
   - Planar cyclic conjugated compounds containing $(4n+2)$ delocalized $\pi$ electrons (Hückel's Rule).
   - Benzene ($C_6H_6$) exhibits extraordinary thermodynamic stability due to resonance energy ($\approx 150\text{ kJ/mol}$). It undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution rather than addition reactions, preserving the intact aromatic $\pi$-system.

### 5.2 Allotropes of Carbon & Advanced Nanostructures

```
                                  ┌── Diamond: 3D sp³, tetrahedral, hardest natural substance, insulator.
                                  ├── Graphite: 2D sp², hexagonal sheets, electrical conductor, solid lubricant, MOST STABLE.
Carbon Allotropes (Pure Carbon) ──┼── Fullerenes (C₆₀): 0D cage, 20 hexagons + 12 pentagons ("buckyball").
                                  ├── Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs): 1D tubules, high tensile strength, ballistic conductivity.
                                  └── Graphene: 2D monolayer, single atom thick, honeycomb lattice, transparent.
```

#### 1. Fullerenes ($C_{60}$)
- Discovered in 1985 by laser vaporization of graphite by Kroto, Curl, and Smalley.
- Consists of 60 equivalent carbon atoms arranged in a spherical cage resembling a soccer ball.
- Geometry: Truncated icosahedron containing **20 hexagonal rings and 12 pentagonal rings**.
- *Isolated Pentagon Rule (IPR)*: No two pentagons share a common edge; every pentagon is entirely surrounded by five hexagons to minimize steric ring strain.
- Each carbon atom is $sp^2$ hybridized with slight pyramidal distortion to accommodate spherical curvature.

#### 2. Graphene
- A single isolated two-dimensional layer of carbon atoms arranged in a planar hexagonal honeycomb lattice.
- Electrons move across the lattice as massless Dirac fermions with Fermi velocities near $10^6\text{ m/s}$, enabling ballistic electronic transport.
- Extreme thermal conductivity ($\approx 5000\text{ W}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$), Young's modulus of $1\text{ TPa}$, and intrinsic tensile strength exceeding $130\text{ GPa}$ ($>100\times$ stronger than structural steel).

### 5.3 Polymer Science: Reaction Mechanisms & Mechanical Classes

#### 1. Polymerization Regimes
- **Addition Polymerization**: Monomers containing multiple bonds join without the loss of any small molecules. The empirical formula of the resulting polymer is identical to that of the starting monomer (e.g., $n CH_2=CH_2 \to [-CH_2-CH_2-]_n$).
- **Condensation Polymerization**: Intermolecular reactions between bi-functional or poly-functional monomers accompanied by the elimination of small byproduct molecules ($H_2O, HCl, NH_3$). The elemental composition of the polymer differs from that of the monomers (e.g., Nylon-6,6, Terylene, Bakelite).

### 5.4 Master Plastics, Resins & Synthetic Fibres

#### 1. Bakelite (Phenol-Formaldehyde Condensation Resin)
- Phenol reacts with formaldehyde in the presence of an acid ($H^+$) or alkaline ($OH^-$) catalyst via electrophilic aromatic substitution to form ortho- and para-hydroxymethylphenols.
- Linear condensation yields **Novolac**, a fusible soluble polymer used in protective varnishes.
- Heating Novolac with hexamethylenetetramine (source of additional formaldehyde) forms methylene bridges ($-CH_2-$) that cross-link the chains into **Bakelite**, a rigid, infusible, thermosetting three-dimensional polymer network.
- Standard industrial application: **Electrical wall switchboards, plugs, appliance sockets, and heat-resistant cookware handles**.

#### 2. Teflon (Polytetrafluoroethylene, PTFE)
- Polymerized from tetrafluoroethylene ($CF_2=CF_2$) using ammonium persulphate catalyst at elevated pressures:
  $$n(CF_2=CF_2) \to [-CF_2-CF_2-]_n$$
- The high $C-F$ bond dissociation energy ($485\text{ kJ/mol}$) and dense protective fluorine sheath render Teflon chemically inert against boiling aqua regia, strong mineral acids, and oxidizing agents up to $260^\circ\text{C}$.
- Exhibits the third-lowest coefficient of friction of any known solid material, making it the standard **non-stick coating on domestic kitchenware** and self-lubricating bearings.

#### 3. Kevlar (Para-Aramid)
- Synthesized by low-temperature polycondensation of **1,4-phenylenediamine** and **terephthaloyl chloride** in an anhydrous amide solvent:
  $$n H_2N-C_6H_4-NH_2 + n ClCO-C_6H_4-COCl \to [-HN-C_6H_4-NH-CO-C_6H_4-CO-]_n + 2n HCl$$
- The polymer chains form a rigid, rod-like conformation that self-assembles into liquid crystalline solutions. Upon spinning, the chains align parallel to the fiber axis, locked by dense intermolecular hydrogen bonds between amide groups and $\pi-\pi$ stacking between aromatic rings.
- Provides exceptional tensile strength-to-weight ratio ($\approx 5\times$ structural steel), high cut resistance, and dimensional stability up to $450^\circ\text{C}$; standard material for **bulletproof vests, military helmets, and aerospace composites**.

### 5.5 Rubber Chemistry & Natural Biopolymers

#### 1. Natural Rubber & Sulphur Vulcanization
- Natural rubber is a natural elastomer harvested as a colloidal suspension (latex) from *Hevea brasiliensis*. Chemically, it is strictly **cis-1,4-polyisoprene**:
  $$[-CH_2-C(CH_3)=CH-CH_2-]_n$$
- In the raw state, individual polyisoprene chains are held only by weak van der Waals interactions. Under mechanical stress, the chains slide past each other, leading to permanent deformation (tacky when warm, brittle when cold).
- **Vulcanization (Charles Goodyear, 1839)**:
  - Heating raw rubber with **$3–5\%$ elemental sulphur** at $140^\circ\text{C}$ in the presence of $ZnO$ catalyst.
  - Sulphur forms covalent **disulphide cross-links ($-S-S-$)** across the allylic carbon positions of adjacent chains:
    ```
    - CH₂ - C(CH₃) = CH - CH₂ -
                 |
               - S - S -
                 |
    - CH₂ - C(CH₃) = CH - CH₂ -
    ```
  - These chemical cross-links prevent chains from slipping permanently while allowing elastic stretching and snapping back, making the rubber durable and resilient for automobile tyres.

#### 2. Synthetic Rubbers
- **Neoprene**: Prepared by addition polymerization of **Chloroprene (2-chloro-1,3-butadiene)**:
  $$n CH_2=C(Cl)-CH=CH_2 \to [-CH_2-C(Cl)=CH-CH_2-]_n$$
  The chlorine substituent provides polarity and chemical stability, making neoprene highly resistant to degradation by mineral oils, gasoline, and ozone.
- **Buna-S (SBR)**: Copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and styrene; standard material for automobile tread tyres.
- **Buna-N (NBR)**: Copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and acrylonitrile; used for fuel hoses and oil seals.

| 4 | Graphite = layered hexagonal; soft; good electrical conductor; used as lubricant and in pencils |

| 10 | Nylon = first synthetic fibre (1935, Du Pont); polyamide; used in parachutes, stockings |
| 11 | Polyester (PET) = from ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid; soft-drink bottles, clothing |
| 12 | Bakelite = first synthetic plastic (1907, Leo Baekeland); thermosetting; phenol + formaldehyde |

| 14 | Polythene (polyethylene) = monomer = ethylene; plastic bags, bottles |
| 15 | Teflon (PTFE) = polytetrafluoroethylene; non-stick cookware; very low friction |
| 16 | Natural rubber = polymer of isoprene (C5H8)n; from Hevea brasiliensis |
| 17 | Vulcanisation = rubber + sulphur at 140 degC; makes rubber stronger and elastic (Goodyear, 1839) |
| 18 | Cellulose = polymer of beta-glucose; plant cell wall; not digestible by humans |
| 19 | Starch = polymer of alpha-glucose; energy storage in plants; digested by amylase |
| 20 | Proteins = polymer of amino acids; peptide bonds; 20 standard amino acids |

## Common Traps & High-Yield Pitfalls

| Trap Area | What Students Confuse | Correct Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Rayon Origin** | Thinking Rayon is a modified starch or fully synthetic polymer. | Rayon is **regenerated cellulose** (derived from natural wood pulp treated with $NaOH$ and $CS_2$). |
| **Neoprene Category** | Classifying Neoprene as a thermoplastic. | Neoprene is an **elastomer** (synthetic rubber), not a thermoplastic. |
| **Advanced Materials Chronology** | Mixing up the discovery timeline of Kevlar, Fullerenes, and Graphene. | **Kevlar (1965)** $\to$ **Fullerenes (1985)** $\to$ **CNTs (1991)** $\to$ **Graphene (2004)** $\to$ **Black Gold (2019)**. |
| **Teflon Monomer** | Forgetting the chemical formula of Teflon monomer. | Teflon is polymerized from **tetrafluoroethylene ($CF_2=CF_2$)**, not vinyl fluoride or chloroprene. |
| **Bakelite Application** | Thinking Bakelite melts easily or is recyclable. | Bakelite is a **thermosetting plastic**; it does not melt upon reheating and is non-recyclable. |
| **Natural Rubber Monomer** | Answering chloroprene for natural rubber. | Natural rubber is **Isoprene** (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene). **Chloroprene** is the monomer of synthetic Neoprene. |
| **Vulcanizing Agent** | Answering phosphorus or carbon for vulcanization. | Rubber is vulcanized using **$3–5\%$ elemental Sulphur** to form disulphide cross-links. |
| **Kevlar Composition** | Confusing Kevlar with polyester or aliphatic nylon. | Kevlar is an **aromatic polyamide (aramid)** with rigid benzene rings in its backbone. |
| **Diamond vs. Graphite Stability** | Assuming diamond is thermodynamically more stable because it is harder. | **Graphite is thermodynamically most stable** at STP ($\Delta_f H^\circ = 0$). Diamond is metastable ($\Delta_f H^\circ = +1.9\text{ kJ/mol}$). |
| **Acetylene Welding Temperature** | Underestimating the oxy-acetylene flame temperature. | Oxy-acetylene burns at **$\approx 3200^\circ\text{C}$**, capable of melting and welding steel structures. |
