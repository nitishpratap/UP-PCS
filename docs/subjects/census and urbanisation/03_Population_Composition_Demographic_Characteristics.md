## Consolidated — 50 Must-Score Facts

1. The proportion of the **Working-Age Population (15–59 years)** in India as per Census 2011 was **60.3%**.
2. The proportion of the **Child Population (0–14 years)** in India in 2011 was **30.8%**, while the **Senior Citizen Population (60+ years)** was **8.6%**.
3. India's **Total Dependency Ratio** in Census 2011 was **65.2%** (down from 75.2% in 2001).
4. In Census classification, a **Main Worker** is an individual who engaged in economic activity for **183 days (6 months) or more** in the preceding year.
5. A **Marginal Worker** is an individual who worked for **less than 183 days** during the preceding reference year.
6. Among India's workforce in 2011, **Agricultural Labourers (30.0%)** formed the largest single occupational category, surpassing Cultivators (24.6%).
7. The overall **Sex Ratio** of India in Census 2011 was **943 females per 1,000 males**.
8. **Kerala** has the highest sex ratio among Indian States at **1,084**, followed by Tamil Nadu (996) and Andhra Pradesh (993).
9. **Haryana** has the lowest sex ratio among Indian States at **879**, followed by Jammu & Kashmir (889) and Sikkim (890).
10. **Puducherry** has the highest sex ratio among Union Territories at **1,037**, while **Daman & Diu** has the lowest at **618**.
11. The **Child Sex Ratio (0–6 years)** of India in Census 2011 was **919**, dropping from 927 in 2001.
12. **Mizoram** recorded the highest Child Sex Ratio among States at **970**, followed by Meghalaya (970) and Chhattisgarh (969).
13. **Haryana** recorded the lowest Child Sex Ratio among States at **834**, followed by Punjab (846).
14. Effective Literacy Rate in India is calculated strictly for the population aged **7 years and above**.
15. India's effective literacy rate in Census 2011 was **73.0%** (Male: **80.9%**, Female: **64.6%**).
16. The national **Gender Gap in Literacy** in Census 2011 was **16.3 percentage points**.
17. **Kerala** has the highest overall literacy rate (**94.0%**) as well as highest female literacy (**92.1%**).
18. **Bihar** has the lowest overall literacy rate (**61.8%**) and the lowest male literacy rate (**71.2%**).
19. **Rajasthan** recorded the lowest female literacy rate among all States at **52.1%** (and the widest gender literacy gap of **27.1%**).
20. **Meghalaya** recorded the narrowest gender gap in literacy in India at only **3.1 percentage points**.
21. The **Scheduled Caste (SC)** population in India in 2011 was **20.14 Crore**, constituting **16.63%** of the national total.
22. The **Scheduled Tribe (ST)** population in India in 2011 was **10.43 Crore**, constituting **8.61%** of the national total.
23. The Sex Ratio among **Scheduled Castes** in India is **945**, which is higher than the national average (943).
24. The Sex Ratio among **Scheduled Tribes** in India is **990**, which is the highest among all major social groups.
25. The Child Sex Ratio (0–6 years) among **Scheduled Tribes** is **957** (compared to 919 at all-India level).
26. **Uttar Pradesh** has the largest absolute Scheduled Caste population in India (**4.14 Crore**).
27. **Punjab** has the highest proportion of Scheduled Castes relative to its total population (**31.9%**).
28. **Madhya Pradesh** has the largest absolute Scheduled Tribe population in India (**1.53 Crore**).
29. **Lakshadweep (94.8%)** and **Mizoram (94.4%)** have the highest percentage of Scheduled Tribes in their total population.
30. The States/UTs of **Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh, Delhi, and Puducherry** have **NO notified Scheduled Tribe population**.
31. The States/UTs of **Nagaland, Mizoram, Meghalaya, Lakshadweep, and Andaman & Nicobar Islands** have **NO notified Scheduled Caste population**.
32. In India, there are **75 Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs)** across 18 States and 1 UT (Odisha has the highest: 13 PVTGs).
33. The **Dhebar Commission (1973)** created the category of Primitive Tribal Groups (PTGs), renamed as PVTGs in 2006.
34. According to Census 2011 religious data, **Hindus** constitute **79.80% (96.63 Crore)** of India's population.
35. **Muslims** constitute **14.23% (17.22 Crore)** of India's population, with the highest decadal growth (**24.6%**) among major religions.
36. **Christians** constitute **2.30% (2.78 Crore)** of India's population and have the highest sex ratio (**1,023**) among all major religious communities.
37. **Sikhs** constitute **1.72% (2.08 Crore)** of India's population and have the lowest sex ratio (**903**) among all major religious communities.

40. Under the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India, **22 languages** are recognized.
41. **Hindi** is the most spoken language in India, spoken by **43.63% (52.83 Crore)** of the total population.
42. **Bengali** is the second most spoken language in India (**8.03%**), followed by **Marathi (6.86%)** and **Telugu (6.70%)**.
43. **Sanskrit** is the least spoken Eighth Schedule language in India, with only **24,821 speakers** recorded in Census 2011.

## Teaching cards

### 2.1 Age Structure, Dependency Ratio, and Workforce Classification

#### A. The Three Broad Age Cohorts
1. **Young / Child Cohort (0–14 Years)**:
   - Constitutes approximately **30.8%** of India's population in Census 2011 (declined from 35.3% in 2001).
   - Biologically and legally dependent; requires social investment in nutrition, immunization, and primary/secondary schooling.
2. **Working-Age Cohort (15–59 Years)**:
   - Constitutes approximately **60.3%** of India's population in Census 2011 (increased from 57.1% in 2001).
   - The economically active, tax-paying, and output-generating engine of the economy. Forms the basis of India's **Demographic Dividend**.
3. **Elderly / Senior Citizen Cohort (60+ Years)**:
   - Constitutes approximately **8.6%** of India's population in Census 2011 (increased from 7.5% in 2001; projected to reach ~15% by 2036).
   - Economically dependent; requires geriatric healthcare, pensions, and assisted-living social security infrastructure.

```
                       AGE COMPOSITION OF INDIA'S POPULATION (CENSUS 2011)
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Child Population (0–14 Yrs): ~30.8%  │ Working Age (15–59 Yrs): ~60.3% │ 60+:8.6%│
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### B. Dependency Ratio Formulations
- **Total Dependency Ratio (TDR)**:
  $$	ext{TDR} = rac{	ext{Population (0–14)} + 	ext{Population (60+)}}{	ext{Working-Age Population (15–59)}} 	imes 100$$
  - India's Total Dependency Ratio fell from **75.2%** in 2001 to **65.2%** in 2011, reflecting the expanding demographic dividend.
- **Young Dependency Ratio (YDR)**:
  $$	ext{YDR} = rac{	ext{Population (0–14)}}{	ext{Working-Age Population (15–59)}} 	imes 100$$
- **Old-Age / Elderly Dependency Ratio (ODR)**:
  $$	ext{ODR} = rac{	ext{Population (60+)}}{	ext{Working-Age Population (15–59)}} 	imes 100$$

#### C. Workforce & Economic Activity Classification in Census
The Census of India classifies the total population into three broad economic categories based on work duration during the reference year:
1. **Main Workers**: Persons who engaged in any economically productive activity for **183 days (6 months) or more** during the preceding 365 days.
2. **Marginal Workers**: Persons who worked for **less than 183 days (less than 6 months)** during the preceding year (further categorized into those working for 0–3 months and 3–6 months).
3. **Non-Workers**: Persons who did not engage in any economic work during the entire year (students, homemakers, dependents, retired persons, beggars).
- **Four Standard Industrial Categories of Workers**:
  - **Cultivators (CL)**: Persons engaged in farming/cultivating land owned or held from government/private persons (24.6% of total workers).
  - **Agricultural Labourers (AL)**: Persons who work on another person's land for wages in money or kind without holding ownership/lease rights (30.0% of total workers).
  - **Household Industry Workers (HHI)**: Workers in manufacturing, processing, servicing, or repairing conducted within the home by household members (3.8%).
  - **Other Workers (OW)**: All workers engaged in factories, government, commerce, IT, transport, mining, and services (41.6%).

### 2.2 Literacy Rates & Educational Attainment

#### A. Effective Literacy Rate Definition
In Indian census terminology, a person aged **7 years and above** who can both read and write with understanding in any language is considered **literate**. A person who can only read but cannot write is classified as illiterate.
$$	ext{Effective Literacy Rate (\%)} = rac{	ext{Total Literate Population Aged 7+}}{	ext{Total Population Aged 7+}} 	imes 100$$
*(Note: Prior to the 1991 Census, literacy was calculated for the population aged 5 years and above).*

#### B. Literacy Disparities & Gender Gap
- **National Level (2011)**: Overall: **73.0%**; Male: **80.9%**; Female: **64.6%**; Gender Gap: **16.3%** (declined from 21.6% in 2001).
- **Highest Literacy States**: Kerala (**94.0%**), Mizoram (**91.3%**), Goa (**88.7%**), Tripura (**87.2%**).
- **Lowest Literacy States**: Bihar (**61.8%**), Arunachal Pradesh (**65.4%**), Rajasthan (**66.1%**), Jharkhand (**66.4%**), Andhra Pradesh (**67.0%**), Uttar Pradesh (**67.7%**).
- **Gender Gap Extremes**:
  - **Widest Gender Gap**: **Rajasthan (27.1%)** (Male: 79.2%, Female: 52.1%), followed by Jharkhand (21.4%) and Dadra & Nagar Haveli (20.9%).
  - **Narrowest Gender Gap**: **Meghalaya (3.1%)** (Male: 76.0%, Female: 72.9%), followed by Kerala (4.0%) and Mizoram (4.0%).
