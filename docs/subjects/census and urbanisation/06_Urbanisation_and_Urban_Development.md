3. **Goa** is the most urbanised State in India, with **62.2%** of its population living in urban areas.
4. **Mizoram** is the second most urbanised State in India (**52.1%**), followed by **Tamil Nadu (48.4%)**.
5. **Himachal Pradesh** is the least urbanised State in India, with only **10.0%** urban population.

7. Among Union Territories, the **NCT of Delhi** is the most urbanised (**97.5%**), followed by **Chandigarh (97.3%)**.
8. **Maharashtra** has the largest absolute urban population in India (**5.08 Crore**), followed by **Uttar Pradesh (4.45 Crore)**.
9. In Census 2011, there were **4,041 Statutory Towns** and **3,894 Census Towns** in India (total 7,935 towns).
10. The number of **Census Towns** surged by 186% from 1,362 in 2001 to **3,894 in 2011**, driving in-situ rural-to-urban transition.
11. A **Census Town** must have a minimum population of **5,000**, at least **75% male non-agricultural workers**, and a density $\ge \mathbf{400	ext{ persons / km²}}$.
12. **Class I Towns (Cities)** are defined as urban centres having a population of **100,000 (1 Lakh) and above**.
13. In Census 2011, there were **468 Class I Cities** in India.
14. Class I Cities account for **70.2% of India's total urban population**, demonstrating a top-heavy urban hierarchy.
15. A **Million-Plus Urban Agglomeration / City (Metropolis)** is defined as an urban area with a population of **10 Lakh (1 Million) or more**.
16. In Census 2011, there were **53 Million-Plus Cities / UAs** in India (up from 35 in 2001).

18. **Delhi Urban Agglomeration** is the second most populous UA (**16.3 Million**), followed by **Kolkata UA (14.1 Million)**.
19. A **Megacity** is defined as an urban agglomeration with a population of **10 Million (1 Crore) and above**.
20. In Census 2011, India had **3 Megacities**: Greater Mumbai (18.4M), Delhi (16.3M), and Kolkata (14.1M).

34. Under **Article 243ZD**, a **District Planning Committee (DPC)** must be constituted in every district (at least **four-fifths / 80%** members elected from Zila Panchayat & Municipalities).
35. Under **Article 243ZE**, a **Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC)** must be constituted in every metropolitan area with population $\ge 10	ext{ Lakh}$ (at least **two-thirds / 66.7%** members elected).

44. **Urban Sprawl** refers to the rapid, unplanned spatial expansion of a city into its surrounding rural-urban fringe.
45. **Primacy / Primate City Law** was formulated by **Mark Jefferson in 1939**, stating that a country's leading city is disproportionately larger than any other city.
46. The **Rank-Size Rule** of urban settlements was formulated by **George Zipf in 1949** ($P_n = P_1 / n$).
47. **Conurbation** is an extensive continuous urban area formed by the coalescing of several expanding cities and towns, a term coined by **Patrick Geddes in 1915**.
48. **Megalopolis** refers to a massive clustered network of adjacent metropolitan areas, a term coined by **Jean Gottmann in 1961** (e.g. Boston to Washington D.C.).
49. **Gentrification** is the socio-economic process of renovating and improving urban deteriorated neighbourhoods, often displacing lower-income residents.
50. In India, the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (**MoHUA**) is the apex Union ministry responsible for urban development policies, planning, and schemes.

### 2.1 Urban Morphology & The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act

#### A. The Demographic Definition of Urban Area in India
Under the Census of India, an Urban Area is defined through a two-fold classification:
1. **Statutory Towns (Administrative Urban)**:
   - All places with a municipality, municipal corporation, cantonment board, or notified town area committee established under State/Central statute.
2. **Census Towns (Demographic Urban)**:
   - All other rural places that satisfy **all three criteria simultaneously**:
     1. A minimum population of **5,000**.
     2. At least **75% of the male main working population** engaged in non-agricultural pursuits.
     3. A population density of **at least 400 persons per square kilometer** (1,000 per square mile).

```

                                               │
                    ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
                    ▼                                                     ▼
         ┌─────────────────────┐                               ┌─────────────────────┐
         │   STATUTORY TOWNS   │                               │    CENSUS TOWNS     │
         ├─────────────────────┤                               ├─────────────────────┤
```

#### B. Constitutional Governance: 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992
- Passed in December 1992; came into force on **1 June 1993**.
- Inserted **Part IX-A (Articles 243P to 243ZG)** and the **Twelfth Schedule (18 functional items)** into the Constitution of India.
- **Three-Tier Structure of Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)**:
  1. **Nagar Panchayat**: For a transitional area (rural-to-urban transition).
  2. **Municipal Council (Nagar Palika Parishad)**: For a smaller urban area.
  3. **Municipal Corporation (Nagar Nigam)**: For a larger urban area.
- **Key Constitutional Committees**:
  - **Wards Committees (Article 243S)**: Mandatory for municipalities having a population of **3 Lakh or more**.
  - **District Planning Committee - DPC (Article 243ZD)**: Consolidates plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities across the district. At least **four-fifths (80%)** of DPC members must be elected by and from among the elected members of the Zila Panchayat and Municipalities.
  - **Metropolitan Planning Committee - MPC (Article 243ZE)**: Mandatory for every metropolitan area with population $\ge 10	ext{ Lakh}$. At least **two-thirds (66.7%)** of MPC members must be elected by and from the elected members of Municipalities and Chairpersons of Panchayats in the metropolitan area.

### 2.2 Slums, Informal Settlements, and Urban Challenges

- **Census Definition of Slum**: A compact area of at least **300 population or about 60–70 households** of poorly built congested tenements in unhygienic environments, lacking basic civic amenities (clean drinking water, drainage, sanitation).
- **Three Categories of Slums (Census 2011)**:
  1. **Notified Slums**: All specified areas in a town formally notified as slums by State Governments or local authorities under Slum Acts.
  2. **Recognized Slums**: Areas recognized as slums by State Governments/ULBs, although not formally notified under legislation.
  3. **Identified Slums**: Compact areas identified by census enumerators satisfying slum criteria (300+ people or 60+ households).
- **Slum Demographics in India (2011)**:
  - Total Slum Population: **6.55 Crore (17.4% of total urban population)**.
  - **Maharashtra** has the largest absolute slum population in India (**1.18 Crore**), followed by Andhra Pradesh (1.02 Cr) and Uttar Pradesh (62.4 Lakh).
  - **Dharavi (Mumbai)** is recognized as one of the largest and most densely populated informal settlements in Asia.
