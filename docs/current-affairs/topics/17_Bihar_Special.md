## 2. Key Policies, Schemes & Governance Landmarks

### 2.1 Bihar Electric Vehicle (Amendment) Policy, 2026
- **Cabinet Approval**: Approved on **13 May 2026**.
- **Core Target**: Ensure that electric vehicles constitute **at least 30% of all new vehicle registrations in Bihar by 2030**.
- **Direct Financial Subsidies**:
  - **Electric Cars**: Direct subsidy of **₹1,00,000** for electric cars registered specifically in the name of **women**.
  - **Electric Two-Wheelers**: **₹10,000** for General category; **₹12,000** for Women and SC/ST beneficiaries.
  - **Goods-Carrying Electric Tricycles**: **₹50,000** for General category; **₹60,000** for SC/ST beneficiaries.
- **Tax Rebate**: **50% rebate on motor vehicle tax** for all electric vehicles registered in Bihar.
- **Scrappage Incentive**: Additional cash incentives on deregistering and scrapping old internal-combustion vehicles under Central Vehicle Scrapping guidelines.

### 2.2 Saat Nischay-3: "Sabka Samman, Jeevan Aasan" (2025–2030)
- Following the successful implementation of *Saat Nischay-1* (2015–20) and *Saat Nischay-2* (2020–25), the Bihar Government framed **Saat Nischay-3** for the period **2025–2030**.
- **Central Vision**: Accelerate household income growth, dignified self-employment, youth skill transitions, education quality, universal healthcare, and climate-resilient infrastructure.

### 2.3 Citizen Grievance & Public Administration Initiatives
- **Centralized Grievance Redressal and Monitoring (CGRM) Portal**: Launched on **5 June 2026** by the Rural Development Department to ensure real-time grievance tracking and transparency across rural schemes.
- **'Sahyog Shivir' (Support Camp) Initiative**:
  - Rolled out on **19 May 2026** by CM Samrat Choudhary.
  - Doorstep grievance redressal camps organized across gram panchayats sequentially on the **1st and 3rd Tuesday of every month**.
  - Statutory Time Limit: Mandates government officers to resolve submitted grievances within **30 days**.
  - Helplines: Supported by the centralized toll-free number **1100** and the **Sahyog Portal** (launched 11 May 2026 under Saat Nischay-3).
- **Creation of Rural SP Posts**: Cabinet approved the creation of dedicated **Superintendent of Police (Rural)** posts in 5 sensitive districts: **East Champaran, Samastipur, Madhubani, Vaishali, and Siwan**.
- **'Police Didi' Scheme**: Announced on **20 February 2026**; deploys trained women police personnel to bridge community communication with adolescent girls in rural/semi-urban areas; backed by procurement of **1,500 scooters and 3,200 motorcycles**.
- **BiharOne Project**: Awarded to Corporate Infotech Pvt. Ltd. (**CIPL**) for **₹87 crore** to create a single-window unified citizen digital portal by July 2026.
- **e-Vidhan (NeVA)**: Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla inaugurated the National e-Vidhan Application in the Bihar Legislative Assembly on **7 February 2026**, making Bihar's legislature completely paperless.
- **PANCHAM Digital Chatbot**: WhatsApp-based *Panchayat Assistance and Messaging Chatbot* launched on **25 January 2026** by Ministry of Panchayati Raj with UNICEF for real-time panchayat data delivery.
- **"Brand Bihar" Initiative**: Institutionalized under the Chief Secretary's office to reshape the state's investment identity and cultural branding.

### 2.4 Youth, Employment & Social Welfare Schemes
- **Mukhya Mantri Pratigya Yojana (FY 2025–26)**:
  - Targeted at youth aged **18 to 28** holding skill training certificates or educational credentials from Class 12 to Post-Graduation.
  - Scale: Enrolls **5,000 youth in 2025–26**, scaling up to **1,00,000 youth over 5 years** (from 2026–27).
  - **Monthly Stipends (via DBT)**:
    - Class 12 Pass: **₹4,000 / month**
    - ITI / Polytechnic Diploma: **₹5,000 / month**
    - Graduate / Post-Graduate: **₹6,000 / month**
  - **Mobility Premium (max 3 months)**: Extra **₹2,000/month** if placed outside home district; Extra **₹5,000/month** if interning outside Bihar.
- **CM Self-Help Allowance Scheme**: Provides **₹1,000 per month for up to 2 years** to non-working graduates aged 20–25 searching for employment under Saat Nischay.
- **Mukhyamantri Mahila Rojgar Yojana**: Provides an initial assistance of **₹10,000** to one woman per eligible family, expandable up to **₹2,00,000** upon launching micro-enterprises via Jeevika Self-Help Groups.
- **Jeevika Milestones**: 11.45 lakh SHGs cover ~1.40 crore families; **31.71 lakh Jeevika Didis have achieved 'Lakhpati Didi' status**.
- **Bihar State Jeevika Nidhi Credit Cooperative Federation Ltd**: Virtually inaugurated by PM Modi on **2 September 2025** to streamline collateral-free credit for women entrepreneurs.
- **Chief Minister Divyangjan Entrepreneur Scheme**: 50:50 loan-to-grant ratio for disabled entrepreneurs up to a project ceiling of **₹10 lakh**.
- **Mukhya Mantri Kalakar Pension Yojana**: Monthly pension of **₹3,000** for artists aged **50+** with at least 10 years of practice in classical, visual, or performing arts (family income ceiling < ₹1.2 lakh/year).
- **Bihar Patrakar Samman Scheme (BPSS) Pension Hike**:
  - Retired journalists (age 60+, 20 years verified experience): Pension hiked from **₹6,000 to ₹15,000 per month**.
  - Dependent spouse pension: Raised from **₹3,000 to ₹10,000 per month**.
- **Project Udaan**: Patna Municipal Corporation (Patna Nagar Nigam) initiative providing free professional competitive coaching for the children of municipal sanitation workers (*safai karmi*).

## 3. Infrastructure, Science, Industry & Mega Projects

### 3.1 3rd NIFTEM of India in Hajipur (Vaishali)
- **Land Allocation**: Bihar Cabinet sanctioned the free transfer of **100 acres** of state land in **Hajipur (Vaishali district)** to the Union Ministry of Food Processing Industries (MoFPI).
- **National Context**: This will be India's **third National Institute of Food Technology Entrepreneurship and Management (NIFTEM)**.
  - 1st Campus: **Kundli (Sonipat district, Haryana)**.
  - 2nd Campus: **Thanjavur (Tamil Nadu)**.
  - 3rd Campus: **Hajipur (Vaishali district, Bihar)**.

### 3.2 Clean Energy & Nuclear Power
- **Kajra Solar Power Plant (Lakhisarai)**:
  - **India's largest battery-energy-storage-supported solar project**.
  - Installed Capacity: **301 MW solar generation capacity paired with 495 MWh Battery Energy Storage System (BESS)**.
  - Enables stored daytime solar electricity to feed the state grid during peak evening hours.
- **Bikram Lock Canal Bank Solar Project**: Solar installations along canal embankments in Patna district inaugurated on **4 June 2025** under the *Jal-Jeevan Hariyali* Mission.
- **First Nuclear Power Plant in Bihar**: Union Power Minister Manohar Lal Khattar announced on **24 June 2025** that Bihar will host one of six planned indigenous **Small Modular Reactor (SMR)** nuclear power plants.

### 3.3 Security, Defence & Transport Infrastructure
- **CISF Regional Training Centre (RTC) in Kishanganj**: Bihar Government approved the transfer of **110.12 acres** to the Union Ministry of Home Affairs to train **1,000 CISF personnel** concurrently.
- **SSB Border Infrastructure**: Union Home Minister Amit Shah inaugurated the **Leti and Inderwa Border Outposts (BOPs)** in **Araria district** on **26 February 2026** (part of ₹170 crore CAPF infrastructure; 14 of 18 segments completed across 554 km of border roads with ₹2,336 crore spent).
- **DRDO Mega Defence Technology Exhibition**: Organized from **15 to 18 April 2026** at Mahatma Gandhi Prekshagrih in **Motihari**. Theme: *"Shanti, Satya aur Vigyan ka Sangam — Surakshit aur Aatmanirbhar Bharat ki Aur"*.
- **Flying Training Hub at Birpur (Supaul)**: MoU executed between Bihar Government and Aero Club of India under MoCA to train commercial pilots.
- **MSME Technology Centre & Extension Centres**:
  - Main Centre: **Sikandarpur (Bihta, Patna)**, inaugurated on **28 April 2026** by Union Minister Jitan Ram Manjhi and CM Samrat Choudhary.
  - 4 Extension Centres: **Muzaffarpur, Rohtas, Darbhanga, and Munger** (training 5,500+ candidates annually in AI/IoT, CNC, CAD/CAM, and 3D printing).
- **Dairy & Milk Processing**: Sanctioned 3 new dairies (5 lakh L/day capacity) in **Darbhanga, Wazirganj (Gaya), and Gopalganj**, alongside 2 milk powder plants (30 MT/day) in **Dehri-on-Sone (Rohtas) and Sitamarhi**.
- **Sex-Sorted Semen Facility**: Inaugurated on **15 September 2025** at Purnea Semen Station under Rashtriya Gokul Mission (5 lakh doses/yr capacity).
- **National Makhana Board in Purnea**: Launched by PM Modi on **15 September 2025** in **Purnea** with a ₹476 crore sector development package.
- **Industrial Corridors**: Major state industrial clusters notified at **Bhagalpur** and **Gopalganj**.
- **"Education City"**: Planned across **250 acres near Patna** with an initial capital outlay of **₹547 crore**.

## 4. Environment, Culture, Tourism & Awards

### 4.1 Wildlife & Wetlands
- **Two New Ramsar Sites Designated in Bihar**:
  1. **Gokul Jalashay** — located in **Buxar district**.
  2. **Udaipur Jheel** — located in **West Champaran district**.
- **First Natural Elephant Corridor of Bihar**: Formally demarcated across the **Garhi forest** of Jamui Forest Division, **Charkapathar and Batiya forests** of Jhajha, and **Madhwa sub-forest** of Chakai Forest Division.
- **Asian Waterbird Census (Kosi Region, Feb 2026)**: Documented 4,982 birds across 92 species; recorded rare sightings of **Baikal Teal** (*Anas formosa*, rare Siberian duck) and **Fulvous Whistling Teal**.
- **Doppler Weather Radars**: ISRO approved deployment of advanced X-band Doppler Weather Radars in **Bhagalpur and West Champaran** to monitor monsoonal flash floods and Chhath Puja weather patterns.

### 4.2 Tourism & Heritage
- **Second Glass Bridge of Bihar**: Under construction at **Ajgaivinath Dham in Bhagalpur** (~30m long, 5m wide, suspended 200 feet above ground). Bihar's first glass bridge is situated at **Rajgir (Nalanda)**.
- **Seven Wonders of the World Park**: A 40-acre cultural theme park built at **Bodh Gaya (Gaya district)** at an outlay of **₹14.85 crore**.
- **Adiyogi Statue in Bhagalpur**: Isha Foundation proposed a **112-foot Adiyogi statue and yoga centre** spanning 100–120 acres in **Bhagalpur**.
- **Vietnam President State Visit to Bodh Gaya**: On **5 May 2026**, Vietnamese President **To Lam** visited Bodh Gaya and offered prayers at the Mahabodhi Temple.
- **India–Portugal Bilateral Dialogue**: Hosted on **11–12 April 2026** at **Nalanda University (Rajgir)** commemorating **50 years of modern diplomatic relations**.
- **Nalanda International Literature Festival**: Held 12–15 March 2026 at Rajgir International Convention Centre (ICC).
- **Nalanda Development Dialogue**: 1st edition organized by Nalanda University on 8–9 March 2026.
- **Gyan Bharatam Manuscript Survey**: Bihar ranked **#3 nationally** with ~7.5 lakh surveyed manuscripts, trailing only Rajasthan (14.5L) and Madhya Pradesh (10.1L).
- **Ultra-Luxury Caravans**: 5-star tourist caravans introduced across the heritage tourist circuit: Nalanda – Rajgir – Bodh Gaya – Vaishali – Vikramshila – Patna Gandhi Circuit.

### 4.3 Awards & Recognitions
- **16th Dadasaheb Phalke Film Festival 2026**: Animated short film **"The Wild Call"** (inspired by **Rajgir Zoo Safari**, produced by Dept of Environment, Forest & Climate Change, directed by Naveendh S. Fareed and Azharuddin Sulaiman) won **Best Animation Film**.
- **Global Water Award 2026**: **BUIDCO** won *"Wastewater Project of the Year"* at the Global Water Summit 2026 in recognition of the **Digha-Kankarbagh Integrated Wastewater Project**.

- **SportsStar Aces Awards 2026**: Bihar was adjudged the **"Best State in Promoting Sports"** by an independent jury chaired by cricket legend **Sunil Gavaskar**.

## 7. Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26 (Master Findings)

### 7.1 Growth Dynamics & Structural Shifts in GSVA

- **GSDP Growth Performance (Constant 2011–12 Prices)**:
  - 2024–25 (Quick Estimates): **8.6%** (Bihar GSDP reached **₹5,31,372 crore**).
  - Outpaced India's overall GDP growth of **6.5%**.
- **GSDP Growth at Current Prices**:
  - 2024–25 (QE): **13.1%** (Bihar GSDP reached **₹9,91,997 crore**; India grew by 9.8%).
  - 2023–24: Bihar 14.9% vs India 12.0%.
  - 2022–23: Bihar 17.9% vs India 14.0%.
- **Sectoral GSVA Growth Rates (2024–25 QE)**:
  - **Secondary Sector**: **11.1%** (constant prices) / **15.5%** (current prices).
  - **Tertiary Sector**: **8.9%** (constant prices) / **13.5%** (current prices).
  - **Primary Sector**: **4.1%** (constant prices) / **9.6%** (current prices).
- **Structural Transformation of Economy (2020–21 to 2024–25)**:
  - **Primary Sector Share in GSVA**: Contracted from 21.9% to **18.3%**.
  - **Secondary Sector Share in GSVA**: Expanded robustly from 21.1% to **26.8%** (driven by manufacturing, construction, and power).
  - **Tertiary Sector Share in GSVA**: Stood at **54.8%** (from 57.0%), maintaining services dominance.
- **Per Capita GSDP Trajectory**:
  - Current Prices: Surged from ₹46,412 in 2020–21 to **₹76,490 in 2024–25**.
  - Constant Prices: Grew from ₹30,159 to **₹40,973 in 2024–25**.
- **District-Level Economic Disparities (GDDP)**:
  - **Top 3 Districts by Per Capita GDDP**:
    1. **Patna**: **₹2,41,220** (highest in the state)
    2. **Begusarai**: **₹1,05,600** (industrial refining/fertilizer hub)
    3. **Munger**: **₹93,921** (manufacturing and railways hub)

### 7.2 Public Finance, Debt & Revenue Profile

- **Expansion of Public Spending**: Total state expenditure expanded from ₹1.66 lakh crore in 2020–21 to **₹2.82 lakh crore in 2024–25**.
- **Capital Quality of Expenditure**:
  - Capital expenditure share in total expenditure rose sharply from 15.8% (2020–21) to **22.3% (2024–25)**.
  - Revenue expenditure proportion declined from 84.2% to **77.7%**.
- **Developmental Expenditure**: Constituted **67.4% of the total budget** in 2024–25 (**₹1,89,892 crore**, up from ₹1,12,122 cr in 2020–21).
- **Tax Revenue Dominance**: Tax revenue comprised **84% of total revenue receipts** in 2024–25 (up from 70% in 2020–21), while non-tax revenue was 2% and grants-in-aid was 14%.
- **Outstanding Debt**: Total outstanding debt reached **₹3,74,134 crore as of 31 March 2025 (37.7% of GSDP)**.
  - Public debt makes up 84.6% of total liabilities (83.8% internal market borrowings; 16.2% Central government loans).
- **Banking Soundness**: Bank Gross NPAs in Bihar fell from 11.8% in 2020–21 to **7.6% in 2024–25**.

### 7.3 Agriculture, Food Security & Irrigation

- **Contribution to GSVA**: Agriculture, forestry, and fisheries accounted for **23.1% of GSVA** in 2023–24 (Crop subsector: 11.5%; Livestock: 8.1%).
- **Cereal Concentration**: Rice, wheat, and maize together constitute **99.9% of total foodgrain production** in Bihar.
  - Foodgrain crop shares: **Rice 40.7%**, **Wheat 32.1%**, **Maize 27.1%**.
  - Production growth in 2024–25: Rice (+4.3%), Wheat (+7.1%), Maize (+12.6%).
- **Horticulture**: Production rose to **230 lakh tonnes** in 2023–24 (up from 206 lakh tonnes in 2019–20).
- **Power in Agriculture**: Electricity share consumed in agriculture expanded nearly threefold from 5.3% (2020–21) to **14.9% (2024–25)**.
- **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)**: Credit disbursement jumped by 32.8% to **₹9,399.24 crore** in 2024–25.
- **Irrigation Spending**: Total expenditure on irrigation stood at **₹2,729.83 crore** (74.3% capital expenditure).

### 7.4 Environment, Forest Cover & Power Capacity

- **Forestry Sector Growth**: Achieved a 10.6% CAGR between 2011–12 and 2024–25; output rose from ₹4,187 crore to **₹15,139 crore**.
- **Forest & Tree Cover Increase**: Total area under forest and tree cover increased by **689 sq km** from 9,214 sq km to **9,903 sq km in 2023**.
  - Forest area alone grew from 6,845 sq km to **7,532 sq km**.
  - **Very Dense Forests (VDF)** expanded from 231 sq km to **387 sq km**.
- **Installed Power Capacity**: Total installed power capacity reached **11,764 MW**.
  - **Thermal**: 7,743 MW
  - **Solar**: 1,922 MW (largest renewable component)
  - **Hydropower**: 1,356 MW
  - **Wind**: 699 MW
- **Eco-Tourism Surge**: **37.8 lakh tourists** visited 28 notified eco-tourism sites in 2024–25 (+24.0% increase).

### 7.5 Human Development & Social Sector Investments

- **Social Spending Multiplier**: Expenditure on social services increased **5-fold in the first decade and 15-fold between 2005–06 and 2024–25**.
  - Health spending increased by **14.8 times**.
  - Education spending increased by **13.2 times**.
- **Life Expectancy at Birth (LEB, 2019–2023)**:
  - **Males**: **68.9 years**
  - **Females**: **69.7 years**
- **Sharp Demographic Drops**:
  - Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) declined by 12 points from 35 to **23** (2017 to 2023).
  - Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) declined by 208 points from 312 (2004–06) to **104 (2021–23)**.
- **Institutional Deliveries**: Rose to **16.17 lakh** in 2024–25 under Janani Suraksha Yojana.
- **School Enrolment**: Total primary school enrolment reached **159.22 lakh** (80.87 lakh boys and 78.35 lakh girls). Secondary enrolment reached 27.54 lakh; Higher secondary reached 20.33 lakh.
