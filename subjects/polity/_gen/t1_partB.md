## 1.10 Simon Commission & Nehru Report

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Simon Commission (Indian Statutory Commission, 1927)** | All-British commission appointed to review constitutional progress under the Government of India Act, 1919 |
| **"Go Back Simon"** | Nationwide boycott slogan against the Commission’s all-white composition |
| **Nehru Report (1928)** | Constitutional draft prepared by an All-Parties Conference committee chaired by **Motilal Nehru** |
| **Dominion Status** | Self-government within the British Empire — the Nehru Report’s goal frame |
| **Jinnah’s Fourteen Points (1929)** | Muslim League counter-demands after rejecting the Nehru Report settlement |

### Causes

1. The Government of India Act, 1919 provided for a statutory review of constitutional arrangements.
2. Britain appointed an **all-white** commission with **no Indian member**, which nationalists treated as a deliberate insult.
3. Congress and a wide spectrum of Indian opinion decided on boycott rather than cooperation.
4. Boycott politics pushed Indians to produce their **own** constitutional draft instead of waiting for British recommendations.
5. Failure of the Nehru Report’s communal clauses hardened League politics into Jinnah’s Fourteen Points.

### Course — Step by Step

1. **1927:** Simon Commission is appointed under Sir John Simon (all-British membership).
2. Nationwide **boycott**, black-flag demonstrations and the slogan **Go Back Simon** spread.
3. Protest politics (including the Lahore agitation memory around Lala Lajpat Rai) nationalise opposition.
4. Indians convene the All-Parties Conference and authorise a drafting committee under **Motilal Nehru**.
5. **1928:** Nehru Report recommends **dominion status**, fundamental rights-style guarantees, and a joint-electorate framework with reserved seats for minorities.
6. Sections of the Muslim League reject the communal settlement; **1929:** Jinnah presents **Fourteen Points**.
7. Younger nationalist opinion rejects dominion as insufficient and moves toward **Purna Swaraj** (Lahore, Dec 1929).
8. Simon’s later recommendations feed British reform debate, but the nationalist legitimacy story locks as: boycott → Indian draft → independence demand.

### Simon Commission & Nehru Report — How It Works

- The single highest-yield Simon fact is **all-British composition / zero Indian members**.
- Appointment year is **1927**; Nehru Report year is **1928**; Jinnah’s Fourteen Points are **1929**.
- Motilal Nehru chaired the Nehru Report committee — **not** Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Nehru Report demanded **dominion status**, not Purna Swaraj; Purna Swaraj comes at Lahore **1929**.
- The Nehru Report was the major Indian constitutional draft of the late 1920s and the constructive reply to Simon’s insult.
- Jinnah’s Fourteen Points are the classic counter-document to the Nehru Report, not an endorsement of it.
- Chronology staple for UPPCS: Simon → Nehru Report → Sharda Act → Dandi (**2021 Q28** = III, II, I, IV).
- Trap: “Simon Commission had Indian members,” “Nehru Report = Purna Swaraj,” or “Jawaharlal chaired the Nehru Report.”

> **Exam note:** Lock **Simon 1927 (all-British) → Nehru Report 1928 (Motilal; dominion) → Jinnah 14 Points 1929**.

## 1.11 Communal Award & Poona Pact

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Communal Award (16 August 1932)** | Ramsay MacDonald’s award of separate electorates to communities, including **Depressed Classes** |
| **Poona Pact (24 September 1932)** | Gandhi–Ambedkar agreement replacing separate electorates for Depressed Classes with **reserved seats in joint electorates** |
| **Yeravada fast** | Gandhi’s fast unto death in Yeravada Jail against separate electorates for Depressed Classes |
| **Separate electorates** | Community votes only for its own reserved candidates (Award scheme for Depressed Classes) |
| **Joint electorates with reserved seats** | All voters vote; seats are reserved for Depressed Classes (Poona Pact scheme) |

### Causes

1. Round Table Conference communal deadlock left Britain to impose a settlement.
2. Depressed Classes leaders sought political safeguards against caste Hindu domination.
3. Gandhi opposed separate electorates as a permanent split within Hindu society.
4. Ambedkar sought robust representation guarantees for Depressed Classes.
5. Gandhi’s Yeravada fast created urgency for an Indian negotiated settlement.

### Course — Step by Step

1. On **16 August 1932**, Communal Award announces separate electorates for Depressed Classes (among other communal schemes).
2. Gandhi, imprisoned in Yeravada Jail, begins a **fast unto death** against separate electorates for Depressed Classes.
3. Intense talks occur between Gandhi’s side and Ambedkar’s side under political and social pressure.
4. On **24 September 1932**, the **Poona Pact** is signed (Gandhi–Ambedkar; **Madan Mohan Malaviya** among key signatories in standard lists).
5. Separate electorates for Depressed Classes are dropped in favour of **reserved seats** in **joint electorates**.
6. Provincial reserved seats rise from **71 → 148**; exam memory also tags about **~18%** reserved seats at the Centre.
7. The fake year **1933** for the Communal Award is a recurring UPPCS trap (**2019 Q19**).
8. The Pact becomes the classic 1932 constitutional compromise on Depressed Classes representation.

### Communal Award & Poona Pact — How It Works

- Award date lock: **16 August 1932** — **not 1933**.
- Pact date lock: **24 September 1932** (after Yeravada fast from about 20 September).
- Award person = **Ramsay MacDonald**; Pact persons = **Gandhi and Ambedkar**.
- Mechanism change: Award’s **separate electorates** → Pact’s **reserved seats + joint electorates**.
- Seat memory: provincial reserved seats **71 → 148**.
- Chronology: after Gandhi–Irwin Pact (Mar 1931) and 2nd RTC (1931); before final end of CDM (1934) — **2025 Q7**.
- Trap: “Communal Award 1933,” “Poona Pact = Gandhi–Irwin Pact,” or “Poona Pact kept separate electorates.”
- Trap: “Poona Pact created Pakistan” — unrelated; it is Depressed Classes representation only.

## 1.12 Round Table Conferences

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |

| **Round Table Conferences (1930–32)** | London conferences on India’s constitutional future after Simon/CDM politics |

| **Third RTC (Nov–Dec 1932)** | Weakly attended; Congress absent again |
| **Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 Mar 1931)** | Truce that suspended CDM and opened Congress entry to 2nd RTC |

### Causes

1. Britain needed a constitutional settlement after Simon politics and Civil Disobedience unrest.
2. Labour government experimented with a multi-party conference method including Indian interests.
3. Congress demanded real transfer of power, not only consultation.
4. Gandhi–Irwin Pact created a temporary opening for Congress participation in the Second RTC.
5. Communal representation and princely federation questions made any settlement extremely complex.

### Course — Step by Step

1. **First RTC (Nov 1930–Jan 1931)** meets in London **without Congress**.
2. **Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 March 1931)** suspends CDM and enables Congress entry to the Second RTC.

4. Deadlock persists on communal representation and federation details.
5. Gandhi returns unsuccessful; Civil Disobedience resumes.
6. **Communal Award (Aug 1932)** follows the RTC communal failure.
7. **Third RTC (Nov–Dec 1932)** is weakly attended; Congress stays out.
8. British later issue a White Paper and move toward the **Government of India Act, 1935**.

### Round Table Conferences — How It Works

- First and Third RTCs: Congress generally absent.
- Second RTC failed to solve the communal problem; Communal Award followed in **1932**.
- Chronology with CDM: Gandhi–Irwin → 2nd RTC → Poona Pact → End of CDM (**2025 Q7**).
- Trap: “Congress attended all three RTCs,” “2nd RTC solved communalism,” or “RTC = Simon Commission.”
- Trap: “Rajendra Prasad attended 2nd RTC with Gandhi” — he did **not**.

## 1.13 August Offer, Cripps Mission & Wavell Plan

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **August Offer (August 1940)** | Viceroy **Linlithgow**’s wartime offer expanding Indian representation and accepting a post-war constitution-making body in principle |
| **Cripps Mission (March 1942)** | Stafford Cripps’s wartime mission offering **post-war dominion status**, a Constituent Assembly, and provincial **right to secede/opt out** |
| **Wavell Plan (1945)** | Proposal to reconstitute the Viceroy’s Executive Council with balanced caste-Hindu/Muslim representation |
| **Shimla / Simla Conference (June–July 1945)** | Conference called by Wavell to implement the plan; **failed** |
| **“Post-dated cheque”** | Phrase associated with Congress scorn for Cripps’s delayed-war offer |

### 1.13.1 August Offer (1940)

#### Causes

1. World War II created British need for Indian wartime cooperation.
2. Congress ministries had resigned (1939) after Britain dragged India into war without consultation.
3. Britain wanted a political gesture short of immediate independence.
4. League and Congress demanded different constitutional futures.
5. Earlier dominion hints had not produced a settlement.

#### Course — Step by Step

1. Viceroy **Linlithgow** issues the **August Offer (1940)**.
2. Offer expands Indian membership of the Viceroy’s Executive Council and War Advisory Council ideas.
3. It accepts in principle that India’s constitution would be framed mainly by Indians after the war.
4. Congress rejects — no immediate independence / inadequate present power.
5. Muslim League also finds it insufficient for Pakistan safeguards.
6. Offer fails as a settlement.
7. Individual Satyagraha follows as Congress’s limited wartime protest form.
8. Next major British package becomes **Cripps Mission (1942)**.

4. League wanted Pakistan safeguards.

#### Course — Step by Step

1. Stafford Cripps arrives in India (**March 1942**).

3. Wartime defence and key controls remain with the British during the war.
4. Congress rejects — no immediate independence; “post-dated cheque” memory.
5. League rejects — Pakistan demand not met as desired (wanted clearer two-nation outcome).
6. Mission fails.

## 1.15 Mountbatten Plan & Indian Independence Act, 1947

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Mountbatten Plan / 3 June Plan (1947)** | Plan for partition of British India into two dominions and early transfer of power |
| **Lord Mountbatten** | Last Viceroy of British India; first Governor-General of free India (initially) |
| **Indian Independence Act, 1947** | British Parliament statute (**18 July 1947**) creating two independent dominions from **15 August 1947** |
| **Radcliffe Boundary Commission** | Hastily demarcated Punjab and Bengal boundaries for Partition |
| **15 August 1947** | Date of transfer of power / Independence |

### Causes

1. Cabinet Mission’s unity framework collapsed in practice amid communal violence.
2. League demanded Pakistan; Congress reluctantly accepted partition as the price of immediate freedom.
3. Mountbatten advanced the transfer date from a June 1948 idea to **August 1947**.
4. Political settlement needed statutory backing in the British Parliament.
5. Princely paramountcy and administrative partition required legal cover.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Mountbatten arrives as Viceroy (**March 1947**).
2. Negotiations with Congress and League intensify.
3. On **3 June 1947**, the Mountbatten Plan is announced (partition + two dominions + early transfer).
4. Punjab and Bengal assemblies decide on partition; NWFP/Sylhet referendum memories attach.
5. Indian Independence Bill is rushed through British Parliament.
6. Act receives assent on **18 July 1947**.
7. Radcliffe Boundary Commission works late and hastily.
8. Power is transferred on **15 August 1947**; princely states face accession after paramountcy lapses.

### Mountbatten Plan — Provisions (checklist)

1. Partition into **India** and **Pakistan** as dominions  
2. Power transfer advanced to **15 August 1947** (not June 1948)  
3. Punjab and Bengal assemblies decide partition  
4. NWFP referendum / Sylhet referendum memory  
5. **Radcliffe** Boundary Commission demarcates borders  
6. Princely states choose accession after paramountcy lapse  
7. Accepted by Congress and League (unlike earlier failed unity packages)

### Indian Independence Act — Provisions (checklist)

1. Two dominions from **15 August 1947**  
2. Act passed **18 July 1947** (date ≠ Independence Day)  
3. Each Constituent Assembly becomes the dominion legislature  
4. Office of Secretary of State for India ends

## 1.16 Constituent Assembly — Composition & Constitution Making Process

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Constituent Assembly (CA)** | Body that framed the Constitution of India; created under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)** |
| **Indirect election** | Provincial CA seats filled by provincial legislative assemblies (not universal adult franchise) |
| **STV / PR** | Single Transferable Vote under Proportional Representation used for provincial elections to CA |
| **Total strength (initial)** | **389** = 292 British Indian provinces + 93 princely states + 4 Chief Commissioner’s provinces |
| **After Partition** | Strength reduced to **299** (229 provinces + 70 princely states in standard breakup) |

### Causes / Background of Constitution Making

1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly grew from M.N. Roy (1934) and Congress formal demand (1935).
2. August Offer (1940) and Cripps (1942) accepted constitution-making ideas in principle but failed as settlements.
3. Cabinet Mission (1946) finally created the workable CA framework.
4. Partition forced recomposition and made the Assembly sovereign for the Indian Dominion.
5. The Assembly had to convert Objectives Resolution ideals into a working written constitution.

### Course — Constitution Making Process (Step by Step)

1. Elections to provincial CA seats are held in **1946** by provincial assemblies (indirect; PR-STV).
2. Congress wins a large majority; Muslim League wins most Muslim seats but later boycotts early sittings.
3. First meeting: **9 December 1946**; temporary President **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha**; permanent President **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**.
4. Objectives Resolution introduced by Nehru (**13 Dec 1946**) and adopted (**22 Jan 1947**).
5. After Independence Act, CA becomes sovereign and also works as dominion legislature (G.V. Mavlankar as legislative speaker).
6. Drafting Committee under Ambedkar (**29 Aug 1947**) prepares the Draft Constitution.
7. Assembly debates the Draft through multiple readings/sessions (about **2 years 11 months 18 days** total framing time).
8. Constitution adopted **26 Nov 1949**; enforced **26 Jan 1950**; last CA meeting **24 Jan 1950**.

## 1.17 Committees, Objectives Resolution, B.N. Rau, Drafting Committee & Timeline

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|

| **Drafting Committee** | Seven-member committee chaired by **B.R. Ambedkar**, appointed **29 August 1947** |

| **Rules of Procedure Committee** | Committee chaired by **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** |
| **Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee** | Sub-committee chaired by **J.B. Kripalani** |

### Causes / Why Committees Mattered

1. A large Assembly needed specialised committees to manage federal, rights, procedural and drafting work.
2. Objectives Resolution was required to fix the republic’s philosophical aims before detailed drafting.
3. An expert Constitutional Adviser (B.N. Rau) was needed to prepare a workable first draft from comparative study.
4. Drafting Committee had to convert committee reports into a coherent Draft Constitution.
5. A clear timeline of introduction, adoption and commencement was needed for legal certainty.

### Course — Step by Step

1. CA meets (**9 Dec 1946**) and elects office-bearers.
2. Nehru moves Objectives Resolution (**13 Dec 1946**); Assembly adopts it (**22 Jan 1947**).
3. Major committees and advisory/sub-committees work through 1947 on union, provinces, rights and minorities.
4. Drafting Committee is appointed on **29 August 1947** with Ambedkar as Chairman.
5. B.N. Rau’s advisory draft feeds the Drafting Committee’s work; Draft Constitution is introduced (**Nov 1948** memory).
6. Assembly debates the Draft over many sitting days (Drafting Committee itself sat about **141 days** in standard memory).
7. Constitution is adopted and enacted on **26 November 1949**.
8. National Anthem/Song adopted and first President elected at last meeting (**24 Jan 1950**); Constitution commences **26 Jan 1950**.
