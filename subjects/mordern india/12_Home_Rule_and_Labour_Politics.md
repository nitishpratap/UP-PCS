## 12.1 Overview — From Home Rule to Labour and Left Politics

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Home Rule Movement** | 1916–18 campaign for Indian self-government within the British Empire, led through two leagues (Tilak and Besant) |
| **Labour politics** | Organised working-class action through unions, strikes, and party-linked trade-union centres |
| **Left parties** | Socialist and communist streams (WPP, CPI, CSP) that pushed anti-imperialism toward class politics |
| **Regional parties** | Province-centred organisations (Justice Party, Akali stream, Self-Respect) with social/identity agendas beyond all-India Congress |

### Causes — Why This Cluster Matters

1. World War I raised Indian expectations of political reward for war support.
2. Extremists returned to open politics after Tilak’s release; Moderates alone looked weak.
3. Irish Home Rule provided a constitutional vocabulary Indians could copy.
4. Industrial growth and wartime prices created a labour audience for unions.
5. After Non-Cooperation’s suspension, socialists built organised left factions inside and beside Congress.

### Overview — How It Works

- Topic 12 links **wartime Home Rule politics (1916–18)** with later **labour, left, and regional** organisation.
- Exams treat Home Rule as a **matching + chronology** topic: who founded which league, which city was HQ, who was interned, who became INC President.
- **Annie Besant** and **B.G. Tilak** are syllabus persons — learn full tags here even if Theosophy/Extremism also appear elsewhere.
- **National Education** and **BHU** sit in the same 1905–16 education–nationalism corridor; do not confuse **NCE 1906** with **BHU 1916**.
- **AITUC 1920** is the labour centre of gravity; later INTUC/BMS/UTUC matching is a UPPCS favourite (**2022 Q123**).
- **Left Parties** means WPP–CPI–CSP chronology and traps (Nehru not in CSP; Ansari not in Kanpur Conspiracy).
- **Regional Parties** means Justice Party (Madras), Periyar/Self-Respect, and Akali politics — not full post-1947 party system.
- Trap: "Home Rule = Quit India." Home Rule is **WWI-era**; Quit India is **1942**.
- Trap: "One single Home Rule League only." There were **two** leagues in **1916**.
- Trap: "CSP founded by Nehru in January 1934." Wrong month/role — **2024 Q131**.

> **Exam note:** Highest ROI cluster = **two leagues table + CSP Neither + Justice Both + AITUC party map + Tilak never President**.

- Besant league: **September 1916**, Madras

## 12.2 Home Rule Movement

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Home Rule** | Self-government for India within the British Empire (Irish-inspired slogan) |
| **Indian Home Rule League (Tilak)** | League founded **April 1916** at Poona |
| **All India Home Rule League (Besant)** | League founded **September 1916** at Madras/Adyar |
| **Montagu Declaration (20 Aug 1917)** | British statement of gradual development of self-governing institutions in India |

### Background

- After Surat Split (1907) and Tilak’s imprisonment, Extremists were sidelined.
- World War I (1914–18) made Britain dependent on Indian men and money.
- Annie Besant’s wartime journalism (**New India**, **Commonweal**) and Tilak’s return created parallel platforms for a year-round Home Rule demand.
- Lucknow Pact (**1916**) Congress–League rapprochement happened in the same political season as Home Rule expansion.

### Master Overview Table — Two Leagues

| Feature | Tilak League | Besant League |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| **Founded** | **April 1916** | **September 1916** |
| **HQ** | **Poona (Pune)** | **Madras / Adyar** |
| **First meeting** | Belgaum | Organised from Madras base |
| **President / key office** | **Joseph Baptista** (President); N.C. Kelkar (Secretary) | Annie Besant leading figure; George Arundale active |
| **Area** | Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, Berar | Rest of India **including Bombay city** |
| **Style** | Tighter provincial organisation | Looser all-India branch network (~200 branches memory) |

### 12.2.1 Tilak’s Indian Home Rule League (April 1916)

#### Causes

1. Tilak needed an open constitutional platform after Extremists’ earlier isolation.
2. War-time expectations made self-government within Empire a credible slogan.
3. Western India required a provincial organisation denser than all-India Congress sessions.
4. Irish Home Rule provided a usable model and vocabulary.
5. Parallel Besant propaganda made April 1916 the moment to launch formally.

#### Course — Step by Step

1. Tilak and associates organise the league at **Poona** in **April 1916**.
2. An early meeting is associated with **Belgaum**.
3. **Joseph Baptista** becomes President; **N.C. Kelkar** becomes Secretary.
4. Branches expand in Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, CP, and Berar.
5. The league runs lectures, pamphlets, and local committees for Home Rule.
6. It cooperates with the wider 1916 nationalist settlement mood without merging into Besant’s HQ.
7. Tilak later travels abroad for propaganda/legal-political work as the movement’s peak passes.
8. After 1918–19 the league’s wartime energy declines with reforms hope and new mass methods.

#### Tilak League — How It Works

- This is the **April 1916 / Poona** league — never confuse with Madras.
- Office-bearer trap: **Baptista = President**, not ILO labour rep.
- Area trap: Bombay **city** is outside Tilak’s zone and inside Besant’s.
- Belgaum is an early-meeting tag, not the permanent HQ (HQ = Poona).
- The league made Extremists visible again in open constitutional politics.
- Trap: "Tilak League HQ = Madras/Adyar."
- Trap: "Tilak League founded September 1916."
- Trap: "Kelkar was league President." Kelkar = Secretary.

> **Exam note:** Tilak League three-cell lock = **April 1916 ↔ Poona ↔ Baptista**.

### 12.2.2 Besant’s All India Home Rule League (September 1916)

#### Causes

1. Besant wanted all-India educative propaganda if Congress did not deliver it.
2. Theosophical networks gave her Madras/Adyar reach beyond one province.
3. Press organs (**New India**, **Commonweal**) needed an organisational vehicle.
4. Tilak’s April launch showed the Home Rule moment had arrived.
5. War contribution arguments strengthened the claim to self-government.

#### Course — Step by Step

1. In **September 1916**, Besant launches the **All India Home Rule League** from **Madras/Adyar**.
2. Branches spread across areas outside Tilak’s provinces, **including Bombay city**.
3. Propaganda through press, lectures, and local leagues intensifies through 1916–17.
4. In **June 1917**, Besant, Arundale, and Wadia are interned; protest surges.
5. On **20 August 1917**, the **Montagu Declaration** announces responsible-government direction.
6. In **December 1917**, Besant becomes **INC President** (first woman).
7. Reform hopes and leadership shifts weaken Home Rule energy by 1918–19.
8. Besant later parts ways with Gandhi’s Non-Cooperation style.

#### Besant League — How It Works

- This is the **September 1916 / Madras** league — later than Tilak’s, broader geographically.
- Internment of Besant–Arundale–Wadia is the repression→popularity turning point.
- Montagu Declaration is partly a response to wartime Home Rule pressure.
- Besant’s 1917 Congress presidency is the highest-yield personal payoff of the league phase.
- Trap: "Besant League founded April 1916 at Poona."
- Trap: "Besant founded Theosophical Society and therefore Home Rule." Theosophy founder ≠ Besant.
- Trap: "Home Rule succeeded as Quit India." Different movement and decade.

> **Exam note:** Besant League three-cell lock = **September 1916 ↔ Madras/Adyar ↔ internment 1917**.

#### Results

| Internment crisis 1917 | Popularity spike |

#### Key Persons

| Person | Role |

### Causes

1. Indians expected political concession after heavy **World War I** contribution of soldiers and resources.
2. Moderate petition politics alone looked insufficient; Extremists needed a **constitutional mass slogan**.
3. The **Irish Home Rule** model gave a familiar British-empire vocabulary for self-government.
4. **Tilak’s release** and return to politics created a ready leadership core in western India.
5. **Annie Besant** threatened to launch a league if Congress did not run educative propaganda — then launched it in September 1916.

### Course of the Home Rule Movement — Step by Step

1. **1915–16:** Besant builds pressure through Theosophical networks and press for Home Rule propaganda.
2. **April 1916:** Tilak founds the **Indian Home Rule League** (Poona); Belgaum hosts an early meeting.
3. **September 1916:** Besant founds the **All India Home Rule League** (Madras), covering areas outside Tilak’s provinces plus Bombay city.
4. **1916–17:** Branches, lectures, pamphlets, and press expand the demand for Home Rule across provinces.
5. **June 1917:** Government internment of **Besant, Arundale, and Wadia** triggers nationwide protest and boosts the movement’s popularity.
6. **20 August 1917:** **Montagu Declaration** announces the goal of responsible government; pressure politics claims a partial victory.
7. **December 1917:** Besant becomes **President of the Indian National Congress** (Calcutta) — first woman President.
8. **1918–19:** Momentum fades as reform hopes, Tilak’s England visit, and the rise of Gandhi’s mass methods reshape nationalist politics.

### Home Rule Movement — How It Works

- Home Rule was **not** a demand for immediate complete independence outside the Empire; it asked for **self-government within the Empire**.
- Two leagues coordinated more than they competed; exams still demand **separate date–HQ–area** memory.
- Tilak’s league was stronger in the Deccan–CP belt; Besant’s league had broader all-India branch reach.
- Internment of Besant (1917) proved repression could **popularise** a constitutional movement.
- The Montagu Declaration was a British response to wartime pressure, not a gift unrelated to agitation.
- Home Rule trained a generation in continuous propaganda, local committees, and press politics between annual Congress sessions.
- Decline came because constitutional reforms seemed forthcoming, leadership energy shifted, and Gandhi’s methods soon dominated.
- Trap: "Tilak founded Home Rule in Madras." Tilak = **Poona**; Besant = **Madras**.
- Trap: "Besant League came before Tilak’s." Tilak **April**; Besant **September** 1916.
- Trap: "Home Rule = 1942 Quit India." Wrong decade and method.

> **Exam note:** Always solve league questions as a three-cell match: **Leader ↔ Month/Year ↔ HQ/Area**.

## 12.3 Annie Besant

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Annie Besant (1847–1933)** | British Theosophist who became a major Indian nationalist, educationist, and Home Rule leader |
| **New India / Commonweal** | Besant’s political journals used for Home Rule propaganda |
| **Central Hindu College (1898)** | Varanasi college founded by Besant; later linked to BHU nucleus |

### Causes — Why Besant Mattered

1. Theosophy gave her an Indian institutional base (Adyar) and cultural sympathy with Hindu traditions.
2. World War I created space for a continuous Home Rule demand beyond annual Congress sessions.
3. She believed Indians needed year-round educative propaganda for self-government.
4. Her English public standing and press skills made colonial repression politically costly.
5. Education work at Banaras linked cultural revival to nationalist institution-building.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Besant rises as a leading Theosophical figure in India (president from **1907** memory), but she is **not** the 1875 founder.
2. In **1898** she founds **Central Hindu College** at Varanasi.
3. Through **New India** and **Commonweal**, she runs wartime Home Rule propaganda.
4. She presses Congress for educative Home Rule work, then launches her league in **September 1916** at Madras/Adyar.
5. In **1917**, she is interned with **Arundale** and **Wadia**; protest makes her a national symbol.
6. After release and the Montagu Declaration climate, she becomes **INC President in December 1917** — first woman.
7. She later disagrees with Gandhi’s Non-Cooperation methods and moves away from the mass-movement centre.
8. Exam memory keeps her as the Home Rule + education + Theosophy-leader triangle.

### Annie Besant — How It Works

- Besant entered Indian public life through the **Theosophical Society**; she became a leading figure but was **not** its 1875 founder (Blavatsky and Olcott were).
- She founded **Central Hindu College, Varanasi (1898)**, tying education to cultural revival.
- Through **New India** and **Commonweal**, she ran continuous wartime Home Rule propaganda.
- In **September 1916** she launched the **All India Home Rule League** from Madras/Adyar.
- Internment in **1917** made her a national symbol; release followed public pressure.
- She became the **first woman President of the INC in 1917** (Calcutta session).
- She later disagreed with Gandhi’s Non-Cooperation methods and faded from the mass-movement centre.
- Trap: "Besant founded Theosophical Society." Founders = **Blavatsky + Olcott (1875, New York)**.
- Trap: "Besant = only education, not politics." She is the classic **Home Rule** match in UPPCS/UPSC.
- Trap: "Besant founded BHU alone." Malaviya is the chief university organiser tag; Besant contributed the college/assets stream.
- Trap: "Besant’s primary UPPCS tag is Quit India." Primary tag is **Home Rule**.

> **Exam note:** Besant triple tag = **Theosophy leader + Home Rule 1916 + INC President 1917** (never "founder of Theosophy").

## 12.4 B.G. Tilak

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856–1920)** | Extremist leader; Home Rule League founder (1916); called Lokmanya |
| **Kesari / Mahratta** | Tilak’s newspapers (Marathi / English) |
| **Gita Rahasya** | Tilak’s prison-period philosophical-political work |

### Causes of Tilak’s Home Rule Politics

1. He believed Moderate petitions alone could not force self-government.
2. After Mandalay imprisonment he returned needing an open constitutional mass slogan.
3. World War I raised Indian expectations of political reward for war support.
4. Irish Home Rule vocabulary gave him a usable Empire-framework demand.
5. He wanted year-round organisation in western India, not only annual Congress speeches.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Tilak builds **Kesari** and **Mahratta** as tools of public political education from Pune.
2. He becomes the Maharashtra pillar of Extremism with the **Lal–Bal–Pal** assertiveness stream.
3. After imprisonment and release, he re-enters open politics in the war years.
4. In **April 1916** he founds the **Indian Home Rule League** at **Poona**; Belgaum hosts an early meeting.
5. **Joseph Baptista** becomes league President; **N.C. Kelkar** serves as Secretary.
6. His league works Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, and Berar, while Besant covers the rest.
7. He cooperates with the broader 1916 nationalist settlement mood (**Lucknow** season) without becoming Congress President.
8. He dies on **1 August 1920**, still without ever having presided a Congress session.

### B.G. Tilak — How It Works

- Tilak was the western-India pillar of Extremism (**Lal–Bal–Pal**) before the Home Rule phase.
- He used **Kesari** and **Mahratta** for political education and critique of colonial rule.
- After release from Mandalay imprisonment, he returned to open politics and founded the **Indian Home Rule League in April 1916 (Poona)**.
- His league covered Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, and Berar.
- **Joseph Baptista** served as league President; **N.C. Kelkar** as Secretary — office-bearer traps appear in match lists.
- Tilak supported Congress–League cooperation around the **Lucknow (1916)** political settlement season.
- He died on **1 August 1920**, just as Gandhi’s Non-Cooperation was taking national shape.

## 12.5 National Education

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **National Education** | Indian-controlled schooling/colleges as a political-cultural alternative to official colonial education |
| **National Council of Education (NCE, 1906)** | Swadeshi-era body for national education (Calcutta stream) |
| **Educative propaganda (Home Rule)** | Home Rule leagues’ continuous political education through lectures, press, and local committees |

### Causes

1. Swadeshi boycott of official schools created demand for Indian-controlled education.
2. Curzon-era university controls pushed nationalists toward alternative colleges.
3. Home Rule needed year-round political education, not only annual Congress speeches.
4. Cultural revivalists wanted modern learning rooted in Indian identity (Besant college; Malaviya university).
5. Later Gandhians developed craft-centred Basic Education (Wardha), a third exam layer.

### Course — Step by Step

1. After Partition of Bengal (**1905**), Swadeshi boycott hits official education.
2. **August 1906:** National Council of Education is set up in the Bengal Swadeshi stream.
3. Bengal National College and related national schools expand the alternative-education idea.
4. Besant’s **Central Hindu College (1898)** already links education to cultural nationalism at Banaras.
5. **1916:** BHU movement converts Banaras education energy into a university (**§12.6**).
6. Home Rule leagues (1916–18) run educative propaganda for self-government through press and local committees.
7. In **1937**, Wardha/Nai Talim appears as Gandhi’s Basic Education scheme (distinct later layer).
8. Exams mix these three layers; sorting by year prevents traps.

### National Education — How It Works

- National Education first surged in the **Swadeshi** years after Partition of Bengal (**1905–06**), with boycott of official institutions.
- The **National Council of Education (August 1906)** and Bengal National College are the classic Swadeshi education tags.
- Home Rule (1916–18) revived **educative propaganda**: Home Rule was as much teaching self-government as petitioning for it.
- Besant’s education work (Central Hindu College) and Malaviya’s BHU project show education as nation-building, not only classroom reform.
- Distinguish three exam layers: **Swadeshi NCE 1906**, **BHU 1916**, **Wardha/Nai Talim 1937**.
- National Education questions often hide inside Swadeshi or Home Rule chronologies rather than as a lone heading.
- Trap: "National Education = only Wardha 1937." Wardha is late Gandhian; national education begins with **Swadeshi**.
- Trap: "NCE 1906 = Banaras Hindu University." Different project, place, and year.
- Trap: "Home Rule had no education agenda." Propaganda and political education were central methods.
- Trap: "BHU replaced NCE in Bengal." BHU is **Varanasi**; NCE is **Bengal Swadeshi** stream.

> **Exam note:** If options mix NCE, BHU, and Wardha, sort by year: **1906 → 1916 → 1937**.

## 12.6 BHU Movement

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Banaras Hindu University (BHU)** | Central university at Varanasi founded **1916** as a major Indian public education project |
| **BHU Movement** | Campaign of fundraising, petitioning, and organisation to secure a Hindu university at Banaras |
| **Madan Mohan Malaviya** | Chief public organiser/founder-tag of BHU in exam memory |
| **Central Hindu College** | Besant’s 1898 college that became part of the BHU institutional nucleus |

### Causes

1. Nationalists wanted a modern university rooted in Indian cultural self-respect, not only official colleges.
2. Swadeshi-era national education mood made a Hindu university scheme politically attractive.
3. Malaviya’s long campaign needed princes’, zamindars’, and public subscriptions to fund a large campus.
4. Annie Besant’s Central Hindu College provided an existing Banaras educational base that could be integrated.
5. Colonial sanction was required; organisers had to negotiate with the Government of India for a university Act/charter.

### Course of the BHU Movement — Step by Step

1. Early twentieth-century proposals and prospectuses circulate for a Hindu university at Banaras.
2. Malaviya mobilises public opinion, donors, and princely support for the scheme.
3. Cooperation develops with Annie Besant’s Banaras education network and Central Hindu College assets.
4. A society/committee for establishing the Hindu University organises formal negotiations with the colonial government.
5. Legislative/charter process culminates in the Banaras Hindu University framework (**1915–16** memory band).
6. On **4 February 1916 (Vasant Panchami)**, Viceroy **Lord Hardinge** lays the foundation stone.
7. Extension lectures in February 1916 feature national figures (including Gandhi’s early public address in India in this Banaras series).
8. BHU grows as a residential university combining modern faculties with Indian cultural emphasis — a lasting UP landmark.

### BHU Movement — How It Works

- Exam tag hierarchy: **Malaviya = founder/organiser**; **Besant = Central Hindu College contributor**; princes/donors = funding backbone.
- BHU is a **UP-centred** Modern India fact: location = **Varanasi/Banaras**.
- Do not collapse BHU into Swadeshi NCE; NCE is a **1906 Bengal** national-education body.
- Foundation year **1916** coincides with Home Rule launch year — useful chronology glue, different movements.
- Hardinge’s foundation-stone role is ceremonial colonial sanction, not "Hardinge founded BHU".
- Trap: "BHU founded in 1906 with NCE." **1916**, not 1906.
- Trap: "Besant alone founded BHU." Malaviya is the standard founder tag.
- Trap: "BHU is in Allahabad/Lucknow." It is **Varanasi**.

> **Exam note:** UP Focus triangle = **Malaviya ↔ BHU 1916 ↔ Varanasi**.

### Results of the BHU Movement

| Integrated Central Hindu College stream | Links Besant education to Malaviya university |
| UP intellectual centre | Permanent Varanasi landmark in UPPCS |
| Model of public fundraising university | "People’s university" memory |

## 12.7 Labour Movement

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Labour Movement** | Organised action by industrial, railway, and mine workers through unions, strikes, and labour representation |
| **Trade union** | Workers' association for wages, hours, conditions, and collective bargaining |
| **ILO** | International Labour Organization (1919); India sent labour representatives to its early conferences |

### Causes

1. Mills, railways, mines, and plantations created a concentrated wage-labour class in colonial cities and industrial belts.
2. Low wages, long hours, racial discrimination, and wartime price rise produced repeated strikes.
3. Nationalist politics after World War I sought organised worker support for mass mobilisation.
4. International labour diplomacy through the ILO pulled Indian labour onto a world stage in **1919**.
5. Left and Congress streams later competed to lead unions, splitting national centres along party lines.

### Course — Step by Step

1. Early twentieth-century local unions and strikes appear in Bombay, Madras, Calcutta, and railway centres.
2. In **1919**, **N.M. Joshi** goes as Indian labour representative to the **ILO Washington Summit**.
3. In **1920**, **AITUC** is founded as the first major all-India trade-union congress.
4. **Lala Lajpat Rai** is associated in exam memory with early AITUC presidentship.
5. Through the 1920s–30s, communist and left influence grows inside AITUC; **Meerut Conspiracy (1929)** hits labour-left organisers.
6. After independence politics crystallises, **INTUC (1947)** emerges as the Congress labour wing.
7. **BMS (1955)** develops as the RSS/BJP-linked labour centre; other left centres complete the match map.
8. UPPCS questions focus on **year + person + party affiliation**, not long strike narratives.

### Labour Movement — How It Works

- Labour Prelims is a matching sport: ILO person, AITUC year, and union↔party pairs.
- **N.M. Joshi = ILO Washington 1919** is a direct UPPCS ask (**2020 Q16**).
- AITUC (**1920**) is the syllabus "Trade Union Congress" core; INTUC is later (**1947**).
- Joseph Baptista appears in Home Rule as Tilak League President and sometimes as an ILO distractor — do not swap him with Joshi.
- V.P. Wadia is a famous early Madras labour figure but is **not** the UPPCS ILO-1919 answer.
- Party splits after 1947 explain why one map has four unions and four parties.
- Bonded rural labour is a different syllabus angle (Topic 8 depth); here the focus is organised industrial unions and labour politics.
- Trap: "ILO 1919 rep = Joseph Baptista or V.P. Wadia." UPPCS key = **N.M. Joshi**.
- Trap: "Labour movement began only after Independence." AITUC is **1920**.
- Trap: "All unions = Congress." Only **INTUC** is the Congress centre in the standard map.

> **Exam note:** Drill **Joshi–1919** and **AITUC–1920** as a locked pair before learning later INTUC/BMS years.

## 12.10 Congress Socialist Party

### Definitions

4. They needed an organised faction to push agrarian, labour, and anti-feudal demands inside the national movement.

4. CSP works as a socialist caucus **inside Congress**, not as a fully separate anti-Congress party at birth.

6. **Jawaharlal Nehru** sympathises with socialist ideas but **never formally joins** CSP.

## 12.11 Regional Parties

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Regional Parties** | Organisations whose primary base and agenda are province/community-centred rather than all-India Congress nationalism alone |
| **Justice Party (1916)** | Madras Presidency non-Brahmin political party |
| **Self-Respect Movement** | Periyar's radical social-equality movement in Tamil country after leaving Congress |
| **Akali stream** | Punjab Sikh political mobilisation around gurdwara reform / Akali Dal politics |

### Causes

1. Province-level social hierarchies (especially Brahmin dominance in Madras services/education) created non-Congress political demand.
2. Morley–Minto communal representation for Muslims inspired other groups to seek similar safeguards.
3. Congress's social composition in Madras looked Brahmin-heavy to non-Brahmin elites.
4. In Tamil country, Periyar's break with Congress opened a radical Self-Respect path.
5. In Punjab, Sikh gurdwara reform politics matured into Akali organisational strength.

### Course — Step by Step

1. **1916:** Justice Party forms in Madras Presidency around non-Brahmin elite leadership.
2. It criticises Congress as Brahmin-dominated and demands communal representation for non-Brahmins.
3. It contests provincial politics and shapes Madras Presidency reservation/representation debates.
4. **E.V. Ramaswami Naicker (Periyar)** participates in Non-Cooperation, then leaves Congress in **1925**.
5. Periyar builds the **Self-Respect Movement** as radical social reform and anti-caste politics.
6. Punjab Akali/SGPC politics organises Sikh public life from gurdwara reform into party politics.
7. Exams treat these as regional alternatives/complements to all-India Congress nationalism.
8. Forward Bloc is all-India left-nationalist (Bose), not a classic "regional party"; keep it under Left/Topic 14.

### Regional Parties — How It Works

- **Justice Party (1916)** is the highest-yield regional-party PYQ for this topic (**2024 Q13 Both**).
- Statement 1: it opposed Congress as a **Brahmin-dominated** organisation — correct.
- Statement 2: it claimed communal representation for non-Brahmins like Muslim gains under **Morley–Minto (1909)** — correct.
- **Periyar 2023 Q44**: participated in NCM **and** left Congress in 1925 → **Both**.
- Self-Respect is social-radical regionalism; Justice Party is elite non-Brahmin constitutional politics — related Madras story, different organisations.
- Akali Dal/SGPC is the Punjab regional identity-politics parallel.
- Trap: "Justice Party supported Congress as natural anti-colonial allies on social composition." It opposed Congress's Brahmin image in Madras.
- Trap: "Periyar never joined NCM." He did participate, then left in 1925.

## Common Traps — Don't Fall For These

1. **Trap:** One Home Rule League only. **Correct:** Two — Tilak (Apr 1916, Poona) and Besant (Sep 1916, Madras).

2. **Trap:** Tilak League HQ = Madras. **Correct:** **Poona**; Besant = Madras/Adyar.

3. **Trap:** Besant founded Theosophical Society. **Correct:** **Blavatsky + Olcott (1875)**; Besant later leader.

4. **Trap:** Tilak was Congress President in Home Rule years. **Correct:** He **never** presided — **2021 Q50**.

5. **Trap:** Besant INC President in 1916. **Correct:** **1917**.

6. **Trap:** Home Rule is last among 1916–21 events. **Correct:** Home Rule earliest; Moplah last — **2023 Q47**.

7. **Trap:** BHU = NCE 1906. **Correct:** BHU **1916**; NCE **1906**.

8. **Trap:** Besant alone founded BHU. **Correct:** **Malaviya** founder tag; Besant linked via Central Hindu College.

9. **Trap:** ILO 1919 labour rep = Wadia/Baptista. **Correct:** **N.M. Joshi** — **2020 Q16**.

10. **Trap:** AITUC founded 1947. **Correct:** AITUC **1920**; INTUC **1947**.

11. **Trap:** AITUC = Congress union. **Correct:** AITUC ↔ **CPI**; INTUC ↔ **Congress**.

12. **Trap:** CSP formed January 1934; Nehru joined. **Correct:** **Neither** — **2024 Q131**.

13. **Trap:** WPP All-India in 1927 + objective both true. **Correct:** **Only 2** — **2024 Q14**.

14. **Trap:** Ansari in Kanpur Conspiracy 1924. **Correct:** **NOT associated** — **2021 Q86**.
