## 6.0 Agriculture Framework, Land Use & Types of Farming

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Agriculture** | Primary activity of cultivating crops and rearing livestock for food, fibre, fodder, and raw materials |
| **Agrarian structure** | Pattern of land holdings, tenancy, and labour in farming |
| **Land-use** | Classification of geographical area by how land is used |
| **Farming type** | System classified by purpose, intensity, technology, and market link |

### Framework — Overview

UPPCS agriculture questions usually test crop growing conditions with region and season, or institutions such as MSP and research bodies. Start with land-use and farming types (NCERT Class 10 backbone), then study each crop with its climate, soil, season and leading states.

#### Land Use & Farming — How It Works

- India's land is classified into categories such as **forests, barren and unculturable land, permanent pastures, miscellaneous tree crops, culturable waste, fallow lands, and net sown area**.
- **Net sown area** is land sown at least once; **gross cropped area** counts every crop sown in the year, so GCA ≥ NSA.
- **Cropping intensity** = (GCA / NSA) × 100; values above 100% mean multiple cropping.
- Irrigation, HYV seeds, fertilisers, and mechanisation raise intensity — highest in Punjab, Haryana, western UP.
- **Primitive subsistence farming** uses simple tools and family labour; **shifting cultivation (jhum)** is its slash-and-burn form in forested hills.
- **Intensive subsistence farming** dominates monsoon Asia: small holdings, high labour, rice/wheat focus, often with irrigation.
- **Commercial grain farming** specialises in wheat/maize for market with machines (more world than Indian smallholder reality).
- **Plantation agriculture** is capital-and-labour intensive single-crop estates: tea, coffee, rubber, some spices — 2024 PYQ lock for tea.
- **Mixed farming** combines crops and livestock on the same farm for income stability and manure cycling.
- **Dairy farming** specialises in milk; India's White Revolution scaled cooperatives rather than only Western-style dairy farms.
- **Mediterranean agriculture** (world) = citrus, vines, olives in winter-rain climates — 2020 citrus PYQ uses this belt.
- Human factors (technology, irrigation, institutions, markets) reshape natural crop limits — Punjab rice is irrigation-made, not rainfall-made.

> **Exam note:** First ask: is the question about **farming type**, **season**, **crop condition**, or **institution**? Wrong bucket = wrong option.

#### Land-Use Categories (exam list)

| Category | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Forests | Official forest area |
| Barren / unculturable | Rocky, desert, etc. hard to cultivate |
| Permanent pastures | Grazing lands |
| Miscellaneous tree crops | Orchards, groves not in NSA logic alone |
| Culturable waste | Can be cultivated with effort |
| Fallow (current / other) | Temporarily uncultivated |
| Net sown area | Actually sown |

#### Farming Types — Comparison

| Type | Purpose | Intensity | India example |
|------|---------|-----------|---------------|
| Primitive subsistence / jhum | Family food | Low | NE hills |
| Intensive subsistence | Family + local market | High labour | Indo-Gangetic rice–wheat |
| Commercial / cash | Market | High capital/inputs | Cotton, sugarcane |
| Plantation | Export/market perennial | Estate scale | Assam tea, Kerala rubber |
| Mixed | Crops + animals | Medium–high | Peasant mixed farms |
| Dairy | Milk | Specialised | Cooperative milk belts |

### Exam Facts (raata — traps only) — §6.0

| # | Fact to remember |
|---|------------------|
| 1 | Cropping intensity = (Gross cropped area ÷ Net sown area) × 100 |
| 2 | Plantation farming (tea, coffee, rubber) is not the same as ordinary food-grain farming |
| 3 | Jhum is a form of primitive shifting cultivation |
| 4 | Mixed farming means crops and livestock on the same farm |
| 5 | World citrus belts are classically linked with Mediterranean climate |

### PYQs — §6.0

**UPPCS Prelims**

1. **(UPPCS 2024, Q34)** Which of the following crops is predominantly grown under plantation agriculture?
   - Options: A. Wheat / B. Tea / C. Rice / D. Maize
   → **B. Tea** — Tea is a perennial estate crop. Wheat, rice and maize are field food grains, not plantation agriculture.

2. **(UPPCS 2024, Q73)** Consider the following statements with reference to cultivated area of some countries of the world:
   1. In comparison to USA, China and Japan, India has highest geographical area under cultivation.
   2. India's location in tropical monsoon region helps diversified cropping all through the year.
   Which of the above statements is/are correct?
   - Options: A. Both 1 and 2 / B. Neither / C. Only 1 / D. Only 2
   → **Keys contested.** Statement 2 is clearly true (kharif–rabi–zaid where water allows). Statement 1 is often treated true on net-cropland rankings; some coaching keys prefer Only 2. Learn the diversified-cropping logic for sure.

## 6.1 Cropping Seasons, Patterns & Intensity

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cropping season** | Climatic sowing–harvest window |
| **Cropping pattern** | Combination and proportion of crops in space/time |
| **Mono-cropping** | Same crop repeatedly on land |
| **Relay cropping** | Next crop sown before previous is harvested |

### Seasons & Patterns — Overview

Knowing whether a crop is kharif, rabi or zaid removes many wrong options at once. Memorise the full seasonal crop lists, not only two or three examples.

#### Seasons — How It Works

- **Kharif** starts with SW monsoon onset (June–July sowing; Sep–Oct harvest for many crops).
- Kharif needs heat + moisture: rice, maize, sorghum (jowar), pearl millet (bajra), finger millet (ragi), cotton, jute, groundnut, soybean, pigeon pea (tur), black gram, green gram, sesame, guar, and sugarcane planting windows.
- **Rabi** uses cool season after monsoon; western disturbances help NW wheat.
- Rabi staples: wheat, barley, gram, mustard/rapeseed, peas, lentil (masoor), linseed, oat.
- **Zaid** is short, hot, irrigated: watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, bitter gourd, fodder, some vegetables.
- **Sugarcane** is long-duration (10–18 months) — do not force it into only one season box.
- **Tea, coffee, rubber** are perennial plantations — not kharif cereals.
- Eastern India uses **Aus, Aman, Boro** rice seasons (boro is irrigated winter–spring).
- Patterns: rice–wheat (NW/W UP), cotton–jowar (Deccan), bajra–mustard (RJ), rice–jute (Bengal), coconut–spice (Kerala).
- Intensity rises where canal/tube-well water + HYV exist; rainfed Deccan stays lower.

> **Exam note:** If options mix tea with wheat as “same season cereal,” reject. Plantation ≠ rabi grain.

#### Season Master Table

| Season | Months | Moisture | Full core crop set |
|--------|--------|----------|--------------------|
| Kharif | Jun–Oct | SW monsoon | Rice, maize, jowar, bajra, ragi, cotton, jute, groundnut, soybean, tur, urad, moong, sesame |
| Rabi | Oct–Mar | Cool + WD / irrigation | Wheat, barley, gram, mustard, peas, masoor, linseed |
| Zaid | Mar–Jun | Irrigation | Melons, cucumber, vegetables, fodder |

#### Pattern Glossary Table

| Pattern | Simultaneous? | Exam trap |
|---------|---------------|-----------|
| Mixed cropping | Yes | ≠ rotation |
| Intercropping | Yes (rows) | Ratio fixed |
| Crop rotation | No (sequence) | Fertility logic |
| Multiple cropping | Year scale | Needs water |
| Relay cropping | Overlap sow | Advanced pattern |
| Plantation | Perennial | Tea 2024 |

### Exam Facts (raata — traps only) — §6.1

| # | Fact to remember |
|---|------------------|
| 1 | Wheat is a rabi crop; rice is mainly a kharif crop |
| 2 | Cotton, jute and groundnut are kharif crops |
| 3 | Mustard and gram are rabi crops |
| 4 | Cropping intensity = (Gross cropped area ÷ Net sown area) × 100 |
| 5 | Aus, Aman and Boro are the eastern India rice seasons |
| 6 | Mixed cropping = crops grown together at the same time; crop rotation = crops grown one after another |
| 7 | Zaid crops usually need irrigation in the hot short season |
| 8 | Sugarcane is a long-duration crop (about 10–18 months), not a short seasonal cereal |
| 9 | Tea is a plantation crop, not a zaid melon-type crop |
| 10 | Highest cropping intensity is in irrigated Green Revolution plains |

**UPPCS Prelims**

   - Options: A. Wheat / B. Tea / C. Rice / D. Maize
   → **B. Tea** — Plantation pattern is estate perennials (tea, coffee, rubber), not seasonal cereals.

## 6.2 Rice

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Rice / Paddy** | Staple cereal of humid/irrigated India; often grown in puddled fields |
| **Aus** | Autumn / pre-monsoon–early monsoon rice (east) |
| **Aman** | Winter rice harvested in winter (main monsoon rice in Bengal) |
| **Boro** | Summer/spring irrigated rice in eastern India |
| **Golden rice** | GE rice with Vitamin A precursor (β-carotene) |
| **Azolla** | Aquatic fern biofertiliser in flooded rice with Anabaena |

### Rice — Overview

Rice is India’s dominant wet cereal. Remember the growing needs (heat, high rainfall or irrigation, clayey alluvial soil, often standing water), the eastern Aus/Aman/Boro calendar, Azolla as a biofertiliser in flooded fields, and that Golden rice is correctly linked with Vitamin A.

#### Rice — How It Works

- Climatic ideal: mean temperature roughly **above 20–27°C** during growing period and rainfall **about 100 cm or more**, else assured irrigation.
- Prefers **clayey or clay-loam alluvial soils** that puddle and hold water; flooded fields suppress weeds and favour rice physiology.
- Main India crop is **kharif (aman-type)**; **boro** is irrigated winter–spring in Bihar–Bengal–Assam belt.
- Leading producers rotate among **West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh/Telangana, Odisha, Bihar, Chhattisgarh** — lock the humid east + irrigated NW belt, not one eternal rank.
- **Punjab–Haryana** rice is a Green Revolution / tube-well creation in a region that is not naturally a 150 cm rain belt.
- Transplanting from nursery is traditional; **direct seeded rice** spreads where labour is costly.
- **Azolla–Anabaena** fixes nitrogen in water-logged fields (2023 Q104).
- Golden rice is correctly matched with Vitamin A. In the 2018 “incorrect pair” question, the wrong pair was ozone layer–troposphere (ozone is in the stratosphere).
- Rice–wheat system of western UP/Punjab is the GR workhorse rotation.
- Upland / hill rice exists but exam defaults to lowland puddled rice unless stated.
- Methane from puddled rice is an environment PYQ border — do not confuse with crop-condition geography.
- India is among the world's top rice producers; export grades (basmati GI) differ from common rice.

> **Exam note:** In water-logged rice fields the biofertiliser plant is Azolla (2023). Golden rice is correctly matched with Vitamin A (2018). Questions on polished rice and beriberi belong to science/nutrition, not crop geography.

#### Rice — Condition Card

| Spec | Value / note |
|------|----------------|
| Temperature | ~20–27°C+ growing |
| Rainfall | ~100 cm+ or irrigation |
| Soil | Clayey / alluvial, water-retentive |
| Water | Standing / puddled typical |
| Season | Mainly kharif; boro irrigated |
| Lab | High (transplanting) |
| Biofertiliser | Azolla |
| Biotech lock | Golden rice – Vitamin A |

#### Rice — Distribution Belts

| Belt | States | Note |
|------|--------|------|
| Lower Ganga / delta | West Bengal, Bangladesh border | Aus/Aman/Boro possible |
| Middle Ganga | E UP, Bihar | Rice–wheat / rice–pulses |
| Coastal east | Odisha, AP/TG, TN deltas | Canal rice |
| Irrigated NW | Punjab, Haryana, W UP | Tube-well rice |
| NE valleys | Assam | Humid rice + tea terraces nearby |
| Chhattisgarh plain | CG | “Rice bowl” tag in many texts |

#### UP Focus — Rice

| Point | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Role | Major kharif cereal statewide |
| West | Rice–wheat with sugarcane |

### PYQs — §6.2

**UPPCS Prelims**

1. **(UPPCS 2023, Q104)** The aquatic plant commonly used as a biofertilizer in water-logged rice fields is—
   - Options: A. Lemna / B. Azolla / C. Wolffia / D. Trapa
   → **B. Azolla** — Azolla hosts Anabaena, which fixes nitrogen in flooded rice. Lemna/Wolffia are duckweeds; Trapa is water chestnut — not the standard rice biofertiliser lock.

2. **(UPPCS 2018, Q57)** Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
   - Options:
     - A. Renneting — Cheese
     - B. Genetic Engineering — Plasmids
     - C. Golden rice — Vitamin A
     - D. Ozone layer — Troposphere
   → **D** — Ozone layer is in the **stratosphere**, not troposphere. Golden rice–Vitamin A is a **correct** pair (do not mark C as wrong).

## 6.3 Wheat

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Wheat** | Premier rabi cereal of cool-season India |
| **Wheat bowl** | Irrigated NW plains specialised in HYV wheat |
| **Dwarf / semi-dwarf wheat** | HYV architecture of Green Revolution (Mexican lineages) |

### Wheat — Overview

Wheat is India’s main **rabi** cereal. It needs cool weather while growing and clearer, brighter weather while ripening. Uttar Pradesh, Punjab and Haryana form the core bowl. Green Revolution personality and western-UP region questions usually sit on this crop.

#### Wheat — How It Works

- Needs **cool weather during growth** (often cited ~10–15°C) and **brighter, warmer conditions while ripening** (~20–25°C range in many textbooks).
- Frost at flowering and rain/hail at harvest cut yield and quality.
- Rainfall ideal about **50–75 cm** or **irrigation**; soils: well-drained **alluvial loams**.
- Sown Oct–Dec; harvested Mar–Apr (rabi).
- Core producers: **Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Bihar** — UP frequently among the very top.
- HYV semi-dwarf wheat + fertiliser + water created the GR surplus in Punjab–Haryana–western UP (2022 Q91).
- **Assam — Wheat is a wrong match** (2022 Q142 style).
- MSP procurement and FCI storage are strongest for wheat (and paddy) among cereals.
- Rust diseases matter in pest matches; geography papers may ask pathogen type separately.
- Temperate world wheat belts (prairies, steppes) differ from India's subtropical winter wheat adaptation.
- Rain at ripening causes sprouting damage — why “bright sunshine at ripening” is an exam phrase.

> **Exam note:** Do not mark wheat as a kharif crop, as Assam’s specialty, or as a plantation crop. Assam–Wheat is a standard wrong option (2022).

| Spec | Value / note |
|------|----------------|
| Season | Rabi |

| Temp ripening | Brighter/warmer |
| Rainfall | ~50–75 cm or irrigation |
| Soil | Alluvial loam, well-drained |

| Belt | States | Note |

| **Tur / Arhar** | Pigeon pea — leading kharif pulse |

### Pulses — Overview

Pulses are protein crops and **biological fertiliser factories**. 2019 locks Rhizobium–pulses.

#### Pulses — How It Works

- Root nodules host **Rhizobium** which fixes atmospheric nitrogen — 2019 Q46 answer is **Pulses**, not rice/wheat/sugarcane.
- Major pulses: **gram, tur/arhar, moong (green gram), urad (black gram), masoor (lentil), peas, moth, kulthi (horse gram)**.
- **Gram** = mainly **rabi**; prefers cool dry ripening; MP, RJ, UP, MH important.
- **Tur** = mainly **kharif**; MH, KA, GJ, UP, MP important.
- **Moong/Urad** = kharif and also short-duration zaid/summer in irrigated niches.
- **Masoor** = rabi lentil in MP, UP, Bihar, RJ belts.
- Prefer moderate water; excess rain at harvest damages quality; often grown on residual moisture.
- India is top producer and still imports in deficit years — geography + economy overlap.
- Pulse–cereal rotation restores nitrogen and breaks pest cycles — pre-chemical fertility wisdom.
- MSP covers major pulses but procurement historically thinner than wheat/rice.
- Do not classify mustard as a pulse (it is an oilseed).

> **Exam note:** Nitrogen-fixing bacteria combine with pulse roots. The answer is pulses, not rice, wheat or sugarcane.

### Oilseeds — Overview

India’s three highest-yield state locks are **groundnut in Gujarat**, **mustard in Rajasthan**, and **soybean in Madhya Pradesh**. Also know the wider NCERT set: sesame, sunflower, castor, linseed, safflower and niger. The **Yellow Revolution** refers to oilseeds — not horticulture (Golden) or fertilisers (Grey).

#### Oilseeds — How It Works

- India grows a basket of oilseeds because no single crop suits all agro-climates.
- **Groundnut (kharif):** sandy loam; Gujarat leadership; also AP/TG, TN, RJ, KA; needs moderate rain and bright ripening.

- **Safflower (kusum):** prefers cool dry rabi in Deccan/central India.
- **Niger (ramtil):** tribal/peninsular pockets; minor but listed in complete sets.
- **Yellow Revolution** and Technology Mission on Oilseeds narratives explain production push; 2022 match **Yellow → oilseed**.
- Oilcakes feed the dairy sector — White Revolution link.

## 6.7 Cotton

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cotton (kapas)** | Premier fibre crop of warm India; “white gold” |
| **Lint** | Fibre after ginning separates seeds |
| **Long / medium / short staple** | Fibre length classes affecting spinning quality |
| **Bt cotton** | Genetically modified cotton with bollworm resistance trait |

### Cotton — Overview

Cotton is India’s main warm-season fibre crop. It grows best on moisture-retentive **black (regur) soils** of the Deccan and Gujarat, needs a long warm period of about **210 frost-free days**, and is sown mainly in the **kharif** season. Maharashtra, Gujarat and Telangana form the core belt. UPPCS 2020 tested whether Ahmedabad being in the cotton region automatically makes it India’s largest cotton textile centre (it does not).

#### Cotton — How It Works

- Needs **warm climate (often >21°C)**, **about 50–100 cm rainfall**, **bright sunshine for boll opening**, and roughly **210 frost-free days**.
- Best on **black regur soils** of Deccan Trap; also grown on alluvial and red soils with management.
- Mainly **kharif**; leading states: **Maharashtra, Gujarat, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan, Haryana, Punjab, MP, Karnataka**.
- **Long staple** (including Egyptian/Sea-island type quality talk) vs **medium** vs **short staple** — industry cares; prelims may ask black-soil link more than micronaire.
- **Bt cotton** adoption transformed yields and bollworm management — pest-control crossover.
- Ahmedabad lies in a major cotton-growing region, so raw cotton is available nearby. Still, Ahmedabad is not treated as India’s largest cotton textile centre in the standard UPPCS question; Mumbai traditionally holds that place.
- Rainfed Vidarbha cotton differs in risk from irrigated north-west cotton.
- Excess cloud/rain at boll opening stains fibre; drought at flowering reduces yield.
- Cotton is not a Himalayan crop and not a delta-jute twin.
- Ginning towns and textile mills create crop–industry geography questions.

> **Exam note:** Remember two separate facts: (1) Ahmedabad is in the cotton belt. (2) Ahmedabad is not India’s largest cotton textile centre. Cotton itself is a black-soil Deccan crop, not a Himalayan crop.

## 6.9 Sugarcane

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Sugarcane** | Long-duration commercial cane for sugar, gur, khandsari, ethanol |
| **Recovery rate** | Sugar obtained per unit cane |
| **FRP** | Fair and Remunerative Price for cane (distinct label from foodgrain MSP) |
| **Tropical vs sub-tropical cane** | South/MH vs north UP belt physiology |

### Sugarcane — Overview

Sugarcane is a long-duration commercial crop. Uttar Pradesh often leads in total area and production, while Maharashtra often shows higher productivity and a strong cooperative factory sector — that contrast is the 2019 Assertion–Reason trap. Cane price policy uses **FRP** (Fair and Remunerative Price), not the same label as cereal MSP.

#### Sugarcane — How It Works

- Needs **hot humid conditions (~21–27°C)**, rainfall **about 75–150 cm** or **heavy irrigation**, and **deep fertile soils** (alluvial or black).
- Duration **~10–18 months** — spans seasons; planting seasons vary north vs south.
- **Uttar Pradesh** frequently leads in **area and total production**.
- **Maharashtra** often leads in **yield/productivity** with strong **cooperative sugar factories**.
- Exam trap: UP may produce more cane overall while Maharashtra has higher productivity; Maharashtra’s cooperative factories are a real fact, but they do not explain UP’s lower productivity.
- Sub-tropical north (UP/Bihar) vs tropical (MH/KA/TN) differ in sucrose accumulation and growth rhythm.
- Heavy water and nutrient demand; linked to groundwater stress debates.
- By-products: **bagasse** (power/paper), **molasses** (ethanol, alcohol).
- Price policy uses **FRP**; states may announce SAP (State Advised Price) — trap vs cereal MSP wording.
- Frost in north and waterlogging both damage cane.
- Philippines coconut & sugarcane agriculture credit: **Spanish and Americans** (2023 Q64).

> **Exam note:** UP can produce more cane overall while still having lower productivity than Maharashtra. Cooperative factories in Maharashtra do not by themselves explain UP’s lower productivity (2019 answer B). In precise questions, cane support price is FRP, not cereal MSP.

## 6.10 Tea

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Tea** | Plantation beverage crop of humid hills/foothills |
| **CTC** | Crush–Tear–Curl manufacturing for quick-brew bulk tea |
| **Orthodox tea** | Leaf tea process for premium grades (e.g., Darjeeling style) |
| **Flush** | Seasonal plucking rounds |

### Tea — Overview

Tea is the classic plantation crop in the syllabus (asked directly in 2024). Main Indian belts are Assam, West Bengal (Dooars and Darjeeling), and the Nilgiri–Anamalai hills. Gujarat–Tea is a wrong match.

#### Tea — How It Works

- Needs **warm humid climate (~20–30°C)**, **150–300 cm well-distributed rain**, and **well-drained sloping soils** (often loamy/lateritic hill soils).
- Frost-free preferred; shade trees common in many Indian gardens.
- Belts: **Assam** (bulk), **West Bengal Dooars & Darjeeling** (Dooars bulk; Darjeeling premium GI), **Nilgiris/Anamalai** (TN/Kerala), smaller HP/UK gardens.
- Grown as **perennial bushes** on estates — not a seasonal cereal.
- **CTC** dominates Indian everyday tea; **orthodox** matters for premium leaf.
- Labour-intensive plucking; women workers feature in textbook social geography.
- **Gujarat — Tea is incorrect** (2022).
- Processing near gardens because green leaf is perishable.
- India is top producer/consumer globally; China and others compete.
- Distinct from coffee: generally wetter and different elevation niches.

> **Exam note:** When asked which crop is grown under plantation agriculture, choose tea — not wheat, rice or maize (UPPCS 2024 Q34).

### Regions & Zones — Overview

Exams test **three number locks** (15 / ~20 / ~127) and **zone ↔ state ↔ crop** links. Memorise all 15 ACZ names.

## 6.16 Irrigation Types & Command Area Development

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Irrigation** | Artificial water application to crops |
| **Canal irrigation** | Surface water via canals from rivers/reservoirs |
| **Inundation canal** | Flood-season canal without headworks regulation (older type) |
| **Perennial canal** | Regulated year-round supply from barrage/dam |
| **Well irrigation** | Groundwater from dug wells |
| **Tube-well irrigation** | Groundwater via powered bore wells |
| **Tank irrigation** | Stored local runoff in tanks (peninsula classic) |
| **Drip irrigation** | Micro-irrigation to root zone drop-wise |
| **Sprinkler irrigation** | Overhead spray irrigation |
| **Command Area Development (CAD/CADWM)** | Programme to utilise created irrigation potential fully |
| **Warabandi** | Timed roster of canal water turns |
| **Irrigation potential created vs utilised** | Gap CAD tries to close |

### Irrigation & CAD — Overview

Map irrigation to physiography. CAD is about **using** water already “created” by projects.

#### Irrigation — How It Works

- **Canal irrigation** dominates large alluvial plains (Punjab, Haryana, UP, Indira Gandhi Canal in RJ).
- **Perennial canals** from dams/barrages differ from old **inundation canals** dependent on flood rise.
- **Tube wells** powered the GR in NW and western UP — flexible but deplete groundwater.
- **Dug wells** still matter in hard-rock and rural settings with shallower water.
- **Tank irrigation** suits peninsular hard rock (TN, KA, AP/TG, MH) storing monsoon pulses.
- **Drip** maximises efficiency for horticulture, sugarcane, cotton, vegetables in water-scarce areas.
- **Sprinkler** suits undulating/sandy fields and some cereals/fodder.
- Over-irrigation without drainage → **salinity/alkalinity (usar)** in canal commands.
- **CAD / CADWM** builds field channels, drainage, warabandi, and farmer participation so tail-enders get water.
- Multipurpose river projects (Bhakra, Hirakud, Nagarjuna Sagar, etc.) create canal commands — link to water-resources topic without dumping dam lists here.
- Rainfed share remains large for millets/pulses — irrigation is unevenly distributed.
- 2020 budget A/R linked doubling farmer income to agriculture–irrigation action points.
- Power/irrigation subsidies are generally **indirect** farm support (2022 contested subsidy Q).

> **Exam note:** Tanks → peninsula; canals → plains; drip → root zone. CAD ≠ building a new dam from scratch — it develops the **command**.

#### Irrigation Types — Master Table

| Type | Best terrain | Strength | Risk |
|------|--------------|----------|------|
| Perennial canal | Alluvial plains | Large area | Waterlogging/salinity |
| Inundation canal | Flood plains | Cheap historic | Unreliable timing |
| Tube well | Alluvial aquifers | On-demand | GW depletion |
| Dug well | Varied | Local | Limited yield |
| Tank | Peninsular hard rock | Stores monsoon | Siltation |
| Drip | Horticulture/arid | High efficiency | Capital cost |
| Sprinkler | Sandy/undulating | Uniform spray | Wind drift |

#### CAD — What It Includes (exam list)

| Component | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Field channels | Carry water to farms |
| Land levelling | Even application |
| Warabandi | Fair turns |
| Drainage | Prevent waterlogging |
| Farmer organisations | Participation / maintenance |

| # | Fact to remember |

| 2 | Tube wells powered the Green Revolution in the north-west |

### Institutions, Seeds & Pests — Overview

ICAR researches; CACP prices — never swap them. Seed chain order is a favourite lock.

#### How It Works

- **ICAR** coordinates institutes, AICRP trials, education standards, and technology generation.
- **IARI Pusa** is the flagship; many agronomy/HYV narratives pass through ICAR–SAU systems.

## 6.19 Shifting Cultivation, Organic Farming & Precision Farming

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Shifting cultivation / Jhum / Bewar / Podu / Dahiya** (local names) | Slash-and-burn temporary cultivation with fallow rotation |
| **Organic farming** | Standards-based farming without synthetic fertilisers/pesticides/GM (as defined) |
| **Precision farming** | Site-specific management using sensors, GIS/GPS, drones, variable rates |
| **Biofertiliser** | Microbial inoculants (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Azolla, PSB) |
| **Biopesticide** | Biological pest control products |

### Alternative & Traditional Systems — Overview

Three syllabus bullets often tested as definition traps. Keep them separate.

#### How It Works

- **Jhum** clears and burns forest plots, grows mixed crops 1–3 years, then fallows for recovery; common in **North-East** (also historic Central Indian pockets under local names).
- Problems: deforestation, erosion, biodiversity loss when fallow cycles shorten under population pressure.
- Policy push: settled terraces, horticulture, rubber/spice alternatives in NE.
- **Organic farming** uses compost, green manure, crop rotation, biofertilisers (**Azolla**, Rhizobium), biopesticides; certification enables premium markets.
- Organic ≠ “any traditional practice” automatically — standards and prohibited substances matter in scheme questions.
- **Precision farming** varies seed/fertiliser/water by zone within a field using soil maps, moisture sensors, satellite advisories, and drones.
- Precision raises efficiency but needs capital, data, and skills — still expanding from pilots.
- 2025 Delhi **bio-decomposer** (fungal mix, free to farmers) is residue management / farm practice PYQ.
- These systems can combine: organic + precision is possible; jhum is neither.

> **Exam note:** Jhum = NE shifting. Organic bans synthetic agrochemicals. Precision = site-specific, not blanket broadcasting.

#### Comparison Table

| System | Core idea | Where / tool |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Jhum | Slash-burn rotation | North-East hills |
| Organic | No synthetic chemicals | Certified farms; Azolla rice |
| Precision | Variable-rate tech | GPS/GIS/sensors |

### Exam Facts (raata — traps only) — §6.19

| # | Fact to remember |
|---|------------------|
| 1 | Jhum (shifting cultivation) is mainly associated with North-East India |
| 2 | Local names include podu, bewar and dahiya in different regions |
| 3 | Organic farming avoids synthetic fertilisers and pesticides (as per standards) |
| 4 | Azolla is used as a biofertiliser in rice |
| 5 | Precision farming means site-specific use of water, seed and fertiliser |
| 6 | Delhi’s bio-decomposer spray is free to farmers and made from a mix of fungi |
| 7 | When jhum fallow period becomes too short, erosion and forest damage increase |
| 8 | Precision farming does not mean stopping irrigation; it means using water more accurately |

## 6.20 MSP & Agricultural Marketing

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **MSP** | Government floor price to prevent distress sale |
| **FRP** | Fair and Remunerative Price for sugarcane |
| **CACP** | Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices — **recommends** MSP |
| **Procurement** | Public purchase at MSP (FCI/state agencies) |
| **APMC mandi** | Regulated market yard under state APMC Acts |
| **e-NAM** | Electronic National Agriculture Market linking mandis |
| **AIDC** | Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess |
| **PM-KISAN** | Income support to farmer families (awareness scheme) |
| **PMFBY** | Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana — crop insurance awareness |

### MSP & Marketing — Overview

For MSP questions, first learn the institutional chain: the **CACP recommends** prices and the **Union Cabinet decides**. Memorising one year’s rupee figure helps only if that exact PYQ year is being revised; the institution lock is permanent.

#### MSP & Marketing — How It Works

- **CACP** analyses costs (including imputed family labour variants in debates) and **recommends** MSPs; **Union Cabinet** decides.
- 2024 Q43: Who recommends MSP? → **CACP**, not RBI / NITI / Finance Ministry.
- MSP aims to insure farmers and nudge crop choices; effectiveness needs **procurement**.
- Wheat and paddy have the deepest procurement historically; pulses/oilseeds often weaker in practice.
- **Sugarcane uses FRP** (and possible state SAP) — precise papers distinguish FRP from cereal MSP.
- 2018 asked paddy MSP Rs. **1,750**/quintal for 2018–19 — historical PYQ, not eternal number.
- **APMC** mandis are traditional regulated markets; reform debates concern alternative channels.
- **e-NAM** networks mandis for transparent online discovery — concept lock.
- **PM-KISAN** = direct income support; **PMFBY** = insurance — scheme awareness layer.
- **AIDC** 2021 PYQ: levied on **29** products.
- Indirect subsidies (fertiliser, cheap power/water) ≠ MSP; 2022 Q74 contested combinations.

> **Exam note:** CACP recommends MSP; the Cabinet decides. Sugarcane uses FRP. e-NAM is the electronic national network of mandis.

## 6.21 Green Revolution & Evergreen Revolution

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Green Revolution** | Mid-1960s+ package of HYV seeds, irrigation, fertilisers, pesticides, credit raising cereal yields |
| **Evergreen Revolution** | Swaminathan’s idea of sustained productivity **without ecological harm** |
| **HYV** | High-yielding varieties responsive to inputs |
| **Norman Borlaug** | HYV wheat scientist; Nobel **Peace** Prize |
| **M.S. Swaminathan** | Leading Indian GR / food policy scientist |

### Green Revolution — Overview

The Green Revolution is the mid-1960s package of HYV seeds, irrigation, fertilisers and related inputs that raised cereal yields, especially in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh. Learn the package, the region, and the personalities (Swaminathan, Borlaug). **Evergreen Revolution** is Swaminathan’s later idea of high productivity without ecological damage.

#### Green Revolution — How It Works

- Food crisis of mid-1960s pushed HYV wheat (then rice) with water and chemicals.
- Package: **HYV + assured irrigation + fertilisers + pesticides + credit/mechanisation**.
- Core geography: **Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh**, plus irrigated pockets.
- 2022 Q91: Western UP developed + witnessed GR → **Both**.
- Gains: yields, marketed surplus, food self-sufficiency narrative.
- Costs: regional inequality, groundwater decline, soil nutrient mining, pesticide load, millet/pulse neglect.
- **Swaminathan ↔ GR** (2022 Q39); **Borlaug Nobel = Peace** not Agriculture (2018 Q56).
- **Kurien = White**, not GR; **Amartya Sen = entitlements**, not GR tech (2025 Q147).
- **Evergreen Revolution** stresses productivity with sustainability — exam word associated with Swaminathan thinking.
- “Second Green Revolution” talk often means eastern India / pulses-oilseeds / tech deepening — awareness phrase.

> **Exam note:** Borlaug’s Nobel is in Peace, not Agriculture (2018). Swaminathan is linked with the Green Revolution and the Evergreen idea. Amartya Sen — not Swaminathan — introduced the entitlements approach to food security (2025).
