# Biodiversity

## Definition

**Biodiversity** (biological diversity) is the **variety and variability of all life forms** on Earth — encompassing diversity at **genetic, species, and ecosystem levels** — and the ecological complexes of which they are part.

**CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity, 1992) definition:**  
*"Biodiversity means the variability among living organisms from all sources including, inter alia, terrestrial, marine and other aquatic ecosystems and the ecological complexes of which they are part; this includes diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems."*

**E.O. Wilson (1988)** — popularised the term "biodiversity."

**Also called:** Biological diversity, biotic diversity.

## Explanation

### Three Levels of Biodiversity

| Level | What It Means | Example |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| **Genetic Diversity** | Variation of genes within a species | Different rice varieties in India |
| **Species Diversity** | Variety of species in a region | 45,000+ plant species in India |
| **Ecosystem Diversity** | Variety of ecosystems/habitats | Forests, wetlands, deserts, coral reefs |

### Dimensions of Biodiversity

1. **Alpha (α) diversity** — Diversity within a single community/ecosystem.
2. **Beta (β) diversity** — Diversity difference between two ecosystems.
3. **Gamma (γ) diversity** — Total regional diversity (alpha + beta).

### Biodiversity as a Concept

- Not just counting species — includes **genetic variability**, **species richness**, **ecosystem variety**, and **ecological processes**.
- **Biodiversity is not evenly distributed** — highest in tropics (latitudinal gradient).
- **Megadiverse countries** — 17 countries harbour ~70% of world's species; India is one.
- **Biodiversity hotspots** — 36 global hotspots covering 2.4% of land but 50% of plant species.

### Why Biodiversity Matters

- Foundation of ecosystem services (food, medicine, climate regulation).
- Insurance against environmental change (genetic reservoir).
- Cultural, aesthetic, and spiritual values.
- Economic value (pharmaceuticals, agriculture, tourism).

## Important Facts

- **~8.7 million species** estimated on Earth (Mora et al., 2011); only ~1.9 million described.
- **India = 4 of 36 biodiversity hotspots** (Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats, Sundaland).
- **India = 1 of 17 megadiverse countries** (2.4% of world's land, 7–8% of recorded species).
- **22 May** — International Day for Biological Diversity.
- **CBD** signed at Rio Earth Summit (1992); India ratified 2003.
- **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** — India's CBD implementation law.
- **IPBES (2019)** — 1 million species at risk of extinction.

# Levels of Biodiversity

## Definition

**Levels of biodiversity** refer to the **hierarchical organisation of biological diversity** at three fundamental levels recognised by the CBD and ecological science:

1. **Genetic Diversity** — Variation of genes within species.
2. **Species Diversity** — Variety and abundance of species in a region.
3. **Ecosystem Diversity** — Variety of habitats, ecosystems, and ecological processes.

Additionally, **alpha, beta, and gamma diversity** are spatial scales of measurement.

## Explanation

### Three Primary Levels (CBD Framework)

```
BIODIVERSITY
├── GENETIC LEVEL
│   ├── Allele diversity
│   ├── Gene pool variation
│   └── Genotype differences
├── SPECIES LEVEL
│   ├── Species richness (number of species)
│   ├── Species evenness (relative abundance)
│   └── Species composition
└── ECOSYSTEM LEVEL
    ├── Habitat diversity
    ├── Ecosystem type variety
    └── Ecological process diversity
```

### Spatial Diversity Measures

| Measure | Definition | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Alpha (α) diversity** | Species diversity within a single habitat/community | Species in one forest patch |
| **Beta (β) diversity** | Change in species composition between habitats | Forest vs grassland species difference |
| **Gamma (γ) diversity** | Total diversity across a landscape/region | Total species in Western Ghats |

**Relationship:** γ = α × β (approximately)

### Hierarchy of Biodiversity Levels

| Level | Unit | Study Field |
|-------|------|-------------|
| **Genetic** | Gene, allele, genotype | Population genetics, conservation genetics |
| **Species** | Species, population | Taxonomy, systematics, species ecology |
| **Ecosystem** | Community, biome, habitat | Landscape ecology, ecosystem ecology |

### Biodiversity Pyramid

```
        ECOSYSTEM DIVERSITY (Broadest — biomes, habitats)
              ↑
        SPECIES DIVERSITY (Middle — species richness)
              ↑
        GENETIC DIVERSITY (Foundation — alleles, genes)
```

- **Genetic diversity** is the **foundation** — without it, species cannot adapt.
- **Species diversity** is the **most commonly measured** level.
- **Ecosystem diversity** provides the **framework** for species and genetic diversity.

## Important Facts

- CBD recognises exactly **3 levels** of biodiversity.
- **Genetic diversity** is highest in tropics and oldest ecosystems.
- **Species diversity** follows **latitudinal gradient** — highest near equator.
- **India ranks 10th** in plant species richness and 11th in mammalian diversity globally.
- **Western Ghats** — highest species diversity per unit area in India.
- **Alpha diversity** measured by Shannon-Wiener Index, Simpson's Index.
- Loss at any level affects all other levels (cascading biodiversity loss).

# Genetic Diversity

## Definition

**Genetic diversity** is the **variety of genes, alleles, and genetic traits** within a single species or between different species. It represents the **total genetic information** contained in the gene pool of a population, species, or community.

It is the **most fundamental level** of biodiversity — the raw material for evolution and adaptation.

**Gene pool** = Total collection of all genes and alleles in an interbreeding population.

## Explanation

### Components of Genetic Diversity

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Allelic diversity** | Number of different alleles per gene locus |
| **Heterozygosity** | Proportion of heterozygous individuals |
| **Nucleotide diversity** | DNA sequence variation |
| **Genotype diversity** | Different genetic combinations in population |

### Importance of Genetic Diversity

| Function | Explanation |
|----------|-------------|
| **Adaptation** | Enables species to adapt to changing environments |
| **Disease resistance** | Diverse gene pool resists epidemics |
| **Evolution** | Raw material for natural selection |
| **Breeding programmes** | Source for crop/livestock improvement |
| **Ecosystem resilience** | Genetically diverse populations stabilise ecosystems |

### Threats to Genetic Diversity

| Threat | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Habitat fragmentation** | Populations isolated → inbreeding |
| **Population bottleneck** | Sharp reduction → loss of alleles |
| **Founder effect** | Small colonising group → limited gene pool |
| **Genetic drift** | Random allele loss in small populations |
| **Monoculture agriculture** | Single variety dominance |
| **Inbreeding depression** | Reduced fitness from mating between relatives |

### Conservation of Genetic Diversity

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Gene banks/seed banks** | Store genetic material ex-situ |
| **Cryopreservation** | Freeze sperm, eggs, tissue at −196°C |
| **Captive breeding** | Managed breeding for genetic diversity |
| **Wildlife corridors** | Allow gene flow between populations |
| **In-situ conservation** | Protect natural populations |

### Indian Genetic Diversity Resources

| Resource | Institution | Content |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **National Gene Bank** | NBPGR, New Delhi | 4.5+ lakh plant accessions |
| **National Bureau of Animal Genetic Resources** | NBAGR, Karnal | Indigenous cattle, buffalo, sheep breeds |
| **National Bureau of Fish Genetic Resources** | NBFGR, Lucknow | Fish genetic diversity |
| **National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources** | NBPGR | World's 8th largest gene bank |

## Important Facts

- **Genetic diversity** is the basis of all other biodiversity levels.
- **Cheetah** — severe genetic bottleneck; all individuals nearly genetically identical.
- **Asiatic Lion (Gir)** — single population; inbreeding depression risk.
- **50/500 rule** — 50 individuals to avoid inbreeding; 500 for long-term viability.
- **India has 30,000–50,000 rice varieties** (genetic diversity).
- **Basmati rice** — genetically unique variety (GI tag).
- **Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium** — theoretical state of no genetic change.
- **DNA barcoding** — COI gene for species identification.

# Species Diversity

## Definition

**Species diversity** is the **variety and abundance of different species** in a given area or community, encompassing two components:

1. **Species Richness** — Total number of different species.
2. **Species Evenness** — Relative abundance/proportion of each species.

**Species diversity = Species Richness + Species Evenness**

Measured by indices: **Shannon-Wiener Index (H')**, **Simpson's Index (D)**, **Margalef's Index**.

## Explanation

### Components

| Component | Definition | High Value Means |
|-----------|------------|-----------------|
| **Species Richness (S)** | Number of species | More species present |
| **Species Evenness (E)** | How equally abundant species are | No single species dominates |
| **Species Dominance** | One or few species very abundant | Low evenness |

### Example

| Community | Species | Abundance | Richness | Evenness |
|-----------|---------|-----------|----------|----------|
| **A** | 10 species | 100 each | High (10) | High (equal) |
| **B** | 10 species | 1 species = 910; others = 10 each | High (10) | Low (unequal) |
| **C** | 3 species | 333 each | Low (3) | High (equal) |

Community A has highest species diversity (high richness + high evenness).

### Latitudinal Gradient of Species Diversity

```
Species Diversity
    ↑
    |  ****
    |      ****
    |          ****
    |              ****
    +──────────────────────→ Latitude
   Equator (0°)          Poles (90°)
```

**Reasons for higher tropical diversity:**
1. **Longer evolutionary time** — tropics undisturbed by ice ages.
2. **More solar energy** — higher productivity supports more species.
3. **Complex habitats** — structurally diverse tropical forests.
4. **Niche specialization** — more opportunities for speciation.
5. **Rapoport's Rule** — species ranges smaller in tropics → more species packed.

### Species-Area Relationship

**S = cA^z** (Arrhenius, 1921)
- S = number of species
- A = area
- c, z = constants
- **Larger area = more species** (z ≈ 0.15–0.35)

## Important Facts

- **Tropical rainforests** — highest species diversity per unit area.
- **Western Ghats** — highest species diversity hotspot in India.
- **India = 10th** in plant species richness globally.
- **Shannon-Wiener Index** — most commonly used diversity index.
- **Endemic species** — found only in one geographic area.

# Ecosystem Diversity

## Definition

**Ecosystem diversity** is the **variety of ecosystems, habitats, biomes, and ecological communities** in a given region or on Earth, including the diversity of ecological processes, interactions, and niches within them.

It is the **broadest level** of biodiversity, encompassing all habitat types and the species and genetic diversity they contain.

## Explanation

### Components of Ecosystem Diversity

| Component | Description | Examples |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **Habitat diversity** | Variety of physical environments | Forests, wetlands, deserts, grasslands |
| **Ecosystem type diversity** | Different ecosystem categories | Terrestrial, aquatic, marine, estuarine |
| **Ecological process diversity** | Variety of ecosystem functions | Nutrient cycling, pollination, predation |
| **Biome diversity** | Major regional ecosystems | Tropical rainforest, tundra, savanna |

### India's Ecosystem Diversity

| Ecosystem Type | Examples in India |
|----------------|-------------------|
| **Tropical evergreen forest** | Western Ghats, NE India |
| **Tropical deciduous forest** | Central India, Terai |
| **Thorn forest/scrub** | Rajasthan, Gujarat |
| **Desert** | Thar Desert |
| **Grassland** | Deccan Plateau, Terai |
| **Wetland** | Chilika, Loktak, Keoladeo |
| **Mangrove** | Sundarbans, Bhitarkanika |
| **Coral reef** | Gulf of Mannar, Lakshadweep |
| **Alpine/Himalayan** | High altitude ecosystems |
| **Marine** | 7517 km coastline ecosystems |

### 10 Biogeographic Zones of India (Rodgers & Panwar, 1988)

1. Trans-Himalaya  2. Himalaya  3. Desert  4. Semi-arid  
5. Gangetic Plain  6. Deccan Peninsula  7. Western Ghats  
8. Coasts  9. Northeast  10. Islands

### Ecosystem Diversity vs Species Diversity

| Feature | Ecosystem Diversity | Species Diversity |
|---------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Focus** | Habitats, ecosystems, biomes | Number and abundance of species |
| **Scale** | Landscape/regional | Community level |
| **Measurement** | Ecosystem types, habitat count | Species richness, Shannon Index |
| **Conservation** | Protected areas, biosphere reserves | Species-specific programmes |

### Threats to Ecosystem Diversity

- Habitat destruction and fragmentation
- Invasive species altering ecosystem structure
- Climate change shifting ecosystem boundaries
- Pollution degrading ecosystem health
- Overexploitation of ecosystem resources

## Important Facts

- India has **10 biogeographic zones** and **4 biodiversity hotspots**.
- **85+ Ramsar wetland sites** — ecosystem diversity in aquatic habitats.
- **104 National Parks + 566 Wildlife Sanctuaries** — ecosystem protection.
- **18 Biosphere Reserves** (14 UNESCO recognised) — ecosystem-level conservation.
- **Wetlands cover ~4.7%** of India's geographical area.
- **Forest cover ~24.6%** (ISFR 2023) — terrestrial ecosystem diversity.
- **UNESCO MAB Programme** — biosphere reserves for ecosystem diversity.

# Global Biodiversity

## Definition

**Global biodiversity** refers to the **total variety of life on Earth** across all ecosystems, encompassing an estimated **8.7 million species** (±1.3 million) with only about **1.9 million described and catalogued** so far.

It includes biodiversity across all biomes, latitudes, and habitat types worldwide.

## Explanation

### Global Species Estimates

| Group | Estimated Species | Described (%) |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Animals** | ~77 lakh | ~12 lakh (16%) |
| **Plants** | ~3 lakh | ~2.95 lakh (98%) |
| **Fungi** | ~6.1 lakh | ~61,000 (10%) |
| **Protists** | ~36,400 | ~8,400 (23%) |
| **Bacteria** | ~1 crore | ~10,000 (<0.1%) |
| **Total** | ~87 lakh | ~19 lakh (22%) |

# Importance (Values) of Biodiversity

## Definition

**Importance/Values of biodiversity** refers to the **multidimensional benefits** that biodiversity provides to humans and the planet — categorised as **direct use values, indirect use values, ethical/aesthetic values, and option values**.

The **Millennium Ecosystem Assessment (2005)** categorised these as **provisioning, regulating, cultural, and supporting ecosystem services** derived from biodiversity.

## Explanation

### Classification of Biodiversity Values

| Category | Type | Examples |
|----------|------|----------|
| **Direct Use Values** | Consumptive + Productive | Food, medicine, timber, fuel, fibre, fish |
| **Indirect Use Values** | Ecological services | Pollination, climate regulation, water purification, soil formation |
| **Option Values** | Future potential | Undiscovered medicines, future crop genes |
| **Existence/Intrinsic Values** | Ethical, aesthetic, spiritual | Right of species to exist; beauty of nature; sacred groves |
| **Bequest Values** | For future generations | Preserving biodiversity for children |

### Direct Use Values (Provisioning Services)

| Value | Examples from Indian Biodiversity |
|-------|----------------------------------|
| **Food** | 50,000+ rice varieties; millets; fish; honey |
| **Medicine** | Ayurveda — 2,000+ medicinal plants; Neem, Tulsi, Ashwagandha |
| **Timber & fuel** | Teak, sal, bamboo for construction and energy |
| **Fibre** | Cotton, jute, silk (sericulture) |
| **Genetic resources** | Wild rice for crop improvement; wild relatives of crops |
| **Tourism** | Wildlife tourism — Ranthambore, Kaziranga, Periyar |

### Indirect Use Values (Regulating & Supporting Services)

| Service | Biodiversity Role |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Climate regulation** | Forests sequester carbon; wetlands store blue carbon |
| **Pollination** | 75% of food crops depend on animal pollinators |
| **Water purification** | Wetlands filter pollutants |
| **Soil formation** | Decomposers, root systems build soil |
| **Flood control** | Mangroves, forests reduce flood intensity |
| **Pest control** | Predators, parasitoids control agricultural pests |
| **Disease regulation** | Biodiverse ecosystems limit disease spread |
| **Oxygen production** | Phytoplankton produce 50% of Earth's O₂ |

### Option Values

- **Bioprospecting** — searching for commercially valuable genetic and biochemical resources.
- **Paclitaxel (Taxol)** — anti-cancer drug from Pacific yew tree.
- **Quinine** — from Cinchona bark — malaria treatment.
- **Rauwolfia** — source of reserpine (blood pressure medicine).
- **Undiscovered species** may hold cures for future diseases.

### Ethical, Aesthetic & Cultural Values

| Value | Indian Context |
|-------|---------------|
| **Sacred groves** | Community-protected forest patches (Meghalaya, Kerala) |
| **Totem species** | Tribal communities revere certain species |
| **Festival-linked species** | Neelakurinji blooms every 12 years |
| **National symbols** | Tiger, Peacock, Lotus, Banyan tree |
| **Eco-tourism** | Economic value from wildlife appreciation |

### Economic Valuation

- **Costanza et al. (1997):** Global ecosystem services worth **$33 trillion/year**.
- **Pollination services:** $200–500 billion/year globally.
- **Indian biodiversity economy:** Fisheries, forestry, agriculture, tourism, pharmaceuticals.

## Important Facts

- **75% of food crops** depend on animal pollination (FAO).
- **80% of world population** depends on plant-based traditional medicine (WHO).
- **25% of prescription drugs** derived from plants.
- **Biodiversity worth $33 trillion/year** in ecosystem services (Costanza, 1997).
- **Rivet Popper Hypothesis** — species are rivets in an aeroplane; losing too many causes crash.
- **India's TKDL** documents 3.6 lakh traditional medicine formulations.
- **Bequest value** — Brundtland's intergenerational equity principle.

# Biodiversity Assessment

## Definition

**Biodiversity assessment** is the **systematic process of identifying, cataloguing, and evaluating** the biodiversity of a region — including species inventory, population monitoring, threat assessment, and diversity index calculation — to inform conservation planning and policy.

## Explanation

### Methods of Biodiversity Assessment

| Method | Description | Application |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Species inventory/survey** | Documenting all species in an area | ZSI, BSI surveys |
| **Remote sensing + GIS** | Satellite-based habitat mapping | Forest cover, wetland mapping |
| **DNA barcoding** | Genetic identification of species | COI gene for animals; rbcL for plants |
| **eDNA (Environmental DNA)** | Detect species from DNA in water/soil | Ganges dolphin detection |
| **Camera trapping** | Photograph elusive species | Tiger census (All India Tiger Estimation) |
| **Line transect** | Count species along a defined path | Bird surveys, herbivore census |
| **Quadrat sampling** | Sample vegetation in defined plots | Plant diversity assessment |
| **Mark-recapture** | Capture, mark, release, recapture | Population size estimation |
| **Diversity indices** | Shannon-Wiener, Simpson's, Margalef's | Quantitative diversity measurement |

### Diversity Indices

| Index | Formula Concept | Higher Value Means |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Shannon-Wiener (H')** | Accounts for richness + evenness | More diverse community |
| **Simpson's (D)** | Probability two individuals are same species | Lower D = more diverse |
| **Margalef's** | Richness relative to sample size | More species per sample |
| **Species richness (S)** | Simple count of species | More species |

### Threat Assessment Tools

| Tool | Organisation | Purpose |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **IUCN Red List** | IUCN | Global species threat categories |
| **Red Data Book** | ZSI/BSI (India) | Indian threatened species |
| **CAMP** | CBSG | Conservation Assessment and Management Plan |
| **Population Viability Analysis (PVA)** | — | Extinction probability modelling |

### India's Biodiversity Assessment Framework

| Programme | Agency | Focus |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **All India Tiger Estimation** | NTCA + WII | Tiger population (every 4 years) |
| **All India Elephant Estimation** | MoEFCC | Elephant census |
| **India State of Forest Report** | FSI | Forest biodiversity (biennial) |
| **National Wetland Inventory** | MoEFCC | Wetland biodiversity mapping |
| **State of India's Birds** | Multiple agencies | Bird population trends |
| **NBA Biodiversity Assessment** | NBA | Comprehensive national assessment |

### IUCN Red List Categories (Assessment Result)

```
EX (Extinct) → EW (Extinct in Wild) → CR (Critically Endangered) →
EN (Endangered) → VU (Vulnerable) → NT (Near Threatened) →
LC (Least Concern) → DD (Data Deficient)
```

## Important Facts

- **All India Tiger Estimation 2022:** 3,167 tigers (up from 2,967 in 2018).
- **IUCN Red List** — global standard since 1964.
- **ZSI** publishes **Red Data Book on Indian Animals**.
- **BSI** publishes **Red Data Book on Indian Plants**.
- **DNA barcoding** standard: **COI gene** (Cytochrome Oxidase I) for animals.
- **Shannon-Wiener Index** — most widely used diversity index.
- **Camera trapping + AI** — modern tiger census methodology.
- **eDNA** — emerging tool for aquatic species detection.

# Biodiversity Registers

## Definition

**Biodiversity Registers** (Peoples' Biodiversity Registers — **PBRs**) are **comprehensive documentation records** of local biodiversity — including species, habitats, traditional knowledge, and conservation practices — maintained at the **village/local body level** by **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)** under the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002**.

## Explanation

### Legal Framework

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Act** | Biological Diversity Act, 2002 |
| **Section** | Section 41 — Local Biodiversity Registers |
| **Authority** | Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) |
| **Level** | Every local body (Panchayat/Municipality) |
| **Purpose** | Document local biodiversity and traditional knowledge |

### Contents of Biodiversity Registers

| Component | What Is Recorded |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Flora** | Local plant species — wild and cultivated |
| **Fauna** | Local animal species — wild and domesticated |
| **Microorganisms** | Locally significant microbes |
| **Habitats** | Local ecosystems and habitats |
| **Traditional knowledge** | Indigenous practices, medicinal uses |
| **Conservation practices** | Sacred groves, community rules |
| **Access information** | Who accesses biological resources |

### Structure of Biodiversity Governance (India)

```
National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) — Chennai
        ↓
State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) — 29 States
        ↓
Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) — Local bodies
        ↓
Peoples' Biodiversity Registers (PBRs) — Village level
```

### Role of BMCs

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Prepare PBRs** | Document local biodiversity |
| **Conservation** | Maintain and protect local biodiversity |
| **Sustainable use** | Regulate local resource use |
| **Benefit sharing** | Channel benefits from commercial use to local communities |
| **Awareness** | Educate community about biodiversity |

### Significance of PBRs

1. **Biopiracy prevention** — documented traditional knowledge protects against misappropriation.
2. **ABS implementation** — Access and Benefit Sharing at local level.
3. **Conservation planning** — local data informs state/national policy.
4. **Community empowerment** — local communities as biodiversity custodians.
5. **Legal evidence** — in disputes over traditional knowledge ownership.

## Important Facts

- **BDA 2002, Section 41** mandates biodiversity registers at local level.
- **BMCs** constituted at every Panchayat/Municipality level.
- **~2.5 lakh BMCs** to be constituted across India.
- **Kerala** — first state to prepare comprehensive PBRs.
- **Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra** — active PBR preparation.
- **PBRs** include traditional knowledge (TK) documentation.
- **TKDL (Traditional Knowledge Digital Library)** — complements PBRs at national level.
- **Benefit sharing** — commercial users pay BMCs for accessing local biodiversity.

# Biodiversity and Environment

## Definition

**Biodiversity and environment** describes the **mutual, inseparable relationship** between biological diversity and the physical environment — where biodiversity shapes environmental quality and environmental conditions determine biodiversity patterns, distribution, and survival.

They are **two sides of the same coin** — environment is the stage; biodiversity is the cast.

## Explanation

### How Environment Shapes Biodiversity

| Environmental Factor | Biodiversity Impact |
|------------------------|---------------------|
| **Climate (temperature, rainfall)** | Determines species distribution (latitudinal gradient) |
| **Altitude** | Creates biodiversity gradients (Himalayan zones) |
| **Soil type** | Edaphic endemism (laterite flora of Western Ghats) |
| **Water availability** | Aquatic vs terrestrial species distribution |
| **Disturbance regimes** | Fire, flood create mosaic habitats |
| **Latitude** | Higher diversity in tropics |
| **Area (species-area law)** | Larger areas support more species |
| **Isolation** | Islands develop unique endemic biodiversity |

### How Biodiversity Shapes Environment

| Biodiversity Role | Environmental Impact |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Oxygen production** | Phytoplankton + forests produce O₂ |
| **Carbon sequestration** | Forests, wetlands, oceans store carbon |
| **Water cycle regulation** | Forests increase rainfall, reduce runoff |
| **Soil formation** | Root systems, decomposers build soil |
| **Pollination** | Maintains plant reproduction → forest regeneration |
| **Pest control** | Predators control herbivore populations |
| **Nutrient cycling** | Decomposers recycle nutrients |
| **Climate moderation** | Forests moderate local microclimate |

### Biodiversity-Environment Feedback Loops

```
Healthy Environment → High Biodiversity → Better Ecosystem Services → Healthier Environment
        ↑                                                                    ↓
        └──────────── Positive Feedback Loop ────────────────────────────────┘

Environmental Degradation → Biodiversity Loss → Reduced Services → Further Degradation
        ↑                                                                    ↓
        └──────────── Negative Feedback Loop (Vicious Cycle) ────────────────┘
```

### Biodiversity as Environmental Health Indicator

| Indicator Species | Environmental Condition |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Lichens** | Air quality (sensitive to SO₂) |
| **Frogs/Amphibians** | Water quality, ecosystem health |
| **Stoneflies** | Clean, oxygen-rich water |
| **Eagles/Hawks** | Top predator — ecosystem integrity |
| **Coral reefs** | Marine water quality, temperature |

### Environmental Threats to Biodiversity

| Threat | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **Climate change** | Range shifts, phenological mismatch, extinction |
| **Pollution** | Biomagnification, habitat degradation |
| **Deforestation** | Habitat loss — #1 driver of biodiversity loss |
| **Urbanisation** | Habitat fragmentation, heat island effect |
| **Invasive species** | Niche displacement, predation on natives |
| **Overexploitation** | Population decline, extinction |

## Important Facts

- **Habitat loss** is the **#1 driver** of biodiversity loss globally (IPBES, 2019).
- **Climate change** is the **fastest-growing threat** to biodiversity.
- **Biodiversity loss and climate change** are twin crises (IPCC + IPBES).
- **Forests** harbour 80% of terrestrial biodiversity.
- **Coral reefs** support 25% of marine species on 0.1% of ocean area.
- **India: 75% of forest cover in 12% of land** — biodiversity-environment concentration.
- **Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)** must consider biodiversity impacts.

# Biodiversity Loss

## Definition

**Biodiversity loss** (biodiversity erosion/depletion) is the **reduction or disappearance of biological diversity** at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels due to natural processes or, predominantly, **human activities** — leading to species extinction, population decline, habitat destruction, and ecosystem degradation.

**IPBES (2019):** *"1 million species already threatened with extinction; nature is declining globally at rates unprecedented in human history."*

## Explanation

### Five Direct Drivers of Biodiversity Loss (IPBES, 2019)

| Driver | Rank | Mechanism | Example |
|--------|------|-----------|---------|
| **Land/Sea Use Change** | #1 | Habitat destruction, fragmentation | Deforestation, urbanisation, agriculture expansion |
| **Direct Exploitation** | #2 | Overhunting, overfishing, logging | Overfishing in oceans; illegal wildlife trade |
| **Climate Change** | #3 (fastest growing) | Range shifts, extreme events | Coral bleaching; species migration |
| **Pollution** | #4 | Chemical, plastic, nutrient pollution | DDT biomagnification; eutrophication |
| **Invasive Alien Species** | #5 | Niche displacement, predation | Lantana in Indian forests; water hyacinth |

### Types of Biodiversity Loss

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Genetic erosion** | Loss of genetic diversity within species | Inbreeding in Asiatic Lion (Gir) |
| **Species extinction** | Complete loss of a species | Passenger Pigeon (1914); Dodo (1681) |
| **Population decline** | Reduced numbers without extinction | Great Indian Bustard (<150) |
| **Ecosystem loss** | Entire ecosystem type destroyed | 50% wetlands lost in 20th century |
| **Functional extinction** | Species too rare to perform ecological role | Vultures in India (diclofenac) |

### Extinction Rates

| Period | Background Rate | Current Rate |
|--------|----------------|--------------|
| **Natural background** | 1–5 species/million species/year | — |
| **Current (Anthropocene)** | — | 100–1,000× background rate |
| **Future projection** | — | Up to 30–50% species loss by 2050 |

### Causes in Indian Context

| Cause | Indian Impact |
|-------|--------------|
| **Deforestation** | Forest cover declining in NE; mining in Western Ghats |
| **Urbanisation** | Habitat fragmentation in Delhi NCR, Bengaluru |
| **Agricultural expansion** | Green Revolution monoculture; wetland drainage |
| **Pollution** | Ganga pollution; Delhi air affecting biodiversity |
| **Invasive species** | Lantana, Parthenium, Prosopis juliflora |
| **Climate change** | Coral bleaching; Himalayan species range shifts |
| **Illegal wildlife trade** | Tiger poaching; pangolin trafficking |
| **Infrastructure projects** | Highway through forests; dam submergence |

### Consequences of Biodiversity Loss

| Consequence | Impact |
|-------------|--------|
| **Ecosystem service decline** | Reduced pollination, water purification, climate regulation |
| **Food security threat** | Loss of crop wild relatives and genetic diversity |
| **Disease emergence** | Biodiversity loss → increased zoonotic diseases |
| **Economic loss** | Fisheries collapse; tourism decline |
| **Cultural loss** | Sacred species/groves disappear |
| **Trophic cascade** | Top predator loss → ecosystem collapse |
| **Climate acceleration** | Less carbon sequestration → more warming |

## Important Facts

- **1 million species** threatened with extinction (IPBES, 2019).
- **68% wildlife population decline** since 1970 (WWF Living Planet Report).
- **Extinction rate** 100–1,000× natural background rate.
- **Habitat loss** = #1 driver globally and in India.
- **50% of wetlands** destroyed in 20th century.
- **60% of ecosystem services** degrading (MEA, 2005).
- **Great Indian Bustard** — <150 individuals remaining.
- **Vulture population** declined 99% in India (1990s–2000s).
- **Rivet Popper Hypothesis** — losing species like removing rivets from an aeroplane.

# Biodiversity Conservation

## Definition

**Biodiversity conservation** is the **protection, management, and sustainable use** of biodiversity at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels to prevent extinction, maintain ecosystem services, and ensure intergenerational equity.

**Two approaches:**
| Approach | Definition | Examples |
|----------|------------|----------|
| **In-situ conservation** | Conservation in natural habitat | National Parks, Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves |
| **Ex-situ conservation** | Conservation outside natural habitat | Zoos, gene banks, botanical gardens, seed banks |

## Explanation

### In-situ Conservation Methods

| Method | Description | India Examples |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| **National Parks** | Highest protection; no human activity | Kaziranga, Jim Corbett, Sundarbans |
| **Wildlife Sanctuaries** | Protection; limited human activity allowed | Bharatpur, Periyar |
| **Biosphere Reserves** | UNESCO MAB; core-buffer-transition zones | Nilgiri, Sundarbans, Nanda Devi |
| **Conservation Reserves** | Buffer zones adjacent to PAs | Tirupati Elephant CR |
| **Community Reserves** | Community-managed conservation | Khonoma (Nagaland) |
| **Sacred groves** | Traditional community conservation | Meghalaya, Kerala |
| **Tiger Reserves** | Project Tiger network | 54 Tiger Reserves |
| **Ramsar Sites** | Wetland conservation | 85+ sites |
| **Eco-Sensitive Zones** | Buffer zones around PAs | Western Ghats ESZ |

### Ex-situ Conservation Methods

| Method | Description | India Examples |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| **Zoological parks** | Captive animal breeding | Delhi Zoo, Mysore Zoo |
| **Botanical gardens** | Living plant collections | Indian Botanic Garden, Kolkata |
| **Gene/Seed banks** | Genetic material storage | NBPGR, New Delhi (4.5 lakh accessions) |
| **Cryopreservation** | Freeze storage at −196°C | NBAGR, Karnal |
| **Tissue culture** | Laboratory plant propagation | Various ICAR institutes |
| **Captive breeding** | Managed reproduction | Crocodile Bank, Tamil Nadu |
| **Reintroduction** | Release captive-bred into wild | Cheetah at Kuno (2022) |

### Legal Framework for Conservation (India)

| Law | Conservation Role |
|-----|------------------|
| **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** | Protected areas; Schedules I–VI |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** | Forest ecosystem conservation |
| **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** | Comprehensive biodiversity governance |
| **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** | EIA; pollution control |
| **Forest Rights Act, 2006** | Community forest conservation rights |
| **CITES** | International trade regulation |

### International Conservation Framework

| Convention/Programme | Focus |
|---------------------|-------|
| **CBD (1992)** | Global biodiversity conservation treaty |
| **CITES (1973)** | Regulate endangered species trade |
| **CMS (1979)** | Migratory species conservation |
| **Ramsar (1971)** | Wetland conservation |
| **World Heritage Convention (1972)** | Natural heritage sites |
| **Kunming-Montreal GBF (2022)** | 30×30 target — protect 30% by 2030 |

### Key Conservation Strategies

1. **Protected Area Network** — cornerstore of in-situ conservation.
2. **Wildlife Corridors** — connect fragmented habitats (Kanha-Pench corridor).
3. **Community-based conservation** — JFM, FRA, sacred groves.
4. **Species Recovery Programmes** — Project Tiger, Project Elephant, Vulture Conservation.
5. **Restoration ecology** — UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration (2021–2030).
6. **Payment for Ecosystem Services (PES)** — economic incentive for conservation.

## Important Facts

- **India: 104 National Parks, 566 Sanctuaries, 18 Biosphere Reserves.**
- **54 Tiger Reserves** — 70% of world's tigers in India.
- **Project Tiger (1973)** — most successful carnivore conservation programme.
- **85+ Ramsar sites** — highest in Asia.
- **NBPGR** — 8th largest gene bank globally.
- **In-situ preferred over ex-situ** — maintains natural evolutionary processes.
- **30×30 target** — protect 30% land and water by 2030 (Kunming-Montreal GBF).
- **India committed** to restoring 26 million hectares by 2030.

# Biodiversity Hotspots

## Definition

**Biodiversity hotspots** are **biogeographic regions** with **exceptionally high species richness and endemism** that are also **severely threatened** by habitat destruction — requiring **priority conservation attention**.

**Criteria (Conservation International / Myers, 1988):**
1. **>1,500 endemic vascular plant species** (species found nowhere else).
2. **>70% of original natural vegetation lost.**

## Explanation

### 36 Global Biodiversity Hotspots

| # | Hotspot | Region |
|---|---------|--------|
| 1 | Tropical Andes | South America |
| 2 | Mesoamerica | Central America |
| 3 | Caribbean | Caribbean islands |
| 4 | Atlantic Forest | Brazil |
| 5 | **Himalaya** | **India, Nepal, Bhutan** |
| 6 | **Indo-Burma** | **NE India, Myanmar, Thailand** |
| 7 | Sundaland | Malaysia, Indonesia, Andaman |
| 8 | Philippines | Philippines |
| 9 | Wallacea | Indonesia |
| 10 | **Western Ghats & Sri Lanka** | **India, Sri Lanka** |
| 11 | Eastern Arc & Coastal Forests | Tanzania, Kenya |
| 12 | Cape Floristic Region | South Africa |
| ... | *(24 more)* | Various regions globally |

*Cover only **2.4% of Earth's land** but contain **>50% of plant species** and **>40% of vertebrate species** as endemics.*

### 4 Hotspots in India

| Hotspot | Region | Key Endemics | Threats |
|---------|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Himalaya** | J&K to Arunachal Pradesh | Snow leopard, musk deer, rhododendrons | Climate change, infrastructure, tourism |
| **Indo-Burma** | NE India, Myanmar border | Hoolock gibbon, clouded leopard, orchids | Jhum cultivation, insurgency, dams |
| **Western Ghats** | Kerala to Gujarat | Lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri tahr, purple frog | Mining, highways, plantations |
| **Sundaland** | Andaman & Nicobar Islands | Narcondam hornbill, endemic birds | Tourism, tsunami recovery, development |

### Western Ghats — India's Richest Hotspot

| Feature | Data |
|---------|------|
| **Length** | ~1,600 km |
| **Area** | ~1,60,000 sq km |
| **Endemic plants** | 5,000+ flowering plant species |
| **Endemic amphibians** | 80%+ of India's amphibians |
| **UNESCO WHS** | Designated 2012 |
| **Key species** | Lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri tahr, Malabar civet |
| **Threats** | Mining, encroachment, climate change |

### Hotspot Conservation Strategies

1. **Protected Area expansion** within hotspots.
2. **Eco-Sensitive Zone** notification (Western Ghats ESZ debate).
3. **Community participation** — sacred groves, BMCs.
4. **Sustainable development** — eco-tourism, organic farming.
5. **Corridor connectivity** — link fragmented habitats.
6. **International funding** — GEF, Critical Ecosystem Partnership Fund (CEPF).

### India in Global Hotspot Context

- **4 of 36 hotspots** — among highest for any country.
- **2.4% of world land** in Indian hotspot areas.
- **8% of global biodiversity** in India.
- **Gadgil Committee (2011)** — entire Western Ghats as ESZ.
- **Kasturirangan Committee (2013)** — reduced ESZ to 37% of Western Ghats.

## Important Facts

- **Norman Myers (1988)** — identified original 10 hotspots; now 36.
- **36 hotspots** cover 2.4% land, 50% plant species, 40% vertebrate endemics.
- **>70% habitat destroyed** in most hotspots.
- **India has 4 hotspots** — Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats, Sundaland.
- **Western Ghats** — richest hotspot in India; UNESCO WHS.
- **Sundaland** includes Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
- **Hottest hotspot** — Indo-Burma (most species under threat).
- **CEPF** — Critical Ecosystem Partnership Fund for hotspot conservation.

# Endemic Species

## Definition

An **endemic species** is a species that is **native to and restricted to** a particular geographic area — found **nowhere else** in the world. The geographic area is its **endemic range**.

**Types:**
| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Palaeoendemic** | Ancient endemic; once widespread, now restricted | Tuatara (New Zealand) |
| **Neoendemic** | Recently evolved endemic | New species on volcanic islands |
| **Local endemic** | Restricted to very small area | Lion-tailed macaque (Western Ghats) |

## Explanation

### Endemism in India

| Region | Endemic Species Examples |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Western Ghats** | Lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri tahr, purple frog, Malabar civet |
| **Andaman & Nicobar** | Narcondam hornbill, Andaman wild pig |
| **Northeast India** | Hoolock gibbon, Bugun liocichla, pitcher plants |
| **Himalayas** | Kashmir stag (Hangul), Western Tragopan |
| **Lakshadweep** | Endemic reef fish species |

### Causes of Endemism

| Factor | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **Geographic isolation** | Islands, mountain ranges isolate populations |
| **Climate specificity** | Unique local climate supports unique species |
| **Evolutionary history** | Long undisturbed evolution in stable habitats |
| **Dispersal barriers** | Oceans, mountains prevent species migration |

### Endemic vs Indigenous vs Native vs Exotic

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Endemic** | Found ONLY in one geographic area |
| **Indigenous/Native** | Naturally occurring in an area (may also occur elsewhere) |
| **Exotic/Alien** | Introduced from outside; not naturally occurring |
| **Invasive** | Exotic species causing ecological/economic harm |

### Conservation Significance

- Endemic species are **most vulnerable to extinction** — entire global population in one area.
- **Habitat destruction** in endemic range = global extinction.
- **Hotspot endemism** is a key criterion for biodiversity hotspot designation (>1,500 endemic plants).
- **India's 4 hotspots** are defined partly by high endemism.

## Important Facts

- **Western Ghats** — 77% of India's amphibian species are endemic.
- **Andaman & Nicobar** — high marine and terrestrial endemism.
- **Lion-tailed macaque** — endemic to Western Ghats; endangered.
- **Purple frog (Nasikabatrachus)** — endemic to Western Ghats; discovered 2003.
- **8% of global biodiversity** in India with high endemism.
- **Palaeoendemism** in India — relict species from Gondwanaland (e.g., Podocarpus).

# Endangered Species

## Definition

An **endangered species** is a species that is **at serious risk of extinction** in the near future throughout all or a significant portion of its range, as assessed by the **IUCN Red List** or national legislation (WPA 1972 Schedules in India).

**IUCN category: EN (Endangered)** — very high risk of extinction in the wild.

## Explanation

### IUCN Threat Categories (Relevant to Endangered)

```
MOST THREATENED ←――――――――――――――――→ LEAST THREATENED

EX → EW → CR → EN → VU → NT → LC → DD
Extinct  Extinct  Critically  Endangered  Vulnerable  Near      Least    Data
         in Wild  Endangered                            Threatened Concern  Deficient
```

### Endangered Species in India (Key Examples)

| Species | Habitat | Threats | Schedule (WPA) |
|---------|---------|---------|----------------|
| **Tiger** | Forests nationwide | Poaching, habitat loss | Schedule I |
| **Asiatic Lion** | Gir, Gujarat | Inbreeding, disease | Schedule I |
| **Great Indian Bustard** | Grasslands (Raj, Guj, MP) | Power lines, habitat loss | Schedule I |
| **Gangetic Dolphin** | Ganga, Brahmaputra | Pollution, dams | Schedule I |
| **Hangul (Kashmir stag)** | Dachigam, Kashmir | Habitat loss, poaching | Schedule I |
| **Lion-tailed macaque** | Western Ghats | Habitat fragmentation | Schedule I |
| **Nilgiri tahr** | Western Ghats | Habitat loss | Schedule I |
| **Indian vulture** | Nationwide | Diclofenac poisoning | Schedule I |
| **Gharial** | Chambal, Girwa rivers | Sand mining, dams | Schedule I |
| **Sangai deer** | Keibul Lamjao, Manipur | Wetland degradation | Schedule I |

### Causes of Endangerment

| Cause | Examples |
|-------|---------|
| **Habitat loss** | Deforestation, wetland drainage |
| **Poaching/illegal trade** | Tiger, rhino, pangolin |
| **Pollution** | Ganga dolphin; vultures (diclofenac) |
| **Invasive species** | Competition with natives |
| **Climate change** | Coral bleaching; range shifts |
| **Human-wildlife conflict** | Retaliatory killing of elephants, leopards |
| **Small population** | Inbreeding depression (Asiatic Lion) |

### Legal Protection in India

| Schedule (WPA 1972) | Protection Level | Penalty |
|---------------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Schedule I** | Highest — endangered/threatened | Up to 7 years imprisonment |
| **Schedule II** | Part II — also high protection | Up to 7 years |
| **Schedule III** | Protected | Up to 3 years |
| **Schedule IV** | Protected | Up to 3 years |

## Important Facts

- **IUCN EN category** — population reduced ≥50% over 10 years or 3 generations.
- **India: 988 species** on IUCN Red List (as of recent assessments).
- **Great Indian Bustard** — <150 individuals; critically endangered (CR, not just EN).
- **Project Tiger (1973)** — recovered tigers from ~1,800 (1970s) to 3,167 (2022).
- **Vulture 99% decline** — functional endangerment across 3 species.
- **CITES Appendix I** — species threatened with extinction; trade prohibited.

# Extinct Species

## Definition

An **extinct species** is a species that **no longer exists anywhere on Earth** — the last individual has died. The IUCN recognises two categories:

| Category | Abbreviation | Definition |
|----------|-------------|------------|

# Invasive Alien Species

## Definition

An **invasive alien species (IAS)** is a **non-native (exotic/alien) species** whose introduction and spread **threatens biological diversity, ecosystem functioning, or human economy/health**. Not all alien species are invasive — only those causing harm.

**CBD definition:** *"Invasive alien species means an alien species whose introduction and/or spread threaten biological diversity."*

**Key distinction:**
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Alien/Exotic/Non-native** | Introduced outside natural range (may or may not be harmful) |
| **Invasive Alien** | Alien species causing ecological/economic harm |
| **Native/Indigenous** | Naturally occurring in the area |

## Explanation

### Pathways of Introduction

| Pathway | Examples |
|---------|---------|
| **Intentional introduction** | Lantana for ornamental; Eucalyptus for forestry |
| **Accidental introduction** | Rats on ships; weeds in crop seeds |
| **Aquaculture escape** | African catfish; tilapia |
| **Pet trade release** | Burmese python in Florida |
| **Ballast water** | Marine species in ship hulls |
| **Climate change** | Range expansion of warm-adapted species |

### Major Invasive Species in India

| Species | Origin | Impact |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **Lantana camara** | Central/South America | Displaces native flora; alters fire regime |
| **Parthenium hysterophorus** | Central America | Allergenic weed; displaces native plants |
| **Prosopis juliflora (Vilayati Babool)** | Central America | Invades arid ecosystems; water depletion |
| **Eichhornia (Water Hyacinth)** | Amazon basin | Chokes water bodies; blocks sunlight |
| **African Catfish (Clarias gariepinus)** | Africa | Predates native fish; aquaculture escape |
| **Amazon Sailfin Catfish** | South America | Damages aquatic ecosystems |
| **Mikania micrantha** | Central America | Smothers native vegetation in NE India |
| **Argemone mexicana** | Mexico | Invades agricultural fields |
| **Eucalyptus** | Australia | Allelopathic; water-intensive monoculture |

### Impacts of Invasive Species

| Impact Type | Mechanism |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Biodiversity loss** | Competition, predation, habitat alteration |
| **Ecosystem modification** | Changed fire regimes (Lantana); altered nutrient cycling |
| **Economic loss** | Crop damage (Parthenium); fisheries decline |
| **Human health** | Allergies (Parthenium); disease vectors |
| **Hybridisation** | Genetic pollution of native species |
| **Co-extinction** | Invasive predators cause native species extinction |

### Management Strategies

| Strategy | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Prevention** | Quarantine; border biosecurity (most cost-effective) |
| **Early detection & rapid response** | Eradicate before establishment |
| **Mechanical removal** | Manual uprooting of weeds |
| **Chemical control** | Herbicides (with caution) |
| **Biological control** | Introduce natural enemies (with risk assessment) |
| **Integrated management** | Combination of above methods |

## Important Facts

- **IPBES 2023 report** — invasive alien species cost global economy **>$423 billion/year**.
- **Invasive species** = #5 driver of biodiversity loss (IPBES, 2019).
- **Lantana** covers **~40% of India's forests** (estimated).
- **Parthenium** — "Congress grass"; causes allergic dermatitis.
- **India has no comprehensive IAS legislation** — managed under EPA 1986, BDA 2002.
- **National Biodiversity Authority** regulates introduction of alien species.
- **Ballast Water Management Convention (IMO)** — prevents marine IAS.

# Keystone Species

## Definition

A **keystone species** is a species that has a **disproportionately large effect** on its ecosystem relative to its abundance or biomass — its removal causes **dramatic changes** in ecosystem structure, function, and biodiversity, analogous to a keystone holding an arch together.

**Concept introduced by:** **Robert Paine (1969)** — sea star (*Pisaster ochraceus*) removal experiment in intertidal zone.

**Key feature:** Not necessarily the most abundant or dominant — but ecologically pivotal.

## Explanation

### Characteristics of Keystone Species

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Disproportionate impact** | Small population, large ecological effect |
| **Not dominant** | May not be most abundant species |
| **Ecosystem engineers** | Modify habitat for other species |
| **Top-down control** | Regulate lower trophic levels |
| **Removal effect** | Ecosystem collapses or restructures significantly |

### Types of Keystone Species

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Predator keystone** | Controls herbivore populations | Sea otter (controls sea urchins) |
| **Herbivore keystone** | Shapes vegetation structure | African elephant (maintains savanna) |
| **Ecosystem engineer** | Creates/modifies habitat | Beaver (builds dams, creates wetlands) |
| **Mutualist keystone** | Critical for other species survival | Fig trees (fruit for 1,200+ species) |
| **Keystone host** | Supports many parasite/species | Coral reefs (25% marine species) |

### Robert Paine's Experiment (1969)

```
Intertidal Zone WITH Sea Star (keystone predator):
  Sea Star → Mussels → Barnacles → Algae (diverse community)

Sea Star REMOVED:
  Mussels dominate → Barnacles eliminated → Low diversity
  (Trophic cascade — ecosystem simplified)
```

### Keystone Species Examples

| Species | Ecosystem | Role |
|---------|-----------|------|
| **Sea otter** | Kelp forest | Preys on sea urchins → protects kelp |
| **Wolf** | Yellowstone | Controls elk → protects riverbank vegetation |
| **Beaver** | Freshwater | Dams create wetlands → habitat for many species |
| **Tiger** | Indian forests | Apex predator — regulates prey populations |
| **Sea star (Pisaster)** | Intertidal | Paine's original keystone example |
| **Prairie dogs** | Grassland | Burrows provide homes for 150+ species |
| **Fig trees** | Tropical forest | Fruit feeds 1,200+ vertebrate species |

### Keystone vs Dominant vs Foundation Species

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Keystone** | Disproportionate effect (not necessarily abundant) | Sea star |
| **Dominant** | Most abundant/high biomass | Grass in prairie |
| **Foundation** | Creates physical habitat structure | Corals, trees |
| **Flagship** | Charismatic; used for conservation marketing | Tiger, Panda |
| **Umbrella** | Protecting it protects many other species | Tiger (large territory) |

## Important Facts

- **Sea otter** — classic keystone; protects kelp forests from urchin overgrazing.
- **Wolf reintroduction in Yellowstone (1995)** — restored keystone predator function.

- **Removing keystone species** → trophic cascade → ecosystem collapse.

# Umbrella Species

## Definition

An **umbrella species** is a species whose **conservation (through protecting its large habitat requirements) simultaneously protects** numerous other species and the entire ecosystem within that habitat — like an umbrella shielding everything beneath it.

**Concept:** Protect one species with large area needs → entire ecosystem and co-occurring species are protected.

## Explanation

### How Umbrella Species Work

```
Umbrella Species (Tiger — needs 50-100 sq km territory)
    ↓
Large Protected Area (Tiger Reserve — 500-3000 sq km)
    ↓
Habitat Protected (forest, water, grassland)
    ↓
ALL co-occurring species protected:
  Deer, birds, reptiles, amphibians, insects, plants, fungi
    ↓
ECOSYSTEM-LEVEL CONSERVATION achieved
```

### Criteria for Umbrella Species

| Criterion | Reason |
|-----------|--------|
| **Large home range/territory** | Protecting range protects large area |
| **Wide habitat requirements** | Multiple habitat types conserved |
| **High trophic level** | Top predator — needs intact food web |
| **Sensitive to habitat disturbance** | Early warning for ecosystem health |
| **Charismatic (often)** | Easier to gain public/policy support |

### Umbrella Species Examples

| Species | Territory/Range | What Gets Protected |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Tiger** | 50–100 sq km per individual | Entire forest ecosystem in Tiger Reserve |
| **Asian Elephant** | 200–600 sq km herd range | Forest corridors, grasslands, water bodies |
| **Gray Wolf** | 100+ sq km pack territory | Wilderness ecosystems in North America |
| **Northern Spotted Owl** | Old-growth forest dependent | Ancient forest ecosystems |
| **Grizzly Bear** | Large range in wilderness | Mountain, forest, river ecosystems |
| **Great Indian Bustard** | Grassland habitat | Grassland ecosystem and associated species |

### Umbrella vs Flagship vs Keystone

| Feature | Umbrella | Flagship | Keystone |
|---------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Primary purpose** | Habitat protection | Awareness/fundraising | Ecological regulation |
| **Mechanism** | Large area needs | Charisma/symbolism | Disproportionate ecological effect |
| **Conservation approach** | Landscape-level PA | Campaign/marketing | Maintain ecological role |
| **Example** | Tiger, Elephant | Panda, Tiger | Sea star, Sea otter |
| **Scale** | Landscape/ecosystem | Social/political | Community/ecosystem |

*Tiger serves as umbrella + flagship + keystone simultaneously.*

### India's Umbrella Species Conservation

| Species | Protected Areas Created | Co-beneficiaries |
|---------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Tiger** | 54 Tiger Reserves (~75,000 sq km) | All forest species in reserves |
| **Elephant** | 33 Elephant Reserves + corridors | Forest, grassland species |
| **Lion** | Gir NP and sanctuary | 300+ bird species, leopards, deer |
| **Rhino** | Kaziranga, Pobitora | Water buffalo, swamp deer, birds |

## Important Facts

# Indicator Species

## Definition

An **indicator species** (bioindicator) is a species whose **presence, absence, or abundance** reflects the **condition of the environment** or a specific environmental change — serving as an early warning system for ecosystem health, pollution, or environmental degradation.

**Principle:** Sensitive species decline or disappear when environmental conditions deteriorate — their status "indicates" environmental quality.

## Explanation

### Types of Indicator Species

| Type | What They Indicate | Examples |
|------|-------------------|----------|
| **Pollution indicators** | Air/water/soil pollution levels | Lichens (air), Stoneflies (water) |
| **Ecosystem health indicators** | Overall ecosystem integrity | Frogs/amphibians, top predators |
| **Disturbance indicators** | Habitat disturbance level | Invasive species presence |
| **Climate change indicators** | Climate shifts | Polar bears, penguins, coral reefs |
| **Water quality indicators** | Aquatic pollution | Mayflies, caddisflies, trout |

### Key Indicator Species

| Species | Indicates | Sensitivity |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Lichens** | Air quality (SO₂, NOₓ) | Die in polluted air |
| **Frogs/Amphibians** | Water quality, ecosystem health | Permeable skin absorbs toxins |
| **Stoneflies (Plecoptera)** | Clean, oxygen-rich water | Cannot tolerate pollution |
| **Trout fish** | Cold, clean, oxygenated water | Sensitive to temperature, pollution |
| **Eagles/Hawks** | Top predator ecosystem health | Biomagnification of toxins |
| **Bees** | Pollinator ecosystem health | Pesticide sensitivity |
| **Coral reefs** | Marine water quality, temperature | Bleach in warm/polluted water |
| **Reindeer/Lichens** | Arctic ecosystem health | Radioactive fallout, climate |
| **Daphnia (water flea)** | Aquatic toxicity | Standard lab bioassay organism |

### How Indicator Species Work

```
CLEAN Environment → Indicator species PRESENT (healthy ecosystem)
        ↓ Pollution/degradation
POLLUTED Environment → Indicator species ABSENT or DECLINING (warning signal)
        ↓ Further degradation
SEVERELY POLLUTED → Only pollution-tolerant species remain (e.g., Tubifex worm)
```

### Indicator Species in India

| Species | Environmental Indicator For |
|---------|---------------------------|
| **Lichens** | Air pollution (SO₂) in cities and forests |
| **Frogs (Western Ghats)** | Water pollution, ecosystem health |
| **Gangetic Dolphin** | Ganga river water quality |
| **Butterflies** | Habitat quality, climate change |
| **Vultures** | Ecosystem health (scavenger function) |
| **Mangroves** | Coastal ecosystem health |
| **Salmon/trout (Himalayan streams)** | Cold, clean mountain water |

### Bioindicator vs Biomonitor

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Bioindicator** | Species indicating environmental condition (qualitative) |
| **Biomonitor** | Species accumulating pollutants — measurable levels (quantitative) |
| **Sentinel species** | Early warning species (like canary in coal mine) |

## Important Facts

- **Lichens** — classic air pollution bioindicator; sensitive to SO₂.
- **Amphibians** — "canaries in the coal mine" for environmental health.
- **Stoneflies** — EPT index (Ephemeroptera, Plecoptera, Trichoptera) for water quality.
- **EPT index** — presence of mayflies, stoneflies, caddisflies = clean water.
- **SLIMS (Stream and Lake Integrity Index)** — uses indicator species.
- **BIVALIFE project** — mussels as water quality biomonitors in Europe.
- **Frog declines worldwide** — indicate global environmental degradation.
- **Butterfly Monitoring Scheme** — climate change indicator in UK/India.

```
INDICATOR SPECIES (Bioindicators) = Environmental health signals

PRESENCE = Healthy environment | ABSENCE/DECLINE = Degradation warning

KEY INDICATORS:
  Lichens → Air pollution (SO₂)
  Frogs/Amphibians → Water + ecosystem health
  Stoneflies/Mayflies → Clean water (EPT index)
  Gangetic Dolphin → Ganga river quality
  Coral reefs → Marine temperature/pollution
  Eagles → Biomagnification/top predator health

EPT Index: Ephemeroptera + Plecoptera + Trichoptera = Clean water
Amphibians = "Canaries in the coal mine" of environment
```
