# Acid Rain

## Definition

**Acid Rain** is **rainfall (or any form of precipitation) that has become acidic** due to the **dissolution of atmospheric pollutants** — primarily **sulphur dioxide (SO₂)** and **nitrogen oxides (NOₓ)** — forming **sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)** and **nitric acid (HNO₃)** in the atmosphere.

**Technical term:** **Acid deposition** (includes wet and dry deposition).  
**pH threshold:** Normal rain pH ~5.6; **acid rain = pH below 5.6**.

**NCERT definition:** *"Acid rain is caused by a chemical reaction that begins when compounds like sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides are released into the air."*

## Explanation

### pH Scale and Acid Rain

| pH Value | Category | Example |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **0** | Extremely acidic | Battery acid |
| **4.0–4.5** | Severe acid rain | Industrial regions (worst cases) |
| **4.5–5.0** | Moderate acid rain | Affected urban-industrial areas |
| **5.6** | Normal rain (slightly acidic) | CO₂ dissolves → carbonic acid |
| **7.0** | Neutral | Pure distilled water |
| **14** | Extremely alkaline | Drain cleaner |

**Why normal rain is pH 5.6 (not 7):** Atmospheric CO₂ dissolves → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid) → pH ~5.6.

### Types of Acid Deposition

| Type | Form | Mechanism | Examples |
|------|------|-----------|----------|
| **Wet deposition** | Rain, snow, fog, dew | Acids dissolved in precipitation | Acid rain, acid snow |
| **Dry deposition** | Gases and particles | SO₂, NOₓ settle on surfaces without rain | Dust, smoke particles |
| **Cloud/fog deposition** | Mountain fog | Highly acidic moisture on vegetation | Mountain forests |

### Chemistry of Acid Rain Formation

```
EMISSION: SO₂ (coal, oil) + NOₓ (vehicles, industry) → atmosphere
        ↓
OXIDATION:
  SO₂ + O₂ → SO₃ (catalysed by particulates)
  SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (sulphuric acid)
  NO + O₂ → NO₂
  NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ (nitric acid)
        ↓
PRECIPITATION: H₂SO₄ + HNO₃ dissolved in rain → acid rain (pH < 5.6)
        ↓
DEPOSITION: Falls on land, water, buildings, vegetation
```

### Acid Rain vs Normal Rain

| Feature | Normal Rain | Acid Rain |
|---------|------------|-----------|
| **pH** | ~5.6 | <5.6 (often 4.0–5.0) |
| **Acid forming** | CO₂ only (weak carbonic acid) | H₂SO₄ + HNO₃ (strong acids) |
| **Cause** | Natural CO₂ dissolution | SO₂ and NOₓ pollution |
| **Impact** | Minimal | Ecosystems, monuments, health |
| **Geographic spread** | Global (natural) | Downwind of industrial areas |

### Acid Rain in India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Severity** | Moderate compared to Europe/North America |
| **Hotspots** | NCR (Delhi), Singrauli (UP-MP), Korba (CG), Mumbai, Kolkata |
| **UP focus** | Singrauli coal belt, NCR industrial belt, Taj Mahal (Agra) |
| **Taj Mahal threat** | SO₂ from Mathura refinery, industries → marble corrosion |
| **pH in India** | Industrial areas: 4.5–5.2; rural: ~5.6 |
| **Vindhya-Satpura** | Sensitive ecosystems receiving acid deposition |

### Acid Rain vs Other Pollution Types

| Feature | Acid Rain | Smog | Greenhouse Effect |
|---------|----------|------|------------------|
| **Primary gases** | SO₂, NOₓ | PM, O₃, CO | CO₂, CH₄ |
| **Effect** | Acidic precipitation | Respiratory damage | Global warming |
| **Impact medium** | Water, soil, stone | Air quality | Atmosphere heat |
| **Geographic** | Downwind of sources | Urban local | Global |
| **Protocol** | Gothenburg Protocol | NCAP | Paris Agreement |

### Measuring Acid Rain

| Method | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **pH meter** | Direct measurement of rainwater acidity |
| **Acid neutralising capacity (ANC)** | Water body's ability to buffer acids |
| **Alkalinity test** | Bicarbonate/carbonate buffering capacity |
| **CPCB monitoring** | National ambient air quality includes SO₂, NOₓ |
| **IMD rain chemistry** | Rainwater pH monitoring stations |

## Important Facts

- **pH < 5.6** — definition of acid rain (normal rain = 5.6 due to CO₂).
- **H₂SO₄ + HNO₃** — primary acids in acid rain.
- **SO₂ and NOₓ** — main precursor pollutants.
- **Taj Mahal (Agra, UP)** — most famous Indian acid rain/monument damage case.
- **Singrauli (UP-MP)** — major acid rain hotspot (coal power plants).
- **"Acid rain capital"** — historically northeastern USA and Scandinavia.
- **pH scale is logarithmic** — pH 4 is 10× more acidic than pH 5.
- **Dry deposition** — can be as damaging as wet deposition.
- **India** — moderate acid rain; growing concern in industrial corridors.

# Causes

## Definition

**Causes of Acid Rain** are the **natural and anthropogenic sources** that release **acid-forming pollutants** — primarily **sulphur dioxide (SO₂)** and **nitrogen oxides (NOₓ)** — into the atmosphere, which undergo chemical transformation to form **sulphuric acid** and **nitric acid** in precipitation.

**Primary anthropogenic cause:** **Burning of fossil fuels** (coal, oil) in power plants, industries, and vehicles.

## Explanation

### Primary Pollutants Causing Acid Rain

| Pollutant | Formula | Acid Formed | Primary Sources |
|-----------|---------|------------|----------------|
| **Sulphur dioxide** | SO₂ | H₂SO₄ (sulphuric acid) | Coal combustion, oil refineries, smelting |
| **Nitrogen oxides** | NOₓ (NO, NO₂) | HNO₃ (nitric acid) | Vehicles, power plants, industries |
| **Ammonia** | NH₃ | Contributes to PM formation | Agriculture (minor indirect role) |
| **Volatile organic compounds** | VOCs | Contribute to NOₓ chemistry | Solvents, vehicles |

### Sources of SO₂ (Sulphur Dioxide)

| Source | Sector | India/UP Example |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| **Coal-fired power plants** | Energy | NTPC Singrauli, UPRVUNL plants |
| **Oil refineries** | Petroleum | Mathura Refinery (near Taj Mahal) |
| **Metal smelting** | Industry | Copper, zinc, aluminium smelters |
| **Volcanic eruptions** | Natural | Natural SO₂ source (minor globally) |
| **Biomass burning** | Agriculture | Stubble burning (UP seasonal) |
| **Diesel generators** | Backup power | Industrial estates |
| **Brick kilns** | Small industry | UP brick kiln belt (NCR, western UP) |

### Sources of NOₓ (Nitrogen Oxides)

| Source | Sector | India/UP Example |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| **Motor vehicles** | Transport | Delhi-NCR, Lucknow, Kanpur traffic |
| **Coal power plants** | Energy | High-temperature combustion |
| **Industrial boilers** | Manufacturing | Fertiliser, cement plants |
| **Lightning** | Natural | Natural NOₓ formation |
| **Aircraft** | Aviation | Airport vicinity emissions |
| **Stubble burning** | Agriculture | UP, Punjab seasonal NOₓ spike |

### Natural vs Anthropogenic Causes

| Cause | Type | Contribution | Examples |
|-------|------|-------------|---------|
| **Volcanic SO₂** | Natural | Minor globally | Volcanic eruptions |
| **Lightning NOₓ** | Natural | Minor | Thunderstorms |
| **Forest fires** | Mixed | Seasonal | Wildfires, stubble burning |
| **Coal combustion** | Anthropogenic | **Major** | Power plants (Singrauli) |
| **Vehicle exhaust** | Anthropogenic | **Major** | Urban NOₓ |
| **Industrial emissions** | Anthropogenic | **Major** | Refineries, smelters |
| **Ocean dimethyl sulphide** | Natural | Minor | Algae metabolism |

### Acid Rain Formation Pathways

```
PATHWAY 1 (SULPHUR):
S (in coal/oil) + O₂ → SO₂ → SO₃ → H₂SO₄ (sulphuric acid)

PATHWAY 2 (NITROGEN):
N₂ + O₂ (high temp combustion) → NO → NO₂ → HNO₃ (nitric acid)

PATHWAY 3 (CATALYTIC):
SO₂ + particulates (Mn, Fe, NH₃ catalyst) → accelerated oxidation → H₂SO₄
```

# Effects

## Definition

**Effects of Acid Rain** are the **harmful impacts on ecosystems, materials, human health, and infrastructure** caused by **acidic deposition** (wet and dry) lowering pH of water bodies, soil, and surfaces — leading to **aquatic life death, forest decline, soil nutrient leaching, monument corrosion, and respiratory illness**.

**Scope:** Affects **aquatic ecosystems, terrestrial vegetation, buildings/monuments, human health, and soil fertility**.

## Explanation

### Effects on Aquatic Ecosystems

| Effect | Mechanism | Threshold |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **Fish death** | Acid lowers pH below 5.0; aluminium leaches from soil → toxic to fish | pH < 5.0 lethal for most fish |
| **Aluminium toxicity** | Acid mobilises Al³⁺ from soil into water | Al³⁺ damages fish gills |
| **Phytoplankton decline** | Acid kills base of aquatic food chain | pH < 5.5 affects reproduction |
| **Amphibian decline** | Sensitive to pH changes | Frog eggs fail below pH 5.5 |
| **Lake acidification** | Buffering capacity exhausted | Granite/bedrock areas most vulnerable |
| **Bioaccumulation** | Mercury becomes methylmercury in acidic water | Enters food chain |

### Effects on Soil and Vegetation

| Effect | Mechanism | Impact |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Nutrient leaching** | H⁺ displaces Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, K⁺ from soil | Soil fertility loss |
| **Aluminium mobilisation** | Al³⁺ toxic to plant roots | Root damage, stunted growth |
| **Forest decline** | Combined soil acidification + direct leaf damage | "Waldsterben" (Germany, 1980s) |
| **Chlorosis** | Nutrient deficiency from leaching | Yellowing of leaves |
| **Reduced seed germination** | Acid soil inhibits germination | Crop yield decline |
| **Forest dieback** | High-altitude forests most vulnerable | Cloud deposition on mountaintops |

### Effects on Monuments and Buildings

| Effect | Mechanism | Indian Example |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| **Marble corrosion** | H₂SO₄ reacts with CaCO₃ → CaSO₄ (gypsum crust) | **Taj Mahal, Agra (UP)** |
| **Limestone erosion** | Acid dissolves calcium carbonate | Historical buildings |
| **Metal corrosion** | Acid accelerates rusting of iron, zinc | Bridges, railings |
| **Gypsum crust formation** | Black-yellow crust on marble surface | Taj Mahal yellowing |
| **Mortar degradation** | Acid dissolves cement/lime mortar | Old structures |
| **Paint damage** | Acid erodes protective coatings | Building exteriors |

### Taj Mahal — Acid Rain Case Study (UP)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Material** | White Makrana marble (calcium carbonate) |
| **Threat** | SO₂ from Mathura Refinery, industries → H₂SO₄ |
| **Damage** | Yellow-brown gypsum patches; loss of lustre |
| **Mechanism** | CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂ |

```

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

```

# Prevention

## Definition

**Prevention of Acid Rain** involves **reducing emissions of precursor pollutants (SO₂ and NOₓ)** at source through **technological, regulatory, economic, and international measures** — including **flue gas desulphurisation, catalytic converters, low-sulphur fuels, emission standards, and transboundary air pollution treaties**.

**Core principle:** **Source reduction** — prevent SO₂ and NOₓ from entering the atmosphere.

## Explanation

### Prevention Strategies — Overview

| Strategy | Target Pollutant | Method |
|----------|-----------------|--------|
| **Source reduction** | SO₂, NOₓ | Use cleaner fuels, reduce consumption |
| **Emission control technology** | SO₂ | FGD (scrubbers) in power plants |
| **Emission control technology** | NOₓ | SCR/SNCR, catalytic converters |
| **Fuel switching** | SO₂ | Low-sulphur coal, natural gas, renewables |
| **Energy efficiency** | Both | Less fuel burned = less emissions |
| **Regulatory standards** | Both | Emission limits, air quality standards |
| **Economic instruments** | Both | Carbon/sulphur taxes, tradable permits |
| **International cooperation** | Both | Transboundary pollution treaties |

### SO₂ Control Technologies

| Technology | Mechanism | Efficiency | India Status |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Flue Gas Desulphurisation (FGD)** | Limestone scrubbing: SO₂ + CaCO₃ → CaSO₃ | 90–95% SO₂ removal | Mandatory for coal plants |
| **Wet scrubber** | Alkaline solution absorbs SO₂ | 90%+ | Being installed in NTPC plants |
| **Dry scrubber** | Dry sorbent injection | 70–80% | Smaller plants |
| **Low-sulphur coal** | Reduce sulphur in fuel | Prevents SO₂ formation | Imported low-S coal |
| **Coal washing** | Remove sulphur before combustion | 20–30% sulphur reduction | Limited adoption |
| **Fluidised bed combustion** | Limestone in bed captures SO₂ | 80–90% | Some Indian plants |

### NOₓ Control Technologies

| Technology | Mechanism | Application |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR)** | NH₃ + NOₓ → N₂ + H₂O (with catalyst) | Power plants |
| **Selective Non-Catalytic Reduction (SNCR)** | NH₃ injected at high temperature | Industrial boilers |
| **Catalytic converters** | Converts NOₓ to N₂ in vehicle exhaust | All BS-IV/VI vehicles |
| **Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR)** | Reduces combustion temperature | Diesel vehicles |
| **Low-NOₓ burners** | Modified combustion design | Power plant boilers |

### Regulatory Measures in India

| Measure | Detail | Year |
|---------|--------|------|
| **NAAQS for SO₂** | Annual average: 50 μg/m³ (residential) | EPA 1986 |
| **NAAQS for NOₓ** | Annual average: 40 μg/m³ (residential) | EPA 1986 |
| **Taj Trapezium Zone** | Ban on polluting industries within 10,400 sq km | 1984 |
| **FGD norms for coal plants** | SO₂ emission limit: 200 mg/Nm³ | 2015/2021 |
| **BS-VI fuel standards** | Ultra-low sulphur (<10 ppm) petrol/diesel | 2020 |
| **NCAP** | 20–30% PM and precursor reduction in 122 cities | 2019 |
| **Zig-zag brick kilns** | Reduces SO₂ and PM from brick kilns | Ongoing |

### International Prevention — Gothenburg Protocol

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Full name** | Protocol to Abate Acidification, Eutrophication and Ground-level Ozone |
| **Under** | UNECE Convention on Long-Range Transboundary Air Pollution |
| **Signed** | 1999 (amended 2012) |
| **Targets** | SO₂, NOₓ, NH₃, VOCs emission reduction |
| **SO₂ reduction** | 75% below 1990 levels (Europe) |
| **India** | Not a signatory (primarily European protocol) |
| **Success** | Significant acid rain reduction in Europe since 1990s |

### India's Acid Rain Prevention Framework

```
CONSTITUTIONAL MANDATE (Art 48A, 51A(g), Art 21)
        ↓
AIR ACT 1981 + EPA 1986 (SO₂/NOₓ standards)
        ↓
SECTORAL CONTROLS:
  → Power plants: FGD mandatory
  → Vehicles: BS-VI, catalytic converters
  → Industry: Emission standards, TTZ restrictions
  → Brick kilns: Zig-zag technology conversion
        ↓
INSTITUTIONS: CPCB | SPCB | EPCA | NTPC
        ↓
INTERNATIONAL: UNEP guidelines | Bilateral cooperation
```
