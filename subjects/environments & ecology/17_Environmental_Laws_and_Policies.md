# Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

## Definition

The **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** is India's **umbrella environmental legislation** enacted under **Article 253** of the Constitution — empowering the **Central Government** to **protect and improve the quality of the environment** and prevent hazards to human beings, other living creatures, plants, and property.

**Enacted:** 19 November 1986 (after Bhopal Gas Tragedy, 2–3 December 1984).  
**Also called:** **EPA 1986** or **Environment Act**.

## Explanation

### Background and Need for EPA 1986

| Event | Impact on EPA |
|-------|--------------|
| **Stockholm Conference 1972** | India committed to environmental protection |
| **Art 48A, 51A(g) added (1976)** | Constitutional mandate for environment |
| **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984)** | Urgent need for comprehensive environmental law |
| **Existing gaps** | Water Act 1974 and Air Act 1981 were sector-specific only |
| **EPA 1986** | Umbrella law filling gaps; co-ordinating existing laws |

### Key Provisions of EPA 1986

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Section 2** | Definitions — environment, environmental pollutant, hazardous substance |
| **Section 3** | Central Government power to take measures to protect environment |
| **Section 4** | Appointment of officers for EPA enforcement |
| **Section 5** | Power to give directions (closure, regulation, standards) |
| **Section 6** | Rules for environmental protection (EIA, hazardous waste, etc.) |
| **Section 7** | Restrictions on hazardous substances |
| **Section 8** | Standards for emissions and discharges |
| **Section 9** | Handling hazardous substances |
| **Section 10** | Power of entry and inspection |
| **Section 15** | Penalties — up to 5 years imprisonment + ₹1 lakh fine |
| **Section 16** | Offences by companies — director liability |
| **Section 19** | Cognizance of offences — courts take notice on complaint |
| **Section 24** | Overriding effect over other laws (in case of inconsistency) |

### Powers of Central Government under EPA 1986

| Power | Application |
|-------|------------|
| **Set standards** | Emissions, discharges, noise, hazardous substances |
| **Regulate industrial locations** | Restrict industries in ecologically sensitive areas |
| **Notify ecologically sensitive areas** | ESZ around protected areas |
| **Issue EIA Notification** | EIA Notification 2006 under EPA |
| **Hazardous waste rules** | PWM, E-waste, BMW Rules under EPA |
| **Closure directions** | Direct closure of polluting units |
| **Environmental laboratories** | Recognise labs for testing |

### Important Rules under EPA 1986

| Rule/Notification | Year | Subject |
|-------------------|------|---------|
| **EIA Notification** | 2006 | Environmental Impact Assessment |
| **Hazardous Waste Rules** | 2016 | Hazardous waste management |
| **Plastic Waste Management Rules** | 2016 | Plastic waste and EPR |
| **E-waste Management Rules** | 2016 (amended 2022) | Electronic waste |
| **Biomedical Waste Rules** | 2016 | Hospital waste |
| **Construction & Demolition Rules** | 2016 | C&D waste |
| **Battery Waste Rules** | 2022 | Battery EPR |
| **CRZ Notification** | 2019 | Coastal Regulation Zone |
| **Fly Ash Notification** | 1999 | Fly ash utilisation |
| **Ozone Depleting Substances Rules** | 2000 | ODS phase-out |

### EPA 1986 vs Other Environmental Acts

| Feature | EPA 1986 | Water Act 1974 | Air Act 1981 |
|---------|----------|---------------|-------------|
| **Scope** | Umbrella — all environmental matters | Water pollution only | Air pollution only |
| **Authority** | Central Government | CPCB + SPCB | CPCB + SPCB |
| **Enacted** | 1986 | 1974 | 1981 |
| **Trigger** | Bhopal Gas Tragedy | Water pollution crisis | Air pollution crisis |
| **EIA** | Yes (Notification 2006) | No | No |
| **Penalty** | Up to 5 years + ₹1 lakh | Up to 6 years + ₹5,000/day | Up to 6 years + ₹5,000/day |

## Important Facts

- **Enacted 19 November 1986** — after Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984).
- **Umbrella legislation** — most comprehensive environmental law.
- **Article 253** — constitutional basis for enactment.
- **EIA Notification 2006** — issued under EPA Section 6.
- **Penalty** — up to 5 years imprisonment + ₹1 lakh fine.
- **Section 24** — EPA overrides inconsistent laws.
- **MoEFCC** — nodal ministry for EPA implementation.
- **2 December** — National Pollution Prevention Day (Bhopal anniversary).
- **Covers** — air, water, soil, noise, hazardous substances, EIA.
- **NGT** — adjudicates EPA-related disputes (since 2010).

# Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972

## Definition

The **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (WPA 1972)** is India's **primary legislation for wildlife conservation and protection** — providing for **protection of wild animals, birds, and plants** and for matters connected therewith to ensure **ecological and environmental security**.

**Landmark:** Replaced outdated wildlife protection laws; unified framework for entire India (except J&K initially).

## Explanation

### Key Provisions of WPA 1972

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Objective** | Protect wild animals, birds, and plants |
| **Hunting ban** | Hunting of scheduled species prohibited (Sec 9) |
| **Schedules I–VI** | Species categorised by protection level |
| **Protected Areas** | National Parks (Sec 35), Sanctuaries (Sec 18), BR |
| **National Board for Wildlife** | Apex advisory body (Sec 5A); PM chairs |
| **State Wildlife Board** | State-level advisory (Sec 6) |
| **Chief Wildlife Warden** | State-level enforcement authority |
| **Penalties** | Up to 7 years imprisonment + fine |
| **Trade ban** | Prohibited wildlife trade (implements CITES) |

### WPA Schedules

| Schedule | Protection Level | Species Examples | Penalty |
|----------|-----------------|------------------|---------|
| **Schedule I** | Highest | Tiger, elephant, rhino, lion, dolphin, bustard | Up to 7 years |
| **Schedule II (Part II)** | High | Deer, cobra, peafowl, crocodiles | Up to 7 years |
| **Schedule III** | Moderate | Chital, hyena, wild boar | Up to 3 years |
| **Schedule IV** | Moderate | Common crow, fox, mongoose | Up to 3 years |
| **Schedule V** | Vermin (unprotected) | Common rat, fruit bat | No protection |
| **Schedule VI** | Protected plants | Orchids, pitcher plant, red sanders | Fine + imprisonment |

### Protected Areas under WPA 1972

| Type | Section | Features |
|------|---------|----------|
| **National Park** | Sec 35 | Highest protection; no human activity in core |
| **Wildlife Sanctuary** | Sec 18 | Moderate protection; limited human activity |
| **Conservation Reserve** | Sec 36A | Added 2002; community-use buffer areas |
| **Community Reserve** | Sec 36B | Added 2002; community-managed conservation |
| **Tiger Reserve** | Sec 38V (2006 amendment) | Core-Buffer-Corridor structure |

### WPA Amendments

| Year | Key Change |
|------|-----------|
| **1982** | Protected area provisions strengthened |
| **1986** | Penalties enhanced |
| **1991** | Trade in animal articles prohibited |
| **1993** | Schedule I species protection enhanced |
| **2002** | Conservation/Community Reserves added; CZA strengthened |
| **2006** | **NTCA established**; Tiger Reserve provisions (Sec 38V); Critical Tiger Habitat |
| **2013** | Enhanced penalties; new species added to schedules |

### Institutions under WPA

| Institution | Role |
|------------|------|
| **NBWL** | Apex wildlife policy body (PM chairs) |
| **NTCA** | Tiger conservation (statutory, 2006) |
| **WCCB** | Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (2007) |
| **Chief Wildlife Warden** | State enforcement head |
| **CZA** | Central Zoo Authority |

### WPA and Uttar Pradesh

| Feature | UP Detail |
|---------|----------|
| **Tiger Reserve** | Dudhwa Tiger Reserve |
| **National Parks** | Dudhwa NP |
| **Wildlife Sanctuaries** | Hastinapur, Katarniaghat, Ranipur, Sur Sarovar |
| **State animal** | Barasingha (swamp deer) — Schedule I |
| **Ganga dolphin** | Schedule I; National Aquatic Animal |
| **Sarus crane** | State bird; Schedule I |
| **Human-wildlife conflict** | Terai region elephant, tiger conflict |

## Important Facts

- **Enacted 1972** — cornerstone of Indian wildlife conservation.
- **Hunting banned** for Schedule I–IV species.
- **6 Schedules** — I to VI categorising species/plants.
- **Schedule I** — highest protection (up to 7 years jail).
- **NBWL chaired by Prime Minister.**
- **NTCA** — statutory body (2006 amendment).
- **54 Tiger Reserves** in India (2022).
- **WCCB** — established 2007 for anti-poaching.
- **India** — only country with both wild tigers and lions.
- **CITES** — implemented through WPA provisions.

# Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980

## Definition

The **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 (FCA 1980)** is legislation enacted to **provide for the conservation of forests** and related matters — **restricting the diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes** and regulating **de-reservation of reserved forests**.

**Core principle:** **No forest land can be diverted without prior approval of the Central Government.**

## Explanation

### Key Provisions of FCA 1980

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Section 2** | Restriction on de-reservation of reserved forests |
| **Section 3** | Constitution of Advisory Committee for forest land diversion |
| **Section 3A** | Penalty for contravention — simple imprisonment up to 1 year |
| **Section 3B** | Offences by authorities — action against govt officers |
| **Section 5** | Power to make rules |

### What FCA 1980 Regulates

| Activity | Requirement |
|----------|------------|
| **De-reservation of reserved forest** | Central Government approval mandatory |
| **Diversion of forest land for non-forest use** | Central Government approval mandatory |
| **Assignment of forest land on lease** | Central Government approval mandatory |
| **Clearing naturally grown trees** | Central Government approval mandatory |
| **Afforestation compensatory** | CAMPA fund — compensatory afforestation |

### Forest Clearance Process

```
PROPOSAL FOR FOREST LAND DIVERSION
        ↓
STATE FOREST DEPARTMENT APPLICATION
        ↓
ADVISORY COMMITTEE REVIEW (FCA Sec 3)
        ↓
CENTRAL GOVERNMENT (MoEFCC) APPROVAL/REJECTION
        ↓
CONDITIONS: Compensatory afforestation | Net Present Value (NPV) payment
        ↓
CAMPA FUND: Money for afforestation on equivalent non-forest land
        ↓
MONITORING: State Forest Department compliance
```

### FCA 1980 Amendments

| Year | Key Change |
|------|-----------|
| **1988** | Strengthened penalties |
| **1992** | User agencies must pay NPV |
| **2002** | Supreme Court orders on CAMPA fund |
| **2003** | Guidelines for diversion strengthened |
| **2016** | **FCA Amendment Bill** proposed (controversial) |
| **2023** | **FCA Amendment Act 2023** — significant changes |

### FCA Amendment 2023 — Key Changes

| Change | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **New category** | Land within 100 km of border/LAC for "national security" — exempt from clearance |
| **Private forest land** | Some exemptions for private plantations |
| **Penalties enhanced** | Higher fines for violations |
| **Controversy** | Environmental groups opposed dilution of protection |

### FCA and Compensatory Afforestation

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **CAMPA Fund** | Compensatory Afforestation Fund (Act 2016) |
| **NPV** | Net Present Value of forest land paid by user agency |
| **Compensatory afforestation** | Plant equivalent forest on non-forest land |
| **Penal compensatory** | Double afforestation for violations |
| **State CAMPA** | State-level fund management |
| **Total CAMPA corpus** | ₹66,000+ crore (2022) |

### FCA and Uttar Pradesh

| Project | Forest Clearance Detail |
|---------|------------------------|
| **Terai forest diversion** | Dudhwa buffer zone projects |
| **Ganga Expressway** | Forest land diversion clearance |
| **Jewar Airport** | Limited forest land involved |
| **Coal mining (Sonbhadra)** | Singrauli forest diversion |
| **Linear projects** | Highway/railway forest clearance |
| **UP forest cover** | Only 6.15% — limited forest to divert |

## Important Facts

- **Enacted 1980** — to check rapid deforestation.
- **Central approval mandatory** — for any forest land diversion.
- **CAMPA Fund 2016** — compensatory afforestation corpus.
- **NPV payment** — required for forest land diversion.
- **Advisory Committee** — reviews forest clearance proposals.
- **FCA Amendment 2023** — controversial amendments passed.
- **India lost 1.6 million ha** forest 1981–1995 before strict enforcement.
- **Supreme Court** — strict interpretation in *T.N. Godavarman case*.
- **MoEFCC** — grants forest clearance.
- **Penalty** — up to 1 year imprisonment for violations.

# Biological Diversity Act, 2002

## Definition

The **Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (BDA 2002)** is legislation enacted to **implement India's obligations under the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)** — providing for **conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair and equitable sharing of benefits** arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge.

**Enacted:** 5 February 2003 (came into force).  
**Also called:** **Biodiversity Act 2002**.

## Explanation

### Objectives of BDA 2002

| Objective | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Conservation** | Protect biological diversity and threatened species |
| **Sustainable use** | Use biological resources without depletion |
| **Benefit sharing** | Fair and equitable sharing with local communities |
| **CBD implementation** | India's domestic law for CBD obligations |
| **Traditional knowledge protection** | Protect indigenous/traditional knowledge (TK) |
| **Access regulation** | Control access to biological resources by foreigners |

### Key Provisions of BDA 2002

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Section 3** | National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) established |
| **Section 4** | Functions of NBA |
| **Section 7** | Prohibition on transfer of biological resources without NBA approval |
| **Section 8** | State Biodiversity Boards (SBB) |
| **Section 22** | Biodiversity Management Committees (BMC) at local level |
| **Section 36** | Biodiversity Heritage Sites (BHS) |
| **Section 37** | Notification of BHS by State Government |
| **Section 48** | Penalties — up to 3 years imprisonment + ₹5 lakh fine |

### Three-Tier Institutional Structure

```
NATIONAL BIODIVERSITY AUTHORITY (NBA) — Chennai
        ↓
STATE BIODIVERSITY BOARDS (SBB) — Each State
        ↓
BIODIVERSITY MANAGEMENT COMMITTEES (BMC) — Panchayat/Municipality level
```

| Institution | Level | Key Functions |
|------------|-------|--------------|
| **NBA** | National | Approve access by foreigners; benefit sharing agreements |
| **SBB** | State | Regulate access by Indians for commercial use |
| **BMC** | Local | Prepare People's Biodiversity Registers (PBR); conserve local biodiversity |

### People's Biodiversity Registers (PBR)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|

| **Content** | Local flora, fauna, traditional knowledge, medicinal plants |
| **Purpose** | Document biodiversity at village level |

| **Legal status** | Evidence in benefit sharing disputes |

### Biological Diversity Rules, 2004

| Rule | Provision |
|------|-----------|
| **Access to biological resources** | Prior approval from NBA/SBB required |
| **Benefit sharing** | 5% of purchase price to National Biodiversity Fund |
| **Research exemption** | Academic research exempted (with conditions) |
| **Traditional knowledge** | Protected from biopiracy |

### BDA 2002 and Uttar Pradesh

| Feature | UP Detail |
|---------|----------|
| **UP State Biodiversity Board** | Established under BDA |

| **PBR** | Documenting Terai, Bundelkhand biodiversity |
| **Medicinal plants** | Himalayan foothills, Vindhyan biodiversity |
| **Sarus crane habitat** | Biodiversity documentation in wetlands |
| **Biopiracy cases** | Neem, turmeric traditional knowledge protection |

## Important Facts

- **Enacted 2002** — implements CBD (1992).
- **NBA headquartered in Chennai.**
- **Three-tier structure** — NBA, SBB, BMC.
- **PBR** — People's Biodiversity Registers at village level.
- **BHS** — Biodiversity Heritage Sites (Section 36).

- **Foreigners need NBA approval** — for accessing Indian biological resources.

- **India** — one of first countries with comprehensive biodiversity law.

# Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

## Definition

The **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974** is India's **first major environmental legislation** — providing for the **prevention and control of water pollution** and maintaining or restoring the **wholesomeness of water** in streams, wells, and other water bodies.

**Also called:** **Water Act 1974**.

## Explanation

### Background and Need

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Enactment** | 23 March 1974 |
| **Trigger** | Severe water pollution crisis in India |
| **First environmental law** | Predates Air Act (1981) and EPA (1986) |
| **CPCB established** | Under this Act (September 1974) |
| **SPCBs established** | State Pollution Control Boards under this Act |

### Key Provisions of Water Act 1974

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Section 3** | Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) established |
| **Section 4** | State Pollution Control Boards (SPCB) established |
| **Section 17** | Functions of CPCB |
| **Section 18** | Functions of SPCB |
| **Section 19** | Stream standards — SPCB sets water quality standards |
| **Section 20** | Powers of Board to take samples |
| **Section 24** | Prohibition on disposal of polluting matter into streams |
| **Section 25** | Restrictions on new outlets and discharges |
| **Section 26** | Provision regarding existing discharge outlets |
| **Section 33** | Power of Board to give directions |
| **Section 43** | Penalties — up to 6 years imprisonment + ₹5,000/day |
| **Section 46** | Cognizance of offences |

### CPCB and SPCB under Water Act

| Board | Level | Key Functions |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| **CPCB** | Central | National water quality standards; coordinate SPCBs; advise Central Govt |
| **SPCB** | State | State water pollution control; issue consent to establish/operate; enforce standards |

### Consent Mechanism under Water Act

```
INDUSTRY/MUNICIPALITY SETUP
        ↓
APPLY TO SPCB: Consent to Establish (CTE)
        ↓
SPCB INSPECTION + STANDARDS CHECK
        ↓
CONSENT TO ESTABLISH GRANTED
        ↓
INDUSTRY BUILT + TREATMENT PLANT INSTALLED
        ↓
APPLY: Consent to Operate (CTO)
        ↓
SPCB MONITORING + PERIODIC RENEWAL
        ↓
VIOLATION → CLOSURE DIRECTION + PENALTY
```

### Water Quality Standards

| Parameter | Standard (BIS/EPA) |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **pH** | 6.5–8.5 |
| **BOD** | < 30 mg/L (inland surface water) |
| **COD** | Varies by use class |
| **Total dissolved solids** | < 500 mg/L (drinking) |
| **Fecal coliform** | < 500 MPN/100ml |
| **Heavy metals** | As per IS standards |

### Water Act and Uttar Pradesh

| Issue | UP Context |
|-------|-----------|
| **UPPCB** | Uttar Pradesh Pollution Control Board (SPCB) |
| **Ganga pollution** | Kanpur tanneries — major polluters |

| **Sugar mill effluent** | Western UP sugar industry pollution |
| **Tannery chromium** | Kanpur — 400+ tanneries |
| **Ganga Action Plan** | Phase I (1986) under Water Act framework |

## Important Facts

- **Enacted 23 March 1974** — India's first environmental law.
- **CPCB established** under this Act (September 1974).
- **SPCB** — every state has its own board (UPPCB in UP).
- **Consent to Establish/Operate** — mandatory for polluting industries.
- **Penalty** — up to 6 years imprisonment + ₹5,000/day fine.
- **Section 24** — prohibits disposal of polluting matter into streams.
- **Water quality standards** — set by CPCB/SPCB.

- **UPPCB** — enforces Water Act in Uttar Pradesh.

# Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

## Definition

The **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981** is legislation providing for the **prevention, control, and abatement of air pollution** — empowering CPCB and SPCBs to monitor and regulate air quality and emissions from industries, vehicles, and other sources.

**Also called:** **Air Act 1981**.

## Explanation

### Background and Need

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Enacted** | 29 March 1981 |
| **Trigger** | Growing air pollution in Indian cities |

| **SPCB role expanded** | Air pollution control added to water mandate |

### Key Provisions of Air Act 1981

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Section 3** | Central Board (CPCB) — air pollution functions added |
| **Section 4** | State Boards (SPCB) — air pollution functions added |
| **Section 19** | Declaration of air pollution control areas |
| **Section 20** | Power to restrict use of certain industrial plants |
| **Section 21** | Restrictions on new outlets and emissions |
| **Section 22** | Existing outlets — permission required |
| **Section 31** | Power of Board to give directions |
| **Section 37** | Penalties — up to 6 years imprisonment + ₹5,000/day |
| **Section 40** | Cognizance of offences |

### Air Pollution Control Areas

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Declaration** | State Government declares areas (Section 19) |
| **Restrictions** | Industries must comply with emission standards |
| **Monitoring** | SPCB continuous air quality monitoring |
| **Closure power** | SPCB can direct closure of violating units |
| **Examples** | Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai declared |

### Emission Standards under Air Act

| Source | Standard |
|--------|----------|

| **Steel plants** | PM, SO₂ standards |

# Forest Rights Act, 2006

## Definition

The **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (FRA 2006)** recognises and vests **forest rights** in **Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers** who have been residing in forests for generations but whose rights were not recorded.

**Also called:** **Forest Rights Act 2006** or **Tribal Rights Act**.

## Explanation

### Background and Need for FRA 2006

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Colonial forest laws** | Forest dwellers treated as encroachers |
| **WPA 1972 / FCA 1980** | Further restricted forest dweller rights |
| **Displacement** | Millions displaced from forests for PAs |
| **N C Saxena Committee** | Recommended forest rights recognition |
| **FRA 2006** | Enacted 29 December 2006 |
| **Rules notified** | 1 January 2008 |

### Key Provisions of FRA 2006

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Section 3** | Forest rights recognised (individual and community) |
| **Section 4** | Vesting of forest rights in forest dwelling communities |
| **Section 5** | Duties of holders — protect wildlife, biodiversity, catchment areas |
| **Section 6** | Gram Sabha role — initiates and verifies claims |
| **Section 7** | Offences and penalties |
| **Section 14** | Act overrides inconsistent laws (except WPA for core areas) |

### Types of Forest Rights under FRA 2006

| Right Type | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Individual forest rights** | Occupation and cultivation of forest land (up to 4 ha) |
| **Community forest rights** | Grazing, fishing, access to water bodies |
| **Community forest resource rights** | Manage and protect community forest resources |
| **Habitat rights** | Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs) |
| **Development rights** | Roads, schools, health centres in forest areas |
| **Conversion rights** | Convert forest villages to revenue villages |
| **Intellectual property rights** | Traditional knowledge protection |

### FRA 2006 Institutional Framework

```

        ↓
SUB-DIVISIONAL LEVEL COMMITTEE (SDLC) — verifies
        ↓
DISTRICT LEVEL COMMITTEE (DLC) — approves
        ↓

        ↓
STATE GOVERNMENT MONITORING
```

## Important Facts

# National Forest Policy, 1952

## Definition

The **National Forest Policy, 1952** is India's **first comprehensive post-independence forest policy** — establishing the principle that **forests must be managed for national needs** including **timber production, fuelwood, and revenue generation** while ensuring **environmental stability and ecological balance**.

**Superseded by:** **National Forest Policy, 1988** (current policy).

## Explanation

### Objectives of National Forest Policy, 1952

| Objective | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **National needs** | Meet requirements of defence, communications, industry |
| **Timber production** | Maximise timber yield from forests |
| **Revenue generation** | Forests as source of state revenue |
| **Fuelwood and fodder** | Supply for rural population |
| **Environmental stability** | Secondary objective (not primary) |
| **Wildlife protection** | Limited mention |

### Key Features of 1952 Policy

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Production-oriented** | Forests primarily for timber and revenue |
| **33% forest cover** | Not mentioned (added in 1988 policy) |
| **Forest classification** | Reserved, Protected, Unclassed forests |
| **Village forests** | For local community needs |
| **Forest industries** | Promote wood-based industries |
| **Scientific management** | Working plans for sustained yield |
| **Wildlife** | Minimal emphasis |

### 1952 vs 1988 Forest Policy Comparison

| Feature | 1952 Policy | 1988 Policy |
|---------|------------|------------|
| **Primary focus** | Timber production, revenue | Environmental stability, conservation |
| **Forest cover target** | Not specified | **33% of geographical area** |
| **Hill/mountain cover** | Not specified | **66% in hills and mountains** |
| **Wildlife** | Minimal mention | **Integral part of forest ecosystem** |
| **Tribal rights** | Not addressed | **Joint Forest Management (JFM)** |
| **Afforestation** | Limited | **Massive afforestation programme** |
| **Community participation** | Not mentioned | **Central to forest management** |
| **Sustainable yield** | Mentioned | **Ecological sustainability primary** |

### Forest Classification under 1952 Policy

| Category | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Reserved Forests** | Strictly protected; state control |
| **Protected Forests** | Limited protection; some rights allowed |
| **Village Forests** | Managed for local community needs |
| **Unclassed Forests** | Not formally classified |
| **Private Forests** | Privately owned forest land |

### Evolution of Indian Forest Policy

```
COLONIAL ERA: Indian Forest Act 1927 (exploitation focus)
        ↓
1952: National Forest Policy (production + revenue focus)
        ↓
1972: WPA enacted (wildlife protection strengthened)
        ↓
1980: FCA enacted (forest land diversion restricted)
        ↓
1988: National Forest Policy (conservation + 33% target)
        ↓
2006: FRA enacted (tribal forest rights)
        ↓
2016: CAMPA Act (compensatory afforestation fund)
```

### 1952 Policy and Uttar Pradesh

| Feature | UP Context |
|---------|-----------|
| **Terai forests** | Managed for timber production under 1952 policy |
| **Sal forests** | Major timber species in UP Terai |
| **Revenue forests** | UP Forest Department revenue generation |
| **Village forests** | Limited community access |
| **Forest cover** | UP only 6.15% — far below 33% target (1988) |
| **Policy shift** | 1988 policy more relevant for UP conservation |

## Important Facts

- **Adopted 1952** — first post-independence forest policy.
- **Production-oriented** — timber and revenue primary goals.
- **Superseded by 1988 policy** — conservation-focused.
- **33% forest cover target** — NOT in 1952 (added in 1988).
- **Village forests** — concept introduced for local needs.
- **Scientific management** — working plans for sustained yield.
- **Wildlife protection** — minimal emphasis in 1952.
- **1988 policy** — current operative forest policy.
- **India forest cover** — 24.62% (ISFR 2023).
- **UP forest cover** — only 6.15% (far below national average).

# Environmental Law

## Definition

**Environmental Law** is the body of **legal rules, regulations, treaties, and customary principles** aimed at **protecting the environment, conserving natural resources, and preventing environmental degradation** — encompassing **constitutional provisions, statutory laws, judicial pronouncements, and international agreements**.

**In India:** Multi-layered framework from Constitution to international treaties.

## Explanation

### Layers of Environmental Law in India

```
INTERNATIONAL LAW (Treaties, Conventions)
        ↓
CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISIONS (Art 48A, 51A, Art 21)
        ↓
STATUTORY LAWS (EPA, Water, Air, WPA, FCA, BDA, FRA, NGT Act)
        ↓
SUBORDINATE LEGISLATION (Rules, Notifications, Standards)
        ↓
JUDICIAL PRONOUNCEMENTS (SC/HC judgments, NGT orders)
        ↓
CUSTOMARY LAW (Traditional community practices)
```

# Wildlife Protection Laws

## Definition

**Wildlife Protection Laws** in India comprise the **legal framework for conservation, protection, and management of wild animals, birds, plants, and their habitats** — primarily governed by the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** and supplemented by **international conventions, state laws, and judicial pronouncements**.

**Cornerstone:** **WPA 1972** — India's primary wildlife protection legislation.

## Explanation

### Wildlife Protection Legal Framework in India

```
WILDLIFE (PROTECTION) ACT, 1972 (Primary law)
        ↓
WPA AMENDMENTS (1982, 1986, 1991, 1993, 2002, 2006, 2013)
        ↓
INTERNATIONAL CONVENTIONS (CITES, CMS, CBD, Ramsar)
        ↓
PROJECTS (Project Tiger 1973, Project Elephant 1992, etc.)
        ↓
INSTITUTIONS (NBWL, NTCA, WCCB, CZA)
        ↓
STATE LAWS (Supplementary state wildlife laws)
        ↓
JUDICIAL PRONOUNCEMENTS (SC/NGT wildlife orders)
```

### WPA 1972 — Core Wildlife Protection Law

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Hunting ban** | Schedule I–IV species protected |
| **Schedules I–VI** | Species categorised by protection level |
| **Protected Areas** | NP, Sanctuary, BR, TR |
| **NBWL** | Apex advisory body (PM chairs) |
| **NTCA** | Tiger conservation (2006 amendment) |
| **WCCB** | Wildlife crime control (2007) |
| **Penalties** | Up to 7 years for Schedule I offences |
| **Trade ban** | Wildlife articles trade prohibited |
