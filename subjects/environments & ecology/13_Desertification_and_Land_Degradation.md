# Desertification

## Definition

**Desertification** is the **process of ecological degradation** in arid, semi-arid, and dry sub-humid areas whereby **biological productivity is lost**, leading to **land becoming desert-like** — caused by **climate variation and human activities** such as overgrazing, deforestation, and unsustainable agriculture.

**UNCCD definition:** *"Desertification is land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas resulting from various factors, including climatic variations and human activities."*

**NOT the same as:** Natural desert expansion — desertification is **degradation of productive land**, not natural deserts themselves.

## Explanation

### Types of Drylands (UNCCD Classification)

| Type | Rainfall | % of Earth's Land | India Example |
|------|----------|------------------|---------------|
| **Hyper-arid** | <100 mm/year | 4% | Thar Desert core (Rajasthan) |
| **Arid** | 100–250 mm/year | 12% | Western Rajasthan, Kutch |
| **Semi-arid** | 250–500 mm/year | 22% | Rajasthan, Gujarat, Deccan |
| **Dry sub-humid** | 500–1000 mm/year | 10% | Bundelkhand (UP-MP), Marathwada |
| **Total drylands** | — | **~48% of land area** | ~30% of India's land area |

### Causes of Desertification

| Cause | Mechanism | India/UP Example |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Overgrazing** | Removes vegetation cover; compacts soil | Rajasthan, Gujarat pastoral areas |
| **Deforestation** | Removes windbreaks and soil binding | Aravalli hills degradation |
| **Unsustainable agriculture** | Monoculture, no fallow, over-irrigation | Canal command areas |
| **Over-extraction of water** | Groundwater depletion, salinisation | Punjab, Haryana, western UP |
| **Climate change** | Reduced rainfall, increased evaporation | Bundelkhand drought cycles |
| **Population pressure** | More demand on fragile land | Dryland population growth |
| **Mining** | Strips vegetation; alters hydrology | Singrauli, Bellary |
| **Salinisation** | Irrigation without drainage | Indo-Gangetic canal areas |
| **Wind erosion** | Removes topsoil in exposed areas | Thar Desert margins |

### Process of Desertification

```
VEGETATION REMOVAL (overgrazing, deforestation, fire)
        ↓
SOIL EXPOSED TO WIND AND WATER EROSION
        ↓
TOPSOIL LOSS → REDUCED FERTILITY
        ↓
WATER RUNOFF INCREASES → LESS INFILTRATION
        ↓
GROUNDWATER DEPLETION → DROUGHT VULNERABILITY
        ↓
LAND PRODUCTIVITY COLLAPSES → DESERT-LIKE CONDITIONS
        ↓
FEEDBACK LOOP: Less vegetation → more erosion → more desertification
```

### Desertification vs Drought

| Feature | Desertification | Drought |
|---------|----------------|---------|
| **Nature** | Long-term land degradation process | Temporary rainfall deficiency |
| **Reversibility** | Difficult; takes decades to restore | Reversible with rainfall return |
| **Cause** | Human + climate combined | Primarily climatic |
| **Impact** | Permanent productivity loss | Temporary crop/water stress |
| **Geographic** | Dryland areas specifically | Can occur anywhere |
| **Linkage** | Drought accelerates desertification | Drought is a driver, not the same thing |

# Land Degradation

## Definition

**Land Degradation** is the **reduction or loss of biological and economic productivity** of land due to **natural processes or human activities** — resulting in decline of **soil fertility, vegetation cover, water resources, and ecosystem functions**.

**Broader than desertification** — land degradation occurs in **all climatic zones** (not just drylands); desertification is a **subset** of land degradation in arid/semi-arid areas.

**UNCCD/FAO definition:** *"Land degradation is the reduction or loss of the biological or economic productivity and complexity of rain-fed cropland, irrigated cropland, range, pasture, forest and woodlands."*

## Explanation

### Types of Land Degradation

| Type | Cause | Impact | India Example |
|------|-------|--------|---------------|
| **Soil erosion** | Water, wind | Topsoil loss | Bundelkhand, Western Ghats |
| **Salinisation** | Over-irrigation, poor drainage | Salt accumulation on surface | Punjab, Haryana, UP canal areas |
| **Waterlogging** | Excess irrigation, poor drainage | Anaerobic soil; crop failure | Indo-Gangetic plain |
| **Acidification** | Acid rain, chemical fertilisers | Soil pH decline | Industrial areas |
| **Compaction** | Heavy machinery, overgrazing | Reduced infiltration | Agricultural plains |
| **Nutrient depletion** | Continuous cropping without replenishment | Fertility loss | Intensive farming areas |
| **Deforestation** | Logging, encroachment | Biodiversity and soil loss | Northeast, Western Ghats |
| **Mining degradation** | Open-cast mining | Complete land destruction | Singrauli, Bellary |
| **Urbanisation** | Construction, paving | Impermeable surfaces | Metro cities |
| **Pollution degradation** | Industrial waste, chemicals | Soil toxicity | Industrial clusters |

### Causes of Land Degradation in India

| Cause | Contribution | Detail |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **Water erosion** | ~56% of degraded area | Monsoon-driven sheet and gully erosion |
| **Wind erosion** | ~28% of degraded area | Thar Desert, coastal areas |
| **Salinisation** | ~6% of degraded area | Irrigation without drainage |
| **Waterlogging** | ~4% of degraded area | Canal command areas |
| **Acidification** | ~2% of degraded area | Industrial pollution |
| **Other** | ~4% | Mining, compaction, pollution |

| Feature | Land Degradation | Desertification | Soil Erosion |

# Soil Erosion

## Definition

**Soil Erosion** is the **physical removal and transport of the upper fertile layer of soil (topsoil)** by **natural agents** (water, wind, gravity) or **human activities** (deforestation, overgrazing, improper farming) — leading to **loss of soil fertility, reduced agricultural productivity, and land degradation**.

**NCERT definition:** *"Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by water, wind or tillage. It is a natural process but can be greatly accelerated by human activities."*

**Topsoil** — the upper 15–30 cm; richest in organic matter and nutrients; takes **500–1000 years** to form 2.5 cm naturally.

## Explanation

### Types of Soil Erosion

| Type | Agent | Mechanism | Visual Feature | India Example |
|------|-------|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| **Sheet erosion** | Water | Uniform thin layer removal over large area | Barely visible; most insidious | Agricultural plains during monsoon |
| **Rill erosion** | Water | Small channels (<30 cm deep) in sloping fields | Tiny grooves after rain | Hilly farmland |
| **Gully erosion** | Water | Deep channels (>30 cm; up to 100m) | Ravines, badlands | **Chambal ravines (UP-MP)** |
| **Wind erosion** | Wind | Fine particles lifted and transported | Dunes, drifting sand | Thar Desert margins |
| **Slip/stream bank erosion** | Water | River banks collapse into stream | Riverbank retreat | Ganga, Yamuna banks (UP) |
| **Mass movement** | Gravity | Landslides, soil creep on steep slopes | Sudden slope failure | Himalayan foothills, Western Ghats |
| **Coastal erosion** | Waves | Sea cliff and beach erosion | Retreating coastline | Odisha, Kerala coasts |

### Water Erosion — Detailed

| Stage | Process | Impact | Prevention |
|-------|---------|--------|-----------|
| **Splash erosion** | Raindrop impact dislodges soil particles | First step of water erosion | Vegetation cover |
| **Sheet erosion** | Overland flow removes uniform soil layer | Most widespread; hard to detect | Contour ploughing |
| **Rill erosion** | Concentrated flow carves small channels | Visible after rain; recoverable | Terracing |
| **Gully erosion** | Channels deepen and widen | Severe; irreversible without engineering | Gully plugging, check dams |
| **Bank erosion** | River undercuts banks | Loss of fertile riverbank land | Riparian vegetation |

### Wind Erosion — Detailed

| Stage | Process | Impact | India Example |
|-------|---------|--------|---------------|
| **Suspension** | Fine particles (<0.1 mm) lifted high | Dust storms; long-distance transport | Delhi dust storms (spring) |
| **Saltation** | Medium particles (0.1–0.5 mm) bounce along surface | Abrades remaining soil | Thar Desert margins |
| **Surface creep** | Large particles (0.5–2 mm) roll along ground | Slow soil movement | Rajasthan sand dunes |
| **Dust storms** | Mass suspension event | Air pollution; soil loss | NCR, Rajasthan (May-June) |

### Causes of Soil Erosion

| Cause | Mechanism | Severity |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Deforestation** | Removes root binding and canopy protection | Very high |
| **Overgrazing** | Removes vegetation; compacts soil | High in drylands |
| **Unsustainable farming** | Monoculture, no cover crops, up/down slope ploughing | High |
| **Construction/mining** | Strips all vegetation and topsoil | Very high |
| **Forest fires** | Removes protective vegetation cover | Moderate-high |
| **Heavy rainfall** | Intense monsoon rain on exposed soil | Natural accelerator |
| **Floods** | Scours topsoil from riverbanks and plains | High in floodplains |
| **Improper irrigation** | Waterlogging then channelised overflow | Moderate |
| **Climate change** | More intense rainfall events | Increasing threat |

### Universal Soil Loss Equation (USLE)

| Factor | Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Rainfall erosivity** | R | Energy of rainfall causing erosion |
| **Soil erodibility** | K | Susceptibility of soil to erosion |
| **Slope length** | L | Length of slope — longer = more erosion |
| **Slope steepness** | S | Angle of slope — steeper = more erosion |
| **Cover management** | C | Vegetation cover reduces erosion |
| **Support practice** | P | Terracing, contouring reduces erosion |

**Formula:** A = R × K × L × S × C × P (Annual soil loss)

### Effects of Soil Erosion

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Loss of topsoil** | 1 mm topsoil loss = 10 tonnes/ha lost |
| **Reduced fertility** | N, P, K nutrients washed away |
| **Decreased water holding** | Eroded soil holds less moisture |
| **Siltation of reservoirs** | Eroded soil fills dams (e.g., Matatila, Rajghat) |
| **River pollution** | Sediment load increases turbidity |
| **Floods worsened** | Silted riverbeds reduce carrying capacity |
| **Crop yield decline** | 20–50% yield loss on eroded land |
| **Desertification link** | Erosion → degradation → desertification |
| **Carbon loss** | Topsoil carbon washed into oceans |
| **Economic cost** | Billions in lost productivity and restoration |

### Soil Conservation and Erosion Control Measures

| Method | Type | Mechanism | UP Application |
|--------|------|-----------|---------------|
| **Contour ploughing** | Agricultural | Plows along contour lines; slows water flow | Bundelkhand slopes |
| **Terracing** | Agricultural | Steps on slopes; reduces runoff speed | Hilly UP regions |
| **Strip cropping** | Agricultural | Alternate crop strips; traps sediment | Agricultural plains |
| **Shelter belts** | Wind break | Tree rows block wind erosion | Western UP, NCR |
| **Afforestation** | Vegetation | Roots bind soil; canopy reduces rain impact | Chambal ravines |
| **Gully plugging** | Engineering | Check dams in gullies; trap sediment | Chambal reclamation |
| **Contour bunding** | Engineering | Earthen banks along contours | Bundelkhand |
| **Riparian buffers** | Vegetation | Trees along riverbanks | Ganga, Yamuna banks |
| **No-till farming** | Agricultural | Crop residue left on surface | Western UP pilots |
| **Cover crops** | Agricultural | Off-season crops protect bare soil | UP farmland |
| **MGNREGA watershed** | Institutional | Community-based erosion control | Pan-UP |
| **Grass waterways** | Engineering | Grassed channels safely convey runoff | Canal command areas |

### Soil Erosion vs Land Degradation vs Desertification

| Feature | Soil Erosion | Land Degradation | Desertification |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Process** | Physical soil removal | Productivity decline (all causes) | Dryland productivity collapse |
| **Agent** | Water, wind, gravity | Multiple (erosion, salinity, pollution) | Climate + human in drylands |
| **Visibility** | Often visible (gullies, dunes) | May be invisible (nutrient loss) | Gradual landscape change |
| **India area** | 81 million ha | 96.4 million ha | 82 million ha |
| **Reversibility** | Moderate (terracing, afforestation) | Variable | Difficult |
