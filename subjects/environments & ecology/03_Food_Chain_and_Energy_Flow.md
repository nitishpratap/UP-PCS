# Food Chain

## Definition

A **food chain** is a **linear sequence** of organisms in an ecosystem in which each organism feeds on the one below it and is eaten by the one above it, showing a **unidirectional flow of energy** and nutrients from producers to various levels of consumers.

**NCERT definition:** *"A food chain is a sequence of organisms in which one organism eats another as food."*

**General form:**
```
Producer → Primary Consumer → Secondary Consumer → Tertiary Consumer → Quaternary Consumer
(Plant)      (Herbivore)         (Carnivore)          (Top Carnivore)      (Apex Predator)
```

## Explanation

### Types of Food Chains

| Type | Starting Point | Pathway | Dominant In |
|------|----------------|---------|-------------|
| **Grazing Food Chain (GFC)** | Living green plants (producers) | Plant → Herbivore → Carnivore | Terrestrial & shallow aquatic ecosystems |
| **Detritus Food Chain (DFC)** | Dead organic matter (detritus) | Detritus → Detritivore → Decomposer → Predator | Forest floor, soil, deep ocean |

### Grazing Food Chain (GFC) — Examples

| Ecosystem | Food Chain |
|-----------|------------|
| Grassland | Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk |
| Aquatic | Phytoplankton → Zooplankton → Small fish → Large fish → Shark |
| Forest | Leaves → Caterpillar → Bird → Hawk |
| Human-influenced | Crop → Human (short chain) |

### Detritus Food Chain (DFC) — Examples

| Ecosystem | Food Chain |
|-----------|------------|
| Forest floor | Dead leaves → Earthworm → Frog → Snake |
| Soil | Dead roots → Termites → Beetle → Bird |
| Ocean floor | Dead plankton → Crustaceans → Fish |

### GFC vs DFC Comparison

| Feature | Grazing Food Chain | Detritus Food Chain |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Energy source** | Living producers | Dead organic matter |
| **Dominance** | Aquatic (shallow), grasslands | Forests, deep oceans |
| **Role** | Direct energy from sun | Nutrient recycling |
| **Proportion** | ~10% of total energy flow | ~90% of total energy flow (in forests) |

### Significance of Food Chains

1. **Energy transfer** — Channels solar energy through ecosystem.
2. **Nutrient cycling** — Moves nutrients through trophic levels.
3. **Ecological balance** — Predator-prey relationships maintain population control.
4. **Biomagnification** — Toxins accumulate at higher trophic levels (DDT, mercury).
5. **Biological control** — Natural pest control through predator chains.

### Limitations of Food Chain Concept

- Too **simplified/linear** — most organisms feed at multiple levels.
- Does not capture **complex interconnections** (solved by food web concept).
- Real ecosystems have **multiple interconnected chains**.

## Important Facts

- **Grazing food chain** starts with living plants; **detritus food chain** starts with dead matter.
- In forests, **~90% of energy** flows through detritus food chain.
- **Shortest food chain** = 2 trophic levels (e.g., phytoplankton → fish).
- **Longest practical food chain** = 4–5 trophic levels (energy limitation).
- **Biomagnification** increases toxin concentration at each higher trophic level.
- **DDT** in water (0.003 ppm) → plankton (0.04 ppm) → fish (0.5 ppm) → fish-eating birds (25 ppm) — classic biomagnification example.
- **Rachel Carson's *Silent Spring* (1962)** documented food chain biomagnification of pesticides.

# Food Web

## Definition

A **food web** is a **network of interconnected food chains** in an ecosystem, showing the **complex feeding relationships** among various organisms, where most organisms consume and are consumed by multiple other organisms.

**NCERT definition:** *"A food web is a network of food chains where different food chains are interconnected."*

Unlike a food chain (linear), a food web is **multi-dimensional** and more accurately represents real ecosystem feeding relationships.

## Explanation

### Food Chain vs Food Web

| Feature | Food Chain | Food Web |
|---------|------------|----------|
| **Structure** | Linear, single pathway | Network, multiple pathways |
| **Complexity** | Simple | Complex |
| **Realism** | Oversimplified | More realistic |
| **Organism position** | One trophic level | Multiple trophic levels possible |
| **Stability** | Less stable (one link breaks → chain collapses) | More stable (alternative pathways) |

### Components of Food Web

```
                    Hawk
                   /    \
              Snake      Owl
              /    \      |
           Frog    Lizard  Mouse
            |       |      |
        Grasshopper  Insect  Seed
            \       |      /
             Grass / Plant
```

### Types Based on Ecosystem

| Ecosystem | Food Web Characteristics |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Terrestrial forest** | Complex; many herbivores, omnivores, carnivores; DFC + GFC intertwined |
| **Grassland** | Moderate complexity; grazing mammals, predators, insects |
| **Aquatic (pond)** | Phytoplankton-based; short chains, rapid turnover |
| **Ocean** | Very complex; multiple trophic pathways; gelatinous food web |
| **Desert** | Simple; few species; water-limited chains |

### Food Web Stability

- **More complex food webs** = **more stable** ecosystems.
- If one species is removed, others can compensate through **alternative food pathways**.
- **Keystone species removal** can collapse food web despite complexity.
- **Invasive species** disrupt food webs by occupying niches and preying on natives.

### Trophic Connections in Food Web

| Connection Type | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Grazing pathway** | Energy from living producers |
| **Detritus pathway** | Energy from dead organic matter |
| **Parasitic pathway** | Energy from living host (parasite food chain) |
| **Omnivory** | Organisms feeding at multiple trophic levels (humans, bears, crows) |

### Ecological Efficiency in Food Webs

- Energy lost at each transfer → limits web complexity.
- **Homage to Santa Rosalia** (Hutchinson, 1959) — why are there so many animal species? Food web complexity enables coexistence.

## Important Facts

- Most organisms occupy **multiple positions** in a food web (omnivores).
- **Food web complexity** correlates with **ecosystem stability** (MacArthur, 1955).
- **Removal of one species** rarely collapses a complex food web (unlike simple chain).
- **Trophic cascade** — removal of top predator restructures entire food web.
- **Gelatinous food web** in oceans — jellyfish-dominated alternative pathway.
- **Microbial food web** in oceans — bacteria and viruses as critical links.
- Human beings are **omnivores** occupying multiple trophic levels simultaneously.

# Trophic Levels

## Definition

**Trophic levels** are the **feeding positions** in a food chain or food web, representing the **step-wise transfer of energy** from producers at the base to various levels of consumers above.

**Etymology:** Greek *trophē* = nourishment/food.

| Trophic Level | Organisms | Feeding Type |
|---------------|-----------|--------------|
| **T1 — Producers** | Green plants, algae, phytoplankton | Autotrophs (photosynthesis) |
| **T2 — Primary Consumers** | Herbivores, zooplankton | Eat producers |
| **T3 — Secondary Consumers** | Carnivores eating herbivores | Eat primary consumers |
| **T4 — Tertiary Consumers** | Carnivores eating secondary consumers | Eat secondary consumers |
| **T5 — Quaternary Consumers** | Apex/top predators | Eat tertiary consumers |
| **Decomposers** | Bacteria, fungi | Not a trophic level per se — act at all levels |

## Explanation

### Detailed Trophic Level Classification

```
TROPHIC LEVEL 5: Quaternary Consumers (Apex Predators)
        ↑
TROPHIC LEVEL 4: Tertiary Consumers (Top Carnivores)
        ↑
TROPHIC LEVEL 3: Secondary Consumers (Carnivores)
        ↑
TROPHIC LEVEL 2: Primary Consumers (Herbivores)
        ↑
TROPHIC LEVEL 1: Producers (Autotrophs)
        ↑
    SOLAR ENERGY
```

### Examples at Each Trophic Level

| Level | Terrestrial Example | Aquatic Example |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **T1 — Producer** | Grass, tree, shrub | Phytoplankton, algae, seagrass |
| **T2 — Primary Consumer** | Deer, rabbit, caterpillar | Zooplankton, small fish (herbivorous) |
| **T3 — Secondary Consumer** | Snake, frog, spider | Carnivorous fish |
| **T4 — Tertiary Consumer** | Hawk, owl, wolf | Large predatory fish |
| **T5 — Quaternary** | Tiger, eagle (apex) | Shark, orca (apex) |

### Special Categories

| Category | Trophic Role |
|----------|-------------|
| **Omnivores** | Occupy T2, T3, T4 simultaneously (bear, human, crow) |
| **Detritivores** | Feed on detritus — bridge DFC and GFC |
| **Decomposers** | Break down dead matter at ALL levels — not assigned a single trophic level |
| **Parasites** | Form separate parasitic food chain |
| **Scavengers** | Feed on dead animals (vulture, hyena) — act between levels |

### Energy at Each Trophic Level

| Transfer | Energy Remaining |
|----------|-----------------|
| Sun → T1 (Producers) | 100% captured (only ~1% of total solar energy) |
| T1 → T2 | ~10% transferred |
| T2 → T3 | ~10% of T2 = ~1% of T1 |
| T3 → T4 | ~10% of T3 = ~0.1% of T1 |
| T4 → T5 | ~10% of T4 = ~0.01% of T1 |

### Ecological Pyramids Represent Trophic Levels

- **Pyramid of Numbers** — organisms at each level
- **Pyramid of Biomass** — total biomass at each level
- **Pyramid of Energy** — energy at each level (always upright)

## Important Facts

- **Producers (T1)** form the base — only level that captures solar energy.
- **Decomposers** are NOT counted in standard trophic level numbering.
- **Maximum 4–5 trophic levels** in most ecosystems (energy constraint).
- **Apex predators** (T4–T5) are fewest in number due to energy limitation.
- **10% Law (Lindeman, 1942)** governs energy transfer between trophic levels.
- **Biomagnification** concentrates toxins most at T4–T5.
- **Ecological efficiency** between levels: ~5–20% (average 10%).
- Humans can occupy **T2** (vegetarian — primary consumer) to **T4** (carnivore — tertiary consumer).

# Energy Flow in Ecosystem

## Definition

**Energy flow in an ecosystem** is the **unidirectional movement of energy** from the sun through producers to various consumers and finally dissipated as **heat** through respiration and metabolic activities at each trophic level. Energy is **not recycled** in ecosystems — unlike nutrients.

**Key principle:** Energy flows in; heat flows out. Ecosystems require **continuous energy input** from the sun to function.

## Explanation

### Energy Flow Model

```
SUN (solar energy)
    ↓ Photosynthesis (~1% captured)
PRODUCERS (GPP → NPP after respiration)
    ↓ ~10% transfer (Lindeman's Law)
PRIMARY CONSUMERS (herbivores)
    ↓ ~10% transfer
SECONDARY CONSUMERS (carnivores)
    ↓ ~10% transfer
TERTIARY CONSUMERS (top carnivores)
    ↓
DECOMPOSERS (at ALL levels — break down dead matter)
    ↓
HEAT (lost at EVERY trophic level through respiration)
```

### Laws Governing Energy Flow

| Law | Statement | Implication |
|-----|-----------|-------------|
| **First Law of Thermodynamics** | Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed | Total energy in ecosystem is conserved |
| **Second Law of Thermodynamics** | Energy transfer involves entropy increase; some energy always lost as heat | 90% energy lost at each trophic level |
| **Lindeman's 10% Law (1942)** | Only ~10% energy transferred between trophic levels | Limits food chain length to 4–5 levels |
| **Lotka-Volterra Principle** | Energy flow governs predator-prey population dynamics | Population oscillations |

### Energy Flow vs Nutrient Cycling

| Feature | Energy Flow | Nutrient Cycling |
|---------|-------------|------------------|
| **Direction** | Unidirectional | Cyclic/recycling |
| **Entry point** | Sun (solar radiation) | Earth/atmosphere (finite pool) |
| **Exit point** | Heat (respiration) | No exit — continuously recycled |
| **Loss** | Lost permanently as heat | Not lost — reused by organisms |
| **Role of decomposers** | Release heat through respiration | Release nutrients back to soil/water |

### Productivity and Energy Flow

| Term | Definition | Role in Energy Flow |
|------|------------|---------------------|
| **GPP** | Gross Primary Productivity — total photosynthesis | Total energy captured by producers |
| **NPP** | Net Primary Productivity = GPP − R | Energy available to consumers |
| **Secondary Productivity** | Consumer biomass production | Energy at T2 and above |
| **Standing Crop** | Biomass at a given time | Snapshot, not a rate |

**NPP is the energy base for all higher trophic levels.**

### Energy Loss Mechanisms at Each Level

| Mechanism | % Energy Lost (approx.) |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Respiration (metabolic heat)** | ~70–80% of consumed energy |
| **Excretion (undigested waste)** | ~10–15% |
| **Movement/locomotion** | ~5–10% |
| **Transferred to next level** | ~10% only |

### Single Channel Energy Flow (Odum)

- Energy enters through **one door** (sun → producers).
- Energy exits through **many doors** (respiration at every level).
- This is why energy flow is **unidirectional** and ecosystems need constant solar input.

### Ecological Efficiency Types

| Efficiency | Formula | Typical Value |
|------------|---------|---------------|
| **Assimilation Efficiency (AE)** | Energy assimilated / Energy ingested × 100 | 30–70% |
| **Net Production Efficiency (NPE)** | Production / Assimilation × 100 | 20–50% |
| **Ecological Efficiency (EE)** | AE × NPE | ~10% |
| **Trophic Level Efficiency** | Production at level n / Production at level n−1 | ~10% |

## Important Facts

- **Sun** is the ultimate source of energy for virtually all ecosystems (exception: deep-sea hydrothermal vent chemosynthetic ecosystems).
- Only **~1% of solar energy** reaching Earth is captured by photosynthesis.
- **Lindeman (1942)** first applied thermodynamics to ecology — 10% Law.
- **Eugene Odum** described energy flow as **"single channel, dissipative system."**
- **Energy pyramid** is ALWAYS upright — never inverted.
- **Deep-sea hydrothermal vent ecosystems** use **chemosynthesis** (not photosynthesis) — energy from chemical reactions (H₂S).
- **Productivity of open ocean is low** despite receiving most solar energy — nutrient limitation in photic zone.
- **Eutrophication** temporarily boosts energy flow but causes ecosystem collapse.

# Ecological Pyramid (Ecological Pyramids)

## Definition

**Ecological pyramids** are **graphical representations** of the **trophic structure** of an ecosystem, showing the relationship between different trophic levels in terms of **energy, biomass, or number of organisms**.

**Introduced by:** **Charles Elton (1927)** in his book *Animal Ecology*.

**Three types:**
| Pyramid Type | Y-axis | X-axis |
|-------------|--------|--------|
| **Pyramid of Numbers** | Number of individuals | Trophic levels |
| **Pyramid of Biomass** | Dry weight biomass (g/m²) | Trophic levels |
| **Pyramid of Energy** | Energy content (kcal/m²/yr) | Trophic levels |

## Explanation

### 1. Pyramid of Numbers

Shows the **number of individuals** at each trophic level.

| Type | Shape | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Upright** | Broad base, narrow top | Grassland: millions of grass → thousands of grasshoppers → hundreds of frogs → few hawks |
| **Inverted** | Narrow base, broad top | Tree ecosystem: 1 tree → thousands of insects → hundreds of birds |
| **Spindle/Spiracle** | Narrow at base AND top, broad in middle | Forest with single tree species |

```
UPRIGHT (Grassland):        INVERTED (Forest tree):
    |  Few hawks               | Thousands of birds
    |  Some snakes             | Hundreds of insects
    |  Many grasshoppers       | 1 tree
    |  Millions of grass       |
```

### 2. Pyramid of Biomass

Shows **total dry weight biomass** (g/m²) at each trophic level.

| Type | Shape | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Upright** | Most terrestrial ecosystems | Forest: high plant biomass → decreasing at higher levels |
| **Inverted** | Aquatic (ocean) ecosystems | Phytoplankton (low biomass, rapid turnover) → zooplankton → fish (higher biomass) |

**Why inverted in oceans?**
- Phytoplankton reproduce and are consumed **very rapidly** (short life, high turnover).
- Standing biomass of phytoplankton is **less** than zooplankton/fish at any instant.
- But **energy flow** is still from phytoplankton upward.

```
UPRIGHT (Forest):           INVERTED (Ocean):
    |  Top carnivores            |  Large fish (high biomass)
    |  Carnivores                |  Small fish
    |  Herbivores                |  Zooplankton
    |  Trees (high biomass)      |  Phytoplankton (low standing biomass)
```

### 3. Pyramid of Energy

Shows **energy content** (kcal/m²/yr or J/m²/yr) at each trophic level.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Shape** | ALWAYS upright — never inverted |
| **Reason** | Energy lost as heat at each level (2nd Law of Thermodynamics) |
| **Most accurate** | Best representation of ecosystem function |
| **Based on** | Lindeman's 10% Law |

```
ALWAYS UPRIGHT:
    |  T5: 1 kcal
    |  T4: 10 kcal
    |  T3: 100 kcal
    |  T2: 1,000 kcal
    |  T1: 10,000 kcal (Producers)
```

### Comparison of Three Pyramid Types

| Feature | Numbers | Biomass | Energy |
|---------|---------|---------|--------|
| **Can be inverted?** | Yes (tree-insect) | Yes (ocean) | Never |
| **Units** | Count of individuals | g/m² dry weight | kcal/m²/yr |
| **Accuracy** | Least accurate | Moderate | Most accurate |
| **Time factor** | No | Snapshot (standing crop) | Rate (flow per year) |
| **Proposed by** | Elton (1927) | — | Lindeman (1942) |

### Limitations of Ecological Pyramids

1. **No decomposers** represented in any pyramid.
2. **Omnivores** cannot be placed in a single trophic level.
3. **Parasitic food chains** not accommodated.
4. **Two or more food chains** merging not shown.
5. **Pyramid of numbers** misleading for irregular ecosystems.

### Ecological Pyramid and 10% Law

```
Energy at T1 (Producers):     10,000 kcal/m²/yr
Energy at T2 (Herbivores):     1,000 kcal/m²/yr  (10%)
Energy at T3 (Carnivores):       100 kcal/m²/yr  (10%)
Energy at T4 (Top carnivores):    10 kcal/m²/yr  (10%)
Energy at T5 (Apex):               1 kcal/m²/yr  (10%)
```

## Important Facts

- **Charles Elton (1927)** introduced ecological pyramids in *Animal Ecology*.
- **Pyramid of Energy** is the **most accurate** representation — always upright.
- **Pyramid of Numbers** can be **inverted** (one tree supporting thousands of insects).
- **Pyramid of Biomass** can be **inverted** in oceans (phytoplankton rapid turnover).
- **Lindeman (1942)** established energy pyramid based on 10% Law.
- **Decomposers are excluded** from all ecological pyramids.
- **Energy pyramid** accounts for **rate of energy flow** (kcal/m²/yr), not standing stock.
- **Biomass pyramid** is a **snapshot** at one point in time.
