# In-situ Conservation

## Definition

**In-situ conservation** (Latin: *in situ* = "in place") is the **conservation of biodiversity within its natural habitat** — protecting **wild species, ecosystems, and genetic diversity** in their **original environment** without removing them from their natural surroundings.

**Preferred approach:** Considered **superior to ex-situ** because it maintains **natural evolutionary processes, ecological interactions, and ecosystem functions**.

## Explanation

### In-situ vs Ex-situ — Core Comparison

| Feature | In-situ Conservation | Ex-situ Conservation |
|---------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Location** | Natural habitat | Outside natural habitat |
| **Ecosystem** | Entire ecosystem protected | Individual species only |
| **Evolution** | Natural selection continues | Artificial selection possible |
| **Cost** | Lower long-term cost | Higher maintenance cost |
| **Scale** | Landscape-level protection | Limited space capacity |
| **Examples** | NP, Sanctuary, BR, ESZ | Zoo, gene bank, botanic garden |
| **Preferred** | Yes — first priority (CBD) | Supplementary — when in-situ fails |

### Methods of In-situ Conservation

```
IN-SITU CONSERVATION METHODS
        ↓
PROTECTED AREAS:
  → National Parks (highest protection)
  → Wildlife Sanctuaries
  → Biosphere Reserves (UNESCO MAB)
  → Tiger Reserves (Project Tiger)
  → Conservation/Community Reserves
        ↓
SPECIAL DESIGNATIONS:
  → Ramsar Sites (wetlands)
  → UNESCO World Heritage Sites
  → Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZ)
  → Biodiversity Heritage Sites (BHS)
        ↓
TRADITIONAL/COMMUNITY:
  → Sacred Groves
  → Joint Forest Management (JFM)
  → Community Reserves (WPA 2006)
```

# Ex-situ Conservation

## Definition

**Ex-situ conservation** (Latin: *ex situ* = "out of place") is the **conservation of biodiversity outside its natural habitat** — protecting **threatened species, genetic material, and biological samples** in **artificial or controlled environments** such as **zoos, botanical gardens, gene banks, seed banks, and captive breeding centres**.

**Role:** **Supplementary** to in-situ — used when in-situ conservation alone is insufficient.

## Explanation

### When is Ex-situ Conservation Needed?

| Situation | Ex-situ Response |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Species critically endangered** | Captive breeding before extinction |
| **Habitat completely destroyed** | Last resort conservation |
| **Genetic diversity loss** | Gene/seed bank preservation |
| **Reintroduction programmes** | Breed in captivity → release to wild |
| **Research and education** | Zoos, botanic gardens for awareness |
| **Disease outbreak in wild** | Isolate healthy individuals |
| **Small population** | Genetic management in captivity |

### Ex-situ Conservation Methods

| Method | Description | India Example |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Zoological parks** | Captive animal breeding | Delhi Zoo, Mysore Zoo |
| **Botanical gardens** | Living plant collections | Indian Botanic Garden, Kolkata |
| **Gene banks** | Genetic material storage | NBPGR (plants), NBAGR (animals) |
| **Seed banks** | Seed storage at low temperature | NBPGR seed vault |
| **Cryopreservation** | Freeze at −196°C (liquid nitrogen) | NBAGR, Karnal |
| **Tissue culture** | Laboratory plant propagation | ICAR institutes |
| **Captive breeding centres** | Species-specific breeding | Crocodile Bank, Tamil Nadu |
| **Pollen banks** | Pollen storage for plants | NBPGR |
| **In vitro fertilisation** | Laboratory animal breeding | WII, IVF for endangered species |
| **Reintroduction** | Release captive-bred to wild | Cheetah at Kuno (2022) |

### Ex-situ Conservation Framework in India

```
EX-SITU CONSERVATION IN INDIA
        ↓
PLANT GENETIC RESOURCES:
  → NBPGR (National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources), New Delhi
  → BSI Botanic Gardens
  → ICAR gene banks
        ↓
ANIMAL GENETIC RESOURCES:
  → NBAGR (National Bureau of Animal Genetic Resources), Karnal
  → CZA-regulated zoos (150+ recognised zoos)
  → WII captive breeding programmes
        ↓
SPECIALISED CENTRES:
  → Crocodile Bank, Tamil Nadu
  → Vulture Conservation Breeding Centres
  → Lion Gene Bank, Junagadh
  → Sea Turtle breeding centres, Odisha
```

### Ex-situ vs In-situ — When to Use Which

| Criteria | In-situ (First Choice) | Ex-situ (Supplementary) |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **Species status** | Stable/populous | Critically endangered |
| **Habitat** | Intact/available | Destroyed/fragmented |
| **Goal** | Ecosystem protection | Species survival |
| **Genetic diversity** | Natural maintenance | Artificial management |
| **Cost** | Landscape-level investment | Facility maintenance |
| **Example** | Tiger in Dudhwa TR | Cheetah bred for Kuno release |

# Gene Bank

## Definition

A **Gene Bank** (also called **Genetic Resource Bank** or **Germplasm Bank**) is a **facility for the long-term storage and preservation of genetic material** (germplasm) of **plants, animals, or microorganisms** — ensuring **genetic diversity is maintained** for **future breeding, research, and conservation** purposes as part of **ex-situ conservation**.

**India's main gene banks:** **NBPGR** (plants) and **NBAGR** (animals).

## Explanation

### Types of Gene Banks in India

| Gene Bank | Full Name | Location | Focus |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------|
| **NBPGR** | National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources | New Delhi | Plant germplasm |
| **NBAGR** | National Bureau of Animal Genetic Resources | Karnal, Haryana | Animal genetic resources |
| **NBFGR** | National Bureau of Fish Genetic Resources | Lucknow, UP | Fish genetic resources |
| **MTCC** | Microbial Type Culture Collection | Chandigarh | Microbial cultures |
| **NFCGR** | National Facility for Cold Chain Gene Resources | — | Cryogenic storage |

### NBPGR — National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1976 |
| **Location** | New Delhi |
| **Parent** | ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) |
| **Accessions** | 4.5+ lakh (450,000+) plant accessions |
| **Global rank** | 8th largest gene bank in the world |
| **Storage methods** | Seed bank, field gene bank, in vitro, cryopreservation |
| **Crop coverage** | 1,500+ plant species including crop wild relatives |
| **Regional stations** | 10 regional stations across India |

### NBAGR — National Bureau of Animal Genetic Resources

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1984 |
| **Location** | Karnal, Haryana |
| **Parent** | ICAR |
| **Focus** | Domestic animal and poultry genetic diversity |
| **Breeds documented** | 200+ indigenous livestock breeds |
| **Storage** | Semen, embryo, DNA, cryopreservation at −196°C |
| **Key breeds** | Gir cow, Murrah buffalo, Garole sheep |

### Gene Bank Storage Methods

| Method | Temperature | Material Stored | Viability |
|--------|------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Seed storage (conventional)** | 5–10°C | Seeds | 10–25 years |
| **Seed storage (cryo)** | −18°C to −20°C | Seeds | 50–100 years |
| **Cryopreservation** | −196°C (liquid N₂) | Seeds, pollen, tissue, semen, embryo | 100+ years |
| **Field gene bank** | Natural conditions | Living plants (trees, perennials) | Continuous |
| **In vitro conservation** | Laboratory (20–25°C) | Plant tissue cultures | Months to years |
| **DNA bank** | −80°C | Extracted DNA | Long-term reference |

### Importance of Gene Banks

| Importance | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Genetic diversity** | Preserve diversity lost in wild/cultivated populations |
| **Crop improvement** | Source material for breeding drought/pest-resistant varieties |
| **Food security** | Insurance against crop failure, disease, climate change |
| **Ex-situ conservation** | Backup when in-situ habitat is destroyed |
| **Research** | Scientists study genetic traits for biotechnology |
| **Svalbard connection** | Global seed vault backup (Nordic Gene Bank) |
| **CBD obligation** | India committed to ex-situ genetic resource conservation |

# Seed Bank

## Definition

A **Seed Bank** (also called **Seed Vault** or **Seed Gene Bank**) is a **specialised facility for the long-term storage of seeds** at **controlled low temperatures and humidity** — preserving **plant genetic diversity** for **future use in agriculture, research, breeding, and ecosystem restoration** as a key component of **ex-situ conservation**.

**India's main seed bank:** **NBPGR, New Delhi** — world's **8th largest** seed/gene bank.

## Explanation

### How Seed Banks Work

```
SEED COLLECTION
  → From wild populations, farmers' fields, botanic gardens
        ↓
SEED PROCESSING
  → Cleaning, drying, moisture testing, viability testing
        ↓
SEED STORAGE
  → Base collection (−18°C to −20°C) — long-term (50-100 years)
  → Active collection (5–10°C) — medium-term (10-25 years)
  → Cryopreservation (−196°C) — indefinite (recalcitrant seeds)
        ↓
SEED REGENERATION
  → Periodic germination testing and field regeneration
        ↓
SEED DISTRIBUTION
  → To breeders, researchers, farmers (with NBA approval)
```

### Seed Storage Categories

| Category | Temperature | Humidity | Viability | Use |
|----------|------------|----------|-----------|-----|
| **Base collection** | −18°C to −20°C | Low (3–7%) | 50–100 years | Long-term conservation |
| **Active collection** | 5–10°C | Controlled | 10–25 years | Regular distribution |
| **Cryopreservation** | −196°C (liquid N₂) | — | Indefinite | Recalcitrant seeds |
| **Field gene bank** | Natural | Natural | Continuous | Trees, perennials |

### Orthodox vs Recalcitrant Seeds

| Type | Characteristics | Storage Method | Examples |
|------|----------------|---------------|----------|
| **Orthodox seeds** | Tolerate drying and freezing; long storage life | Conventional seed bank (−18°C) | Wheat, rice, maize, pulses, most crops |
| **Recalcitrant seeds** | Cannot tolerate drying/freezing; short viability | Cryopreservation (−196°C) or field gene bank | Mango, jackfruit, coconut, sal, oak |
| **Intermediate seeds** | Partial drying tolerance | Modified storage conditions | Coffee, papaya, citrus |

# Botanical Garden

## Definition

A **Botanical Garden** (Botanic Garden) is a **cultivated collection of living plants** maintained for **scientific research, conservation, education, and display** — serving as an important **ex-situ conservation** facility for **threatened plant species, medicinal plants, and genetic diversity** outside their natural habitat.

**India's oldest:** **Indian Botanic Garden, Kolkata (1787)** — formerly **Royal Botanic Garden, Shibpur**.

## Explanation

### Functions of Botanical Gardens

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Ex-situ conservation** | Grow and protect threatened plant species |
| **Taxonomic research** | Plant identification, classification, nomenclature |
| **Horticultural research** | Propagation, cultivation techniques |
| **Education** | Public awareness about plant diversity |
| **Medicinal plant conservation** | Preserve traditional medicinal species |
| **Seed collection** | Supply seeds to gene/seed banks |
| **Herbarium maintenance** | Dried plant specimen collections |
| **Breeding programmes** | Develop new plant varieties |

# Zoological Park

## Definition

A **Zoological Park** (Zoo) is a **facility where wild animals are kept in captivity** for **conservation breeding, research, education, recreation, and species recovery programmes** — serving as a key **ex-situ conservation** method for **endangered species** that cannot be adequately protected in their natural habitat alone.

**India regulation:** **Central Zoo Authority (CZA)** — statutory body regulating **150+ recognised zoos**.

## Explanation

### Functions of Zoological Parks

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Ex-situ conservation** | Captive breeding of endangered species |
| **Species recovery** | Breed threatened species for reintroduction |
| **Education** | Public awareness about wildlife conservation |
| **Research** | Animal behaviour, nutrition, reproduction studies |
| **Genetic management** | Maintain genetic diversity in captive populations |
| **Rescue and rehabilitation** | Care for injured/confiscated wild animals |
| **Veterinary care** | Medical treatment for captive and rescued animals |
| **Recreation** | Public engagement with wildlife (secondary role) |

### Central Zoo Authority (CZA)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1992 (statutory body) |
| **Legal basis** | Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (amendment) |
| **Parent ministry** | MoEFCC |
| **Chairperson** | Environment Minister |
| **Member Secretary** | Chief executive |
| **Recognised zoos** | 150+ zoos across India |
| **Functions** | Regulate, recognise, and monitor zoos |
| **Standards** | Minimum standards for animal housing and care |

### Zoo vs Wildlife Sanctuary — Comparison

| Feature | Zoological Park | Wildlife Sanctuary |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Conservation type** | Ex-situ | In-situ |
| **Animals** | Captive, confined | Wild, free-ranging |
| **Habitat** | Artificial enclosures | Natural ecosystem |
| **Breeding** | Managed captive breeding | Natural breeding |
| **Visitors** | Primary purpose (education) | Ecotourism (regulated) |
| **Legal basis** | CZA under WPA 1972 | WPA 1972 Sec 18 |
| **UP example** | Lucknow Zoo | Hastinapur WLS |
