# Wildlife Conservation

## Definition

**Wildlife conservation** is the **practice of protecting wild animal and plant species and their habitats** to ensure their survival, maintain ecological balance, and preserve biodiversity for present and future generations.

It encompasses **in-situ** (natural habitat) and **ex-situ** (outside habitat) conservation strategies, legal frameworks, community participation, and species-specific recovery programmes.

**NCERT definition:** *"Wildlife conservation means the protection, preservation, and management of wildlife and their habitats."*

## Explanation

### Approaches to Wildlife Conservation

| Approach | Method | Examples |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **In-situ conservation** | Protect in natural habitat | National Parks, Sanctuaries, Tiger Reserves |
| **Ex-situ conservation** | Protect outside natural habitat | Zoos, gene banks, captive breeding |
| **Species-specific programmes** | Targeted recovery | Project Tiger, Project Elephant |
| **Community-based** | Local participation | Sacred groves, JFM, BMCs |
| **Legal protection** | Legislation and enforcement | WPA 1972, CITES |
| **Habitat connectivity** | Wildlife corridors | Kanha-Pench, Kaziranga-Karbi Anglong |
| **International cooperation** | Global treaties | CITES, CMS, CBD |

### Why Wildlife Conservation Matters

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Biodiversity maintenance** | Each species plays a unique ecological role |
| **Ecosystem services** | Pollination, seed dispersal, nutrient cycling |
| **Ecological balance** | Predator-prey regulation; food web integrity |
| **Economic value** | Tourism, pharmaceuticals, agriculture |
| **Cultural/spiritual value** | Sacred animals, totem species, heritage |
| **Ethical responsibility** | Right of species to exist (intrinsic value) |
| **Climate regulation** | Forest wildlife maintains carbon sinks |

### Wildlife Conservation in India — Framework

```
CONSTITUTIONAL MANDATE (Art 48A, 51A(g))
        ↓
LEGAL FRAMEWORK (WPA 1972, EPA 1986, BDA 2002, FCA 1980)
        ↓
INSTITUTIONAL SETUP (MoEFCC, NBWL, NTCA, WII, ZSI, BSI)
        ↓
PROTECTED AREA NETWORK (104 NP, 566 Sanctuary, 54 TR, 18 BR)
        ↓
SPECIES-SPECIFIC PROJECTS (Tiger, Elephant, Lion, Rhino, etc.)
        ↓
INTERNATIONAL TREATIES (CITES, CMS, CBD, Ramsar)
```

### Major Threats to Indian Wildlife

| Threat | Impact | Example |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Habitat loss** | Species extinction | Deforestation in Western Ghats |
| **Poaching** | Population decline | Tiger, rhino, pangolin |
| **Human-wildlife conflict** | Retaliatory killing | Elephants, leopards, tigers |
| **Pollution** | Health decline | Ganga dolphin; vultures (diclofenac) |
| **Invasive species** | Native displacement | Lantana in forests |
| **Climate change** | Range shifts | Snow leopard habitat loss |
| **Infrastructure** | Habitat fragmentation | Highways through forests |

## Important Facts

- **India = 7–8% of world species** on 2.4% of land area.
- **WPA 1972** — cornerstone of Indian wildlife conservation.
- **104 NP + 566 Sanctuary + 54 TR** — protected area network.
- **70% of world's tigers** in India (3,167 in 2022).
- **Largest Asian Elephant population** (~30,000).
- **Only country** with both wild tigers and lions.
- **CITES Appendix I** — bans international trade in endangered species.
- **IPBES 2019** — 1 million species at extinction risk globally.

# Wildlife Protection

## Definition

**Wildlife protection** refers to the **legal, institutional, and enforcement mechanisms** designed to safeguard wild animals and plants from poaching, hunting, trade, habitat destruction, and other anthropogenic threats — primarily governed by the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** in India.

**Wildlife protection** is the **legal arm** of wildlife conservation.

## Explanation

### Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 — Key Provisions

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Objective** | Protect wild animals, birds, and plants |
| **Hunting ban** | Hunting of scheduled species prohibited |
| **Schedules I–VI** | Species categorised by protection level |
| **Protected Areas** | NP (Sec 35), Sanctuary (Sec 18), BR |
| **National Board for Wildlife** | Apex advisory body (Sec 5A) |
| **State Wildlife Board** | State-level advisory (Sec 6) |
| **Chief Wildlife Warden** | State-level enforcement authority |
| **Penalties** | Up to 7 years imprisonment + fine |

### WPA Schedules

| Schedule | Protection Level | Species Examples | Penalty |
|----------|-----------------|------------------|---------|
| **Schedule I** | Highest | Tiger, elephant, rhino, lion, dolphin | Up to 7 years |
| **Schedule II (Part II)** | High | Deer, cobra, peafowl | Up to 7 years |
| **Schedule III** | Moderate | Chital, hyena | Up to 3 years |
| **Schedule IV** | Moderate | Common crow, fox | Up to 3 years |
| **Schedule V** | Vermin (unprotected) | Common rat, fruit bat | No protection |
| **Schedule VI** | Protected plants | Orchids, pitcher plant, red sanders | Fine + imprisonment |

### Institutional Framework for Wildlife Protection

| Institution | Role |
|-------------|------|
| **MoEFCC** | Nodal ministry |
| **NBWL** | Policy apex body; chaired by PM |
| **NTCA** | Tiger protection (statutory, 2006) |
| **State Forest Departments** | Ground enforcement |
| **Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB)** | Anti-poaching intelligence |
| **WII (Dehradun)** | Research and training |
| **ZSI + BSI** | Species documentation |
| **CPCB/SPCBs** | Pollution affecting wildlife |

### Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 2007 |
| **Statutory body** | Under MoEFCC |
| **HQ** | New Delhi |
| **Role** | Combat organised wildlife crime |
| **Coordination** | INTERPOL, CITES, state agencies |
| **Operations** | Operation Thunderbird, Operation Save Kurma |

### International Wildlife Protection Treaties

| Treaty | Focus | India Status |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| **CITES (1973)** | Regulate international trade | Member (Appendix I, II, III) |
| **CMS (1979)** | Migratory species | Member |
| **CBD (1992)** | Biodiversity conservation | Ratified 2003 |
| **Ramsar (1971)** | Wetland protection | 85+ sites |
| **UNESCO WHS** | Outstanding natural sites | 7 natural WHS |

### CITES Appendices

| Appendix | Trade Restriction | Examples |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Appendix I** | Commercial trade prohibited | Tiger, elephant, rhino, orangutan |
| **Appendix II** | Trade regulated with permits | Most CITES species |
| **Appendix III** | Species listed by a country | Country-specific |

## Important Facts

- **WPA 1972** — enacted on recommendation of **Indian Wildlife Board (1952)** and **IBWL (1960)**.
- **Hunting banned** for all Schedule I–IV species.
- **NBWL chaired by Prime Minister.**
- **WCCB established 2007** — combats wildlife trafficking.
- **India 3rd largest wildlife trafficking hub** (after China, US) — WCCB data.
- **Pangolin** — most trafficked mammal globally.
- **Tiger poaching** — bones used in traditional medicine (illegal).
- **Amendments:** 1982, 1986, 1991, 1993, 2002, 2006, 2013.

# Project Tiger

## Definition

**Project Tiger** is a **centrally sponsored scheme** launched in **1973** for **in-situ conservation of the Royal Bengal Tiger** in India, establishing a network of **Tiger Reserves** governed by the **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)**.

It is considered the **most successful carnivore conservation programme** in the world.

## Explanation

### Project Tiger Timeline

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| **1972** | WPA enacted; tiger population ~1,800 |
| **1973** | Project Tiger launched; Jim Corbett = first TR |
| **2006** | NTCA established (statutory); WPA amended |
| **2010** | TX2 goal announced (double tigers globally) |
| **2018** | 2,967 tigers (census) |
| **2022** | 3,167 tigers; TX2 achieved; 54 TRs |

### Tiger Reserve Structure

| Zone | Features |
|------|----------|
| **Core/CTH** | Inviolate; no human activity; breeding habitat |
| **Buffer** | Regulated activity; eco-development for communities |
| **Corridor** | Connects TRs for gene flow |

### 54 Tiger Reserves (Selected)

| TR | State | Unique Feature |
|----|-------|----------------|
| Jim Corbett | Uttarakhand | First TR (1973) |
| Bandipur | Karnataka | Nilgiri landscape |
| Kanha | MP | Barasingha recovery |
| Ranthambore | Rajasthan | Tourism icon |
| Sundarbans | West Bengal | Mangrove tiger |
| Kaziranga | Assam | Rhino + tiger |
| Panna | MP | Recovered from extinction |
| Sariska | Rajasthan | Reintroduction success |
| Pench | MP/Maharashtra | Jungle Book setting |
| Ramgarh Vishdhari | Rajasthan | Newest (2022) |

### NTCA Functions

1. Approve Tiger Conservation Plans.
2. Monitor tiger population (quadrennial census).
3. Anti-poaching coordination.
4. M-STrIPES monitoring app.
5. Tourism regulation in TRs.
6. Research and capacity building.

### Tiger Census Methodology

| Method | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Camera traps** | Individual tiger identification (stripe pattern) |
| **AI/ML** | Automated stripe matching |
| **Sign surveys** | Pugmarks, scat, scratch marks |
| **CARNIVORE software** | Pattern recognition |
| **Spatially Explicit Capture-Recapture (SECR)** | Population estimation |

### Tiger Population Trend

| Year | Tigers | Change |
|------|--------|--------|
| 1973 | ~1,800 | Project launch |
| 2006 | 1,411 | First scientific census |
| 2010 | 1,706 | +21% |
| 2014 | 2,226 | +30% |
| 2018 | 2,967 | +33% |
| 2022 | 3,167 | +7%; 70% of global |

## Important Facts

- **Launched 1973** by PM Indira Gandhi.
- **54 Tiger Reserves** in 18 states; ~75,000 sq km.
- **3,167 tigers (2022)** — 70% of world's wild tigers.
- **TX2 goal achieved** — India doubled tiger numbers.
- **NTCA** — statutory body (2006).
- **M-STrIPES** — Monitoring System for Tiger-Intensive Protection and Ecological Status.
- **First TR:** Jim Corbett (1973).
- **Panna** recovered from **zero tigers (2009)** to 50+.

# Project Elephant

## Definition

**Project Elephant** is a **centrally sponsored scheme** launched in **1992** for the **conservation of Asian Elephants**, their habitats, and elephant corridors in India — managed by **MoEFCC** in coordination with State Forest Departments.

**India has the largest population of Asian Elephants** (~30,000 out of ~50,000 globally).

## Explanation

### Project Elephant Components

| Component | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Elephant Reserves** | 33 notified ERs across 14 states |
| **Elephant corridors** | 101 identified; 28 priority |
| **Anti-depredation measures** | Squads, early warning systems |
| **Conflict mitigation** | Compensation, barriers, chilli fences |
| **Research** | Census, telemetry, behaviour studies |
| **Centres of Excellence** | Bengaluru, Guwahati |
| **Veterinary care** | Mobile units for injured elephants |

### 33 Elephant Reserves (Key)

| ER | State | Feature |
|----|-------|---------|
| Singhbhum | Jharkhand | First ER (2001) |
| Mayurbhanj | Odisha | Similipal landscape |
| Kameng | Arunachal Pradesh | Himalayan foothills |
| Periyar | Kerala | Western Ghats |
| Garo Hills | Meghalaya | NE population |
| Eastern Dooars | West Bengal | Terai habitat |
| Lemru | Chhattisgarh | Newest (2022) |

### Human-Elephant Conflict (HEC)

| Aspect | Data |
|--------|------|
| **Human deaths** | 500+ annually (India) |
| **Elephant deaths** | 100+ annually |
| **Causes** | Crop raiding, habitat fragmentation, railway hits |
| **Mitigation** | Early warning, underpasses, compensation |
| **Hotspots** | Odisha, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Assam, W Bengal |

### Elephant Census Results

| Year | Population (approx.) |
|------|---------------------|
| **2005** | ~25,000 |
| **2012** | ~27,000 |
| **2017** | ~27,300 |
| **2020** | ~29,964 |

## Important Facts

# Crocodile Conservation Project

## Definition

The **Crocodile Conservation Project** is a **species recovery programme** launched in **1975** (with UNDP/FAO assistance) for the **conservation and captive breeding of three crocodilian species** in India — **Gharial, Mugger (Marsh Crocodile), and Saltwater Crocodile** — followed by reintroduction into the wild.

## Explanation

### Three Target Species

| Species | Scientific Name | Habitat | IUCN Status |
|---------|----------------|---------|-------------|
| **Gharial (Gavial)** | *Gavialis gangeticus* | Freshwater rivers | Critically Endangered |
| **Mugger (Marsh Crocodile)** | *Crocodylus palustris* | Rivers, lakes, reservoirs | Vulnerable |
| **Saltwater Crocodile** | *Crocodylus porosus* | Estuaries, mangroves, coasts | Least Concern (India) |

### Crocodile Breeding Centres

| Centre | Location | Species |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Nandankanan** | Odisha | Gharial, mugger, saltwater |
| **Crocodile Bank** | Mamallapuram, Tamil Nadu | All three + international species |
| **Hamirpur** | Uttar Pradesh | Gharial |
| **Dharmapuri** | Tamil Nadu | Mugger |
| **Bhitarkanika** | Odisha | Saltwater crocodile (wild) |
| **Sunderbans** | West Bengal | Saltwater crocodile (wild) |

### Gharial — Flagship Species

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Distinctive snout** | Long, thin, fish-eating specialist |
| **Male ghara** | Bulbous nasal appendage (mature males) |
| **Habitat** | Deep, fast-flowing rivers (Ganga, Chambal, Girwa) |
| **Population** | ~200–250 mature individuals (wild) |
| **Threats** | Dam construction, fishing nets, sand mining, pollution |
| **IUCN** | Critically Endangered |
| **WPA** | Schedule I |
| **National Chambal Sanctuary** | Primary gharial habitat |

### Project Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| **1975** | Project launched with UNDP/FAO |
| **1978** | Crocodile Bank established (Rom Whitaker) |
| **1990s** | Gharial population crash |
| **2007** | Gharial crisis — mass die-off in Chambal |
| **2010s** | Continued breeding and reintroduction |

### Rom Whitaker & Crocodile Bank

- **Romulus Whitaker** — "Snake Man of India"; founded **Madras Crocodile Bank Trust (1976)**.
- **Mamallapuram, Tamil Nadu** — world's largest crocodile breeding centre.
- **Conservation breeding** → reintroduction into wild rivers.

## Important Facts

- **Launched 1975** — one of India's earliest species recovery projects.
- **Gharial** — Critically Endangered; only ~200–250 wild adults.
- **Chambal River** — primary gharial habitat (National Chambal Sanctuary).
- **Bhitarkanika (Odisha)** — largest saltwater crocodile population in India.
- **Crocodile Bank, Mamallapuram** — Rom Whitaker; established 1976.
- **Mugger** — most widespread Indian crocodilian.
- **Gharial ghara** — bulb on male snout (mating display).

# Asiatic Lion Conservation

## Definition

**Asiatic Lion Conservation** refers to the **protection and recovery programme** for the **Asiatic Lion (*Panthera leo persica*)**, found **only in Gir National Park and surrounding areas of Gujarat** — the **sole wild population** of Asiatic Lions in the world.

**Project Lion** launched in **2020** for landscape-level conservation.

## Explanation

### Asiatic Lion — Key Facts

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Wild population** | ~700 (2020 census) |
| **Only habitat** | Gir NP, Gujarat |
| **IUCN status** | Endangered |
| **WPA** | Schedule I |
| **Distinctive feature** | Smaller than African lion; prominent belly fold |
| **Social structure** | Smaller prides than African lions |
| **Prey** | Chital, sambar, nilgai, wild boar |

### Gir National Park

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1965 (as NP); Sanctuary since 1965 |
| **Area** | ~1,412 sq km (NP + Sanctuary + PA) |
| **Location** | Junagadh, Gir Somnath, Amreli districts, Gujarat |
| **Ecosystem** | Dry deciduous forest, thorn scrub |
| **Also found** | Leopard, hyena, 300+ bird species |
| **UNESCO** | Not WHS (but conservation model studied globally) |

### Conservation History

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| **1900** | ~50 lions (near extinction) |
| **1913** | Nawab of Junagadh protected lions |
| **1965** | Gir declared National Park |
| **1972** | WPA — Schedule I protection |
| **2010** | Lion shifted from Schedule I to... (remains Schedule I) |
| **2013** | Supreme Court — translocation to Kuno (MP) ordered |
| **2020** | Project Lion launched; ~700 lions |
| **2022** | Cheetah introduced at Kuno (not lion yet) |

### Project Lion (2020)

| Component | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Landscape approach** | Beyond Gir — entire Saurashtra landscape |
| **Barda Wildlife Sanctuary** | Second lion habitat being developed |
| **Kuno NP (MP)** | SC-ordered translocation site (pending) |
| **Lion Gene Bank** | Genetic diversity conservation |
| **Conflict mitigation** | HWC in expanding lion territory |
| **Zoonotic disease** | Canine distemper virus (CDV) monitoring |
| **Radio collaring** | Movement tracking |

### Threats to Asiatic Lion

| Threat | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Single population** | Genetic bottleneck; disease outbreak risk |
| **Inbreeding depression** | Low genetic diversity |
| **Canine Distemper Virus** | 2018 outbreak killed 23 lions |
| **Human-wildlife conflict** | Lions expanding beyond Gir |
| **Infrastructure** | Roads, railways through habitat |
| **Tourism pressure** | Overcrowding in Gir |

### Kuno Translocation Controversy

- **Supreme Court (2013)** — ordered translocation of lions to Kuno NP, MP.
- **Gujarat Government** — resisted citing adaptation concerns.
- **IUCN guidelines** — recommend second population for species survival.
- **Kuno** — cheetahs introduced 2022; lions still pending.

## Important Facts

- **~700 Asiatic Lions** — only in Gir, Gujarat.
- **1900: ~50 lions** → recovered to 700 (conservation success).
- **Only country** with both tigers and lions in the wild.
- **Project Lion (2020)** — landscape-level conservation.
- **2018 CDV outbreak** — 23 lions died in Gir.
- **Supreme Court (2013)** — ordered Kuno translocation.
- **Barda WLS** — second lion habitat being prepared in Gujarat.
- **Nawab of Junagadh** — early lion protector (1913).

# Rhino Conservation

## Definition

**Rhino Conservation** refers to the **protection and recovery programmes** for the **Greater One-Horned Rhinoceros (*Rhinoceros unicornis*)** in India — primarily through **Kaziranga National Park** and the **Indian Rhino Vision 2020 (IRV 2020)** programme for population expansion to new habitats.

India holds **~80% of the world's Greater One-Horned Rhino population** (~4,000+).

## Explanation

### Greater One-Horned Rhino — Key Facts

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Scientific name** | *Rhinoceros unicornis* |
| **Population** | ~4,000+ (India) |
| **IUCN status** | Vulnerable |
| **WPA** | Schedule I |
| **Distinctive feature** | Single horn; armour-plated skin folds |
| **Habitat** | Grasslands, wetlands, floodplains |
| **Weight** | 1,800–2,700 kg (largest rhino species) |
| **Lifespan** | 35–45 years |

### Rhino Habitats in India

| Protected Area | State | Rhino Population |
|----------------|-------|-----------------|
| **Kaziranga NP** | Assam | ~2,600 (largest) |
| **Pobitora WLS** | Assam | ~100 |

| **Manas NP** | Assam | ~40 (reintroduced) |
| **Jaldapara NP** | West Bengal | ~250 |
| **Gorumara NP** | West Bengal | ~50 |
| **Dudhwa NP** | Uttar Pradesh | ~35 (reintroduced) |

| Component | Detail |

# Vulture Conservation

## Definition

**Vulture Conservation** refers to the **protection and recovery programmes** for **vulture species** in India, which suffered a **catastrophic population decline (99%+) since the 1990s** due to **diclofenac poisoning** — addressed through the **Vulture Action Plan**, **diclofenac ban (2006)**, and **captive breeding centres**.

India had **~40 million vultures** in the 1980s; now **~10,000** remain.

## Explanation

### Vulture Crisis in India

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Peak population** | ~40 million (1980s) |
| **Current population** | ~10,000 (all species combined) |
| **Decline rate** | 95–99% in 15 years |
| **Primary cause** | Diclofenac (NSAID) in cattle carcasses |
| **IUCN status** | Multiple species Critically Endangered |
| **Ecological role** | Nature's scavengers; prevent disease spread |
| **Economic value** | Saves crores in carcass disposal |

### Three Critically Endangered Vulture Species

| Species | Scientific Name | Population (approx.) |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| **White-rumped Vulture** | *Gyps bengalensis* | ~6,000 |
| **Long-billed Vulture** | *Gyps indicus* | ~2,000 |
| **Slender-billed Vulture** | *Gyps tenuirostris* | ~1,000 |

### Other Vulture Species in India

| Species | Status |
|---------|--------|
| **Egyptian Vulture** | Endangered |
| **Red-headed Vulture** | Critically Endangered |
| **Himalayan Griffon** | Near Threatened |
| **Cinereous Vulture** | Near Threatened |
| **Bearded Vulture (Lammergeier)** | Near Threatened |

### Diclofenac — The Killer Drug

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **What** | Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug (NSAID) |
| **Use** | Cattle pain relief and inflammation |
| **Problem** | Vultures feeding on diclofenac-treated cattle carcasses suffer renal failure |
| **Discovery** | BNHS/WII research (2003–2004) |
| **Ban** | Veterinary diclofenac banned in 2006 |
| **Meloxicam** | Safe alternative NSAID for cattle |
| **Residue problem** | Human diclofenac still misused for cattle |

### Vulture Conservation Action Plan

| Phase | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Phase I** | Diclofenac ban, captive breeding, surveys |
| **Phase II** | Safe zones, meloxicam promotion, release programmes |
| **Phase III** | Population recovery, vulture restaurants |
| **MoEFCC** | National coordination |
| **BNHS** | Bombay Natural History Society — lead research |

### Vulture Breeding Centres

| Centre | Location | Species |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Pinjore** | Haryana | White-rumped, Long-billed, Slender-billed |

| **Rani** | Assam (Guwahati) | White-rumped, Slender-billed |
| **Junagadh** | Gujarat | Long-billed |
| **Hyderabad** | Telangana | All three species |
| **Muta** | Madhya Pradesh | White-rumped |

### Vulture Safe Zones

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Concept** | Areas free of diclofenac contamination |
| **First VSZ** | Pinjore, Haryana |
| **Criteria** | No diclofenac use; meloxicam availability |
| **Goal** | Release captive-bred vultures into safe zones |
| **Monitoring** | Satellite tagging, health checks |

### Vulture Restaurants (Jatayu Restaurants)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Concept** | Provision of diclofenac-free carcasses |
| **Locations** | Rajasthan, MP, Assam |
| **Purpose** | Supplement wild vulture food supply |
| **Community role** | Local participation in carcass supply |

### SAVE (Saving Asia's Vultures from Extinction)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Founded** | 2011 |
| **Members** | BNHS, RSPB, ZSL, WCS, etc. |
| **Goal** | Coordinate Asian vulture recovery |
| **India role** | Model for South Asia vulture conservation |

## Important Facts

- **99% decline** in vulture population since 1990s.
- **Diclofenac ban (2006)** — veterinary use prohibited.
- **Meloxicam** — safe alternative for cattle.
- **6 breeding centres** — Pinjore, Buxa, Rani, Junagadh, Hyderabad, Muta.
- **BNHS** — lead research organisation.
- **3 Gyps species** — all Critically Endangered.
- **Vulture Safe Zones** — diclofenac-free release areas.
- **First Saturday of September** — International Vulture Awareness Day.
- **Ecosystem service** — prevents anthrax, rabies spread from carcasses.
