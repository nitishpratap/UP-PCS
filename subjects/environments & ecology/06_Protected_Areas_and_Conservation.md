# Wildlife Sanctuary

## Definition

A **Wildlife Sanctuary** is a **protected area** established under the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** for the purpose of **protecting, propagating, and developing wildlife and their habitat**, where certain human activities (grazing, collection of forest produce) may be **permitted** under regulation.

**Legal basis:** Section 18–27 of WPA 1972.

**Key feature:** Less restrictive than National Parks — limited human activity allowed.

## Explanation

### Characteristics of Wildlife Sanctuaries

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Declaration** | By State Government (Section 18, WPA 1972) |
| **Purpose** | Protect wildlife and habitat |
| **Human activity** | Limited — grazing, firewood collection may be permitted |
| **Boundaries** | Can be altered by State Government |
| **Tourism** | Allowed with permits |
| **Research** | Permitted with approval |
| **Private rights** | May exist — not automatically extinguished |
| **Weapons** | Prohibited without permission |

### Wildlife Sanctuary vs National Park

| Feature | Wildlife Sanctuary | National Park |
|---------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Human activity** | Limited grazing, collection allowed | Strictly prohibited |
| **Boundaries** | Can be changed by State Govt | Cannot be altered without Parliament |
| **Declaration** | Section 18, WPA 1972 | Section 35, WPA 1972 |
| **Private rights** | May continue | Extinguished |
| **Strictness** | Moderate | Highest |
| **IUCN Category** | Category IV (Habitat/Species Management) | Category II (National Park) |

### IUCN Protected Area Categories

| Category | Type | Indian Equivalent |
|----------|------|-------------------|
| **Ia** | Strict Nature Reserve | — |
| **Ib** | Wilderness Area | — |

| **V** | Protected Landscape/Seascape | Biosphere Reserve (partially) |
| **VI** | Sustainable Use of Natural Resources | Biosphere Reserve (transition zone) |

## Important Facts

- **India has 566 Wildlife Sanctuaries** (latest MoEFCC data).
- **First sanctuary in India:** **Jim Corbett National Park** area was first sanctuary (1936 as Hailey National Park).
- **Oldest sanctuary:** **Jhira Bagh Sanctuary** concept; **Jim Corbett** — first NP (1936).
- **WPA 1972** is the governing legislation.
- **Sanctuary can exist inside or outside forest land.**
- **Collectors** can regulate entry into sanctuaries.
- **Grazing** of livestock permitted only by Chief Wildlife Warden's order.
- Some areas are **both NP and Sanctuary** (e.g., Corbett, Ranthambore, Manas).

# National Park

## Definition

A **National Park** is a **protected area** established under **Section 35 of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**, where **all human activity** (hunting, poaching, grazing, forestry, cultivation) is **strictly prohibited** except for tourism, research, and management purposes approved by the Chief Wildlife Warden.

**Purpose:** To protect the **natural environment, wildlife, and biodiversity** in their pristine state.

## Explanation

### Characteristics of National Parks

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Declaration** | By State Government; alteration requires Parliament approval |
| **Human activity** | Strictly prohibited (except regulated tourism/research) |
| **Private rights** | Automatically extinguished on notification |
| **Boundaries** | Cannot be altered without Parliament approval |
| **Weapons** | Strictly prohibited |
| **Grazing** | Not permitted |
| **Forestry operations** | Not permitted |
| **IUCN Category** | Category II (National Park) |
| **Minimum area** | No minimum specified in WPA; generally large |

### National Park vs Wildlife Sanctuary

| Feature | National Park | Wildlife Sanctuary |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Strictness** | Highest | Moderate |
| **Grazing** | Prohibited | Permitted (regulated) |
| **Private rights** | Extinguished | May continue |
| **Boundary change** | Parliament approval needed | State Govt can alter |
| **WPA Section** | Section 35 | Section 18 |
| **IUCN** | Category II | Category IV |

## Important Facts

- **India has 104 National Parks** covering ~1.3% of geographical area.
- **First National Park:** **Jim Corbett** (1936 as Hailey NP, renamed 1957).
- **Largest NP:** **Hemis NP** (Ladakh) — 4,400 sq km.
- **Smallest NP:** **South Button Island NP** (Andaman) — 0.03 sq km.
- **Gir NP** — only home of Asiatic Lions (~700 individuals).
- **Kaziranga** — hosts 2/3 of world's one-horned rhinos.
- **No grazing, forestry, or cultivation** allowed in any NP.
- **Project Tiger** reserves often include or overlap with NPs.

# Biosphere Reserve

## Definition

A **Biosphere Reserve** is a **large protected area** designated under **UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme** for **conserving biodiversity, promoting sustainable development, and facilitating research and education** — with a **three-zone system** (core, buffer, transition).

**India declaration:** Through MoEFCC; not directly under WPA 1972 but often overlaps with NP/Sanctuary.

**First BR in India:** **Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve** (1986).

## Explanation

### Three-Zone System of Biosphere Reserves

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           TRANSITION ZONE (Outer)           │
│     Sustainable development, human          │
│     settlements, tourism, research        │
│  ┌───────────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │        BUFFER ZONE (Middle)           │  │
│  │   Limited human activity, research,   │  │
│  │   tourism, restoration activities     │  │
│  │  ┌─────────────────────────────────┐  │  │
│  │  │      CORE ZONE (Inner)          │  │  │
│  │  │  Strictly protected — no human  │  │  │
│  │  │  activity; pristine ecosystem   │  │  │
│  │  └─────────────────────────────────┘  │  │
│  └───────────────────────────────────────┘  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Zone | Activity Level | Purpose |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **Core Zone** | No human activity | Strict conservation; research only |
| **Buffer Zone** | Limited activity | Research, education, restoration |
| **Transition Zone** | Sustainable development | Human settlements, eco-tourism, sustainable resource use |

### Objectives of Biosphere Reserves (UNESCO MAB)

1. **Conservation** — Biodiversity and ecosystem conservation.
2. **Development** — Sustainable economic and human development.
3. **Logistics** — Research, monitoring, education, training.

### Biosphere Reserve vs National Park

| Feature | Biosphere Reserve | National Park |
|---------|------------------|---------------|
| **Governing body** | UNESCO MAB + MoEFCC | WPA 1972 (State Govt) |
| **Zones** | 3 (core, buffer, transition) | Single strict zone |
| **Human activity** | Allowed in buffer/transition | Prohibited |
| **Focus** | Conservation + sustainable development | Strict conservation only |
| **Size** | Generally larger (landscape level) | Variable |

## Important Facts

- **India has 18 Biosphere Reserves** — 12 UNESCO recognised.
- **First BR:** Nilgiri (1986) — Western Ghats + Eastern Ghats confluence.
- **Largest BR:** Gulf of Mannar (~10,500 sq km).
- **UNESCO MAB Programme** launched in **1971**.
- **BRs cover ~6.9%** of India's geographical area.
- **Khangchendzonga BR** — includes world's third highest peak.
- **Sundarbans BR** — largest mangrove ecosystem.
- **BRs can include NP, Sanctuary, and human settlements.**

# Conservation Reserve

## Definition

A **Conservation Reserve** is a **protected area** category added to the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** through the **2002 Amendment**, covering **government-owned lands** (often buffer zones adjacent to National Parks or Sanctuaries) that are **not suitable for NP/Sanctuary status** but are important for wildlife conservation.

**Legal basis:** Section 36A of WPA 1972 (added 2002).

**Purpose:** Protect areas that are **contiguous with or near existing PAs** and have conservation value.

## Explanation

### Characteristics

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Declaration** | By State Government (Section 36A) |
| **Land type** | Government-owned land (reserved forests, protected forests) |
| **Location** | Usually adjacent to or near NP/Sanctuary |
| **Human activity** | Limited — less strict than NP, similar to Sanctuary |
| **Private land** | Cannot be declared Conservation Reserve |
| **Community involvement** | Limited compared to Community Reserve |
| **Alteration** | State Government can alter boundaries |

### Conservation Reserve vs Community Reserve

| Feature | Conservation Reserve | Community Reserve |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Land ownership** | Government-owned | Private or community-owned |
| **WPA Section** | Section 36A (2002) | Section 36B (2002) |
| **Declaration** | State Government | State Government (community initiative) |
| **Management** | State Forest Department | Community + State |
| **Human activity** | Limited (State regulated) | Community-managed sustainable use |

### Examples of Conservation Reserves in India

| Conservation Reserve | State | Adjacent to |
|---------------------|-------|-------------|
| **Tirupati Elephant CR** | Andhra Pradesh | Near Seshachalam hills |
| **Bir Bhadra CR** | Chhattisgarh | Near Barnawapara Sanctuary |
| **Aravalli CR** | Haryana | Near existing PAs |
| **Jhilmil CR** | Uttarakhand | Near Rajaji NP |
| **Ramgarh Vishdhari CR** | Rajasthan | Near Ranthambore (now Tiger Reserve) |

## Important Facts

- **Added in 2002 Amendment** to WPA 1972 (along with Community Reserve).
- **Section 36A** — Conservation Reserve; **Section 36B** — Community Reserve.
- **India has ~100+ Conservation Reserves** (state-wise notifications).
- **Ramgarh Vishdhari** upgraded to **Tiger Reserve** in 2022.
- **Cannot be declared on private land** — only government land.
- **Less known** than NP/Sanctuary but important for habitat connectivity.

# Community Reserve

## Definition

A **Community Reserve** is a **protected area** category added through the **WPA 2002 Amendment (Section 36B)** on **private or community-owned land** where the **local community** voluntarily offers their land for biodiversity conservation and sustainable use.

**Legal basis:** Section 36B of WPA 1972 (added 2002).

**Purpose:** Enable **community-led conservation** on private/community lands.

## Explanation

### Characteristics

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Declaration** | State Government on community initiative |
| **Land type** | Private or community-owned land |
| **Management** | Community + State Forest Department |
| **Voluntary** | Community voluntarily offers land |
| **Human activity** | Community-managed sustainable use |
| **Rights** | Community retains certain use rights |
| **WPA Section** | Section 36B (2002 Amendment) |

### Community Reserve vs Conservation Reserve

| Feature | Community Reserve | Conservation Reserve |
|---------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Land** | Private/community-owned | Government-owned |
| **Section** | 36B | 36A |
| **Management** | Community-led | State-led |
| **Initiative** | Community voluntary | Government initiative |
| **Rights** | Community retains rights | State manages |

### Examples of Community Reserves in India

| Community Reserve | State | Community |
|-------------------|-------|-----------|
| **Khonoma Nature Conservation Tragedy** | Nagaland | Angami Naga tribe |
| **Senchal CR** | West Bengal | Near Darjeeling |
| **Amarkantak CR** | Madhya Pradesh | Community forest |

### Khonoma — India's First Community Reserve

- **Khonoma village, Nagaland** — often cited as first Community Reserve.
- **Angami Naga tribe** voluntarily declared 1,250 hectares for conservation.
- **Hunted Blyth's tragopan** (endangered pheasant) — now protected.
- **Model** for community-led conservation in Northeast India.

## Important Facts

- **Section 36B** — Community Reserve (2002 Amendment).
- **Khonoma, Nagaland** — pioneering community conservation model.
- **India has few notified Community Reserves** — concept still emerging.
- **Forest Rights Act (2006)** complements community conservation.
- **Sacred groves** are informal community conservation; CR is formal legal status.
- **Community initiative** is essential — government cannot force CR on private land.

# Tiger Reserve

## Definition

A **Tiger Reserve** is a **protected area** designated for **in-situ conservation of the Royal Bengal Tiger** under **Project Tiger (1973)**, governed by the **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)** through the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (amended 2006)**.

**Legal basis:** Section 38V of WPA 1972 (added 2006); Project Tiger (1973).

**India hosts ~70% of the world's wild tigers.**

## Explanation

### Structure of Tiger Reserves

```
TIGER RESERVE
├── CORE/CRITICAL TIGER HABITAT (CTH)
│   └── Inviolate — no human activity (like NP)
└── BUFFER ZONE
    └── Regulated human activity; eco-development for local communities
```

| Zone | Activity | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Core/CTH** | No human activity; inviolate | Tiger breeding and habitat protection |
| **Buffer** | Regulated activity; eco-development | Reduce man-animal conflict; community welfare |

### Project Tiger (1973)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Launched** | 1973 by Government of India |
| **First TR** | Jim Corbett (1973) |
| **Governing body** | NTCA (established 2006) |
| **Current TRs** | 54 Tiger Reserves |
| **Tiger population 2022** | 3,167 (up from 2,967 in 2018) |
| **Global share** | ~70% of world's tigers |
| **TX2 goal** | Double tiger numbers — achieved in India |

# Elephant Reserve

## Definition

An **Elephant Reserve (ER)** is a **protected area network** designated under **Project Elephant (1992)** for the **conservation of Asian Elephants** and their habitat, managed by the **MoEFCC** in coordination with State Forest Departments.

**Legal basis:** Project Elephant (1992); not a separate WPA category but notified by MoEFCC.

**India has the largest population of Asian Elephants** (~30,000).

## Explanation

### Project Elephant (1992)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Launched** | 1992 by Government of India |
| **Purpose** | Elephant conservation and habitat protection |
| **Governing body** | MoEFCC + State Forest Departments |
| **Elephant Reserves** | 33 notified ERs |
| **Elephant population** | ~30,000 (2020 census) |
| **Elephant corridors** | 101 identified; 28 priority corridors |
| **Centres of Excellence** | Bengaluru, Guwahati |

|-----------------|-------|-------------|

| **Eastern Dooars** | West Bengal | Terai elephant habitat |
| **Periyar** | Kerala | Western Ghats elephants |
| **Nilambur** | Kerala | Malabar elephant population |
| **Rayala** | Andhra Pradesh | Eastern Ghats |
| **Sambalpur** | Odisha | Central Indian elephants |
| **Lemru** | Chhattisgarh | Newest ER (2022) |

### Elephant Corridors

| Corridor | States | Significance |

### Threats to Elephants

| Threat | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Habitat fragmentation** | Isolated populations; inbreeding |
| **Human-elephant conflict** | 500+ humans and 100+ elephants killed annually |
| **Railway accidents** | Train hits in corridor areas |
| **Electrocution** | Fence and power line deaths |
| **Poaching** | Ivory trade (though less than Africa) |
| **Mining** | Habitat destruction in mineral-rich areas |

### Elephant Conservation Measures

1. **Elephant Reserves** — landscape-level habitat protection.
2. **Corridors** — connect fragmented habitats.
3. **Anti-depredation squads** — reduce human-elephant conflict.
4. **Early warning systems** — alert communities of elephant movement.
5. **Railway speed restrictions** — in corridor areas.
6. **Compensation schemes** — for crop damage and human fatalities.

## Important Facts

- **~30,000 Asian Elephants** in India (largest population globally).
- **Project Elephant** launched **1992**.
- **101 elephant corridors** identified; **28 priority corridors**.
- **National Heritage Animal** — declared 2010.
- **Human-elephant conflict** — major conservation challenge.
- **Karnataka** has highest elephant population (~6,000).
- **Lemru ER (Chhattisgarh)** — newest ER (2022).

# Ramsar Sites

## Definition

**Ramsar Sites** are **wetlands of international importance** designated under the **Ramsar Convention (1971)** — the only global environmental treaty focused exclusively on wetlands.

**Ramsar Convention** signed in **Ramsar, Iran (1971)**; came into force **1975**.

**India ratified:** 1982. **India has 85+ Ramsar sites** — highest in Asia.

## Explanation

### Ramsar Convention Key Facts

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Signed** | 1971, Ramsar, Iran |
| **Came into force** | 1975 |
| **India ratified** | 1982 |
| **Focus** | Wetland conservation worldwide |
| **India's sites** | 85+ (highest in Asia) |
| **Wise Use Principle** | Sustainable use of wetlands without degradation |
| **Montreux Record** | List of Ramsar sites in danger |
| **World Wetlands Day** | 2 February |

### Criteria for Ramsar Designation

1. Contains a **representative, rare, or unique wetland type**.
2. Supports **vulnerable, endangered, or critically endangered species**.
3. Supports **species important for biodiversity conservation**.
4. Supports **20,000+ waterbirds** or **1% of a population**.
5. Regularly supports **20,000+ waterbirds**.
6. Important **fish habitat** (food, spawning, nursery).
7. Supports **1% of a fish species population**.
8. Important for **maintaining biological diversity** of a region.
9. **Cultural/historic significance**.

# Eco-Sensitive Zones

## Definition

**Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZs)** are **buffer areas** around **Protected Areas (National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves)** where regulated development is permitted to create a **shock absorber** for the protected area — notified under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.

**Purpose:** To protect the core PA from negative impacts of development in surrounding areas.

**Governing law:** EPA 1986 + MoEFCC notifications.

## Explanation

### ESZ Framework

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         ECO-SENSITIVE ZONE (ESZ)        │
│    (Buffer — regulated development)     │
│  ┌───────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │     PROTECTED AREA (Core)         │  │
│  │   NP / Sanctuary / BR Core        │  │
│  │   (No/minimal development)        │  │
│  └───────────────────────────────────┘  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Zone | Activity Level |
|------|---------------|
| **Protected Area (Core)** | No development; strict conservation |
| **ESZ (Buffer)** | Regulated development; prohibited and permitted activities listed |

### ESZ Width

| Category | Width |
|----------|-------|
| **Default** | 0–10 km from PA boundary |
| **Varies** | Can be less than 10 km based on landscape |
| **Western Ghats** | 0–2 km (Kasturirangan) vs 10 km (Gadgil) — debated |
| **Case-specific** | Determined by expert committee recommendations |

### Permitted vs Prohibited Activities in ESZ

| Prohibited | Permitted (regulated) |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Mining and quarrying | Organic farming |
| Polluting industries | Rainwater harvesting |
| Commercial mining | Use of renewable energy |
| Major hydroelectric projects | Eco-tourism (small scale) |
| Setting up saw mills | Existing agriculture/horticulture |
| Commercial use of firewood | Felling of trees (with permission) |
| Establishment of major hotels/resorts | — |

### Western Ghats ESZ Debate

| Committee | Recommendation | Coverage |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| **Gadgil Committee (WGEEP, 2011)** | Entire Western Ghats as ESZ | ~164,280 sq km (100% as ESZ) |
| **Kasturirangan Committee (HLWG, 2013)** | Only ecologically sensitive areas | ~37% of Western Ghats (~60,000 sq km) |
| **Current status** | Draft notification pending | Centre-State negotiations ongoing |

### ESZ Notification Process

1. **MoEFCC** proposes ESZ based on expert committee report.
2. **Public consultation** — objections/suggestions invited.
3. **Final notification** under EPA 1986.
4. **State Government** implements ESZ regulations.
5. **Supreme Court (2022)** — minimum 1 km ESZ mandatory for all PAs.

### Supreme Court Direction (2022)

- **Minimum 1 km ESZ** mandatory around all Protected Areas.
- **Mining prohibited** within national parks and wildlife sanctuaries.
- **ESZ applies** to all PAs regardless of existing notifications.
- **States** must notify ESZs within 6 months.

## Important Facts

- **ESZ notified under EPA 1986** — not WPA 1972.
- **Default width:** 0–10 km (varies case to case).
- **Supreme Court (2022):** Minimum 1 km ESZ for all PAs.
- **Western Ghats ESZ** — most controversial ESZ notification.
- **Gadgil vs Kasturirangan** — major policy debate.
- **Mining banned** in all PAs and ESZs (SC direction).
- **~600+ PAs** need ESZ notification across India.
- **Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra** — states with most ESZ notifications.
