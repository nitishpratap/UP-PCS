# Sustainable Development

## Definition

**Sustainable Development** is development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs** — integrating **economic growth, social equity, and environmental protection** into a balanced, long-term framework.

**Brundtland Report (1987) definition:** *"Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."*

**Key concept:** **Inter-generational equity** — fairness between current and future generations.

## Explanation

### Three Pillars of Sustainable Development

| Pillar | Focus | Examples |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **Economic** | Prosperity, growth, employment | Green jobs, renewable energy industry |
| **Social** | Equity, inclusion, human rights | Poverty reduction, education, health |
| **Environmental** | Resource conservation, pollution control | Clean air/water, biodiversity, climate action |

### Sustainable Development vs Traditional Development

| Feature | Traditional Development | Sustainable Development |
|---------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **Focus** | GDP growth only | Economic + social + environmental |
| **Time horizon** | Short-term profits | Long-term inter-generational |
| **Resources** | Exploitative use | Renewable and efficient use |
| **Environment** | External cost (ignored) | Integrated into planning |
| **Equity** | Growth benefits few | Inclusive growth for all |
| **Measurement** | GDP per capita | HDI, SDG indicators, GPI |

### Principles of Sustainable Development

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Inter-generational equity** | Future generations deserve same resources |
| **Intra-generational equity** | Fair distribution among current generation |
| **Precautionary principle** | Prevent environmental harm even without full scientific certainty |
| **Polluter pays principle** | Those who pollute bear cleanup costs |
| **Public participation** | Communities involved in development decisions |
| **Integration** | Environment integrated into all policies |
| **Conservation of biodiversity** | Protect ecological diversity |
| **Resource efficiency** | Minimise waste; maximise utility |

# Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

## Definition

The **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** are a **collection of 17 interlinked global goals** adopted by the **United Nations General Assembly in 2015** as part of the **2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development** — designed to be a **"blueprint to achieve a better and more sustainable future for all"** by addressing poverty, inequality, climate change, environmental degradation, peace, and justice.

**Predecessor:** Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) — 8 goals (2000–2015).

## Explanation

### 17 SDGs — Complete List

| SDG | Goal | Key Target |
|-----|------|-----------|
| **SDG 1** | No Poverty | End extreme poverty everywhere |
| **SDG 2** | Zero Hunger | End hunger; food security; sustainable agriculture |
| **SDG 3** | Good Health and Well-being | Universal health coverage |
| **SDG 4** | Quality Education | Inclusive, equitable education for all |
| **SDG 5** | Gender Equality | Empower all women and girls |
| **SDG 6** | Clean Water and Sanitation | Universal access to safe water |
| **SDG 7** | Affordable and Clean Energy | Renewable, accessible energy |
| **SDG 8** | Decent Work and Economic Growth | Inclusive, sustainable economic growth |
| **SDG 9** | Industry, Innovation, Infrastructure | Resilient infrastructure; innovation |
| **SDG 10** | Reduced Inequalities | Reduce inequality within and among countries |
| **SDG 11** | Sustainable Cities and Communities | Safe, inclusive, sustainable cities |
| **SDG 12** | Responsible Consumption and Production | Sustainable consumption patterns |
| **SDG 13** | Climate Action | Combat climate change and its impacts |
| **SDG 14** | Life Below Water | Conserve oceans, seas, marine resources |
| **SDG 15** | Life on Land | Protect terrestrial ecosystems, forests, biodiversity |
| **SDG 16** | Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions | Promote peaceful, inclusive societies |
| **SDG 17** | Partnerships for the Goals | Strengthen global partnerships |

### SDGs vs MDGs

| Feature | MDGs (2000–2015) | SDGs (2015–2030) |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Number of goals** | 8 | 17 |
| **Targets** | 21 | 169 |
| **Indicators** | 60 | 232 |
| **Scope** | Primarily developing countries | **Universal** — all countries |
| **Focus** | Poverty, health, education | Comprehensive — all dimensions |
| **Environment** | Limited (MDG 7 only) | Integrated (SDGs 12–15) |
| **Participation** | Top-down | Inclusive, participatory |
| **India role** | Significant progress | Active implementer |

### Environment-Related SDGs

| SDG | Environmental Focus | India Initiative |
|-----|-------------------|-----------------|
| **SDG 6** | Water and sanitation | Jal Jeevan Mission, Swachh Bharat |
| **SDG 7** | Clean energy | Solar Mission, 500 GW renewable |
| **SDG 11** | Sustainable cities | Smart Cities Mission |
| **SDG 12** | Sustainable consumption | Mission LiFE, plastic ban |
| **SDG 13** | Climate action | NAPCC, Net Zero 2070 |
| **SDG 14** | Oceans | Blue Economy policy |
| **SDG 15** | Life on land | Green India Mission, Project Tiger |

### SDG Implementation in India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Nodal body** | NITI Aayog |
| **SDG India Index** | Annual state ranking (since 2019) |
| **Localisation** | SDGs integrated into state plans |
| **Top performing states** | Kerala, Himachal Pradesh, Tamil Nadu |
| **UP performance** | Moderate; improving in SDG 1, 3, 4 |
| **Funding** | SDG coordination through budget allocation |
| **Monitoring** | 232 global indicators; India has national indicators |

### SDG Interlinkages

```
SDG 1 (No Poverty) ↔ SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) ↔ SDG 3 (Health)
        ↓
SDG 6 (Water) ↔ SDG 13 (Climate) ↔ SDG 15 (Life on Land)
        ↓
SDG 7 (Clean Energy) ↔ SDG 12 (Consumption) ↔ SDG 11 (Cities)
        ↓
SDG 17 (Partnerships) enables ALL other SDGs
```

## Important Facts

- **17 SDGs** adopted on **25 September 2015** by UN General Assembly.
- **169 targets** and **232 indicators** for monitoring.
- **Universal application** — all countries, not just developing nations.
- **MDGs** — 8 predecessor goals (2000–2015).
- **SDG 13** — Climate Action (most environment-focused).
- **SDG 15.3** — Land Degradation Neutrality.
- **NITI Aayog** — publishes India SDG Index.
- **2030** — deadline for achieving all SDGs.
- **"Leave no one behind"** — core SDG principle.
- **India** — among active SDG implementers globally.

# SDGs 2030

## Definition

**SDGs 2030** refers to the **global commitment to achieve all 17 Sustainable Development Goals by the year 2030** — adopted at the **UN Sustainable Development Summit (New York, 25 September 2015)** as the **deadline for transforming the world** through ending poverty, protecting the planet, and ensuring prosperity for all.

**Official name:** **"Transforming Our World: The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development"**

## Explanation

### 2030 Agenda — Key Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Adopted** | 25 September 2015, UN General Assembly |
| **Duration** | 2016–2030 (15 years) |
| **Goals** | 17 SDGs |
| **Targets** | 169 specific targets |
| **Indicators** | 232 measurable indicators |
| **Principle** | "Leave no one behind" |
| **Universality** | Applies to ALL countries |
| **Integration** | Economic, social, environmental dimensions |
| **Partnership** | SDG 17 enables all others |

### SDGs 2030 Timeline

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| **2000** | MDGs adopted (predecessor) |
| **2012** | Rio+20 — SDG framework proposed |
| **2015** | SDGs 2030 Agenda adopted (25 September) |
| **2016** | Implementation begins (1 January) |
| **2019** | First India SDG Index (NITI Aayog) |
| **2020** | COVID-19 disrupts SDG progress globally |
| **2023** | SDG Summit — midpoint review |
| **2030** | **Target deadline** for all SDGs |

### India's SDGs 2030 Commitments

| Area | Commitment | Programme |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Poverty (SDG 1)** | End extreme poverty | MGNREGA, PM-KISAN, PMAY |
| **Hunger (SDG 2)** | Zero hunger | NFSA, PM POSHAN |
| **Health (SDG 3)** | Universal health coverage | Ayushman Bharat, NHM |
| **Education (SDG 4)** | Quality education for all | Samagra Shiksha, NEP 2020 |
| **Water (SDG 6)** | Safe water and sanitation | Jal Jeevan Mission, Swachh Bharat |
| **Energy (SDG 7)** | Clean, affordable energy | 500 GW renewable by 2030 |
| **Climate (SDG 13)** | Climate action | NAPCC, Net Zero 2070 |
| **Land (SDG 15)** | Life on land | LDN target, Green India Mission |

| **India strengths** | SDG 6 (sanitation), SDG 7 (solar), SDG 12 (consumption) |

# Objectives of Sustainable Development

## Definition

The **Objectives of Sustainable Development** are the **specific goals and outcomes** that sustainable development seeks to achieve — encompassing **poverty eradication, environmental protection, social equity, economic prosperity, and inter-generational justice** — as articulated in the **Brundtland Report, Rio Declaration, Agenda 21, and SDGs**.

**Primary objective:** Achieve **human well-being** within **planetary boundaries**.

## Explanation

### Core Objectives of Sustainable Development

| Objective | Detail | SDG Link |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Poverty eradication** | End extreme poverty globally | SDG 1 |
| **Food security** | Adequate, nutritious food for all | SDG 2 |
| **Health for all** | Universal health coverage | SDG 3 |
| **Education** | Quality education accessible to all | SDG 4 |
| **Gender equality** | Empower women and girls | SDG 5 |
| **Clean water** | Safe drinking water and sanitation | SDG 6 |
| **Clean energy** | Affordable, renewable energy | SDG 7 |
| **Economic growth** | Inclusive, sustainable growth | SDG 8 |
| **Environmental protection** | Conserve ecosystems and biodiversity | SDG 13, 14, 15 |
| **Peace and justice** | Inclusive institutions, rule of law | SDG 16 |
| **Global partnership** | International cooperation | SDG 17 |

### Brundtland Report Objectives (1987)

| Objective | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Revive growth** | Economic growth in developing countries |
| **Change quality of growth** | Growth that is less resource-intensive |
| **Meet essential needs** | Food, water, shelter, employment for poor |
| **Control population** | Sustainable population levels |
| **Conserve resources** | Renewable resource management |
| **Reorient technology** | Environment-friendly technology |
| **Merge environment and economics** | Integrate environmental costs |
| **International cooperation** | Global partnership for sustainability |

### Rio Declaration Objectives (1992) — Selected Principles

| Principle | Objective |
|-----------|-----------|

| **Principle 3** | Right to development fulfilled equitably |

# Indicators of Sustainable Development

## Definition

**Indicators of Sustainable Development** are **measurable variables** used to **track progress toward sustainable development goals** — providing **quantitative and qualitative evidence** of whether economic, social, and environmental conditions are improving or deteriorating over time.

**Purpose:** Move beyond **GDP-only measurement** to **holistic assessment** of well-being and environmental health.

## Explanation

### Types of Sustainable Development Indicators

| Type | Examples | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Economic** | GDP, GNI, employment rate, poverty headcount | Economic progress |
| **Social** | HDI, literacy rate, life expectancy, Gini coefficient | Social well-being |
| **Environmental** | Carbon emissions, forest cover, air quality index, water quality | Environmental health |
| **Composite** | SDG Index, HDI, Ecological Footprint, GPI | Integrated assessment |

### Key Global Indicators

| Indicator | Measures | Developer |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| **HDI (Human Development Index)** | Health + education + income | UNDP (since 1990) |
| **SDG Index** | Progress on all 17 SDGs | SDSN/Bertelsmann |
| **India SDG Index** | State-wise SDG progress | NITI Aayog |
| **Ecological Footprint** | Human demand on nature vs biocapacity | Global Footprint Network |
| **GPI (Genuine Progress Indicator)** | Economic welfare adjusted for social/environmental costs | Redefining Progress |
| **Carbon footprint** | GHG emissions per capita | Various |
| **Environmental Performance Index (EPI)** | Environmental health + ecosystem vitality | Yale/Columbia |
| **Happy Planet Index** | Well-being per ecological footprint | NEF |

### India SDG Index Indicators (NITI Aayog)

| Category | Sample Indicators |
|----------|------------------|
| **SDG 1** | Poverty headcount ratio, MGNREGA employment |
| **SDG 2** | Malnutrition rate, food production |
| **SDG 3** | Infant mortality, immunisation coverage |
| **SDG 4** | Literacy rate, school enrollment |
| **SDG 6** | Households with tap water, sanitation coverage |
| **SDG 7** | Renewable energy share, electricity access |
| **SDG 13** | CO₂ emissions, climate disaster deaths |
| **SDG 15** | Forest cover, biodiversity indicators |

### HDI Components

| Component | Indicator | Weight |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Health** | Life expectancy at birth | 1/3 |
| **Education** | Mean years of schooling + expected years | 1/3 |
| **Income** | GNI per capita (PPP) | 1/3 |

**HDI range:** 0 (lowest) to 1 (highest). India HDI ~0.64 (medium category).

### GDP vs Sustainable Development Indicators

| Feature | GDP | SD Indicators (HDI, SDG Index) |
|---------|-----|-------------------------------|
| **Measures** | Economic output only | Economic + social + environmental |
| **Environmental damage** | Counted as positive (cleanup adds GDP) | Accounted as negative |
| **Inequality** | Not captured | Gini coefficient, SDG 10 |
| **Well-being** | Not directly measured | HDI, life satisfaction |
| **Sustainability** | Ignores resource depletion | Ecological footprint, carbon |
| **Limitation** | "GDP growth ≠ development" | More holistic but complex |

# Dimensions of Sustainable Development

## Definition

**Dimensions of Sustainable Development** refer to the **interconnected aspects or pillars** that sustainable development must balance — traditionally **economic, social, and environmental dimensions** — ensuring that progress in one dimension does not come at the expense of others.

**Extended dimensions:** Some frameworks add **cultural** and **institutional/governance** dimensions.

## Explanation

### Three Core Dimensions (Triple Bottom Line)

| Dimension | Focus | Key Issues | India Priority |
|-----------|-------|-----------|---------------|
| **Economic** | Production, consumption, employment, innovation | GDP growth, poverty, employment, infrastructure | Make in India, Digital India |
| **Social** | Equity, inclusion, health, education, culture | Poverty, inequality, gender, human rights | MGNREGA, Ayushman Bharat, RTE |
| **Environmental** | Ecosystems, resources, pollution, climate | Biodiversity, climate change, water, air | NAPCC, Project Tiger, Swachh Bharat |

### Interrelationship of Dimensions

```
ECONOMIC DIMENSION
  → Provides resources for social programmes and environmental protection
  → BUT unchecked growth degrades environment and increases inequality
        ↓
SOCIAL DIMENSION
  → Educated, healthy population drives economic growth
  → BUT population pressure strains environmental resources
        ↓
ENVIRONMENTAL DIMENSION
  → Healthy ecosystems provide economic resources and social well-being
  → BUT environmental protection may limit short-term economic growth
        ↓
BALANCE = Sustainable Development (all three in harmony)
```

### Economic Dimension — Details

| Aspect | Sustainable Approach | Unsustainable Approach |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Growth** | Inclusive, green growth | GDP-only, polluting growth |
| **Employment** | Green jobs, decent work | Exploitative labour |
| **Technology** | Clean, renewable technology | Polluting, resource-intensive |
| **Trade** | Fair, sustainable trade | Resource extraction for export |
| **Investment** | Green finance, ESG investing | Short-term profit maximisation |
| **India example** | Solar industry, EV manufacturing | Coal-only energy path |

### Social Dimension — Details

| Aspect | Sustainable Approach | Unsustainable Approach |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Poverty** | Social protection, empowerment | Wealth concentration |
| **Education** | Universal quality education | Education for elite only |
| **Health** | Universal healthcare | Healthcare as luxury |
| **Gender** | Gender equality and empowerment | Gender discrimination |
| **Equity** | Reduced inequalities | Growing rich-poor gap |
| **India example** | Ayushman Bharat, Beti Bachao | Manual scavenging (being eliminated) |

### Environmental Dimension — Details

| Aspect | Sustainable Approach | Unsustainable Approach |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Resources** | Renewable, efficient use | Over-exploitation |
| **Pollution** | Prevention, clean production | Pollute and cleanup later |
| **Biodiversity** | Conservation, protected areas | Habitat destruction |
| **Climate** | Mitigation and adaptation | Business-as-usual emissions |
| **Waste** | Reduce, reuse, recycle | Linear take-make-dispose |
| **India example** | Green India Mission, Net Zero 2070 | Singrauli coal expansion |

### Extended Dimensions (5-Dimension Model)

| Dimension | Focus | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **Economic** | Prosperity, employment | Green GDP |
| **Social** | Equity, inclusion | Gender equality index |
| **Environmental** | Ecosystem health | Carbon footprint |
| **Cultural** | Heritage, identity, diversity | UNESCO heritage protection |
| **Institutional** | Governance, rule of law, participation | EIA, NGT, transparency |

### Dimensions and SDGs Mapping

| Dimension | Related SDGs |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Economic** | SDG 1, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12 |
| **Social** | SDG 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 16 |
| **Environmental** | SDG 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 |
| **Cross-cutting** | SDG 17 (partnerships) |

### Trade-offs and Synergies

| Trade-off | Synergy |
|-----------|---------|
| Economic growth vs environmental protection | Green technology creates jobs AND protects environment |
| Development vs conservation | Ecotourism generates income AND protects biodiversity |
| Urbanisation vs agriculture | Smart cities reduce per-capita resource use |
| Energy access vs climate | Solar provides energy AND reduces emissions |

## Important Facts

- **Three dimensions** — Economic, Social, Environmental (triple bottom line).
- **Triple bottom line** — People, Planet, Profit (3Ps).
- **Interconnected** — neglecting one dimension undermines others.
- **5-dimension model** — adds Cultural and Institutional dimensions.
- **Brundtland Report** — established three-dimensional framework.
- **SDGs** — cover all three dimensions across 17 goals.
- **India challenge** — balancing rapid economic growth with environmental protection.
- **Green growth** — economic dimension aligned with environmental dimension.

# Brundtland Report

## Definition

The **Brundtland Report** (officially **"Our Common Future"**) is the **landmark 1987 report** of the **World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED)** — chaired by **Norwegian Prime Minister Gro Harlem Brundtland** — which **coined and defined the term "sustainable development"** and laid the foundation for the **Rio Earth Summit (1992)** and subsequent global environmental governance.

## Explanation

### Brundtland Report — Key Details

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Official title** | "Our Common Future" |
| **Published** | 1987 |
| **Commission** | World Commission on Environment and Development (WCED) |
| **Chairperson** | **Gro Harlem Brundtland** (Norway PM) |
| **UN mandate** | 1983 — UN General Assembly resolution |
| **Key contribution** | Defined "sustainable development" |
| **Impact** | Led to Rio Earth Summit 1992; Agenda 21; SDGs |

### WCED (World Commission on Environment and Development)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1983 by UN General Assembly |
| **Also known as** | Brundtland Commission |
| **Members** | 22 commissioners from 22 countries |
| **Chair** | Gro Harlem Brundtland |
| **Duration** | 1984–1987 |
| **Mandate** | Propose long-term strategies for sustainable development |
| **Report** | "Our Common Future" (1987) |

### Key Recommendations of Brundtland Report

| Recommendation | Detail |
|---------------|--------|
| **Define sustainable development** | "Meet present needs without compromising future generations" |
| **Revive growth** | Economic growth needed in developing countries |
| **Change quality of growth** | Less resource-intensive, more equitable |
| **Population policy** | Sustainable population levels |
| **Food security** | Adequate food for growing population |
| **Species and ecosystems** | Conserve biodiversity |
| **Energy path** | Transition to efficient, renewable energy |
| **Industry** | Cleaner production, less waste |
| **Urbanisation** | Sustainable cities planning |
| **International cooperation** | Global partnership essential |

### Brundtland Definition — Analysis

| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **"Needs of the present"** | Essential needs of world's poor (priority) |
| **"Without compromising"** | Limits imposed by technology and social organisation |
| **"Future generations"** | Inter-generational equity — core principle |
| **"Meet their own needs"** | Same opportunity for future as present enjoys |

### Brundtland Report → Rio Summit Chain

```
BRUNDTLAND REPORT (1987)
  → Defined sustainable development
  → Recommended UN follow-up conference
        ↓
RIO EARTH SUMMIT (1992)
  → Agenda 21 adopted
  → Rio Declaration (27 principles)
  → CBD, UNFCCC signed
        ↓
MILLENNIUM SUMMIT (2000)
  → MDGs (8 goals)
        ↓
RIO+20 (2012)
  → SDGs framework proposed
        ↓
SDGs ADOPTED (2015)
  → 17 goals, 2030 deadline
```

| Pillar | Report's Emphasis |
|--------|------------------|
| **Economic** | Revive growth; change its quality; technology reorientation |
| **Social** | Meet essential needs; population policy; equity |
| **Environmental** | Conserve species/ecosystems; resource management; merge env+economics |

| Impact | Detail |

| **Political** | Led to Rio Summit 1992 |

| **Policy** | National sustainable development strategies worldwide |
| **Academic** | New field of sustainability science |
| **Current** | Foundation for SDGs 2030 Agenda |
| **India** | Influenced NAPCC, environmental policy framework |

### Criticisms of Brundtland Report

| Criticism | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Too vague** | Definition allows multiple interpretations |
| **Growth-centric** | Still emphasises economic growth |
| **Weak enforcement** | Recommendations non-binding |
| **Northern bias** | Commissioners mostly from developed nations |
| **Inadequate on consumption** | Does not challenge over-consumption in rich countries |
| **Population focus** | Critics say it blames developing world population |

# Environmental Governance

## Definition

**Environmental Governance** refers to the **systems, institutions, processes, and policies** through which **society manages environmental resources and addresses environmental challenges** — involving **government, civil society, private sector, and international organisations** in **decision-making, regulation, enforcement, and accountability**.

**Goal:** Ensure **sustainable management** of environment through **transparent, participatory, and accountable** institutions.

## Explanation

### Levels of Environmental Governance

| Level | Institutions | Examples |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Global** | UNEP, UNFCCC, CBD, IPCC | Paris Agreement, Montreal Protocol |
| **National** | MoEFCC, CPCB, NGT, Parliament | EPA 1986, EIA Notification |
| **State** | SPCB, State Forest Departments | State pollution control |
| **Local** | Municipal corporations, panchayats | Waste management, local EIA |
| **Community** | Gram sabhas, SHGs, NGOs | JFM, community conservation |

### Principles of Good Environmental Governance

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Rule of law** | Environmental laws enforced equally |
| **Transparency** | Open access to environmental information |
| **Participation** | Public involvement in decisions (EIA hearings) |
| **Accountability** | Government answerable for environmental outcomes |
| **Responsiveness** | Institutions respond to environmental needs |
| **Equity** | Fair distribution of environmental benefits/burdens |
| **Integration** | Environment in all sectoral policies |
| **Precautionary principle** | Prevent harm despite scientific uncertainty |

# Environmental Justice

## Definition

**Environmental Justice** is the **fair treatment and meaningful involvement of all people** regardless of race, colour, national origin, or income — in the **development, implementation, and enforcement of environmental laws, regulations, and policies** — ensuring **no group bears a disproportionate share of negative environmental consequences**.

**Core principle:** **Equitable distribution** of environmental benefits and burdens.

## Explanation

### Dimensions of Environmental Justice

| Dimension | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Distributive justice** | Fair distribution of environmental benefits/burdens | Polluting industries not only in poor areas |
| **Procedural justice** | Fair decision-making processes | Public hearings in EIA accessible to all |
| **Recognition justice** | Acknowledging different community values | Tribal forest rights recognised |
| **Inter-generational justice** | Fairness to future generations | Brundtland inter-generational equity |

# Environmental Ethics

## Definition

**Environmental Ethics** is the **branch of philosophy that studies the moral relationship between humans and the natural environment** — examining **ethical obligations toward nature, other species, and future generations**, and whether **nature has intrinsic value** independent of human utility.

**Central question:** *"What moral obligations do humans have toward the environment?"*

## Explanation

### Approaches to Environmental Ethics

| Approach | Core Belief | Proponents |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| **Anthropocentrism** | Only humans have intrinsic value; nature is valuable only for human use | Traditional Western ethics |
| **Biocentrism (Biocentric)** | All living beings have intrinsic value | Albert Schweitzer, Paul Taylor |
| **Ecocentrism (Ecocentric)** | Whole ecosystems have intrinsic value | Aldo Leopold, Arne Næss |
| **Deep Ecology** | Nature has inherent worth; radical restructuring of society needed | Arne Næss, Devall & Sessions |
| **Shallow Ecology** | Environmental protection for human benefit only | Reformist conservation |
| **Ecofeminism** | Environmental degradation linked to patriarchal domination | Vandana Shiva, Maria Mies |
| **Land Ethic** | "A thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity of the biotic community" | Aldo Leopold (1949) |

### Anthropocentrism vs Ecocentrism

| Feature | Anthropocentrism | Ecocentrism |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Value centre** | Human beings | Ecosystems/nature |
| **Nature's value** | Instrumental (useful to humans) | Intrinsic (valuable in itself) |
| **Conservation motive** | Protect nature for human benefit | Protect nature for its own sake |
| **Species extinction** | Bad only if affects humans | Bad regardless of human impact |
| **Development** | Human needs paramount | Ecological limits paramount |
| **India tradition** | Less dominant | Hindu/Buddhist/Jain traditions closer |

### Key Environmental Ethical Principles

| Principle | Meaning | Application |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Intrinsic value of nature** | Nature valuable independent of human use | Protect species regardless of utility |
| **Inter-generational equity** | Duties to future generations | Sustainable development |
| **Precautionary principle** | Act to prevent harm despite uncertainty | Ban untested chemicals |
| **Polluter pays** | Moral duty to bear pollution costs | Industrial liability |
| **Stewardship** | Humans as caretakers of Earth | Conservation responsibility |
| **Ahimsa (non-violence)** | Minimise harm to all living beings | Jain, Buddhist, Gandhian ethics |
| **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** | "World is one family" | Indian holistic ethics |

### Indian Environmental Ethics Tradition

| Tradition | Ethical Teaching | Environmental Link |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Hinduism** | Sacredness of rivers, trees, animals | Ganga worship, sacred groves, cow protection |
| **Buddhism** | Ahimsa; interdependence of all life | Compassion for all beings |
| **Jainism** | Strict non-violence; soul in all life | Most rigorous environmental ethic |
| **Gandhian ethics** | Simple living, trusteeship, village economy | Limits to consumption |
| **Vedic philosophy** | Panch Mahabhuta (five elements) | Holistic view of nature |
| **Chipko Movement** | "Ecology is permanent economy" | Sundarlal Bahuguna |
| **Vandana Shiva** | Ecofeminism; seed sovereignty | Biodiversity ethics |

### Deep Ecology — Arne Næss (1973)

| Platform | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **1. Life affirmation** | All life has intrinsic value |
| **2. Diversity** | Richness and diversity have intrinsic value |
| **3. Vital needs** | Humans have no right to reduce diversity except for vital needs |
| **4. Population** | Human population must decrease |
| **5. Lifestyle change** | Significant change in life conditions required |
| **6. Policy change** | Must change policies affecting environment |
| **7. Appreciation** | Appreciate life quality over material standard |
| **8. Obligation** | Those who agree must implement changes |

### Environmental Ethics and Law

| Ethical Principle | Legal Expression |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Right to environment** | Art 21 (SC expansion) |
| **Duties to environment** | Art 51A(g) |
| **State's duty** | Art 48A |

| **Tribal rights** | Forest Rights Act 2006 |
| **Polluter pays** | EPA 1986; *Vellore Citizens* case |
| **Precautionary principle** | Rio Declaration Principle 15; SC adoption |

# Green Economy

## Definition

The **Green Economy** is an economy that results in **improved human well-being and social equity**, while significantly **reducing environmental risks and ecological scarcities** — characterised by **low carbon, resource efficient, and socially inclusive** growth.

**UNEP definition (2011):** *"One that results in improved human well-being and social equity, while significantly reducing environmental risks and ecological scarcities."*

## Explanation

### Green Economy vs Brown Economy

| Feature | Brown Economy (Traditional) | Green Economy |
|---------|---------------------------|---------------|
| **Energy** | Fossil fuels (coal, oil) | Renewables (solar, wind) |
| **Resources** | Extractive, linear use | Efficient, circular use |
| **Waste** | Dispose after use | Reduce, reuse, recycle |
| **Jobs** | Polluting industries | Green jobs |
| **Growth measure** | GDP only | GDP + environmental/social indicators |
| **Pollution** | External cost ignored | Polluter pays; internalised |
| **India example** | Coal-dominated power | Solar/wind expansion |

# Circular Economy

## Definition

The **Circular Economy** is an economic system aimed at **eliminating waste and the continual use of resources** through **reuse, sharing, repair, refurbishment, remanufacturing, and recycling** — creating a **closed-loop system** that minimises resource input, waste, emissions, and energy leakage.

**Opposite of:** **Linear economy** — "take, make, dispose" model.

**Ellen MacArthur Foundation definition:** *"An industrial system that is restorative or regenerative by intention and design."*

## Explanation

### Linear vs Circular Economy

| Feature | Linear Economy | Circular Economy |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Model** | Take → Make → Use → Dispose | Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover |
| **Resources** | Extract virgin materials | Keep materials in use |
| **Waste** | End-of-pipe disposal | Designed out of system |
| **Value** | Products sold once | Multiple value cycles |
| **Energy** | Fossil fuel intensive | Renewable powered |
| **Design** | Planned obsolescence | Design for durability/repair |
| **Example** | Single-use plastic bottle | Refillable glass bottle system |

### Principles of Circular Economy (Ellen MacArthur Foundation)

| Principle | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **1. Design out waste** | Waste = design flaw; eliminate at source |
| **2. Keep products in use** | Maintain, repair, reuse, remanufacture |
| **3. Regenerate natural systems** | Return biological nutrients to nature |
| **4. Use renewable energy** | Power circular systems with clean energy |
| **5. Think in systems** | Whole-system approach, not single product |

### Circular Economy Strategies (R-Framework)

| Strategy | Action | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **R0 Refuse** | Don't use unnecessary products | Ban single-use plastic |
| **R1 Rethink** | Rethink product/service model | Product-as-a-service (leasing) |
| **R2 Reduce** | Minimise resource use | Lightweight packaging |
| **R3 Reuse** | Use again without processing | Refillable containers |
| **R4 Repair** | Fix broken products | Right to repair movement |
| **R5 Refurbish** | Restore to good condition | Refurbished electronics |
| **R6 Remanufacture** | Rebuild to original specs | Remanufactured engines |
| **R7 Recycle** | Process into new materials | PET bottle to polyester |
| **R8 Recover** | Extract energy from waste | Waste-to-energy plants |
| **R9 Repurpose** | Use for different function | Pallet to furniture |

### Circular Economy in India

| Sector | Circular Approach | Initiative |
|--------|------------------|-----------|
| **Plastic** | Ban single-use; EPR | PWM Rules 2016; SUP ban 2022 |
| **E-waste** | EPR, recycling | E-waste Rules 2016; 2022 amendment |
| **Construction** | Recycled aggregates | C&D Waste Rules 2016 |
| **Agriculture** | Composting, biogas | Gobardhan scheme |
| **Textiles** | Recycling, upcycling | Emerging startup sector |
| **Water** | Recycling, rainwater harvesting | Jal Shakti Mission |
| **Automobiles** | Vehicle scrappage | Vehicle Scrappage Policy 2021 |

### Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Concept** | Producers responsible for product's end-of-life |
| **Legal basis** | PWM Rules 2016; E-waste Rules 2016; Battery Rules 2022 |
| **Mechanism** | Producers register; meet collection/recycling targets |
| **CPCB** | Monitors EPR compliance |
| **Benefit** | Shifts waste management cost to producers |
| **India status** | EPR framework for plastic, e-waste, batteries, tyres |

### India's Circular Economy Policies

| Policy | Circular Element |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016** | EPR for plastic producers |
| **E-waste Management Rules 2016** | EPR for electronics manufacturers |
| **C&D Waste Rules 2016** | Construction waste recycling |
| **Vehicle Scrappage Policy 2021** | End-of-life vehicle recycling |
| **Battery Waste Rules 2022** | EPR for battery producers |
| **Gobardhan Scheme** | Biogas from organic waste |
| **Swachh Bharat** | Waste segregation at source |

### Circular Economy Benefits

| Benefit | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Resource security** | Less dependence on virgin material imports |
| **Waste reduction** | Less landfill, incineration needed |
| **Emission reduction** | Lower carbon from reduced extraction/production |
| **Job creation** | Repair, recycling, remanufacturing jobs |
| **Cost savings** | Reduced material costs for industry |
| **Innovation** | New business models (sharing, leasing) |
| **SDG alignment** | SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption) |

### Circular Economy Challenges in India

| Challenge | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Informal sector** | Most recycling done informally (kabadiwala) |
| **EPR compliance** | Producers often fail to meet targets |
| **Infrastructure** | Inadequate formal recycling facilities |
| **Consumer awareness** | Low awareness of circular practices |
| **Design** | Products not designed for circularity |
| **Data gaps** | Material flow data inadequate |
| **Policy enforcement** | Rules exist but weak enforcement |
