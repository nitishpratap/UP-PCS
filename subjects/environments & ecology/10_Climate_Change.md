# Global Warming

## Definition

**Global Warming** is the **long-term increase in Earth's average surface temperature** due primarily to **anthropogenic emissions of greenhouse gases** (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, etc.) that trap heat in the atmosphere.

**IPCC definition:** *"Global warming refers to the increase in global surface temperature relative to a baseline reference period, due to human activities that increase greenhouse gas concentrations."*

**Distinct from weather** — global warming is a **climate trend** measured over decades; weather is short-term atmospheric conditions.

## Explanation

### Global Warming vs Climate Change

| Feature | Global Warming | Climate Change |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| **Focus** | Temperature rise | Broader — temperature, rainfall, sea level, extremes |
| **Cause** | GHG emissions | GHGs + land-use change + aerosols |
| **Scale** | Global average temperature | Regional and global patterns |
| **Relationship** | Component of climate change | Umbrella term including warming |

### Causes of Global Warming

| Cause | Mechanism | Contribution |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| **Fossil fuel combustion** | CO₂ release | ~75% of GHG emissions |
| **Deforestation** | Reduced CO₂ absorption | ~10–15% |
| **Agriculture** | CH₄ (rice, livestock), N₂O (fertilisers) | ~25% of total emissions |
| **Industrial processes** | Cement, chemicals, refrigerants | Significant CO₂ + HFCs |
| **Land-use change** | Albedo change, carbon release | Amplifies warming |
| **Black carbon** | Absorbs solar radiation | Regional warming (Himalayas) |

### Global Temperature Trends

| Period | Temperature Change | Source |
|--------|-------------------|--------|
| **Pre-industrial (1850–1900)** | Baseline | IPCC |
| **20th century** | +0.6°C | IPCC AR6 |
| **1971–2020** | +0.85°C above pre-industrial | IPCC AR6 |
| **2011–2020** | Warmest decade on record | WMO |
| **2023** | ~1.45°C above pre-industrial | WMO/Copernicus |
| **IPCC projection (2100)** | +1.5°C to +4.4°C (scenario-dependent) | IPCC AR6 |

### Global Warming Mechanism

```
FOSSIL FUELS + DEFORESTATION + AGRICULTURE
        ↓
GHG EMISSIONS (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs)
        ↓
GREENHOUSE EFFECT (heat trapped in atmosphere)
        ↓
RISING GLOBAL MEAN SURFACE TEMPERATURE
        ↓
CLIMATE CHANGE IMPACTS (glaciers, sea level, extremes)
```

# Greenhouse Effect

## Definition

The **Greenhouse Effect** is the **natural process by which certain gases in Earth's atmosphere trap outgoing infrared (heat) radiation**, warming the planet to a habitable average temperature of about **15°C** (without it, Earth would be **−18°C**).

**Enhanced Greenhouse Effect** = **anthropogenic increase in GHG concentrations** intensifying this natural process → global warming.

**NCERT definition:** *"The greenhouse effect is a naturally occurring phenomenon that is responsible for heating the Earth's surface and atmosphere."*

## Explanation

### Natural vs Enhanced Greenhouse Effect

| Feature | Natural | Enhanced (Anthropogenic) |
|---------|---------|--------------------------|
| **GHG source** | Water vapour, natural CO₂ cycles | Fossil fuels, deforestation, industry |
| **Temperature** | ~15°C average (habitable) | Additional +1.1°C and rising |
| **Balance** | Radiation in = radiation out | More heat trapped than released |
| **Result** | Life-sustaining climate | Global warming, climate change |

### How the Greenhouse Effect Works

```
SOLAR RADIATION (shortwave) → passes through atmosphere → heats Earth's surface
        ↓
EARTH EMITS INFRARED RADIATION (longwave/heat)
        ↓
GHGs ABSORB AND RE-EMIT IR RADIATION → heat trapped in lower atmosphere
        ↓
SURFACE TEMPERATURE RISES (Greenhouse Effect)
```

### Major Greenhouse Gases and Warming Potential

| Gas | Natural Source | Anthropogenic Source | GWP (100-yr) |
|-----|---------------|---------------------|--------------|
| **CO₂** | Respiration, volcanoes | Fossil fuels, deforestation | 1 (reference) |
| **CH₄ (Methane)** | Wetlands, termites | Livestock, rice, landfills, gas leaks | 27–30 |
| **N₂O** | Soil bacteria | Fertilisers, combustion | 273 |
| **Water vapour (H₂O)** | Evaporation | Feedback gas (not direct emission) | Feedback |
| **CFCs/HFCs** | None natural | Refrigerants, aerosols | 1,000–14,000 |
| **O₃ (Ozone)** | Stratosphere | Tropospheric pollution | Variable |

### Radiative Forcing

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Radiative forcing** | Change in energy balance at top of atmosphere (W/m²) |
| **Positive forcing** | Warming effect (GHGs) |
| **Negative forcing** | Cooling effect (aerosols, sulphates) |
| **CO₂ forcing** | Largest positive forcing agent |
| **Aerosol masking** | Air pollution partially offsets warming |

### Factors Affecting Greenhouse Effect Intensity

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **GHG concentration** | Higher = stronger effect |
| **Atmospheric lifetime** | Longer-lived gases = sustained effect (CO₂: 100–1000 yrs) |
| **Feedback loops** | Water vapour, ice-albedo, permafrost methane |
| **Cloud cover** | Complex — low clouds cool, high clouds warm |
| **Deforestation** | Less CO₂ absorption; reduced transpiration |

### Greenhouse Effect vs Ozone Depletion

| Feature | Greenhouse Effect | Ozone Depletion |
|---------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Gases** | CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs | CFCs, halons |
| **Layer** | Troposphere (mainly) | Stratosphere |
| **Effect** | Warming | UV radiation increase |
| **Protocol** | UNFCCC, Paris | Montreal Protocol |
| **Linkage** | CFCs are both ozone-depleting AND potent GHGs | |

## Important Facts

- **Without greenhouse effect** — Earth temperature would be **−18°C** (uninhabitable).
- **Natural greenhouse effect** maintains **+15°C** average temperature.
- **Water vapour** — most abundant GHG (~60% of natural effect) but **feedback gas**, not primary driver.
- **CO₂** — primary anthropogenic driver of enhanced greenhouse effect.
- **Enhanced effect** — responsible for ~1.1°C additional warming since pre-industrial.
- **Arrhenius (1896)** — first calculated CO₂-temperature relationship.
- **Tyndall (1859)** — demonstrated gases absorb infrared radiation.
- **Radiative forcing of CO₂** — ~1.8 W/m² (IPCC AR6).

# Greenhouse Gases (GHGs)

## Definition

**Greenhouse Gases (GHGs)** are **gaseous constituents of the atmosphere, both natural and anthropogenic, that absorb and emit infrared radiation** at specific wavelengths, causing the **greenhouse effect** and global warming.

**Kyoto Protocol GHGs:** CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, SF₆, HFCs, PFCs.  
**UNFCCC also tracks:** Water vapour, ozone, CFCs, aerosols.

## Explanation

### Major Greenhouse Gases — Detailed Profile

| Gas | Formula | Pre-industrial | Current (approx.) | Lifetime | GWP (100-yr) |
|-----|---------|---------------|-------------------|----------|--------------|
| **Carbon dioxide** | CO₂ | 280 ppm | 420+ ppm | 100–1000 yrs | 1 |
| **Methane** | CH₄ | 722 ppb | 1,900+ ppb | 12 yrs | 27–30 |
| **Nitrous oxide** | N₂O | 270 ppb | 336 ppb | 114 yrs | 273 |
| **CFC-12** | CCl₂F₂ | 0 | Phased out | 100 yrs | 10,900 |
| **HFC-134a** | CH₂FCF₃ | 0 | Rising | 14 yrs | 1,530 |
| **SF₆** | SF₆ | 0 | Rising | 3,200 yrs | 25,200 |
| **Water vapour** | H₂O | Variable | Feedback | Days | Feedback |

### Sources and Sinks of Major GHGs

| Gas | Major Sources | Major Sinks |
|-----|--------------|-------------|
| **CO₂** | Fossil fuels, cement, deforestation | Oceans, forests, soil (photosynthesis) |
| **CH₄** | Rice paddies, livestock, landfills, natural gas leaks | Atmospheric oxidation (OH radicals) |
| **N₂O** | Fertilisers, fossil fuel combustion, biomass burning | Soil bacteria (denitrification) |
| **HFCs** | Refrigeration, AC, foam blowing | Minimal natural sinks |
| **PFCs** | Aluminium smelting, semiconductors | No significant sinks |
| **SF₆** | Electrical switchgear, magnesium industry | No significant sinks |

### Global GHG Emissions by Gas (2023 approx.)

| Gas | Share of Total Emissions | Sector Dominance |
|-----|-------------------------|------------------|
| **CO₂** | ~74% | Energy, industry, transport |
| **CH₄** | ~17% | Agriculture, energy, waste |
| **N₂O** | ~6% | Agriculture (fertilisers) |
| **F-gases (HFCs, PFCs, SF₆)** | ~2% | Refrigeration, industry |
| **Other** | ~1% | Black carbon, tropospheric ozone |

### India's GHG Emissions Profile

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Total emissions** | ~2.9 GtCO₂e/year (3rd globally) |
| **Per capita** | ~2.4 tCO₂e/person |
| **CO₂ share** | ~70% of total emissions |
| **CH₄ share** | ~20% (livestock, rice, waste) |
| **Energy sector** | ~45% of total |
| **AFOLU (agriculture, forestry)** | ~18% |
| **UP contribution** | Significant — coal, agriculture, transport |

### GHG Measurement Units

| Unit | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **ppm** | Parts per million (CO₂) |
| **ppb** | Parts per billion (CH₄, N₂O) |
| **GtCO₂e** | Gigatonnes CO₂ equivalent |
| **tCO₂e** | Tonnes CO₂ equivalent |
| **GWP** | Global Warming Potential — compares gases to CO₂ over 100 years |

### GHG Reporting Framework

```
NATIONAL INVENTORY (UNFCCC Biennial Update Reports)
        ↓
SECTORAL EMISSIONS: Energy | IPPU | AFOLU | Waste
        ↓
GAS-WISE: CO₂ | CH₄ | N₂O | F-gases
        ↓
MITIGATION: NDC targets | Carbon markets | Technology
```

### F-Gases and Montreal/Kigali Protocols

| Gas Class | Use | Protocol | Status |
|-----------|-----|----------|--------|
| **CFCs** | Old refrigerants | Montreal (1987) | Phased out globally |
| **HCFCs** | Transitional refrigerants | Montreal | Phase-out ongoing |
| **HFCs** | Modern refrigerants | Kigali (2016) | Phase-down by 2047 (India) |
| **SF₆** | Electrical insulation | Voluntary reduction | High GWP concern |

## Important Facts

- **CO₂** — most important anthropogenic GHG (~74% of emissions).
- **CH₄** — 80× more potent than CO₂ over 20 years; 27–30× over 100 years.
- **N₂O** — 273× GWP; mainly from agricultural fertilisers.
- **SF₆** — most potent GHG tracked (GWP 25,200); used in switchgear.
- **India ratified Kigali Amendment (2021)** — HFC phase-down.
- **Global Methane Pledge** — 30% CH₄ cut by 2030 (India not signatory initially).
- **GHG Protocol** — standard for corporate/national inventories.
- **Kyoto gases** — 6 gases under Kyoto Protocol (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs, PFCs, SF₆).

# Carbon Footprint

## Definition

**Carbon Footprint** is the **total amount of greenhouse gases (expressed as CO₂ equivalent) generated directly and indirectly** by an individual, organisation, event, product, or nation through its activities and consumption patterns.

**Formula concept:** Carbon Footprint = **Direct emissions (Scope 1) + Indirect emissions (Scope 2 + Scope 3)**

**NCERT context:** *"The carbon footprint measures the impact human activities have on the environment in terms of the amount of greenhouse gases produced, measured in units of carbon dioxide."*

## Explanation

### Scopes of Carbon Footprint (GHG Protocol)

| Scope | Definition | Examples |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Scope 1 (Direct)** | Emissions from owned/controlled sources | Factory boilers, company vehicles, on-site fuel |
| **Scope 2 (Indirect — Energy)** | Emissions from purchased electricity/heat | Grid electricity consumption |
| **Scope 3 (Indirect — Value chain)** | All other indirect emissions | Supply chain, commuting, product use, waste |

### Carbon Footprint by Sector (Global)

| Sector | Share of Global Footprint | Key Activities |
|--------|--------------------------|----------------|
| **Energy** | ~35% | Coal, oil, gas combustion |
| **Industry** | ~20% | Cement, steel, chemicals |
| **Transport** | ~15% | Road, aviation, shipping |
| **Agriculture** | ~12% | Livestock, fertilisers, rice |
| **Buildings** | ~10% | Heating, cooling, lighting |
| **Waste** | ~3% | Landfills, incineration |
| **Land-use change** | ~5% | Deforestation |

# Carbon Sink

## Definition

A **Carbon Sink** is any **natural or artificial reservoir that accumulates and stores carbon-containing chemical compounds**, thereby **removing CO₂ from the atmosphere** and offsetting greenhouse gas emissions.

**Natural sinks:** Forests, oceans, soil, wetlands.  
**Artificial sinks:** Carbon capture and storage (CCS), biochar, enhanced weathering.

**Opposite:** **Carbon Source** — releases more CO₂ than it absorbs (fossil fuel burning, deforestation).

## Explanation

### Major Natural Carbon Sinks

| Sink | Carbon Storage | Absorption Rate | Mechanism |
|------|---------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Oceans** | ~38,000 GtC (largest) | ~25% of annual emissions | Dissolution + biological pump |
| **Forests** | ~1,100 GtC | ~30% of annual emissions | Photosynthesis |
| **Soil** | ~2,500 GtC | Significant long-term | Organic matter decomposition |
| **Wetlands/Mangroves** | High per unit area | Coastal carbon sequestration | Peat, root biomass |
| **Grasslands** | Moderate | Savanna, prairie ecosystems | Root carbon storage |

### Global Carbon Cycle — Sources and Sinks

```
SOURCES (Emissions): Fossil fuels + Deforestation + Industry ≈ 40 GtCO₂/year
        ↓
ATMOSPHERE: CO₂ concentration rising (420+ ppm)
        ↓
SINKS (Absorption):
  → Oceans (~25% of emissions absorbed)
  → Land/Terrestrial (~30% absorbed — forests, soil)
  → Remaining ~45% stays in atmosphere
```

### Ocean as Carbon Sink

| Process | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Solubility pump** | CO₂ dissolves in cold surface waters |
| **Biological pump** | Phytoplankton absorb CO₂; sink to ocean floor |
| **Ocean acidification** | CO₂ → carbonic acid; pH declining (threat to sinks) |
| **Warming impact** | Warmer oceans absorb LESS CO₂ (positive feedback) |
| **India's coast** | Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal — significant regional sinks |

### Forest as Carbon Sink

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Tropical forests** | Highest carbon density per hectare |
| **Boreal forests** | Largest total forest carbon store |
| **India's forests** | ~24% forest cover; ~7 billion tonnes carbon stock |
| **Mangroves** | Store 3–5× more carbon per ha than tropical forests |
| **Threat** | Deforestation converts sink → source |

### India's Carbon Sinks

| Sink | Status | Detail |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Forests** | Growing sink | +1.5% forest cover increase (ISFR 2021) |
| **Mangroves** | Important coastal sink | Sundarbans, Pichavaram, Bhitarkanika |
| **Himalayan forests** | Major terrestrial sink | UP, Uttarakhand, NE states |
| **Soil carbon** | Underutilised sink | Conservation agriculture potential |
| **Green India Mission** | Enhancing forest sink | 5 million ha afforestation target |
| **UP** | Moderate sink | Terai forests, plantation drives |

### Carbon Sink vs Carbon Source

| Feature | Carbon Sink | Carbon Source |
|---------|------------|---------------|
| **Net flow** | Absorbs > releases | Releases > absorbs |
| **Examples** | Growing forest, ocean | Coal plant, deforested land |
| **Climate role** | Mitigates warming | Accelerates warming |
| **Measurement** | Net ecosystem production (NEP) positive | NEP negative |

### Threats to Carbon Sinks

| Threat | Impact on Sink |
|--------|---------------|
| **Deforestation** | Forest sink → source (land-use change emissions) |
| **Ocean warming** | Reduced CO₂ solubility |
| **Ocean acidification** | Damages marine carbon pump |
| **Permafrost thaw** | Releases stored methane and CO₂ |
| **Forest fires** | Releases stored carbon (increasing with warming) |
| **Soil degradation** | Reduces soil carbon storage |
| **Invasive species** | Alters ecosystem carbon dynamics |

### Artificial Carbon Sinks

| Technology | Mechanism | Status |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Afforestation/Reforestation** | Plant trees | Active globally (India CAMPA fund) |
| **Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS)** | Capture CO₂ at source; store underground | Pilot projects |
| **Biochar** | Charcoal added to soil | Emerging |
| **Enhanced weathering** | Crushed minerals absorb CO₂ | Research stage |
| **Direct Air Capture (DAC)** | Machines extract CO₂ from air | Very expensive, nascent |

## Important Facts

- **Oceans** — largest carbon sink (~38,000 GtC stored).
- **Forests** absorb ~30% of annual human CO₂ emissions.
- **Oceans absorb ~25%** of annual emissions.
- **~45% of emissions** remain in atmosphere annually.
- **India's forest carbon stock** — ~7.2 billion tonnes (ISFR 2021).
- **Mangroves** — "blue carbon" ecosystems; highest per-hectare storage.
- **Amazon** — was carbon sink; now near net-zero (deforestation + drought).
- **Sundarbans** — largest mangrove carbon sink in India.
- **Deforestation** — converts forest from sink to source (~10% of global emissions).

# Carbon Sequestration

## Definition

**Carbon Sequestration** is the **process of capturing and storing atmospheric carbon dioxide** in **long-term reservoirs** (terrestrial, oceanic, or geological) to **mitigate climate change** by reducing the concentration of CO₂ in the atmosphere.

**Types:** **Biological** (photosynthesis, soil carbon), **Geological** (CCS — underground storage), **Industrial** (DAC, mineral carbonation).

## Explanation

### Types of Carbon Sequestration

| Type | Mechanism | Storage Duration | Examples |
|------|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| **Biological (Biosequestration)** | Photosynthesis, soil organic matter | Decades to centuries | Afforestation, regenerative agriculture |
| **Geological** | Compressed CO₂ injected underground | Millennia | CCS in depleted oil/gas fields |
| **Oceanic** | Iron fertilisation, alkalinity enhancement | Variable | Research stage |
| **Industrial (DAC)** | Chemical capture from ambient air | Depends on storage | Climeworks, Carbon Engineering |
| **Mineral carbonation** | CO₂ reacts with minerals → carbonates | Permanent | Basalt rock storage |

### Biological Carbon Sequestration

| Method | Mechanism | Carbon Storage | India Example |
|--------|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| **Afforestation** | Tree planting absorbs CO₂ | Above + below ground biomass | Green India Mission |
| **Reforestation** | Restoring degraded forests | Forest biomass + soil carbon | CAMPA-funded projects |
| **Agroforestry** | Trees on farmland | Soil + biomass carbon | UP agroforestry programmes |
| **Soil carbon sequestration** | Conservation tillage, cover crops | Soil organic carbon | Zero-tillage in Punjab |
| **Wetland restoration** | Peat and organic matter accumulation | High per-unit area | Chilika, Loktak restoration |
| **Mangrove plantation** | Coastal blue carbon sequestration | Root + sediment carbon | Sundarbans afforestation |
| **Biochar application** | Charcoal stabilises soil carbon | Centuries in soil | Pilot projects |

### Geological Carbon Sequestration (CCS)

| Component | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Capture** | CO₂ separated at power plant/industry source |
| **Transport** | Pipeline to storage site |
| **Storage** | Depleted oil/gas reservoirs, saline aquifers, unmineable coal seams |
| **Monitoring** | Ensure no leakage (seismic, well monitoring) |
| **India status** | Pilot projects; not yet commercial scale |
| **Challenges** | High cost, storage site identification, public acceptance |

### Carbon Sequestration Potential in India

| Sector | Potential (MtCO₂/year) | Method |
|--------|----------------------|--------|
| **Forestry** | 200–300 | Afforestation, REDD+ |
| **Agriculture** | 50–100 | Soil carbon, agroforestry |
| **Coastal/mangrove** | 10–20 | Blue carbon restoration |
| **Industrial CCS** | 50+ (potential) | Cement, steel, power plants |
| **Total NDC target** | 2.5–3 GtCO₂e additional sink by 2030 | Combined strategies |

### Carbon Sequestration vs Carbon Capture

| Feature | Carbon Sequestration | Carbon Capture |
|---------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Scope** | Storage in long-term reservoirs | Capture at emission source |
| **Includes** | Biological + geological + oceanic | Mainly industrial/geological |
| **Natural process** | Yes (forests, oceans) | No (technology-based) |
| **CCS** | Capture + Sequestration combined | Only capture step |
| **Cost** | Biological = low cost; DAC = very high | Industrial CCS = moderate-high |

### Enhanced Carbon Sequestration Techniques

| Technique | Detail | Status |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **REDD+** | Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and forest Degradation | India implementing |
| **Afforestation bonds** | Finance tree planting via green bonds | Emerging |
| **Carbon farming** | Farmers paid for soil carbon storage | Pilot in India |
| **Bioenergy with CCS (BECCS)** | Biomass energy + carbon capture | Research |
| **Iron fertilisation** | Stimulate phytoplankton CO₂ uptake | Controversial |

## Important Facts

- **Photosynthesis** — primary natural carbon sequestration mechanism.
- **1 hectare of forest** — sequesters ~5–10 tonnes CO₂/year.
- **India's NDC** — create additional 2.5–3 GtCO₂e carbon sink by 2030.
- **Mangroves** — sequester 3–5× more carbon per ha than terrestrial forests.
- **Soil holds 3× more carbon** than atmosphere and 2× more than vegetation.
- **CCS technology** — captures up to 90% of CO₂ from power plants.
- **DAC cost** — ~$600–1000/tonne CO₂ (declining with technology).
- **REDD+** — UNFCCC mechanism for forest carbon sequestration credits.
- **Green India Mission** — target 5 million ha afforestation.

# Climate Change Impacts

## Definition

**Climate Change Impacts** are the **effects of long-term changes in climate patterns** — including rising temperatures, altered precipitation, sea-level rise, and increased extreme weather — on **natural systems, human societies, economies, and ecosystems**.

**IPCC AR6:** *"Human-induced climate change is already affecting many weather and climate extremes in every region across the globe."*

## Explanation

### Categories of Climate Change Impacts

| Category | Examples | Severity |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Physical** | Temperature rise, sea-level rise, glacier melt | Global |
| **Ecological** | Species extinction, coral bleaching, range shifts | High |
| **Hydrological** | Droughts, floods, groundwater depletion | Regional |
| **Agricultural** | Crop yield changes, pest outbreaks | High for India |
| **Health** | Heat stress, vector-borne diseases, malnutrition | Increasing |
| **Economic** | Infrastructure damage, insurance losses | Trillions USD |
| **Social** | Migration, conflict, inequality | Disproportionate on poor |

### Physical Impacts

| Impact | Current Status | Projection (2100) |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Global temperature rise** | +1.1°C above pre-industrial | +1.5°C to +4.4°C |
| **Sea-level rise** | ~20 cm since 1900; 3.7 mm/year now | 0.3–1.0 m |
| **Arctic sea ice** | Declining ~13% per decade | Ice-free summers possible |
| **Glacier mass loss** | Himalayan glaciers retreating | 1/3 to 2/3 volume loss |
| **Ocean warming** | 90% of excess heat absorbed by oceans | Marine heatwaves increasing |
| **Permafrost thaw** | Siberia, Arctic releasing methane | Positive feedback loop |

### IPCC Impact Assessment Framework

```
HAZARD (Temperature rise, sea-level, extremes)
        ↓
EXPOSURE (Population, infrastructure in vulnerable areas)
        ↓
VULNERABILITY (Poverty, lack of adaptation capacity)
        ↓
RISK = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability
        ↓
IMPACT: Loss of life | Economic damage | Ecosystem degradation
```

# Climate Adaptation

## Definition

**Climate Adaptation** refers to **adjustments in ecological, social, or economic systems** in response to **actual or expected climate change impacts**, aimed at **moderating harm or exploiting beneficial opportunities**.

**IPCC definition:** *"The process of adjustment to actual or expected climate and its effects."*

**Adaptation vs Mitigation:** Adaptation = **coping with impacts**; Mitigation = **reducing causes** (GHG emissions).

## Explanation

### Adaptation vs Mitigation

| Feature | Adaptation | Mitigation |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **Focus** | Deal with climate impacts | Reduce GHG emissions |
| **Approach** | Reactive + anticipatory | Preventive |
| **Examples** | Drought-resistant crops, sea walls | Renewable energy, afforestation |
| **Timeframe** | Immediate to near-term | Long-term (decades) |
| **Beneficiary** | Local/regional | Global |
| **Paris Agreement** | Adaptation + Loss & Damage | NDC emission reductions |

### Types of Climate Adaptation

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Anticipatory (Proactive)** | Before impacts occur | Climate-resilient infrastructure |
| **Reactive** | After impacts occur | Post-flood rehabilitation |
| **Incremental** | Small adjustments to existing systems | Changing crop planting dates |
| **Transformational** | Fundamental system change | Relocating coastal communities |
| **Structural** | Physical/engineering measures | Sea walls, dams, irrigation |
| **Non-structural** | Policy, institutional, behavioural | Early warning systems, insurance |

### India's Climate Adaptation Framework

```
NATIONAL ACTION PLAN ON CLIMATE CHANGE (NAPCC, 2008)
```

# Climate Mitigation

## Definition

**Climate Mitigation** refers to **human interventions to reduce greenhouse gas emissions or enhance carbon sinks**, thereby **limiting the magnitude of climate change** and its adverse impacts.

**IPCC definition:** *"A human intervention to reduce the sources or enhance the sinks of greenhouse gases."*

**Goal:** Limit global warming to **well below 2°C**, preferably **1.5°C** above pre-industrial levels (Paris Agreement).

## Explanation

### Mitigation Strategies

| Strategy | Mechanism | Examples |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Energy transition** | Shift from fossil fuels to renewables | Solar, wind, green hydrogen |
| **Energy efficiency** | Reduce energy consumption per unit output | PAT scheme, LED bulbs, building codes |
| **Afforestation** | Enhance carbon sinks | Green India Mission, CAMPA |
| **Carbon pricing** | Economic incentive to reduce emissions | Carbon tax, cap-and-trade |
| **Technology innovation** | Low-carbon technologies | EVs, CCS, green steel |
| **Behavioural change** | Reduce consumption emissions | Mission LiFE |
| **Fuel switching** | Coal → gas → renewables | Ujjwala LPG, coal phase-down |
| **Waste management** | Reduce methane from landfills | Waste-to-energy, composting |

### India's Mitigation Commitments (NDCs)

| Target | Original NDC (2015) | Updated NDC (2022) |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Emission intensity reduction** | 33–35% by 2030 (vs 2005) | **45% by 2030** (vs 2005) |
| **Non-fossil fuel capacity** | 40% by 2030 | **50% by 2030** |
| **Carbon sink** | 2.5–3 GtCO₂e forest cover | **2.5–3 GtCO₂e additional sink** |
| **Net Zero** | Not pledged | **2070** (announced COP26) |
| **Renewable target** | 175 GW by 2022 | **500 GW non-fossil by 2030** |

### India's Mitigation Missions (NAPCC)

### Global Mitigation Framework

```

```
