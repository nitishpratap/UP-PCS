# Forests and their Types

## Definition

**Forests** are **large areas dominated by trees and woody vegetation** that form complex ecosystems providing habitat for wildlife, regulating climate, conserving soil and water, and supplying timber and non-timber forest produce (NTFP).

**Forest types** are classified based on **climate, latitude, altitude, rainfall, and dominant tree species** — globally and in India using systems like **Champion & Seth Classification (1968)**.

## Explanation

### Functions of Forests

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Ecological** | Biodiversity habitat, oxygen production, carbon sequestration |
| **Hydrological** | Watershed protection, groundwater recharge, flood control |
| **Soil conservation** | Prevent erosion, maintain fertility |
| **Climatic regulation** | Temperature moderation, rainfall influence |
| **Economic** | Timber, fuelwood, NTFP (tendu, bamboo, honey) |
| **Social/cultural** | Livelihoods, sacred groves, tribal identity |
| **Recreational** | Eco-tourism, wildlife viewing |

### Global Forest Classification

| Type | Climate/Location | Characteristics |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Tropical Rainforest** | Equatorial; high rainfall (>200 cm) | Evergreen, dense canopy, high biodiversity |
| **Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon)** | Tropical; seasonal rainfall | Shed leaves in dry season |
| **Tropical Thorn/Scrub** | Low rainfall (<75 cm) | Xerophytic, thorny vegetation |
| **Temperate Deciduous** | Mid-latitudes; distinct seasons | Oak, maple; autumn leaf fall |
| **Temperate Coniferous (Taiga)** | High latitude/cold | Pine, spruce, fir |
| **Mediterranean (Sclerophyll)** | Winter rain, summer drought | Hard-leaved evergreen shrubs |
| **Montane/Alpine** | High altitude | Altitude zonation of vegetation |

### Champion & Seth Classification (India — 1968)

| Group | Type | Rainfall/Climate |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| **Group 1: Wet Tropical** | Tropical Wet Evergreen | >200 cm; Western Ghats, NE India |
| **Group 2: Moist Tropical** | Moist Deciduous | 100–200 cm; most of India |
| **Group 3: Dry Tropical** | Dry Deciduous, Dry Evergreen, Thorn | 50–100 cm; Deccan, Rajasthan |
| **Group 4: Montane** | Sub-tropical, Temperate, Alpine | Himalayas altitude zones |
| **Group 5: Littoral & Swamp** | Mangrove, Tidal, Swamp | Coasts, deltas, wetlands |
| **Group 6: Dry Deciduous (Northern)** | Northern Dry Deciduous | Gangetic plains |

### Forest Cover vs Tree Cover vs Recorded Forest Area

| Term | Definition | India Data (ISFR 2023) |
|------|------------|----------------------|
| **Forest Cover** | Lands with tree canopy density ≥10% and area ≥1 ha (satellite-based) | **7,13,789 sq km (21.76%)** |
| **Tree Cover** | Trees outside forests (agriculture, homesteads, urban) | **1,02,330 sq km (3.11%)** |
| **Total Green Cover** | Forest Cover + Tree Cover | **24.87%** |
| **Recorded Forest Area (RFA)** | Legally notified forest land (gazetted) | **7,67,419 sq km (23.36%)** |

### Forest Canopy Density Classes (India)

| Class | Canopy Density | Area (approx.) |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| **Very Dense Forest (VDF)** | ≥70% | ~99,779 sq km |
| **Moderately Dense Forest (MDF)** | 40–70% | ~3,06,192 sq km |
| **Open Forest (OF)** | 10–40% | ~3,07,818 sq km |
| **Scrub** | <10% | ~46,583 sq km |

### Major Forest Biomes of the World

| Biome | % of Land | Key Feature |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Tropical forests** | ~18% | Highest biodiversity |
| **Boreal (Taiga)** | ~11% | Largest terrestrial biome |
| **Temperate forests** | ~7% | Seasonal deciduous |
| **Mangroves** | ~0.7% | Coastal tidal forests |

## Important Facts

- **India's forest cover: 21.76%** (ISFR 2023) — target 33% (National Forest Policy 1988).
- **Champion & Seth (1968)** — standard Indian forest classification.
- **Very Dense Forest** — only ~3% of India's geographical area.
- **Madhya Pradesh** — largest forest cover by area.
- **Arunachal Pradesh** — highest % forest cover among large states (~80%).
- **Mangrove cover in India** — ~4,992 sq km (Sundarbans largest).
- **Boreal forests** — world's largest terrestrial carbon sink.
- **Tropical rainforests** — contain 50%+ of world's species.

# Forest Types in India

## Definition

**Forest Types in India** refer to the **distinct vegetation communities classified by climate, rainfall, soil, altitude, and dominant species** — primarily using the **Champion & Seth Classification (1968)**, which divides Indian forests into **5 major groups and 16 types**.

India has **one of the richest forest diversities** — from tropical rainforests in the Western Ghats to alpine meadows in the Himalayas and mangroves in the Sundarbans.

## Explanation

### Five Major Forest Groups in India

| Group | Types | Distribution |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| **1. Tropical Wet Evergreen** | Wet Evergreen, Semi-Evergreen | Western Ghats, NE hills |
| **2. Tropical Moist Deciduous** | Moist Deciduous, Littoral & Swamp | Central India, Gangetic plains |
| **3. Tropical Dry Deciduous** | Dry Deciduous, Dry Evergreen, Thorn | Deccan plateau, Rajasthan |
| **4. Montane Forests** | Sub-tropical, Temperate, Alpine | Himalayas (altitude zones) |
| **5. Littoral & Swamp** | Tidal, Mangrove, Freshwater swamp | Coasts, deltas, wetlands |

### Tropical Wet Evergreen Forests

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Rainfall** | >200 cm annually |
| **Distribution** | Western Ghats, Andaman & Nicobar, NE states |
| **Trees** | Rosewood, ebony, mahogany, mesua, white cedar |
| **Canopy** | Multi-layered; always green |
| **Undergrowth** | Dense shrubs, climbers, epiphytes |
| **Biodiversity** | Highest in India |
| **Examples** | Silent Valley (Kerala), Namdapha (Arunachal) |

### Tropical Semi-Evergreen Forests

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Rainfall** | 150–200 cm |
| **Distribution** | Transition zones of wet evergreen |
| **Trees** | Mix of evergreen and deciduous species |
| **Examples** | Parts of Odisha, Jharkhand foothills |

### Tropical Moist Deciduous Forests

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Rainfall** | 100–200 cm |
| **Distribution** | MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand, Maharashtra |
| **Trees** | Teak, sal, shisham, sandalwood, bamboo |
| **Leaf fall** | Dry season (winter) |
| **Largest forest type** | Covers maximum area in India |
| **Examples** | Kanha NP, Satpura, Simlipal |

### Tropical Dry Deciduous Forests

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Rainfall** | 70–100 cm |
| **Distribution** | UP, Bihar, Delhi, Punjab, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu |
| **Trees** | Tendu, palas, amaltas, bel |
| **Grass** | Abundant in understorey |
| **Examples** | Gir NP, Ranthambore |

### Tropical Thorn Forests

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Rainfall** | <75 cm |
| **Distribution** | Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana, Deccan |
| **Trees** | Babool (khejri), ber, khair, neem, cactus |
| **Adaptation** | Xerophytic; small leaves, thorns |
| **Examples** | Desert NP (Rajasthan) |

### Montane Forests (Himalayan Altitude Zones)

| Zone | Altitude | Vegetation |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Wet temperate** | 1,000–2,000 m | Oak, chestnut, deodar |
| **Moist temperate** | 1,500–2,400 m | Fir, spruce, deodar |
| **Sub-alpine** | 2,900–3,500 m | Birch, juniper, rhododendron |
| **Alpine** | >3,500 m | Dwarf shrubs, meadows, lichens |
| **Examples** | Valley of Flowers, Dachigam, Great Himalayan NP |

### Mangrove Forests

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Rainfall** | Coastal tidal zones |
| **Distribution** | Sundarbans (WB), Bhitarkanika (Odisha), Pichavaram (TN), Andaman |
| **Trees** | Sundari, rhizophora, avicennia, sonneratia |
| **Adaptation** | Salt-tolerant; pneumatophores (breathing roots) |
| **India mangrove cover** | ~4,992 sq km |
| **Largest** | Sundarbans — UNESCO WHS |

### State-wise Forest Cover Highlights (ISFR 2023)

| State/UT | Forest Cover (% of GA) | Notable Forest Type |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **Madhya Pradesh** | ~19% (largest area) | Moist deciduous (teak, sal) |
| **Arunachal Pradesh** | ~80% | Wet evergreen, montane |
| **Chhattisgarh** | ~44% | Moist deciduous |
| **Odisha** | ~33% | Moist deciduous, mangrove |
| **Maharashtra** | ~16% | Moist & dry deciduous |
| **Uttar Pradesh** | ~6% | Dry deciduous, plantations |

| **Kerala** | ~52% | Wet evergreen (Western Ghats) |

### Forest Types vs Vegetation Zones (Simplified)

```
WEST TO EAST (Rainfall gradient):
Thorn (Rajasthan) → Dry Deciduous (Gujarat/UP) → Moist Deciduous (MP/CG)
→ Wet Evergreen (Western Ghats/NE)

SOUTH TO NORTH (Altitude gradient):
Tropical (Plains) → Sub-tropical (Foothills) → Temperate (Mid-Himalaya)
→ Alpine (High Himalaya)
```

## Important Facts

- **Moist Deciduous** — largest forest type by area in India.
- **Wet Evergreen** — highest biodiversity; Western Ghats + NE India.
- **Teak** — dominant in moist deciduous (Central India).
- **Sal** — dominant in eastern moist deciduous (Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand).
- **Sundari tree** — gives Sundarbans its name; mangrove species.
- **Khejri (Prosopis cineraria)** — state tree of Rajasthan; desert thorn.
- **Deodar** — Himalayan temperate conifer; "wood of the gods."
- **India has all major forest types** except boreal (taiga).

# Forest Conservation

## Definition

**Forest Conservation** is the **protection, management, and sustainable use of forest ecosystems** to prevent deforestation, degradation, and biodiversity loss while maintaining ecological balance and meeting legitimate human needs.

It involves **legal protection (FCA 1980), policy frameworks (National Forest Policy 1988), afforestation programmes, and community participation (JFM, FRA 2006)**.

## Explanation

### Causes of Deforestation and Forest Degradation in India

| Cause | Impact | Example |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Agricultural expansion** | Forest land conversion | Shifting cultivation in NE |
| **Urbanisation/industry** | Habitat fragmentation | Mining in Odisha, Chhattisgarh |
| **Infrastructure** | Road, dam, railway projects | Highway through Western Ghats |
| **Overgrazing** | Regeneration failure | Rajasthan, Gujarat |
| **Fuelwood collection** | Forest thinning | Rural India |
| **Forest fires** | Massive tree loss | Uttarakhand, Himachal |
| **Invasive species** | Native species displacement | Lantana camara |
| **Climate change** | Drought, pest outbreaks | Himalayan forests |
| **Illegal logging** | Timber theft | Central Indian forests |

### Forest Conservation Strategies

| Strategy | Method | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **Legal protection** | FCA 1980, WPA 1972 | No forest diversion without clearance |
| **Protected Areas** | NP, Sanctuary, BR | 104 NP, 566 Sanctuary |
| **Afforestation** | CAMPA, Green India Mission | Compensatory afforestation |
| **Community participation** | JFM, FRA 2006 | Village forest protection committees |
| **Eco-Sensitive Zones** | Buffer around PAs | 10 km ESZ around NPs |
| **Sustainable forestry** | Selective felling, NTFP | Tendu leaf collection |
| **Fire management** | Fire lines, early warning | Forest fire alerts (FSI) |
| **International cooperation** | REDD+, CBD | Carbon credits |

### Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 — Key Provisions

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Section 2** | Restriction on dereservation of forests |
| **Diversion ban** | Forest land cannot be used for non-forest purposes without Central approval |
| **Applicability** | All forests (reserved, protected, unclassed) |
| **Penalty** | Imprisonment + fine for violations |
| **Compensatory Afforestation** | Mandatory when forest land diverted |
| **FCA Amendment 2023** | Controversial; delegated powers to States |

### National Forest Policy, 1988 — Key Objectives

| Objective | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| **Forest cover** | 33% of total geographical area |
| **Hill/mountain areas** | 66% forest cover |
| **Plains** | 20% forest cover |
| **Deserts** | 25% forest cover |
| **Sustainable management** | Environmental stability, ecological balance |
| **Tribal welfare** | NTFP rights, livelihood support |
| **Fuelwood/fodder** | Meeting domestic needs sustainably |

# Forest Management

## Definition

**Forest Management** is the **scientific planning and administration of forest resources** for sustained yield of timber, NTFP, and ecosystem services while maintaining ecological health — involving **silvicultural practices, working plans, inventory, and institutional frameworks**.

In India, forest management is governed by the **Indian Forest Act, 1927**, **State Forest Departments**, and **working plans** approved by the Centre.

## Explanation

### Principles of Scientific Forest Management

| Principle | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Sustained yield** | Harvest ≤ annual growth |
| **Biodiversity conservation** | Maintain species diversity |
| **Ecological integrity** | Protect soil, water, wildlife |
| **Social equity** | Include local communities |
| **Economic viability** | Generate revenue sustainably |
| **Adaptive management** | Adjust based on monitoring |

### Indian Forest Act, 1927 — Forest Classification

| Category | Definition | Rights |
|----------|------------|--------|
| **Reserved Forest (RF)** | Highest protection; State monopoly | No private rights without permission |
| **Protected Forest (PF)** | Moderate protection | Limited rights (grazing, collection) |
| **Village Forest** | Managed for village benefit | Community rights |
| **Private Forest** | Owned by individuals/institutions | Subject to regulation |
| **Unclassed Forest** | Not formally classified | ~40% of NE forest area |

### Working Plan System

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Definition** | 10-year scientific management plan for each forest division |
| **Prepared by** | State Forest Department |
| **Approved by** | MoEFCC (Central) |
| **Contents** | Inventory, felling cycles, regeneration, protection |
| **Legal basis** | Indian Forest Act, 1927 + National Forest Policy 1988 |
| **Coverage** | ~80% of India's forest area under working plans |

### Silvicultural Systems

| System | Method | Used For |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Clear felling** | All trees cut at once | Plantations (eucalyptus, poplar) |
| **Shelterwood** | Gradual removal over years | Natural regeneration |
| **Selection felling** | Selective mature trees cut | Uneven-aged forests |
| **Coppice system** | Cut to stump for regrowth | Tendu, bamboo |
| **Taungya** | Agriculture between tree rows | Afforestation with tribal participation |

### Forest Management Practices in India

| Practice | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Compartmentalisation** | Forest divided into compartments for rotation |
| **Felling cycle** | 20–60 years depending on species |
| **Regeneration** | Natural or artificial after felling |
| **Fire protection** | Fire lines, watchtowers, community alerts |
| **Pest control** | Biological and chemical methods |
| **NTFP management** | Tendu leaves, mahua, honey, lac |
| **Wildlife management** | Habitat improvement, water holes |

### Institutions for Forest Management

| Institution | Role |
|------------|------|
| **State Forest Departments** | Primary management authority |
| **MoEFCC** | Policy, working plan approval |
| **FSI** | Forest inventory and mapping |
| **ICFRE** | Research (Dehradun HQ) |
| **IFGTB (Coimbatore)** | Tree breeding and genetics |
| **IWST (Bangalore)** | Wood science and technology |

### Forest Revenue and Economic Value

| Source | Contribution |
|--------|-------------|
| **Timber** | Teak, sal, sandalwood, plywood |
| **NTFP** | Tendu leaves (₹2,000+ crore annually), bamboo |
| **Forest-based industry** | Paper, matchwood, furniture |
| **Eco-tourism** | Corbett, Ranthambore, Periyar |
| **Carbon credits** | REDD+ potential |
| **Employment** | 25+ million people depend on forests |

### Sustainable vs Unsustainable Practices

| Sustainable | Unsustainable |
|------------|--------------|
| Selective felling | Clear felling natural forests |
| JFM with communities | Exclusion of locals |
| NTFP-based livelihoods | Timber-only focus |
| Working plan adherence | Illegal logging |
| Mixed species plantations | Monoculture (eucalyptus) |
| Assisted natural regeneration | Complete deforestation |

## Important Facts

- **Indian Forest Act, 1927** — colonial-era law still governing forest management.
- **Working Plans** — mandatory 10-year management plans.
- **Reserved Forest** — highest protection category.
- **~40% NE forests** — unclassed (not formally protected).
- **Tendu leaves** — among most valuable NTFP (Chhattisgarh, MP, Odisha).
- **ICFRE headquarters** — Dehradun.
- **Taungya system** — tribal agriculture + forestry combined.
- **Sandalwood** — Karnataka State Tree; high value timber.

# Afforestation

## Definition

**Afforestation** is the **establishment of a forest or stand of trees in an area where there was no previous tree cover** (or where forest was lost), as opposed to **reforestation** which restores forests in recently deforested areas.

In India, afforestation is driven by **CAMPA, Green India Mission, National Afforestation Programme (NAP), and state schemes** to achieve the **33% forest cover target**.

## Explanation

### Afforestation vs Reforestation vs Regeneration

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Afforestation** | Creating forest on non-forest land | Planting on wasteland, degraded land |
| **Reforestation** | Restoring recently cleared forest | Replanting after felling |
| **Natural Regeneration** | Forest regrows naturally | Protected area recovery |
| **Assisted Natural Regeneration** | Protecting + aiding natural regrowth | Fencing degraded forest |
| **Compensatory Afforestation** | Mandatory replacement when forest diverted | CAMPA-funded planting |

### Major Afforestation Programmes in India

| Programme | Launch | Detail |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **National Afforestation Programme (NAP)** | 2000 (restructured 2012) | Centrally sponsored; JFM-based |
| **Green India Mission (GIM)** | 2008 | 10 million ha under NAPCC |
| **CAMPA** | 2016 (Act) | Compensatory afforestation fund |
| **National Mission for a Green India** | 2014 | Revised GIM under NAPCC |
| **Haritha Haram** | 2015 | Telangana state programme |
| **Van Mahotsav** | 1950 | Annual tree plantation week |
| **Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam** | 2024 | PM's plantation campaign |

### National Afforestation Programme (NAP)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Implementing agency** | NAEB (National Afforestation and Eco-development Board) |
| **Approach** | Community-based through JFM committees |
| **Funding** | Centre:State = 60:40 (90:10 for NE/Himalayan states) |
| **Focus areas** | Degraded forest lands, wastelands |
| **Components** | Afforestation, eco-restoration, JFM institutional strengthening |
| **Coverage** | All states except those with >33% forest cover |

### Compensatory Afforestation (CAMPA)

| Step | Process |
|------|---------|
| **1. Forest diversion** | Project requires forest land (mining, road, dam) |
| **2. FAC approval** | Forest Advisory Committee clears proposal |
| **3. Compensatory afforestation** | Equal area planted elsewhere |
| **4. Net Present Value (NPV)** | Payment for ecosystem services lost |
| **5. CAMPA fund** | Money deposited in CAMPA account |
| **6. Plantation** | State CAMPA executes afforestation |

### Net Present Value (NPV) of Forests

| Forest Type | NPV (per hectare, approx.) |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| **Very Dense Forest** | ₹10.43 lakh/ha |
| **Moderately Dense Forest** | ₹8.54 lakh/ha |
| **Open Forest** | ₹5.65 lakh/ha |
| **Scrub** | ₹3.08 lakh/ha |

### Species Used in Afforestation

| Category | Species | Use |
|----------|---------|-----|
| **Timber** | Teak, sal, eucalyptus, poplar | Commercial plantations |
| **Fuelwood** | Subabul, casuarina, acacia | Energy plantations |
| **Fruit/medicinal** | Amla, neem, harad, baheda | Multi-purpose |
| **Ecological** | Native species mix | Biodiversity restoration |
| **Mangrove** | Rhizophora, avicennia | Coastal afforestation |
| **Desert** | Khejri, babool, ber | Arid zone greening |

### Van Mahotsav (Tree Plantation Festival)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Started** | 1950 by Dr. K.M. Munshi (then Agriculture Minister) |
| **Week** | First week of July annually |
| **Purpose** | Mass awareness and plantation drive |
| **Participation** | Schools, colleges, government, communities |
| **Significance** | Largest annual plantation campaign in India |

### Afforestation Challenges in India

| Challenge | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Survival rate** | Only 30–50% saplings survive |
| **Monoculture plantations** | Eucalyptus, poplar reduce biodiversity |
| **Wrong site selection** | Planting in unsuitable ecology |
| **Water intensive species** | Eucalyptus depletes groundwater |
| **Lack of maintenance** | Plant-and-forget approach |
| **Land tenure issues** | Disputed land for plantation |
| **Climate change** | Drought kills young plantations |

## Important Facts

- **Van Mahotsav** — started 1950 by K.M. Munshi; first week of July.
- **NAP** — community-based afforestation through JFM.
- **CAMPA** — compensatory afforestation mandatory for forest diversion.
- **Green India Mission** — 10 million ha target.
- **Telangana Haritha Haram** — planted 230+ crore saplings.
- **India plants ~35 crore saplings** annually (Van Mahotsav + schemes).
- **Compensatory afforestation** — equal area + equal quality required.
- **Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam (2024)** — PM Modi plantation campaign.

# Social Forestry

## Definition

**Social Forestry** is a **forestry management approach that involves local communities in planting and managing trees** on **non-forest lands** (farm boundaries, wastelands, community lands, roadsides) to meet **fuelwood, fodder, timber, and fruit needs** while improving the environment.

Coined in India during the **1970s**; formalised in the **National Commission on Agriculture (1976)** and **National Forest Policy 1988**.

## Explanation

### Objectives of Social Forestry

| Objective | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Fuelwood security** | Reduce pressure on natural forests |
| **Fodder supply** | Cattle feed from tree leaves |
| **Timber and poles** | Construction and fencing material |
| **Fruit and NTFP** | Income generation for rural households |
| **Employment** | Nursery, planting, maintenance jobs |
| **Environmental benefits** | Soil conservation, microclimate, biodiversity |
| **Community empowerment** | Local participation in forestry |

### Three Components of Social Forestry

| Component | Location | Purpose |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Farm Forestry (Agroforestry)** | Private farmland | Farmer-owned tree plantations |
| **Extension Forestry** | Community/wasteland | Village woodlots, panchayat land |
| **Recreational Forestry** | Urban/peri-urban | Avenue planting, parks, gardens |

### Social Forestry vs Community Forestry vs Joint Forest Management

| Feature | Social Forestry | Community Forestry | JFM |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|-----|
| **Land** | Non-forest lands | Forest + non-forest | Reserved/protected forests |
| **Ownership** | Individual/community | Community-owned | State-owned forest |
| **Focus** | Meeting local needs | Community control | Sharing forest benefits |
| **Legal basis** | Policy-based | FRA 2006 | MoEFCC resolution 1990 |
| **Started** | 1970s | 2006 (FRA) | 1990 |

# Agroforestry

## Definition

**Agroforestry** is a **land-use system that deliberately integrates trees and shrubs with crops and/or livestock** on the same land unit, combining **agricultural and forestry technologies** to create more diverse, productive, profitable, and sustainable land-use systems.

Recognised as a **climate-smart practice** under India's **National Agroforestry Policy, 2014** — the world's first national agroforestry policy.

## Explanation

### Basic Agroforestry Systems

| System | Description | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Agrisilviculture** | Trees + crops | Mango + wheat; poplar + sugarcane |
| **Silvopasture** | Trees + livestock | Khejri + sheep (Rajasthan) |
| **Agrisilvopasture** | Trees + crops + livestock | Traditional Indian farming |
| **Forest farming** | NTFP under forest canopy | Medicinal plants under teak |
| **Home gardens** | Multi-storey tree-crop mix | Kerala homestead gardens |
| **Boundary plantation** | Trees on field borders | Eucalyptus windbreaks |

### National Agroforestry Policy, 2014

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **First in world** | India's national agroforestry policy |
| **Launched by** | MoAFW (Ministry of Agriculture) |
| **Goal** | Increase tree cover outside forests |
| **Target** | Include agroforestry in NAPCC |
| **Institutional** | Sub-Mission on Agroforestry (SMAF) under RKVY |
| **Simplification** | Tree felling/transit regulations eased |
| **Research** | ICAR-CAFRI (Jhansi) — Central Agroforestry Research Institute |

### Benefits of Agroforestry

| Benefit | Detail |

| **Water conservation** | Improved infiltration, reduced runoff |

# Joint Forest Management (JFM)

## Definition

**Joint Forest Management (JFM)** is a **partnership programme between State Forest Departments and local communities** for **protecting and managing forests** — where communities share **benefits** (NTFP, timber share) in return for **protecting forests** from fire, grazing, and illegal felling.

Formalised by **MoEFCC Resolution, 1990**; governed by **State JFM Rules**.

## Explanation

### JFM Framework

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Legal basis** | MoEFCC Resolution (1 June 1990) + State Rules |
| **Institution** | Forest Protection Committee (FPC) / Village Forest Committee (VFC) |
| **Membership** | Village community + Forest Department officials |
| **Forest type** | Degraded reserved/protected forests |
| **Benefit sharing** | 20–100% of produce to community (varies by state) |
| **Community role** | Protection, fire prevention, plantation |
| **Forest Dept role** | Technical guidance, working plan implementation |

### How JFM Works

```
STATE FOREST DEPARTMENT
        ↓ (enters agreement)
FOREST PROTECTION COMMITTEE (FPC)
        ↓ (community protects)
DEGRADED FOREST LAND
        ↓ (regeneration occurs)
BENEFIT SHARING (NTFP, timber, employment)
        ↓
COMMUNITY LIVELIHOOD IMPROVEMENT
```

### JFM Resolution (1990) — Key Guidelines

| Guideline | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Eligibility** | Degraded forests near villages |
| **FPC formation** | General body of all village adults |
| **Executive committee** | 10–15 elected members |
| **Women participation** | Mandatory representation |
| **Benefit sharing** | Minimum 25% of final harvest to community |
| **NTFP rights** | Community collects tendu, mahua, etc. |
| **Micro-plan** | Village-level forest management plan |
| **Review** | Periodic assessment of JFM effectiveness |

### JFM Coverage in India

| Indicator | Data |
|-----------|------|
| **States implementing** | 27 states |
| **JFM committees** | ~1,18,000 FPCs/VFCs |
| **Forest area under JFM** | ~22 million hectares |
| **% of total forest area** | ~28% under JFM |
| **Beneficiaries** | ~25 million people |

### State-wise JFM Benefit Sharing

| State | Community Share | Notable Feature |
|-------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Odisha** | 50% of timber | Pioneer JFM state |
| **West Bengal** | Joint management model | Pre-1990 origin (Arabari project) |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | VSS (Vana Samrakshana Samithi) | 100% NTFP to community |
| **Gujarat** | Van Suraksha Samiti | Early adopter |
| **Madhya Pradesh** | 30% timber share | Large JFM area |
| **Jharkhand** | Tendu leaf cooperatives | NTFP focus |

### Arabari Project — JFM Origin

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Location** | Arabari, West Bengal |
| **Year** | 1972 (formalised as JFM model) |
| **Forest officer** | **Ajit Kumar Banerjee** |
| **Concept** | Community protects forest → shares benefits |
| **Result** | Sal forest regenerated; community income increased |
| **Significance** | Model for national JFM programme (1990) |

### JFM vs FRA 2006 vs Community Forestry

| Feature | JFM | FRA 2006 | Community Forestry |
|---------|-----|----------|-------------------|
| **Legal basis** | Executive resolution | Parliamentary Act | FRA/CFR rights |
| **Forest ownership** | State | Community (CFR rights) | Community |
| **Rights type** | Benefit sharing | Title/ownership rights | Full management |
| **Permanence** | MoU (renewable) | Permanent legal right | Permanent |
| **Started** | 1990 | 2006 | 2006 onwards |

### JFM Achievements

| Achievement | Detail |
|------------|--------|
| **Forest regeneration** | Degraded forests recovered |
| **Community income** | NTFP, wage employment |
| **Fire reduction** | Community fire watchers |
| **Illegal felling control** | Local vigilance |
| **Women empowerment** | FPC membership |
| **Biodiversity recovery** | Habitat improvement |

### JFM Challenges

| Challenge | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Weak legal backing** | Resolution, not Act — can be withdrawn |
| **Unequal benefit sharing** | Large farmers dominate FPCs |
| **Forest Dept dominance** | Officials override community decisions |
| **Elite capture** | Local elites control benefits |
| **FRA overlap** | CFR rights may conflict with JFM MoUs |
| **Declining interest** | Low benefits reduce participation |

## Important Facts

- **JFM formalised 1990** — MoEFCC Resolution (1 June 1990).
- **Arabari project (1972)** — West Bengal; origin of JFM concept.
- **Ajit Kumar Banerjee** — forest officer behind Arabari success.
- **~1,18,000 JFM committees** across 27 states.
- **~22 million ha** under JFM (~28% of forest area).
- **Odisha** — 50% timber share to community.
- **FPC** — Forest Protection Committee at village level.
- **JFM is not legally binding** — executive resolution only.

# Forest Rights Act (FRA 2006)

## Definition

The **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006** (commonly **Forest Rights Act — FRA 2006**) is a **landmark legislation** recognising the **rights of tribal and forest-dwelling communities** to **forest land, resources, and governance** — correcting **historical injustices** from colonial forest laws.

**Passed:** 29 December 2006; **Rules notified:** 1 January 2008.

## Explanation

### Historical Context

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Colonial forest laws** | Indian Forest Act 1927 — excluded tribals |
| **Forest as State property** | Communities treated as encroachers |
| **Evictions** | Millions displaced from forests |
| **NFFPF (2005)** | National Forest Policy conflict with tribal rights |
| **Sunderlal Bahuguna/Chipko** | Forest rights movement |
| **Bhuriya Committee (1995)** | Recommended forest rights recognition |
| **N C Saxena Committee** | Drafted FRA provisions |

### Types of Rights under FRA 2006

| Right | Section | Detail |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **Individual Forest Rights (IFR)** | Sec 3(1)(a) | Occupation of forest land (max 4 ha) |
| **Community Forest Rights (CFR)** | Sec 3(1)(b) | Community ownership and management of forest |
| **Development Rights** | Sec 3(1)(c)–(f) | Roads, schools, health centres in forest areas |
| **Rights to NTFP** | Sec 3(1)(c) | Collect, use, dispose of minor forest produce |
| **Grazing rights** | Sec 3(1)(d) | Cattle grazing in forests |
| **Traditional resource access** | Sec 3(1)(e) | Water, fish, cultural sites |
| **Intellectual property** | Sec 3(1)(h) | Traditional knowledge protection |
| **Biodiversity conservation** | Sec 3(1)(i) | Community conservation practices |
| **Habitat rights (PVTGs)** | Sec 3(1)(e) | Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups |

### Key Provisions of FRA 2006

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Eligible claimants** | STs and OTFDs (Other Traditional Forest Dwellers) |
| **OTFD condition** | 3 generations (75 years) residence before 13 Dec 2005 |
| **Maximum land** | 4 hectares per claimant (not ownership of entire forest) |
| **Authority** | Gram Sabha initiates claims → SDLC → DLC |
| **Gram Sabha role** | Primary authority for verifying claims |
| **CFR rights** | Community manages, protects, conserves forest |
| **Eviction ban** | No eviction until FRA process complete |
| **Women rights** | Joint pattas (titles) in name of both spouses |
| **PVTGs** | Special habitat rights |

### FRA Implementation Process

```
CLAIMANT → GRAM SABHA (verifies) → SDLC (reviews) → DLC (decides)
                                                          ↓
                                              FOREST RIGHTS CERTIFICATE
                                              (Individual or Community)
```

| Level | Body | Role |
|-------|------|------|
| **Village** | Gram Sabha | Receives claims, verifies, recommends |
| **Sub-division** | SDLC (Sub-Divisional Level Committee) | Reviews Gram Sabha recommendations |
| **District** | DLC (District Level Committee) | Final decision on claims |
| **State** | State Level Monitoring Committee | Oversees implementation |

### FRA Implementation Status

| Indicator | Data (approx.) |
|-----------|---------------|
| **IFR titles distributed** | ~20 lakh (2 million) |
| **CFR titles distributed** | ~70,000 |
| **CFR area recognised** | ~60 lakh hectares (6 million ha) |
| **Claims rejected** | ~50% of total claims |
| **States leading** | Odisha, Maharashtra, Tripura, Karnataka |
| **States lagging** | UP, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand |

### FRA and Conservation

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **CFR and biodiversity** | Communities conserve sacred groves, wildlife |
| **Odisha CFR** | Kandhamal — community forest management success |
| **Mendha Lekha (Maharashtra)** | First village with CFR title |
| **Niyamgiri (Odisha)** | Gram Sabha rejected Vedanta mining (2013) |
| **Tiger reserves conflict** | FRA rights vs critical tiger habitats |
| **Critical Wildlife Habitat (CWH)** | Sec 4 — forest rights can be modified in CWH |

### FRA vs JFM vs Forest Department

| Feature | FRA 2006 | JFM (1990) | Forest Department |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Legal status** | Parliamentary Act | Executive resolution | IFA 1927 |
| **Rights type** | Ownership/management | Benefit sharing | Full state control |
| **Community power** | High (CFR management) | Moderate (protection) | None |
| **Permanence** | Permanent legal right | MoU (renewable) | State ownership |
| **Gram Sabha** | Primary decision-maker | FPC member | Not involved |

### Criticisms and Challenges

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Slow implementation** | 18+ years; many claims pending |
| **High rejection rate** | ~50% claims rejected |
| **Forest Dept resistance** | Reluctance to share control |
| **Elite capture** | Non-tribals claiming OTFD status |
| **Conservation conflict** | Tiger reserve vs FRA rights |
| **Documentation** | Tribals lack proof of residence |
| **CWH notification** | Used to override forest rights |

## Important Facts

- **FRA enacted 29 December 2006** — Rules 1 January 2008.
- **First village CFR title** — Mendha Lekha, Maharashtra.
- **Niyamgiri Gram Sabha (2013)** — rejected Vedanta bauxite mining.
- **Max 4 ha** individual forest right per claimant.
- **OTFD eligibility** — 3 generations (75 years) before 13 Dec 2005.
- **Gram Sabha** — primary authority under FRA.
- **~20 lakh IFR titles** distributed; ~70,000 CFR titles.
- **Odisha** — leading state in CFR implementation.
- **PESA 1996** — complements FRA in Schedule V areas.
