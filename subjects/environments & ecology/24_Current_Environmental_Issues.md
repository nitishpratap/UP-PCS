# Microplastics

## Definition

**Microplastics** are **plastic particles smaller than 5 mm in size** — resulting from the **breakdown of larger plastic waste** or manufactured as **microbeads** in cosmetics and personal care products — pervasive pollutants that contaminate **oceans, rivers, soil, air, food chains, and human bodies**, posing serious **environmental and health risks**.

**Types:** Primary microplastics (manufactured small) | Secondary microplastics (degraded from larger plastics).

## Explanation

### Types of Microplastics

| Type | Source | Examples |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Primary microplastics** | Manufactured intentionally small | Microbeads in cosmetics, toothpaste, scrubs |
| **Secondary microplastics** | Breakdown of larger plastic items | Bottles, bags, fishing nets fragmenting |
| **Fibres** | Synthetic textile washing | Polyester, nylon microfibres from laundry |
| **Fragments** | Physical breakdown of plastics | Hard plastic fragments |
| **Films** | Thin plastic sheet breakdown | Packaging film particles |
| **Pellets (nurdles)** | Raw plastic manufacturing material | Industrial plastic pellets spilled |

### Sources of Microplastics

| Source | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Plastic waste degradation** | UV radiation, wave action break down plastics |
| **Cosmetics & personal care** | Microbeads in face wash, toothpaste (banned in many countries) |
| **Synthetic textiles** | Washing clothes releases microfibres |
| **Tire wear** | Road abrasion produces rubber microplastics |
| **Fishing gear** | Nets, lines degrade in oceans |
| **Industrial pellets** | Nurdle spills during transport |
| **Paint particles** | Ship hull paint, road markings |
| **Cigarette butts** | Cellulose acetate filters |
| **Single-use plastics** | Bags, straws, cups fragmenting |
| **Wastewater** | Microplastics pass through treatment plants |

### Microplastics in the Environment

| Ecosystem | Impact |
|-----------|--------|
| **Oceans** | 5.25 trillion particles estimated globally; Great Pacific Garbage Patch |
| **Rivers** | Ganga, Yamuna among most plastic-polluted rivers |
| **Soil** | Agricultural plastic mulch degrades into soil |
| **Air** | Microplastics found in atmospheric fallout |
| **Food chain** | Ingested by plankton → fish → humans |
| **Drinking water** | Found in tap water and bottled water globally |
| **Human body** | Detected in blood, lungs, placenta, breast milk |
| **Arctic/Antarctic** | Even remote polar regions contaminated |

# Carbon Credit

## Definition

**Carbon Credit** is a **tradeable permit or certificate** representing the **right to emit one metric tonne of CO₂ equivalent (tCO₂e)** — issued for **verified greenhouse gas emission reductions** — allowing entities to **offset their emissions** by purchasing credits from projects that have **reduced or removed carbon** from the atmosphere.

**Mechanism:** 1 carbon credit = 1 tonne CO₂ equivalent reduced/removed.

## Explanation

### How Carbon Credits Work

```
EMITTER (Company/Country) exceeds emission limit
        ↓
PURCHASES CARBON CREDIT from reduction project
        ↓
CREDIT = 1 tonne CO₂ equivalent verified reduction
        ↓
PROJECT TYPES: Renewable energy | Afforestation | Methane capture | Energy efficiency
        ↓
EMITTER offsets excess emissions → Compliance achieved
```

### Types of Carbon Credits

| Type | System | Detail |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Compliance credits** | Mandatory cap-and-trade systems | EU ETS, Kyoto Protocol CDM |
| **Voluntary credits** | Voluntary carbon market | VCS, Gold Standard certified |
| **CDM credits (CERs)** | Kyoto Protocol Clean Development Mechanism | Developing country projects |
| **JI credits (ERUs)** | Joint Implementation — developed countries | Emission Reduction Units |
| **AAUs** | Assigned Amount Units | Kyoto national allocations |
| **Removal credits** | Carbon capture and storage, afforestation | Actual CO₂ removal |
| **Avoidance credits** | Preventing emissions that would have occurred | Renewable energy displacing coal |

### Carbon Credit Certification Standards

| Standard | Organisation | Detail |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| **VCS (Verified Carbon Standard)** | Verra | Most widely used voluntary standard |
| **Gold Standard** | Gold Standard Foundation | High-quality sustainable development credits |
| **CDM (Clean Development Mechanism)** | UNFCCC | Kyoto Protocol mechanism |
| **CAR (Climate Action Reserve)** | USA-based | North American standard |
| **CCB Standards** | Climate, Community & Biodiversity | Co-benefits certification |
| **India PAT scheme** | BEE | Perform, Achieve, Trade — energy efficiency credits |

# Carbon Trading

## Definition

**Carbon Trading** (Carbon Market / Emissions Trading) is a **market-based mechanism** where **emitters buy and sell carbon credits** to meet **emission reduction targets** — operating under a **cap-and-trade system** where a **regulatory authority sets an overall emission cap** and distributes or auctions **tradable emission permits** among participants.

**Principle:** Companies that reduce emissions below their cap sell surplus credits; those exceeding their cap must buy credits.

## Explanation

### How Carbon Trading Works (Cap-and-Trade)

```
REGULATOR sets TOTAL EMISSION CAP (e.g., 100 million tonnes)
        ↓
CAP divided into TRADEABLE PERMITS (carbon credits)
        ↓
COMPANY A reduces below cap → SELLS surplus credits
COMPANY B exceeds cap → BUYS credits to comply
        ↓
MARKET PRICE determined by supply and demand
        ↓
OVERALL EMISSIONS stay within CAP → Environment protected
```

### Types of Carbon Markets

| Market Type | Nature | Examples |
|------------|--------|---------|
| **Compliance market** | Mandatory; government-regulated | EU ETS, California cap-and-trade |
| **Voluntary market** | Optional; corporate social responsibility | VCS, Gold Standard credits |
| **International market** | Cross-border trading | Kyoto Protocol mechanisms |
| **National market** | Country-specific compliance | India CCTS 2023 |
| **Regional market** | Multi-country trading | EU ETS (27 countries) |

### Major Global Carbon Markets

| Market | Region | Detail |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **EU ETS** | European Union | World's largest carbon market |
| **California Cap-and-Trade** | USA (California) | Second-largest |
| **China National ETS** | China | World's largest by emissions covered (2021) |
| **RGGI** | US Northeast states | Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative |
| **UK ETS** | United Kingdom | Post-Brexit independent market |

### Carbon Trading vs Carbon Tax

| Feature | Carbon Trading | Carbon Tax |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| **Mechanism** | Market-based cap-and-trade | Fixed price per tonne CO₂ |
| **Price** | Market-determined (variable) | Government-set (fixed) |
| **Emission certainty** | Cap guarantees total emissions | Tax rate fixed, emissions uncertain |
| **Flexibility** | Companies choose how to reduce | Uniform tax on all emissions |
| **Revenue** | Auction revenue to government | Tax revenue to government |
| **Examples** | EU ETS, India CCTS | Sweden, British Columbia |
| **India's choice** | Carbon trading (CCTS) | Not adopted carbon tax |

# Net Zero Emissions

## Definition

**Net Zero Emissions** (Carbon Neutrality) means achieving a **balance between anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions** and their **removal from the atmosphere** — so that the **net addition of GHGs to the atmosphere is zero** — through a combination of **emission reduction** and **carbon removal/sequestration**.

**Formula:** Net Zero = Total GHG Emissions − GHG Removals = 0

## Explanation

### Pathways to Net Zero

```
CURRENT EMISSIONS (Industry, Transport, Agriculture, Energy)
        ↓
PATHWAY 1: REDUCE emissions (renewable energy, efficiency, electrification)
        ↓
PATHWAY 2: REMOVE carbon (afforestation, CCS, direct air capture)
        ↓
REMAINING EMISSIONS = REMOVALS → NET ZERO achieved
        ↓
TIMELINE: India 2070 | UK 2050 | EU 2050 | China 2060
```

### Net Zero vs Related Concepts

| Concept | Definition | Difference from Net Zero |
|---------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Net Zero** | Emissions = Removals (balanced) | Complete balance |
| **Carbon Neutral** | Same as Net Zero (often used interchangeably) | Same concept |
| **Climate Neutral** | All GHGs balanced (not just CO₂) | Broader scope |
| **Gross Zero** | Zero emissions without offsets | Stricter — no trading |
| **Carbon Negative** | Removals > Emissions | Beyond Net Zero |
| **Net Zero Emissions** | Specific to GHG accounting | UNFCCC standard term |

# Blue Economy

## Definition

**Blue Economy** is the **sustainable use of ocean resources for economic growth, livelihoods, and jobs** — while preserving the **health of ocean ecosystems** — encompassing **fisheries, aquaculture, maritime transport, coastal tourism, renewable ocean energy, marine biotechnology, and seabed mining** in an **environmentally sustainable and socially equitable** manner.

**Concept:** Oceans as drivers of economic development — not just conservation.

# LiFE Mission

## Definition

**LiFE Mission** (Lifestyle for Environment) is a **global initiative launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi** to promote **environmentally conscious lifestyle choices** as a key solution to **climate change and environmental degradation** — shifting from **"mindless and destructive consumption" to mindful and deliberate utilisation"** of natural resources.

**Launched:** COP27 Egypt, November 2022 (global launch); concept introduced at COP26 Glasgow 2021.

## Explanation

### Core Philosophy of LiFE Mission

```
PROBLEM: Mindless and destructive CONSUMPTION → Climate change
        ↓
SOLUTION: Mindful and deliberate UTILISATION → Sustainable lifestyle
        ↓
APPROACH: Individual + Community action (not just government policy)
        ↓
7 ACTION AREAS: Energy | Water | Food | Waste | Transport | Health | Biodiversity
        ↓
GLOBAL MOVEMENT: Every individual as climate warrior
```

### Seven Action Areas of LiFE Mission

| Action Area | Lifestyle Changes | Impact |
|------------|------------------|--------|
| **Save Energy** | Switch off lights, energy-efficient appliances | Reduce carbon footprint |
| **Save Water** | Fix leaks, rainwater harvesting, shorter showers | Water conservation |
| **Reduce Waste** | Refuse single-use plastic, compost, recycle | Less landfill pollution |
| **Sustainable Food** | Local/seasonal food, reduce food waste | Lower food miles emissions |
| **Green Transport** | Walk, cycle, public transport, carpool | Reduce transport emissions |
| **Healthy Lifestyle** | Plant-based diet options, outdoor exercise | Health + environment co-benefit |
| **Protect Biodiversity** | Plant trees, protect wildlife, no littering | Ecosystem preservation |

### LiFE Mission vs Government-Led Climate Action

| Feature | Government Action (Supply-side) | LiFE Mission (Demand-side) |
|---------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Focus** | Renewable energy, emission caps | Individual lifestyle choices |
| **Actor** | Government, industry | Every citizen |
| **Scale** | National policy | Global movement |
| **Examples** | Solar parks, carbon trading | Save water, reduce plastic |
| **Complement** | LiFE complements supply-side action | Both needed for Net Zero |
| **Cost** | High capital investment | Low/no cost behaviour change |
