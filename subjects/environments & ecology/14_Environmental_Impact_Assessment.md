# Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

## Definition

**Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)** is a **systematic process of identifying, predicting, evaluating, and mitigating the environmental, social, and health impacts** of proposed development projects **before** decisions are made to approve or reject them.

**Purpose:** Ensure that environmental considerations are **integrated into development planning** at the earliest stage.

**NCERT definition:** *"Environmental Impact Assessment is a process of evaluating the likely environmental impacts of a proposed project or development, taking into account inter-related socio-economic, cultural and human-health impacts."*

**Legal basis in India:** **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** → **EIA Notification, 2006** (amended periodically).

## Explanation

### EIA Process in India — 8 Stages

| Stage | Activity | Detail |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **1. Screening** | Decide if EIA required | Based on project category (Schedule) |
| **2. Scoping** | Identify key impacts to study | Terms of Reference (ToR) prepared |
| **3. Baseline data collection** | Current environmental status | Air, water, soil, biodiversity, socio-economic |
| **4. Impact prediction** | Forecast project impacts | Modelling, expert analysis |
| **5. Mitigation measures** | Propose impact reduction | EMP (Environmental Management Plan) |
| **6. EIA Report (EIA/EMP)** | Draft report prepared | By accredited consultant |
| **7. Public consultation** | Stakeholder hearing | Public Hearing (for Category A/B1) |
| **8. Appraisal & decision** | Expert committee reviews | Approve/reject with conditions |

### EIA Process Flow

```
PROJECT PROPOSAL
        ↓
SCREENING (Is EIA required? Which category?)
        ↓
SCOPING (Terms of Reference — what to study)
        ↓
BASELINE DATA + IMPACT PREDICTION + MITIGATION (EMP)
        ↓
EIA REPORT PREPARED (by accredited EIA consultant)
        ↓
PUBLIC CONSULTATION (Public Hearing)
        ↓
APPRAISAL (Expert Appraisal Committee / State EAC)
        ↓
ENVIRONMENTAL CLEARANCE (EC) GRANTED / REJECTED
        ↓
MONITORING (Compliance during construction & operation)
```

### EIA Categories (EIA Notification 2006)

| Category | Appraisal Authority | Public Hearing | Examples |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|----------|
| **Category A** | **MoEFCC (Central)** — Expert Appraisal Committee (EAC) | Mandatory | Mining >50 ha, thermal power >50 MW, major dams |
| **Category B1** | **State** — State EAC (SEAC) | Mandatory | Mining 5–50 ha, thermal 5–50 MW |
| **Category B2** | **State** — SEAC | Not mandatory | Small mining <5 ha, small industries |
| **General Condition** | If in sensitive area → Category A | Applies regardless of size | Protected areas, ESZ, coastal regulation zone |

### Projects Requiring Mandatory EIA (Schedule of EIA Notification 2006)

| Sector | Threshold for EIA | Examples |
|--------|-------------------|----------|
| **Mining** | >5 ha (B1); >50 ha (A) | Coal (Singrauli), limestone |
| **Thermal power** | >5 MW (B1); >50 MW (A) | NTPC, UPRVUNL plants |
| **River valley projects** | >50 MW hydro or >10 m height dam | Tehri, Ken-Betwa link |
| **Infrastructure** | Highways >100 km; airports | Expressways, airports |
| **Industries** | As per Schedule list | Cement, steel, refineries, tanneries |
| **Building/construction** | >20,000 sq m or >50,000 sq m (amended) | Large townships |
| **Special economic zones** | All SEZs | Noida SEZ, etc. |

### Key Components of EIA Report

| Component | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Executive summary** | Non-technical overview |
| **Project description** | Location, technology, capacity, land use |
| **Baseline environment** | Pre-project air, water, soil, noise, biodiversity |
| **Impact assessment** | Predicted impacts on all environmental parameters |
| **Alternatives analysis** | Site, technology, design alternatives considered |
| **Environmental Management Plan (EMP)** | Mitigation, monitoring, institutional measures |
| **Public consultation report** | Summary of public hearing proceedings |
| **Risk assessment** | Hazardous substances, disaster potential |

### Environmental Clearance (EC) Process

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **EC validity (construction)** | 10 years (renewable by 3 years) |
| **EC validity (operation)** | 30 years for mining; project life for others |
| **Conditions** | EC granted with specific compliance conditions |
| **Post-EC monitoring** | Six-monthly compliance reports to MoEFCC/SPCB |
| **Violation penalty** | Project closure, fines, NGT orders |
| **Revised EC** | Required for capacity expansion >50% |

### Strengths and Weaknesses of EIA in India

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|-----------|
| Legal mandate under EPA 1986 | Poor compliance monitoring |

## Important Facts

- **EIA Notification 2006** — current governing regulation in India.

- **Category A** — appraised by MoEFCC (Central government).
- **Category B** — appraised by State (SEIAA).
- **Public hearing** — mandatory for Category A and B1 projects.
- **EC validity** — 10 years for construction; renewable.
- **NGT** — adjudicates EIA disputes since 2010.
- **Rio Summit 1992** — Principle 17 mandated EIA for signatories.
- **NABET accreditation** — mandatory for EIA consultants (QCI).
- **Six-monthly compliance** — post-EC monitoring reports required.

# Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA)

## Definition

**Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA)** is a **systematic process of evaluating the environmental consequences** of **governmental policies, plans, and programmes (PPPs)** — at a **strategic level** — **before** individual projects are proposed, to **integrate environmental considerations into decision-making** at the earliest planning stage.

**Key difference from EIA:** SEA assesses **policies/plans/programmes** (strategic); EIA assesses **individual projects** (operational).

**UNEP definition:** *"SEA is a range of analytical and participatory approaches that aim to integrate environmental considerations into policies, plans and programmes and evaluate the inter-linkages with economic and social considerations."*

## Explanation

### SEA vs EIA — Comprehensive Comparison

| Feature | EIA | SEA |
|---------|-----|-----|
| **Level** | Individual project | Policy, plan, programme |
| **Timing** | After project conception | Before projects are identified |
| **Scope** | Specific project site | Regional/sectoral/cumulative |
| **Focus** | Direct impacts of one project | Cumulative, synergistic, indirect impacts |
| **Alternatives** | Site/technology alternatives | Policy/plan alternatives |
| **Legal status in India** | Mandatory (EIA Notification 2006) | **Not yet mandatory** (guidelines only) |
| **Public participation** | Public hearing | Broader stakeholder engagement |
| **Example** | EIA for one thermal power plant | SEA for National Electricity Plan |

### Levels of Environmental Assessment

```
STRATEGIC LEVEL (SEA)
  → Policies, Plans, Programmes
  → Example: National Mineral Policy, State Tourism Plan
        ↓
PROJECT LEVEL (EIA)
  → Individual projects within the plan
  → Example: EIA for specific mining lease
        ↓
OPERATIONAL LEVEL
  → Compliance monitoring during construction/operation
  → Example: Six-monthly EC compliance reports
```

### What SEA Evaluates

| Element | SEA Assessment | Example |
|---------|---------------|---------|
| **Cumulative impacts** | Combined effect of multiple projects | All mines in Singrauli basin |
| **Synergistic impacts** | Interactions between different impacts | Industrial + mining + transport together |
| **Indirect impacts** | Downstream consequences of policy | Population influx from industrial policy |
| **Regional carrying capacity** | Can the region sustain the plan? | Water availability for all planned industries |
| **Alternatives** | Different policy/plan options | Renewable vs coal-based energy plan |
| **Sustainability** | Long-term environmental sustainability | 20-year tourism plan impact on forests |

### SEA Process (Generic Framework)

| Stage | Activity | Output |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **1. Screening** | Is SEA required for this policy/plan? | Decision to conduct SEA |
| **2. Scoping** | What environmental issues to address? | SEA scope and methodology |
| **3. Baseline** | Current environmental status of region | Regional environmental profile |
| **4. Assessment** | Predict impacts of plan implementation | Impact analysis report |
| **5. Alternatives** | Compare policy/plan alternatives | Preferred alternative selection |
| **6. Mitigation** | Strategic mitigation at plan level | Environmental framework for plan |
| **7. Consultation** | Stakeholder and public engagement | Consultation report |
| **8. Decision** | Integrate SEA findings into plan | Environmentally adjusted plan |
| **9. Monitoring** | Track plan implementation impacts | Strategic monitoring programme |

### International SEA Framework

| Instrument | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **EU SEA Directive (2001/42/EC)** | Mandatory SEA for plans/programmes in EU |
| **Espoo Convention (1991)** | Transboundary EIA; SEA elements |
| **Kiev Protocol on SEA (2003)** | UNECE Protocol on Strategic Environmental Assessment |
| **Rio Principle 17** | Extended to include strategic assessment |
| **World Bank OP 4.01** | Environmental assessment for Bank-funded programmes |
| **UNEP SEA Guidelines** | Global best practice framework |

### Kiev Protocol on SEA (2003)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Full name** | Protocol on Strategic Environmental Assessment to Espoo Convention |
| **Under** | UNECE (UN Economic Commission for Europe) |
| **Adopted** | 2003 (entered force 2010) |
| **Scope** | Plans and programmes with environmental effects |
| **India status** | **Not a signatory** (primarily European protocol) |
| **Key provision** | Public participation; transboundary consultation |
| **Significance** | Only legally binding international SEA instrument |

### SEA in India — Current Status

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Legal mandate** | **No mandatory SEA law** in India |
| **EIA Notification 2006** | Covers projects only; not policies/plans |
| **Guidelines** | MoEFCC has issued SEA guidance (not binding) |
| **Practice** | Occasionally done for major plans (voluntary) |
| **Gap** | Policies approved without strategic environmental review |
| **Need** | Cumulative impact assessment lacking at plan level |
| **Advocacy** | Environmental groups demand mandatory SEA law |

### Benefits of SEA

| Benefit | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Early integration** | Environment considered at policy stage, not just project stage |
| **Cumulative impact** | Addresses combined effects of multiple projects |
| **Cost saving** | Avoids costly project-level conflicts later |
| **Better decisions** | Informed policy choices with environmental data |
| **Public participation** | Broader engagement than project-level hearings |
| **Sustainability** | Promotes long-term sustainable development |
| **Conflict prevention** | Identifies environmentally unsuitable regions early |
| **Legal certainty** | Developers know environmental constraints in advance |

### Limitations/Challenges of SEA in India

| Challenge | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **No legal mandate** | SEA is voluntary; not enforceable |
| **Institutional capacity** | Lack of trained SEA practitioners |
| **Political will** | Policies often prioritise growth over environment |
| **Data gaps** | Regional baseline environmental data lacking |
| **Overlap with EIA** | Confusion between SEA and EIA roles |
| **Enforcement** | Even EIA poorly enforced; SEA more neglected |
| **Cumulative impact** | No mechanism for regional cumulative assessment |
| **Monitoring** | No strategic-level environmental monitoring |

### SEA vs Related Concepts

| Concept | Level | Focus | India Status |
|---------|-------|-------|--------------|
| **SEA** | Policy/Plan/Programme | Strategic environmental integration | Not mandatory |
| **EIA** | Project | Individual project impacts | Mandatory (2006) |
| **Cumulative Impact Assessment (CIA)** | Regional/multiple projects | Combined project impacts | Sometimes in EIA |
| **Carrying Capacity Study (CCS)** | Region | Maximum sustainable development | Occasionally done |
| **Social Impact Assessment (SIA)** | Project/Plan | Social consequences | Sometimes combined with EIA |
| **Risk Assessment (RA)** | Project | Hazard and disaster risk | For hazardous industries |

### How SEA Would Improve Indian Environmental Governance

```
CURRENT (EIA only):
  Policy approved → Multiple projects proposed → Individual EIAs → Conflicts arise
        ↓
WITH SEA:
  SEA of policy/plan → Environmentally sustainable plan → Projects within plan
  → EIA for individual projects (within SEA framework) → Fewer conflicts
        ↓
BENEFIT: Singrauli would have regional SEA before 10+ coal plants approved
         Ganga basin would have SEA before multiple river projects
         NCR would have air quality SEA before industrial expansion
```

## Important Facts

- **SEA** — assesses policies, plans, programmes (not individual projects).
- **EIA** — assesses individual projects (mandatory in India since 2006).
- **India has NO mandatory SEA law** — only voluntary guidelines.
- **Kiev Protocol (2003)** — only binding international SEA treaty (UNECE).
- **EU SEA Directive (2001)** — mandatory SEA in European Union.
- **Rio Principle 17** — extended to include strategic-level assessment.
- **Cumulative impact** — key advantage of SEA over project-level EIA.
- **MoEFCC** — issued SEA guidance documents (non-binding).
- **Espoo Convention (1991)** — transboundary EIA; SEA elements included.
- **Gap in India** — policies approved without strategic environmental review.

## One Page Revision

```
SEA = Environmental assessment of POLICIES, PLANS, PROGRAMMES (strategic level)
EIA = Environmental assessment of INDIVIDUAL PROJECTS (project level)

SEA vs EIA: SEA = earlier, broader, cumulative | EIA = specific, mandatory
INDIA: NO mandatory SEA law | Only voluntary MoEFCC guidelines
EIA Notification 2006 = Projects only

INTERNATIONAL: Kiev Protocol 2003 (UNECE) | EU SEA Directive 2001
               Espoo Convention 1991 (transboundary)
KEY BENEFIT: Cumulative impact assessment at plan level
GAP: Singrauli, Ganga basin, NCR — no regional SEA before multiple projects
```
