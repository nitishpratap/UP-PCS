# IPCC

## Definition

The **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)** is the **United Nations body for assessing the science related to climate change** — established in **1988** by **WMO (World Meteorological Organization) and UNEP** — providing **scientific assessments** to governments for **climate policy decisions** without conducting its own original research.

**Established:** 1988.  
**Headquarters:** Geneva, Switzerland.  
**Nobel Peace Prize:** 2007 (jointly with Al Gore).

## Explanation

### IPCC — Key Details

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1988 |
| **Founded by** | WMO + UNEP |
| **Headquarters** | Geneva, Switzerland |
| **Chairperson** | Elected by member governments |
| **Type** | Intergovernmental scientific body |
| **Function** | Assess climate science; not conduct original research |
| **Members** | 195 member governments |
| **Nobel Prize** | Peace Prize 2007 (with Al Gore) |

### Structure of IPCC

```
IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change)
        ↓
THREE WORKING GROUPS:
  → WG I: Physical Science Basis of Climate Change
  → WG II: Climate Change Impacts, Adaptation, Vulnerability
  → WG III: Mitigation of Climate Change
        ↓
TASK FORCE:
  → TFI: National Greenhouse Gas Inventories
        ↓
BUREAU: Elected scientists and government representatives
        ↓
ASSESSMENT REPORTS: Published every 5-7 years
```

| Working Group | Focus |
|--------------|-------|
| **WG I** | Physical science — temperature, GHGs, climate models |
| **WG II** | Impacts on ecosystems, human systems; adaptation |
| **WG III** | Mitigation strategies — renewable energy, carbon reduction |
| **TFI** | Guidelines for national GHG inventory reporting |

### IPCC Assessment Reports (AR)

| Report | Year | Key Finding |
|--------|------|------------|
| **FAR (First)** | 1990 | Human activities causing warming |
| **SAR (Second)** | 1995 | Discernible human influence on climate |
| **TAR (Third)** | 2001 | Most warming due to human activities |
| **AR4 (Fourth)** | 2007 | Very high confidence in human causation; Nobel Prize |
| **AR5 (Fifth)** | 2014 | 95% certainty humans cause warming; basis for Paris Agreement |
| **AR6 (Sixth)** | 2021–2023 | Code Red for humanity; 1.5°C limit critical; irreversible changes |
| **Synthesis Report AR6** | 2023 | Final AR6 synthesis for policymakers |

### Key IPCC Concepts and Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Radiative forcing** | Change in energy balance of Earth system (W/m²) |
| **Carbon budget** | Total CO₂ that can be emitted for temperature target |
| **1.5°C pathway** | Limit warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial |
| **Tipping points** | Irreversible climate system changes |
| **Confidence levels** | Very high, high, medium, low, very low |
| **Representative Concentration Pathways (RCPs)** | AR5 emission scenarios |
| **Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs)** | AR6 emission scenarios |
| **Attribution** | Linking observed changes to human activities |

## Important Facts

- **Established 1988** — by WMO and UNEP.
- **Does NOT conduct research** — assesses existing peer-reviewed science.
- **AR6 (2021–2023)** — latest assessment report.
- **Nobel Peace Prize 2007** — IPCC + Al Gore.
- **195 member governments** — including India.
- **Headquarters** — Geneva, Switzerland.
- **Three Working Groups** — Science, Impacts, Mitigation.
- **AR5 (2014)** — scientific basis for Paris Agreement.
- **Code Red for humanity** — AR6 headline message.
- **1.5°C limit** — critical threshold in AR6.

# UNEP

## Definition

The **United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP)** is the **principal UN body for global environmental coordination and action** — established at the **Stockholm Conference (1972)** as the **first major outcome of global environmental diplomacy**, headquartered in **Nairobi, Kenya** (first UN headquarters in a developing country).

**Established:** 1972 (Stockholm Conference).  
**Headquarters:** Nairobi, Kenya.  
**World Environment Day:** 5 June (since 1973).

## Explanation

### UNEP — Key Details

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1972 (Stockholm Conference outcome) |
| **Headquarters** | Nairobi, Kenya |
| **First HQ in** | Developing country (Global South) |
| **Executive Director** | Elected by UN General Assembly |
| **Parent body** | United Nations |
| **World Environment Day** | 5 June (since 1973) |
| **UNEP@50** | 50th anniversary celebrated 2022 |

### Key Functions of UNEP

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Environmental coordination** | Coordinate UN environmental activities |
| **Treaty administration** | Administers multiple environmental conventions |
| **Scientific assessment** | Global Environment Outlook (GEO) reports |
| **Capacity building** | Support developing countries' environmental capacity |
| **Funding mechanisms** | GEF, Multilateral Fund (Montreal) |
| **Sustainable consumption** | Promote sustainable lifestyles |
| **Early warning** | Environmental disaster and risk monitoring |
| **Policy guidance** | Advise governments on environmental policy |

### Environmental Conventions Administered by UNEP

| Convention | Subject | Year |
|-----------|---------|------|
| **Vienna Convention + Montreal Protocol** | Ozone layer | 1985/1987 |
| **Basel Convention** | Hazardous waste | 1989 |
| **Rotterdam Convention** | Hazardous chemicals (with FAO) | 1998 |
| **Stockholm Convention** | POPs | 2001 |
| **Minamata Convention** | Mercury | 2013 |
| **CMS (Bonn Convention)** | Migratory species | 1979 |
| **CITES** | Wildlife trade (originally IUCN) | 1973 |

# UNDP

## Definition

The **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)** is the **UN's global development network** — working in **170+ countries** to **eradicate poverty, reduce inequalities, and build resilience** — with a growing focus on **environmental sustainability, climate action, and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** as integral to development.

**Established:** 1965 (merged UN Special Fund + Expanded Programme of Technical Assistance).  
**Headquarters:** New York, USA.  
**Administrator:** Highest UN development official (third in UN hierarchy after SG and DSG).

## Explanation

### UNDP — Key Details

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Established** | 1965 |
| **Headquarters** | New York, USA |
| **Countries** | 170+ country offices |
| **Administrator** | Appointed by UN Secretary-General |
| **Parent body** | UN General Assembly |
| **Funding** | Voluntary contributions from member states |
| **India office** | UNDP India (New Delhi) |

| **SDGs implementation** | Primary UN agency for SDG achievement |
| **Gender equality** | Women's empowerment across all programmes |
| **HIV/AIDS** | Health and development linkage |

# IUCN

## Definition

The **International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)** is the **world's oldest and largest global environmental organisation** — a **unique membership union** of **government and civil society organisations** providing **trusted knowledge and tools** for **biodiversity conservation and sustainable use of natural resources**.

**Founded:** 1948 (Fontainebleau, France).  
**Headquarters:** Gland, Switzerland.  
**Key contribution:** **IUCN Red List of Threatened Species** — global standard for species conservation status.

| **Commission experts** | 16,000+ volunteer scientists |
| **Status** | Observer at UN General Assembly |
| **Former name** | International Union for Protection of Nature (1948–1956) |

### IUCN Structure

```
IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature)
        ↓
MEMBERS: 140+ governments + 1,300+ NGOs
        ↓
SIX COMMISSIONS:
  → SSC: Species Survival Commission (Red List)
  → WCPA: World Commission on Protected Areas
  → CEC: Commission on Education and Communication
  → CEESP: Environmental, Economic and Social Policy
  → WCEL: World Commission on Environmental Law
  → CEGL: Commission on Ecosystem Management
        ↓
REGIONAL OFFICES: Including Asia (Bangkok)
        ↓
KEY OUTPUTS: Red List | Green List | Protected Planet
```
