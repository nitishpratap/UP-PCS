# Ozone Layer

## Definition

The **Ozone Layer** is a **region of Earth's stratosphere** (approximately **15–35 km altitude**) that contains a **high concentration of ozone (O₃) molecules**, which **absorb most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation**, especially **UV-B and UV-C**, thereby protecting life on Earth.

**NCERT definition:** *"The ozone layer is a deep layer in the stratosphere, encircling the Earth, that has large amounts of ozone in it."*

**Ozone (O₃)** — triatomic form of oxygen; formed by photochemical reaction: O₂ + UV → 2O; O + O₂ → O₃.

## Explanation

### Structure of Earth's Atmosphere — Ozone Location

| Layer | Altitude | Key Features | Ozone Role |
|-------|----------|-------------|------------|
| **Troposphere** | 0–12 km | Weather, clouds, human activity | Ground-level ozone = pollutant |
| **Stratosphere** | 12–50 km | **Ozone layer (15–35 km)** | Protective ozone shield |
| **Mesosphere** | 50–85 km | Coldest layer | Minimal ozone |
| **Thermosphere** | 85–600 km | Auroras, satellites | No ozone |
| **Exosphere** | 600+ km | Outer space boundary | — |

### Types of Ozone

| Type | Location | Nature | Impact |
|------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Stratospheric ozone** | 15–35 km | Beneficial — UV shield | Protects life |
| **Tropospheric ozone** | 0–12 km | Harmful pollutant | Smog, respiratory damage |
| **Ground-level ozone** | Near surface | Secondary pollutant (NOₓ + VOCs + sunlight) | Health hazard |

### How the Ozone Layer Forms (Chapman Cycle)

```
STEP 1: O₂ + UV-C radiation → 2O (oxygen atoms)
STEP 2: O + O₂ + M → O₃ + M (ozone formation; M = third molecule)
STEP 3: O₃ + UV-B/UV-C → O₂ + O (ozone absorbs UV — protective)
STEP 4: O + O₃ → 2O₂ (ozone destruction — natural balance)
```

**Natural equilibrium** — ozone production ≈ ozone destruction; concentration stable at ~300 Dobson Units (DU).

### Ultraviolet Radiation Classification

| Type | Wavelength | Absorbed By | Impact if Reaches Earth |
|------|-----------|-------------|------------------------|
| **UV-C** | 100–280 nm | Ozone + oxygen (fully absorbed) | Germicidal; lethal to life |
| **UV-B** | 280–315 nm | Mostly absorbed by ozone | Skin cancer, cataracts, crop damage |
| **UV-A** | 315–400 nm | Not absorbed by ozone | Aging, minor damage; reaches surface |

### Importance of the Ozone Layer

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **UV-B shield** | Absorbs 97–99% of harmful UV-B radiation |
| **Prevents skin cancer** | Without ozone, skin cancer rates would soar |
| **Protects eyes** | Prevents cataracts and corneal damage |
| **Ecosystem protection** | Phytoplankton, crops, forests depend on UV shield |
| **Immune system** | UV-B suppresses human and animal immunity |
| **Climate regulation** | Stratospheric ozone absorbs UV → heats stratosphere |

### Ozone Layer Measurement

| Unit | Definition | Normal Value |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Dobson Unit (DU)** | 0.01 mm ozone column at STP | Global average ~300 DU |
| **1 DU** | Equivalent to 0.01 mm thick pure ozone layer | — |
| **Ozone Monitoring** | TOMS, OMI satellites; ground stations | Dobson spectrophotometer |

### Ozone Layer vs Greenhouse Effect

| Feature | Ozone Layer | Greenhouse Effect |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| **Location** | Stratosphere | Troposphere (mainly) |
| **Gases involved** | O₃ (ozone) | CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs |
| **Primary issue** | Depletion (thinning) | Enhancement (warming) |
| **Effect of damage** | More UV reaches Earth | More heat trapped |
| **Protocol** | Montreal Protocol | UNFCCC, Paris Agreement |
| **Linkage** | CFCs destroy ozone AND are potent GHGs | |

## Important Facts

- **Ozone layer altitude** — 15–35 km in stratosphere.
- **300 DU** — normal global average ozone column thickness.
- **Absorbs 97–99%** of harmful UV-B radiation.
- **Without ozone layer** — no terrestrial life possible (UV-C lethal).
- **Ozone = O₃** — triatomic oxygen; different from O₂ (breathing oxygen).
- **Chapman (1930)** — proposed natural ozone formation/destruction cycle.
- **Dobson Unit** — standard ozone measurement (1 DU = 0.01 mm).
- **Tropospheric ozone** — harmful pollutant (unlike stratospheric ozone).
- **India** — among leading countries in Montreal Protocol compliance.

# Ozone Depletion

## Definition

**Ozone Depletion** is the **thinning or reduction of stratospheric ozone concentration** caused primarily by **anthropogenic release of Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS)** — especially **CFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, and methyl chloroform** — which catalytically destroy ozone molecules in the stratosphere.

**NOT caused by:** CO₂, automobiles directly, or aerosol sprays (modern ones are ODS-free).

## Explanation

### Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS)

| ODS | Chemical Formula | Main Uses | ODP (Ozone Depletion Potential) |
|-----|-----------------|-----------|--------------------------------|
| **CFC-11** | CCl₃F | Refrigerants, foam blowing | 1.0 (reference) |
| **CFC-12** | CCl₂F₂ | Refrigerants, aerosols | 1.0 |
| **Halon-1301** | CBrClF₂ | Fire extinguishers | 10.0 |
| **Halon-1211** | CBrClF₂ | Fire extinguishers | 3.0 |
| **Carbon tetrachloride (CCl₄)** | CCl₄ | Solvent, fire extinguisher | 1.1 |
| **Methyl chloroform (CH₃CCl₃)** | CH₃CCl₃ | Solvent, degreasing | 0.1 |
| **HCFCs** | Mixed H-C-Cl-F | Transitional refrigerants | 0.01–0.1 |
| **Methyl bromide (CH₃Br)** | CH₃Br | Pesticide, fumigant | 0.6 |

### How ODS Destroy Ozone (Mechanism)

```
STEP 1: CFCs released at surface → insoluble in water → reach stratosphere (years)
STEP 2: UV radiation breaks CFC → releases chlorine (Cl) free radical
        CCl₂F₂ + UV → Cl + other products
STEP 3: Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂ (ozone destroyed)
STEP 4: ClO + O → Cl + O₂ (chlorine regenerated — catalytic cycle)
STEP 5: One Cl atom destroys ~100,000 ozone molecules before deactivation
```

**Key point:** Chlorine acts as a **catalyst** — one atom destroys thousands of O₃ molecules.

### Causes of Ozone Depletion

| Cause | Mechanism | Status |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| **CFCs** | Release chlorine radicals in stratosphere | Phased out globally |
| **Halons** | Release bromine (even more destructive than chlorine) | Phased out |
| **Carbon tetrachloride** | Releases chlorine | Phased out |
| **Methyl bromide** | Releases bromine | Partially restricted |
| **HCFCs** | Transitional; less destructive but still ODS | Phase-out ongoing |
| **N₂O** | Indirect depletion (not controlled by Montreal) | Rising concern |
| **Volcanic eruptions** | Natural; temporary ozone reduction | Mount Pinatubo 1991 |

### Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP) vs Global Warming Potential (GWP)

| Property | ODP | GWP |
|----------|-----|-----|
| **Measures** | Ozone destruction ability | Heat-trapping ability |
| **Reference gas** | CFC-11 (ODP = 1) | CO₂ (GWP = 1) |
| **Highest offender** | Halons (ODP up to 10) | SF₆ (GWP 25,200) |
| **CFC-12** | ODP = 1.0 | GWP = 10,900 |
| **Both high** | CFCs, halons | CFCs are both ODS and potent GHGs |

### Effects of Ozone Depletion

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Increased UV-B** | More UV-B reaches Earth's surface |
| **Skin cancer** | 1% ozone depletion → 2–5% more skin cancer cases |
| **Cataracts** | UV-B causes eye lens clouding |
| **Immunosuppression** | Weakened immune response in humans and animals |
| **Crop damage** | UV-B reduces photosynthesis; rice, wheat, soy affected |
| **Phytoplankton death** | Marine food chain base damaged |
| **Forest damage** | Tree seedlings and leaves harmed |
| **Plastic degradation** | UV accelerates material weathering |

# Ozone Hole

## Definition

The **Ozone Hole** is a **severe seasonal thinning of the stratospheric ozone layer over Antarctica**, where ozone concentrations drop below **220 Dobson Units** (about 30% below normal), creating an area of dramatically reduced ozone coverage — **not an actual hole**, but a **thinning zone**.

**First discovered:** 1985 by **Farman, Gardiner, and Shanklin** (British Antarctic Survey, Halley Bay).

**Seasonal:** Forms each **Antarctic spring (August–October)**; recovers by **December**.

## Explanation

### Ozone Hole — Key Characteristics

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Location** | Primarily over **Antarctica** (South Pole region) |
| **Size (max)** | Up to **25–30 million km²** (larger than Antarctica itself) |
| **Timing** | Forms **August–October** (Antarctic spring) |
| **Threshold** | <220 DU defined as "hole" |
| **Worst year** | 2006 — largest recorded hole |
| **Recovery** | Expected by **2066** over Antarctica |
| **Arctic** | Smaller thinning events; no permanent "hole" |

### Why Over Antarctica? (Polar Stratospheric Clouds)

```
ANTARCTIC WINTER (March-September): Polar vortex traps cold air
        ↓
TEMPERATURE drops below −78°C in stratosphere
        ↓
POLAR STRATOSPHERIC CLOUDS (PSCs) form (ice crystals + nitric acid)
        ↓
PSCs provide surface for chlorine activation (Cl₂ → 2Cl)
        ↓
ANTARCTIC SPRING (September-October): Sunlight returns
        ↓
MASSIVE OZONE DESTRUCTION → "OZONE HOLE" forms
        ↓
SUMMER (November-December): Vortex breaks; ozone-rich air flows in → recovery
```

### Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs)

| Type | Composition | Role |
|------|------------|------|
| **Type I (nitric acid)** | HNO₃ + H₂O crystals | Chlorine activation |
| **Type II (ice)** | Water ice crystals | More efficient chlorine activation |
| **Temperature needed** | Below −78°C (−108°F) | Only in polar stratosphere |
| **Why Antarctica not Arctic** | Colder, more isolated vortex | Arctic is warmer; vortex less stable |

### Why Antarctica and Not Equatorial Regions?

| Factor | Antarctica | Equator/Tropics (India) |
|--------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Temperature** | Extremely cold stratosphere | Warmer stratosphere |
| **Polar vortex** | Strong, stable, long-lasting | No polar vortex |
| **PSC formation** | Extensive PSCs | No PSCs |
| **Ozone depletion** | Severe seasonal "hole" | Minimal (<5% thinning) |
| **India ozone levels** | Normal ~300 DU | Not affected by ozone hole |

### Impacts of the Ozone Hole

| Impact | Detail |

# Montreal Protocol

## Definition

The **Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer** is an **international treaty (1987)** designed to **phase out the production and consumption of Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS)** — primarily **CFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, and methyl chloroform** — to **protect and restore the stratospheric ozone layer**.

Widely regarded as the **most successful international environmental treaty** ever — **only UN treaty ratified by all 198 UN member states**.

## Explanation

### Montreal Protocol Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| **1985** | Vienna Convention for Protection of Ozone Layer |
| **1987** | **Montreal Protocol signed** (16 September) |
| **1989** | Entered into force |
| **1990** | London Amendment — accelerated CFC phase-out |
| **1992** | Copenhagen Amendment — further acceleration |
| **1992** | India ratifies Montreal Protocol |
| **1999** | Beijing Amendment — bromochloromethane added |
| **2007** | Accelerated HCFC phase-out schedule |
| **2016** | **Kigali Amendment** — HFC phase-down added |
| **2019** | India ratifies Kigali Amendment |

### Key Provisions of Montreal Protocol

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Phase-out schedule** | Graduated timelines for developed and developing countries |
| **ODS listed** | Annex A (CFCs, halons), B (other CFCs), C (HCFCs), E (methyl bromide) |
| **Multilateral Fund** | Financial assistance to developing countries for phase-out |
| **Trade provisions** | Ban on trade in ODS with non-parties |
| **Reporting** | Annual data reporting on ODS production/consumption |
| **Non-compliance mechanism** | Assistance rather than punishment |
| **Technology transfer** | Developed countries share alternatives |

### Controlled Substances and Phase-Out Schedule

| Substance | Developed Countries | Developing (India) |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **CFCs** | 1996 (complete) | **2010** (India completed) |
| **Halons** | 1994 | **2010** (India completed) |
| **Carbon tetrachloride** | 1996 | 2010 |
| **Methyl chloroform** | 1996 | 2015 |
| **HCFCs** | 2020 (99.5%) / 2030 (100%) | **2030** (India: 67.5% by 2025) |
| **Methyl bromide** | 2005 (with exemptions) | 2015 (with exemptions) |

| Feature | Detail |

### Montreal Protocol vs Other Environmental Treaties

### Kigali Amendment (2016) — Climate-Ozone Nexus

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Adds to Montreal Protocol** | HFC phase-down schedule |
| **Why HFCs** | Ozone-safe substitute for CFCs but potent GHGs (GWP 1000+) |
| **India schedule** | Freeze 2028; 10% reduction 2032; 85% by 2047 |
| **India ratified** | 2019 |
| **Global impact** | Avoid 0.5°C warming by 2100 |
| **Alternatives** | R-290 (propane), R-32, R-600a (isobutane), CO₂ |
