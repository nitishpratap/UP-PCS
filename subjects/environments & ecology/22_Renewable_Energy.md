# Solar Energy

## Definition

**Solar Energy** is **energy derived from the Sun's radiation** — harnessed through **photovoltaic (PV) cells** that convert sunlight directly into electricity, or **solar thermal systems** that use sunlight to generate heat — representing the **most abundant renewable energy source** on Earth.

**India target:** **500 GW non-fossil fuel capacity by 2030** (majority solar).

## Explanation

### Types of Solar Energy Technology

| Technology | Principle | Application |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Solar Photovoltaic (PV)** | Sunlight → electricity via semiconductor cells | Rooftop, solar farms, grid-connected |
| **Solar Thermal** | Sunlight → heat → steam → electricity | CSP (Concentrated Solar Power) plants |
| **Concentrated Solar Power (CSP)** | Mirrors focus sunlight to heat fluid | Large-scale power plants (Rajasthan) |
| **Solar water heating** | Sunlight heats water directly | Domestic, industrial hot water |
| **Solar cooking** | Parabolic/ box cookers | Rural household cooking |
| **Off-grid solar** | Standalone PV + battery | Remote villages, solar pumps |

### India's Solar Energy Programme

| Initiative | Year | Detail |
|-----------|------|--------|
| **National Solar Mission (JNNSM)** | 2010 | Target 100 GW solar by 2022 (achieved) |
| **International Solar Alliance (ISA)** | 2015 | India-led; HQ Gurugram, Haryana |
| **PM-KUSUM** | 2019 | Solar pumps for farmers |
| **Rooftop Solar Programme** | 2014 | Residential/commercial rooftop PV |
| **Solar Parks Scheme** | 2014 | Ultra Mega Solar Power Parks |
| **Production Linked Incentive (PLI)** | 2021 | Domestic solar manufacturing |
| **500 GW target** | 2030 | Non-fossil fuel installed capacity |

### Solar Energy Advantages and Limitations

| Advantages | Limitations |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Abundant** — 5,000 trillion kWh/year on Earth | **Intermittent** — only during daylight |
| **Zero emissions** during operation | **Land intensive** — large area needed |
| **Low operating cost** after installation | **High initial capital cost** |
| **Decentralised** — rooftop possible | **Weather dependent** — clouds reduce output |
| **Scalable** — small to utility scale | **Energy storage needed** for 24/7 supply |
| **Water-free** (PV) — unlike thermal plants | **Panel manufacturing** has carbon footprint |

# Wind Energy

## Definition

**Wind Energy** is **kinetic energy of moving air** harnessed by **wind turbines** that convert wind's mechanical energy into **electrical energy** — one of the **fastest-growing renewable energy sources** globally and in India.

**Principle:** Wind turns turbine blades → spins generator → produces electricity.

## Explanation

### How Wind Energy Works

```
WIND (kinetic energy of moving air)
        ↓
TURBINE BLADES rotate (like giant fans)
        ↓
GEARBOX increases rotation speed
        ↓
GENERATOR converts mechanical → electrical energy
        ↓
TRANSFORMER steps up voltage
        ↓
GRID transmits electricity to consumers
```

### Types of Wind Farms

| Type | Location | Features |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Onshore wind farms** | Land-based | Most common in India; cheaper |
| **Offshore wind farms** | Sea/coastal waters | Higher wind speeds; more expensive |
| **Distributed wind** | Small turbines near load | Rural, remote applications |
| **Hybrid wind-solar** | Combined installations | Complementary generation patterns |

### Wind Energy Potential in India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Total potential** | ~695 GW at 120 m hub height (NIWE estimate) |
| **Installed capacity** | ~42 GW (2023) — 4th largest globally |
| **Best states** | Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Rajasthan |
| **Offshore potential** | Gujarat and Tamil Nadu coasts |
| **Monsoon advantage** | Strong monsoon winds boost generation |
| **Target** | Part of 500 GW non-fossil by 2030 |

### Major Wind Energy States in India

| State | Installed Capacity (approx) | Key Features |
|-------|--------------------------|-------------|
| **Tamil Nadu** | ~10 GW | Muppandal — one of largest wind farms in world |
| **Gujarat** | ~9 GW | Coastal wind corridors |
| **Karnataka** | ~5 GW | Chitradurga wind farms |
| **Maharashtra** | ~5 GW | Satara wind corridor |
| **Rajasthan** | ~4 GW | Jaisalmer wind farms |
| **Andhra Pradesh** | ~4 GW | Anantapur wind corridor |

### Wind Energy Advantages and Limitations

| Advantages | Limitations |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Clean** — zero emissions during operation | **Intermittent** — wind not constant |
| **Cost-competitive** — among cheapest renewables | **Site-specific** — needs consistent wind |
| **Quick installation** — faster than thermal plants | **Land use** — large area per MW |
| **Rural employment** — maintenance jobs | **Noise** — turbine blade noise |
| **Water-free** — no cooling water needed | **Bird/bat mortality** — wildlife impact |
| **Mature technology** — well-established | **Visual impact** — landscape change |

### Wind Energy and Uttar Pradesh

| Feature | UP Detail |
|---------|----------|
| **Wind potential** | Limited — not among top wind states |
| **Installed capacity** | Very low compared to TN, Gujarat |
| **Reason** | Lower average wind speeds in Indo-Gangetic plain |
| **Potential areas** | Bundelkhand ridges, Vindhyan hills (limited) |
| **Policy** | UP Renewable Energy Policy includes wind |
| **Hybrid potential** | Wind-solar hybrid in Bundelkhand |
| **Exam note** | TN, Gujarat lead — UP has minimal wind |
| **Offshore** | Not applicable — UP is landlocked |
| **Future** | Small-scale distributed wind possible |

## Important Facts

- **695 GW potential** — India's wind energy potential at 120 m height.
- **42 GW installed** — 4th largest wind capacity globally.
- **Tamil Nadu leads** — highest wind capacity state.
- **Muppandal TN** — one of world's largest onshore wind farms.
- **NIWE** — National Institute of Wind Energy (Chennai).

- **UP limited wind** — Indo-Gangetic plain has low wind speeds.
- **Part of 500 GW** — wind contributes to 2030 target.

# Biomass Energy

## Definition

**Biomass Energy** is **energy derived from organic matter (biomass)** — including **plant materials, agricultural residues, forest waste, animal dung, and municipal organic waste** — converted into **heat, electricity, or biofuels** through **combustion, gasification, or biochemical processes**.

**Also called:** **Bioenergy** or **Organic Energy**.

## Explanation

### Sources of Biomass Energy

| Source | Examples | India Context |
|--------|----------|--------------|
| **Agricultural residues** | Rice husk, wheat straw, sugarcane bagasse | Most abundant biomass source |
| **Forest waste** | Wood chips, sawdust, forest residues | Forest-dependent communities |
| **Animal waste** | Cow dung, poultry litter | Gobar gas (biogas) plants |
| **Energy crops** | Jatropha, sweet sorghum, bamboo | Dedicated energy plantations |
| **Municipal solid waste** | Organic fraction of garbage | Waste-to-energy plants |
| **Industrial waste** | Paper mill, textile waste | Cogeneration in industries |
| **Aquatic biomass** | Algae, water hyacinth | Emerging technology |

### Biomass Energy Conversion Technologies

| Technology | Process | Output |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Direct combustion** | Burn biomass → heat → steam → electricity | Electricity, heat |
| **Gasification** | Heat biomass in low oxygen → syngas | Syngas for engines/turbines |
| **Pyrolysis** | Heat biomass without oxygen → bio-oil, char | Bio-oil, biochar |
| **Anaerobic digestion** | Bacteria decompose organic matter → biogas | Biogas (methane + CO₂) |
| **Fermentation** | Yeast converts sugars → ethanol | Bioethanol |
| **Transesterification** | Oils + alcohol → biodiesel | Biodiesel |
| **Cogeneration** | Combined heat and power (CHP) | Electricity + process heat |

### Biomass Energy in India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Potential** | ~18,000 MW from agricultural residues alone |
| **Installed** | ~10 GW biomass/co-generation capacity |
| **MNRE programme** | Biomass power and cogeneration programme |
| **SATAT scheme** | 2018 — Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation (CNG from biomass) |
| **Gobar-Dhan scheme** | 2018 — Galvanising Organic Bio-Agro Resources Dhan |
| **National Bioenergy Programme** | 2022 — unified biomass energy scheme |
| **Ethanol blending** | E20 target by 2025 (20% ethanol in petrol) |

### Major Biomass Energy Sectors in India

| Sector | Biomass Source | States |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| **Sugar industry** | Bagasse cogeneration | UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka |
| **Rice mills** | Rice husk | Punjab, Haryana, AP, UP |
| **Paper mills** | Wood waste | Multiple states |
| **Biogas plants** | Cow dung | Rural India nationwide |
| **Waste-to-energy** | Municipal waste | Delhi, Mumbai, Pune |
| **Ethanol production** | Molasses, grains | UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka |

# Geothermal Energy

## Definition

**Geothermal Energy** is **heat energy stored beneath the Earth's surface** — harnessed by tapping **natural steam, hot water, or hot rocks** from **geothermal reservoirs** to generate **electricity or provide direct heating** — a **reliable, baseload renewable energy** source available **24/7 regardless of weather**.

| **Direct use** | 25–150°C | Shallow | Space heating, greenhouses, bathing |
| **Enhanced Geothermal (EGS)** | Any | Deep | Artificially fractured hot rocks |
| **Ground source heat pump** | 10–20°C | Very shallow | Building heating/cooling |

# Tidal Energy

## Definition

**Tidal Energy** (Tidal Power) is **energy harnessed from the rise and fall of ocean tides** — using the **gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun** on Earth's oceans to drive **turbines and generate electricity** — a **predictable and renewable** form of energy with **high energy density**.

**Principle:** Tidal barrages or turbines capture kinetic/potential energy of moving tidal water.

## Explanation

### How Tidal Energy Works

| Technology | Principle | Application |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Tidal barrage** | Dam across estuary; water flows through turbines as tide rises/falls | Large-scale power generation |
| **Tidal stream turbines** | Underwater turbines in fast tidal currents | Open ocean/estuary turbines |
| **Tidal lagoon** | Enclosed lagoon fills/empties with tides | Coastal lagoon power |
| **Dynamic tidal power** | Long dam perpendicular to coast creates tidal phase difference | Experimental technology |
| **Tidal kite** | Tethered turbine moved by tidal currents | Emerging technology |

### Tidal Energy Cycle

```
MOON'S GRAVITY pulls ocean water → HIGH TIDE
        ↓
Water stored behind BARRAGE (potential energy)
        ↓
TIDE RECEDES → LOW TIDE
        ↓
Stored water flows through TURBINES → ELECTRICITY
        ↓
CYCLE REPEATS every ~12.5 hours (2 tides per day)
        ↓
PREDICTABLE generation — unlike solar/wind
```

### Tidal Energy Potential in India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated potential** | ~8,000–12,000 MW |
| **Installed** | Negligible — no commercial tidal power plant operational |
| **Best locations** | Gulf of Kutch (Gujarat), Gulf of Khambhat, Sundarbans |
| **Tidal range** | Gulf of Khambhat has highest tidal range in India (up to 11 m) |
| **Status** | Feasibility studies conducted; not yet commercialised |
| **Challenges** | High capital cost, environmental impact on estuaries |

### Major Tidal Energy Sites in India

| Site | State | Tidal Range | Potential |
|------|-------|------------|-----------|
| **Gulf of Kutch** | Gujarat | 4–6 m | Highest feasibility |
| **Gulf of Khambhat** | Gujarat | 8–11 m | Highest tidal range in India |

| **Kanyakumari** | Tamil Nadu | 1–2 m | Low tidal range |

### Global Tidal Energy Projects

| Project | Country | Capacity | Type |
|---------|---------|----------|------|
| **Sihwa Lake** | South Korea | 254 MW | Tidal barrage (largest) |

| **MeyGen** | Scotland | 6 MW (phase 1) | Tidal stream turbines |

### Tidal Energy Advantages and Limitations

| Advantages | Limitations |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Predictable** — tide tables known years ahead | **High capital cost** — expensive infrastructure |
| **Renewable** — gravity-driven, inexhaustible | **Environmental impact** — affects estuary ecosystems |
| **High energy density** — water 800x denser than air | **Limited sites** — needs high tidal range |
| **Long lifespan** — barrages last 75–100 years | **Sedimentation** — barrages trap silt |
| **No fuel cost** — gravity is free | **Fish migration** — barrages block fish passage |
| **Baseload capable** — 2 generation cycles/day | **India: not yet commercialised** |

# Small Hydro Projects

## Definition

**Small Hydro Projects (SHP)** are **hydroelectric power projects with installed capacity up to 25 MW** — harnessing the **energy of flowing water** in **rivers and streams** to generate **clean, renewable electricity** without large dams, reservoirs, or significant environmental displacement.

**Classification in India:** Projects up to **25 MW** classified as small hydro (MNRE).

## Explanation

### Classification of Hydro Projects in India

| Category | Capacity | Features |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Large hydro** | >25 MW | Big dams, reservoirs, displacement |
| **Small hydro (SHP)** | Up to 25 MW | Run-of-river, minimal storage |
| **Mini hydro** | 100 kW – 2 MW | Small streams, remote areas |
| **Micro hydro** | 5–100 kW | Village-level, off-grid |
| **Pico hydro** | <5 kW | Single household/community |

### Types of Small Hydro Projects

| Type | Principle | Environmental Impact |
|------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Run-of-river** | Uses natural river flow without large dam | Minimal — no reservoir |
| **Canal-based** | Uses existing irrigation canal drop | Very low — no new dam |
| **Dam-toe** | Small dam at toe of existing large dam | Low — uses existing structure |
| **Storage type (small)** | Small reservoir for peaking power | Moderate |

### Small Hydro Potential in India

### Small Hydro vs Large Hydro

| Feature | Small Hydro (≤25 MW) | Large Hydro (>25 MW) |
|---------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Classification** | Renewable energy | Not classified as renewable |
| **Reservoir** | Run-of-river (minimal) | Large dam and reservoir |

# Green Hydrogen

## Definition

**Green Hydrogen** is **hydrogen gas (H₂) produced through electrolysis of water using electricity generated from renewable energy sources** (solar, wind, hydro) — making it a **zero-carbon fuel** with **no greenhouse gas emissions** during production, unlike grey or blue hydrogen.

**Colour coding:** Green = renewable electrolysis | Blue = natural gas + CCS | Grey = natural gas (no CCS).

## Explanation

### Types of Hydrogen by Production Method

| Type | Production Method | Carbon Emissions | Colour Code |
|------|------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Green Hydrogen** | Electrolysis + renewable electricity | Zero | Green |
| **Blue Hydrogen** | Natural gas reforming + Carbon Capture (CCS) | Low | Blue |
| **Grey Hydrogen** | Natural gas reforming (no CCS) | High | Grey |
| **Black/Brown Hydrogen** | Coal gasification | Very high | Black/Brown |
| **Turquoise Hydrogen** | Methane pyrolysis (solid carbon byproduct) | Low | Turquoise |
| **Pink Hydrogen** | Electrolysis + nuclear electricity | Zero (nuclear) | Pink |

### How Green Hydrogen is Produced

```
RENEWABLE ENERGY (Solar/Wind/Hydro)
        ↓
ELECTRICITY powers ELECTROLYSER
        ↓
WATER (H₂O) split into:
  → HYDROGEN (H₂) — fuel/energy carrier
  → OXYGEN (O₂) — byproduct
        ↓
GREEN HYDROGEN stored/transported/used
        ↓
APPLICATIONS: Fuel cells | Industry | Transport | Power generation
```
