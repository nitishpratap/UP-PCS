# Red Data Book

## Definition

The **Red Data Book** is a **catalogue or inventory of species** whose **existence is threatened or endangered** — providing **information on the conservation status, distribution, and threats** to rare and endangered plants and animals, serving as a **national/regional supplement** to the global **IUCN Red List**.

**India:** Published by **ZSI (animals)** and **BSI (plants)** — documenting India's threatened species.

## Explanation

### Red Data Book vs IUCN Red List

| Feature | Red Data Book | IUCN Red List |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| **Scope** | National/regional | Global |
| **Publisher (India)** | ZSI (fauna), BSI (flora) | IUCN (international) |
| **Colour coding** | Red = endangered/threatened | Category abbreviations (CR, EN, VU) |
| **Purpose** | National conservation planning | Global species threat assessment |
| **First published** | India — ZSI Red Data Book | IUCN — 1964 |
| **Legal linkage** | Informs WPA Schedule listing | Informs CITES, CBD priorities |
| **Update frequency** | Periodic national publications | Continuous global updates |

### History of Red Data Book Concept

| Event | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **1963** | IUCN Species Survival Commission creates first Red Data Book concept |
| **1964** | First IUCN Red Data Book published |
| **1980s** | India begins national Red Data Book publications |
| **ZSI Red Data Book** | Documents threatened Indian fauna |
| **BSI Red Data Book** | Documents threatened Indian flora |
| **Red = danger** | Colour symbolises species at risk of extinction |
| **Green Data Book** | Sometimes used for species of least concern (informal) |

### ZSI Red Data Book (Indian Fauna)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Publisher** | Zoological Survey of India (ZSI), Kolkata |
| **Content** | Threatened and endangered Indian animal species |
| **Categories** | Extinct, Endangered, Vulnerable, Rare, Insufficiently Known |
| **Species covered** | Mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, invertebrates |
| **Use** | Wildlife conservation planning; WPA Schedule amendments |
| **Complementary** | Works with IUCN Red List global assessments |

### BSI Red Data Book (Indian Flora)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Publisher** | Botanical Survey of India (BSI), Kolkata |
| **Content** | Threatened and endangered Indian plant species |
| **WPA Schedule VI** | Protected plants listed in Red Data Book |
| **Species** | Orchids, pitcher plant, red sanders, endemic flora |
| **Herbarium linkage** | Based on Central National Herbarium specimens |

### Colour Categories in Red Data Book (Traditional)

# IUCN Red List Categories

## Definition

The **IUCN Red List Categories** are a **standardised system of classification** developed by the **International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)** to **assess and categorise the extinction risk** of **species, subspecies, and populations** globally — forming the **world's most comprehensive and authoritative inventory** of species conservation status.

**Established:** 1964 (Red List); current categories revised **2001**.  
**Threatened categories:** **CR + EN + VU**.

## Explanation

### Complete IUCN Red List Categories (2001 System)

| Category | Abbreviation | Extinction Risk | Criteria |
|----------|-------------|----------------|----------|
| **Extinct** | EX | None surviving | No reasonable doubt last individual died |
| **Extinct in the Wild** | EW | Only in captivity | Survives only in cultivation/captivity |
| **Critically Endangered** | CR | Extremely high | Facing extremely high risk of extinction |
| **Endangered** | EN | Very high | Facing very high risk of extinction |
| **Vulnerable** | VU | High | Facing high risk of extinction |
| **Near Threatened** | NT | Close to threatened | Likely to become threatened soon |
| **Least Concern** | LC | Low | Widespread and abundant |
| **Data Deficient** | DD | Unknown | Insufficient data to assess |
| **Not Evaluated** | NE | Not assessed | Not yet evaluated against criteria |

### Threatened Species — Three Categories

```
THREATENED SPECIES = CR + EN + VU

CRITICALLY ENDANGERED (CR) ← Highest risk
        ↓
ENDANGERED (EN) ← Very high risk
        ↓
VULNERABLE (VU) ← High risk
        ↓
NEAR THREATENED (NT) ← Not yet threatened
        ↓
LEAST CONCERN (LC) ← Safe
```

### IUCN Red List Criteria (Summary)

| Criterion | Basis | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **A** | Population reduction rate | >80% decline in 10 years (CR) |
| **B** | Geographic range | <100 km² extent of occurrence (CR) |
| **C** | Small population size | <250 mature individuals (CR) |
| **D** | Very small/restricted population | <50 mature individuals (CR) |
| **E** | Quantitative analysis | Probability of extinction >50% in 10 years (CR) |

## Important Facts

- **9 categories** — EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC, DD, NE.

- **India: 1000+ species** assessed on IUCN Red List.

# Wetlands

## Definition

**Wetlands** are **areas where water is the primary factor controlling the environment and associated plant and animal life** — including **marshes, swamps, bogs, fens, peatlands, floodplains, lakes, rivers, and coastal areas** where **water covers the soil or is present near the surface** for all or part of the year.

**Ramsar definition:** "Areas of marsh, fen, peatland or water, whether natural or artificial, permanent or temporary, with water that is static or flowing, fresh, brackish or salt."

## Explanation

### Types of Wetlands

| Type | Characteristics | India Examples |
|------|----------------|---------------|
| **Inland wetlands** | Freshwater; lakes, rivers, marshes | Loktak, Wular, Deepor Beel |
| **Coastal wetlands** | Salt/brackish water; mangroves, estuaries | Sundarbans, Chilika, Gulf of Kutch |
| **Riverine wetlands** | Along river banks and floodplains | Ganga, Yamuna, Brahmaputra floodplains |
| **Lacustrine wetlands** | Lake-associated wetlands | Dal Lake, Nainital lakes |
| **Palustrine wetlands** | Marshes, swamps, bogs | Keoladeo Bharatpur |
| **Peatlands** | Waterlogged organic soil | Himalayan peat bogs |
| **Artificial wetlands** | Human-made water bodies | Reservoirs, fish ponds, rice paddies |
| **High-altitude wetlands** | Himalayan tso (lakes) | Pangong, Tsomoriri |

### Ecological Importance of Wetlands

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Water purification** | Filter pollutants, sediment trapping |
| **Flood control** | Natural water storage and sponge effect |
| **Groundwater recharge** | Replenish aquifers |
| **Biodiversity hotspot** | 40% of all species depend on wetlands |
| **Carbon sequestration** | Peatlands store massive carbon |
| **Fish breeding** | Nursery grounds for fisheries |
| **Migratory bird habitat** | Critical stopover and wintering sites |
| **Livelihood support** | Fishing, agriculture, tourism |
| **Climate regulation** | Moderate local temperature and rainfall |

### Ramsar Convention and India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Convention** | Ramsar Convention 1971 |
| **India ratified** | 1981 |
| **Ramsar sites** | 80+ (as of 2024) — 3rd highest globally |

| **Wise use principle** | Sustainable utilisation without degradation |
| **Montreux Record** | Register of threatened Ramsar sites |

# Mangroves

## Definition

**Mangroves** are **salt-tolerant trees and shrubs** that grow in the **intertidal zones of tropical and subtropical coastlines** — forming unique **coastal wetland ecosystems** that serve as **nurseries for marine life, coastal protection barriers, and carbon sinks** — among the **most productive ecosystems** on Earth.

**Also called:** **Mangrove forests** or **tidal forests**.

## Explanation

### Characteristics of Mangroves

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Salt tolerance** | Adapted to saline/brackish water |
| **Location** | Intertidal tropical/subtropical coasts |
| **Root systems** | Pneumatophores (breathing roots), stilt roots, prop roots |
| **Reproduction** | Viviparous germination (seeds germinate on tree) |
| **Biodiversity** | High — fish, crabs, birds, reptiles |
| **Carbon storage** | Blue carbon — 4x more than terrestrial forests |
| **Coastal protection** | Buffer against cyclones, tsunamis, erosion |
| **Nursery grounds** | Fish, shrimp, crab breeding habitats |

| **Sonneratia apetala** | Mangrove apple | Sundarbans |
| **Excoecaria agallocha** | Blind-your-eye mangrove | Sundarbans, Odisha |
| **Bruguiera gymnorrhiza** | Large-leafed mangrove | Andaman, east coast |
| **Ceriops tagal** | Indian mangrove | Gujarat, Maharashtra |
| **Aegiceras corniculatum** | Black mangrove | East coast |

# Coral Reefs

## Definition

**Coral Reefs** are **underwater ecosystems formed by colonies of tiny marine animals called coral polyps** that secrete **calcium carbonate skeletons** — creating the **largest biological structures on Earth** and among the **most biodiverse marine ecosystems**, often called the **"rainforests of the sea"**.

**Cover:** Less than **0.1%** of ocean area but support **25% of all marine species**.

## Explanation

### Types of Coral Reefs

| Type | Formation | Location | Example |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Fringing reef** | Directly attached to shore | Near coastline | Andaman fringing reefs |
| **Barrier reef** | Separated from shore by lagoon | Offshore parallel to coast | Great Barrier Reef (Australia) |
| **Atoll** | Ring-shaped around lagoon | Submerged volcanic islands | Lakshadweep atolls |
| **Patch reef** | Isolated small reefs | Continental shelf | Gulf of Mannar |

### Coral Types

| Type | Feature | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Hard/Stony corals** | Build reef structure (calcium carbonate) | Brain coral, staghorn coral, table coral |
| **Soft corals** | No reef-building; flexible | Sea fans, sea whips |
| **Reef-building corals** | Hermatypic — contain zooxanthellae algae | Most shallow water corals |
| **Non-reef-building** | Ahermatypic — no symbiotic algae | Deep-water corals |

### Coral Bleaching

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Cause** | Stress from elevated water temperature (>30°C) |
| **Mechanism** | Corals expel symbiotic zooxanthellae algae |
| **Result** | Coral turns white (bleached); may die if prolonged |
| **Recovery** | Possible if stress removed within weeks |
| **Mass bleaching** | 2016, 2019 global mass bleaching events |
| **Climate change** | Primary long-term threat to coral reefs |
| **Other stressors** | Pollution, ocean acidification, overfishing |

### Coral Reefs in India

| Reef Location | State/UT | Type | Significance |
|--------------|----------|------|-------------|
| **Gulf of Mannar** | Tamil Nadu | Fringing | First marine biosphere reserve in India |
| **Lakshadweep** | Lakshadweep | Atoll | Pristine coral atolls |
| **Andaman & Nicobar** | A&N Islands | Fringing + Barrier | Richest coral diversity in India |
| **Gulf of Kutch** | Gujarat | Fringing | Northernmost Indian coral reefs |

### Ecological Importance of Coral Reefs

| Function | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Biodiversity** | 25% of marine species on 0.1% of ocean |
| **Fish nursery** | Commercial and artisanal fisheries |
| **Coastal protection** | Wave energy reduction up to 97% |
| **Tourism** | Snorkelling, diving revenue |
| **Medicine** | Compounds for cancer, HIV drugs |
| **Carbonate production** | Build islands and coastlines |
| **Food security** | Protein source for coastal communities |
| **Climate indicator** | Sensitive to temperature and pH changes |

### Threats to Coral Reefs

| Threat | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Climate change** | Warming → bleaching; acidification → skeleton dissolution |
| **Pollution** | Sedimentation, nutrient runoff, oil spills |
| **Overfishing** | Destroys food chain balance |
| **Destructive fishing** | Blast fishing, cyanide fishing |
| **Coastal development** | Sedimentation, direct damage |
| **Invasive species** | Crown-of-thorns starfish predation |
| **Coral mining** | Limestone extraction for construction |

### Coral Reefs and Uttar Pradesh

| Feature | UP Context |
|---------|-----------|
| **No coral reefs in UP** | Landlocked state — no marine ecosystems |
| **Exam relevance** | Gulf of Mannar, Lakshadweep, Andaman frequently asked |
| **Coral bleaching** | Climate change concept for UP PCS |
| **Marine biosphere** | Gulf of Mannar — first in India |
| **Comparative ecology** | Reef vs freshwater (Ganga) ecosystem distinction |

## Important Facts

- **"Rainforests of the sea"** — highest marine biodiversity.
- **0.1% ocean area** — supports 25% marine species.
- **Gulf of Mannar** — first marine biosphere reserve in India.
- **Lakshadweep** — coral atoll islands.
- **Andaman & Nicobar** — richest coral diversity in India.
- **Coral bleaching** — expulsion of zooxanthellae algae.
- **Zooxanthellae** — symbiotic algae providing colour and food.
- **Calcium carbonate** — coral skeleton material.
- **4 types** — fringing, barrier, atoll, patch.
- **Climate change** — primary threat to coral reefs globally.

# Grasslands

## Definition

**Grasslands** are **terrestrial ecosystems dominated by grasses and herbaceous plants** with **few or no trees** — occurring in regions where **rainfall is too low for forests** but **sufficient to support grass growth** — serving as **critical habitats for herbivores, grazing livestock, and unique biodiversity**.

**Types:** **Tropical grasslands (savannas)** and **temperate grasslands (prairies/steppes)**.

## Explanation

### Types of Grasslands

| Type | Climate | Location | Indian Example |
|------|---------|----------|---------------|
| **Tropical savanna** | Wet-dry seasons; 50–130 cm rain | Sub-tropical belt | Terai grasslands, Shola |
| **Temperate grassland** | Cold winters; 25–75 cm rain | Mid-latitudes | Himalayan alpine meadows |
| **Montane grassland** | High altitude; cold | Mountains | Alpine pastures, bugyals |
| **Semi-arid grassland** | Low rainfall; 25–50 cm | Arid/semi-arid | Rajasthan, Gujarat grasslands |
| **Wet grassland** | Seasonally flooded | Floodplains | Terai-Duar savanna |
| **Desert grassland** | Very low rainfall | Desert margins | Thar desert grassland |

### Major Grassland Ecosystems in India

| Grassland | Region | Key Species | Status |

# Himalayan Ecosystem

## Definition

The **Himalayan Ecosystem** is the **complex mountain ecosystem** spanning the **Himalayan mountain range** across **India, Nepal, Bhutan, China, and Pakistan** — characterised by **extreme altitudinal variation, diverse climate zones, unique biodiversity, and fragile ecology** that supports **snow leopards, musk deer, Himalayan monals, and thousands of endemic species**.

**Also called:** **Himalayan biodiversity hotspot** — part of **Himalaya-Hindu Kush global biodiversity hotspot**.

## Explanation

### Himalayan Altitudinal Zones (Ecological Stratification)

| Zone | Altitude | Vegetation | Key Species |
|------|----------|-----------|------------|
| **Tropical zone** | Up to 1,200 m | Sal, teak forests | Elephant, tiger, gaur |
| **Subtropical zone** | 1,200–2,400 m | Chir pine, broadleaf | Leopard, barking deer |
| **Temperate zone** | 2,400–3,600 m | Oak, rhododendron, deodar | Black bear, goral, monal |
| **Subalpine zone** | 3,600–4,800 m | Birch, juniper, meadows | Musk deer, blue sheep |
| **Alpine zone** | 4,800–5,500 m | Dwarf shrubs, bugyals (meadows) | Snow leopard, ibex |
| **Nival zone** | Above 5,500 m | Lichens, mosses, bare rock | Snow leopard, yak |
| **Trans-Himalayan** | Rain shadow (Ladakh) | Cold desert vegetation | Wild ass, Tibetan antelope |

### Himalayan Biodiversity

| Category | Numbers | Examples |
|----------|---------|----------|
| **Mammals** | 300+ species | Snow leopard, red panda, musk deer |

# Marine Ecosystem

## Definition

A **Marine Ecosystem** is an **aquatic ecosystem in saltwater (ocean/seas)** — encompassing **open ocean, coastal zones, estuaries, coral reefs, mangroves, and deep-sea environments** — covering **71% of Earth's surface** and containing **80% of global biodiversity**, making it the **largest and most diverse ecosystem** on the planet.

**India's coastline:** **7,516 km** — supporting diverse marine ecosystems.

## Explanation

### Zones of Marine Ecosystem

| Zone | Depth | Characteristics | Life Forms |
|------|-------|----------------|------------|
| **Intertidal (Littoral)** | 0–200 m (shore) | Tides, waves; high variability | Crabs, barnacles, seaweed, mangroves |
| **Neritic (Coastal)** | 0–200 m | Sunlit; high productivity | Fish, coral reefs, mangroves, dolphins |
| **Oceanic (Pelagic)** | 200 m to surface open ocean | Vast open water | Plankton, tuna, whales, sharks |
| **Benthic** | Sea floor (all depths) | Bottom-dwelling organisms | Starfish, sea cucumbers, deep-sea fish |
| **Abyssal** | 3,000–6,000 m | Dark, cold, high pressure | Bioluminescent fish, tube worms |
| **Hadal** | 6,000–11,000 m | Ocean trenches | Extreme-adapted organisms |

### Types of Marine Ecosystems in India

| Ecosystem | Location | Key Features |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Coral reefs** | Andaman, Lakshadweep, Gulf of Mannar | Highest marine biodiversity |
| **Mangroves** | Sundarbans, Bhitarkanika, Gujarat | Coastal wetland forests |
| **Estuaries** | Cochin, Hooghly, Narmada mouths | Fresh-salt water mixing |
| **Seagrass beds** | Gulf of Mannar, Palk Bay | Marine meadows; dugong habitat |
| **Open ocean** | Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal | Pelagic fisheries |
| **Deep sea** | Indian Ocean | Unique deep-sea species |
| **Tidal flats** | Gulf of Kutch | Migratory shorebirds |
| **Marine Protected Areas** | 31 MPAs in India | Conservation zones |
