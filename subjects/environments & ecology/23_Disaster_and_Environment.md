# Floods

## Definition

**Floods** are **natural disasters caused by the overflow of water** onto normally dry land — occurring when **rivers exceed their carrying capacity**, **heavy rainfall**, **dam breaches**, **glacial lake outburst**, or **storm surges** inundate areas, causing **loss of life, property, agriculture, and environmental damage**.

**Types:** Riverine floods | Flash floods | Coastal floods | Urban floods | Glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF).

## Explanation

### Types of Floods

| Type | Cause | Characteristics |
|------|-------|----------------|
| **Riverine flood** | Prolonged rainfall; river overflow | Slow onset; affects floodplains |
| **Flash flood** | Intense rainfall; cloudburst | Rapid onset; mountainous areas |
| **Coastal flood** | Cyclone storm surge; sea level rise | Coastal inundation |
| **Urban flood** | Heavy rain + poor drainage | Cities — waterlogging |
| **GLOF** | Glacial lake breach | Himalayan regions |
| **Dam-induced flood** | Dam failure or sudden release | Downstream inundation |

### Causes of Floods in India

| Cause | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Monsoon rainfall** | 75% annual rain in 4 months — river swelling |
| **Deforestation** | Reduced infiltration; increased runoff |
| **Siltation** | Reduced river carrying capacity |
| **Urbanisation** | Encroachment on floodplains; impervious surfaces |
| **Poor drainage** | Cities lack stormwater systems |
| **Dam releases** | Sudden water release from reservoirs |
| **Cyclones** | Storm surge in coastal areas |
| **Climate change** | Increased extreme rainfall events |
| **Embankment failure** | Breach of river embankments |
| **Glacial melt** | Himalayan river flooding |

# Drought

## Definition

**Drought** is a **prolonged period of abnormally low rainfall** leading to **water shortage** — affecting **agriculture, drinking water supply, livestock, and ecosystems** — classified as a **hydrological, meteorological, agricultural, or socio-economic** disaster depending on its impact.

**India:** Drought declared when **rainfall deficit ≥25%** for a prolonged period, or when **crop failure/water scarcity** affects livelihoods.

## Explanation

### Types of Drought

| Type | Definition | Indicator |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Meteorological drought** | Rainfall significantly below normal | Rainfall deficit % |
| **Hydrological drought** | Water bodies (rivers, lakes, groundwater) depleted | Reservoir/well levels |
| **Agricultural drought** | Soil moisture insufficient for crops | Crop wilting, failure |
| **Socio-economic drought** | Water shortage affects human activities | Fodder, drinking water crisis |

### Causes of Drought in India

| Cause | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Monsoon failure** | Weak/delayed monsoon — El Niño effect |
| **El Niño** | Pacific warming — suppresses Indian monsoon |
| **La Niña** | Generally favourable for monsoon |
| **Deforestation** | Reduced groundwater recharge |
| **Over-extraction** | Excessive groundwater pumping |
| **Climate change** | Changing rainfall patterns |
| **Poor water management** | Lack of rainwater harvesting |
| **Soil degradation** | Reduced water retention capacity |
| **Cropping patterns** | Water-intensive crops in dry areas |
| **Geographical location** | Rain shadow regions |

### Drought-Prone Areas in India

| Region | States | Characteristics |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| **Northwest** | Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana | Arid/semi-arid; low rainfall |
| **Central India** | MP, Maharashtra (Vidarbha, Marathwada) | Rainfall variability |
| **Deccan Plateau** | Karnataka, Telangana, AP (Rayalaseema) | Frequent drought |
| **Bundelkhand** | UP, MP border | Chronic water scarcity |
| **Western Rajasthan** | Thar Desert | Perennial drought conditions |
| **Odisha** | Kalahandi, Bolangir | Drought despite coastal proximity |

# Landslides

## Definition

**Landslides** are the **downward movement of rock, soil, and debris** on slopes due to **gravity** — triggered by **heavy rainfall, earthquakes, deforestation, construction, or volcanic activity** — causing **loss of life, infrastructure damage, and environmental degradation** in **hilly and mountainous regions**.

**Also called:** Mass wasting | Rockfall | Mudslide | Debris flow.

## Explanation

### Types of Landslides

| Type | Characteristics | Speed |
|------|----------------|-------|
| **Rockfall** | Free fall of rock from cliff | Very fast |
| **Slides** | Mass moves along defined surface | Moderate-fast |
| **Flows** | Mass moves like fluid (mudflow) | Fast |
| **Creep** | Very slow downhill movement | Very slow |
| **Topple** | Forward rotation of rock mass | Fast |
| **Debris avalanche** | Rapid flow of debris | Very fast |
| **Slump** | Rotational slide along curved surface | Moderate |

### Causes of Landslides

| Cause | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Heavy rainfall** | Water saturates soil — reduces friction |
| **Earthquakes** | Ground shaking triggers slope failure |
| **Deforestation** | Root binding lost; soil loosened |
| **Construction/road cutting** | Slope destabilisation |
| **Mining/quarrying** | Removes slope support |
| **Cloudburst** | Intense rainfall in short time |
| **Glacial retreat** | Unstable moraine slopes |
| **Volcanic activity** | Ash and debris flows |
| **Dam/reservoir** | Water level changes destabilise slopes |
| **Climate change** | Increased extreme rainfall |

### Landslide-Prone Areas in India

| Region | States | Characteristics |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| **Himalayas** | Uttarakhand, Himachal, Sikkim, J&K | Young, fragile mountains |
| **Western Ghats** | Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra | Monsoon-triggered |
| **North-East** | Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram | Heavy rainfall slopes |
| **Nilgiri Hills** | Tamil Nadu, Kerala | Steep slopes |
| **Eastern Ghats** | Odisha, Andhra Pradesh | Moderate risk |
| **Vindhyan** | MP, UP (limited) | Low-moderate risk |

# Cyclones

## Definition

**Cyclones** (Tropical Cyclones) are **intense low-pressure systems** over tropical oceans characterised by **spiralling winds ≥62 km/h** around a calm **eye** — bringing **heavy rain, storm surge, and destructive winds** — called **cyclones in Indian Ocean**, **hurricanes in Atlantic**, and **typhoons in Pacific**.

**India:** Most cyclones form in **Bay of Bengal** (more frequent and intense than Arabian Sea).

## Explanation

### Structure of a Tropical Cyclone

```
                    OUTER REGION (spiral rain bands)
                           ↓
                    EYEWALL (strongest winds)
                           ↓
                    EYE (calm centre, low pressure)
                           ↓
    WIND DIRECTION: Anti-clockwise (Northern Hemisphere)
    STORM SURGE: Sea water pushed inland → coastal flooding
```

### Classification of Cyclones (IMD)

| Category | Wind Speed (km/h) | Terminology (India) |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Low pressure** | < 31 | Depression |
| **Deep depression** | 31-49 | Deep Depression |
| **Cyclonic storm** | 50-62 | Cyclonic Storm |
| **Severe cyclonic storm** | 63-88 | Severe Cyclonic Storm |
| **Very severe cyclonic storm** | 89-118 | Very Severe Cyclonic Storm |
| **Extremely severe** | 119-165 | Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm |
| **Super cyclone** | > 222 | Super Cyclonic Storm |

### Cyclone Seasons in India

| Basin | Season | Frequency |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| **Bay of Bengal** | Pre-monsoon (April-May) + Post-monsoon (October-December) | ~5-6 per year |
| **Arabian Sea** | Pre-monsoon + Post-monsoon | ~1-2 per year |
| **Peak period** | May and November | Most intense cyclones |
| **Retreating monsoon** | October-December | Bay of Bengal active |

### Why Bay of Bengal Gets More Cyclones

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Warm water** | Temperature >27°C — fuels cyclone formation |
| **Shallow basin** | Continental shelf — storm surge amplification |
| **Low salinity** | River freshwater input — easier water heating |
| **Coriolis effect** | Sufficient latitude for rotation |
| **Monsoon remnants** | Low pressure systems from monsoon |
| **La Niña** | Favourable for Bay of Bengal cyclones |

# Heat Waves

## Definition

**Heat Waves** are **prolonged periods of excessively hot weather** — defined by **IMD** as when **maximum temperature reaches ≥40°C in plains** (or **≥30°C in hills**) with **departure of 4.5-6.4°C above normal** (heat wave), or **>6.4°C above normal** (severe heat wave) — causing **heatstroke, dehydration, mortality, and environmental stress**.

**India:** Heat waves are a **notified disaster** under Disaster Management Act 2005.

## Explanation

### IMD Heat Wave Criteria

| Region | Heat Wave Condition | Severe Heat Wave |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Plains** | Max temp ≥40°C + departure 4.5-6.4°C above normal | Departure >6.4°C above normal |
| **Coastal** | Max temp ≥37°C + departure 4.5-6.4°C | Departure >6.4°C |
| **Hilly** | Max temp ≥30°C + departure 4.5-6.4°C | Departure >6.4°C |
| **Duration** | Must persist ≥2 consecutive days | Same criteria, more extreme |

### IMD Heat Wave Alert Levels

| Alert | Colour | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Heat Wave Alert** | Yellow | Caution — heat wave conditions likely |
| **Severe Heat Wave** | Orange | Warning — severe conditions |
| **Extreme Heat Wave** | Red | Danger — extreme heat; high mortality risk |

### Causes of Heat Waves in India

| Cause | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **High pressure system** | Stagnant air — no cooling wind |
| **Delay in monsoon onset** | Prolonged pre-monsoon heat |
| **Deforestation** | Reduced shade and evapotranspiration |
| **Urban heat island** | Concrete absorbs and radiates heat |
| **Climate change** | Increasing frequency and intensity |
| **El Niño** | Often associated with hotter summers |
| **Western disturbances absence** | No cooling rain in north India |
| **Loo winds** | Hot dry winds from Rajasthan/Thar |
| **Reduced green cover** | Less evaporative cooling |
| **Industrial heat** | Urban industrial areas hotter |

### Heat Wave-Prone Areas in India

| Region | States/Cities | Characteristics |
|--------|--------------|----------------|
| **Northwest India** | Rajasthan, Gujarat, Delhi, Haryana, Punjab | Highest temperatures (50°C+) |
| **Central India** | MP, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh | Pre-monsoon extreme heat |
| **Indo-Gangetic plain** | UP, Bihar | May-June peak heat |
| **Deccan** | Telangana, Andhra Pradesh | Summer heat waves |
| **Urban centres** | Delhi, Ahmedabad, Nagpur, Lucknow | Urban heat island effect |

# Forest Fires

## Definition

**Forest Fires** (Wildfires) are **uncontrolled fires that spread through forest vegetation** — caused by **natural factors** (lightning, dry conditions) or **human activities** (arson, agricultural burning, carelessness) — destroying **forest cover, wildlife habitat, biodiversity**, and releasing **massive CO₂** contributing to **climate change**.

**India fire season:** **February to June** (dry pre-monsoon period); peak in **April-May**.

## Explanation

### Types of Forest Fires

| Type | Characteristics | Spread |
|------|----------------|--------|
| **Surface fire** | Burns ground vegetation, leaf litter | Slow, low intensity |
| **Ground fire** | Burns underground organic matter (peat) | Very slow, hard to detect |
| **Crown fire** | Spreads through tree canopies | Fast, most destructive |
| **Creeping fire** | Low flame, surface spread | Slow |
| **Running fire** | Fast-spreading with wind | Rapid, dangerous |
| **Spot fire** | Firebrands carried by wind start new fires | Ahead of main fire |

### Causes of Forest Fires in India

| Cause | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Human negligence** | Unextinguished campfires, cigarettes |
| **Agricultural burning** | Stubble/crop residue burning spreads |
| **Arson** | Deliberate fire setting |
| **Lightning** | Natural ignition in dry forests |
| **Friction (bamboo)** | Dry bamboo rubbing in wind |
| **Mahua collection** | Tribals set fires to collect mahua flowers |
| **Shifting cultivation** | Jhooming/slash-and-burn |
| **Dry weather** | Low humidity, high temperature, wind |
| **Pine needles** | Chir pine forests — highly flammable |
| **Climate change** | Longer dry seasons, more fire weather |

### Forest Fire-Prone Areas in India

| Region | States | Forest Type |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| **Himalayas** | Uttarakhand, Himachal, J&K | Chir pine, oak forests |
| **Central India** | MP, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra | Dry deciduous forests |
| **North-East** | Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram | Tropical forests |
| **Western Ghats** | Kerala, Karnataka | Dry season fires |
| **Andhra-Odisha** | Eastern Ghats | Dry deciduous |
| **Rajasthan** | Sariska, Ranthambore | Dry thorn forests |

| Impact | Detail |
