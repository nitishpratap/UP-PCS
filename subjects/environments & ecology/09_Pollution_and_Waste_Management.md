# Environmental Pollution

## Definition

**Environmental Pollution** is the **introduction of harmful substances or contaminants into the natural environment** (air, water, soil, land) that cause **adverse changes, harm to living organisms, and degradation of ecosystem quality**.

**NCERT definition:** *"Pollution is the addition of any substance or form of energy to water, air, or land at a rate faster than it can be dispersed, diluted, decomposed, recycled, or stored in some harmless form."*

## Explanation

### Types of Environmental Pollution

| Type | Primary Pollutants | Major Sources |
|------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Air Pollution** | PM2.5, PM10, SO₂, NOₓ, CO, O₃ | Vehicles, industry, stubble burning |
| **Water Pollution** | BOD, COD, heavy metals, pathogens | Sewage, industrial effluents |
| **Soil Pollution** | Pesticides, heavy metals, plastics | Agrochemicals, mining, waste dumping |
| **Noise Pollution** | dB levels > prescribed limits | Traffic, industry, construction |
| **Radioactive Pollution** | Uranium, radon, nuclear waste | Nuclear plants, mining |
| **Thermal Pollution** | Heated water discharge | Power plants, industries |
| **Marine Pollution** | Oil, plastic, sewage | Shipping, coastal discharge |
| **Plastic Pollution** | Microplastics, single-use plastic | Packaging, waste dumping |
| **Light Pollution** | Excess artificial light | Urban areas |
| **E-waste** | Lead, mercury, cadmium | Discarded electronics |

### Sources of Pollution

| Source | Category | Examples |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Point source** | Identifiable single outlet | Factory chimney, sewage pipe |
| **Non-point source** | Diffuse, scattered | Agricultural runoff, urban runoff |
| **Natural** | Non-human origin | Volcanic ash, forest fires, dust storms |
| **Anthropogenic** | Human activities | Industry, transport, agriculture |

### Effects of Environmental Pollution

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Human health** | Respiratory diseases, cancer, waterborne diseases |
| **Ecosystem damage** | Species loss, habitat degradation |
| **Climate change** | GHG emissions from pollution |
| **Economic cost** | Healthcare, lost productivity, cleanup |
| **Bioaccumulation** | Toxins accumulate in food chain |
| **Eutrophication** | Nutrient overload in water bodies |
| **Acid rain** | SO₂, NOₓ react with atmospheric moisture |

### Pollution Control Framework in India

```
CONSTITUTIONAL MANDATE (Art 48A, 51A(g), Art 21)
        ↓
ENVIRONMENT (PROTECTION) ACT, 1986 (Umbrella law)
        ↓
SPECIFIC ACTS: Water Act 1974 | Air Act 1981 | Hazardous Waste Rules
        ↓
INSTITUTIONS: MoEFCC | CPCB | SPCB | NGT
        ↓
STANDARDS: National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)
          Water Quality Standards | Noise Standards
```

# Environmental Degradation

## Definition

**Environmental Degradation** is the **deterioration of the environment through depletion of resources** (air, water, soil, forests, biodiversity) and **destruction of ecosystems** — caused by human activities and natural processes — leading to **loss of ecological integrity and sustainability**.

**Broader than pollution** — includes deforestation, desertification, land degradation, biodiversity loss, and climate change impacts.

## Explanation

### Forms of Environmental Degradation

| Form | Cause | Impact |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Deforestation** | Logging, agriculture, urbanisation | Biodiversity loss, climate change |
| **Desertification** | Overgrazing, deforestation, drought | Land becomes unproductive |
| **Soil erosion** | Deforestation, overgrazing, floods | Loss of fertile topsoil |
| **Water scarcity** | Over-extraction, pollution | Groundwater depletion |
| **Air degradation** | Emissions, dust | Respiratory diseases |
| **Biodiversity loss** | Habitat destruction, pollution | Species extinction |
| **Land degradation** | Mining, industrial waste, chemicals | Reduced productivity |
| **Wetland destruction** | Drainage, encroachment | Loss of ecosystem services |
| **Coral reef degradation** | Pollution, warming, acidification | Marine biodiversity loss |
| **Glacier retreat** | Climate change | Water security threat |

### Causes of Environmental Degradation

| Cause | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Population growth** | Increased resource demand |
| **Industrialisation** | Pollution, resource extraction |
| **Urbanisation** | Habitat loss, waste generation |
| **Agricultural intensification** | Pesticides, monoculture, water depletion |
| **Mining** | Land destruction, toxic waste |
| **Climate change** | Amplifies all degradation processes |
| **Deforestation** | 14.7 million ha lost globally/year |
| **Overgrazing** | Desertification in arid zones |
| **Plastic waste** | Soil and water contamination |

### India-Specific Degradation Issues

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Land degradation** | 30% of India's land area degraded (ISRO DESA report) |
| **Desertification** | 25% land affected; Rajasthan worst hit |
| **Ganga degradation** | Sewage + industrial effluents |
| **Western Ghats** | Mining, encroachment, deforestation |
| **Himalayan degradation** | Deforestation, landslides, glacial retreat |
| **Coastal degradation** | Mangrove loss, marine pollution |
| **Groundwater depletion** | Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan |
| **Stubble burning** | Air degradation in NCR |

### Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **SDG Target 15.3** | Achieve land degradation neutrality by 2030 |
| **India's target** | 30 million ha restoration by 2030 |
| **DESA report** | ISRO Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas |
| **UNCCD** | United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification |

### Environmental Degradation vs Pollution

| Feature | Environmental Degradation | Pollution |
|---------|--------------------------|-----------|
| **Scope** | Broader — resource depletion + ecosystem loss | Specific — contaminant addition |
| **Examples** | Deforestation, desertification | Smog, sewage discharge |
| **Time scale** | Often gradual, cumulative | Can be sudden or gradual |
| **Reversibility** | Often difficult/irreversible | Sometimes reversible with cleanup |

## Important Facts

- **30% of India's land** is degraded (ISRO DESA 2016).
- **96.4 million ha** — degraded land area in India.
- **25% land desertified** in India.
- **SDG 15.3** — Land Degradation Neutrality by 2030.
- **UNCCD** — India is signatory; combat desertification.
- **Ganga most degraded river** — 3,000 MLD sewage discharged.
- **Western Ghats** — 35% area ecologically degraded.
- **India loses 5,334 million tonnes** topsoil annually to erosion.

# Air Pollution

## Definition

**Air Pollution** is the **contamination of the atmosphere by harmful substances** (gases, particulate matter, biological molecules) that pose **health risks, reduce visibility, and damage ecosystems** — measured through **Air Quality Index (AQI)** and **National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)**.

**Primary pollutants:** PM2.5, PM10, SO₂, NOₓ, CO, O₃, Pb, NH₃, Benzene.

## Explanation

### Major Air Pollutants

| Pollutant | Source | Health Effect |
|-----------|--------|--------------|
| **PM2.5** | Vehicles, industry, burning | Lung cancer, heart disease, stroke |
| **PM10** | Dust, construction, roads | Respiratory illness |
| **SO₂** | Coal burning, industry | Asthma, acid rain |
| **NOₓ** | Vehicles, power plants | Smog, respiratory problems |
| **CO** | Incomplete combustion | Reduces blood oxygen |
| **O₃ (Ground-level)** | Sunlight + NOₓ + VOCs | Lung damage |
| **Lead (Pb)** | Leaded fuel (banned), industry | Neurological damage |
| **Ammonia (NH₃)** | Agriculture, industry | Respiratory irritation |

### Air Quality Index (AQI)

| AQI Range | Category | Health Impact |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| **0–50** | Good | Minimal impact |
| **51–100** | Satisfactory | Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people |
| **101–200** | Moderate | Breathing discomfort to people with lungs/heart disease |
| **201–300** | Poor | Breathing discomfort to most people |
| **301–400** | Very Poor | Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure |
| **401–500** | Severe | Affects healthy people; serious impact on sick |
| **500+** | Severe+ / Hazardous | Serious health impact even for healthy |

### Sources of Air Pollution in India

| Source | Contribution | Region |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Vehicular emissions** | 40–60% in cities | Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore |
| **Industrial emissions** | 20–30% | Vapi, Singrauli, Kanpur |
| **Stubble burning** | Major seasonal contributor | Punjab, Haryana, UP (NCR impact) |
| **Construction dust** | 10–15% | Urban areas |
| **Coal power plants** | SO₂, NOₓ, PM | Singrauli, Korba, Talcher |
| **Brick kilns** | PM, SO₂ | UP, Bihar, Bengal |
| **Domestic fuel burning** | PM2.5 | Rural India (biomass, dung cakes) |
| **Dust storms** | Natural PM10 | Rajasthan, Delhi |

### Air Pollution Control Measures in India

| Measure | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Air Act 1981** | Primary legislation for air pollution control |
| **NAAQS** | National Ambient Air Quality Standards (revised 2009) |

| **CNG/LPG push** | Clean fuel for transport and cooking |

# Water Pollution

## Definition

**Water Pollution** is the **contamination of water bodies** (rivers, lakes, groundwater, oceans) by **harmful substances** (sewage, industrial effluents, agricultural runoff, plastics) that make water **unfit for drinking, aquatic life, and other uses**.

**Measured by:** BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand), COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand), DO (Dissolved Oxygen), coliform count.

## Explanation

### Major Water Pollutants

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **Sewage** | Domestic wastewater | Pathogens, eutrophication |
| **Industrial effluents** | Factories, tanneries | Heavy metals, toxins |
| **Agricultural runoff** | Fertilisers, pesticides | Eutrophication, chemical contamination |
| **Oil spills** | Shipping, pipelines | Marine life destruction |
| **Plastics** | Waste dumping | Microplastics, marine harm |
| **Thermal discharge** | Power plants | Reduced dissolved oxygen |
| **Heavy metals** | Mining, industry | Bioaccumulation, toxicity |
| **Arsenic/Fluoride** | Natural geological | Groundwater contamination |

### Water Quality Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning | Safe Limit (drinking) |
|-----------|---------|----------------------|
| **BOD** | Oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter | Low = good |
| **COD** | Oxygen needed for chemical oxidation of pollutants | Low = good |
| **DO** | Dissolved Oxygen available for aquatic life | >5 mg/L |
| **pH** | Acidity/alkalinity | 6.5–8.5 |
| **Coliform count** | Bacterial contamination indicator | 0 MPN/100ml |
| **TDS** | Total Dissolved Solids | <500 mg/L |
| **Heavy metals** | Lead, mercury, cadmium, chromium | Below permissible limits |

# Soil Pollution

## Definition

**Soil Pollution** (Soil Contamination) is the **presence of toxic chemicals, heavy metals, pesticides, and waste materials in soil** at levels that pose **risks to human health, plant growth, groundwater, and ecosystem functioning**.

**Sources:** Industrial waste, agrochemicals, mining, sewage sludge, plastic waste, oil spills.

## Explanation

### Major Soil Pollutants

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **Pesticides** | Agriculture (DDT, endosulfan) | Bioaccumulation, groundwater contamination |
| **Heavy metals** | Mining, industry (Pb, Cd, Hg, Cr, As) | Toxic to plants and humans |
| **Industrial waste** | Chemical factories, tanneries | Soil sterility, toxin buildup |
| **Sewage sludge** | Untreated waste disposal | Pathogens, heavy metals |
| **Plastic/microplastics** | Waste dumping | Soil structure damage |
| **Acid rain** | SO₂, NOₓ emissions | Soil acidification |
| **Oil/petroleum** | Leakage, spills | Soil impermeability |
| **Radioactive materials** | Nuclear waste, mining | Long-term contamination |
| **Construction waste** | Demolition debris | Landfill contamination |

### Sources of Soil Pollution in India

| Source | Region | Pollutant |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| **Tanneries** | Kanpur, Unnao (UP) | Chromium (hexavalent Cr⁶⁺) |
| **Mining** | Singrauli, Korba, Jharia | Heavy metals, acid mine drainage |
| **Pesticide use** | Punjab, Haryana, Maharashtra | DDT residues, endosulfan |
| **Industrial clusters** | Vapi (Gujarat), Patancheru (Telangana) | Multiple chemicals |
| **E-waste dumping** | Moradabad (UP), Delhi | Lead, cadmium, mercury |
| **Fly ash** | Coal power plants | Heavy metals, alkalinity |
| **Urea overuse** | Indo-Gangetic plains | Nitrate soil contamination |

### Effects of Soil Pollution

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Crop contamination** | Heavy metals enter food chain |
| **Reduced soil fertility** | Microbial death, nutrient loss |
| **Groundwater pollution** | Leaching of toxins into aquifers |
| **Health impacts** | Cancer, neurological disorders, birth defects |
| **Ecosystem damage** | Soil organisms killed |
| **Land unusability** | Brownfield sites, wastelands |
| **Bioaccumulation** | Toxins concentrate up food chain |

### Soil Pollution Cases in India

|------|----------|-----------|
| **Bhopal Gas Tragedy** | Bhopal, MP | MIC gas; soil contamination persists |

### Soil Pollution Prevention and Remediation

| Method | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Bioremediation** | Microorganisms degrade pollutants |
| **Phytoremediation** | Plants absorb/remove toxins |
| **Soil washing** | Chemical extraction of pollutants |
| **Vitrification** | Heat treatment to immobilise toxins |
| **Organic farming** | Reduce pesticide/fertiliser use |
| **Crop rotation** | Maintain soil health |
| **Waste treatment** | Proper industrial waste disposal |
| **Fly ash utilisation** | Use in cement, bricks (not landfill) |

- **DDT** — banned 1989 in India; residues persist in soil.

# Noise Pollution

## Definition

**Noise Pollution** is the **excessive, unwanted, or harmful sound** in the environment that **exceeds permissible decibel (dB) limits**, causing **hearing loss, stress, sleep disturbance, and cardiovascular problems**.

**Measured in:** Decibels (dB); **A-weighted decibels (dB(A))** used for human perception.

## Explanation

### Sources of Noise Pollution

| Source | dB Level (approx.) | Location |
|--------|-------------------|----------|
| **Traffic** | 70–90 dB | Urban roads, highways |
| **Construction** | 80–100 dB | Building sites |
| **Industrial machinery** | 80–110 dB | Factories |
| **Airports** | 90–120 dB | Near runways |
| **Railways** | 80–100 dB | Near tracks |
| **Firecrackers** | 100–140 dB | Festivals (Diwali) |
| **Loudspeakers** | 90–110 dB | Religious/political events |
| **DG sets** | 80–95 dB | Residential areas |

### Noise Level Standards (India — EPA Rules)

| Zone | Day (6am–10pm) | Night (10pm–6am) |
|------|----------------|------------------|
| **Silence Zone** (hospitals, schools) | 50 dB(A) | 40 dB(A) |
| **Residential** | 55 dB(A) | 45 dB(A) |
| **Commercial** | 65 dB(A) | 55 dB(A) |
| **Industrial** | 75 dB(A) | 70 dB(A) |

*Silence zones: 100 metres around hospitals, educational institutions, courts, religious places.*

### Effects of Noise Pollution

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Hearing loss** | NIHL (Noise-Induced Hearing Loss) |
| **Sleep disturbance** | Insomnia, fatigue |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, heart disease |
| **Psychological** | Stress, anxiety, irritability |
| **Cognitive impairment** | Reduced concentration (children) |
| **Wildlife impact** | Bird communication disruption |
| **Productivity loss** | Workplace efficiency decline |

### Noise Pollution Control in India

| Measure | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **EPA 1986 + Noise Rules 2000** | Noise pollution standards |
| **Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000** | Permissible limits |
| **Supreme Court orders** | Firecracker restrictions, loudspeaker bans |
| **CPCB noise monitoring** | Real-time noise monitoring stations |
| **Green firecrackers** | CSIR-NEERI developed reduced-emission crackers |
| **Airport noise zones** | NOCA (Noise Exposure Maps) |
| **Vehicle honking rules** | No unnecessary honking in silence zones |

### Supreme Court Orders on Noise

| Order | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Firecrackers** | Restricted hours; green crackers only |
| **Loudspeakers** | Banned 10 pm–6 am (except 15 days/year) |
| **Silence zones** | Strict enforcement around hospitals/schools |
| **Diwali restrictions** | Time-bound cracker bursting |

## Important Facts

- **Noise Rules 2000** — under EPA 1986; permissible dB limits.
- **Silence zone limit** — 50 dB day, 40 dB night.
- **Firecrackers** — can reach 140 dB (pain threshold = 120 dB).
- **WHO** — recommends <55 dB day, <40 dB night for residential.
- **Delhi** — among noisiest cities in India.
- **Green firecrackers** — developed by CSIR-NEERI (2018).
- **24 April** — International Noise Awareness Day.
- **Hearing damage** — begins at 85 dB prolonged exposure.

# Radioactive Pollution

## Definition

**Radioactive Pollution** (Nuclear/Radiation Pollution) is the **contamination of air, water, and soil by ionising radiation** from **radioactive isotopes** released through **nuclear power plants, uranium mining, nuclear weapons testing, medical waste, and nuclear accidents**.

**Measured by:** Becquerel (Bq), Sievert (Sv), Gray (Gy); **Geiger counter** detects radiation.

## Explanation

### Sources of Radioactive Pollution

| Source | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Nuclear power plants** | Normal operations + accidents |
| **Uranium mining** | Tailings, radon gas |
| **Nuclear weapons testing** | Fallout (historical) |
| **Medical institutions** | Radioactive isotopes (Co-60, Cs-137) |
| **Nuclear accidents** | Chernobyl (1986), Fukushima (2011) |
| **Nuclear waste disposal** | Long-lived isotopes (Plutonium-239) |
| **Cosmic radiation** | Natural background radiation |
| **Thorium/monazite sands** | Natural radioactive beaches (Kerala) |

### Types of Radiation

| Type | Penetration | Source |
|------|------------|--------|
| **Alpha (α)** | Stopped by paper/skin | Uranium, radon, plutonium |
| **Beta (β)** | Stopped by aluminium | Strontium-90, carbon-14 |
| **Gamma (γ)** | Stopped by lead/concrete | Cobalt-60, cesium-137 |
| **Neutron** | Stopped by water/concrete | Nuclear reactors |

| **Number of reactors** | 22 operational reactors |

| **Three-stage programme** | Uranium → Plutonium → Thorium cycle |
| **Largest plant** | Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu) — 2,000 MW |
| **UP connection** | Narora Atomic Power Station (Bulandshahr, UP) |

### Effects of Radioactive Pollution

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|

| **Soil/water contamination** | Long half-life isotopes persist |

| **Ecological damage** | Sterility in plants and animals |

# Thermal Pollution

## Definition

**Thermal Pollution** is the **degradation of water quality by changing its temperature** — primarily through **discharge of heated water from power plants, industries, and deforestation** — causing **reduced dissolved oxygen, disrupted aquatic ecosystems, and species mortality**.

**Most common source:** Once-through cooling systems in **thermal power plants**.

## Explanation

### Sources of Thermal Pollution

| Source | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Thermal power plants** | Cooling water discharge (5–10°C warmer) |
| **Nuclear power plants** | Cooling system water discharge |
| **Industries** | Steel, paper, chemical plants |
| **Deforestation** | Reduced shade over water bodies |
| **Soil erosion** | Increased water temperature |
| **Urban runoff** | Heated pavements, stormwater |
| **Hydroelectric dams** | Altered water flow and temperature |

### How Thermal Pollution Affects Water Bodies

| Effect | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **Reduced DO** | Warm water holds less dissolved oxygen |
| **Fish mortality** | Temperature shock kills cold-water species |
| **Algal blooms** | Warm water promotes algae growth |
| **Metabolic changes** | Aquatic species metabolism accelerates |
| **Biodiversity loss** | Heat-tolerant species dominate |
| **Migration disruption** | Fish spawning patterns altered |
| **Eutrophication** | Combined with nutrients, worse blooms |

### Thermal Power Plants and Water Use

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **India's thermal capacity** | ~200+ GW (coal dominant) |
| **Water consumption** | 3–4 litres/kWh (once-through cooling) |
| **Coal plants in UP** | Singrauli, Obra, Anpara, Harduaganj |
| **Cooling methods** | Once-through (most polluting) vs cooling towers |
| **ZLD mandate** | Zero Liquid Discharge for new plants |
| **Water Act violation** | Heated discharge without permission |

### Thermal Pollution in India

| Location | Source | Impact |
|----------|--------|--------|
| **Singrauli (UP-MP)** | Coal power plants | Rihand reservoir temperature rise |
| **Korba (CG)** | NTPC plants | Hasdeo river thermal pollution |
| **Talcher (Odisha)** | Coal plants | Brahmani river impact |
| **Ennore (TN)** | Thermal plants | Coastal water heating |
| **Farakka (WB)** | NTPC | Ganga water temperature |

### Control Measures

| Measure | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Cooling towers** | Reduce thermal discharge |
| **Cooling ponds** | Allow heat dissipation before discharge |
| **Wet cooling systems** | More efficient heat exchange |
| **Regulatory limits** | EPA/Water Act temperature standards |
| **Fish ladders** | Mitigate migration disruption |
| **Afforestation** | Shade water bodies |
| **Closed-loop cooling** | Recycle cooling water |

## Important Facts

- **Thermal power plants** — largest source of thermal pollution in India.
- **Singrauli (UP)** — India's energy capital; severe thermal pollution.
- **1°C rise** in water temperature significantly reduces DO.
- **Fish die-off** — common near thermal power plant outfalls.
- **Once-through cooling** — banned/restricted for new plants.
- **Coal plants consume** ~4 litres water per unit electricity.
- **Rihand reservoir** — Singrauli thermal pollution hotspot.
- **EPA sets limits** on temperature increase in receiving water bodies.

# Marine Pollution

## Definition

**Marine Pollution** is the **introduction of harmful substances or energy into oceans and seas** — including **oil, plastics, sewage, heavy metals, agricultural runoff, and noise** — causing **damage to marine ecosystems, fisheries, coral reefs, and coastal communities**.

**Defined by UNCLOS (1982):** *"Introduction of substances or energy into the marine environment which results in deleterious effects."*

| **Land-based (80%)** | Sewage, plastics, industrial waste | Coastal degradation |
| **Shipping** | Oil spills, ballast water, garbage | Marine life death |
| **Offshore drilling** | Oil leaks, drilling muds | Ecosystem destruction |
| **Fishing industry** | Ghost nets, discarded gear | Entanglement of marine life |
| **Agricultural runoff** | Fertilisers, pesticides | Eutrophication, dead zones |
| **Atmospheric deposition** | Mercury, PCBs from air | Ocean bioaccumulation |
| **Plastic waste** | Microplastics, single-use items | Ingestion by marine animals |
| **Sewage discharge** | Pathogens, nutrients | Coastal water contamination |

# Plastic Pollution

## Definition

**Plastic Pollution** is the **accumulation of plastic objects and particles** (including microplastics <5mm) in the **environment — land, water, oceans** — that **harms wildlife, ecosystems, and human health** through ingestion, entanglement, and chemical leaching.

India generates **~3.5 million tonnes** of plastic waste annually; **~30% is recycled**.

## Explanation

### Types of Plastic Waste

| Type | Examples | Degradation Time |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| **Single-use plastic (SUP)** | Bags, straws, cups, cutlery | 100–500 years |
| **PET bottles** | Water, soft drink bottles | 450 years |
| **Polyethylene bags** | Carry bags | 200–1000 years |
| **Styrofoam** | Food containers, packaging | 500+ years |
| **Microplastics** | <5mm particles from breakdown | Persistent |
| **Microbeads** | Cosmetics, toothpaste | Pass through water filters |
| **Ghost nets** | Discarded fishing nets | Entangle marine life |
| **Mulch films** | Agricultural plastic films | Soil contamination |

### Plastic Waste in India

| Indicator | Data |
|-----------|------|
| **Annual plastic waste** | ~3.5 million tonnes |
| **Per capita waste** | ~11 kg/year |
| **Recycling rate** | ~30% (informal sector dominant) |
| **SUP consumption** | ~14 million tonnes/year (global: India 4th largest) |
| **CPCB estimate** | 25,940 tonnes/day plastic waste generated |
| **Uncollected waste** | ~40% not collected |

### Effects of Plastic Pollution

| Effect | Detail |

| **Microplastics in food** | Found in salt, fish, drinking water |

# Solid Waste Management

## Definition

**Solid Waste Management (SWM)** is the **collection, segregation, transportation, treatment, processing, and disposal of solid waste** (municipal, industrial, construction) in an **environmentally sound manner** — following the **waste hierarchy: Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover → Dispose**.

**India generates ~1.5 lakh tonnes** of municipal solid waste daily.

## Explanation

### Types of Solid Waste

| Type | Source | Examples |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Municipal Solid Waste (MSW)** | Households, commercial | Food waste, paper, plastic, glass |
| **Industrial waste** | Factories | Scrap, slag, packaging |
| **Construction & Demolition (C&D)** | Building sites | Concrete, bricks, debris |
| **Hazardous waste** | Industry, hospitals | Chemicals, biomedical |
| **E-waste** | Electronics | Computers, phones, appliances |
| **Agricultural waste** | Farms | Crop residue, stubble |
| **Plastic waste** | Packaging, SUP | Bags, bottles, wrappers |

| **Inert (dust, ash)** | 15–20% |

# Biomedical Waste

## Definition

**Biomedical Waste (BMW)** is any **waste generated during diagnosis, treatment, immunisation, or research activities** in **hospitals, clinics, laboratories, and blood banks** — including **infectious, pathological, pharmaceutical, and sharps waste** that poses **health and environmental risks** if not properly treated.

**India generates ~550 tonnes/day** of biomedical waste.

## Explanation

### Categories of Biomedical Waste (BMW Rules 2016)

| Category | Type | Colour Code | Examples |
|----------|------|-------------|---------|
| **Yellow** | Infectious/human/animal waste | Yellow bag | Dressings, swabs, body parts |
| **Red** | Contaminated recyclable waste | Red bag/discontainer | Tubing, bottles, gloves |
| **White (translucent)** | Sharps waste | White puncture-proof | Needles, blades, scalpels |
| **Blue** | Glass, metallic body implants | Blue container | Medicines, vials, ampoules |
| **Black** | General waste (non-BMW) | Black bag | Non-contaminated waste |

### BMW Treatment and Disposal Methods

| Method | Waste Type | Detail |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Autoclaving** | Infectious waste | Steam sterilisation (121°C, 15 psi) |
| **Microwaving** | Infectious waste | Microwave disinfection |
| **Incineration** | Pathological, pharmaceutical | High-temperature burning |
| **Chemical disinfection** | Liquid waste, sharps | Sodium hypochlorite treatment |
| **Deep burial** | Pathological (rural areas) | Underground disposal |
| **Shredding + disinfection** | Sharps | Destroyed before disposal |

### BMW Rules 2016 — Key Provisions

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Segregation at source** | Colour-coded bags at point of generation |
| **CBMWTF** | Common Biomedical Waste Treatment Facility |
| **Pre-treatment** | Labs must pre-treat waste before disposal |
| **Bar coding** | Waste tracking system |
| **Annual report** | Healthcare facilities report BMW generation |
| **Phase-out** | Mercury-containing devices by 2020 |
| **Training** | Staff trained in BMW handling |
| **PPE** | Personal Protective Equipment mandatory |

### Biomedical Waste in India

| Indicator | Data |
|-----------|------|
| **Daily generation** | ~550 tonnes/day |
| **Healthcare facilities** | 2+ lakh hospitals/clinics |
| **CBMWTFs** | 200+ common treatment facilities |
| **Compliance rate** | ~50% (many small clinics non-compliant) |
| **COVID-19 impact** | BMW generation increased 3–4 times |
| **UP generation** | ~50 tonnes/day (approx.) |

### COVID-19 Biomedical Waste Crisis

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Increased generation** | Masks, PPE kits, testing waste |
| **CPCB guidelines** | Special COVID BMW handling protocol |
| **Colour coding** | Yellow bags for COVID waste |

| Risk | Detail |
|------|--------|

# Hazardous Waste

## Definition

**Hazardous Waste** is waste that exhibits **dangerous properties** — **ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, or toxicity** — and poses **substantial threats to human health and the environment** if not managed properly.

Includes **industrial chemicals, solvents, heavy metals, pesticides, batteries, and radioactive materials**.

## Explanation

### Characteristics of Hazardous Waste (HWM Rules 2016)

| Characteristic | Property | Example |
|---------------|----------|---------|
| **Ignitable** | Catches fire easily | Solvents, paints, oils |
| **Corrosive** | Dissolves metals, burns skin | Acids (H₂SO₄), bases (NaOH) |
| **Reactive** | Explosive, unstable | Cyanides, sulfides |
| **Toxic** | Harmful when ingested/absorbed | Heavy metals, pesticides |
| **Infectious** | Contains pathogens | Biomedical waste (separate rules) |
| **Eco-toxic** | Harmful to ecosystems | Pesticides, PCBs |

### Categories of Hazardous Waste in India

| Category | Source | Examples |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **Industrial process waste** | Manufacturing | Sludge, spent catalysts, residues |
| **Chemical waste** | Chemical industry | Acids, alkalis, solvents |
| **Metal-bearing waste** | Metallurgy, electroplating | Chromium, cadmium, lead sludge |
| **Pesticide waste** | Agriculture, industry | Expired pesticides, containers |
| **Petroleum waste** | Oil refineries | Oily sludge, tank bottom sludge |
| **Battery waste** | Used batteries | Lead-acid, lithium-ion |
| **Asbestos waste** | Construction | Asbestos sheets, insulation |

### Hazardous Waste Management Rules 2016

| Provision | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Authorisation** | Hazardous waste generators need SPCB authorisation |
| **Storage** | Labelled containers; max 90 days storage |
| **Treatment** | At authorised Treatment, Storage, Disposal Facility (TSDF) |
| **Manifest system** | Waste tracking from generator to disposal |
| **Import/export** | Regulated under Basel Convention |
| **Prohibited import** | Hazardous waste import banned (mostly) |
| **Co-processing** | Use in cement kilns as alternative fuel |
| **Landfill** | Secure hazardous waste landfills only |

### Treatment, Storage, and Disposal Facilities (TSDFs)

| Facility | Location | Capacity |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Taloja, Maharashtra** | Navi Mumbai | Western India TSDF |
| **Dindoli, Gujarat** | Surat | Chemical waste disposal |
| **Bhiwandi, Maharashtra** | Thane | Industrial waste |
| **Ghuma, Gujarat** | Ahmedabad | Hazardous landfill |
| **Whitefield, Karnataka** | Bangalore | Southern India TSDF |
| **Mathura, UP** | Uttar Pradesh | Northern India facility |

### Basel Convention

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Adopted** | 1989 (entered force 1992) |
| **Purpose** | Control transboundary movement of hazardous waste |
| **Principle** | Generator pays; prior informed consent |
| **India** | Signatory since 1992 |
| **Ban amendment** | Prohibits export of hazardous waste from developed to developing countries |
| **Rotterdam Convention** | Prior informed consent for hazardous chemicals |
| **Stockholm Convention** | Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) |

## Important Facts

- **HWM Rules 2016** — replaced 2008, 2003 rules.

# E-waste

## Definition

**E-waste (Electronic Waste)** is **discarded electrical and electronic equipment** (EEE) — including **computers, mobile phones, televisions, refrigerators, and circuit boards** — that contains **toxic materials** (lead, mercury, cadmium, beryllium) and **valuable recoverable metals** (gold, silver, copper).

India is the **3rd largest e-waste generator** globally (~1.6 million tonnes/year).

## Explanation

### Sources of E-waste

| Source | Examples |
|--------|----------|
| **Household** | TVs, refrigerators, washing machines, ACs |
| **IT/telecom** | Computers, servers, mobile phones, printers |
| **Industrial** | Medical equipment, monitoring instruments |
| **Consumer electronics** | Cameras, music players, gaming consoles |
| **Lighting** | CFL, LED bulbs, tube lights |
| **Batteries** | Lithium-ion, lead-acid, nickel-cadmium |

### E-waste Composition

| Material | Percentage | Hazard Level |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Iron/steel** | 35–40% | Low |
| **Plastics** | 20–25% | Moderate (BFRs) |
| **Glass** | 5–10% | Low |
| **Copper** | 10–15% | Low (valuable) |
| **Precious metals** | 0.5–1% | Gold, silver, palladium |
| **Toxic metals** | 1–5% | Lead, mercury, cadmium, chromium |
| **Others** | 10–15% | Ceramics, rubber, wood |

### E-waste Management Rules

| Rule | Year | Key Provision |
|------|------|--------------|
| **E-waste Rules** | 2016 (amended 2018, 2022) | EPR mandatory |
| **EPR framework** | 2022 | Producers collect e-waste |
| **Collection targets** | Increasing yearly | 60% → 70% → 80% |
| **RoHS compliance** | Restriction of Hazardous Substances | Limit lead, mercury, cadmium |
| **Authorised recyclers** | CPCB registered | Only authorised dismantlers/recyclers |
| **Consumer responsibility** | Hand over to authorised collection centres |

### Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) — E-waste

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Producers** | Manufacturers, importers, brand owners |
| **Responsibility** | Collect and recycle post-consumer e-waste |
| **Registration** | Mandatory on CPCB portal |
| **Collection targets** | 60% (2023–24) increasing to 80% |
| **EPR certificates** | Tradeable recycling certificates |
| **Penalties** | Environmental compensation for non-compliance |
| **Deposit-refund scheme** | Proposed for consumer returns |

### E-waste Recycling in India

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Formal recycling** | ~5% (authorised recyclers) |
| **Informal sector** | ~95% (ragpickers, small workshops) |
| **E-waste hubs** | Moradabad (UP), Seelampur (Delhi), Mumbai |
| **Health impact** | Lead poisoning, respiratory diseases |
| **Child labour** | Common in informal e-waste dismantling |
| **Gold recovery** | Informal acid leaching (highly toxic) |
| **Authorized recyclers** | 400+ CPCB-registered recyclers |

### E-waste Hotspots in India

| Location | Specialisation | Health Impact |
|----------|---------------|--------------|
| **Moradabad, UP** | Circuit board processing | Lead, cadmium poisoning |
| **Seelampur, Delhi** | Circuit board dismantling | Acid burns, respiratory |
| **Mumbai** | General e-waste recycling | Heavy metal exposure |
| **Bangalore** | IT e-waste hub | Growing formal sector |
| **Chennai** | Electronics manufacturing waste | Industrial e-waste |
| **Loni, Ghaziabad (UP)** | Wire burning | Dioxin, furan release |

### Toxic Components in E-waste

| Component | Toxic Substance | Health Effect |
|-----------|----------------|--------------|
| **CRT monitors** | Lead (4–8 lbs/screen) | Neurological damage |
| **Circuit boards** | Lead, cadmium, mercury | Kidney, brain damage |
| **Batteries** | Lithium, cadmium, lead | Heavy metal poisoning |
| **Plastics** | Brominated flame retardants | Endocrine disruption |
| **Capacitors** | PCBs, polychlorinated biphenyls | Cancer |
| **Solder** | Lead-tin alloy | Developmental disorders |
