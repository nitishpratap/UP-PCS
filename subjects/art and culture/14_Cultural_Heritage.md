## 14.1 Tangible Cultural Heritage

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Tangible cultural heritage** = **physical, material** cultural assets — monuments, buildings, sculptures, archaeological sites, historic towns, cultural landscapes — that can be **preserved in situ or museums** |
| **UNESCO** | Tangible heritage includes **movable and immovable** cultural property of **historical, artistic, scientific, social, or spiritual value** |
| **Exam usage** | Distinguish from intangible (dance, ritual); link to ASI protection, museums, World Heritage inscription |

### Tangible Cultural Heritage — How It Works

- **Tangible cultural heritage** is what tourists **photograph and archaeologists excavate** — the **material evidence** of civilisation.
- **Categories of tangible heritage**:

  | Category | Examples in India |
  |----------|-------------------|
  | **Monuments** | Taj Mahal, Qutub Minar, Sanchi Stupa |
  | **Archaeological sites** | Dholavira, Nalanda ruins, Hampi |
  | **Historic cities/towns** | Ahmedabad walled city, Jaipur pink city |
  | **Cave complexes** | Ajanta, Ellora, Elephanta, Bhimbetka |
  | **Temple ensembles** | Khajuraho, Hoysala, Chola temples |
  | **Museums/collections** | National Museum Delhi, Indian Museum Kolkata |
  | **Cultural landscapes** | Champaner-Pavagadh, Nanda Devi landscape |

- **Legal protection in India**:
  - **ASI** — maintains **protected monuments** under **AMASR Act 1958** (~3,600+ centrally protected).
  - **State archaeology departments** — additional monuments.
  - **National Mission on Monuments and Antiquities (NMMA)** — documentation.
  - **Trap**: ASI protected ≠ automatically UNESCO World Heritage — separate inscription process.

- **Threats to tangible heritage**:
  - **Pollution** (Taj Mahal marble yellowing — Mathura oil refineries debate).
  - **Urbanisation** encroachment around monuments.
  - **Tourism pressure** — wear on fragile sites (Ajanta caves closed cyclically).
  - **Natural disasters** — floods, earthquakes (Kedarnath context).
  - **Illegal excavation/antiquity smuggling** — Antiquities and Art Treasures Act 1972.

- **Conservation methods**:
  - **Structural stabilisation** — underpinning, drainage (Humayun's Tomb restoration model).
  - **Chemical cleaning** — controlled marble/sandstone treatment.
  - **Documentation** — laser scanning, photogrammetry (Hampi, Taj).
  - **Heritage bylaws** — buffer zones around WHS (Agra Taj Trapezium Zone).

- **Tangible vs natural heritage overlap**: Some sites are **natural** (Kaziranga) or **mixed** (Khangchendzonga) — still tangible in physical sense but classified under natural/mixed WHS criteria.

- **UP tangible heritage highlights**:
  - **3 UNESCO WHS** — Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri (see §14.5).
  - **Sarnath** — ASI protected Buddhist site (not WHS individually; part of Buddhist circuit).
  - **Varanasi ghats** — on **Tentative List**, not yet inscribed.
  - **Lucknow Residency, Bara Imambara** — state/ASI heritage, not WHS.

> **Exam note:** **Tangible = physical monument/site** | **Intangible = Ramlila/Kumbh performance** — never classify Taj Mahal as intangible.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- Tangible = physical monuments, sites, artefacts
- ASI protects monuments under AMASR Act 1958
- Categories: monuments, caves, cities, temples, museums
- Threats: pollution, encroachment, tourism, smuggling
- Conservation: structural, chemical, documentation, buffer zones
- ASI protected ≠ UNESCO WHS automatically
- UP tangible WHS = Taj, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri (only 3)
- Varanasi on Tentative List, not inscribed yet
- Movable heritage = museum collections, antiquities
- Tangible heritage primary focus of World Heritage Convention 1972

## 14.2 Intangible Cultural Heritage

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Intangible cultural heritage** = **non-physical** traditions — oral expressions, performing arts, social practices, rituals, festive events, traditional craft knowledge — transmitted **generation to generation** |
| **UNESCO 2003 Convention** | ICH = practices, representations, expressions, knowledge, skills — communities recognise as part of **cultural identity** |
| **Exam usage** | Match element ↔ state; distinguish from tangible WHS; know India has 15 Representative List elements |

### Intangible Cultural Heritage — How It Works

- **Intangible cultural heritage** is **"living culture"** — it exists only while **communities perform and transmit** it.
- **Five domains of intangible heritage** (UNESCO 2003):

  | Domain | India examples |
  |--------|----------------|
  | **Oral traditions** | Vedic chanting, folk epics (Pandavani) |
  | **Performing arts** | Kutiyattam, Ramlila, Chhau, Garba, Sankirtana |
  | **Social practices/rituals** | Kumbh Mela, Ramman festival, Durga Puja |
  | **Knowledge of nature/universe** | Traditional medicine systems (Ayurveda context — separate UNESCO debates) |
  | **Traditional craft skills** | Punjab brass/copper craft (2014 inscription) |

- **Why intangible heritage needs separate protection**:
  - **Globalisation** kills dialects, rituals, artisan skills.
  - **Unlike monuments**, intangible forms **die when last practitioner dies** without transmission.
  - **2003 Convention** created after realising World Heritage Convention (1972) covered only **tangible** sites.

- **Transmission mechanism**:
  - **Guru-shishya parampara** — classical and folk (Ramlila akhadas).
  - **Community festival cycle** — Kumbh Mela, Durga Puja pandals.
  - **Family craft guilds** — brass craft Punjab, Chikankari Lucknow (GI + intangible overlap).

- **India's intangible heritage status** (overview):
  - **15 elements** on UNESCO **Representative List** (as of 2023).
  - **Yoga** inscribed 2016 — global Indian soft power symbol.
  - **Kumbh Mela 2017** — largest peaceful gathering intangible element.
  - **Garba 2023** — latest major Gujarat inscription.
  - **NOT inscribed as named items**: Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Nautanki individually — trap in negative questions.

- **Safeguarding measures** (India):
  - **Sangeet Natak Akademi** — documents performing arts.
  - **IGNCA Janapada Sampada** — folk/tribal intangible documentation.
  - **Zonal Cultural Centres** — support folk troupes.
  - **Guru Shishya Parampara Scheme** — financial support to masters.
  - **UNESCO inscription** — raises international profile + domestic conservation funds.

- **Intangible vs tangible trap examples**:

  | Item | Classification |
  |------|----------------|
  | **Taj Mahal** | Tangible WHS |
  | **Ramlila performance** | Intangible |
  | **Ajanta cave paintings** | Tangible (physical murals in cave) |
  | **Vedic chanting** | Intangible (oral recitation tradition) |
  | **Khajuraho temples** | Tangible |
  | **Garba dance during Navratri** | Intangible |

> **Exam note:** **Bharatanatyam NOT on UNESCO intangible list as named element** — Garba, Ramlila, Kumbh ARE — classic negative MCQ trap.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- Intangible = living practices, not physical buildings
- UNESCO 2003 Convention for intangible heritage
- Five domains: oral, performing, social, knowledge, craft skills
- India = 15 elements on Representative List (2023)
- Yoga inscribed 2016; Kumbh 2017; Garba 2023
- Ramlila 2008 — UP-North India tradition
- Dies without community transmission
- SNA + IGNCA document intangible heritage
- Guru Shishya Parampara Scheme supports masters
- Classical dances NOT individually listed (trap)

2. **(UPSC Prelims 2016 — pattern)** Yoga was inscribed on UNESCO Intangible Heritage in:

### Examples (14.2)

| Example | What it teaches |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Ramlila, UP** | Intangible performance tradition (2008) |

## 14.3 UNESCO World Heritage Sites

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **UNESCO World Heritage Sites** = places inscribed under **World Heritage Convention 1972** for **Outstanding Universal Value (OUV)** to humanity — cultural, natural, or mixed |
| **Legal** | Convention Concerning the Protection of World Cultural and Natural Heritage — adopted **16 November 1972**; operational guidelines updated periodically |
| **Exam usage** | Convention year, criteria i–x, WHC role, cultural vs natural vs mixed; India total count |

### UNESCO World Heritage Sites — How It Works

- **World Heritage Convention 1972** — mechanism:
  - **States Parties** (member countries) nominate sites.
  - **World Heritage Committee (WHC)** — 21 rotating member states — decides inscription.
  - **UNESCO World Heritage Centre** (Paris) — secretariat coordination.
  - **ICOMOS** evaluates cultural nominations; **IUCN** evaluates natural nominations.

- **Outstanding Universal Value (OUV)** — must prove site is **exceptional for all humanity**, not just one nation.

- **Selection criteria** (complete — exam uses numbers):

  | Criteria | Type | Meaning |
  |----------|------|---------|
  | **(i)** | Cultural | Masterpiece of human creative genius (Taj Mahal) |
  | **(ii)** | Cultural | Cultural interchange/influence (Ajanta Buddhist art) |
  | **(iii)** | Cultural | Unique cultural tradition evidence (Hampi Vijayanagara) |
  | **(iv)** | Cultural | Outstanding building/landscape type (Fatehpur Sikri city) |
  | **(v)** | Cultural | Traditional human settlement (Khajuraho-Nagara context) |
  | **(vi)** | Cultural | Associated with living beliefs/events (limited use — Bodh Gaya) |
  | **(vii)** | Natural | Superlative natural phenomena (Western Ghats) |
  | **(viii)** | Natural | Earth history/geology (Dholavira geological context) |
  | **(ix)** | Natural | Ecological processes (Sundarbans mangrove) |
  | **(x)** | Natural | Biodiversity/habitat (Kaziranga rhino) |

- **Site types**:

  | Type | Definition | India example |
  |------|------------|---------------|
  | **Cultural** | Criteria i–vi | Taj Mahal, Ajanta |
  | **Natural** | Criteria vii–x | Kaziranga, Sundarbans |
  | **Mixed** | Both sets | **Khangchendzonga National Park** (only Indian mixed) |

- **Inscription process steps**:
  1. Site on country's **Tentative List**.
  2. Nomination dossier prepared (management plan, buffer zone, OUV justification).
  3. ICOMOS/IUCN advisory body evaluation.
  4. WHC session vote — **inscribe**, **defer**, or **reject**.
  5. **Periodic reporting** — state of conservation monitoring.

- **Global statistics (exam context)**:
  - **~1,199** sites worldwide (2024 approximate).
  - **Italy + China** lead in site count globally.
  - **India = 43 sites** (2024–25) — ranks among top 5–6 countries.

- **Danger list**: Sites threatened by war, pollution, uncontrolled tourism — e.g., **Manas Wildlife Sanctuary** (India) was on danger list, later removed after recovery.

- **Related but DIFFERENT designations** (trap):

  | Designation | Convention/year | Focus |
  |-------------|-----------------|-------|
  | **World Heritage** | 1972 | Outstanding sites |
  | **Ramsar wetland** | 1971 | Wetland conservation |
  | **Biosphere Reserve** | UNESCO MAB 1971 | Sustainable development zones |
  | **Intangible Heritage** | 2003 | Living traditions |

> **Exam note:** **World Heritage Convention = 1972** | **Ramsar = 1971** — different treaties; Ramsar site ≠ WHS (Keoladeo is BOTH, but not all Ramsar are WHS).

### Exam Facts (raata)

- World Heritage Convention adopted 1972
- OUV = Outstanding Universal Value required
- WHC = 21-member committee decides inscription
- ICOMOS = cultural evaluation; IUCN = natural
- Criteria i–vi cultural; vii–x natural
- Mixed site = both criteria sets
- India has 43 WHS (2024–25)
- Tentative List = nomination pipeline
- World Heritage Day = 18 April
- Ramsar/Biosphere/ICH = separate conventions

## 14.4 UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage** framework = **2003 Convention** safeguarding **living cultural practices** through international cooperation and national policies |
| **Legal** | Convention for the Safeguarding of Intangible Cultural Heritage — adopted **17 October 2003**; entered force 2006 |
| **Exam usage** | Two lists (Representative + Urgent Safeguarding); 2003 vs 1972 convention trap; safeguarding measures |

### UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — How It Works

- **2003 Intangible Heritage Convention** — why created:
  - **1972 World Heritage Convention** could not protect **Ramlila, Vedic chanting, craft skills**.
  - Globalisation + urbanisation accelerated **cultural extinction**.
  - Convention emphasises **safeguarding through community participation**, not freezing traditions.

- **Two UNESCO lists for intangible heritage**:

  | List | Purpose | India status |
  |------|---------|--------------|
  | **Representative List** | Practices showing **cultural diversity** and community identity | **15 Indian elements** (2023) |
  | **Urgent Safeguarding List** | Heritage **threatened with extinction** — needs emergency action | India has **few/none** prominently examined (focus Representative List) |

- **Safeguarding measures** (Convention Article 2):
  - **Documentation** — audio-visual archives.
  - **Transmission programmes** — guru-shishya, school workshops.
  - **Legal protection** — national heritage laws.
  - **Funding** — grants to practitioner communities.
  - **Education** — include ICH in curricula.
  - **Trap**: Safeguarding ≠ **fossilising** — traditions must **evolve** while maintaining core identity.

- **Inscription process**:
  1. State Party submits nomination with **community consent**.
  2. **Intergovernmental Committee** (12 members initially, rotates) evaluates.
  3. Inscription on Representative or Urgent Safeguarding List.
  4. **Periodic reporting** on safeguarding status.

- **Global context**:
  - **700+ elements** on Representative List worldwide.
  - **India among active nominators** — Yoga, Kumbh, Garba successes.
  - **Multinational elements possible** — Nawrouz shared by several countries including India.

- **Key intangible heritage days**:
  - **17 October** — anniversary of 2003 Convention adoption (Intangible Heritage related observance).
  - **18 April** — **World Heritage Day** (tangible focus — ICOMOS) — UPPCS 2024 chronology question pattern.

- **Relationship to Indian policy**:
  - **Ministry of Culture** — nodal for UNESCO culture conventions.
  - **Sangeet Natak Akademi + IGNCA** — documentation partners.
  - **Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat** — promotes interstate intangible exchange.

- **Intangible vs World Heritage comparison**:

  | Feature | World Heritage 1972 | Intangible Heritage 2003 |
  |---------|-------------------|--------------------------|
  | **Object** | Sites/monuments | Practices/skills |
  | **Committee** | World Heritage Committee | ICH Intergovernmental Committee |
  | **India count** | 43 sites | 15 elements |
  | **Example** | Taj Mahal | Ramlila |

> **Exam note:** **2003 = Intangible Convention** | **1972 = World Heritage** — year-reversal trap; **17 Oct vs 18 April** dates.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- Intangible Heritage Convention adopted 2003
- Two lists: Representative + Urgent Safeguarding
- India = 15 Representative List elements
- Safeguarding requires community consent
- Intergovernmental Committee evaluates nominations
- Safeguarding ≠ freezing tradition
- Ministry of Culture = nodal ministry
- 17 October = 2003 Convention date
- 18 April = World Heritage Day (tangible)
- Nawrouz = multinational intangible element

## 14.6 UNESCO Intangible Heritage List (India)

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **UNESCO Intangible Heritage List (India)** = **15 elements** on the **Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** (as of 2023) — India's officially inscribed living traditions |
| **Exam usage** | Element ↔ year ↔ region; full list recall; NOT-listed traps (Bharatanatyam, Nautanki) |

### UNESCO Intangible Heritage List (India) — How It Works

- **India's complete Representative List** (all 15 elements — no gaps):

  | # | Element | Year inscribed | Region/state |
  |---|---------|----------------|--------------|
  | 1 | **Kutiyattam (Koodiyattam)** — Sanskrit theatre | 2001 | **Kerala** |
  | 2 | **Tradition of Vedic chanting** | 2003 | Pan-India (Kerala, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Odisha, Tamil Nadu traditions) |
  | 3 | **Ramlila — traditional performance of Ramayana** | 2008 | **North India (especially UP)** |
  | 4 | **Ramman — religious festival and ritual theatre** | 2009 | **Uttarakhand** |
  | 5 | **Mudiyettu — ritual theatre of Kerala** | 2010 | **Kerala** |
  | 6 | **Kalbelia folk songs and dances** | 2010 | **Rajasthan** |
  | 7 | **Chhau dance** | 2010 | **Jharkhand/West Bengal/Odisha** |
  | 8 | **Buddhist chanting of Ladakh** | 2012 | **Ladakh (J&K)** |
  | 9 | **Sankirtana — ritual singing/drumming of Manipur** | 2013 | **Manipur** |
  | 10 | **Traditional brass and copper craft of Punjab** | 2014 | **Punjab** |
  | 11 | **Nawrouz** (multinational) | 2016 | Shared — Indian communities included |
  | 12 | **Yoga** | 2016 | Pan-India |
  | 13 | **Kumbh Mela** | 2017 | **Prayagraj-Haridwar-Nashik-Ujjain** |
  | 14 | **Durga Puja in Kolkata** | 2021 | **West Bengal** |
  | 15 | **Garba of Gujarat** | 2023 | **Gujarat** |

- **Regional grouping for memorisation**:

  | Region | Intangible elements |
  |--------|---------------------|
  | **North India** | Ramlila (UP), Ramman (Uttarakhand), Vedic chanting, Yoga, Kumbh |
  | **South India** | Kutiyattam, Mudiyettu (Kerala) |
  | **East India** | Chhau (WB/JH/Odisha), Sankirtana (Manipur), Durga Puja (WB) |
  | **West India** | Garba (Gujarat) |
  | **Northwest** | Kalbelia (Rajasthan), Punjab metal craft |
  | **Himalayan** | Buddhist chanting Ladakh |

- **UP-relevant intangible heritage**:
  - **Ramlila (2008)** — **only UP-focused intangible inscription** — Braj, Varanasi, Ayodhya, Ramnagar traditions.
  - **Kumbh Mela (2017)** — **Prayagraj (UP)** is primary site among four rotation cities.
  - **Vedic chanting + Yoga** — pan-India, not UP-specific.
  - **NOT inscribed**: **Nautanki, Braj Holi, Chhath** — trap in "which is NOT listed."

- **Chronology chain (exam)**:
  - **2001** Kutiyattam (India's first intangible inscription among early batch).
  - **2008** Ramlila batch.
  - **2016** Yoga + Nawrouz.
  - **2017** Kumbh Mela.
  - **2021** Durga Puja.
  - **2023** Garba (most recent).

- **Multinational element**: **Nawrouz** — New Year festival shared by Iran, Afghanistan, Central Asia, and **Indian communities** — inscribed jointly.

- **What India has NOT inscribed (common wrong options)**:
  - **Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali** (as individual named items).
  - **Diwali, Holi** (as generic festivals — unlike Garba specific form).
  - **Nautanki** (UP folk theatre — not listed).
  - **Taj Mahal** (tangible WHS, not intangible).

- **Urgent Safeguarding List**: India has **not prominently featured** in UPSC/UPPCS questions on Urgent List — focus remains **Representative List 15 elements**.

> **Exam note:** **Full 15-element list** must be recallable | **Ramlila = UP** | **Garba 2023 = latest** | **Bharatanatyam NOT listed**.

## Common Traps — Don't Fall For These

| Trap | Correct answer |
|------|----------------|
| Varanasi ghats already UNESCO WHS? | **NO** — on **Tentative List** only |
| UP has 5+ World Heritage Sites? | **NO** — **only 3** (Taj, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri) |
| Bharatanatyam on UNESCO intangible list? | **NO** — Garba, Ramlila, Kumbh, Yoga ARE |
| Nautanki UNESCO intangible? | **NO** — Ramlila is UP intangible, not Nautanki |
| Taj Mahal = intangible heritage? | **NO** — **tangible** WHS |
| Ramsar = World Heritage same thing? | **NO** — **1971 Ramsar** vs **1972 WHS** |
| Intangible Convention = 1972? | **NO** — **2003** |
| World Heritage Convention = 2003? | **NO** — **1972** |
| Kaziranga = cultural WHS? | **NO** — **natural** WHS |
| Khangchendzonga = only natural? | **NO** — **mixed** (only Indian mixed) |
| Rudrasagar Ramsar in UP? | **NO** — **Tripura** (UPPCS 2025 Q58) |
| Sultanpur Ramsar in UP? | **NO** — **Haryana** (Q58) |
| World Heritage Day = 17 October? | **NO** — **18 April** (17 Oct = 2003 Convention date) |
| Garba inscribed 2017? | **NO** — **2023** |
| Nalanda WHS = 2021? | **NO** — **2016** |
