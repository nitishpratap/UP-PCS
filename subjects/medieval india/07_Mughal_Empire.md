## 7.1 Babur — Establishment of the Mughal Empire & Literary Contribution

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Zahiruddin Muhammad Babur** (1483–1530) — Timurid prince from Ferghana; founded **Mughal Empire** in India after **First Battle of Panipat (1526)** |
| **NCERT** | **Mughal Empire** = Indo-Persian-Timurid polity combining Central Asian military tradition with Indian revenue base; "Mughal" from Mongol descent claim via Timur |
| **Literary** | **Baburnama** (also **Tuzk-e-Babri / Tuzk-e-Baburi**) — Babur's autobiography in **Chaghtai Turki**; vivid memoir of India, flora, politics |
| **Exam use** | Establishment = **1526 Panipat**, not 1527 Khanwa; court language under Babur = **Turki**, not Persian (2025 Q3 trap) |

### Babur & Establishment — How It Works

- **Lineage:** Babur descended from **Timur** (paternal) and **Chengiz Khan** (maternal); lost Ferghana-Samarkand struggles; captured **Kabul 1504** — base for Indian campaigns.
- **Invitation to India:** **Daulat Khan Lodi** (Punjab governor) and **Rana Sanga** separately invited Babur against **Ibrahim Lodi** — Babur used invitation but pursued own imperial agenda.
- **First Battle of Panipat (20 April 1526):** Babur's army (~12,000) vs **Ibrahim Lodi** (~1 lakh); **Tulughma** (flanking envelopment) + **Araba** (wagon fortification) + field artillery broke Afghan mass charge; **Ibrahim killed** — Delhi Sultanate ended.
- **Delhi & Agra occupation:** Babur entered **Delhi** and **Agra**; assumed title **Padshah**; began Indo-Mughal state distinct from Afghan Lodi polity.
- **Consolidation battles:** **Khanwa (16 March 1527)** vs **Rana Sanga** + Mahmud Lodi — Rajput confederacy broken; **Chanderi (1528)** vs Medini Rai; **Ghagra (1529)** vs Afghan remnant + **Nusrat Shah** of Bengal — north Indian resistance crushed.
- **Administrative start:** Babur granted **iqta-like assignments**; retained **Lodi revenue officers** locally; distributed plunder; no full Mansabdari yet — structure developed under **Humayun/Akbar**.
- **Religious policy:** Babur ordered **wine broken** on accession; destroyed **idols at Gwalior** per memoir; also respected some local arrangements — early Mughal pragmatism.
- **Death & succession:** Babur died **26 December 1530** at Agra; buried first at Agra, later **Kabul (Bagh-e-Babur)** — UPPCS tomb match **Kabul = Babur**.
- **Literary contribution:** **Baburnama** written in **Chaghtai Turki** (later translated to Persian); describes **Hindustan** geography, **papaya, mango, peacock**; personal style — foundation of Mughal court literary culture.
- **UP angle:** Panipat on **Delhi–UP border**; Babur's route through **Punjab–Delhi–Doab**; early Mughal control of **Agra** gateway to **UP heartland**.

> **Exam note:** **UPPCS 2025 Q3** — A true (Babur wrote *Tuzk-e-Babri* in Chaghtai Turki); R false (**Persian** became Mughal court language — Turki was Babur's personal literary language, not permanent imperial official language) → answer **C**.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- Babur born **1483** Ferghana | Kabul base **1504**
- **Panipat I — 20 April 1526** — Ibrahim Lodi killed
- **Khanwa 1527** — Rana Sanga defeated
- **Chanderi 1528** | **Ghagra 1529**
- Babur died **1530** | buried **Kabul**
- **Baburnama** = Chaghtai **Turki** (NOT Persian court language)
- Tactics: **Tulughma + Araba + gunpowder**
- Mughal Empire **established 1526**, consolidated 1527–29

## 7.2 Humayun — Exile, Return & Brief Interregnum

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Nasiruddin Muhammad Humayun** (1508–1556) — Babur's eldest son; ruled **1530–1540** and **1555–1556**; lost empire to **Sher Shah Suri**, recovered with Safavid help |
| **NCERT** | Humayun's reign = **instability + Persian cultural infusion**; **Humayun's Tomb (Delhi)** = first major garden tomb on Indian soil — architectural milestone |
| **Exam** | **Sher Shah Suri (1540–1555)** = brief interregnum only here — administration detail covered in Sher Shah Suri syllabus block |
| **Literary** | **Humayunnama** by sister **Gulbadan Begum** — Mughal women's history source |

### Humayun — How It Works

- **Accession 1530:** Humayun inherited unstable empire; brothers **Kamran, Askari, Hindal** given provinces — **Kabul, Sambhal, Lahore** — chronic fraternal rivalry weakened centre.
- **Early campaigns:** Captured **Gujarat (1535)** from Bahadur Shah but lost quickly; failed siege coordination — reputation damaged among nobles.
- **Sher Shah rise:** **Farid Khan (Sher Shah)** consolidated Bihar–Bengal; defeated Humayun at **Chausa (1539)** and **Kannauj/Bilgram (1540)** — Humayun fled India.
- **Chausa 1539:** Night attack across **Ganga**; Humayun nearly drowned; escaped with small retinue — prelude to final defeat.
- **Kannauj 1540:** **Decisive** battle on Ganga plain; Mughal army routed; Humayun exiled — **15-year absence** from India (1540–1555).
- **Sher Shah interlude (brief):** Sher Shah ruled **1540–1545**; built **Grand Trunk Road**, **sarais**, **Rupiya** coin — Humayun took refuge at **Safavid court of Shah Tahmasp**; ceded **Kandahar** for military aid.
- **Return 1555:** With Persian support, Humayun defeated **Sikandar Shah Suri** at **Sirhind**; recovered **Delhi** — Mughal restoration.
- **Death 1556:** Fell from stairs of **Dinpanah (Sher Mandal)** library — **26 January 1556**; young **Akbar** (13) succeeded under **Bairam Khan** regency.
- **Family:** Wife **Hamida Banu Begum** (Akbar's mother); sister **Gulbadan Begum** wrote **Humayunnama**; **Bairam Khan** later regent — received title **Khan-i-Khana** from **Humayun** (2024 Q4 trap).
- **Architecture:** **Humayun's Tomb (1565 completion under Akbar)** — garden charbagh; influenced **Taj Mahal**; Delhi site — tomb match code **3**.

> **Exam note:** **2019 Q16** — Humayunnama–Gulbadan Begum = **correct match**; wrong pair in same question is Tughlaqnama–Ibn Battuta.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- Humayun reign: **1530–40**, **1555–56**

- Exile **1540–1555** | Safavid patronage
- Died **Jan 1556** — library fall
- **Hamida Banu** = wife | **Gulbadan** = Humayunnama
- **Bairam Khan** = regent for Akbar
- **Humayun's Tomb** = Delhi

## 7.3 Major Mughal Battles — Chronology & Significance

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Major Mughal battles** = decisive engagements establishing, losing, recovering, or contesting Mughal imperial sovereignty (1526–1707) |
| **NCERT** | Three **Panipat** battles — only **I (1526)** and **II (1556)** are Mughal foundation/consolidation; **III (1761)** = Mughal decline era |
| **Exam** | **2025 Q79** tests **Chausa → Kannauj → Dharmat → Samugarh** — NOT Panipat in that set |

### Major Battles — How It Works

- **Panipat I (20 April 1526):** **Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi** — Mughal empire **born**; end of Delhi Sultanate; fought near **Karnal/Panipat** corridor.
- **Khanwa (16 March 1527):** **Babur vs Rana Sanga** — Rajput-Afghan coalition broken; secured Mughal hold on **Rajasthan gateway**.
- **Chanderi (1528):** Babur vs **Medini Rai** — Bundelkhand fort taken; Jauhar performed — central India subdued.
- **Ghagra (1529):** Babur vs **Mahmud Lodi + Nusrat Shah (Bengal)** — last major Afghan challenge in north under Babur.
- **Chausa (26 June 1539):** **Humayun vs Sher Shah** — Mughal defeat; Humayun escaped across Ganga — first blow before empire lost.
- **Kannauj / Bilgram (17 May 1540):** **Humayun vs Sher Shah** — decisive; Humayun exiled — Mughal interregnum begins.
- **Panipat II (5 November 1556):** **Akbar (Bairam Khan) vs Hemu** — Hemu wounded by arrow, beheaded; Mughal rule **re-consolidated** after Humayun's return.
- **Haldighati (1576):** **Akbar (Raja Man Singh) vs Rana Pratap** — indecisive tactically; Mughal strategic advantage in Mewar plains; **not** in 2025 Q79 set but high UPPCS priority with Rajput topic.
- **Dharmat / Daurah (April 1658):** **Aurangzeb vs Jaswant Singh (Rathor) + Qasim Khan** — near **Ujjain**; Dara's allies defeated — opens succession war.
- **Samugarh (29 May 1658):** **Aurangzeb vs Dara Shikoh** — near **Agra**; Dara fled; **decisive** for Aurangzeb's accession.
- **Deorai / Khajwa (1658):** Final defeat of **Dara Shikoh**; executed **1659**; Aurangzeb empire secured.
- **2025 Q79 chronology code:** Question list **1=Kannauj, 2=Daurah, 3=Samugarh, 4=Chausa** → official answer **C (4-2-3-1)**; **historical sequence to raata:** Chausa **1539** → Kannauj **1540** → Dharmat **1658** → Samugarh **1658**.

> **Exam note:** Do not confuse **Dharmat (1658 succession)** with **Khanwa (1527 Babur)** or **Kannauj (1540 Sher Shah)** — three different wars, three different outcomes.

- **1526** Panipat I | **1527** Khanwa | **1528** Chanderi | **1529** Ghagra
- **1539** Chausa | **1540** Kannauj

- **2025 Q79 = C (4-2-3-1)**

## 7.4 Mughal Military Campaigns — Framework & Per Reign

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Mughal military campaigns** = territorial expansion, rebellion suppression, and succession wars fought by Mughal emperors and mansabdars across subcontinent |
| **NCERT** | Mughal army combined **Central Asian cavalry**, **Indian infantry**, **artillery**, **elephants**; **Mansabdari** linked military obligation to revenue (**jagir**) |
| **Exam** | Campaign **chronology** and **commander** matching — Raja Man Singh, Bir Singh Bundela, Mir Jumla |

### Military Campaigns — How It Works

- **Babur (1526–30):** North India conquest — **Delhi, Agra, Panipat, Khanwa, Chanderi, Ghagra**; introduced **field guns** systematically in north Indian warfare.
- **Humayun (restoration):** **1555 Sirhind** campaign vs Suris — recovered Delhi with Persian cavalry support; short second reign — no major fresh conquests.
- **Akbar (1556–1605):** **Malwa (1561–62)**, **Garh-Katanga (1564)**, **Chittor (1567–68)**, **Ranthambore (1569)**, **Gujarat (1572–73)**, **Bihar-Bengal (1574–76)**, **Kashmir (1586)**, **Sind (1591)**, **Khandesh (1601)**, **Ahmadnagar entry (1596 onward)** — empire reaches maximum under Akbar–early Jahangir.
- **Jahangir (1605–27):** **Kangra (1620–22)** fort taken; **Mewar submission (1615)** of Amar Singh; Deccan pressure continued via **Khurram (Shah Jahan)** — campaign against **Mewar, Kangra, Deccan sultanates**.
- **Shah Jahan (1628–58):** **Deccan**: **Ahmadnagar annexed (1636)**, campaigns in **Bijapur, Golkonda**; **Central Asia**: **Balkh-Badakhshan (1646–47)** — failed; **1632–33**: **Portuguese Hooghly** expelled from Bengal.
- **Aurangzeb (1658–1707):** Long **Deccan stay (1681–1707)**; **Bijapur 1686**, **Golkonda 1687** (last ruler **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah** — 2020 Q34); **Maratha wars**; **Yusufzai (1670s)**, **Assam (Mir Jumla)**, **Sikh Guru Tegh Bahadur executed 1675**.
- **Artillery & fort warfare:** Mughals excelled at **siege artillery** — Chittor, Kangra, Deccan forts; combined **mining + bombardment + negotiation**.
- **Naval dimension:** Limited Mughal navy — **Siddi of Janjira**, **Mughal fleet at Surat**; Bengal coast campaigns vs **Arakan, Portuguese**.

## 7.9 Mughal Administration, Structure & Nature of the Empire

### Administration — How It Works

- **Emperor (Padshah):** Supreme sovereign — **legislation, appointment, revenue, war**; **farmans** carried imperial orders; under Akbar **wakil** role reduced.

- **Nature of empire:** **Composite ruling class** — Turani, Irani, Shaikhzada, **Rajput**, Indian Muslim nobles; loyalty via **mansab + jagir**, not ethnicity alone.

## 7.10 Mansabdari System

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Mansabdari** — rank system assigning **zat (personal)** and **sawar (cavalry)** grades; tied to **jagir** revenue for salary |
| **NCERT** | Introduced in organized form under **Akbar**; refined by **Jahangir (du-aspah sih-aspah)** and **Aurangzeb (increased sawar obligations)** |
| **Exam** | **NOT hereditary** — son does not automatically inherit father's mansab (2019 Q92) |

### Mansabdari — How It Works

- **Origin & development:** Early Mughal **tankhwah** cash salary; **Akbar** systematized **mansab** numbers (10 to 7000 for nobles; emperor **7000+** or **8000+** symbolically).
- **Zat rank:** Personal status grade — determined **salary scale**, **court precedence (maratab)**, **administrative eligibility**.
- **Sawar rank:** Obligation to maintain **horses/troopers** — **sawar** could equal, half, or third of **zat** (rules tightened under Jahangir).
- **Jagir assignment:** Mansabdars received **jagirs** (revenue assignments) — **jama** (estimated revenue) vs **hasil** (actual collection); **wazifajat** for pay.
- **Dagh & chehra:** **Branding horses** + ** descriptive rolls** — prevent fraud in troop reviews (Akbar period enforcement).
- **Non-hereditary principle:** Mansab held **during emperor's pleasure**; on death, **jagir resumed** (escheat); sons might get lower rank after **tazkira** review — **not automatic inheritance** (2019 Q92 trap).
- **Categories of nobles:** **Turani, Irani, Shaikhzada, Rajput, Deccani** — **mixed recruitment** strengthened empire.
- **Du-aspah sih-aspah:** Jahangir's rule — some mansabdars maintain **2–3 horses per sawar** — increased military readiness cost.
- **Civil-military fusion:** Same nobles ran **provinces, armies, karkhanas** — no separate civil service cadre.
- **Decline factor:** **Jagir shortage** under Aurangzeb — **jagir transfer (tabadla)** to less productive areas — nobles dissatisfied.

> **Exam note:** **2019 Q92** — Statement 1 only correct: Mansabdari was **NOT hereditary** → answer **A**.

- **Dagh** = horse branding
- Jahangir: **du-aspah sih-aspah**

## 7.11 Mughal Revenue System — Zabt, Dahsala & Todar Mal

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Mughal revenue system** — land tax (**mal/mal guzar**) based on **measurement**, **crop rates**, and **local intermediaries (zamindars)** |
| **Zabt** | **Measurement-based assessment** — classify land by quality, fix **dastur-ul-amal** crop rates per bigha |
| **Dahsala** | **Ten-year (dah = ten) average yield** system — **1580–81** settlement under Akbar via **Raja Todar Mal** |
| **Exam** | Todar Mal served **Sher Shah** first — then Akbar's **Diwan-i-Ashraf** |

### Revenue System — How It Works

- **Pre-Akbar chaos:** Early Mughal **fixed demand (qabuliat)** + **middlemen** — unstable; **Bairam Khan** period mixed assignments.
- **Sher Shah legacy (brief):** Todar Mal learned **measurement (jarib)** under Sher Shah — **zabti** principles carried to Mughal reform under Akbar.
- **Todar Mal's role:** **Raja Todar Mal** — Akbar's finance minister; earlier **Bihar & Gujarat** revenue reform success → empire-wide **1580–81 dahsala settlement**.
- **Dahsala method:** Average produce of **past ten years** calculated per **bigha**; cash revenue fixed in **rupees** using **dastur** (schedule) rates — reduces peasant uncertainty.
- **Zabt measurement:** **Jarib** (bamboo rope with iron rings — standard length); land classified **polaj (annual), parati (fallow), chachar, banjar** — different rates.
- **Zamindars:** **Local revenue collectors/intermediaries** — often **hereditary**; paid **peshkash** to state; difference between **jama (demand)** and **hasil (collection)** their concern.
- **Nasaq system:** **Rough estimation** without full measurement — used in some areas (Bengal, parts of Deccan) where zabt impractical.
- **Revenue terminology:** **Mal** = land revenue | **Abwab** = illegal cesses (Akbar tried to curb) | **Taccavi** = agricultural loans (revived under Aurangzeb).
- **Khalsa vs jagir:** **Khalisa** lands revenue to **imperial treasury**; **jagir** lands revenue to **mansabdars** — accounting via **Diwan-i-Khalisa** and **Diwan-i-Jagir**.
- **UP implementation:** **Doab**, **Awadh**, **Allahabad**, **Agra subah** — early zabt heartland; **Banaras** region zamindars integrated into Mughal rolls.

> **Exam note:** **Zabt** = method of assessment; **Dahsala** = ten-year averaging time frame — both **Todar Mal + Akbar**; do not credit **Jahangir** for dahsala.

### Exam Facts (raata)

- **Todar Mal** = revenue minister (Akbar)
- **Dahsala 1580–81** = ten-year average
- **Zabt** = measured assessment
- **Jarib** = measuring rope
- Land types: **polaj, parati, chachar, banjar**
- **Zamindar** = intermediary collector
- **Mal** = land revenue

## 7.12 Mughal Coinage & Currency System

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **General** | **Mughal coinage** — standardized **gold (mohur), silver (rupiya), copper (dam)** tri-metallic system |
| **NCERT** | **Akbar's reforms** — uniform weight, wide circulation; **Ilahi coins** with religious slogans |
| **Exam** | **2019 Q12** — Akbar introduced **dam**; A true, R explains → **A** |

### Coinage — How It Works

- **Pre-Akbar mix:** **Sher Shah's rupiya** (silver 178 grains) influenced Akbar; Lodi-Suri-Mughal transition needed standardization.
- **Akbar's tri-metallic system:** **Mohur (gold)** — high value; **Rupiya (silver)** — standard unit; **Dam (copper)** — **1/40 rupiya** — small transactions.
- **Dam significance:** **Copper dam** made daily market trade efficient — **2019 Q12 A/R** — Akbar introduced dam for **small change** (R explains A).
- **Ilahi coinage:** **1580s** — coins bore **Ilahi slogans** (Allah-u-Akbar) — linked to **Din-i-Ilahi** period symbolism.
- **Jahangir's innovations:** **Zodiac coins** — portrait with **sign of zodiac**; **Nur Jahan** coins in name; artistic peak of Mughal numismatics.
- **Shah Jahan:** Standard **Shah Jahani rupiya** — continued silver stability; **portrait-less** orthodox style after early reign.
- **Aurangzeb:** Removed **Kalima** from some coins initially; **proselytizing epigraphs** on later issues — religious policy reflected on currency.
- **Weight standards:** **Rupiya ~11.6 grams** (Akbar-Shah Jahan variants); regional mints — **Agra, Delhi, Lahore, Surat, Allahabad**.
- **Economic role:** Revenue **assessed in cash (rupees)** under zabt — coin circulation essential; **hundi** bills for long-distance trade.
- **Trap pairs:** **Dam = copper** (not gold); **Mohur = gold**; **Rupiya = silver** — match correctly in NOT-matched questions.

## 7.17 Rajput Policy, Sulh-i-Kul, Religious & Social Reforms, Din-i-Ilahi

### Definitions (learn all — exams pick different ones)

| Source | Definition |

| **Rajput policy** | **Matrimonial alliances** (Akbar married **Rajput princesses**); **Rajput mansabdars**; selective **annex vs alliance** |

### Rajput Policy & Reforms — How It Works

- **Early conflict phase:** **Chittor, Ranthambore** sieges — **hard military** against **Mewar (Rana Udai Singh)**.
- **Alliance phase:** Marriage to **Harkha Bai (Jodha Bai)** of **Amber** — **1562**; **Rajput sons-in-law** at court — **Bhagwan Das, Man Singh** rose high.
- **Mansabdari integration:** **Raja Bhagwan Das (2500)**, **Raja Man Singh (7000)** — **Rajputs at highest mansab**; **Rathor, Kachhwaha, Sisodia** differential treatment.
- **Mewar exception:** **Rana Pratap** refused submission — **Haldighati 1576** — **Mewar hills** outside full Mughal control until **Jahangir (1615)** peace with **Amar Singh**.
- **Sulh-i-Kul:** Policy of **universal peace** — respect all religions; **no forced conversion** as state policy under Akbar; **Ibadat Khana** debates informed this.
- **Religious reforms:** **Jizya abolished 1564**; **pilgrim tax** on Hindus abolished; **cow slaughter ban** in empire (political-religious balance); **Shia-Sunni** neutrality attempted.

- **Social reforms:** **Sati** — required **permission of local authorities** (Bengal-Bihar rules); **slave trade** curbed; **legal age** for marriage (**14 girls, 16 boys** in regulations); **foeticide** discouraged.
- **Aurangzeb reversal:** **Jizya reimposed 1679**; **temple policy** hardened — marks break from **Sulh-i-Kul** era.
- **UP Rajput zones:** **Amer, Marwar, Karauli** connections; **Bundelkhand** alliances; **Allahabad** governorships to royal princes managing Doab-Rajput interface.

## Common Traps — Don't Fall For These

1. **Babur court language** — **Turki/Chaghtai**, NOT Persian (**2025 Q3 = C**).
2. **Buland Darwaza purpose** — **Gujarat victory 1572**, NOT Jahangir's birth (**2025 Q49 = C**).
3. **Tombs swap** — Babur = **Kabul**, not Delhi; Shah Jahan buried **Agra**, not Lahore (**2025 Q57 = A**).
4. **Battle chronology** — **Chausa 1539 before Kannauj 1540** before **1658 Dharmat/Samugarh** (**2025 Q79 = C**).
5. **Khanwa vs Kannauj vs Panipat** — Khanwa **1527 Babur-Sanga**; Kannauj **1540 Humayun-Sher Shah**; Panipat I **1526 Babur-Lodi**.
6. **Bairam Khan title** — **Khan-i-Khana from Humayun**, not Akbar (**2024 Q4 trap**).
7. **Hamida Banu** — **Humayun's wife**, not Alauddin Khalji (**2023 Q34**).
8. **Tappa music** — **Muhammad Shah**, not Jahangir (**2023 Q38 = D**).
9. **Hawkins** — **Jahangir's court (1608)**, not Akbar (**2023 Q31**).
10. **Aurangzeb succession order** — **Shuja beaten → Dharmat → Samugarh → Deorai** (**2022 Q103 = A**).
11. **Kavindra Acharya** — **Shah Jahan**, not Akbar (**2022 Q146**).
12. **Mansabdari hereditary** — **FALSE** (**2019 Q92 = 1 only**).
13. **Dam coin A/R** — Both true, R explains A (**2019 Q12 = A**).
14. **Tughlaqnama author** — **Barani**, not Ibn Battuta (**2019 Q16** — Humayunnama–Gulbadan pair is correct in same Q).
15. **Monument chronology** — **Atala → Sher Shah tomb → Humayun → Taj** (**2019 Q91 = B**).
16. **Din-i-Ilahi ≠ Sulh-i-Kul** — Din-i-Ilahi = small **1582 court circle**; Sulh-i-Kul = broad tolerance policy.
17. **Din-i-Ilahi ≠ state religion** — NOT imposed on all subjects — tiny elite initiation.
18. **Golkonda last ruler** — **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah 1687**, not Ibrahim Adil Shah (**2020 Q34**).
19. **Nahr-i-Bihisht** — **Shah Jahan**, not Babur/Akbar (**2020 Q42 = C**).
20. **Military state A/R** — Both true; R explains A (**2021 Q126 = A**).
21. **Abul Fazl vs Faizi** — Abul Fazl = **Akbarnama**; Faizi = **poet laureate** — brothers.
22. **Akbarnama vs Ain-i-Akbari** — Both **Abul Fazl** — narrative vs administrative gazetteer.
23. **Akbar's tomb** — **Sikandra (Agra)** — not in 2025 four-match but frequently tested separately.
24. **Panipat II** — **Hemu 1556**, not Ibrahim Lodi (that's Panipat I).
25. **Jahangir vs Akbar on expansion** — **Allahabad fort 1583 = Akbar**; Buland Darwaza = Akbar's **Gujarat** victory.
