### Question 301
**Subject:** Modern Indian History | **Topic:** Dandi March & Civil Disobedience Movement (1930)

Consider the following statements regarding the historic Dandi March:
1. Mahatma Gandhi began the march from Sabarmati Ashram on March 12, 1930, accompanied by 78 chosen ashram followers.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

B. 2 and 3 only  
C. 1 and 3 only

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:**
- **Core Fact & Solution:**

    - **March Launch:** Started March 12, 1930, from Sabarmati Ashram with 78 volunteers; arrived at coastal Dandi (Navsari district, Gujarat) on April 5; broke the salt monopoly on **April 6, 1930**.

- **आयोग का Trap / Ratta Point:** Sarojini Naidu accompanied Gandhi at Dandi and famously hailed him: *"Hail, Deliverer!"*

### Question 302
**Subject:** Indian Polity | **Topic:** Pardoning Powers: President (Article 72) vs Governor (Article 161)

Consider the following statements comparing the pardoning powers of the President and the Governor:
1. The President can grant pardon, reprieves, respites, or remissions of punishment in all cases where the sentence is by a Court Martial (Military Court), whereas the Governor has no such power.
2. The President is the exclusive constitutional authority empowered to grant pardon in cases of death sentence; the Governor CANNOT pardon a death sentence.
3. The Governor can suspend, remit, or commute a sentence of death under Article 161.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

**Options:**
A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 2 and 3 only  
C. 1 and 3 only  
D. 1, 2 and 3  

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:**
- **Core Fact & Solution:**
  - All three statements are correct:
    - **Court Martial:** President alone possesses the power to pardon court-martial sentences (Article 72(1)(a)). Governor has zero power regarding military courts.
    - **Death Sentence:** Under Article 72, the **President alone can PARDON a death sentence** (completely wiping out guilt and sentence).
    - **Governor's Power regarding Death Sentence:** Under Article 161, the Governor CANNOT pardon a death sentence, BUT the Governor **CAN suspend, remit, or commute** a death sentence under state law!
- **आयोग का Trap / Ratta Point:** Landmark ruling *Maru Ram (1980)* and *Kehar Singh (1989)*: The pardoning power under Articles 72 and 161 is exercised by the President/Governor on the **binding advice of the Council of Ministers**, not at personal discretion!

### Question 304
**Subject:** General Science (Biology & Physics) | **Topic:** Pathologies of the Human Eye

Match List-I (Eye Disorder) with List-II (Pathological Cause / Nature) and select the correct answer:

**List-I (Disorder)**
A. Presbyopia  
B. Astigmatism  
C. Cataract  
D. Glaucoma  

**List-II (Pathology)**
1. Progressive clouding/opacity of the crystalline eye lens, blocking light from reaching the retina  
2. Loss of elasticity of the eye lens and gradual weakening of ciliary muscles with aging  
3. Increased intraocular fluid pressure (aqueous humor) causing irreversible damage to the optic nerve  
4. Aspherical / uneven curvature of the cornea or lens, causing light to focus at multiple points  

**Options:**
A. A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3  
B. A-4, B-2, C-1, D-3  
C. A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3  
D. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2  

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:**
- **Core Fact & Solution:**
  - **Presbyopia:** Aging defect (usually after age 40); ciliary muscles lose contractility; near-point recedes; corrected with **Bifocal lenses**.
  - **Astigmatism:** Cornea is curved like a rugby ball rather than a spherical football; horizontal and vertical rays focus at different planes; corrected with **Cylindrical lenses**.
  - **Cataract (Motiyabind):** Age-related denaturing of lens proteins (*crystallins*); lens becomes cloudy/opaque; treated surgically via phacoemulsification and Intraocular Lens (IOL) implantation.
  - **Glaucoma (Kala Motia):** Obstruction in the drainage canal (Canal of Schlemm) leads to elevated Intraocular Pressure (IOP > 21 mm Hg), permanently damaging optic nerve axons.
- **आयोग का Trap / Ratta Point:** Myopia uses Concave lens; Hypermetropia uses Convex lens; Presbyopia uses Bifocal lens; Astigmatism uses Cylindrical lens!

### Question 306

- **Core Fact & Solution:**

### Question 307
**Subject:** Indian Economy | **Topic:** Taxation Principles & Gini Coefficient

Match List-I (Tax Structure) with List-II (Definition / Impact on Income Inequality) and select the correct answer:

**List-I (Tax Structure)**
A. Progressive Tax  
B. Regressive Tax  
C. Proportional Tax  
D. Degressive Tax  

**List-II (Definition & Impact)**
1. Tax rate remains constant regardless of the taxpayer's income level  
2. Tax rate increases as the income increases, reducing the post-tax Gini coefficient (e.g. Personal Income Tax)  
3. Tax rate increases up to a certain maximum income limit, after which it remains flat / uniform  
4. Effective tax burden falls more heavily on lower-income earners than on the wealthy (e.g. Indirect taxes on essential goods)  

**Options:**
A. A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3  
B. A-1, B-2, C-4, D-3  
C. A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3  
D. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2  

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:**
- **Core Fact & Solution:**
  - **Progressive Tax:** Marginal tax rate increases as taxable base increases; redistributive; narrows the rich-poor gap (reduces Gini coefficient).
  - **Regressive Tax:** Poor people spend a higher proportion of their income on consumed goods; thus indirect consumption taxes (like GST or VAT) place a disproportionately higher percentage burden on lower-income earners.
  - **Proportional Tax (Flat Tax):** Rate remains constant across all income brackets.
  - **Degressive Tax:** Blend of progressive and proportional; rate rises progressively up to a threshold income, and thereafter remains fixed at that upper rate.
- **आयोग का Trap / Ratta Point:** Direct taxes (income/corporate tax) are generally PROGRESSIVE; Indirect taxes (excise/GST) are inherently REGRESSIVE!

### Question 311
**Subject:** Indian Polity | **Topic:** Money Bills (Article 110)

Consider the following statements regarding a Money Bill in the Indian Parliament:
1. Under Article 110, a Bill is deemed to be a Money Bill if it contains ONLY provisions dealing with taxation, borrowings, custody of the Consolidated Fund of India, or matters incidental thereto.
2. A Money Bill can be introduced ONLY in the Lok Sabha, and ONLY on the prior recommendation of the President of India.
3. The Rajya Sabha has no power to amend or reject a Money Bill; it can only make recommendations, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject, within a mandatory period of 14 days.
4. If a question arises whether a Bill is a Money Bill or not, the decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha thereon is final.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

**Options:**
A. 1, 2 and 3 only  
B. 2, 3 and 4 only  
C. 1, 3 and 4 only  
D. 1, 2, 3 and 4  

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:**
- **Core Fact & Solution:**
  - All four statements are correct:
    - **Definition (Article 110(1)):** Enlists 7 specific fiscal matters (imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, regulation of any tax; government borrowings; custody of Consolidated or Contingency Fund).
    - **Introduction (Article 109 & 117(1)):** Originates ONLY in Lok Sabha with President's recommendation. Cannot be introduced in Rajya Sabha.

Consider the following statements regarding the Doppler Effect:
1. The Doppler Effect is the apparent change in the observed frequency or wavelength of a wave due to relative motion between the source and the observer.

**Explanation:**
- **Core Fact & Solution:**
  - All three statements are correct:
    - Formulated by Christian Doppler in 1842:
      - **Sound Waves:** When a train approaches an observer blowing its horn, apparent pitch/frequency is **HIGHER** ($f' > f$). When receding, apparent pitch is **LOWER** ($f' < f$).
